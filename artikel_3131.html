Does Bill Ackman Know Something Wall Street Doesn't? The Billionaire Investor Keeps Buying a Tech Stock That's Now Down 44% Over the Past Year.

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Ackman and his team at Pershing Square Capital Management typically hold just 12 to 15 stocks in their portfolio at any one time.

By Bram Berkowitz – Nov 27, 2026 at 5:20AM EST

Bill Ackman and his team at Pershing Square, the parent company of the investment manager Pershing Square Capital Management, run a concentrated portfolio. Ackman is not afraid to play contrarian, and he isn't afraid to hold stocks long term, which is a different strategy from that employed by most standard hedge funds.

Ackman has also purchased stakes in several hyperscalers driving the artificial intelligence revolution. Given Pershing's impressive track record and Ackman's huge following, investors are always curious about what the billionaire is buying.

In the third quarter, Ackman and his team continued to load up on one tech stock that's down by about 44% over the past year. Does he know something Wall Street doesn't?

Nearly all sectors have been disrupted by the emergence of artificial intelligence, even companies that were considered cutting-edge tech stocks very recently. The video streaming business faces threats too. Investors are now concerned about lower barriers to entry for content creation, as well as the rising popularity of short-form content from platforms like YouTube.

These concerns have spooked investors about the outlook for streaming giant Netflix (NFLX +2.39%). The stock, which hit an all-time high in June 2025, has been crushed over the past year.

Reports of declining viewer engagement have led many to believe an industrywide shift is underway, and Netflix's management team more or less acknowledged those concerns on its Q3 earnings call last month.

"Turning to the U.S., the other region, our engagement is solid, and it will allow us to continue to grow as we have for the last several years," co-CEO Greg Peters said on the call. "We know that we can do better, and that better is our upside."

While Ackman and Pershing had previously owned a stake in Netflix several years ago, they closed it. Then, they opened a new position in the second quarter of 2026. In Q3, Pershing increased that position by 18%; as of the end of the quarter, Netflix made up 3.5% of the portfolio.

In its June interim report, Pershing said that it believes investors are underestimating the cost of creating long-form, high-quality content, which it said remains "among the most compute-intensive tasks." Pershing also noted that the popularity of short-form content has had no significant effect on Netflix's results.

In Pershing's Q3 update earlier this month, the company reiterated its belief in Netflix and said investors are focusing too much on engagement as measured by viewer watch time. It believes the company's foray into live programming and other new content will make this less of a concern.

Pershing is also excited about Netflix's advertising business.

"Advertising revenue continues to scale rapidly, and the lower-priced ad-supported tier is meaningfully expanding Netflix's addressable market," Pershing wrote. "AI should further enhance the business by improving content recommendation and ad targeting algorithms, driving efficiencies and enabling new forms of interactive and personalized content."

Pershing believes Netflix can generate double-digit percentage revenue growth over the next few years, while compounding earnings at 20% annually.

There's no question that the rise of short-form content poses a challenge for companies like Netflix, which, once upon a time, may not have considered short form as a competitor.

I agree with Ackman and his team that the market may be underestimating how difficult it is to replicate high-quality, long-form content with cheaper new technologies. I still think that is evident whether you look at successful Netflix shows or at long-form podcasts that continue to do well. Netflix has also raised subscription prices across all its tiers and continues to see healthy retention, according to management.

Netflix will likely need to invest in new types of content and keep adjusting its strategy to stay ahead in the entertainment space, but the company has the expertise and resources to do so. AI can also help advance and optimize its existing business.

While nobody knows exactly how AI and short-form content will continue to affect the streaming video space, I like the risk-reward proposition here.
