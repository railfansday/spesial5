The 8 Best Standing Desks for Walking Pads, Tested and Reviewed

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

From budget picks to tricked-out options for a better WFH space.

You log a lot of time at your desk, making it fair to wonder how to create a workspace that’s as comfortable and healthy for you as possible. If sitting on your butt all day isn’t your idea of a good time, it makes sense to consider investing in a walking pad. To create a seamless workspace, you’ll need a special desk to go with that. The best standing desks for walking pads allow you to cruise along as you work, while still providing all the usual trappings you expect from a desk.

While TikTok is packed with happy-looking people working this way, the treadmill desk is more than a trend. Research ties sitting all day with a higher risk of developing back pain and even issues with cardiovascular health, making it a good idea to try to get up and move as much as possible during your day.

“Prolonged sitting has been linked to a number of health concerns, which is why many people are turning to standing desks,” says Andrew J. Bach, DO, a physiatrist at Cedars-Sinai Spine Center in Los Angeles “A standing desk allows you to get your usual computer-based work done while on your feet, and it comes with several benefits over sitting all day.” (He lists off better circulation, less strain on your back, more calories burned, and better digestion as just a few.)

But proper form is critical with these. “People should keep in mind how long they have been in a certain position, and their posture specifically,” says Meg Darmofal, DPT, a physical therapist at Michigan State University. You don’t want to shrug your shoulders, reach too far forward, bend over, or crane your neck up or down to see your screen, she says—all of those can put you at risk for developing aches and pains.

The desk is admittedly basic, and the treadmill doesn't offer adjustable incline. Geller also found the walking surface a little narrow for jogging, making this a better option for walking while working than more intense workouts.

Every standing desk has either a set height or height range. Bach points out that height is a “key factor” in your desk. “If you’re especially tall or short, make sure the desk can adjust to a level that works for you,” he says. Just keep in mind that you’ll want to include the height of your walking pad when figuring out the right standing desk height for you, since you’ll be on top of it while you use your desk. “Returning a bulky standing desk can be a hassle, so it’s best to get the right fit from the start,” Bach says. Ideally, your desk should be high enough that you can type comfortably while standing on your walking pad, without having to hunch over or raise your shoulders.

Not all standing desks are adjustable, but many are—and it’s a nice feature to have. “Being able to easily lower and raise the desk is important because, in some scenarios, returning to sitting is good as well,” Darmofal says. Some desks can be heavy and awkward to move manually, which can be frustrating if you plan to switch between sitting and standing during the day, Bach says. “Motorized options are the most convenient, since they transition with the push of a button,” he adds. (Just keep in mind that motorized options tend to cost more.)

At the end of the day, this is still a desk, and you need one that will hold your stuff. Make sure you factor in space to hold your computer, pens, notepad, water bottle, and anything else you usually have on your desk. If you're using a walking pad, you'll also want enough room for your keyboard and monitor so you don't have to lean forward while walking.

It’s important to make sure that your desk’s legs and any footing are wide enough to leave space for your walking pad to slip between. Pay close attention to the walking pad dimensions and desk dimensions, leaving a little room around the pad so you’re not jamming it in there.
