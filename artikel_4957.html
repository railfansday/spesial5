Nvidia, AMD, Micron Sink as Anthropic Closes 17% Above IPO Price

Anthropic made stock market history Thursday, raising $81.34 billion in the largest initial IPO share sale ever. But Wall Street's response was considerably less enthusiastic than the demand for the Claude developer's shares.

The stock opened at $111.50 on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, a 34% premium over its $83 offering price, briefly pushing the company's fully diluted valuation past $2 trillion.

The rally faded quickly, however, with shares dropping toward $93 before closing today at $97.17.

The offering involved 980 million shares and surpassed SpaceX's original $75 billion IPO in June. As Decrypt previously reported, SpaceX's expanded final offering reached $86.25 billion, meaning Anthropic has not surpassed that completed deal.

Investors initially welcomed the listing, sending technology stocks higher before the opening bell. By the afternoon, however, the Nasdaq Composite was down 2.06%, the S&P 500 had fallen 1.9%, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average was off 2.17%.

The selloff extended across the AI supply chain. A Thursday afternoon market heatmap showed Nvidia falling 3.4%, Broadcom losing 2.07%, and memory chipmaker Micron slipping 2.17%. Microsoft was down 1.97%, while cloud infrastructure and software provider Oracle declined 2.97%.

AMD was among the hardest hit, down 7.73% in the day, although its decline had a separate catalyst. Reuters reported that the chipmaker came under pressure after investigative outlet Hunterbrook disclosed that its affiliated investment fund had taken a short position in the company.

The picture was not uniformly negative. Palo Alto Networks rose 6%, CrowdStrike gained 2.24%, and Intel added 2.25% in the same snapshot. The divergence shows that investors were distinguishing between companies rather than selling every technology stock indiscriminately.

The broader weakness comes as investors question the cost of building AI infrastructure and the returns companies can generate from that spending. Nvidia's latest earnings beat expectations, but uncertainty following Jensen Huang's resignation has continued to weigh on the semiconductor giant.

Anthropic also faces an expensive expansion. Its IPO disclosures detailed approximately $900.9 billion in infrastructure and computing commitments extending through 2045, alongside another $150 billion to $175 billion in estimated payments under less precisely defined arrangements.

"Anthropic's record-breaking IPO has failed to rekindle enthusiasm for tech stocks," Joe Maher, market economist at Capital Economics, told Reuters.

Nvidia's weakness was notable given its latest results. The chipmaker reported $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue on Tuesday, up 103% from a year earlier, and forecast stronger-than-expected sales for the following quarter.

Even those numbers failed to erase concerns over the departure of longtime CEO Jensen Huang after allegations of extramarital affairs and possible leaks of corporate secrets and the sustainability of the AI spending boom.

The pressure also reached companies that rent out computing capacity. Neocloud operators CoreWeave and Nebius fell 5.0% and 7.8%, respectively. Software companies were weak as well: Salesforce declined 3.17% and Adobe lost 2.03% in the afternoon heatmap. From chips and data centers to enterprise software, the losses extended beyond any single corner of the AI trade.

Anthropic's IPO prospectus shows how closely these companies are tied together. Nvidia and Google each committed roughly $10 billion to a conditional private share placement alongside the IPO.

AMD agreed to invest $2.5 billion; Broadcom, Micron and Microsoft committed $1 billion apiece; and TSMC pledged $500 million. The $26 billion placement is separate from the public offering. Several of these investors are also key suppliers or infrastructure partners in the AI ecosystem.

That creates a central question for investors: How much of the money flowing into AI companies will ultimately translate into profitable demand for chips, cloud capacity and software?

Anthropic's blockbuster debut demonstrates demand for its shares, but Thursday's broader decline shows that Wall Street is not ready to extend that confidence to every company financing or supplying the AI boom.
