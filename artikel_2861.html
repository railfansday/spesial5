Explosive plays again haunt Ohio State football's defense in upset loss to Oregon

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Oregon running back Dierre Hill Jr. found the crease.

It was early in the third quarter when two pulling offensive linemen opened a hole off the right side just past midfield.

Hill slipped through the space and emerged past Ohio State linebacker Christian Alliegro, who dove toward his feet but missed the tackle.

As Hill emerged at the second level, he turned around nickel back Jay Timmons and outran cornerback Devin Sanchez before using a block by wide receiver Dakorien Moore that escorted him into the end zone.

The 48-yard touchdown was emblematic of issues that plagued No. 12 Ohio State in its 41-28 upset loss to No. 23 Oregon on Nov. 7.

As the Buckeyes suffered a second straight defeat, their defense remained vulnerable to explosive plays, much as it had a week earlier at USC when it allowed completions as long as 69 and 70 yards.

"The number of explosive plays has really hurt," coach Ryan Day said.

The long pickups kept the Buckeyes' defense on the field, allowing the Ducks to extend drives and string together 38 unanswered points. Dylan Raiola, a former backup quarterback who has taken over for Dante Moore after he suffered a season-ending concussion in September, completed eight passes of at least 15 yards, including five on third down.

The Buckeyes struggled to strike a balance between dropping into coverage or bringing extra pressure.

When Oregon faced a third-and-15 at Ohio State's 29-yard line late in the second quarter, the back end drifted too deep, leaving Hill open underneath. Raiola checked down to Hill, who gained 20 yards on the completion and set up the Ducks' first touchdown.

Defensive coordinator Matt Patricia dialed up a blitz on a third-and-11 on Oregon's following series, sending linebacker Riley Pettijohn off the edge, but the pressure never got home. Raiola found running back Brandon Smith on a screen that went for 30 yards. The Ducks ended the drive with another touchdown to gain a 17-14 lead just before halftime.

Both of Raiola's touchdown passes came against the blitz, including a 15-yard score early in the fourth quarter.

While the Buckeyes were on the heels, Raiola sensed the all-out blitz and dumped off a pass to Smith, who took off for the end zone.

To safety Jaylen McClain, the breakdowns weren't a result of play calls.

"We've just got to execute the way we're supposed to," McClain said, "and it won't happen."

McClain said the Buckeyes maintain confidence in Matt Patricia, who is in his second year as the Buckeyes' defensive coordinator.

"Coach Patricia has been doing a good job of play-calling," McClain said. "We trust him a whole lot. That's the reason why he's here."

But Ohio State's trouble containing explosive plays has been a sudden development.

Before the Buckeyes traveled to USC, they were one of just two teams left in the Football Bowl Subdivision that had not allowed a play from scrimmage longer than 40 yards. They've now allowed three in two weeks, matching their total from 2025 when they surrendered the fewest in the country.
