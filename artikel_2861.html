Rapid Recap: Heat 137, Bucks 130

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

In the first matchup since the biggest trade of the summer, the Miami Heat took it to the Milwaukee Bucks all night, and despite the final score being decided by single digits, the Heat led by over 20 for the majority of the game. Giannis led the Heat with 27 points, 13 rebounds and nine assists, while Ryan Conwell led their bench scorers with 13 points. Meanwhile, Tyler Herro struggled mightily in his return to Miami, scoring just eight points in a dud of a game while Kyle Kuzma led Bucks starters with 26 points. Brayden Burries had an impressive night, scoring 20 points and grabbing seven boards, while Kasparas Jakucionis led Milwaukee off the bench with 20 and five helpers.

The energy and perhaps nerves were palpable at the start of the game. Immediately after Klay Thompson's first three-pointer, Brayden Burries drove through the paint, drew a foul, and put Milwaukee on the board. Neither team got many paint touches through the first half of the quarter, and the game came down to made three-pointers. Miami continued its assault from deep, while the Bucks showed some solid ball movement; after a 35-second possession that produced three offensive rebounds, Jakucionis finally put it away with a layup. Miami made 18 of its first 23 field goals from three, and the Bucks got shots from all over the floor, though much less effectively. The first quarter ended with Miami up 34-23.

Burries carried the load early in the second with five quick points and a steal to keep the Bucks within shouting distance, but the rest of the team offered little help, and before you could say, "GTA 6", the Bucks were down 20. As Heat coach Spoelstra began to stagger his bigs, it felt like the game got more out of control for Milwaukee. Bam Adebayo terrorized the Bucks, scoring from pretty much anywhere, and just when you thought the visitors might get a break with Bam checking out, Giannis checked back in. By the four-minute mark, though, Giannis was just 0/1 from the field with 2 points and five assists. That said, GA took what the defense gave him and found the open man in the corner more often than not. The Heat made just four shots in the paint in the quarter but kept raining fire from downtown, shooting 6/10 from beyond the arc. The Bucks entered halftime down big, 74-48.

If there was one positive from the first half into the third, it was that the Bucks didn't look totally lost in the paint. Coming into the game, the expectation was that Miami's frontcourt would be too much to handle, but Milwaukee did pretty well there. Thankfully, through the first four minutes, the Heat were held to just two 3-pointers, which let Burries put up eight quick points after scoring back-to-back threes and giving the Bucks a sliver of hope. Burries continued to try to bring life to the starting lineup, but ultimately his efforts were not enough; other than Kuzma, the other three starters combined for just 12 total points through three quarters. Despite the Bucks scoring 38 to keep up their league-leading offensive pace in the third quarter, Miami matched it with 38 to stay up big, 112-86.

Miami had been shooting 34.3% from three for the season, but tonight they couldn't miss, going 22/48 while the Bucks shot just 14/41. Despite the huge lead, Miami didn't start subbing their starters out until later in the quarter, and to their credit, the Bucks kept the energy high and the pressure on. Milwaukee went on a 12-0 run led by Jakucionis and Caris LeVert, who saw his first minutes of the season and looked great, scoring eight points in the quarter. Of course, Giannis checked back in and pulled his team's lead back out to 20 in short order. However, credit to Jakucionis, Ament, and Green for keeping the energy up; the Bucks outscored the Heat 18-5 to close it out, but it was too little, too late.

43: the total number of shots the Bucks made, which, despite being down by 20-30 for most of the night, was 3 more than the Heat. This game was decided from downtown, and the hail of three-pointers from Vice City Miami completely overwhelmed the Bucks.

The Bucks need to trade Tyler Herro urgently. Herro may be the only trade chip the Bucks have outside of Jaime Jaquez Jr., and the sooner Milwaukee makes this trade, the sooner they can more permanently give crucial developmental minutes to the other young Bucks, who set a better tone than the veterans seem capable of.
