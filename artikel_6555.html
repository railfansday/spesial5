Show Us the Receipts: What a Corporate Employee, a Stylist, and a Content Creator Bought This Month

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Welcome to Show Us the Receipts, our recurring column on what fashion people really buy and spend.

Money remains taboo in almost every corner of life, but in fashion, dollars and cents can take on an especially complex dimension—from both inside and out. With a high buy-in to the luxury market and wages that don’t often allow professionals access to the goods offered by their own industry, we often find ourselves wondering how, exactly, our contemporaries afford their wardrobes. Do they come from generational wealth? Are they on every PR gifting list known to man? Are they savvy sale shoppers and bargain hunters? Or are they just really shrewd budgeters?

Here, we asked three stylish people in or adjacent to the fashion industry to chronicle one month of their fashion consumption—and to note what was purchased at full price, discounted, and gifted.

This month, a corporate employee at a luxury house, a stylist, and a content creator all offered Vogue a peek at their receipts.

Read the first edition of Show Us the Receipts here.

While this corporate employee prefers her discretionary funds to go toward experiences like concerts or dining out, she found herself buying more clothing than usual this month. “I try to only shop when I truly need something, partially to save money but also to reduce the number of items in my closet,” she says. She chalks her purchases—mostly cold weather pieces from Zara—up to the changing season. Her one big purchase, a pair of Chanel earrings, was an anomaly. “I also don’t normally buy expensive jewelry or designer items, but when the opportunity presents itself, it’s hard to say no,” she says.

Though salary and closet space are her main limitations, the corporate employee hopes that she’ll be able to allocate more of her salary toward clothing (right now she estimates she spends 2-10% of her monthly income on clothes). “By the time I pay rent and daily expenses, I have little budget left for clothes,” she says. “As I continue to grow in my career and earn more money, I plan to make clothes a bigger part of my budget, because my style has always been important to me and I always feel more confident when I’m wearing an outfit that I love.”

“I’m a very selective shopper,” says the stylist, who spent far more than he originally anticipated this month. Like the corporate employee, the stylist also attributed some of his purchases to the onset of fall. Due to the nature of his work, the stylist knows exactly the best times to make a purchase. “I really try to be cautious and thrifty for the most part,” he says. “I’m always watching for discounts and markdowns.”

He estimates that he spends roughly 15% of his monthly income on clothes—“I think, like any millennial New Yorker, budget often comes last,” he adds—the stylist is in the unique position of using his personal purchases in his work. “The Tom Ford boots were a splurge, but I’ve had them on my wishlist for months now and was able to also use for a client, so it felt like a good investment for myself and my ‘work closet,’” he says. “In running my own business, I’m more often than not making purchases that feed back into the overall scope of the business.”

“This was one of the higher spending months … at least I think,” says the content creator, citing the end of fashion month when new items arrive in-store and pre-orders are placed. Being in Europe for much of the month also directly contributed to higher costs than usual, as “I could actively track vintage items dropping on platforms from the European market,” she explains. “Normally when I’m back home in New York, by the time I wake up, it’s already 1 p.m. in Europe, so I miss six prime hours of good vintage items getting listed.”

Though she admits “I’ve never actually stepped back and looked at how much I spend on clothing in a month,” the content creator estimates she spends around 35% of her fluctuating monthly income on clothing. “This month was my highest-paid month of the year, so I definitely also had more spending,” she says. That said, clothing is her highest expenditure by a large margin, always taking precedence over eating out and travel. “A Citi Bike loves to see me coming in my vintage McQueen,” she says.

But the content creator sees her clothing purchases as something that will pay off in dividends. (In addition to content creation, she also rents her clothing to celebrities.) “I try to only buy rare archival pieces, and most of them I’m buying for 10-20% of the value they are always worth. These are pieces that will continue to appreciate in value over time,” she says. “I always make a joke that my retirement is my closet.”
