Puppy Chow Balls

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Recipe by Margaret Knoebel, Milwaukee, Wisconsin

Puppy chow is our favorite Midwestern snack, and it’s the inspiration these holiday-ready truffles. You’ll find all the same ingredients—peanut butter, Chex, chocolate and confectioners’ sugar—but with a truffle redesign that’s sophisticated enough for a Christmas dessert table (and that keeps hands considerably less messy!).

Like puppy chow, Chex cereal (crushed for easier rolling) and peanut butter are melted together to make the crunchy truffle filling. Each truffle is enrobed in a thick layer of melted chocolate then coated generously with confectioners’ sugar. These puppy chow truffles are a two- to three-bite affair and totally remind me of those big puppy chow clusters everyone fights over.

In a large microwave-safe bowl, heat the peanut butter in 30-second increments until it’s completely melted, stirring occasionally. Add 2 tablespoons of confectioners’ sugar and stir until smooth. Add the crushed Chex and stir until everything is well combined.

Scoop the puppy chow truffles by the tablespoonful onto wax-paper-lined baking sheets. Freeze them until they’re firm, about 30 minutes.

In a microwave-safe bowl, melt the semisweet chocolate and shortening at 50% power, and stir until they’re smooth. Dip each puppy chow ball into the melted chocolate and shake off the excess. Place the puppy chow truffles on a waxed paper–lined baking sheet.

Place the confectioners’ sugar in a large airtight container or resealable bag. In batches, add the puppy chow balls and shake them in the bag until they’re well coated in confectioners’ sugar. Store the puppy chow truffles in an airtight container.

Store puppy chow truffles in an airtight container at room temperature for up to four days. They can also last in the fridge for up to one week.

Yes, you can freeze puppy chow truffles, but wait to coat them with confectioners’ sugar until after they’ve thawed. To freeze the puppy chow truffles, place them on a parchment-lined baking sheet and freeze them for one hour. Transfer them to a zip-top bag or airtight container, and freeze them for up to two months. Thaw them overnight in the fridge before enjoying them.

Your chocolate may not be smooth for a few reasons. The first is you may have burned it in the microwave. When you burn chocolate, it will seize and become clumpy. Unfortunately, this is unfixable. It’s also possible that you left out the shortening. Shortening makes the chocolate slightly less viscous, which makes it easier to evenly coat the truffles. It also makes it smooth and glossy. Don’t skip this ingredient!

To help puppy chow balls hold their shape, be sure to use a no-stir creamy peanut butter. It’s much thicker than the natural peanut butters you have to stir. Because their oils separate so easily, natural peanut butters will make the mixture softer and more malleable.
