‘Definitely a cool moment’: AJ Dybantsa shines in front of his childhood-favorite Celtics

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

AJ Dybantsa grew up in Brockton, Mass., about 30 minutes away from TD Garden, rooting for the Celtics his entire life.

On Tuesday night, the 19-year-old Wizards rookie hosted them in Washington's NBA Cup group finale. The Celtics won, 131-116, but it was a night Dybantsa won't soon forget.

"Definitely a cool moment," Dybantsa told reporters in Washington. "I've been a Celtics fan since I came out the womb. I was born in Boston, been going to Celtics games since I was two years old, wearing Kevin Garnett's jersey, then Paul Pierce's jersey to Isaiah Thomas', all the way to [Jayson] Tatum and [Jaylen] Brown."

Before Dybantsa committed to BYU, became a collegiate superstar, and went No. 1 in the 2026 NBA Draft, he attended Tatum's basketball camp. Back then, he was a youth prospect just starting to build buzz at Saint Sebastian's School in Needham, Mass., before transferring to Prolific Prep in Napa, Calif., and once more to Utah Prep Academy in Hurricane, Utah.

Tatum took the time to get to know Dybantsa and share some advice. So when the two crossed paths again, this time as fellow professionals, Dybantsa was struck by the moment. He did, however, admit it could be even bigger when they meet for the rematch on Dec. 14 at TD Garden.

"I think it'll be more surreal when I play over there, but today was definitely a cool moment," Dybantsa said.

It won't be the first time Dybantsa has played on Boston's parquet.

Last season at BYU, Dybantsa won over the crowd as a freshman facing No. 3 UConn at TD Garden. He relished the homecoming, scoring 25 points (including 21 in the second half) with six rebounds and two steals in an 86-84 loss to the Huskies. Jaylen Brown and Derrick White sat courtside, watching New England rally behind one of its own on his way to the NBA.

Nearly a year later, Dybantsa put on a show to win over Washington's crowd, finishing with 20 points, 11 rebounds and a career-high seven assists against the No. 2 seed Celtics.

Dybantsa is averaging 20 points, 9.2 rebounds and 4.8 assists on 45.6 percent shooting over his last five games. He shot 7-for-18 on Tuesday night and knocked down a pair of threes, which, to Tatum, is just a sneak peek of what's to come in the near future from Dybantsa.

"The sky's the limit for him," Tatum told reporters. "[He's] somebody that I met a long time ago when he was a kid and got to know him pretty well. He was at my first Elite Camp. I know his father pretty well, so he has a really, really bright future."

Tatum put on a show himself, eager to get Boston back in the win column amid its four-game losing streak.

He dropped 41 points with six rebounds and eight assists, his 31st career 40-point game. That moved him into second place on the Celtics' all-time list, behind only Larry Bird (47).

Dybantsa, guarding Tatum for the first time, admitted the assignment was a challenge, as was defending Tatum's new running mate, Paul George, who scored 28 points on 11-for-20 shooting.

In his mind, and now from experience, there's only so much Dybantsa can do against those two.

"At the end of the day, Jayson Tatum, Paul George, they are who they are," Dybantsa said. "They're gonna get shots up, but it's about making it tough. We don't want them just coming off a ball screen, getting a wide-open three, getting them uncomfortable, getting them out of rhythm. But they're gonna make shots. They're All-Stars, they're future Hall of Famers, but just trying to make it tough on them."

Tatum got the better of Dybantsa twice. First, he slipped free off a Sam Hauser screen for an uncontested dunk. Then he attacked Dybantsa's closeout from the perimeter and finished with a right-handed layup.

Fighting through Boston's screens troubled the Wizards all night long, Dybantsa included. For the rookie, it was a reminder of how much work remains before he's where he wants to be.

"I wanna be one of the better defenders in this league, and I can't just be dying on screens," Dybantsa said. "I gotta contest and get over the screen."

After the final buzzer, Tatum shared a handshake and hug with the former camper he once mentored years ago. He leaned in to say a few words, then patted Dybantsa on the chest and headed off.

They'll meet again in less than three weeks.
