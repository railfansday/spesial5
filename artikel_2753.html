Braelon Allen making case for more touches in Jets’ crowded backfield

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Braelon Allen averaged less than six carries per game as a rookie. Last season, the Jets' backup running back was limited to only four games due to a knee injury.

But the 134th player — and 11th running back — selected in the 2024 draft was ready when opportunity arrived.

While Breece Hall was sidelined by a quad injury, Allen ran for 504 yards (5.4 yards per carry) in his first five career starts, opening this month with a 147-yard effort that made him the first Jets running back in 11 years (Chris Ivory) to rush for 100 yards in consecutive games.

Entering Week 11, the 22-year-old is one of only eight running backs in the NFL averaging at least five yards per carry.

New York Jets running back Braelon Allen (0) looks on before a game against the Buffalo Bills in East Rutherford, New Jersey, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

"I know what I can be in this league," Allen told The Post on Friday. "You needed the platform to show what you're capable of. I always knew when that time came I was gonna take advantage.

"Fortunately, but unfortunately, I came to a place where they had a really good running back already."

Once again, touches have become hard to find for one of the Jets' most dynamic players.

In returning to a backup role over the past two games, Allen has rushed for 73 yards on 16 carries (three receptions, 27 yards), while Hall had 26 rushes for 102 rushing yards (seven receptions, 93 yards). This season, Hall has 265 rushing yards (3.4 yards per carry) and one touchdown in five games, while Allen has 638 rushing yards and three touchdowns in 10 games.

Still, head coach Aaron Glenn reiterated Friday that Hall — who signed a three-year, $43.5 million deal in the offseason — remains the team's top option in the backfield.

New York Jets running back Breece Hall (20) runs past Cleveland Browns linebacker Carson Schwesinger (49) in East Rutherford, N.J., Nov. 9, 2025. USA TODAY Network via Reuters Connect

"Obviously, Breece and Garrett [Wilson] are guys that we really try to target for the most part, and then we make sure the rest of the guys get their touches when they come," Glenn said. "I think, obviously, Breece will end up getting a tick more [touches] because, again, he's our starting running back, but we want to make sure that Braelon gets his touches."

There has been a greater effort made to include the backup in the past two weeks than there was in the Jets' first three games, when Hall received 51 carries and Allen got 19.

But there is no clear solution to finding the right balance.

"We should talk about it because that's what people want to know," offensive coordinator Frank Reich said this week. "I think that's a great problem to have. For me, it's terrible because I can't win. I know it's never gonna be enough. I'm not worried about that to the outside. I'm worried about that when I walk in the room with the guys.

"I'm not worried about the fantasy stuff and all that stuff. I'm worried about those guys in the locker room and I want them to know — and I know they do — how much I believe in each one of them and how much I want to give each one of them the ball, but there's only so many touches in a game.

"They have a lot of respect for each other. I wish we could get it to them both more, but that's just the way it's gonna be."
