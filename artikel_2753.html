Former Seahawks corner Riq Woolen benched by Eagles for a familiar reason

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

At Field Gulls, we keep tabs on former Seattle Seahawks players whether it's going well or not, and there was one Week 11 game that went poorly for a couple of ex-'Hawks.

When the Pittsburgh Steelers lost to the Philadelphia Eagles on Sunday, one of the headlines was former Seattle Seahawks receiver DK Metcalf getting tossed for two unsportsmanlike conduct penalties, the second of which was an attempted eye poke of Eagles defensive back Cooper DeJean.

Another notable ex-Seahawks in that game was Eagles cornerback Riq Woolen, whose own day didn't go a whole lot better. Woolen didn't play on defense for almost the entire second half, only to eventually come back later in the game when Quinyon Mitchell suffered a hamstring injury. Jonathan Jones replaced Woolen in the lineup while Riq's snaps were limited to special teams.

In his post-game media availability, Woolen confirmed he was benched and it's for an all too familiar reason for Seahawks fans and maybe the Seahawks organization.

"I can't say too much," Woolen said. "It's just the [coach's] job. I mess up, they take me out. And I messed up, he took me out, and I just gotta be a great teammate just like how the next guy is.

"It was just hard," Woolen added. "I didn't play the whole second half. To go in the last three, four minutes is tough. The DB position is a mental position more than physicality, so to be able to try to check back in mentally when you've been out so long is hard, but I have to be there for my guys no matter what. No matter how I'm feeling, it's a team sport."

When asked if he was surprised if he was taken out, Woolen said he was not.

"I had a talk with a coach before the game and then it was just basically saying I may be taken out a series just to help, you know, because I had a couple mental lapses. But it just turned into the whole second half and that's how it went."

Putting two and two together, it looks as if this was the sequence that was the final straw for the Eagles coaching staff.

"You know, we just wanted to reset there a little bit," Eagles head coach Nick Sirianni said of Woolen's benching. He also noted that Woolen did "a nice job responding" after injuries forced him back into the game.

Woolen has been either benched or at least lost snaps in four consecutive seasons dating back to 2023. Tackling and effort issues resulted in a benching against the San Francisco 49ers and then scarcely playing against the Philadelphia Eagles. Under Mike Macdonald, Woolen sat for a series versus the Minnesota Vikings for an off-the-field team violation, and then last year saw his snaps reduced to effectively a split role with Josh Jobe after a horrible fourth quarter on opening day against the 49ers.

After an encouraging start to Woolen's Eagles career, ongoing penalty issues and tackling woes have not subsided on a new team. Having signed a one-year, $12 million "prove-it" contract in free agency, it doesn't look like Riq is set to cash in with a major multi-year contract when he hits the market again in 2027.

So if you're so minded, Steelers-Eagles was distilled vindication for those who wanted to move on from Metcalf and/or Woolen. They both had plenty of positives in Seattle, but their negatives not only became persistent problems, they haven't gone away elsewhere for either player.
