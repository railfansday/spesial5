Ex-Chelsea star defends Cole Palmer after criticism over reaction to Leeds loss

Former Chelsea striker Jimmy Floyd Hasselbaink has defended Cole Palmer’s commitment after the forward faced scrutiny following Saturday’s 4-1 thrashing at home against Leeds.

Palmer failed to score once again, leaving him with just a single goal across his last nine Premier League appearances during a difficult run for Xabi Alonso’s side.

The England outcast attracted attention after the defeat due to an apparent exchange of words with injured team-mate Joao Pedro near the dugout, while reports suggested the Chelsea playmaker did not acknowledge the frustrated home support at Stamford Bridge.

However, Hasselbaink told talkSPORT: "No, I don’t think there is discontent. I think there is a frustration that things are not going the way they want to go and certain individuals deal with that better than others.

"The expectation is a lot higher than what is being delivered at the minute and that’s from in the camp, but also from outside the camp and let’s not talk about the commitment of Cole Palmer. I don’t think that is the problem.

"At the moment you see he is playing different because he is looking for the ball, but looking for the ball way deeper when he should get it way higher.

"It’s the midfielders that should find him in a higher position and at the moment the majority of the time he is starting the majority of the play, so he is not playing as high and as much around the box."

Following Palmer’s high-profile omission from Thomas Tuchel’s England squad for the summer World Cup, the 24-year-old launched the new campaign with goals in victories over Fulham and Brighton to help Alonso make a bright start in west London.

Palmer’s form has dipped since, however, during a spell in which Tuchel admitted the Chelsea attacker "misses too many opportunities to impress" to earn an international recall.

Alonso and Palmer are both under pressure following a painful first home loss to Leeds in 27 years, which leaves them with five defeats from 11 league fixtures, though Hasselbaink laid the blame on the team as a whole.

"When a new manager comes in and especially with the pedigree of Xabi, yeah you think he would make straight away an impact or a big impact but the players are still the same," Hasselbaink said.

"Managers do need time to assess things and adjust things, but the same problems keep on occurring at the moment and that’s really the disappointment."
