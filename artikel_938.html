Quiz question: what was unique about Tony Cottee’s goal for West Ham against Charlton, in the FA Cup third round in January 1986? Answer: during that domestic season, it was the first live televised goal in either league or cup.

There hadn’t been loads of goalless draws before that – actually, there had been a complete TV blackout. No live football, not even Match of the Day highlights, after a dispute between the Football League and the broadcasters, BBC and ITV.

Even when tensions thawed, only 13 English club games were shown live during the second half of that campaign.

Fast forward 40 years, and you’d struggle to find a time where there weren’t at least 13 domestic league games televised in a single weekend. This season, a minimum of 267 Premier League matches will be shown live on Sky and TNT, plus 1,000 games from the EFL, League Cup and EFL Trophy, with the FA Cup and European games on top of that.

That’s just one example of how football has changed over the years – new state-of-the-art arenas have also been built around the country.

But as football has got bigger, what price has it all come at? Political interference, financial cheating and the quest to get every refereeing decision right to within a millimetre, leading to moments of spontaneous joy being sucked away from those in the stands.

In this issue, we ask if football is broken – and if so, how do we fix it?
