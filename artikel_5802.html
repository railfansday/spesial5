Protesters heckle Trump's ex-Border Patrol chief offstage after Dem governor condemns College Republican event

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Video shows protesters heckling former Border Patrol chief Gregory Bovino off the stage at a Rutgers College Republicans event.

Protesters heckled former Trump Border Patrol commander-at-large Gregory Bovino off stage during a Monday night speech at Rutgers University, hours after New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill called the Rutgers College Republicans’ decision to host him "wrong" and urged the club to reconsider.

Bovino previously criticized Sherrill online, accusing her of prioritizing illegal immigrants over New Jersey residents. After returning to the stage, he called her "Sugar Pop Sherrill" and "Kim Jong Sherrill." Speaking to reporters afterward, he said she wanted to "trounce this freedom of speech," described the "Kim Jong Sherrill" label as "very fitting" and called the governor a "totalitarian." He also told Blaze News that Sherrill had timed her social media comments to "foment violence" against conservatives.

At Monday night’s Rutgers College Republicans event, protesters began shouting within minutes of Bovino taking the stage. Rutgers police escorted him offstage and ordered the hecklers to leave or face arrest, according to local reports. Bovino returned about ten minutes later to resume his remarks and answer questions. Hundreds of protesters also gathered outside.

"What’s so crazy is that you all allowed a Nazi to be here," a protester yelled as the heckling began. Another shouted, "Your fascist death cult is failing!" Video from inside the event captured chants of "Get out of New Jersey!" and "Immigrants are welcome here!" Outside, hundreds of protesters carried images of Renee Good and Alex Pretti, who were shot and killed by federal agents in Minneapolis.

Before Bovino’s Monday appearance, Rutgers told Fox News Digital it was committed to protecting freedom of expression and maintaining campus security. The university also said hosting a student-organized event did not constitute an endorsement of the speaker or his views. Rutgers had not responded to Fox News Digital's follow-up request for comment about the disruption by publication.

Hours before Bovino’s speech, Sherrill said in a social media post that she was "deeply disappointed" by the Rutgers College Republicans’ invitation, adding, "Giving him a platform is the wrong decision."

Sherrill said that if the club went ahead with the event, she would ensure the state worked with Rutgers to protect "the safety of everyone and the rights enshrined in our Constitution," and she urged attendees to question Bovino about his record.

Fox News Digital contacted Sherrill's team for comment on the disruption at Bovino’s event but received no response before publication.

Bovino served as Trump's commander-at-large of the U.S. Border Patrol and oversaw immigration enforcement and deportation operations, including Operation Metro Surge in Minneapolis. He was removed from command in January following the shootings of Good and Pretti and retired from the Border Patrol in March.

During the Rutgers College Republicans event, a student asked Bovino about the deaths of Good and Pretti. He replied, "Poor decisions come with consequences," then said the circumstances were "still being sorted out." Bovino called ICE agents the "real victims" and added, "I love them and salute them."

Asked after Monday’s event whether he stood by the deaths of Pretti and Good, Bovino said, "I didn’t stand by anybody’s death."

He clarified that his earlier comment about going "harder" referred to mass deportation operations, not the shootings of Good or Pretti. Bovino said he would rejoin the Border Patrol "in an instant" to continue those operations and called the end of enforcement activity in Minnesota "the great surrender." He added that he hoped the administration would complete its mass deportation efforts but feared it would "surrender" its aggressive deportation agenda.
