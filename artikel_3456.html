‘The Rings of Power’s Best Storyline Kicks Off With an Emotional Death

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Editor's note: The below contains spoilers for The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power Season 3 Episode 2.

It's likely that the Rings of Power plotline that longtime fans of J.R.R. Tolkien's larger Middle-earth saga are most invested in is everything regarding Númenor. Certainly one of the draws of exploring the Second Age is seeing this island kingdom at its peak, as well as the inevitable descent into civil unrest between two warring factions vying for the nation's well-being. But only two episodes into its third season, the Prime Video series raises the stakes when Eärien (Ema Horvath), the daughter of Elendil (Lloyd Owen), is discovered as a spy for the Faithful, leading to a harrowing self-sacrifice that no one saw coming.

Eärien's mere existence in The Rings of Power was an issue for some viewers. After all, Elendil is said to have two sons — Isildur (Maxim Baldry) and Anárion (Andrew Richardson), with the latter finally arriving this season — but not a daughter. Created specifically for Prime Video's adaptation, Eärien has always been presented as a woman torn between loyalty and duty, and Season 3 has finally forced her to pick a side, despite the high cost. Having officially joined the Faithful in the five years between the second season and the third, Eärien works stealthily under Ar-Pharazôn (Trystan Gravelle), feeding intel to her father through her brother Isildur, who has returned home. Upon being discovered, however, she chooses not to betray her family or convictions, but rather to throw herself from Pharazôn's window, a drastic but understandable action that costs her life. Although Rings of Power had finally pushed Eärien to the right side, her sudden demise reminds us that the threat the Faithful are facing is real — and that Númenor is worse off than things may appear on the surface.

There's an argument to be made that The Rings of Power has always been at its best when it's given the Númenor plot the time of day. Elendil was one of the more interesting and complex characters in the first season, and Tar-Míriel (Cynthia Addai-Robinson) is, naturally, essential to the nation's fate. While last season began the conflict between the King's Men faction and the Faithful, Season 3 gives the struggle real legs. From the moment viewers return to the island kingdom, the show sets up a game of undercover cat and mouse, as Eärien attempts to balance complex political maneuvers with keeping a low profile. From what's revealed up front, it feels as if The Rings of Power aimed to spend the season exploring Isildur and Eärien's strategic rebellion, but all of that quickly changed when Eärien was discovered and thus flung herself onto the streets of Armenelos. Although Eärien refuses to betray her kin or the Faithful, her betrayal has forced Pharazôn's hand, as he aims to wage war against his fellow Númenóreans.

As Elendil and Míriel wrestle for power against Pharazôn, The Rings of Power seems to be setting up the cataclysm to come. In Tolkien's writings, he reveals that Pharazôn eventually forces Míriel to marry him before Sauron (Charlie Vickers) seduces the ruler into a full-scale assault on Valinor to usurp the Valar in an effort to claim eternal life for Men. This angers Eru Ilúvatar, the God of Tolkien’s world, who not only annihilates Pharazôn and his forces, but, like the biblical Great Flood or the tragedy of Atlantis, sends a massive wave to destroy Númenor itself. This is exactly what occurs in the vision that Míriel has in the first season after contact with the palantír, and it's undoubtedly where the Prime Video series is heading. That said, even with the outcome in mind, how Númenor gets there has always been the most interesting part of that story — with Eärien's demise being one more step toward that ultimate fate.

Of course, Elendil and his sons have a destiny of their own, one that doesn't include their fallen daughter and sister. Elendil would abandon the depraved Númenor to establish the united Kingdoms of Gondor and Arnor, kingdoms his sons would inherit. Through this royal lineage, Aragorn is eventually born during the Third Age. Not only does Aragorn reunite the fractured kingdoms, but he becomes pivotal in the final defeat of Sauron centuries later. Now, it's hard to say when exactly this will all take place in The Rings of Power, but we can rest easy knowing the show has not only firmly set its course, but is now driving full-speed ahead.
