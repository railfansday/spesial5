Robert Pattinson's Forgotten Dark Fantasy Thriller Proves He's a Perfect James Bond

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Not everybody is convinced Robert Pattinson is the right choice to be the next James Bond. Some are complaining Bond should be played by someone less famous, like he has in the past. Others feel he’s not masculine enough, or good-looking enough, or charismatic enough. Most of all, though, it seems many people don't love the idea that “the Twilight guy” is playing Bond.

Honestly, I do think there is some merit to the idea that Bond should be played by an up-and-comer, if only because that’s been the tradition. (Clearly Amazon, which took over the franchise in 2025, has other ideas.) As for the more cosmetic complaints, I guess those all come down to personal taste.

As for the Twilight-related concerns, I understand them because, when Pattinson was announced as Batman back in 2019, I thought​​​​​​​ “Oh no, the Twilight guy? How could this guy, who seemed so…soft, be the Dark Knight?” I hated the idea. Then, a few months after the announcement, I saw the Robert Eggers film The Lighthouse and completely changed my mind.

The Lighthouse, which is currently streaming on HBO Max, is generally described as a horror film or as psychological horror with some fantasy elements, but I found it to be more of a comedy than anything else. I honestly think that, up until The Lighthouse, I’d literally never seen Pattinson in a movie before. Literally nothing I’d heard of with him had appealed to me, so it wasn’t Pattinson that lured me to The Lighthouse. Part of the reason I went to see The Lighthouse was Willem Dafoe, who I love. Another factor was a review I’d read by film critic Tim Grierson, who described​​​​​​​ the movie as “a terrific comedy about having a terrible roommate.” As a fan of The Odd Couple who regularly laments a lack of comedies in theaters, that was enough to convince me to check it out.

The Lighthouse really is just about two guys in a lighthouse dealing with the loneliness of the job, which requires them to be stuck at sea for weeks at a time. Dafoe plays a grouchy old sea-dog, while Pattinson is his young apprentice. The movie is a laugh riot, with lots of drinking, some wild singing, and both men becoming increasingly unhinged. One dreams he has sex with a mermaid, the other masturbates all the time, and both are brought to their breaking point by the end.

To be clear, nothing about Pattinson’s character in The Lighthouse made me think that he would be the perfect Batman. His character is a young man who’s green to this profession, but he’s a bit jaded from past failures and secretive about his personal life. He's fairly serious at the beginning, but he jumps into a rage more and more as the film progresses. I guess there’s some overlap with Bruce Wayne/Batman there, but not very much.

The reason why The Lighthouse convinced me of Pattinson's ability to be Batman was that he completely disappears into the role. Mere minutes into the movie, I entirely forgot who I was watching and any skepticism I ever had about the guy. Of course, the mustache and 19th-century New England accent helped with that, but it was more than that. Robert Pattinson is just one of those actors who can completely disappear into a role without any trace of the guy you thought you knew. As much as that’s the desire for any acting role, it doesn’t really happen quite that often, but Pattinson does it in The Lighthouse and I’ve seen him do it in several films since.

Of course, with Pattinson in the news right now, a lot of his movies are being recommended, and while I love him in The Batman, The Odyssey, Tenet, and others, all of those movies have a lot of plot and action going on. In contrast, The Lighthouse​​​​​​​ is pretty bare-bones. You get to see two guys just acting without much else to distract from it.

The chemistry between Pattinson and Dafoe is also just fantastic. Those two are so funny and interesting together, especially when they are at each other’s throats. It kind of makes me hope for a Lighthouse reunion in one of Pattinson's​​​​​​​ Bond films, with Dafoe cast as a Bond villain. I mean, Dafoe feels like a guy who would make a perfect Bond villain anyway, and, given his chemistry with Pattinson, I think that would be really exciting to see.​​​​​​​

Perhaps I’m getting ahead of myself a little bit there, but if you, like I did, think of Pattinson as nothing more than that Twilight guy, I’m telling you, you’re wrong, and you only need to see him have sex with a mermaid to wipe that impression clean from your mind​​​​​​​.

The Lighthouse is available to stream on HBO Max
