Social Security Retirees Get a Big COLA in 2027, but Benefits Will Increase Most in These 10 States

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Social Security benefits and COLAs are indirectly influenced by the state of residence.

By Trevor Jennewine – Nov 21, 2026 at 4:48AM EST

Social Security is a critical source of income for millions of older Americans. Nearly 72 million people receive Social Security benefits, including more than 55 million retirees. And according to Gallup, nearly 9 in 10 of those retired workers depend on Social Security to some degree.

That makes annual cost-of-living adjustments (COLAs) particularly important. These automatic pay increases are designed to protect the purchasing power of Social Security payments against inflation, helping retirees keep pace with rising prices.

Fortunately, Social Security recipients will get a relatively large raise in 2027. Benefits will increase 3.6%, up from the 2.8% COLA in 2026. The increase is also slightly larger than the 3.4% average COLA over the preceding decade.

Importantly, while all Social Security payments will increase by the same percentage next year, the extra income will vary widely when measured in dollars. Read on to see the 10 states in which retired workers will receive the largest COLAs in 2027.

Social Security's annual cost-of-living adjustments (COLAs) are based on a subset of the Consumer Price Index known as the CPI-W. Specifically, the CPI-W from the third quarter (July to September) of the current year is compared with the average from the same period in the prior year, and the percentage increase becomes the COLA for the next year.

Social Security's COLA in 2027 will be 3.6%. The Social Security Administration estimates the average monthly retired-worker benefit will increase from $2,095 before the COLA to $2,170 after the COLA, meaning the average retired worker will receive an additional $75 per month or $900 for the full year.

To be clear, all retirees will receive the exact same 3.6% pay increase, but those with larger benefits will receive larger COLAs when measured in nominal dollars. To that end, retirees in the 10 states with the highest median Social Security benefits will receive some of the largest pay increases next year.

Importantly, a retiree's state of residence has no direct impact on the amount of Social Security benefits they receive, but there is an indirect relationship. Benefits are primarily based on lifetime earnings and claiming age, so states with high median household incomes are likely to have high median Social Security benefits.

Indeed, six states listed in the table above -- New Jersey, New Hampshire, Maryland, Washington, Massachusetts, and Utah -- rank among the 10 states with the highest median income, according to the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis. And three others -- Connecticut, Delaware, and Minnesota -- have median incomes above the national median.

What about the other state? Michigan ranks among the 10 states with the highest median Social Security benefits despite having a median household income below the national median. That could mean more workers in Michigan claim Social Security at older ages, which results in larger benefits. Alternatively, workers with relatively high lifetime earnings may choose to retire in Michigan.

Here's the bottom line: Social Security benefits are not directly linked to where a retired worker lives, so moving to a different state will not automatically increase your monthly payments. Rather, workers in certain states tend to earn more money, often reflecting differences in local labor markets and living costs. And because Social Security benefits are based partly on lifetime earnings, those workers generally qualify for larger payments when they retire.
