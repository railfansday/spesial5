Jensen Huang's Sudden Resignation Has Created a Flash Sale For These 3 AI Stocks

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Huang's sudden departure has caused a meltdown across AI stocks. These three picks look like good buys.

By Marc Guberti – Nov 20, 2026 at 6:30AM EST

The ouster of former Nvidia (NVDA -3.81%) CEO Jensen Huang came relatively quickly and was a surprise to many. Less than two weeks ago, everything was normal for the company. With Huang gone, some investors are panicking.

Favorites in the AI trade have endured sharp corrections in recent weeks, and the news about Huang's departure has accelerated those drawdowns. However, those same businesses have delivered enhanced fundamentals, making the stocks compelling at current levels.

Here are the three AI stocks that are strong buy-the-dip candidates.

Sandisk (SNDK -8.23%) stock is trading down by more than 60% from its all-time high and has shed more than 50% of its value in less than two months. The stock only trades at a 7.5 price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio, making it cheaper than most bank stocks.

While news has largely shifted to negative sentiment, Sandisk continues to deliver impressive results that imply a multi-year boom.

The NAND flash memory chipmaker reported $11 billion in first-quarter fiscal year 2027 revenue. That was a 23% sequential jump, and it also came with $5 billion in stock buybacks. Sandisk delivered these results at the end of October, so the bullish report was pretty recent.

Sandisk also expects its second-quarter FY27 revenue to range from $12.2 billion to $12.6 billion. High sequential growth isn't going away, and multi-year deals with key customers make future revenue more visible. The announced 8-for-1 stock split is certainly flashy, but it's high revenue growth and enticing profit margins that should excite investors more.

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -5.97%) is the clear No. 2 to Nvidia. It's down by more than 20% from its all-time high, but the confusion with Nvidia can turn AMD into one of the largest winners.

The investment theory is that a leadership vacuum in Nvidia lets AMD gobble up more market share. While AMD won't become the top chipmaker overnight, gaining some ground on Nvidia can unlock billions of dollars in quarterly revenue.

The great thing about AMD stock is that it outperformed the broader market before Nvidia's leadership suddenly became a weak point. The chipmaker delivered 45% year-over-year revenue growth in the third quarter, and its data center revenue almost doubled. The data center segment includes AMD's graphics processing units (GPUs) and central processing units (CPUs).

AMD CFO Jean Hu said in the press release that the company anticipates accelerating revenue growth in the fourth quarter. The AI boom is not slowing down, and if Nvidia loses some of its innovative edge, AMD will be more than happy to fill the gap.

Silicon Motion Technology (SIMO -7.48%) is a smaller memory company that makes NAND flash controllers. These products are vital for key memory products, which is why it counts companies like Sandisk, Micron Technology (MU -6.22%), and SK Hynix (SKHY -3.54%) as some of its top customers.

The company has been delivering impressive revenue growth and guidance, but it's trading down by more than 40% since early October. Ongoing AI concerns are the main headwind, and as companies address this fear, Silicon Motion Technology and others should rebound.

In the meantime, fundamentals aren't the issue. Silicon Motion Technology delivered 131% year-over-year revenue growth in Q3, and that included a 24% sequential boost. Silicon Motion Technology has a strong track record of beating guidance, and its current Q4 guidance implies up to 10% sequential growth.

Like other companies in the AI trade, Silicon Motion Technology has shown no hints of slowing down. Management remains optimistic about future growth, and the intense demand for AI data centers helps to justify that stance. Self-driving vehicles and humanoid robots will create new demand for artificial intelligence and its corresponding components.

Once the Nvidia drama subsides from the news cycle, Silicon Motion Technology is in a good position to regain some of its recent losses.
