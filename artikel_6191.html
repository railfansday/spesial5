AirAsia now has a date for Bahrain but less need for it. A Pegasus deal routing Europe traffic through Istanbul undercuts the case for a Gulf hub.

AirAsia is targeting March 2027 for its first passenger flights to Bahrain. It is the group’s first firm timeline for its highly anticipated Gulf hub since the U.S.-Iran war forced it to adjust its plans.

The group gave the new date as it appointed a seven-member, all non-executive board for AirAsia Bahrain on November 1.

“While the geopolitical situation in the region has required us to adjust our initial plans, our commitment to Bahrain has not changed,” said Tony Fernandes, CEO of AirAsia's owner Capital A and adviser to AirAsia Group. “We continue to monitor developments in the region closely and are targeting the commencement of AirAsia flights to Bahrain by March 2027, as conditions allow.”

AirAsia has set March 2027 as its target for the first passenger flights to Bahrain, the first firm timeline for its Gulf hub since the U.S.-Iran war forced the group to adjust its plans. Capital A CEO Tony Fernandes said the commitment to Bahrain has not changed, but framed the date as conditional — "as conditions allow." The plan, signed with Bahrain's transport ministry in November 2025, envisions a Bahrain-registered subsidiary running up to 25 daily flights by 2030 and serving as AirAsia X's bridge from Southeast Asia to Europe and North America. Since then the group has suspended 81 routes and is returning 25 aircraft early as fuel prices climb, and it has shifted its Europe approach toward a codeshare with Pegasus Airlines that routes Southeast Asia–Europe traffic through Istanbul. Only cargo is flying in Bahrain today, via twice-weekly Teleport freighters, with maintenance capabilities planned but no timeline or investment figure disclosed. The March target matches a wider pattern: Wizz Air moved its Abu Dhabi and Dubai restart to March 2027, nearly 20 non-Gulf airlines are still not serving Dubai, and Virgin Atlantic has suspended Dubai flights into 2027.
