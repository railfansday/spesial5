The EU Sustainable Tourism Strategy sits on top of existing tourism strategies released by the economic bloc’s Commission. But it still falls short of presenting measurable targets that would actually support its implementation.

The European Commission wants to spread tourism across more destinations and seasons, according to its tourism strategy — which does not allocate new funding or set numerical targets.

The European Commission released its EU Sustainable Tourism Strategy on Wednesday, setting out a vision to spread visitors across lesser-known destinations and across the full year — without new funding, binding mandates, or numerical targets. The Commission said 42% of nights spent in a destination fall, on average, in its three busiest months, and the strategy arrives after a summer of extreme heat, wildfires, and continued overtourism backlash. Its sustainability pillar rests on three voluntary instruments: the EU Model for Tourism Management, a proposed Council Recommendation for Balanced Tourism, and an EU Toolbox for Balanced Tourism that will include shared approaches to short-term rentals. A Cruise Cooperation Dialogue was also proposed. The Commission said €18 billion in EU support is expected to have benefited tourism from 2021 to 2027 but committed no fresh money, pointing instead to the 2028–2034 budget still under negotiation and to combining private investment with financial instruments. Industry voices quoted by Skift welcomed the volume-to-value direction while pressing for measurable targets, simpler funding access, and practical fixes such as interoperable transport ticketing, which the Commission said it would only "explore" by 2028. The strategy also flags that AI trip-planning tools could reinforce existing tourism hubs if destinations stay passive.
