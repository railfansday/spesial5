US Treasury’s FinCEN takes systems offline after a cybersecurity incident

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The bureau that holds banks’ suspicious activity reports has not said what was accessed or who did it. Filers now have until 30 December for reports due from 18 November.

The US Treasury Department in Washington, DC.

The US Treasury’s Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) is investigating a cybersecurity incident and has taken some systems offline. The bureau posted an alert on its filing website on Tuesday, which The Verge first reported.

“FinCEN is investigating a cybersecurity incident that resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems. As a proactive measure, FinCEN has taken some systems offline,” the bureau said in its alert.

FinCEN has not said which systems the intruders reached, what data they accessed or who was responsible. Its Bank Secrecy Act E-Filing website is still running, although some services may be unavailable for now.

What the bureau holds is sensitive. Banks and other financial institutions file suspicious activity reports with FinCEN under the Bank Secrecy Act. The bureau shares that financial data with law enforcement to fight money laundering and terrorist financing.

The disclosure followed days of disrupted bank filings. Filing providers reported problems as early as 18 November, when automated batch submissions to FinCEN stopped, The Deep Dive reported. FinCEN posted an alert about a disruption to that batch service, known as SDTM, on 20 November. It has not said whether the intrusion caused the outages.

On Tuesday, FinCEN also issued a relief order. Filers now have until 30 December for reports due between 18 November and 29 December. The extension covers institutions that used SDTM as of 18 November and companies due to file beneficial ownership reports. It also covers people who must update a FinCEN Identifier.

The order blames a service disruption and does not mention a cyberattack, The Deep Dive noted. Other anti-money-laundering duties, including monitoring for suspicious transactions, still apply. Banks that need to get suspicious activity information to law enforcement quickly can still call the Financial Institutions Hotline. FinCEN says the disruption has not affected it.

FinCEN’s alert came hours after reports that hackers had reached an unclassified CIA system. The breach, of an open-source intelligence system, also hit other spy agencies. Investigators suspect the intruders used the Artex hacking tool. It is unclear whether the two incidents are related, The Verge reported.

Last week, hackers also reached the FBI’s crime database, and the Agriculture Department took applications offline after a security incident. In December 2024, hackers broke into Treasury’s departmental offices through a stolen key from the vendor BeyondTrust. The department blamed a Chinese state-sponsored group.

Treasury’s chief information officer, Sam Corcos, heads the office in charge of the department’s cybersecurity. While FinCEN dealt with the incident, Corcos was pushing updates to Legatum, his open-source strategy game modelled on Civilization VI, 404 Media reported.

The game is built largely with AI coding agents, Claude and OpenAI’s Codex, according to its readme on GitHub. Corcos co-founded the glucose-monitoring start-up Levels and joined Treasury as a DOGE hire.
