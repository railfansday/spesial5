An annoying ‘mistake’ in macOS was actually prep for the latest MacBook Pro

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

You may remember that Apple made a seeming mistake in the user interface for macOS 26, meaning that attempts to resize windows would often fail. The reason for this has now become clear, and Apple has done something both fun and clever in macOS 27 to resolve it …

I was one of a number of people to note that trying to grab the corner of a window in macOS Tahoe in order to resize it would often fail. Developer Norbert Heger pointed out this is because Apple changed the radius of the corners without changing the grabbable area.

It turns out that my initial click in the window corner instinctively happens in an area where the window doesn’t respond to it. The window expects this click to happen in an area of 19 × 19 pixels, located near the window corner. If the window had no rounded corners at all, 62% of that area would lie inside the window. But due to the huge corner radius in Tahoe, most of it – about 75% – now lies outside the window.

At the time, the radius change itself looked like an inexplicable mistake on Apple’s part, and the company did revert it. However, Macworld’s Michael Simon appears to have discovered the reason for the original change.

The new rounder corners on the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR are basically a perfect match for the macOS 26 radii.

So if you move a window to the corner of the new machine, it would fit perfectly. However, since Apple reverted the corner radius, it would no longer do so. The company solved this by a clever UI transition.

What’s happening there is Golden Gate’s 20-point corner radius is subtly and gradually expanding when It approaches the bottom corner of the screen to match the exact corner of the display, which is much larger. I can’t be certain it’s exactly 26-point, but it sure looks like it to me. The effect is incredible. I’ve watched it like 20 times and I can barely see it changing, yet when it reaches the corner it’s perfectly expanded to match.

You can see this illustrated by a GIF in the Macworld piece. While the original change annoyed me, Apple’s solution is the perfect example of the company sweating the details.
