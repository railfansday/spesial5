SAB Biotherapeutics (SABS) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Full enrollment in adult and adolescent SAFEGUARD cohorts sets up a pivotal 2027 data readout.

SAB Biotherapeutics, Inc. (SABS +3.06%) reported a fiscal transition in the third quarter of 2026 as it increased research spending to advance its lead clinical programs. Management reported that the company achieved full enrollment for its adult and adolescent patient cohorts in the SAFEGUARD trial and received FDA Fast Track designation for its primary immunotherapy candidate. The company maintains a multiyear cash runway intended to support the development of treatments for Stage 3 type 1 diabetes through the release of topline data in 2027. Strategic focus remains on leveraging the proprietary Tc Bovine platform and expanding the addressable patient population through the initiation of additional clinical studies.

We remain committed to advancing new medicines with the potential to change the course of the disease and improve the lives of those affected.

That focus continues to drive our work at SAB BIO, and we're pleased to have you with us today to review the progress we made during the third quarter. We continue to execute on our priorities for SAB-142 with strong momentum in SAFEGUARD enrollment and the FDA granting Fast Track designation to SAB-142 for the treatment of new-onset Stage 3 type 1 diabetes. For context, SAB-142 is a fully human anti-thymocyte immunoglobulin designed to preserve insulin-producing beta cells in order to slow disease progression in autoimmune type 1 diabetes. It was developed using our proprietary Tc Bovine platform, which allows us to generate fully human, multi-specific antibodies without the need for human donors.

Because SAB-142 is fully human, it has the potential to overcome key limitations of non-human approaches and enable safe redosing over the long term. We believe SAB-142 represents an opportunity to help people with type 1 diabetes redefine what a Stage 3 diagnosis can mean. SAB-142 is currently being evaluated in 2 clinical trials, the registrational Phase 2b SAFEGUARD study for those newly diagnosed with Stage 3 T1D and the investigator-led Phase 3 PRISE study for people up to 2 years from a Stage 3 T1D diagnosis. Let's start with an enrollment update for SAFEGUARD. The performance of our clinical team this quarter has been exemplary.

In September, the study's Data Monitoring Committee approved the final step-down to children ages 5 to 11, helping to further accelerate enrollment. We continue to see strong interest in SAFEGUARD from both patients and investigators, and we're excited to share that the adult and adolescent cohorts are now fully enrolled and closed for screening. With that, we are reaffirming our guidance to complete enrollment of SAFEGUARD in Q4, with topline data expected in the second half of '27. Building on that progress, the PRISE study expects to dose the first patients this quarter. There are 7 clinical sites planned across the United States, Europe and the UK.

PRISE is an important extension of the SAB-142 clinical program as it would potentially allow a future label expansion for SAB-142 to T1D patients up to 2 years from a Stage 3 diagnosis. As a reminder, PRISE is an investigator-led study supported by a grant from Breakthrough T1D and co-funded by SAB BIO. We are grateful to Breakthrough T1D for its support and to Dr. Michael Haller at the University of Florida, who serves as the study's principal investigator. We also want to recognize Dr. Chantal Mathieu at UZ Leuven and Dr. Timothy Tree at King's College London as co-principal investigators for their important contribution to the study.

Beyond our clinical progress, we reached an important regulatory milestone in October when the FDA granted Fast Track designation to SAB-142 for the treatment of new-onset Stage 3 type 1 diabetes. This designation is intended to facilitate the development and expedite the review of therapies for serious conditions where there is an unmet medical need, with the goal of bringing important new treatments to patients earlier. For SAB-142, this designation gives the program some serious aura. And importantly, it provides valuable procedural benefits as we advance our program.

These include opportunities for more frequent meetings and written communications with the FDA, eligibility for rolling review of the future BLA and potential eligibility for Priority Review if the applicable criteria are met. While Fast Track designation does not change the FDA's standards for approval or ensure that SAB-142 will ultimately be approved, we believe the benefits are meaningful as we move toward topline data. We will continue to work closely with the FDA as we advance the SAB-142 clinical program. And we believe this designation represents meaningful progress toward our goal of bringing SAB-142 to people living with type 1 diabetes as quickly as possible.
