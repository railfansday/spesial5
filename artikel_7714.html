California coalitions sue to block Gov. Newsom’s commutation of over 500 death row sentences

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

California Gov. Gavin Newsom looks on during a bill-signing event Aug. 21, 2025, in Sacramento, California. (Photo by Justin Sullivan/Getty Images)

District attorneys and victims’ families have taken their fight to the California Supreme Court over California Gov. Gavin Newsom’s plan to commute over 500 death row penalty sentences.

The Criminal Justice Legal Foundation, which represents the plaintiffs, argued Monday that the governor was required to give formal notice to district attorneys before commuting 548 death penalty sentences. The plaintiffs also challenged Newsom’s broad death penalty commutations without considering individual circumstances, beyond whether a person has more than one felony conviction. Existing law permits crime victims the opportunity to speak before a governor can commute someone.

“Gov. Newsom decided his personal views should override all those who actually participated in the process, followed and applied the law,” the plaintiffs said. “He did none of that, as he freely admits. That alone is an abuse of power that must not stand,” according to coverage by Courthouse News Service.

Newsom announced Nov. 5 the commutations of all people on death row to life in prison without parole. The governor wrote a letter that said state-sanctioned killing undermines the legal system’s foundation and counters California’s values.

Newsom, who has served two terms, declared a moratorium on all executions in 2019 and leaves office in January.
