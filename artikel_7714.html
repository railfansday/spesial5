Northeast Georgia Man Charged with International Money Laundering and Destroying Evidence Tied to ISIS

A Lavonia, Georgia, resident is in federal custody charged by criminal complaint with international money laundering and destruction of evidence following an FBI investigation into allegations of sending more than $26,000 to individuals and groups affiliated with a designated foreign terrorist organization, the Islamic State of Iraq and al Sham (ISIS).

Younes Moustafa Younes, 34, of Lavonia, Georgia, was charged by complaint with one count of international money laundering and one count of destruction of evidence. If convicted, Younes faces a maximum penalty of 20 years in prison, to be followed by three years of supervised release, and a $500,000 fine for international money laundering, and a maximum penalty of 20 years in prison, to be followed by three years of supervised release, and a $250,000 fine for destroying evidence. He made his initial appearance today in federal court before U.S. Magistrate Judge Charles Weigle and will remain in federal custody pending his preliminary and detention hearings on Nov. 23 in Macon.

According to the complaint, between December 2025 and June 2026, Younes sent $16,807 through cryptocurrency transactions using multiple phone numbers and accounts intended to conceal his identity. Investigators allege that at least some of the funds were intended to promote the efforts of ISIS in Syria. In addition, Younes is alleged to have altered and destroyed evidence up until his arrest on Nov. 18, when FBI Atlanta agents executed warrants to search his apartment, car, electronic devices, and workplace. The FBI found three cellular phones submerged in an unknown solution inside his Lavonia apartment, including one device that was smashed and another with its identification number removed. Agents also found shredded documents submerged in an unknown solution in a bathtub.

The FBI first identified Younes in 2020 after he allegedly accessed ISIS propaganda online. When interviewed by law enforcement, Younes acknowledged viewing the material but denied supporting extremist ideologies. The complaint alleges that, over time, Younes became increasingly radicalized, expressing support for ISIS and other terrorist figures and organizations through encrypted communications with associates.

The complaint further alleges that in 11 separate transactions between December 2021 and April 2022, Younes is alleged to have sent approximately $9,206 to cryptocurrency addresses associated with this effort, using an alias to conceal his support. Encrypted communications cited in the complaint allegedly show Younes sharing extremist content, discussing support for ISIS, and praising individuals associated with terrorist organizations.

In September 2022, Younes traveled to Turkey on a one-way ticket and remained there for approximately two years after allegedly selling his vehicle, cashing out retirement funds, and leaving his employment in the United States. Following his arrest and deportation from Turkey back to the United States, investigators allege that Younes continued to express support for extremist causes and maintained contacts with individuals previously convicted on terrorism-related charges.

FBI Atlanta is investigating the case, with assistance from FBI Newark and the National Counterterrorism Center.

Criminal Chief Leah McEwen and Assistant U.S. Attorney Daniel Peach for the Middle District of Georgia, Assistant U.S. Attorney Sammi Malek for the District of New Jersey, and the National Security Division's Counterterrorism Section are prosecuting the case for the government.
