USWNT roster for Concacaf W Championship: Ashley Sanchez back, Sam Coffey returns, and Naomi Girma misses out

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

The biggest roster of the year for the U.S. women's national team is here. Head coach Emma Hayes has named a 23-player roster for the upcoming 2026 Concacaf W Championship.

The tournament serves as the World Cup qualifiers, and four teams will earn spots in the 2027 FIFA Women's World Cup, with future Olympic spots also in play. Fans can watch all the Concacaf W Championship matches on Paramount+.

In just nine short days, eight teams will compete in a quarterfinal round. The U.S. and Canada earned direct byes into the tournament as Concacaf's top two ranked teams, and six teams now join them after a preliminary qualifying round. The USWNT will face El Salvador on Nov. 27 in the quarterfinal round, and if they advance, they'll clinch a spot in next year's World Cup. The other matchups are Canada against Panama, Mexico vs. Haiti, and Jamaica will play Costa Rica.

"It's been a great year of growth and learning for our team, and for a long time the focus has been on qualifying for the World Cup, so now that time has arrived," said Hayes.

"I want to see relentless consistency from our players, individually and as a team, and I want us to be elite in both penalty boxes. If we can achieve that, I'm confident those performances will carry us through to meet our standards and goals. This is my first Concacaf event and the first for many of our players, so we're all looking forward to the experience."

Hayes selected a full roster that features several players competing in their first-ever senior-level World Cup qualifying tournament, and a handful of veterans. Just eight of the 23 players selected were members of the 2022 Concacaf W Championship roster: forwards Mallory Swanson, Trinity Rodman, Sophia Wilson, and Ashley Sanchez; defender Emily Fox; and midfielders Lindsey Heaps, Rose Lavelle, and Sam Coffey -- who joined mid-tournament that year.

Coffey returns to the roster after missing two previous international windows while managing a lower-leg injury. Defender Naomi Girma is out as she continues to manage a lingering calf injury. The centerback has not featured with the national team since April, and has yet to play with Chelsea FC this season. Midfielder Olivia Moultrie is currently rehabbing from an ACL injury sustained in August. Other notable omissions on the roster are Jaedyn Shaw and Ally Sentnor, both of whom have been part of U23 and senior camps throughout the year.

This is just the third camp for the attacking line of "triple espresso" since the 2024 Olympics. If the trio starts a match during the Concacaf W Championship, it will be the first time they start a game together since the gold medal match. Both Swanson and Wilson have worked their way back into senior camps over the year following maternity leave.

Goalkeepers (3): Claudia Dickey (Seattle Reign FC; 12/0), Mandy McGlynn (Utah Royals FC; 6/0), Phallon Tullis-Joyce (Manchester United, ENG; 8/0)

Defenders (8): Tierna Davidson (Gotham FC; 72/3), Kate Del Fava (Utah Royals FC; 1/0), Emily Fox (Arsenal FC, ENG; 80/1), Avery Patterson (Houston Dash; 15/1), Tara Rudd (Washington Spirit; 12/0), Evelyn Shores (Angel City FC; 0/0), Gisele Thompson (Angel City FC; 12/0), Kennedy Wesley (San Diego Wave FC; 9/1)

Midfielders (6): Croix Bethune (Kansas City Current; 7/1), Sam Coffey (Manchester City, ENG; 46/5), Lindsey Heaps (Denver Summit FC; 180/40), Claire Hutton (Bay FC; 22/1), Rose Lavelle (Gotham FC; 124/29), Lily Yohannes (OL Lyonnes, FRA; 22/1)

Forwards (6): Michelle Cooper (Kansas City Current; 16/1), Trinity Rodman (Washington Spirit; 59/13), Ashley Sanchez (North Carolina Courage; 30/4), Mallory Swanson (Chicago Stars; 105/39), Alyssa Thompson (Chelsea FC, ENG; 34/4), Sophia Wilson (Portland Thorns FC; 65/25)

Ashley Sanchez's return to the roster comes after standout performances against Spain last window and a stellar 2026 NWSL regular season where she won the Golden Boot with 20 goals scored. She was also nominated for the 2026 NWSL MVP award. A positional shift to left winger and an outrageous amount of scoring have all led her back to the USWNT due to consistent performances, a key factor for Hayes with her large player pool.

"Be consistent first of all in performance. Ashley Sanchez wasn't on our radar, not because she isn't talented, but there were certain things we demanded from the performance that I think she has delivered in her game on a regular basis. So, I think that's the first thing. I think you've seen that includes Croix Bethune coming into this camp -- been outstanding in the number 10 position all year, and would have been in last camp as well if she was fit. You do have to consistently find consistent performances, particularly if you're a player that doesn't have credit in the bank," Hayes said.

As the World Cup gets nearer, finalizing a roster for the 2027 summer tournament is even closer. Things like consistent form and senior-level experience will come into play when naming a final 23 for the World Cup for Hayes.

"If you're a Mallory Swanson and delivered on a world stage on a regular basis for the national team, it does alter things slightly. No, we don't want players that are in our group that are in a place where they're not fighting to improve every day or be as competitive as they could possibly be, but I think when players have stacked and accumulated a lot of experience over a period of time, that is always going to put them in a steady position," Hayes explained.

"But it doesn't guarantee anything. I will be unafraid to leave anybody off that plane if I don't feel that they are applying themselves to put ourselves in the best possible position to compete. Which is why this upcoming camp, our job is to qualify for the World Cup, and so everything we have worked towards is ensuring that every player that comes in at camp makes sure they lift their level and they position the team to have the most positive outcome, regardless of the opponent. And I'd be demanding as much as I always do, but I have a big expectation from our senior players to step up."

El Salvador is the eighth-ranked team in the Concacaf W Championship and earned its spot following a preliminary qualifying round. The group is coached by manager Eric Acuña, who recently made headlines for harshly criticizing his players in a press conference following losses to Paraguay last month.

Still, Hayes and the staff aren't taking any opponent lightly no matter the rankings or state of the locker room.

"It's a team that knows is competing for something, so it doesn't matter the opponent; they're competing to go to a World Cup, and do I think that will be it will be demanding and taxing? And yes, I do. In many different ways, because the expectations are all on us. I think for them, it's how are they going to disrupt us and make it difficult? But they have two players in the front line that can cause a lot of problems, particularly in transition. So we have to set up for that," Hayes said.

"I think when it when it comes to the players, I've always championed creating positive environments for players. And yeah, I'm sad for the players to hear what they've gone through, but as always, I can't control that. I want the best for players all of the time in their environments, but I am here to represent the U.S. and try and help us qualify for the World Cup. So my focus is on that. Even if my heart goes out to the players and I think we still have a lot of work to do in the women's game to keep driving female-centered, female-lens environments where women can thrive."
