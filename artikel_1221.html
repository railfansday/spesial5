Referee hauled off at half-time after team accused him of 'being DRUNK' following bizarre yellow card handed out to goalkeeper

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Published: 08:48 EST, 26 November 2026 | Updated: 10:35 EST, 26 November 2026

A referee was replaced at half-time of an Asian Football Confederation (AFC) Champions League Two match after he was accused of 'being drunk' by a manager of one of the teams – a claim the official has denied.

Syrian referee Hanna Hattab oversaw the match between United Arab Emirates' side Al Jazira and Iran's Gol Gohar in Asia's second tier club competition.

Controversy emerged in the 32nd minute after Hattab awarded a free-kick after a foul by Al Jazira's Felício Milson.

Hattab initially waved away protests from Al-Jazira players and approached Milson holding a yellow card.

The official then stunned players after appearing to receive a message in his earpiece, before turning and walking towards Al-Jazira goalkeeper Ersin Destanoglu.

Hattab showed the goalkeeper a yellow card, despite him being nearly half the pitch away from the incident.

Referee Hanna Hattab was accused by a manager of 'being drunk' following a controversial decision in an Asian Champions League Two match - a claim the official has denied

The referee was then seen speaking to his fourth official, leading to him rescinding the goalkeeper's yellow card while Milson escaped being shown a second yellow card for his challenge.

Al Jazira scored the only goal of the game minutes later through Anderson Talisca, but the bizarre incident involving Hattab was reportedly the centre of discussion at half-time.

Gol Gohar head coach Mehdi Rahmati had reportedly been seen shouting 'he's drunk' at the official, while the club's sporting director claimed post-match they would have refused to play the second half had Hattab remained in charge.

'The referee against Al Jazira was not in a normal state, and even Al Jazira's players told us the same thing,' said Heydar Afsari.

'The club immediately informed the match officials and warned them that we would not play if the situation remained the same.'

Hattab was ultimately replaced by the fourth official for the second half of the match.

The referee, who is part of the AFC's elite refereeing pool, has since released a statement hitting back at the allegation.

'I will wait for the official communication and official stance from the Syrian Arab Football Federation, and I will also communicate with the people and parties responsible for me locally and internationally, before issuing an official statement that clarifies all the details and facts related to what happened,' Hattab wrote.

The claim had centred on the referee appearing to listen to his earpiece and changing his mind on booking an Al Jazira player, before bizarrely showing a yellow card to their goalkeeper

Al Jazira's goalkeeper appeared stunned by his yellow card, which was later rescinded with the referee then being replaced at half-time of the match

'In conclusion, I confirm that family and personal life are not the responsibility of others, and are not a game in your hands.

'I also confirm my full right to take all legal and judicial measures against anyone who published or made a statement or contributed to the insult and defamation of me, regardless of their position or status, whether they are an individual, an institution, or a sports entity.'

Omani sports journalist Issa Said, who covers Asian competitions, also came to Hattab's defence and insisted the claim the official was drunk is false.

'What happened to him was due to a drop in his blood sugar, which caused him to lose concentration and focus,' he wrote on social media,

'He underwent a medical examination after the match and was not intoxicated, contrary to what has been circulating.'

The Syrian Football Federation added in a statement: 'Following an assessment of his health, it has been confirmed - contrary to various claims circulating on social media- that the reports regarding his situation are entirely unfounded.

'The Committee reiterates its unwavering support for him and is committed to thoroughly investigating all details and circumstances surrounding the incident.

'In this regard, the Committee has contacted the Asian Football Confederation (AFC) to request the referee’s medical report, the match commissioner’s report, and the referee assessor’s report, in order to accurately ascertain the facts of the incident and take the necessary follow-up actions.'
