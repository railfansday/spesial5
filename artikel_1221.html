Every Time Manchester United Were Relegated

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Relegation is not supposed to happen to clubs like Manchester United, but since their inception in 1878, it is something that has occurred on multiple occasions.

United's slump down the Premier League table in 2026/27 has dragged that conversation to Old Trafford as a record-breaking start, for all the wrong reasons, has become their new reality.

A humbling 4-0 defeat at Liverpool in Mauricio Pochettino's first game left them 19th with eight points from 11 games, their first spell in the bottom two at this stage in a season for 40 years.

Still, it would not be uncharted territory to go down, as United have suffered the drop five times before — here's the story of each one.

The Red Devils' latest managerial debut was a disaster, as they faced a 4-0 defeat at Anfield, but where does it rank among the worst manager debuts?

All seasons played under two points for a win.

Still known as Newton Heath at this point, the club finished bottom of the First Division for a second straight season, winning six of their 30 games. A year earlier, they had survived by beating Second Division champions Small Heath in a test match. Relegation was then settled by a test match against a Second Division side, and Liverpool won 2-0 at Ewood Park on 28 April 1894. It took 12 years to return.

A 5-0 defeat at Everton on the opening day set the tone. Jack Robson stepped down through ill health in October, John Chapman took over, and United won only eight league games to finish bottom. Chapman eventually led them back up in 1925.

The worst season in the club's history. United lost their first 12 league games, shipped 115 goals and suffered a 7-0 defeat at Aston Villa and a 6-0 home loss to Huddersfield Town. Herbert Bamlett did not survive the fallout, with secretary Walter Crickmer taking charge in 1931, while the Great Depression dragged average home crowds down to around 12,000. Just under 4,000 turned up for the final game, a 4-4 draw with Middlesbrough.

Scott Duncan's side had won the Second Division title a year earlier but lasted only one season back in the top flight. A 3-2 derby win over Manchester City in front of 68,796 at Old Trafford was a rare high. United bounced straight back as runners-up to Aston Villa in 1937/38.

No top-flight team has been spared the cruel blow of relegation.

United's most recent relegation came just six years after they became the first English club to win the European Cup. Tommy Docherty, in his first full season after replacing Frank O'Farrell in December 1972, oversaw a side in transition. The stars of 1968 were fading, and George Best played his final game for the club in a 3-0 defeat at Queens Park Rangers on New Year's Day 1974.

Goals were a constant problem. United managed only 38 in 42 league games, with Sammy McIlroy their top league scorer on six.

Docherty even handed penalty duties to goalkeeper Alex Stepney, whose two spot-kicks made him joint top scorer for ten matches.

A nine-game winless run before Christmas and a seven-game streak after it left United adrift. A late rally, including a 3-1 win at Chelsea and a 3-0 home victory over Everton, revived hope, but defeat in the return fixture at Goodison Park on 23 April undid much of that work.

That left the final Saturday on April 27, where United had to beat Manchester City at Old Trafford in front of 56,996 and rely on others dropping points. Instead, Denis Law, one of the greatest football players of all time, who had joined City the previous summer, fired in the only goal in the 81st minute via a cheeky backheel attempt.

Law barely reacted. He later described himself as "inconsolable", and City goalkeeper Joe Corrigan felt Law had not grasped the significance of what he had done. United fans invaded the pitch, the game was abandoned in the 85th minute, and the Football League ruled the result should stand.

Contrary to folklore, Law did not send United down. Birmingham City's win over Norwich City and West Ham's draw with Liverpool meant United were relegated regardless, alongside Southampton and Norwich, in the first season the Football League sent three clubs down. However, the headline of Law relegating a club in which he now has a statue outside is a far more interesting myth to run with.

Their stay in the Second Division lasted one season. Docherty, kept on by the board, led United to the title in 1974/75, and they have not dropped out of the top flight since.
