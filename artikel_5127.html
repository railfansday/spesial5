Trezor Tells Crypto Users Never to Enter Wallet Backups on Phones Amid Theft Probe

Crypto wallet provider Trezor has issued a security warning to cryptocurrency users following a recent theft report: "Never type your wallet backup into a phone, app or website."

According to Onchain Lens, a user reported losing over $600,000 across TRON and Solana while using an iPhone 17 Pro Max running iOS 27.2.1. Both the Trezor Safe 7 and the Exodus wallet were reportedly affected. The victim had been using a Trezor Safe 7 for nearly two months.

According to the victim, the Trezor wallet arrived sealed, with no visible tampering; the recovery phrase was generated on the Trezor screen and not on the iPhone; the seed was written on paper and never stored digitally. The victim used Trezor Suite on an iPhone 17 Pro Max via Bluetooth and also had the Exodus wallet on the same iPhone.

The device was running iOS 27.2.1, and the victim never approved the unauthorized transactions. The Trezor wallets for TRON and Solana shared one recovery phrase.

The user said $580,000 was stolen from Trezor on TRON and that an unauthorized transaction moved 49,999 USDT on Nov. 21, 2026. The transaction used the default owner permission, making the signing authority central to the investigation.

Another $23,400 was drained from Trezor on Solana, and the victim says they never approved that transfer using the hardware device. The iPhone remains the main focus, as both Trezor Suite and Exodus were reportedly used on the same iPhone 17 Pro Max running iOS 27.2.1, and both were affected.

Trezor is designed to keep private keys inside the hardware device. However, compromising the phone alone should not normally provide access to Trezor's private keys, and the mechanism behind both thefts remains largely unknown.

On November 9, 2026, Apple issued iOS 27.2.1, which patched several security holes (including some related to Bluetooth). The victim was reportedly running this version when the theft occurred. Thus, there is currently no evidence connecting those vulnerabilities to these transactions. Possible causes outlined included compromised recovery credentials, unauthorized signing, or a device or software security failure.

Trezor responded to this disclosure, saying that Trezor wallets don’t get drained out of nowhere and that there are currently no reports of funds being stolen remotely from any device. It noted that similar cases usually trace back to a compromised wallet backup.

@OnchainLens @binance @Bybit_Official UPDATE: This device came directly from Trezor and shows no sign of an implant. We’re still investigating what happened. So far there’s no confirmed loss of funds from implants and no implant found in any Trezor Safe 7. Never type your wallet backup into a phone, app or website.

In a further update, Trezor confirmed that the device affected came directly from the company and showed no sign of an implant. The wallet provider added that it continues to investigate what happened.

This appears to be a standalone case, as Trezor reiterated that, so far, no confirmed loss of funds from implants has been reported, and no implant has been found in any Trezor Safe 7. It also reiterated an important security warning: users should never enter their wallet backups into a phone, application, or website.
