Latest findings point to fuel supply, not Blue Origin’s engines, in loss of Amazon Leo satellites

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

by Todd Bishop on Nov 18, 2026 at 10:01 amNovember 18, 2026 at 10:08 am

Blue Origin’s engines look like they’re off the hook, at least for now.

The two BE-4 engines Blue Origin makes for United Launch Alliance’s Vulcan rocket weren’t to blame for the low thrust in Sunday’s failed launch, according to preliminary findings released Wednesday. Instead, it appears the engines didn’t get enough fuel from the rocket, ULA said.

In its latest update on the investigation, ULA said it had completed an initial integrated review of flight data. Preliminary data indicate the first stage’s low thrust came from “inadequate fuel supply from the vehicle to the BE-4 engines, not from the performance of the engines,” ULA said, adding that work remains to determine the cause.

On Tuesday, ULA said early data suggested the first stage underperformed just after liftoff, leaving the rocket too low and too slow to put 40 of Amazon’s Leo internet satellites into orbit. It didn’t say then whether the engines played a role.

The BE-4s are made by Jeff Bezos’ Kent, Wash.-based Blue Origin. Blue Origin CEO Dave Limp said Wednesday on X that he was proud of the BE-4 team for its “rapid response and support of ULA’s investigation” and that the company looks forward to Vulcan’s return to flight.

The finding matters for Blue Origin beyond Vulcan. Its own New Glenn rocket uses seven BE-4s on its first stage, and Amazon has 24 New Glenn launches reserved for Leo.

ULA repeated that Vulcan’s second stage appears to have started normally and that its RL10 engines operated as planned but couldn’t make up for the earlier loss of performance. The six Northrop Grumman solid rocket boosters, the source of problems on two earlier Vulcan flights, appear to have performed normally, ULA said.

The Federal Aviation Administration is overseeing the ULA-led investigation. Amazon’s next Leo launch is an Ariane 6 mission carrying 36 satellites, targeted for no earlier than Nov. 23 from French Guiana.

ULA said it will share more as the investigation continues.
