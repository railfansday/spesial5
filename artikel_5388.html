Bitcoin Settles Its $10.9 Billion Options Expiry Right on Max Pain After the Binance Hack

Bitcoin settled Friday’s $10.92 billion Deribit options expiry at $78,119, almost exactly on the $78,000 max pain level, after the $6.36 billion Binance hack knocked it from $80,850 to a $76,500 low overnight.

That low touched BTC’s 200-day EMA, the long-term trend line, to within $32. BTC trades at $77,620, down 3.2% in 24 hours, and the Fear & Greed Index fell from 51 to 30 in a day.

With about a third of Deribit’s open interest now gone, the options market is lighter than it has been all month. The next large date is November 27, when $3.55 billion expires with max pain at $80,000.

The drop came in two legs. BTC opened the 20:00 UTC hour on Thursday at $80,850 and hit $78,297 within minutes as news of the Binance breach spread, a 3.2% fall. It bounced, then slid again after 23:00 UTC and printed its $76,500 low just after 02:00 UTC on Friday, 5.4% below where the hour began.

Forced selling did much of the work. CoinGlass data showed $1.55 billion of crypto positions liquidated in 24 hours, 83.6% of them longs, with $501.6 million in BTC alone.

Smaller accounts bought the dip: the share of OKX accounts that are long jumped as the price fell, and open interest rose by about $210 million into the low. Funding on OKX’s BTC perpetual stayed positive throughout, so longs are still paying shorts.

Implied volatility told a more cautious story. Deribit’s DVOL index, which tracks the 30-day volatility options traders are pricing in, jumped from 37 before the hack to 42 just before midnight UTC and has only eased back to 38.7.

About 135,000 BTC of options expired on Deribit at 08:00 UTC on Friday, the last Friday of the month, when monthly contracts settle. Max pain, the strike where the most options expire worthless, sat at $78,000. Deribit settled the contracts at $78,119.01.

The book was heavily tilted toward calls. Deribit data reported before the expiry put the put/call ratio at 0.53, close to two calls for every put. With BTC settling just above $78,000, most of those calls, bets that BTC would be higher by the end of October, expired worthless.

Once the expiry cleared, Deribit’s BTC options open interest stood at 285,073 BTC, or about $22.1 billion, as of 11:17 UTC.

BTC options notional by Deribit expiry as of 11:17 UTC on October 30, 2026. Source: Deribit.

Max pain isn’t a forecast; BTC often settles far from it. But the November levels frame the range traders are betting on: puts clustered at $74,000 to $76,000, right around the 200-day EMA, and calls stacked from $90,000 up.

One more sign of caution: in the 24 hours to 11:17 UTC on Friday, Deribit traders bought 1.63 BTC of puts for every BTC of calls, the reverse of the call-heavy book that just expired.

On the daily chart since early August, BTC is back where it spent most of September: between its 200-day EMA at $76,468 and its 50-day EMA at $80,593. Friday’s low wick stopped $32 above the 200-day line, which BTC has closed above every day since its late-August breakout.

BTC has tested this 200-day zone before. In mid-September, it dipped to $74,968, held, and ran to $87,395 within a week. Friday’s buyers are betting on the same bounce, as the OKX long/short ratio shows.

For Bitcoin, the next test is Binance itself. If withdrawals reopen smoothly within the promised 48 hours, the call-heavy positioning that just expired worthless could rebuild toward the $90,000 strikes. If they don’t, the puts at $74,000 to $76,000 are where traders have already placed their bets.
