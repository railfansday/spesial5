‘We’re defending’ | Improved defense sparks Wizards’ three-game winning streak

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Washington Wizards claimed defense as their identity entering the 2026-27 season. Gone were the days of the bottom-five defensive rating they'd owned each of the previous three seasons.

That proved true during a 4-3 start where their defensive rating ranked seventh in the NBA. But that figure dropped to 26th amid an eight-game losing streak in which they allowed 129.5 points per game. Their defensive effort had waned, and so, too, had their production.

Then a players-only meeting was held. The main point: The Wizards needed to play with more physicality, to show more fight. They responded with a three-game winning streak in which they held their opponent under 100 points in three straight contests for the first time since Nov. 13, 2021.

"We're defending. I think that's the biggest thing for us," Brian Keefe said. "You obviously want to make shots like we did tonight, but you've got to hang your hat on something. Got to hang your hat on defense … We're not where we want to be. We can still get better in that, but those are the consistent habits that we want to establish for this team."

The Wizards started their winning streak with a 103-99 win over the Toronto Raptors, followed by a 98-94 victory over the Chicago Bulls. Both contests featured improved defensive intensity for a group that lacked it over its previous eight games.

Key in those wins was Bilal Coulibaly's defense. The fourth-year wing held Norman Powell to three points in the second half of Friday's win over Chicago, including a fourth-quarter sequence in which he blocked the 2026 All-Star twice in one possession.

Two nights later, Coulibaly helped hold Franz Wagner to just 18 points on 7-for-18 shooting. His defense is already elite. But he's striving for even more production.

"I always take a matchup personal. I really want to be one of the best defensive player in the league. I think I'm showing that I'm getting there."

The Wizards have jumped from 26th to 20th in defensive rating since that win over Toronto. They remain bottom-six in offensive efficiency and bottom-five in offensive rating, yet they're playing their best basketball of the season.

After Washington's 136-120 loss to the Memphis Grizzlies, Kyshawn George said the team lacked accountability. That was clear by the team's play and the coaching staff's actions. The same players were playing 20 minutes a night despite poor effort, limited offensive production and nonexistent defense.

Keefe has since altered those rotations. Bub Carrington has played just 11 minutes over the past four games — most of which came at the end of blowouts. Tre Johnson didn't play in the second half against Toronto and Chicago. Deandre Ayton played just five minutes against Orlando.

Keefe has also put the ball in AJ Dybantsa's hands, which has unlocked the No. 1 pick's downhill scoring ability. Dybantsa is averaging 19.7 points and 10.0 rebounds on 48.9% shooting over those three games.

At 7-11, the Wizards are ninth in the Eastern Conference. They're finally holding each other accountable. And it's producing wins.

"We have high character players. We're not here to just go through the motions and just be average," George said. "We want to accomplish something. We're playing to establish ourselves as not only players but also an organization. When the whole team has the same feeling, it's on us to just go out there and do our job. I think that's what we've been doing past the games."
