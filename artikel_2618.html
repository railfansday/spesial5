Air Force Football: Winning despite the War of Attrition

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Sometimes you have to struggle and endure to really appreciate things. This is universally applicable in life in general, and certainly as it relates to what we are experiencing with this years Air Force football team. Troy Calhoun and staff have put together so many stretches of highly successful football, it's easy to forget that level of success is incredibly difficult to sustain. I would submit that in the current economics of college football, it's nearly impossible for a majority of programs.

This program just concluded two challenging years of football in 2024 and 2025, fresh off the heels of going 40-12, winning 9 games or more over the previous four uninterrupted football seasons (this excludes the shortened 2020 Covid year). After that stretch, they won "just" nine games over the past two campaigns. An obvious drop from what we had experienced in that prior run.

Fast forward to the present, the Falcons are 8-2 (5-2 MW) in a dog fight for a spot in the Mountain West Conference Championship, with just two weeks remaining in the regular season. We are at POSIT 'time to appreciate'.

A cornerstone to the more lethal Air Force attacks has been the ability to connect on a crippling downfield pass at just the right time. We've seen it from Evan Medders and Liam Szarka this year, and it's deployment will be key in particular, when playing some of the more formidable defenses.

Unfortunately, Troy Calhoun has had to rely on going very deep on personnel far more than OC Mike Thiessen has had to dig into his bag of pass game creativity. To be fair though, this team has prospered immensely when not throwing a pass. Where they have not been so fortunate is at health in a few key positions.

It's well documented that Air Force has been forced to start four different quarterbacks throughout this year. In reality, five different players have seen meaningful playing time under center. Yet the offense continues to thrive. That is not usually the case when you test your quarterback depth to this degree. The one-two punch of Medders and Kemper Hodges has proven a highly effective fly-by-wire.

Probably less documented is what has happened in the middle of their defensive line. The team has rotated three different players inside. Granted, their D-Line leverages some versatile personnel. But when you consider the size of some of the offensive lines they face, relative to already light experience along that defensive front, it's quite impressive how guys like Oquendo, Tillet and Alcine performed.

The latest in this saga of injury news hits right at the heart of the team. Blake Fletcher exited last weeks victory over San Jose State late in the game. This is a linebacker who impacts the offense at every turn. Playing at an All-American level, Fletcher isn't someone that will simply be replaced. Dallas Dailey has played at a high level alongside him. Now he, is going to really have to shine with a new counterpart if this defense wants to continue their dominant play.

The last position we are going to mention is cornerback. There is no crazier statistic than the number of players that have had to not just contribute, but start at corner. Opposite an All-Conference performer in Korey Johnson has been an absolute carousel of names. Because of yet another injury, Steve Russ' defense is going to feature their sixth different player to start at the position. When you consider how most of college football spreads the ball all over the field, it's insane that with this many injuries, the Air Force defense hasn't just held serve, they've become dominant.

For perspective, this team ranks top three in the Mountain West in scoring defense, rushing defense, passing defense (2), pass efficiency and opponent first downs. If you watched this team last year, I don't know how you can't be blown away by their performance. Furthermore, that they are playing at this high of a level with the litany of injuries occurred— in particular at cornerback.

Entering the season, if you were to tell any Air Force fan the pass defense would be top 75 nationally, they'd likely be ecstatic. Just a year ago, this was the 127th ranked pass defense out of 134 teams. Today, there are just four teams allowing less pass yards than the Falcons (160 ypg allowed). It's not just pass yards that they are limiting, they rank 28th in pass defense efficiency. That same pass defense has been complemented by the 44th rush defense and 30th ranked scoring defense overall. A major leap forward.

Yes, I will acknowledge they have not faced a lot of elite pass attacks. But if you watched last year, you saw the likes of Navy and Army carving up the defense with career passing performances. That was not the case for either this season. This is a major improvement over last year. And I like to think part of why some of the teams Air Force has played don't have better passing numbers, is they had to play Steve Russ' defense.

So how has the pass defense been able to take such giant a leap forward starting seven different players? There's plenty of credit to go around, but one person in particular that really deserves a salute is Korey Johnson. This guy has basically removed one side of the field from play. It shouldn't come as a great surprise, there were signs last year as just a sophomore he showed great promise. Then he kicked off the 2026 season with a spectacular one-handed interception that he ripped away from a receiver. All this guy has done is stay healthy, shut down receivers and ball out.

You can't overstate what having a steady presence like Johnson does for a defense, especially when you are dealing with the rotating personnel the Falcons have opposite him. The ability to roll coverages to support opposite field and leave Johnson on an island without the concern of paying a big play price every time is invaluable.

I want to be careful not to suggest I'm ignoring the fine safety play of Max Mustell, Roger Jones jr. and Cade Paterson. But one of the reasons that guys like Mustell can get active in defending the run and collect sacks is the confidence someone like Johnson gives you to hold up in coverage. In reality, there may be two or three All-Mountain West team selections in this secondary.

No matter how you slice it, Korey Johnson has proven invaluable to this team. If you like Pro Football Focus data, he ranks 21st in defending the pass by passer rating. Opposing quarterbacks hold just a 30.6 pass rating when throwing at him. When you consider how infrequently teams throw his way, it's actually quite impressive that he's collected two interceptions and posted a 70.8 coverage grade. I guess when you've allowed just 12 catches on the year, it's wise of the quarterback to look elsewhere.

I also don't want to short change what a complete player Johnson has become either. This guy led the team in tackles and had an interception against Army, helping secure the Commander-in-Chief's Trophy. On the year, he's fourth on the team in tackles with 40, and 2.5 of which were tackles for loss. You get the point, in a team that has flourished with contributions from the many, this is one player having a fantastic season.

And let's not forget the other side of playing six guys opposite Johnson. It's not as if Oyamendan, Inweh, Ellie-Stuart, Young or Drain haven't done their part. It's beyond impressive that these guys have held serve in a time when programs with $50 million dollar rosters are crying about an individual starter being injured, crashing their season.
