Reviewing the Bears’ defensive PFN grades from Week 11 vs. Saints

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The Chicago Bears picked up a key win on Sunday, beating the Saints 21-13 to improve their record to 8-2. It wasn't always pretty, but when you're competing for a playoff spot in a loaded division like the NFC North, every win counts.

As has been the case since the Bears traded for All-Pro defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons, their defense was efficient yet again in their victory over New Orleans on Sunday. Any time you can hold an NFL team to just one touchdown over the course of an afternoon, it should be considered a good day, and Chicago got home against quarterback Tyler Shough to the tune of six sacks.

Each week, I'll review how Chicago's eligible performers graded by Pro Football Network's Impact Scoring model. Coming out of the bye week and picking up its fifth win in a row, here's how Week 11 looked for the team by the numbers for the defense and special teams.

Jordan van den Berg: 60.4 (64th of 86 DTs)

Clark Phillips III: 83.8 (10th of 86 CBs)

As a unit, the Bears finished fifth among all 26 NFL teams that played in Week 11 with an 82.7 PFN Defense Impact grade. In particular, they finished in the top ten in such categories as takeaways, sacks, fewest explosive plays allowed, success rate allowed, EPA per play allowed, and drives allowed involving points scored.

Finishing the game with four tackles and 3.0 sacks, it's no surprise that Jeffery Simmons was a top performer by PFN Impact grading in Week 11. His 93.1 grade marked the second-highest grade he's had all year, as well as the best grade he's had in his three games with Chicago thus far. Alongside him on the defensive line, Montez Sweat had his most efficient game of the season with an 85.2 PFN EDGE Impact grade, two tackles for loss, and a sack. He has had three of his top four performances of the season after the Bears acquired Simmons, his former defensive line running mate at Mississippi State.

The top performer on either side of the ball for the Bears was Dillon Thieneman, however. He finished Sunday off with seven tackles, one of which going for a loss, and an interception on the only pass that Saints quarterback Tyler Shough threw his way. Shough had more success throwing against Jaylon Johnson, who fell back down to Earth in Week 11 after leading the NFL in PFN CB Impact grading in Week 9 against the Buccaneers.

Another notable top performer for the Bears' defense against New Orleans was Clark Phillips III. The cornerback, whom Chicago acquired in the Gervon Dexter trade with the Falcons, had a sack and a forced fumble on Sunday. He also allowed just two catches for eight receiving yards in coverage, having been targeted four times, with an allowed passer rating of just 56.2. The Bears' defensive tackle room was pretty thin outside of Simmons' performance on Sunday, but a handful of starters performed at a solid enough level to keep the defense afloat.

Cairo Santos missed one of his two extra point attempts, but he nailed both of his field goal tries. He ended the game with +0.3 field goals over expected, hitting kicks from 41 and 35 yards out. Tory Taylor was also pretty average with his three punts against New Orleans, ranking No. 16 with 40.0 net yards per punt and No. 15 with +1.8 net yards per punt above expected.
