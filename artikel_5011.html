15 Chic and Surprising Christmas Tree Decorating Ideas

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Nothing says the holidays have arrived like a glowing pine strung with tinsel. If you’re looking for fresh inspiration, this collection of designer-approved Christmas tree decorating ideas has it all. From playful color palettes to chic lighting ideas and nostalgic personalization, designers are sharing their best tips for decorating full-sized, pencil, and even tabletop trees. Like a freshly pruned fir, their ideas are lush and abundant: oversized bows, classic red ornaments, blue Christmas trees, and velvet ribbon turned into handmade ornaments are just a few of their favorite festive touches. Whether you prefer a real tree, artificial Christmas tree, or even a flocked Christmas tree, lean minimalist or fully embrace maximalist holiday decor, we’ve rounded up our favorite Christmas decorating ideas to help you deck your halls in style.

To personalize your tree, take inspiration from Chanel Sansregret of the Charleston-based Studio Miles Design who purchases frame ornaments with the year to fill with photos of her children every holiday season. “For me, there’s something especially moving about hanging those memories on the branches and seeing so many stages of their childhood together in one place,” she shares. “As a designer, I love a beautifully considered tree, but the sentimental details are what give it soul,” Sansregret states.

“There’s something so special about turning a corner and finding another beautifully decorated tree,” says Emily Hewett of A Well Dressed Home based in Dallas and Kansas City. In her son’s bedroom, she opted for a slender pencil tree given the room’s space constraints. “My son’s tree sits directly in front of his bedroom window, which faces the street; one of my favorite details is seeing it all lit up from the front of our home at night,” Hewett says. A pencil tree has a small footprint, yet Hewett notes it brings a little extra Christmas magic to both the inside and outside of the home.

To make a Christmas tree feel like an integral part of a room, take design cues from the room itself. Here, Hewett adorned her tree with a collection of glass bauble Christmas ornaments that she’s curated over the years. “The color palette features varying shades of blue, green, and pink, along with clear iridescent ornaments that almost resemble delicate bath bubbles,” she says. The Christmas theme was inspired by her living room palette. “Every color was intentionally pulled from the wallpapers and fabrics in my living room, creating a Christmas tree that feels like a natural extension of the space,” Hewett says.

When decorating your Christmas tree in a room with dark walls, Renee DiSanto of the Chicago area–based Park & Oak recommends using lots of twinkle lights and weaving them into the tree in a zigzag pattern. “I tend to opt for a warm white light rather than a cool blue tone for a more cozy, inviting look,” she shares. Here, the designer used bright colored decorations, a bead garland, a simple tree skirt—and plenty of twinkle lights. “A well-lit tree will always pop against dark walls,” DiSanto says.

“Bows are such a classic, but including them on a tree in a dramatic scale is what makes something traditional feel unexpected and even a bit whimsical,” says Amy Studebaker of Amy Studebaker Design. She approaches Christmas tree decorating the same way she approaches designing a home: ”With a fresh take on traditional, layers and layers of charm, and a little sprinkle of the unexpected.” For a fun and festive look, replace a traditional tree topper with a large bow and adorn branches with smaller, coordinating bows.

Brown has become the neutral this season, adding cozy sophistication to interiors and now, to holiday decor. Anastasia Casey of The Interior Collective loves the color for its warmth and versatility, and used it as the primary color on this tree. “I layered matte and metallic brown ornaments with neutrals—think muddy hues of bone, chocolate, and champagne—to create depth and avoid a flat look,” Casey shares. While the metallics catch light, the browns ground the palette, resulting in a tree that Casey says is both elevated and inviting.

To add charm and a touch of whimsy to a Christmas tree, Anastasia Casey of The Interior Collective looks for ornaments that feel collected and tell a story, whether they’re vintage finds, handmade pieces, or unique shapes. “In order to make it feel cohesive, I recommend mixing textures and scales, like pairing glass baubles with fabric or wood. It brings a sense of personality and keeps the tree from feeling too uniform,” she shares. Casey also loves incorporating dynamic elements like ribbon or bells to add movement and playfulness.

What is the correct order to decorate a Christmas tree?

The first thing in Griffin’s step-by-step approach is adding twinkle lights to the tree, then tucking in larger ornaments. “Next, I add the smaller decorative ornaments, and finally, I add the tinsel or bows on the edges of the tree limbs,” Griffin shares. Studebaker says you should have fun with decorating and not take the process too seriously; “It can still be sophisticated, but it should absolutely be a happy and playful process along the way,” the designer says.

What are the most popular Christmas tree decorations?

Designers say German glass ornaments, Christopher Radko ornaments, cascading ribbon, festive bows, and warm white lights are among the most popular Christmas tree decorations. Metallic decorations, whether gold, silver, or copper, are another favorite. “I love decorating with metallic ornaments because they reflect the light so beautifully,” Bonney-James shares.

For Bonney-James, a timeless Christmas theme includes a fresh tree. “I’ll trade messy needles for an aromatic, nostalgic experience any day,” she says. Another must-have are warm white lights, which is why she recommends checking the temperature of LED lights to make sure they don’t appear blue. The addition of silver, gold, and white decorations completes the timeless look and adds festive shine.
