Bristol Myers Squibb vs. Johnson & Johnson: Which Healthcare Giant's Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Johnson & Johnson's diversified medicines and MedTech mix contrasts sharply with Bristol Myers Squibb's oncology focus, and the valuation gap between them may tip the scales for investors.

By Brendan Coffey – Updated Nov 23, 2026 at 11:11AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Bristol Myers Squibb ›

Choosing between two healthcare leaders requires a close look at their evolving drug portfolios and financial foundations. You must decide whether Bristol Myers Squibb (BMY +1.16%) or Johnson & Johnson (JNJ +0.10%) offers the better path for long-term growth.

Both companies operate within the pharmaceutical stocks category, yet they serve different niches. Bristol Myers Squibb focuses heavily on biopharmaceuticals for serious diseases, while Johnson & Johnson offers a mix of medicines and surgical devices. These businesses are often compared due to their massive scale and importance to global healthcare infrastructure.

Bristol Myers Squibb focuses on discovering and developing medicines for oncology, hematology, neuroscience, and cardiovascular diseases. The company distributes its products to wholesalers, specialty pharmacies, and government agencies. It recently expanded its reach with direct-to-patient offerings for several drugs, providing roughly 80% discounts to cash-paying patients. Additionally, a December 2025 agreement with the U.S. government involves providing Eliquis to the Medicaid program for free and donating ingredients to the national reserve.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $50 billion, representing growth of approximately 4.2% over the prior period. Net income for the same time frame was around $9.2 billion. This performance shows a recovery in profitability compared to certain prior periods. The company relies on its core segments to offset pricing pressures and changes in government healthcare policies.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was nearly 2.6x. This ratio, which measures total debt relative to shareholders' equity, suggests the company uses a significant amount of borrowed capital. The current ratio of 1.3x compares short-term assets to short-term liabilities, indicating the company has enough resources to cover its upcoming obligations. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow reached roughly $12.8 billion. Free cash flow is the cash left over after the business covers its day-to-day operations and equipment upgrades.

Johnson & Johnson operates through two primary segments: Innovative Medicine and MedTech. Its Innovative Medicine division develops treatments for oncology, immunology, and neuroscience, while the MedTech arm provides surgical and orthopedic devices. The company serves a diverse group of customers, including hospitals, retailers, and healthcare professionals globally. It maintains an extensive global supply chain and frequently enters licensing agreements with strategic partners to co-develop new products.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 27, 2026, revenue reached approximately $99.3 billion. This was supported by revenue growth of nearly 7.8% compared to the previous year. Net income for the period was about $16.1 billion. These figures highlight the scale of the company, which continues to generate substantial profits across its various medical and technology segments.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was roughly 0.6x. This indicates a more conservative use of debt relative to its equity than many of its peers. The current ratio is approximately 1.0x, indicating that its short-term assets are roughly equal to its short-term liabilities. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 28, 2025, free cash flow was about $19.7 billion. This strong cash generation provides the company with flexibility for acquisitions and research and development initiatives.

Bristol Myers Squibb faces intense pricing pressure from government policies such as the Inflation Reduction Act, which has led to price caps on key drugs like Eliquis and Orencia. Patent cliff risks are also significant, as generic versions of Revlimid and Eliquis threaten to take market share. The company deals with high research and development failure rates and operational reliance on partners like BioNTech (BNTX +5.19%), Zai Lab (ZLAB +5.75%), Zenas BioPharma (ZBIO +5.28%), and Merck (MRK +0.94%). High debt levels from recent acquisitions like Karuna and RayzeBio also limit its financial flexibility.

Johnson & Johnson faces extensive litigation over its talc-based products, which recently resulted in a $5.5 billion settlement. The company also deals with high competitive pressure and the impending loss of patent exclusivity for key products like Darzalex and Stelara. Regulatory scrutiny and evolving laws regarding artificial intelligence and cybersecurity pose ongoing compliance costs. Additionally, the plan to separate its orthopedics business could present integration challenges and unforeseen financial costs in the coming years.

Based on both Forward P/E and P/S ratio metrics, Bristol Myers Squibb appears more conservatively priced than Johnson & Johnson.

Johnson & Johnson is an overlooked behemoth compared with faster-growing biotech companies. The business has 28 products and platforms that generate more than $1 billion in annual revenue. Its pharmaceuticals arm, Innovative Medicine, has received two FDA approvals this year and has two more approved drug product launches in the E.U. and the U.S. The Innovative Medicine division accounts for 65% of JNJ's annual revenue and appears to have a robust pipeline beyond the pending products. MedTech, its other arm, sells a wide range of healthcare products, including wound care, surgical instruments and implants, hip and knee replacements, and spinal implants. The recent buy of Atraverse Medical bolsters that business. Revenue for Johnson & Johnson is expected to rise to $101 billion in the current fiscal year. However, Wall Street anticipates a $10 billion drop in net income, to around $16.6 billion.

Bristol Myers Squibb, meanwhile, has made positive strides, with its newer growth brands gaining traction and some interesting candidates in the pipeline. The stock is cheap, and that seems to be for good reason. Investors have already priced in much of the upcoming difficulty, which softens the downside.

Revenue for the current fiscal year should edge up 3% to $49.8 billion, with a much healthier rise in net income of more than 40% to $11.2 billion.

Bristol Myers has attractively low forward price-to-earnings and price-to-sales ratios, but, of course, that reflects some continued rough sailing ahead. Johnson & Johnson does come at a bit of a premium to the average S&P 500 stock, but not so much as to be prohibitive. Still, the healthy rise in net income for Bristol Myers is a sign its rebound is underway. For 2027, go with BMY.
