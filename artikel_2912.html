3 Dividend Kings to Buy in November That Could Pay You for the Rest of Your Life

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Walmart, Lowe's, and Genuine Parts have raised their dividends annually for more than 50 straight years, and each one is making big moves this month.

This is a big week for two retail chains that tens of millions of Americans walk into regularly without a second thought. Lowe's reports its third-quarter results on Wednesday before the opening of trading, and Walmart follows on Thursday.

Both are Dividend Kings — companies that have raised their dividends every year for at least 50 consecutive years. Not just paid one, but raised it, through recessions, oil shocks, and every ugly market in between. Fewer than 60 companies are currently on that list. Another is Genuine Parts, the company behind NAPA Auto Parts, which I also view as a smart buy this month.

That steadiness is what makes all three worth a look if you want income streams that will likely still be growing decades from now. When a dividend-hiking run gets that long, the annual increases become more than habit — they become a central part of the investment thesis, and no board wants to break the streak. Here's what each of these companies is doing right now to keep theirs going.

Walmart (WMT -0.69%) raised its dividend for the 53rd straight year in February. CFO John David Rainey called the raise "a proof point of our continued confidence in our business performance and forward momentum." What I like more is how it's treating shoppers this fall.

Walmart paid billions in tariffs during the earlier phases of President Donald Trump’s trade wars, and it raised prices to pass them along to customers. But the Supreme Court ruled early in 2026 that those tariffs were illegal, and as a result, Walmart alone got back about $2.9 billion in tariff refunds this year. Instead of pocketing the cash, it has been cutting prices. It had more than 11,000 rollbacks running in the second quarter, up from about 7,200 three months earlier.

"We're investing heavily in price because customers need us to, and because we believe it drives market share gains over time," CEO John Furner said.

That's also the catch for Thursday. Walmart told investors to expect only 2% to 4% operating income growth this quarter because of all those price reductions, and Furner admitted customers are "still feeling some pressure."

The retailer’s holiday push is aimed squarely at stretched budgets. Walmart's holiday meal basket feeds 10 people for $35, with more food than last year's. It's running three sales events between Nov. 12 and Dec. 2, and now bundles Walmart+ and Sam's Club Plus memberships for $120 a year.

Walmart is getting better at helping people shop, too. The number of customers using Sparky, its AI shopping assistant, is up 70% from last year, and they spend 40% more per order than shoppers who skip it. Furner described one shopper asking Sparky for a week of high-protein meals and receiving recipes, with every ingredient added to the cart with one click.

Walmart sells groceries and household basics, the heart of consumer staples, so it doesn't need a strong economy to keep its customers. I'm buying the stock before Thursday.

Lowe's (LOW -1.99%) has paid a dividend every quarter since going public in 1961, and it has raised that payout every year for more than half a century.

This year has been harder than many. Home improvement is a consumer discretionary business, and people may put off building a new patio or renovating a kitchen when gas prices are unusually high.

"Elevated fuel prices combined with broader economic uncertainty have influenced household budgets," CEO Marvin Ellison said in August. At the time, Lowe's said it expected sales at existing stores to be roughly flat this year.

Contractors, online orders, and installation services kept it growing anyway, for a fifth straight quarter. Ellison said that growth came "despite pressure in discretionary DIY spending." The shelves are changing to match. Lowe's is adding more than 150 new DeWalt items and bringing Traeger grills into stores and online nationwide. It also bought two businesses that serve builders and contractors, though high mortgage rates are slowing new home construction, which is hurting the performance of both of those acquired businesses for now.

The retailer is also finding new ways to reach people. Its AI shopping assistant, Mylow, has fielded more than 25 million questions from customers and employees, and online shoppers who use it buy at triple the rate of those who don't.

On Nov. 10, Lowe's became the exclusive home improvement partner of Bilt, a rewards platform with more than 8 million members who earn points on their housing payments.

"Home improvement is personal," said Lowe's Chief Marketing Officer Jen Wilson, and the deal lets Lowe's "meet customers in a community they already know and love." A curated Lowe's marketplace inside Bilt is planned for early 2027.

The DIY slowdown is already built into its forecast, so Wednesday’s results don't need to be great, just steady. I'd buy ahead of it and Lowe's Dec. 9 investor conference.

Genuine Parts (GPC -1.29%) has raised its dividend for 70 straight years. Early next year, it splits into two companies: the NAPA-led auto parts business, which keeps the Genuine Parts name, and Motion, its industrial parts supplier.

Court Carruthers, who will run the auto side, said the split gives it "greater flexibility and focus to successfully compete across our global markets." He also put the business plainly: "The mechanic in the garage turning a wrench. This is the core of who GPC serves and how the business was built." He praised NAPA's independent store owners, too: "They know their markets. They know their customers. They build trusted local relationships."

Behind the counter, NAPA just opened its first heavily automated distribution center in eastern Pennsylvania to get parts to those garages faster.

The weak spot for the company is drivers' wallets.

"Our experience indicates that higher energy prices from the Iran conflict are weakening consumer sentiment," CFO Bert Nappier said. Comparable sales to its retail customers fell 3% last quarter, and the company trimmed its profit forecast for the year.

CEO Will Stengel, who will lead Motion, said the company remains "on track to launch GPC and Motion as two independent, leading companies in the first quarter of 2027." The next quarterly dividend goes out to shareholders on Jan. 5, with an ex-dividend date of Dec. 4.
