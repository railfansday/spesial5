Worried About a Market Crash? 2 Gold Mining Stocks to Buy Before 2026 Ends.

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Agnico Eagle Mines and Franco-Nevada are two of the best stocks to buy in advance of a market crash.

By James Halley – Nov 26, 2026 at 10:30AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Agnico Eagle Mines ›

If you're building a portfolio to survive a potential market crash or economic downturn, Agnico Eagle Mines (AEM +3.03%) and Franco-Nevada (FNV +3.06%) should be on your list. The Toronto-based companies combine exposure to gold, a traditional safe-haven asset, with strong balance sheets that can withstand liquidity crunches.

All gold mining stocks present a relatively high level of risk. The price of gold vacillates wildly. The precious metal hit an all-time high of $5,589.38 per ounce at the end of January but is now trading at around $3,572 per ounce.

It's cost-intensive to run a mining company, and even when gold prices are rising, operational costs and debt loads can easily outweigh a company's profit potential. Agnico Eagle Mines and Franco-Nevada both mitigate those risks, but in substantially different ways.

Agnico Eagle's gold mines present less jurisdictional risk from geopolitical and labor disruptions than typical gold mining stocks. The company operates in Tier-1 mining-friendly locations, including Canada, Australia, Finland, and Mexico.

Agnico also hasn't had to make expensive acquisitions of late thanks to continued underground expansion at its Canadian Malartic location and project growth at Hope Bay in Nunavut, Canada, and at Ikkari in Finland.

In the third quarter, the company reported nine-month all-in sustaining costs (AISC) of $1,529 per ounce of gold while selling each ounce for an average of $4,537. Agnico reported nine-month earnings per share (EPS) of $9.25, up 58% year over year. The stock is up more than 14% so far this year, and even with that gain, the stock is trading at below 13 times earnings.

The company also raised its dividend by 12.5% this year to $0.45 per share quarterly, yielding around 1.11% at its current share price, which is right around the S&P 500 average. The cash dividend payout ratio is only 19.3%, so it is more than well-covered.

Franco-Nevada is a gold streaming company. It doesn't actually mine for gold but earns royalties from the gold-mining companies to which it has provided up-front funds. When gold prices rise, the company benefits either from royalty payments or the right to purchase a portion of the mine's gold production at a pre-agreed-upon discounted price, known as a stream.

The company has interests in 445 assets, including 129 operating mines. Most of its money comes from gold mining, roughly 70%, though it also makes money from natural gas liquids, oil, gas, and silver.

Because it only needs a handful of employees -- 44 full-time and five part-time contractors, as of the end of 2025 -- to operate, its margins are higher than typical mining companies.

Through nine months, the company reported EPS of $6.30, up 63.1% year over year, and record revenue of $1.78 billion, up 45% over the same period a year ago. Adjusted net income margin improved by 710 basis points to 65.8%. Even with those numbers, its shares have fallen more than 7% so far this year.

Franco-Nevada's dividend yields 0.72% at its current share price. The company has raised its quarterly dividend for 19 consecutive years, including a 16% hike this year to $0.44 per share. Like Agnico Eagle, its dividend is well-covered, with a cash dividend payout ratio of only 26.3%.

Gold's price has been weighed down this year by high interest rates, as high-yield bonds have become a more attractive vehicle for passive investing. However, if the stock market declines sharply, rather than raising rates, the Federal Reserve would likely lower them, making gold more attractive.

In the meantime, Agnico Eagle and Franco-Nevada are financially healthy and more stable than most mining stocks, with safe dividends.
