3 bleak thoughts after the Mavericks’ no-show in 131-94 loss at the Boston Celtics

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The last three games make it clear. This season is all about managing expectations for fans of the Dallas Mavericks (7-7). But good grief.

The Mavericks dropped the second game of a back-to-back set in horrifying fashion, 131-94, in a Sunday matinee against the Boston Celtics (11-2) at TD Garden.

The unbridled optimism that came along with a surprise 7-4 start has been tempered, to say the least, after a pair of stinkers at the Minnesota Timberwolves and the Cleveland Cavaliers, followed by Sunday's scheduled loss, when the Mavs were again without star 34-year-old guard Kyrie Irving, due to injury management (rest).

The Celtics poured in three of their first four 3-point attempts to jump out to an 11-2 lead three minutes into the game, and the tone was set quickly. The Mavs didn't make their first shot from the field until Zaccharie Risacher hit a long jumper from the corner with 5:22 left in the first. The starting lineup of Cooper Flagg, Caleb Martin, Naji Marshall, Daniel Gafford and PJ Washington provided zero floor spacing on the offensive end.

And so, naturally, the Celtics' early lead swelled to 19-4 after Jordan Walsh went to the basket for six straight points on three straight possessions less than five minutes into the game. If Cooper Flagg isn't going out of his mind on offense, the roster as constructed for Sunday's game just doesn't stand a chance against the league's best teams.

Boston canned seven triples in the first quarter, while the Mavs shot an abysmal 2-of-18 from the field. The game was basically over as the Celtics led by an appalling 42-9 count after one. It goes without saying that the nine points were the lowest scoring output for the Mavs of the young 2026-27 season, but what was worse is that the team appeared to let it affect their effort on defense, as well.

The Mavericks woke up a little in the second quarter, but waking up after you're down 35 points is not worth a whole hell of a lot. A little 9-0 run late in the frame pulled the Mavs to within 59-34, but it seems laughable to even mention developments like that in the course of such a monumental shellacking.

Dallas turned the ball over 12 times in the first half to offset the team's five second-quarter 3-point makes, and Boston carried a lopsided 67-37 lead into the halftime break.

Flagg's early run of brilliance to start the season has hit a hard car crash of a stop. He shot a combined 11-of-31 in the two losses leading up to Sunday's game at Boston, and he followed that up by missing all three of his first-quarter shot attempts.

We see apex Flagg, as we've learned, when he plays next to competent guards, and that was simply not the case against the Celtics. The starting lineup at Boston featured exactly zero guards, and when Walter Clayton Jr. and Marcus Sasser entered halfway into the first, they offered little to no help on either end. Clayton came back in the second and scored eight points on two made 3-pointers, but the damage had been done at that point.

The Celtics' defense concentrated all their efforts into containing Flagg, because Flagg was the only viable source of offense left on Dallas' injury-riddled roster. He finally connected on his first field goal early in the second, on the low block through the foul of Payton Pritchard. But after two more 3-balls from the Celtics on the other end, the Celtics' lead had ballooned to 50-15 with nine minutes left in the first half.

There will be angst, and a certain amount of angst is warranted here, but circumstances aren't helping the 19-year-old star, either. He scored just five points in the first half and finished with 17 on 5-of-15 shooting in the loss.

Let's just get this out of the way and move on, shall we? Jayson Tatum torched the Mavericks for 21 of his game-high 32 points in the third quarter to lead way for the Celtics in the win. Derrick White added 16. Eight different Celtics made two or more 3-pointers, including Tatum, who put in five of them.

Risacher led the Mavericks with 18 off the bench on 8-of-9 shooting.
