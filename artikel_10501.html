The simplest well-being strategy leaders overlook

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

As leaders, we spend a lot of time thinking about employee well-being. I’ve found that supporting it often comes down to the everyday choices we make. That includes some of the simplest choices we tend to overlook. One of them is getting outside.

We know spending time in nature is good for us. What’s striking is how hard it can be to do.

The American Nature and Health Survey, which was supported by the REI Cooperative Action Fund in partnership with the Nature and Health Alliance, highlights this increasing disconnect. In the 2026 survey, Americans shared that nature is essential to their lives, yet we’re spending less time in it. A decade ago, 25% of respondents spent less than two hours a week in nature. Today, that number has nearly doubled to 45%.

You can likely imagine why. Our lives are increasingly designed around convenience and screens. We can work, shop, connect, and entertain ourselves without ever stepping outside. Technology has made much of our lives easier and better, but it has also changed how we spend our time, often without us consciously making that choice.

That matters because time in nature is linked to lower stress and better mental and physical health. And it doesn’t have to mean hours on the trail. Research suggests even 20 minutes outside can help us feel more centered, in mind and body.

A RETAILER CLOSING ON BLACK FRIDAY

For many people, there are real barriers to spending time outdoors, including access and safety. For others, it’s simply the competing demands of work and daily life. We still value nature, but too often, life makes it difficult to choose.

That point has stuck with me as a business leader. There are plenty of things leaders can’t control. But we do have influence over how people spend their time at work and the conditions we create for people to do their best. With well-being, that means asking whether the way we work creates room for people to step away, recharge, and spend time outside.

At REI Co-op, we’ve been exploring one version of that idea for more than a decade. In 2015, the co-op made an unusual choice for a retailer: We closed our stores on Black Friday, one of the most deeply ingrained traditions in American retail. We paid our employees to spend the day outside and encouraged customers to do the same. We called it Opt Outside.

We didn’t tell employees how to spend the day or define what counted as “outside.” It could be a long hike, a walk through the neighborhood, time at a park with loved ones, or simply a quiet moment outdoors. The point was to create room for something that’s remarkably easy to push aside.

MAKE CHOICES

More than a decade since that first Opt Outside, I think there’s a leadership lesson in that choice, and it extends beyond REI. Not every company should do what we did. As leaders, we all make choices about what we make room for.

Those choices don’t have to be big. Could your next one-on-one be a walk outside? Could a team gathering happen outdoors? Where in the workday could we make stepping outside, even for a few minutes, a little easier?

These are small choices, but that’s the point. Supporting well-being and creating a better experience at work doesn’t always require another program or initiative. It starts with the everyday choices we make as leaders, and the things those choices make possible for others.

CHOOSE TIME OUTSIDE

This Black Friday, REI will once again close our stores and give our employees the day to spend outside. I’ll be doing the same, heading out for a hike with my family.

One day outside doesn’t solve everything, and it’s not meant to. But after more than a decade of Opt Outside, at REI we still believe there’s value in choosing time outside. It has reinforced something for me about leadership: Our job is sometimes to look at what’s standing in the way of making different choices, and see what we can do to change it.

Mary Beth Laughton is president and CEO of REI Co-op.
