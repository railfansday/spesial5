All these outages show how corporate America needs to modernize out-of-date tech

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Over the past five days, a string of high-profile tech outages has left Americans struggling to send emails, catch flights, and access their bank accounts.

While the disruptions raised concerns about AI-powered cybersecurity threats, the real culprit may be something less sinister: corporate America’s tech systems are simply out of date. 

Outages like those at JPMorgan Chase, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), and Microsoft can occur when computer systems are undergoing major upgrades, Marva Bailer, CEO of tech advisory firm Qualaix, told the New York Post. Bolting on new technology can leave the systems vulnerable.

Although it is unclear whether the recent outages were directly related to artificial intelligence, many organizations are currently upgrading their networks to handle AI tools. Microsoft attributed its outage to an issue that arose during a network capacity upgrade, according to the Financial Times.

The time of year could also be a factor, as technicians rush to make sure systems are ready for the holiday shopping and travel season.

“It’s holiday time, it’s travel time, it’s shopping,” Bailer told the New York Post. “So, you have all those higher spikes in volume and more pressure to [update your systems].”

AI is also allowing companies to identify software vulnerabilities far more quickly, possibly leaving IT workers with too much work on their hands, the Post reported.

Like Bailer, U.S. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin attributed the disruptions to companies trying to upgrade aging systems, during an appearance on CNBC earlier today.

“[They’re] pushing new technology on top of really old computer systems, and it just seems to overwhelm the system,” he said.

The disruptions serve as a reminder of how cutting-edge technology is often still dependent on centralized, aging systems.

Friday’s nationwide ground stop, which resulted in more than 18,000 flight cancellations over three days, was triggered by the installation of a single router at a remote facility in Arkansas, Reuters reported. Monday’s software glitch at JPMorgan Chase disrupted transactions for customers of other major banks, even though their own computer systems were functioning normally.

Indeed, third-party tech providers have accounted for roughly two-thirds of publicly reported IT outages over the past nine years, according to a recent report from Uptime Institute, a global digital infrastructure advisory firm based in New York City.
