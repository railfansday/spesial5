The furnace clicks: what an AI voice agent needs to answer before the appointment

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A homeowner calls a heating company just after 7 a.m.: "My furnace clicks every time it kicks on. Should I worry?"

For a person at the front desk, that's an easy call if they know the customer and the equipment. For an AI voice agent, it's a good test of whether the agent is actually wired into the business or just reading a script. Here's what has to be in place for the answer to be right.

The agent has to match the caller ID to a customer record before it says anything useful. Without that, "on your model" is impossible, and every call starts with twenty questions.

"That's the igniter; on your model it usually means a dirty flame sensor" is technician knowledge. It should come from the company's own knowledge base, written once by the people who do the work, not from whatever a general-purpose model remembers about furnaces. In Aura, that's the knowledge base you attach to the agent: the agent answers from it.

"Booked for tomorrow at nine" only counts if a booking exists afterwards. The agent needs a connection to the scheduling system the business already uses, so the slot is real and the confirmation email goes out from that system. In Aura that's an HTTP action against the company's own tools; the agent doesn't keep a separate calendar.

All of this happens on a live call, so latency and hand-off matter. When the voice agent runs inside the phone platform rather than as an outside service bolted onto it, a caller who needs a person can be transferred like any other call, and the call record, transcript and summary land in the same place as every other call.

A useful voice agent is mostly plumbing: caller lookup, a knowledge base the business owns, and actions into the systems it already runs. The voice is the easy part.

Bookings and equipment details come from the business's connected systems. Aura is the AI voice agent built into SIPSTACK.
