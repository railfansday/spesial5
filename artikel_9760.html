Using RAG in AML: Why Retrieval Quality Matters More Than Similarity

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) can help AML teams access relevant information from large collections of case records, policies and investigative material. But retrieving a similar document is not the same as retrieving useful evidence.

This distinction matters in financial crime investigations. A system might find a previous case with comparable transaction activity, but that case may involve a different customer profile, payment purpose or set of circumstances. The use of RAG in explainable AML investigations must account for those differences.

A practical RAG workflow should consider three questions:

Is the source relevant? The system should identify why a previous record has been retrieved, rather than relying only on keyword overlap.

Is the information reliable? The investigator should be able to inspect the source, its date and its limitations.

Does it support the current assessment? Retrieved information should help frame further questions, not automatically determine the outcome.

This is especially important when historical case records contain outdated assumptions or incomplete reasoning. A previous decision should not become a permanent instruction for future cases.

The quality of the underlying records also matters. If firms want AI to retrieve meaningful investigative context, they need structured and consistently documented information. This connects directly with the wider challenge of organisational memory in AML investigations.

RAG should therefore be evaluated on more than response speed. Firms should test whether it retrieves appropriate evidence, identifies uncertainty and allows investigators to verify the material used.

These are important considerations when assessing an AML investigation platform.
