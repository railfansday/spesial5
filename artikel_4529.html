Ineos Announces Plans to Move Production of Grenadier SUV, Quartermaster Pickup Truck to the United States

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The move will bring assembly straight to Ineos Automotive’s largest market.

Back in 2016, Land Rover ended production of the old-school Defender. Ineos CEO Jim Ratcliffe then entered the automotive market to fill the niche with the defiantly old-school Grenadier SUV and its pickup truck counterpart, the Quartermaster. Ineos describes the vehicles as offering “rugged British spirit and design with German engineering rigor.” And soon, the brand will add “American-made” to that mix.

On Tuesday, Ineos Automotive announced it is moving Grenadier and Quartermaster production to the United States. The move will happen in 2028, with production moving to an undetermined location in America; an announcement on that should come within a few months. As part of the shuffle, Ineos Automotive says it will sell its current plant in Hambach, France to Chinese automaker BYD, but the brand will still build Grenadier and Quartermaster vehicles in Hambach while it sets up American facilities.

“Americans have already taken the Grenadier to their hearts, and U.S. policies that have created an incredibly attractive, pro-growth business environment. Setting up production there makes so much sense. It will empower us to invest, expand, and reach even more customers around the world,” Ineos Automotive CEO Lynn Calder said in a statement.

This move should make a lot of sense for Ineos. 70 percent of the brand’s global sales are in the U.S. market, so moving production stateside would leave the brand far less exposed to American import tariffs. The Trump administration has imposed a 15 percent tariff on European Union-built vehicles, and the Quartermaster is subject to the 25 percent “chicken tax,” which dates back to the 1960s and protects the U.S. pickup truck market. Alleviating the tariff burden would allow Ineos Automotive to lower prices further, improve profit margins—or, potentially, both.

Ineos Automotive’s announcement made no mention of the purportedly still-upcoming Fusilier, expected to launch as an EV and range-extended EV later this decade. Initial plans called for Austrian manufacturer Magna, maker of the Mercedes G-Class, to engineer and build the Fusilier at its plant in Austria.
