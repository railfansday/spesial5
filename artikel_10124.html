Arm strongarms site that hosts ad-free documentation and it may disappear

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Easy-to-use, popular, not collecting data for us, or serving our ads? Down with you!

Arm is swinging the hammer at an enthusiast’s site hosting a bloat-free version of its instruction reference documentation, demanding it be taken offline after several years of availability.

The demand from Arm lawyers to Jon Palmisciano concern Jon’s Arm Reference, a site that provides the same arm Instruction Set Architecture (ISA) info available on Arm’s site, but with what Palmisciano describes as “without a million popups and all of the bloat” that Arm provides on its own.

According to Palmisciano, his copy of the Arm ISA was culled from machine-readable XML files and stripped of the bloat. He built his version in 2022 with new CSS on top of it, and the addition of a quick search feature that, according to his comments on Hacker News, isn’t available in the official version.

“The aim has always been to make it the most comfortable and bullshit-free browsing experience for people who need these docs daily,” Palmisciano wrote in HN. Based on the responses the site seems to have plenty of fans.

That doesn’t matter to Arm, though: As far as the lawyers are concerned, Palmisciano’s site is a copyright violation due to its use of Arm trademarks and inclusion of copyrighted reference material for the Armv9-A and Armv9-A architecture.

Arm doesn’t care if Palmisciano’s project is well intentioned or for the benefit of the many software developers that write code for its chipsets, either: They want it offline by December 4 – 10 days from the publication of this story – or else.

As for what is going to happen, it seems like Jon’s Arm Reference will likely cease to exist in a little over a week – at least in its current form.

“Ideally I'd love to just get permission from Arm to keep the site up, but I'm pretty pessimistic about that,” Palmisciano told us. That said, Palmisciano has “already seen a few archives and mirrors already.” The HN thread discussing the takedown order includes links to several versions, and Palmisciano has another trick up his sleeve that he’s considering playing: Open-sourcing the software he used to build his ISA page.

“I'd also consider open-sourcing my tooling so that people can (for their own private use) turn the original XML downloads from Arm into an identical site they can host locally,” Palmisciano told us. He said that he'll likely tweet about opening the tooling if he does do so. He's also considered directing the URL for Jon's Arm reference to point to the open-source tool, but Arm wants the subdomain it's hosted on removed from his site as well, so that may not happen.

Arm has been in touch to defend itself as well, saying that Arm appreciates the efforts of people like Palmisciano, but that it still has to protect its brand, and documentation.

"We have a responsibility to ensure Arm is the primary source of technical resources and information and to protect the Arm brand," an Arm spokesperson told us. "Striking the right balance between third parties sharing helpful peer-to-peer resources while ensuring copyrighted Arm-related technical content is accurate has been critical to building the thriving ecosystem we have today."

In other words, if you’re an Arm dev worried about losing a valuable resource and having to turn to a website that bombards you with junk just to look up a few simple opcodes and operands you’d better find a backup host before next month.

That said, Arm is willing to work something out with Palmisciano, the company told us.

"We have recently flagged our concerns with regard to the use of Arm copyright content and the Arm brand on this website and shared the Arm trademark guidelines," Arm explained. "Our hope is to open a constructive dialogue, and we've asked that the website owner reach out as we seek to resolve this."As Palmisciano posted on HN, Arm is asking for removal of its trademark and copyrighted documentation. That, Palmisciano said, "seems hard to work around while still keeping the site useful."  He also followed up around 8.30 pm GMT on Tuesday to let us know that he has not yet heard directly from Arm. ®
