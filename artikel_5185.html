HIVE Reports $84.9M Revenue as AI GPU Business Grows 71% Amid Mining Pressures

TL;DR: HIVE Digital reported $84.9 million in fiscal second-quarter revenue and roughly 71% growth in its AI-focused BUZZ GPU business. The company also recorded a substantial quarterly loss, including a mining-equipment impairment. The results show both the promise and cost of shifting computing capacity toward AI services.

Bitcoin miners have spent much of 2026 trying to turn their access to electricity, land and data centers into businesses that can serve more than one market. HIVE Digital’s latest numbers put a price tag on that strategy.

The company said its fiscal second quarter, ended September 30, 2026, brought in approximately $84.9 million of revenue. Its BUZZ high-performance-computing division generated about $8.8 million, an increase of 70.7% from the comparable period. HIVE also reported about $139 million in cash on the balance sheet.

The growth is meaningful, but the AI arm remains far smaller than the company’s combined operations. A rapidly growing new division does not automatically replace the economics of Bitcoin mining.

HIVE reported a quarterly net loss in the region of $78 million and included a mining-equipment impairment of approximately $31.2 million. Impairments are generally noncash accounting charges in the period they are recorded, but they can still indicate that expected future economic value has fallen.

That distinction is important. A loss containing a major impairment is not the same as the business spending an identical amount of cash in the quarter. Equally, treating impairments as irrelevant would miss what they say about the pressure on older machines or assumptions about their earning potential.

Mining hardware has a useful life shaped by electricity prices, network difficulty, equipment efficiency and Bitcoin’s market value. When those assumptions change, a fleet that looked attractive two years ago may not justify its former carrying value.

The case for AI data centers is that demand for specialized computing may offer a revenue stream less closely tied to Bitcoin’s mining difficulty. But supplying GPU services requires capital, customer relationships, network infrastructure and operational expertise different from the work of running application-specific mining rigs.

NewsBTC has tracked miners making that transition, including Riot’s Bitcoin sales alongside its AI-data-center ambitions. HIVE’s BUZZ expansion is another version of the same strategic question: how quickly can an established miner earn durable income from compute services?

Partnerships and infrastructure projects are relevant here too. Our reporting on TeraWulf’s growth in AI leasing shows how the business of securing power-intensive sites and monetizing them through leases has become competitive. Possessing a data center does not guarantee demand for the GPUs housed in it.

Future reports should clarify how much revenue the AI division earns, whether its margins justify continued capital investment and how the mining segment performs as difficulty changes. Total company revenue can rise or fall for reasons unrelated to the profitability of the new computing business.

Investors should also watch the company’s liquidity, debt commitments and equipment spending. The $139 million cash figure is helpful context, but not a substitute for understanding cash consumption and future construction obligations.

HIVE is presenting itself as a dual-engine business. The latest quarter demonstrates that GPU services are growing quickly, while the bottom-line loss shows why the transition remains a work in progress rather than an accomplished turnaround.
