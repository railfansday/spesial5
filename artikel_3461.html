The UK and some other countries don’t get more Siri AI usage with iCloud+

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Apple this week revealed the additional Siri AI usage you’ll get if you have an iCloud+ subscription. This ranges from doubled limits for a 200GB plan to 20 times for a 12TB plan.

However, the company’s support document reveals that some countries will not get access to these increased usage allowances …

Apple hasn’t yet specified the standard limits applicable to Siri AI usage, instead saying that these will vary depending on anything from the feature used to the overall level of demand at the time. However, it did list the proportional increases you get with paid iCloud plans.

However, tucked away in the small print in the company’s support document are some exclusions. One of them is no surprise: EU countries.

Siri AI isn’t available at all in the EU due to Apple’s perception of potential antitrust issues. Essentially, Apple believes that if it launches Siri AI there, it will be required to give competing AI services the same level of access to its devices. But three other countries are specifically excluded:

Apple gives no explanation, simply saying:

Increased access to Siri AI with iCloud+ is subject to Siri AI availability and is not available in Brazil, the EU, UK, or South Korea.

However, I have a strong suspicion about the reason.

What all three countries have in common is that they’ve taken a strong antitrust stance with Apple on a number of occasions over a range of issues.

It may be that Apple has genuine concerns about how regulators might respond if they effectively throw in free access to increased Siri AI usage. The company may fear that this would be viewed as anti-competitive by implicitly disadvantaging other AI services.

A less charitable take would be that Apple is firing a warning shot across the bows of these countries, seeking to communicate that there are consequences for what it views as attacks on its ecosystem. If customers are angered by missing out, Apple might be hoping that these feelings would be directed at regulators rather than the iPhone maker.
