St Mirren boss McLeish claims 'self-interest' is hampering Scottish football development

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Published: 09:55 EST, 20 November 2026 | Updated: 09:55 EST, 20 November 2026

St Mirren manager Craig McLeish claims self-interest and poor governance are killing Scottish football’s hopes of improvement.

McLeish is frustrated that Scottish Football Association chief football officer Craig Mulholland has reportedly had to dilute plans for young player quotas to appease five William Hill Premiership clubs.

Mulholland was hoping to ensure teams featured Scotland Under-21 eligible players for an average of 60 minutes per league game and had two on the pitch for each cup tie.

McLeish said: 'That’s damning that he’s having to change his plans to pander to clubs who aren’t really on board with it and don’t really have the best interests of the Scottish national team at heart.

'I understand why. We have owners from outwith this country. We have managers from outwith this country. They’re in jobs, they’re all fighting for their clubs.

'But why then are those people allowed to be the ones that hold the keys to the future of our country and the development of our national team?

St Mirren boss Craig McLeish believes self-interest at clubs is hampering Scottish football

'It’s the way governance is set up and we’ve killed ourselves with that because the people who actually have a say in it have, not have no interest but they’ve got self-interest, and there’s a lot of self-interest at the top end.

'I just don’t see a way that we can really properly change unless something to do with governance changes and I just don’t ever see that happening. It’s really sad.'

Although McLeish backs quotas, he fears that would not address the root cause of Scottish football’s problems.

'We always focus at the top end, what our league size is, quotas for young players, a national base,' said the 36-year-old, who worked in St Mirren’s youth academy before his current role.

'We have a fundamental problem that starts at our youngest age groups, at four and five years of age, about how we actually teach kids to play. And until you fix that, you can’t fix the top end of it.

'It’s crazy some of the stuff that’s going on, the formats, how we teach kids. There’s just a massive lack of education and expertise at the very bottom of our pathway.

'In this country, football’s everyone’s sport and everyone gets access to it but if you take your young kids along to swimming or gymnastics or whatever other sport, you’re there with properly qualified coaches, teachers who have maybe done things at the top levels of that sport.

'We have nothing without the volunteers that work at the bottom end of the grassroots game, but they’re not teaching kids how to play football properly, in my mind.

'And you’re just hoping that they get put with a volunteer coach that can teach them something, that they get in with a really good group at the youngest ages.

'Because that development between the ages of three and eight, if you don’t get that right, you’re dead. You can’t do anything after that point, and we don’t do it well enough. And you’re just chasing yourself and we’ll never catch up.'
