Binance Says Withdrawals Reopened on 87 Networks as Recovery Moves Toward Full Access

Binance’s recovery has reached another practical milestone: the exchange says it reopened withdrawals across 87 networks during the week covered by its November 14 update. For customers waiting to move assets, that is more immediately useful than a general assurance about reserves.

The company notice targets reopening the remaining networks and assets by the end of the following week. It is a target for the restoration programme, rather than confirmation that every supported route is already operating.

The update names networks including Cardano, Avalanche, Stellar, Litecoin and NEAR. Binance asks customers to confirm the particular asset and network in its withdrawal interface, and warns that the rollout can involve processing delays.

The distinction between an asset and a network is operationally important. A token can be supported on more than one blockchain, while a customer’s receiving service may accept only a particular version. A broad announcement therefore cannot replace confirmation of the actual route at the time of transfer.

This extends the phased process described in NewsBTC’s report on the November 3 restart. The latest notice adds a larger disclosed set of restored networks and a target for the remaining work.

In a separate coverage announcement, Binance said it had completed replacement of affected assets after committing more than $6.34 billion of company resources. It described funding from SAFU, part of its futures insurance fund and its treasury.

The same disclosure said a November 12 reserve snapshot covered 198 tokens and that Binance intended to replenish SAFU to about $1 billion. These are company statements about funding and backing, with their own verification requirements.

They answer a different question from whether a customer can withdraw immediately. Restoring a balance, rebuilding an emergency reserve and enabling reliable transfers can progress at different speeds. Combining them into a single recovery percentage would hide the remaining operational work.

Binance’s proof-of-reserves explanation describes cryptographic checks that let customers confirm inclusion of their balances in a liabilities snapshot. Its system uses Merkle trees and zero-knowledge proofs to assess the relationship between included account data and the reported total.

That can provide useful evidence about the balances covered by a particular assessment. It does not, by itself, demonstrate that every security weakness has been removed or that every withdrawal request will process successfully.

NewsBTC recently examined the scope and limits of Bybit’s reserve reporting. The general lesson applies here too: readers need to know the date, assets and liabilities covered before deciding what a financial verification establishes.

A restoration programme becomes more convincing when published notices match the experience of customers using the affected services. That calls for clear lists of outstanding routes, updated explanations for delays and evidence that reopened services remain dependable.

The economic consequences can vary even among customers holding the same asset. Someone moving funds to a long-term wallet and a market maker relying on frequent transfers have different timing needs. Recovery quality includes predictable processing as well as the eventual return of a withdrawal button.

Binance has now supplied a concrete network count and a completion goal. The remaining question is whether the exchange can meet that goal and explain any exceptions clearly. Asset replacement and cryptographic reporting support that assessment, while actual access remains a separate result to observe.
