Elderly Florida man leads police on wild 16 mph e-bike chase. Thank goodness they caught him

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Residents of New Smyrna Beach, Florida, can finally breathe a sigh of relief. A dangerous menace to society has been taken off the streets after police apprehended a 75-year-old man accused of leading deputies on a high-stakes electric bike chase at speeds reaching a blistering 16 mph (26 km/h).

According to the Volusia Sheriff’s Office, 75-year-old Robert Joseph Mitchell was riding his electric bicycle along the beach on November 13 when a sheriff’s deputy clocked him traveling 16 mph in a 10 mph zone (26 km/h in a 16 km/h zone).

The deputy attempted to stop Mitchell, who allegedly responded with a middle finger and some choice words before continuing on his way.

What followed was apparently the world’s slowest police chase.

Mitchell reportedly continued riding along the beach, eventually exiting onto city streets, where he allegedly rode against traffic before making it back to his home. Deputies followed him, and when Mitchell attempted to retreat into his garage, he reportedly resisted arrest by grabbing onto a golf cart and refusing to let go.

The ordeal ended with Mitchell in custody, facing four charges, including felony fleeing or attempting to elude law enforcement, resisting an officer without violence, speeding on the beach, and riding outside the designated beach driving area. His bond was set at $4,000.

And just like that, another dangerous electric bicycle criminal was brought to justice. This was clearly a success story like no other, and American streets are now safer.

Now, before anyone gets too worked up, let’s be clear: I’m not suggesting that e-bike riders should be allowed to ignore traffic laws, endanger pedestrians, or flee from police. Reports indicate that Mitchell was riding recklessly around beachgoers, including children, and that’s certainly something worth addressing. And once he allegedly decided to flee from deputies, he turned what could have been a minor infraction into a much more serious situation.

But can we also acknowledge how absurd this whole thing is?

We’ve reached a point where a 75-year-old man riding a bicycle at 16 mph can end up the subject of a police pursuit and felony charges, while we’ve largely accepted that multi-ton vehicles routinely kill and seriously injure people on our roads as an unavoidable cost of modern transportation.

Every day, drivers speed through neighborhoods, roll through stop signs, stare at their phones, and operate vehicles capable of killing pedestrians with a moment’s inattention. Or perhaps I shouldn’t say ‘capable’, but rather that they do. 40,000 people every year die in the US from car violence. Yet somehow, electric bicycles have become one of the great transportation menaces of our time, despite contributing much, much less to major bodily harm or death.

And yes, there are absolutely legitimate concerns about dangerous riding. I’ve repeatedly argued that we need better enforcement, especially when it comes to teenagers riding high-powered electric dirt bikes and e-motos at 40 mph (64 km/h) down sidewalks and bike paths. Those aren’t electric bicycles, and treating them as such has created a real problem for everyone.

But if we’re going to devote police resources to cracking down on dangerous two-wheeled behavior, perhaps we could start there instead of turning a 1 mph beach ride into the crime of the century.

What is absurd is that he didn't simply stop and follow the officer's directions to slow down. If he is going to try to flee from the police by riding dangerously including going against traffic then he should face charges. As presumably a first time offender, he'll be given probation if he agrees not to do it again.

To be fair, Mitchell’s alleged behavior after being stopped certainly didn’t help his case. But the larger point remains: our priorities around road safety have become wildly distorted.

We’ve normalized the dangers of cars so thoroughly that we barely blink at the daily carnage they cause, yet we’re increasingly treating comparatively low-speed electric bicycles as an existential threat to public safety.

I’m all for enforcing sensible e-bike rules. I’m all for throwing the book at major infractions and scofflaws who pose a public menace. But I’m also for applying a little perspective.

Because if a 75-year-old man riding an electric bicycle at 16 mph is among our most pressing transportation safety concerns, then I have some very good news about the state of our roads. But unfortunately, there is no good news to share today.
