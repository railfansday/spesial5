Every Pokémon Gimmick, Ranked

Over the years, we have seen many different Pokémon gimmicks pop up. It seems like every generation of Pokémon needs to have its own gimmick to make it stand out from the others.

It has come to a point where many fans of the series are tired of seeing new gimmicks pop up, and they would rather Pokémon go back to its roots with simple turn-based battles.

Unfortunately, that is never going to be the case, so you must accept whatever gimmick is in each game. Some of these gimmicks aren’t bad, but other ones are rather questionable. Let’s compare each of these gimmicks and see how they stack up against each other.

After introducing Mega Evolutions to Pokémon, Game Freak needed something to top it. Unfortunately, the gimmick in the next games, Sun and Moon, ended up being what is considered the worst gimmick in the Pokémon series.

The gimmick in question is Z-Moves. Z-Moves allow your Pokémon to use a massively powerful move, provided they are holding the appropriate Z-Crystal. Many have said that requiring an item to use one all-powerful move is a bit lame.

These all-powerful moves are often viewed as the one-hit option, allowing you to clear battles easily without much strategy being involved. The gimmick often felt unbalanced, and the addition of annoying, unskippable cutscenes, made using the moves extremely annoying.

I am someone who absolutely loves the Paldea region. I think that it is a beautiful area, and the idea that battles are celebrated in massive arenas is actually really cool to me. However, this generation of Pokémon games has one of the worst gimmicks, and that is Dynamaxing.

Dynamaxing allows a Pokémon to grow massive, amplifying its power in the process, and giving it access to different moves. In theory, it sounds decent. However, it is a nightmare for competitive play, as it makes already extremely powerful Pokémon even more powerful instead of lifting up and helping weaker Pokémon.

In addition to this, the moves given to the Pokémon are not unique, as they become different moves based on the previous move’s type, so a flying-type move will always become the same move when Dynamaxed. There are even more issues with it, but it all boils down to the gimmick feeling like not much thought went into it, as it is rather unbalanced and makes battles a bit of a slog.

If you were around for the Ruby and Sapphire days of Pokémon, then you remember double battles. These changed the way that Pokémon battles were played, and then they were expanded on in a rather weird way when triple Battles and rotational Battles appeared in Pokémon Black and White.

These battle types have rarely appeared since their inception, and it is because they weren’t very popular at the time. Triple battles are simple, involving three Pokémon being on the field at once instead of two. This is a bit unnecessary, as double battles were already fine.

Additionally, rotational battles were annoying because you had the ability to rotate your Pokémon around whenever you wanted, making some moves completely obsolete. However, it did make things interesting because the mechanic of rotating your Pokémon was different from switching them in and out of battle.

Terastallization is a gimmick that has the fanbase rather mixed, as there are many who enjoy the mechanic, and then there are those who absolutely hate it. As far as things go, it is a rather middle-of-the-road gimmick that could have been better, but it isn’t that bad.

This gimmick, which was introduced in Pokémon Scarlet and Violet, allows you to crystalize your Pokémon, changing its typing in the process. Many enjoy the mechanic because it lets you change the Pokémon’s typing, making them more resistant to moves they were once weak against.

The argument against Terastallization is that it can be unbalanced in certain battle formats, but it is mostly a balanced mechanic that works well with the series. The other argument is that every Pokémon looks absolutely terrible when it Terastalizes, and to be honest, they aren’t wrong.

As stated earlier in the article, double battles were introduced in Pokémon Ruby and Sapphire, and they have remained to this day. It is one of the few earlier gameplay gimmicks that people sometimes forget started as a gimmick because it was so beloved.

Never before had players thrown two Pokémon into battle at once, and it felt like a natural progression of the battle system, allowing players to have two Pokémon whose types complimented each other.

Plus, this generation introduced new moves that were meant to be used during double battles. This gimmick has not changed since its inception, and it is because it feels natural for the series. There is nothing that needs to be changed about it, which makes it one of the best Game Freak has added to the series.

Why is it that I always forget that Mega Evolution is something that started in Pokémon X and Y? I always think that it was the gimmick from Pokémon Black and White. Tangent aside, Mega Evolution has become the most popular gimmick that Game Freak has added to the games.

It is so popular that people have continuously asked for more and more Pokémon to receive Mega Evolutions because the transformations are not only cool, but they are meaningful.

When Mega Evolution happens, it can sometimes change a Pokémon’s type, it can change the Pokémon’s ability, and it boosts the Pokémon’s stats tremendously. This can change the tide of a battle, but it isn’t so powerful that it is the ultimate trump card. It doesn’t allow you to blow away the opposition, but rather gives you more of a fighting chance and can dramatically help in certain situations. To this day, there are still Mega Evolutions being created, and it has been the focus in some competitive games as well.

I've missed Pokemon's Mega Evolution mechanic, and it's going to be a huge draw for Pokemon Legends: Z-A players.
