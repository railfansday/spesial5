If your kid is ready to take to the not-so-open road, here’s what to look for in a kids’ electric car.

While it can be tempting to get a ride-on that your child will grow into and use for years, there are safety risks involved with choosing one that’s too large or powerful for your kid to control easily. Check the manufacturer’s recommendations for age and weight. Some even offer parental remote controls and seatbelts, while others let you remove features like side panels as your child grows.

Electric ride-ons come in multiple styles to match kids’ varying interests. Classic cars and trucks mimic real-life vehicles, offering features like working headlights and sound effects for a realistic experience. ATVs and UTVs are built for rougher terrain with “all-terrain” tires and higher clearances. For kids who love motorcycles, bike-style ride-ons can often include training wheels for added stability.

Most electric ride-ons are powered by rechargeable batteries, typically ranging from six to 24 volts. Higher voltage generally translates to more power and a longer run time, but it’s important to match the power to the terrain and age level.

Models with 12-volt batteries provide a good balance of speed and battery life for mixed-terrain use, while 24-volt versions can handle tougher trails and inclines. Be mindful that all batteries will gradually lose capacity, so check for compatible replacement options if you plan to use the ride-on for years to come.

Interactive features can make a big difference in the overall experience. Some ride-ons include Bluetooth speakers or FM radios, letting kids listen to their favorite songs while driving. Others offer realistic details such as functional doors, working headlights and taillights, and engine sound effects.

Many models are designed for children ages three and up, but age recommendations vary. Check the manufacturer’s guidance, along with the car’s weight limit and seat size, to make sure it fits your child.

They can be fun with adult supervision and age-appropriate use. Look for features such as a seatbelt, a slow-start function, speed settings, and a parent remote. Have kids ride in a safe, open area away from traffic, steep slopes, and obstacles.

Speed varies by model. Some top out around 2 or 3 mph, while more powerful options go faster. Lower speed settings can help younger or first-time drivers get comfortable.

Runtime depends on the model, battery, speed, terrain, and rider’s weight. A manufacturer’s estimate is a useful guide, but driving on grass or hills and using higher speeds can drain the battery sooner.

Some models have rugged tires and suspension for uneven ground, while others are best suited to pavement or smooth surfaces. Check the manufacturer’s recommendations before heading off-road, and avoid wet or steep terrain.

Start with your child’s age, height, and weight, then consider speed, battery life, terrain, and whether you want a parent remote. Features such as working lights, sounds, and Bluetooth can add fun, but fit and ease of use matter most.
