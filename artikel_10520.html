How Verizon keeps its finger on the pulse of culture, starting with ‘Heated Rivalry’ and ‘Love Island’ stars

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

In a five-minute short film released in March 2026, Heated Rivalry star Connor Storrie spends a night in a remote cabin in the woods. First, his smart speakers turn on by themselves. Then his alarm system goes off. And then his smart deadbolt unlocks itself. He tentatively opens the door to find . . . a perfectly normal rideshare driver, who sees the knife he’s holding, turns, and runs.

When Storrie pulls his phone from his back pocket, he realizes that he was being terrorized by . . . his butt, which had been opening various apps and pressing buttons every time he bent or sat down (helped, of course, by Verizon’s strong connection, even at his cabin. “There’s no escape,” the tagline says).

The cinematic spot, directed by Nia DaCosta (28 Years Later: The Bone Temple), went gangbusters—partially from the high production values and horror-movie pacing, and partially from Storrie’s ability at the moment to turn anything he touches into internet gold. It’s racked up nearly 80 million views, with 91% positive sentiment on social media, according to Verizon.

Verizon spots have featured zeitgeisty famous people for years (including Keke Palmer and Lindsay Lohan), but recently the company’s marketing has “become very fan-centric and fan-obsessed,” says chief marketing officer Leslie Berland, an American Express and Twitter veteran who assumed the role in early 2024.

As Verizon developed campaigns around its Simplicity plan and revamped its Verizon Shine loyalty program—which includes giveaways to events like the World Cup and its monthly loyalty cashback offering Verizon Dollars—it has moved quickly to partner with other timely celebrities. Love Island USA winner Trinity Tatum, whose season ended in July, appeared in Verizon’s August ads for Verizon Dollars, and Stephen Kalyn, who starred in the (heterosexual) hockey romance Off Campus, which debuted in May, appeared in ads that ran in June.   

“We are fast, and we’re watching intently,” Berland says. “We don’t plan a campaign a year, even six months out.”
