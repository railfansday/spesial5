Sad but true: Many of our sexual rights have been eroded in the last 10 years. School sex education is the least accurate and most harmful it’s been in decades. Restrictions on adult entertainment and reproductive rights, as well as the coercion of physicians' and psychologists' speech, are getting increasingly onerous.

That said, let’s be thankful for the sexual freedoms, technologies, and options we do have right now. Here are some that many people don’t take full advantage of—and I encourage you to do so.

The morning-after pill prevents pregnancy by preventing or delaying ovulation. If you don’t ovulate, you can’t get pregnant.

The morning-after pill will not end a pregnancy that has already begun. No opinion, no matter how passionate, can accurately contradict this fact: Emergency contraception is not an abortion, and cannot create an abortion.

There are now a dozen brands of emergency contraception available over the counter at drugstores and chains in virtually every city in America. With brand names like Plan B, My Choice, and Aftera, this product is a modern miracle, effective as much as 98 percent of the time if taken soon enough.

Why oh why oh why doesn’t every fertile, sexually active American have this product in their medicine chest? It’s inexpensive and lasts for five years; after that, celebrate that you didn’t need it, and replace it with a fresh package.

After 44 years as a sex therapist, very little surprises me. But I’m still amazed at the number of people who don’t use lube when they masturbate or have intercourse.

Some women are insulted when I suggest lube for intercourse. “I get plenty wet,” they protest indignantly. And when I suggest lube to men who have trouble entering a vagina, or whose erections are wobbly when they’re alone or with a partner, they respond as if I’ve accused them of being inept.

“Stop everything and reach for lube, right,” some patient will periodically sneer, as if I’m suggesting they reach for a diesel generator.

But we don’t use lube to solve a problem or address a failure. We use lube to make sex more enjoyable, with or without a partner. If you keep it handy where you usually have partner or solo sex, reaching for it can be simple and nonintrusive.

Lube is better than spit (which quickly disappears when absorbed), and better than olive oil or any other oil (which are difficult to get out of a vagina or anus when you’re done).

Every drugstore carries lube, typically near the condoms or tampons. Amazon carries 100 brands, which suggests just how common this product is. The easiest kind to use (and mandatory with condoms) are the water-soluble brands. A tip for condom users—put two drops inside the condom to increase heat transfer and make it stick to the penis better.

Pelvic floor therapy (PFPT) is a type of physical therapy that helps rehabilitate the muscles in the pelvic floor.

Women and men are typically referred to PFPT for painful sex or orgasm, postpartum issues, pelvic organ prolapse, hemorrhoids, and incontinence. In addition to treating chronically tight muscles, the therapist can teach exercises to strengthen and relax pelvic floor muscles.

PFPT is one of those treatment programs after which people say, “I feel so much better—and didn’t even realize how tight and sore I actually was until we fixed it.” If sex or orgasm hurt, or you have issues that your physician can’t explain or resolve, get a referral to one of these fabulous practitioners.

The more we know about estrogen replacement for middle-aged women, the better it looks.

And with 2,000,000 women entering menopause every year, the late 40s (a year or two before menopause often starts) is a special window when many women can use estrogen to protect themselves against health problems including dementia, heart disease, and osteoporosis.

But isn’t estrogen dangerous? Doesn’t it cause breast cancer? In a word, no.

That myth became widespread overnight in 2002 from methodological mistakes in a single report. Subsequent studies around the world have convincingly demolished this myth—but because “no actual problem” never gets the headlines that “wow—huge problem!” gets, millions of women and their doctors still believe that estrogen is dangerous. There’s also a role for testosterone supplements in women’s health.

The best books on this subject are Estrogen Matters by Bluming & Tavris and The Menopause Moment by Kelly Casperson. Each is fascinating, uplifting, and totally useful.

The sex therapy field was formalized by Masters & Johnson some 60 years ago. Usually offered by a psychologist or marriage and family therapist, sex therapy is sometimes offered by a nurse practitioner, social worker, or psychiatrist.

Sex therapists treat problems with erection, orgasm, and desire, as well as guilt, shame, anxiety, and curiosity. We also help individuals and couples think and talk about infidelity, nonmonogamy, kink, sexual orientation, pornography, and past trauma.

There are several thousand experienced sex therapists in the United States—at least a few in every medium and large city. And now with Zoom sessions, everyone can meet with a sex therapist if they want to.

So why don’t more people take advantage of these marvelous interventions?

So on Thanksgiving Day, give thanks that we in America have many sexual rights and access to many sex-related products. If appropriate, do consider pursuing one or more of them.
