The Rivian Commercial Van (RCV) should be familiar to anyone with an Amazon Prime account. Those boxy blue behemoths that pause outside your house and annoy your dogs come from EV specialist Rivian, and as of 2025, other fleet-owning businesses can outfit them for their own cargo-transport needs as well.

Two configurations of this front-wheel-drive electric van are available: The Delivery 500 is 248.5 inches long, while the Delivery 700 stretches to 278 inches. (For reference, a Chevrolet Suburban is about 226 inches long.) That makes for a sizable cargo hold, though Rivian limits maximum payload to 2663 pounds, which is below average for an electric cargo van.

The RCV's range is decent (between 153 and 161 miles), and its design is distinctive, from the lens-like headlights and wraparound windshield in front to the roof-lining taillights and roll-up door in the rear. We can see many startups wanting to wrap the RCV's slab-sided body with their logos.

Deliveries of non-Amazon-branded RCVs began in April 2025. The vans are likely to see few if any changes for 2026.

If this year's model is largely unchanged and you're comfortable with a lightly used car, you may want to consider shopping previous model years. A one-to-three-year-old model will likely have a warranty and it'll cost less than buying new.

Both versions of the Commercial Van are powered by a 320-hp electric motor that drives the front wheels. All-wheel drive is not an option, although it wouldn't be difficult for Rivian to strap a second motor to the rear axle and make that possible.

We haven't driven either van yet, but given how quickly Amazon drivers move about, we suspect the electric motor provides adequate acceleration, even when the back is fully loaded with cargo.

Though not intended for towing, the Delivery 500 can haul a decent bit of gear, with a payload of 2663 pounds and a cargo capacity of 487 cubic feet. The longer-wheelbase Delivery 700 offers 652 cubic feet of space but a lower payload rating of 2258 pounds.

Rivian claims the Delivery 500 can go 161 miles on a charge, while the longer Delivery 700 has a maximum range of 153 miles. Both models use a lithium-iron-phosphate battery pack, capable of 100-kW fast-charging, and the vans have both J1772 and CCS ports.

The EPA doesn't publish fuel-economy numbers for commercial vehicles, and Rivian hasn't released estimates. If we get a chance, we'll take the RCV on our 75-mph highway fuel-economy route and update this story with real-world test results.

First Row: Like the exterior, the RCV's interior is simple yet modern. Behind the sliding front door is an elevated driver's chair and a chunky plastic dash that supports a flat-bottom steering wheel and two large screens—one for instruments and the other for infotainment. There's also a fold-down jump seat for the occasional passenger. The RV-like wraparound windshield provides good forward visibility, and a standard 360-degree camera system ensures the driver can see what's behind them.

Cargo Capacity: Behind the roll-up rear door is an empty cavity that fleet owners can spec with shelves and various flooring options. The Delivery 500 van offers 487 cubic feet of cargo volume, matching that of the Ford E-Transit and coming in one cube short of the high-roof, long-wheelbase Mercedes-Benz eSprinter’s volume. The Delivery 700 bests all comers with its 652-cubic-foot hold.

On the dashboard are two large display screens, one serving as a digital gauge cluster and the other as an infotainment touchscreen. The software interface is essentially the same thing you'll find in the Rivian R1T and R1S models. A 360-degree camera system displays images on the central screen to aid in maneuvering.

Rivian equips the Commercial Van with a host of driver-assistance features, including lane-keeping assist, automatic high-beam headlamps, blind-spot monitoring, and adaptive cruise control.

For information about the RCV's crash-test results, visit the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) and Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS) websites.

The Commercial Van receives less favorable warranty terms than Rivian’s other vehicles.
