Klarna tops $1bn in revenue for a fourth quarter, still loses $65m

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

US spending through Klarna rose 24%, led by its Fair Financing loans. The Swedish company kept its full-year outlook and named a new CFO from Robinhood.

Klarna grew revenue 12% to $1.009bn in the third quarter of 2026, its fourth quarter in a row above $1bn. Adjusted operating income reached $21m, up from a $14m loss a year ago, the Swedish payments company said on Tuesday.

It still made a net loss of $65m, compared with a $95m loss in the same quarter of 2025. The operating loss was $53m. Klarna put this down to share-based payments, which rose 38% to $54m in its annual pay cycle, according to its earnings release.

“Q3 was our fourth straight quarter with revenue above 1 billion dollars and positive adjusted operating income. We are keeping more of each dollar we process through our everyday finance network,” CEO Sebastian Siemiatkowski said.

Shoppers spent $36.1bn through Klarna in the quarter, up 10%. In the US, spending rose 24% to $8.2bn, and the US now makes up 23% of the total.

Most of that US growth came from Fair Financing, Klarna’s longer-term loans for bigger purchases. Globally, Fair Financing grew 61% to $5.2bn. Pay Later, its interest-free split payments, still accounts for about three quarters of volume.

Klarna now has 122 million active users and 1.35 million merchants, up 59%. Klarna credits payment processors that switch it on by default, such as Fiserv’s Clover, which went live at 100,000 US stores in October. The Klarna Card more than doubled to $4.5bn in volume, and subscription revenue rose more than 500% to $43m.

Klarna also said Apple Upgrade is gaining traction. Apple launched the Klarna-backed device leasing programme in July.

Transaction margin, the revenue left after processing, credit and funding costs, rose 32% to $371m. Provisions for bad loans fell to 0.56% of volume, from 0.72%. Customer deposits of $12.7bn made up 88% of its funding. In June, it launched US savings accounts.

Some credit measures moved the other way. In the US, Fair Financing loans more than 30 days overdue rose to 3.7%, from 3.5% a year ago. Klarna said it expected the rise, which reflects financing on the Klarna Card. Adjusted general and administrative costs rose 43%.

Klarna kept its full-year outlook: $4.08bn to $4.16bn in revenue, and $280m to $300m in adjusted operating income. It expects a net profit in the fourth quarter.

Klarna has also changed its leadership this month. On 12 November, it named Chris Koegel of Robinhood as chief financial officer from January, replacing Niclas Neglén. On Monday, it hired Brandon Gracey from Shopify to run North America.

Klarna listed in New York at $40 a share. By April, its shares were 76% below their high. In July, it applied for a US banking licence.
