two luminous eggs nestle inside hand-woven metal frame on old shipyard mast in estonia

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

inimruum and un-like repurpose a former shipyard lighting mast for nest 2, pairing a hand-woven angle-iron framework with two internally illuminated rotomolded polyethylene eggs.

T.Triibmann edited by christina vergopoulou November 15, 2026 3 min

Installed in Tallinn’s Noblessner district, a former pre-World War I submarine shipyard redeveloped into a mixed-use seafront neighborhood, Nest 2 is a site-specific light installation by Inimruum and lighting brand un-like, commissioned by real estate developer Merko. Continuing a public art series initiated with the 2024 Nest installation on Kalaranna Street (inspired by a five-year-old’s drawing of a stork nest), the new intervention repurposes a decommissioned industrial lighting mast on Lennusadama Street. Elevated approximately 20 meters above grade, the mast stands between the Ellingu Quarter waterfront park around the historic slipway and Taru, the upcoming timber nature house set to house the Estonian Museum of Natural History starting in 2027.

Measuring over 2.5 meters in diameter, the sculptural structure by lighting brand un-like and spatial design studio Inimruum adopts a denser, wind-weathered geometry than its predecessor, which utilized curved roof-edge trim. Hand-woven by a metal artist from structural angle iron, the dark latticework echoes the steel profile of the existing mast legs below. Nested inside the framework are two rotomolded polyethylene eggs, cast from the original 2024 mold and hoisted into position by crane, with one volume tilted steeply on its side. During daylight hours, the dark metal branches create a linear silhouette against the coastal sky; at night, integrated micro-luminaires trace the perimeter of the angle-iron weave while individually controlled internal LEDs illuminate the translucent eggs with programmable color shifts, preserving the historic shipyard infrastructure as a vertical landmark.
