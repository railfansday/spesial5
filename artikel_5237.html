HSBC lets Hong Kong customers buy a money-market fund with HK$100 in RedCoin

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Eligible bank-app customers can buy tokenized Class SD units. The fund’s 11 a.m. Hong Kong cutoff determines which dealing day prices a redemption.

HSBC’s Hong Kong banking app now lets eligible customers buy into its Hong Kong dollar money-market fund from HK$100 using RedCoin, the bank’s HKD-denominated stablecoin. The fund’s 11 a.m. Hong Kong dealing cutoff still determines which dealing day’s valuation applies when investors redeem.

The South China Morning Post reported on Nov. 19 that HSBC was opening RedCoin subscriptions that day. HSBC Asset Management dates the public launch of the tokenized Class SD to Nov. 18. HSBC had previewed fund subscriptions in its banking app when it rolled out RedCoin for PayMe payments in October.

The fund is available in the Wealth tab of the HSBC HK App. HSBC advertises a minimum purchase of HK$100, payable with 100 RedCoin. Its app guide says the process combines payment, order confirmation and unit issuance, which HSBC says can speed settlement.

Getting a RedCoin wallet in the app requires customers to be at least 18 and to have a sole-name integrated account, a valid Hong Kong ID on record, a local residential address and current contact details. They must be in Hong Kong to activate the wallet. Customers with RedCoin in PayMe need to fund the banking app wallet separately under the RedCoin terms.

The tokenized-class key facts list a standard minimum of HK$10,000 for both initial and subsequent purchases of Class SD and Class T. The fund manager can accept smaller amounts under the prospectus. The lower figure applies to the app route.

The fund’s cutoff is 11 a.m. Hong Kong time on dealing days for tokenized and conventional units. The prospectus says the transfer agent must receive a complete tokenized redemption request through the distributor by then for that dealing day’s net asset value to apply. Distributors may set earlier deadlines. Requests that miss the fund’s cutoff roll to the next dealing day.

Class SD holders redeem through their distributor. The prospectus provides for the fund to pay HKD into the distributor’s or its nominee’s settlement account within seven business days after the relevant dealing day, once the paperwork is complete. It sets a 28-day maximum from receipt of a properly documented request to fund payment. The distributor handles the onward payment. HSBC’s public app guide leaves the timing, destination account and currency of the customer’s credit unspecified.

Tokenized and conventional classes share the same portfolio of short-term deposits and other money-market instruments. Class-specific fees can produce different unit prices, and the fund can lose value. HSBC reinvests any dividends it declares for Class SD in more units. Its conventional Classes A, B, C and D make no distributions. HSBC has generally closed Class A to new investors, while existing holders can still add to their holdings.

The payment and the investment also use separate records. RedCoin moves on HSBC’s own distributed ledger. The prospectus identifies Ethereum as the initial blockchain for the tokenized units and limits transactions to approved wallets. The registrar keeps the official ownership record off-chain, and the distributor holds the units as nominee for the customer.

HSBC’s tokenized units are offered through subscriptions and redemptions with the fund. The terms exclude peer-to-peer transfers and secondary trading. On Oct. 26, Hong Kong’s Securities and Futures Commission said round-the-clock secondary trading had begun for two other funds, ChinaAMC’s HKD and USD Digital Money Market Funds, on a licensed virtual asset platform.

A ChinaAMC holder can look for a buyer on the venue around the clock. A Class SD holder submits a redemption through a distributor and receives the price for the applicable dealing day.
