These are the 3 best headphones under $300 I tested in 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The best headphones shouldn't be the ones that bring on credit card debt. As a professional headphone reviewer, I'm partial to a pair of well-executed flagship headphones, but I don't always find the $450 price point practical for most folks — especially when you can often find headphones for literally hundreds of dollars cheaper that perform 80 percent as well.

These are the tech, tools, and products — from laptops to e-readers, from earbuds to robovacs, and more — that Mashable ranks best in class.

Especially as we head into the holiday season and Black Friday deals heat up, it can be tempting to grab the $450 flagship headphones on sale. And yes, I always recommend squeezing all the value you can, but great sales also mean mid-range headphones that are already under the $300 mark at full price are not only likely to drop to $200 or less — they already have. With that in mind, I rounded up my three favorite pairs of headphones under $300 that I tested in 2026. I also included one bonus option in honor of the holiday sale season.

Check out our full review of the Sony XM4C headphones.

Anyone looking for the best blend of sound, comfort, and price: Sony brought back its viral XM4 headphones with the second-generation XM4Cs. The design remains unchanged from the first generation, but sound gets a boost, making them the best-sounding headphones under $300 I've tried, and a no-brainer for students, office workers, or anyone looking to get as close as possible to the flagship headphone experience, minus the price.

Plenty of $450 headphones don't feature 10-band equalizers. Sony's WH-1000XM4C headphones do. That said, even if you don't like to mess around with a companion app, these headphones sound balanced and rich out of the box, making everything from classical piano concertos to classic rock shine. While their sound quality is certainly a huge draw, it's far from the only standout feature of these headphones.

Sony struck simplistic gold with the design of the XM4 headphones in 2020 — which is why some fans were sad to see the iconic look go in the XM5 and XM6 headphones. Now, prospective buyers don't have to decide between the latest tech and the coveted XM4 design. While Sony didn't add any improvements to the ANC (it still features the same four mics and QN1 processor it did in 2020), the internals of the sound quality make these a much easier (and easier to get) recommendation in 2026. Like the OG XM4s, these headphones also are relatively low profile, with plenty of cushioning in the headband and ear cups, minus any overbearing squeeze. The result is solid passive noise cancellation, with comfort enough to get you through a work or school day's worth of wear.

The biggest cons of these headphones are that they lack spatial audio and the battery life got bumped down three hours from the first to the second gen — 30 to 27 hours. However, the 27-hour range still sits above other (more expensive) mid-range options, so it's not the most devastating loss. And at full price, they're still less than half the price of Sony's other 2026 release: the ultra-premium 1000X the Collexion headphones.

Check out our full review of the Nothing Headphone (a).

People who like their headphones to make a statement: The Nothing Headphone (a) are going to turn some heads. The rectangular ear cup and semi-transparent design meld together the past and the future in a way that is unmistakably Nothing. Don't get me wrong, though; they don't just look good. These headphones pack a relatively balanced soundscape and decent ANC.

They're also the perfect headphones for anyone who isn't too great about plugging their devices in regularly, as they pack a whopping 75 hours of listening time per charge, with the ANC on.

These are the best headphones Nothing makes, thanks to their combo of performance and value, and the value prop here is strong. When brainstorming the best headphones under $300, the Headphone (a) immediately came to mind because they offer something most budget-oriented headphones don't (or can't, due to budget constraints): an unmistakable style. These headphones aren't trying to dupe any other one on the market.

Instead, Nothing uses the more plastic-heavy design to its advantage, securing a cool retro look, all the while offering four colorways: White, black, pink, and yellow. The power of the design doesn't stop there. The on-ear controls of these headphones are so intuitive, both in their function and placement, that I rarely reached for my device when wearing them.

Sound-wise, these aren't going to blow you away — there are likely better options for sound quality and ANC around this price point. But they get the job done, and likely won't make you regret making a decision based on aesthetics. They're a little fussy to carry around, but with headphones that look this good, I'm betting most folks will want to wear them more often than not.

On sale, these headphones have dropped as low as $109, giving them a truly impressive value prop.

Check out our full review of the Soundcore Space 2.

Bargain hunters: If you're looking for the cheapest possible option that will still offer a mid-range experience, the Soundcore Space 2 headphones are the way to go. With sound, comfort, and an overall experience that punches above its price point, I recommend these headphones to anyone looking to spend less than $200.

As nice as the XM4C headphones are, they're still an investment. These Soundcore headphones keep performance high on the core features of headphones, while showing their price point in places that don't detract too much from the overall user experience.

Let's start with how they wear. The memory foam cushions are plush, and the headphones themselves squeeze enough to offer some passive noise cancellation and stay secure, without skewing into headache-inducing territory. In other words, I could wear them for hours at a time without issue. The on-ear controls were great too, with buttons easy to find blindly and use for playback. The look isn't half bad either — at a glance, you could almost mistake them for a pair of Beats.

The sound and active noise cancellation did the job. On the former front, it came out balanced, if a bit unremarkable. Still, that's not a knock at this price point — they could sound a lot worse. ANC-wise, these feel like great daily drivers, but maybe not the satisfactory answer for anyone who has extra noisy neighbors, co-workers, or lives near a construction site. A plane ride, however? Yeah, these will do the job just fine.

My biggest warning on the sound front? Without audio playing, I could hear some minor squeaking while moving my head around, but not every single time. Not ideal, but certainly not a dealbreaker, either.

Check out our full review of the Bose QuietComfort headphones (2nd gen).

Frequent travelers, those who prioritize comfort, and anyone who happens to catch a sale: The thing about Bose is they're going to make an incredibly comfortable pair of headphones, and the second-generation QuietComforts are no exception. I wore these headphones through long work and travel days, and they felt comfortable the entire time I wore them — glasses, earrings, and all.

The only major catch? At full price, they don't fall into the under-$300 criteria. That said, I've seen them fall below that price point thanks to sales enough times that they at least deserve an honorable mention spot on this list.

Similar to the Sonys, these Bose headphones verge on the premium headphone experience. This is especially the case thanks to the balanced sound and presence of spatial audio, which narrows the gap between the $450 Bose QC Ultra headphones and these. The active noise cancellation is some of the best available, and while I dealt with some clipping on especially loud noises (think plane engines during takeoff or trains screeching up to the platform), for the most part these headphones give the cocoon of silence to expect from Bose.

I used them while flying up the west coast, and found them to be the perfect travel headphones. Though they're on the shorter side of battery life, it was still plenty to last me through my trip, and that was with heavy wear during the travel days — these headphones are comfortable enough to simply keep on. While better equalizer options would truly take these headphones over the top to more premium territory, the Bose QCs make up for it in other areas. The replaceable ear cups, for instance, mean that you can extend the life of your headphones once they start to wear down. And while the price point is on the higher side, grabbing them on sale makes them one of the best value finds in the headphones market, especially if you prioritize comfort and noise cancellation above all else.
