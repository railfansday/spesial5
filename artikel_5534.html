Landlord With Dark Past Is Charged With the Murder of Disabled Tenant Who Was ‘Late on Rent’

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A New Mexico landlord accused of murdering his disabled tenant and burying the body beneath the porch of the victim’s home gave conflicting accounts about whether he had evicted the man over purported unpaid rent, according to court documents.

Jonah Jeter, 33, was charged last week in Albuquerque with murder, aggravated burglary, and three counts of tampering with evidence in the death of Robert Debeary, 51, whose remains were discovered in October, more than two months after his family reported him missing.

For nearly five years, Debeary, a four-time stroke survivor, lived in an apartment at 233 Alcazar St. NE, where he received housing assistance through the federal Section 8 program, according to the criminal complaint filed in Bernalillo County Metropolitan Court.

Debeary, who walked with a cane after becoming paralyzed on one side of his body, regularly kept in touch with his loved ones, so when a week passed without hearing from him, his family grew concerned, according to his future daughter-in-law, Latajanae Evangelista, who spoke with Realtor.com®.

In mid-August, Debeary’s brother contacted Albuquerque Community Safety to request a welfare check, but was told that he had been evicted from the apartment the previous week. His family questioned that account, saying he had never mentioned facing eviction despite complaining of “ongoing issues” with Jeter.

“He was just a man, a disabled man living in his home, and he would never just go missing. He would never leave his family,” Evangelista tells Realtor.com in a phone interview, her voice breaking as she struggled to find the words. “And then he’s just found buried under his own porch where he lived, where he was supposed to be safe.”

Throughout the investigation, questions emerged about an apparent dispute between Debeary and his landlord. When confronted by the family, Jeter allegedly told them that he had evicted Debeary for failing to pay rent for several months, then later denied the claim, according to court documents.

The family later discovered that Jeter had previously served time in prison for a fatal stabbing in 2014, which left them questioning how he could be allowed to rent to a disabled tenant receiving federal housing assistance when he has a violent crime history.

Evangelista says she believes Debeary's death could have been prevented and wonders whether more stringent background checks on landlords might have protected him.

“So basically I can't be a tenant with an eviction, but I can be a landlord who is a murderer,” Evangelista notes. “It doesn't make any sense. All I know is that this was a tragedy—a tragedy that was 100% preventable.”

While Jeter claimed Debeary had fallen behind on rent, his family insisted the payments had been made—and questioned whether he had even been evicted.

Jeter told investigators that Debeary was late on his last two rent payments but admitted that he had never initiated formal eviction proceedings, according to the complaint.

There is no evidence that formal eviction proceedings were initiated, and authorities have not established whether the rent dispute played a role in Debeary's killing.

Evangelista and her fiancé, Micah Debeary, who is Debeary's son, both disputed the claim that the elder Debeary had stopped paying rent, explaining that he received Social Security benefits and rental assistance through a housing program. They claimed that they had reviewed bank records showing his monthly rent payments were being withdrawn.

“He was getting Social Security, and housing paid the rest, and he paid about $200, and the $200 came out every month,” Evangelista says. “So he was paying every month.”

Evangelista says the family contacted the Albuquerque Housing Authority and was told that no eviction proceedings had been initiated and that the agency had not received any reports of unpaid rent.

“They did not file any evictions, so he was not kicked out,” she says. “He was never evicted.”

She alleges that Jeter then moved his own relatives into Debeary's apartment roughly three weeks after he disappeared, while his family was still desperately searching for him.

“Three weeks after my father-in-law went, quote unquote, missing, his family moved in,” she claims, adding that she believes Jeter may have wanted the apartment for his relatives. Authorities, however, have not publicly identified a motive for the killing.

Meanwhile, investigators uncovered other inconsistencies in Jeter's account. When Debeary's family entered his apartment in August after not being able to reach him, they discovered a “reddish/brownish stain that they assumed was blood” on his bed, according to the complaint.

But when they returned to the property at a later date, the bed was gone. Jeter denied removing furniture or cleaning out the apartment and allegedly told detectives that Debeary did not have a bed at all, contradicting the family's account.

Debeary was last seen on surveillance footage leaving his apartment on Aug. 12. Over a month later, a tip about suspicious activity in the backyard of his rental home offered a grim new lead.

On Sept. 18, a neighbor called police and reported that, around the time Debeary disappeared, they had seen Jeter working in the backyard, “specifically digging up dirt and burying boards and other objects,” according to the complaint.

Investigators searched Jeter's car and discovered multiple firearms. He was arrested on Oct. 21 and charged with unlawful possession of a firearm by a felon.

Six days later, investigators returned to Debeary's home with a search warrant and cadaver dogs. The dogs alerted the team to the area beneath the porch, which had loose soil and what appeared to be newly installed turf, according to the complaint.

The area was excavated, and 2 feet below the surface, investigators discovered Debeary's body wrapped in a blanket and buried along with his bed frame and mattress. He had been shot at least once in the head, the autopsy revealed.

Jeter told police that he had done some landscaping in the backyard but denied burying Debeary’s body or the bed. He remains in custody at the Bernalillo County Metropolitan Detention Center. It was not immediately clear whether he had retained an attorney.

Jeter had previously served time in prison for another killing. In 2014, he was sentenced to five years in prison for fatally stabbing Oliver Yazzie at a truck stop when he was 17. He was released from probation in 2020.

It was Jeter's criminal past that has raised questions from Debeary's family about how he was even able to work as a landlord, particularly one renting to vulnerable tenants who rely on federal housing assistance.

It remains unclear what screening, if any, Jeter underwent before renting to Debeary or whether the local housing authority was aware of his criminal history.

Evangelista tells Realtor.com that she contacted the Albuquerque Housing Authority shortly after her father-in-law's disappearance to ask whether landlords were required to undergo criminal background checks.

“When Robert first went missing, I asked them, I said, ‘So, do you guys do background checks on your landlords? Because he's a convicted felon,’” she recalled. “And they were like, ‘No, we don't. We only do the background checks on the tenants.’”

She questioned why renters with past evictions struggle to find housing while landlords with violent criminal records may still be allowed to be part of housing assistance programs.

“Some people have been evicted because of hardships. They can't get an apartment. But someone can be a murderer, a rapist, a child molester, whatever, as long as they're a landlord, then somehow it's OK,” she says.

For Evangelista, the issue extends beyond the tragic fate of her future father-in-law.

“You're placing kids and mothers and elderly and people who are disabled in these homes, and that's dangerous," she says. “If I can help other people to change that, then I want to make it happen, because I would hate for this to keep happening—here in New Mexico, or anywhere else.”

Evangelista described Debeary as a devoted father and grandfather who had worked hard to regain his independence after suffering multiple strokes that left him partly paralyzed.

Despite needing a cane to walk, Debeary had planned to walk Evangelista down the aisle when she married his son, stepping into a role that held special meaning after her own father died in a car accident in 2022.

“All he wanted to do was live a normal life. That's all,” she says through her tears. “He wanted to be with his grandbabies. He wanted to spend all his time with his family.

“But I guess time wasn't on our side, unfortunately. I really wish it would have been.”
