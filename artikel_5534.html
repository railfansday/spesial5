Saudi Arabia told OPEC its oil output hit a wartime high in October

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Production rose from 8.372 million barrels a day in September as Brent futures fell about 21% so far in November to $93

Andrey Rudakov / Bloomberg via Getty Images

Saudi Arabia told OPEC it pumped 10.563 million barrels a day of crude in October, a rise from September's 8.372 million.

That was a 26% increase and the highest level since before the war began, according to Bloomberg. Saudi Arabia told OPEC it produced 10.882 million barrels a day in February, the final month before the Iran war upended Middle East supply, according to Reuters.

The Saudi rebound is spearheading a wider pickup in production across the region, which is easing prices, according to Bloomberg. Brent futures have dropped about 18% since the start of the month, leaving them at $93 a barrel.

Saudi Arabia's reported figure sits above its OPEC quota, according to Bloomberg. OPEC measures compliance with figures drawn from third-party estimates instead of the numbers members submit themselves. Those secondary sources put Saudi output at 9.194 million barrels a day in October, OPEC said.

OPEC+ producers pumped 41.23 million barrels a day in October, up 2.04 million from September, according to secondary sources cited by OPEC. Iraq told OPEC it produced 4.888 million barrels a day, which Bloomberg described as a new wartime high for the country.

Saudi Arabia has run covert shuttle voyages with its tankers via the Strait of Hormuz, copying an approach the United Arab Emirates first used earlier in the war, according to Bloomberg. The kingdom has additionally leaned on a second pipeline to the Red Sea, which has been attacked.

Speaking at an Abu Dhabi industry event last week, Saudi Aramco CEO Amin Nasser said the company intends to "maximize production going forward in the fourth quarter," according to Bloomberg.

The output increase comes as OPEC and its allies examine the volumes each member can technically produce as groundwork for 2027 output targets. The review is expected to wrap up within the next few days, according to Bloomberg. When the full OPEC+ group convenes online on Nov. 29 to talk over 2027 targets, it will draw on that assessment.

The U.S.-Israeli war on Iran has held Gulf OPEC+ producers far below their output ceilings in recent months. Brent traded around $73 a barrel before the conflict began in late February.
