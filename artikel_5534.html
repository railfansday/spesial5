Jensen Huang's spy scandal throws Nvidia's South Korea AI event into disarray

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Madison Huang canceled her Seoul trip, a planned ministerial meeting fell through and Nvidia declined to discuss the controversy

Nvidia $NVDA held an AI partnership event in Seoul on Monday without Madison Huang, a senior director and the daughter of CEO Jensen Huang, who canceled her appearance, according to Bloomberg.

Madison Huang scrapped her trip over the weekend and cited a “family matter,” according to Bloomberg. Her withdrawal canceled the sit-down that had been scheduled with Science & ICT Minister Bae Kyung-hoon. A less senior official filled in for Vice Science & ICT Minister Ryu Je-myung at the event.

Nvidia Vice President Ashish Karandikar and Jesse Clayton, principal product marketing manager, represented the company, according to Bloomberg. Organizers permitted only a pair of pre-arranged questions for the executives, and each dealt strictly with the AI collaboration.

At the end of the ceremony, Nvidia consented to one more question once reporters crowding into the conference room kept insisting, according to Bloomberg. The company declined to respond to a query on why Madison Huang pulled out and on the uproar involving her father.

Nvidia unveiled a pact tying its open infrastructure platform into AI chips from startup Rebellions and into SK Telecom's AI data center, according to Bloomberg. Jensen Huang has made himself a highly visible figure in South Korea, where he has hosted meals with business tycoons in front of the public.

Multiple reports tied Jensen Huang to a woman whom American officials believed to be a Chinese spy, according to Bloomberg. Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation on Sunday after reports that Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence operation. A panel of independent directors, supported by outside legal advisers, is overseeing the inquiry.

Jensen Huang apologized to staff for being unfaithful to his wife. Reports have identified the woman as Alice Huang, a business executive whom U.S. intelligence agencies suspect of spying for China. She is no relation to the Nvidia CEO. The board said it has seen nothing suggesting that U.S. security interests or Nvidia’s proprietary information were put at risk.

China's Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said on Monday that the "so-called Chinese spy narrative" was unsubstantiated.

Nvidia stock was down about 5% to 6% in premarket trading on Monday. The company is scheduled to report fiscal third-quarter results on Nov. 17.
