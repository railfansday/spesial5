A Doctor's Moment of Thanks

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Instead of my usual column this week, I thought I'd offer a partial list of the things I'm thankful for.

As usually happens with this kind of list, there are always things and people we forget to thank. Just think of the Academy Awards: you forgot to thank your family who are sitting right there in the front row of the audience looking right at you.

But for now, instead of writing about some small innovative idea in healthcare or some tidal change that needs to come about, I'd like to reflect on the year gone by and think about the year ahead.

First of all, I am thankful for my family and friends. They keep me sane, help me avoid burnout, make me laugh, and make me cry.

I'm thankful for my health and for the things I can do to improve and maintain it.

I'm thankful for all the opportunities I have, the blessings in my life, love, and career that all make getting up each day worthwhile.

I'm thankful for the job I have, which lets me go to work every morning and make a difference (at least, I hope); that lets me take care of patients, teach medical students and residents, do my small part to help change the healthcare system for the better, for each patient we care for, for all those on the care team, and in the healthcare system as a whole.

I'm so incredibly thankful for my patients, who have given me the honor of allowing me to take care of them, to be involved in such a critical part of their lives, who've trusted me to help them find the best healthcare solutions, to let me be their advocate.

I'm thankful to all my colleagues, my partners in practice and all the people that help us take care of everybody day in and day out: our nurse practitioners, nurses, medical technicians, registrars, administrators, finance people, and on and on.

I'm thankful that my knees have held up enough for me to continue playing squash, which I love, which lets my brain unwind.

I am thankful that years ago, when I developed a health issue, one of my doctors suggested I take up meditating. It has been a godsend -- at least when I remember to find the time and get to it.

I'm thankful for all the incredible advances we've made in healthcare, and all the changes that are coming. I'm thankful for the possible political sea change, and all the health good that could come from it.

I'm thankful for my institution for always supporting me as I try to bring about innovation to improve the lives of our patients and our providers and the healthcare system in general.

I'm thankful for the possibilities that lie ahead, for innovations around artificial intelligence (AI), which I firmly believe can, in healthcare, possibly more than in any other sector, make a huge difference.

And I'm thankful for so many people who are fighting to help fix the broken systems, to champion the inequities that we and our patients face every day, the barriers to care, voices speaking out and standing firm and never taking no for an answer, to make everybody's lives better, to give everyone a fair shot at getting healthy.

I'm thankful to my editors and the whole team at MedPage Today, who let me spout off every week, helping to clean up the regurgitations and eruptions from my tangled brain and turning it into this column, week after week (guaranteed 100% AI free!).

And I'm thankful to all of you for reading it, for pondering the words I put down, and for your thoughtful comments, even when they're a little bit snarky and call me a fool for wishing and believing we can make everything better.

I wish you all a healthy and happy Thanksgiving.

May you all be blessed with the comfort of family and friends and good health and good food. And hopefully an even better holiday season and 2027 ahead for everybody.
