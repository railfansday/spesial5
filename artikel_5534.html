5 small habits that can make you more grateful

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Bring more appreciation into your daily routine with simple ways to recognize life's little joys, strengthen relationships and focus on the good

Gratitude doesn't have to mean making a long list of everything you're thankful for or pretending life is perfect when it isn't. Sometimes it's as simple as appreciating a good cup of coffee, recognizing a friend who showed up when you needed them or taking a moment to notice something that made your day better.

Those small moments can add up, and just might boost feelings of happiness, and even strengthen your relationships. A grateful outlook is also associated with benefits such as better sleep, improved mood and a lower risk of certain health problems.

The good news is that gratitude isn't just a feeling that appears when everything is going well. It's a skill you can develop through everyday habits. You can practice it by writing down what you appreciate, telling someone how much they mean to you or simply setting aside a few minutes to reflect on the good things in your life.

You don't need a complicated routine or a special journal to get started. The trick is finding small, realistic ways to make gratitude part of your day. Reader’s Digest highlighted simple strategies to help you focus more on what you have, strengthen your connections with others and find more reasons to appreciate the everyday.

Here are five simple habits you can start today to bring more gratitude into your everyday life.

Thinking about the good things in your life is a useful start, but actually saying them out loud can make the experience more meaningful, according to Reader’s Digest. Putting gratitude into words helps you identify exactly what you're appreciating, while hearing yourself say it can reinforce the positive feeling.

You don't need to make a big announcement or deliver a speech about everything that's going right. Start with something small. Maybe you're thankful for a quiet morning, a helpful coworker or a conversation that made you laugh. Saying those things aloud gives you a chance to recognize why they matter instead of letting the moment pass unnoticed.

One easy way to build the habit is to record a quick voice memo. Use your phone to talk through a few things you're grateful for, then save the recordings so you can revisit them when you need a reminder of the good in your life.

You can also make gratitude a shared routine. At dinner, invite everyone at the table to mention one thing they're thankful for that day. It doesn't have to be profound. A funny moment, a favorite meal or a small act of kindness all count.

The goal isn't to force positivity or come up with something impressive every time. It's to make a habit of noticing what you appreciate and giving those feelings a little more attention.

A gratitude journal is a simple way to turn positive thoughts into a daily habit. Writing down what you're thankful for helps you slow down and give those moments more attention, rather than letting them disappear into the rush of everyday life, notes Reader’s Digest.

You don't need an elaborate notebook or a complicated journaling routine. Keep a small notebook on your desk, in your purse or beside your bed so it's easy to reach when something comes to mind. You can also use a notes app if you'd rather keep everything on your phone.

One straightforward approach is to write down three things you're grateful for each day. They can be big milestones or ordinary pleasures: a good night's sleep, a delicious lunch, a supportive friend or a few minutes of sunshine. The important part is being specific enough to remember what made the moment meaningful.

You can also use your journal to record gratitude as it happens. If someone helps you with a difficult task or you enjoy an unexpectedly pleasant afternoon, jot it down before you forget. Those entries can become a useful reminder when you're having a rough day and struggling to see the positives.

Writing a thank-you note is another way to put gratitude on paper. Whether you send it to someone or simply use it to organize your thoughts, taking the time to express appreciation can help reinforce the feeling.

Gratitude can be good for your own outlook, but sharing it with other people can make it even more meaningful. A sincere thank-you lets someone know their actions matter, and the exchange can strengthen the relationship between you, according to Reader’s Digest.

It doesn't have to be a grand gesture. Thank a friend for checking in, tell a family member you appreciate their support or acknowledge a coworker who made your day easier. Even thanking a stranger for a small kindness can create a positive moment for both of you.

The key is to be specific. Instead of offering a quick, automatic thank-you, explain what you appreciated and why. Let a friend know that their advice helped you through a stressful situation, or tell someone that you enjoyed the time you spent together. A little detail can make your gratitude feel more personal.

You can also find different ways to express it. Call someone you haven't spoken to in a while, write a note to a former teacher or give a small thank-you gift to someone who has made a difference in your life. If you don't have time for a lengthy message, a thoughtful text can still make someone's day.

Try making this a daily goal: identify one person whose presence or actions you're grateful for, then let them know. Gratitude becomes more than a private exercise when you share it, and those small moments of appreciation can help build stronger connections over time.

A busy mind can make it difficult to notice what's going well. Taking a few minutes to meditate on gratitude gives you a chance to slow down and direct your attention toward the people, experiences and everyday comforts you appreciate.

You don't need to be an experienced meditator to try it, notes Reader’s Digest. Start by finding a quiet spot where you can sit comfortably without distractions. Take a few steady breaths, clear your mind as best you can and bring to mind something or someone you're grateful for. It could be a supportive relationship, a recent accomplishment or simply having a safe place to come home to.

Reader’s Digest recommends setting aside one to five minutes each morning for a guided gratitude meditation or quiet reflection. You can use an audio meditation if you prefer some direction, or simply think through a few things you're thankful for.

The point is to give those thoughts your attention rather than rushing through them. If your mind wanders, gently bring it back to what you're appreciating. You don't have to feel especially cheerful or peaceful for the practice to count.

Making gratitude part of your morning routine can also help set a more intentional tone for the day ahead. Instead of immediately focusing on your to-do list or the things that might go wrong, you begin by recognizing something positive that's already part of your life.

You don't have to wait for something wonderful to happen before you practice gratitude, according to Reader’s Digest. In fact, planning ahead can help you notice positive moments even on days when your mood is low or your schedule feels overwhelming.

Start by thinking about the parts of your life that tend to frustrate or disappoint you. Is there a daily task you dread, a stressful commute or a responsibility that leaves you feeling drained? Consider whether there's anything about those situations you can appreciate, even if the circumstances themselves aren't ideal.

You can make a written list of specific things you're grateful for and carry it with you. When you're having a difficult day, revisit it as a reminder of what matters to you. Another option is to set an alarm on your phone as a prompt to pause and look for something positive.

You can also plan activities that give you opportunities to feel grateful. Sign up to volunteer, make time for someone you care about or schedule an activity you genuinely enjoy. Helping others, in particular, can offer a sense of purpose while giving you another reason to appreciate what you have.

The goal is simply to make room for gratitude before you need a mood boost. When you build the habit into your routine, you're more likely to notice the good things that might otherwise go overlooked.
