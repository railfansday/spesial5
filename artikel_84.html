Ukraine critically damages Kerch bridge linking occupied Crimea with Russia

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Zelenskyy praises ‘unprecedented attack’ but Kremlin claims bridge will be restored and will be ‘better than ever’

Ukraine has critically damaged the Kerch bridge linking occupied Crimea with Russia in an overnight missile attack, in what Volodymyr Zelenskyy hailed as an “unprecedented” military operation.

Footage showed a Ukrainian Flamingo cruise missile seconds before it hit the road and railway crossing, followed by an explosion. The strike follows an earlier attack on Monday that targeted an arch across its central section.

Writing on social media, the Ukrainian president described the attack as “a response to Russian strikes on our infrastructure”. He said it was the culmination of a long and complex campaign to demolish the bridge, involving domestically produced drones and ballistic missiles. Palianytsia drones hit the road section of the bridge, while Neptune and other missiles targeted the adjacent rail section, he added.

The Ukrainian general staff said an arch and a span of the bridge’s road section had collapsed, severing the road link, while the railway line was still standing but its arch was damaged. Train travel across the bridge resumed on Tuesday after a brief pause.

Earlier, Russian forces struck ⁠two major bridges in Kyiv that straddle the Dnipro River late on Monday, along with some other critical infrastructure, amid drone ⁠and missile attacks ​across Ukraine.

Ukraine has declared that the Kerch bridge, built eight years ago after Vladimir Putin’s 2014 annexation of Crimea and known officially as the Crimean Bridge, is illegal. It has repeatedly attempted to destroy it, using a truck bomb in 2022 and a sea drone strike the following year.

The latest drone and missile barrage appears to have succeeded after months in which Ukrainian unmanned systems forces have systematically knocked out the peninsula’s multi-layered air defences.

The Kremlin-appointed governor of Crimea, Sergey Aksyonov, confirmed that road traffic on the bridge had been suspended. “We ask residents and visitors in Crimea to refrain from travelling toward the bridge and to take these restrictions into account when planning their routes,” he posted on Telegram.

Photos on Tuesday suggest that a 227-metre road arch span has fallen into the Kerch strait, which connects the Sea of Azov with the Black Sea. The adjacent railway arch span has also sustained damage.

Ukrainian commentators say the massive steel arch cannot easily be replaced. The closure of the bridge means traffic from Crimea can travel out of the peninsula only via a land corridor through occupied southern Ukraine, vulnerable to Ukrainian drones.

The debris appears to have blocked the main navigable route beneath the bridge, used by maritime traffic from the Sea of Azov and the ports of Berdiansk and Mariupol. The channel is 120 metres wide and 8.3 metres deep. Submerged steel struts could prevent large vessels from passing until the debris is removed.

The Russian government convened an emergency meeting to assess the attack, according to the Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov. Peskov added that the bridge would be repaired and would be “even better than before”, in comments to the Russian newspaper Izvestia.

But pro-war Russian commentators acknowledged that repairing the bridge would be very difficult, warning that, unlike in 2022 and 2023, Ukraine now had the capability to strike floating cranes and repair crews.

“Even installing a prefabricated arch under such conditions would be a formidable challenge,” wrote the influential Rybar Telegram channel, with close links to the defence ministry.

Ukrainian observers were gleeful at the bridge’s long-awaited destruction. They also pointed out that Ukraine’s armed forces could hit the bridge again to prevent repair work from being carried out. Serhii Sternenko, a former adviser to the defence ministry in Kyiv, wrote: “In response to your many questions. Yes, the bridge is fucked.”

The Ukrainian blogger Anton Gerashchenko pointed out that Putin personally opened the bridge in 2018, driving across it in a Kamaz truck. “The Kerch bridge stands as the primary symbol of the Russian occupation of Ukrainian Crimea and the embodiment of its illegal annexation. It is Putin’s flagship project,” he wrote.

He added: “For years, Russian authorities claimed the bridge was ‘the most protected facility in the world’. Consequently, every successful strike shatters the myth of the invincibility of Russian air defences – as well as the notion that Ukraine is incapable of reaching key targets deep behind enemy lines.”

Ukrainians celebrated on Tuesday with memes. The company Fire Point, which manufactures the Flamingo missile, posted a popcorn emoji. Other social media users shared video of “Kerch Bridge on Fire”, a popular song by Saint Javelin which has become an anti-Moscow anthem.

Russian military bloggers were gloomy. One, Russian Engineer, acknowledged the Ukrainian military operation to paralyse the bridge had been “perfect”. Hundreds of unmanned aerial vehicles distracted Russian air defences, he said, before a clinical strike by ballistic missiles.

The attack will further complicate supplies to Crimea, after a summer in which the peninsula struggled with fuel shortages and an intensifying Ukrainian campaign to isolate it from mainland Russia.

Ukraine has for months focused its strikes on the key transport routes supplying Crimea, particularly the so-called Novorossiya highway, a key logistics corridor linking the peninsula to Russia’s Rostov region through the occupied cities of Melitopol and Mariupol.

Russia had already sharply restricted traffic across the Crimean Bridge. Moscow has largely stopped using the crossing for rail fuel shipments since the 2022 Ukrainian attack damaged the bridge and set a fuel train ablaze.

Rybar wrote that the latest strike would force Moscow to “completely overhaul” its supply routes to Crimea, shifting more traffic to the Novorossiya highway. Russia would also have to step up the installation of protective netting along the route, Rybar added, lamenting that the measure had not been introduced in the spring.

Meanwhile in Kyiv, loud explosions could be heard as Russian forces struck two bridges over the Dnipro River. The mayor of the Ukrainian capital, Vitali Klitschko, wrote on Telegram that according to “preliminary information, there have been hits on the north and south bridges in ⁠the capital”. There were water and power cuts in parts of the city, he added later.
