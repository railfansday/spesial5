The moment you can’t do the thing you love most is the moment you understand how much it defines you.

Kate Mihevc Edwards, DPT, has spent more than 16 years watching that moment play out in her clinic. Runners walk in after seeing two, three, sometimes five other providers, still without answers, still hurting, still searching. The problem, she says, has almost never been the injury itself.

“Runners kept finding that they weren’t getting the information they needed,” says Edwards. “They searched everywhere, asked so many people, went to so many providers. And the reason was fragmentation. Nobody was talking. Nobody was working together.”

Edwards is the founder of Precision Performance and Physical Therapy in Atlanta, Director of the Medical Team for the Atlanta Track Club Elite and the creator of RunSource, an expert-filtered running health app launched in June 2026. She did not set out to build an app. She set out to solve a problem she had been watching for over a decade, and the app became the only solution that made sense at scale.

The evidence for what she was building came from the Atlanta Track Club. Six years ago, Edwards helped construct a fully integrated medical team for the club’s elite athletes: physical therapists, a sports dietitian, a sports psychologist, a mental performance coach, a chiropractor and a physician, meeting every week to review athlete health across disciplines. The results have compounded every year. This season marked the first time in the program’s history that no athlete sustained a bone stress injury, an outcome Edwards attributes directly to that coordinated care.

The model worked. The problem was access.

“In real life, it’s not something most people can do,” says Edwards. “There isn’t really a world where a recreational runner can go somewhere and have a dietician and a PT and an MD and get that education from all of them. Not everyone can come to Atlanta. And not everyone can afford to.”

She points to three barriers that define the recreational runner’s experience with injury: time, money and physical location. Most running specialists are out of network. Seeing more than one provider in a single week becomes expensive fast. And outside of a few major cities, finding a provider who truly understands running medicine can feel impossible.

What goes unspoken in most clinical settings, Edwards says, is what happens to a person while they’re still searching.

“When somebody has seen multiple providers and nobody has an answer, they start to question themselves,” she says. “They ask, am I crazy? Is this real? Some people get fearful and stop running entirely. Others push through and make it worse. Others end up dealing with depression or anxiety.”

Edwards knows this from more than clinical observation. When she was diagnosed with a cardiac condition that forced her off the road, she found herself on the receiving end of the same fragmented system. Test after test. No answers. She spiraled. That experience did not leave her practice. It shaped it.

RunSource launched with more than 40 vetted running medicine professionals contributing content across physical therapy, sports medicine, registered dietetics, certified strength coaching and sports psychology. Every piece is curated through what Edwards calls the expert filter, a vetting process that mirrors the same clinical rigor she applies at Precision Performance.

The app features two proprietary AI agents. Katherine walks users through an educational symptom checker that draws from the same protocols Edwards uses in her practice. Andrew answers general running questions using a curated knowledge base of peer-reviewed research and expert-produced content. Neither guesses. If either agent doesn’t know the answer, it says so. If Katherine flags signs of a bone stress injury, it tells the user to see a physician.

“Much of what we do as clinicians is educate,” says Edwards. “Ninety percent of an appointment is the education and what you need to do to help yourself. That piece is what I built the app around.”

One philosophy sits at the center of all of it: she does not tell runners to stop running.

With limited exceptions, such as confirmed bone stress injuries, Edwards believes that pulling a runner entirely off the road does as much harm as it does good. A tendon injury, she explains, is not helped by rest alone. What it needs is appropriate loading, a modification in training volume and targeted eccentric work. Stopping affects the runner not just emotionally but physiologically, in ways that slow recovery rather than support it.

Edwards spent three years building the answer she once searched for herself. When her cardiac diagnosis forced her off the road and left her cycling through specialists with no explanation, she did not find coordinated care. She found the same fragmented system her patients describe every day.

RunSource is her response to that system.

“Every runner deserves access to the kind of care that elite athletes get,” says Edwards. “RunSource does not replace a provider when you need one. It tells you when you need one, what kind to find, and gives you expert guidance in the meantime.”

That is the model she helped build for the Atlanta Track Club Elite, distilled into an app and handed to every runner with a phone.
