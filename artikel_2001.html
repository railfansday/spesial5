Scotland hero tells Pocognoli the change Tartan Army are desperate to see

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Robert Snodgrass reckons Scotland boss Sebastien Pocognoli should tap into the country's love affair with flying wingers as he continues to mould the national team in his image.

The former Scotland star accepts Pocognoli needs time to impose his ideas after succeeding Steve Clarke and believes the work between international camps will be crucial.

Pocognoli has favoured a 3-4-1-2 during the opening four games of his reign, a system which leaves little room for two out-and-out wingers. But Snodgrass would love to see Scotland eventually find a way of getting genuine wide men into the team and believes it could provide the attacking spark the Tartan Army crave.

Snodgrass, speaking to BOYLE Sports, who offer football odds, said: “It was a big decision him coming in. He hasn't started so well that people are completely sold on the formation and style giving immediate hope or suitability for the squad. That's the general feeling coming back from the first camp.

“But he needs time to implement his ideas.

“A lot of international managers say they don't get enough time on the grass in international football.

“During the gaps between camps, your analysts need to reach out to players and share work. Take the time to meet up with key players, your right-backs, wingers, strikers, and go through the footage so that when the squad meets up, you're not starting from scratch.

“Meet them, show them the work and get these fresh ideas into their mindsets. They might already be doing that behind the scenes.

“When you step into the job, the workload increases as you try to get players comfortable with new concepts.

“When people feel an uncomfortable change, you have to put the work in so the lads feel it's simple, know their roles and believe in what we're trying to achieve. Once that happens, it creates belief within the squad that we're heading in the right direction.”

Snodgrass believes Pocognoli already has a solid platform to work from after the Clarke era. But the former winger naturally wants to see Scotland add another dimension higher up the pitch.

He added: “Momentum and positivity are key for Scotland. When they're in a good flow, you know Scotland will always be in games.

“They aren't expansive to the point of leaving themselves wide open, but are built off a solid base.

“That's a good starting point, but as confidence grows, I'd like to see us play with out-and-out wingers. They have Gannon-Doak, Josh McPake, lads who can go one-v-one and cause problems. I'd love to see that.

“It creates a spark and an atmosphere that Scotland fans are desperate for.

“We're used to good wingers in Scottish football. Being a winger myself, I might sound biased, but who doesn't want to see exciting, attacking football for Scotland?

“Steve Clarke built a strong defensive base, and the next challenge for the manager is to build on that by adding a real attacking style with players who can cause teams serious problems.”

And Snodgrass has a clear idea of who should be leading that attack.

Kieron Bowie has impressed and strengthened his case during the early stages of Pocognoli's reign, but Snodgrass reckons Oli McBurnie remains Scotland's best option through the middle.

McBurnie hasn't provided a glut of goals, but his performances across Pocognoli's opening four-game run appear to have changed the conversation around his international prospects.

Snodgrass said: “Oli McBurnie has proved he can score goals everywhere he's been. He's a great lad to have around the place, has matured a lot and has experience leading the line in the Premier League.

“Even in that game down to ten men against Switzerland, his performance was excellent. He showed everyone what he brings.

“In Scotland, we love a big number nine who can hold the ball up, attack crosses and link play. He has a lot in his armoury, so we need to stick with him.

“I feel for Bowie and the other young forwards, but having them come off the bench is a great option.

“Right now, leading the line in the Premier League gives Oli the edge, but Bowie and the others aren't far behind. They're three great attacking options for Scotland.

“They tried two up top, but it takes a few camps to realise what your best setup is.”
