How To Identify Stolen Items In Probably Stolen

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

What counts as stolen goods, who brings them in, and how to get away with selling them in Probably Stolen.

You know, for a game labeled Probably Stolen, it's pretty easy to play an entire run without dealing in stolen property at all. But that's no fun; who doesn't want to play the bad guy sometimes? Or at least, the morally grey guy.

If you're interested in dealing with some not-so-legally-acquired goods to make a profit, here's what you need to know and who you need to avoid.

Typically, stolen goods will be things like pharmaceuticals, chemistry products, or sometimes food. All stolen goods will have some level of heat attached to them based on their value in credits. Heat is the game's metric for how obviously stolen they are — higher heat objects will be easily identified as stolen by buyers and Security, while low heat items might be easier to get away with. Heat disapates over time; the longer you have the item in your inventory, the less attention it'll attract. When it hits zero heat, it's no longer considered stolen and can be sold normally.

Stolen goods are also typically sold by a shady customer or, in some cases, wanted criminals. They'll say something to the effect of needing to get rid of their goods quickly, of "finding" them, or similar phrasing. Sometimes the customer will simply be labeled Thief, which does make identifying them much easier, but sometimes you'll be buying from a chemist or pharmacist who's stolen from their workplace.

On day 14 (or day five if you skip the intro), you'll be visited by The Butcher, a wanted Lower Levels criminal who sells meat that's identified as stolen with fairly high heat...don't ask where the meat comes from. He comes back once a week to sell off his goods. You can choose to report him to Security if you want to, but that can have some pretty dire consequences. You can also try shooting him with the shotgun, but this isn't particularly effective. This is, in my opinion, one of the scariest characters in the game.

Storing stolen goods is relatively easy; keep them in a smuggler's bay, which can hold up to a five by five grid of objects. This isn't a ton of room, but you can get an expanded smuggler's bay using the Underground Exchange (you'll need to leave a normal smuggler's bay and eight Oxymore injectors in your trash can overnight), which holds an eight by eight grid of objects.

Put stolen goods into your smuggler's bay immediately upon buying them; don't call up your next customer until they're put away if you can avoid it. My first run of Probably Stolen ended because I got caught with stolen goods on the counter one too many times by Security and got arrested.

Once they're in the smuggler's bay, you can sell off your stolen goods to anyone willing to buy them. Don't try and sell them to Security, for obvious reasons, but most Lower Levels citizens won't care where their product came from as long as you have what they need. ou can hold onto stolen goods until their heat is gone if you want to sell them off normally; again, once they hit a heat of zero, they can safely be removed from the smuggler's bay.

Be careful selling stolen goods; every sale of a good with heat attached to it ups your Possession (Stolen Goods) crime, which can eventually lead to an arrest, which ends your run. To combat this, you can hire Joe Wild's Fixers through his network, who will, for a nightly fee, eliminate these crimes from your record, which can keep you from getting arrested.
