No Moreno, big problem: Kentucky's thin frontcourt faces Wichita State

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Coaches like to say a basketball team is built from the middle out. On Friday night, Mark Pope may have to build one with the middle missing.

Malachi Moreno, Kentucky basketball's sophomore center, is in concussion protocol after last week's 90-76 loss to Indiana. The unbeaten Hoosiers, now 6-0 and ranked No. 21, dominated that game. Moreno is not expected to play when Wichita State visits Rupp Arena the night after Thanksgiving.

For a Kentucky team still looking for its footing six games into the season, the timing could hardly be worse. The opponent coming to Lexington wins with size, rebounding and patience. Those are exactly the things Moreno gives Kentucky.

The hit itself was the kind of play that happens a dozen times a night. It most likely came in the first half. Moreno lost his footing on a drive to the basket and went down. Indiana's Samet Yigitoglu, a 270-pound center, dove in after the loose ball and landed with his full weight on Moreno, whose face was flat against the floor.

Seven days between games usually feels like plenty of time. With a concussion, the calendar doesn't get a vote. Moreno has to clear each stage of the protocol on his own timeline, and Kentucky has to prepare as if he won't make it back by Friday.

Few centers fill a box score the way Moreno does. He's averaging 13.8 points, 8.7 rebounds, 3.0 assists, 1.8 blocks and 1.2 steals per game. When a Kentucky possession stalls, the ball usually finds him. When a teammate gets beat off the dribble, he's usually the one waiting at the rim.

Pope has options. None of them is Moreno.

Franck Kepnang, the veteran transfer from Washington, is the most likely starter. Reece Potter figures to see more of the floor than he has all season. Ousmane N'Diaye, a 6-foot-11 forward from Senegal who played professionally in Europe, and Justin McBride, the James Madison transfer, could be asked to play bigger than usual.

Here's the hard part. McBride is averaging 5.7 points and 2.3 rebounds. N'Diaye is at 5.3 points and 3.8 rebounds. Kepnang is at 2.2 and 1.7. Potter has yet to score and averages 1.5 rebounds.

Add it all up and you get 13.2 points and 9.3 rebounds a game. That's roughly one Moreno, spread across four bodies, four sets of minutes and 20 fouls to give.

This isn't a question of effort. Kentucky can live with one big man having a quiet night. What it can't easily survive is four of them trying to cover for one, against an opponent built to win the exact battle Kentucky is least equipped for.

Wichita State is not coming to Lexington as holiday filler. The Shockers are 5-1 and lead the American Athletic Conference's early standings. They're scoring 86.3 points a game and allowing 69.2.

KenPom ranks Wichita State 61st overall, 69th on offense and 56th on defense. Kentucky sits 63rd. That was the gap before Moreno got hurt.

The Shockers' calling card is finishing possessions. They rank fourth in the nation in defensive rebounding rate, so opponents rarely get a second look. For a Kentucky team without its best rebounder, no number on the scouting report matters more.

The man in the middle of all of it is Will Berg. The 7-foot-2 Swede spent three seasons at Purdue, where Matt Painter's offense is built around big centers. Now a 265-pound redshirt senior in his second year at Wichita State, he's having a breakout season. He averaged 8.8 points and 8.0 rebounds last year. This year he's at 19.0 points and 12.2 rebounds on 70.8% shooting.

Watch where Berg catches the ball. A center shooting above 70% isn't living on contested hook shots. He's scoring off deep post seals, dump-off passes and second chances. Kentucky's first job is to keep him from getting position. Its second job is to end every defensive possession with the ball in its hands.

Pope's best counterpunch may come from the 3-point line. Make Berg guard a big man who can shoot, and the paint opens up for Kentucky's guards. N'Diaye is the likeliest candidate to pull him out. If N'Diaye can make Wichita State pay for leaving him open, Kentucky changes the shape of the game.

After the Indiana loss, Pope didn't sell a quick fix. He sounded like a coach who knows how long this climb will take.

"We just have to get better every day, and that's what this team is built to do," Pope said. "This group is gonna be relentless in terms of growing and improving. They have a really high ceiling, and we're not close to it right now.

"So there's no magic pills; we're not chasing that. This is like a daily, every single day commitment. That's how you become great, and that's what this team's committed to doing. That's what I expect us to see."

It's an honest message, and the right one for November. But honest messages don't box out 7-footers. Friday is the first real test of Pope's promise, and it comes without the player best equipped to keep it.

The formula is simple to say and hard to pull off. If Kentucky holds its own on the glass, it can win this game. If it doesn't, Wichita State leaves Lexington with a résumé win, and Kentucky leaves with more questions than answers.

When: Friday, Nov. 27, 8 p.m. CT (9 p.m. ET)
