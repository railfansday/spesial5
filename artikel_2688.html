Lakers eye biggest test of season with more questions than answers

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

LOS ANGELES — It feels like we've already experienced various phases of the Lakers between the start of the season and Monday's 109-94 win over the Utah Jazz.

The team's fast launch out of the gates was highlighted by a nuclear start to the season for Luka Dončić. Despite surviving a regression to what feels far below the mean, the Lakers came out of their worst stretch of the season on Monday with a 4-1 record and 11 wins in their last 12 games to remain atop the conference with a 15-3 overall record.

And yet, with their biggest test of the season against the San Antonio Spurs on the horizon, Los Angeles's last week of games has assuredly raised some questions. The first being: are they ready for it?

"It'll be good to see," Walker Kessler said of the forthcoming clash against the Spurs. "It'll be a challenge for sure, but I think we're ready. I'm excited to go out there and compete."

San Antonio is tied with the Lakers for the league's top record. Los Angeles has three days off before Friday's game while the Spurs play Portland before the marquee showdown. Nonetheless, the matchup will be perhaps the most anticipated of the season as both teams will play for early bragging rights.

The biggest wildcard — and another one of the Lakers' biggest questions — is if Austin Reaves will be available. The star guard has missed the last five games with a left heel bruise and his absence has been felt in all facets of the game.

When Dončić is on the floor, opposing defenders can focus more on him. When he's off, the time typically reserved for Reaves to run the lineup has seen the Lakers struggle to create offense. Head coach JJ Redick said pregame that he's optimistic Reaves will return to the lineup against San Antonio, but actions speak louder than words.

Just as Redick pointed out in the lead-up to Monday's game, the Lakers had the No. 1 offense through the first 12 games with Reaves on the court for a majority of that time. Since then, with Reaves out, their offense has struggled. And yet, again, they're 4-1 without Reaves, which is a major reference point for the team.

"It's encouraging," Kessler said. "Obviously Austin is a very important piece to this team. And so seeing how we compete without him and not at our full strength is good. It builds resiliency because [you're] not going to be always at full strength going in the season. Things happen. It's a long season

"So it's encouraging to see, but, obviously, we miss him and excited for him to be back out there."

Trials and tribulations are part of an 82-game season. But the fact that the Lakers have endured all of this and still stay atop the league early in the season might be the answer to every question.

"Every day we continue to get closer and closer," said Lakers rookie Cameron Carr after his career-high nine-point outing on Monday. "We have a very unique group of guys and I think God put us here for a reason. We just connect. … The puzzle pieces are put together."
