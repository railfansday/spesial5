20 Years Later, Guillermo del Toro’s Dark Fantasy Classic Gets a Stunning 4K Release

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Hannah covers horror, sci-fi, and games at Collider, where she writes news and features, reviews new releases, and interviews the people who make them. She’s helping build out Collider’s gaming coverage, with Pokémon as one of her specialties. If she isn’t writing about a monster, she’s probably watching one on a tape from her VHS collection.

One of the first things Captain Vidal does in Pan’s Labyrinth is correct the way a little girl shakes his hand. Ofelia (Ivana Baquero) has her books tucked under her right arm, so she offers him her left. He could take the books for her. He could let the whole thing go. Instead, he takes her hand just long enough to tell her she’s given him the wrong one. Guillermo del Toro gives Ofelia plenty of reasons to be afraid of her new stepfather, but that’s the moment you understand why she’d rather take her chances with whatever lives in the woods.

Ofelia has better luck with Mercedes (Maribel Verdú), the housekeeper who can walk through Vidal’s rooms almost unnoticed. To him, she’s the woman who brings dinner. To the rebels hiding in the forest, she’s the reason supplies keep reaching them. It’s one of the pleasures of revisiting Pan’s Labyrinth twenty years later. Once you know what Mercedes does whenever she leaves the captain’s sight, even watching her set his table becomes suspenseful.

Collider can exclusively debut a clip from the 20th anniversary edition of Pan’s Labyrinth featuring Cate Blanchett, Jessica Chastain, and Jacob Elordi. All three know what it’s like to play someone del Toro dreamed up. Blanchett had Bradley Cooper’s number from the moment he sat down in her office in Nightmare Alley, and Chastain made a cup of tea feel like grounds to leave the house in Crimson Peak. They join Frankenstein’s Elordi to discuss how del Toro writes women, something he has been consistently applauded for throughout his career. Watch the exclusive clip below ahead of the movie's 4K Steelbook and Blu-ray release on December 1.

Four franchises, four completely different ways of getting underneath your skin. This is the one built out of your particular fear, why it works so well on you, and what it would leave behind.

Your nightmare does not wait outside the door. It waits for you to close your eyes, which you are going to have to do eventually, because nobody has ever won that particular argument.

Your nightmare is not really the doll. It is the face of the person you tell, and the way they decide, very kindly, that you have imagined all of it.

Your nightmare has your number, knows your name, and has almost certainly been inside your house before. The worst part is that you already know them.

Your nightmare is not really about you. It is about the people asleep down the hall, and the feeling that whatever is here is not interested in leaving.

Del Toro oversaw the new 4K restoration using the original 35mm elements. The collector’s editions pair a new retrospective and contributions from actors and directors with the commentary del Toro recorded in 2007. That last feature makes for a good double bill with the anniversary material. You can hear what other filmmakers took away from Pan’s Labyrinth after living with it for twenty years, then go back to what del Toro wanted viewers to notice when the movie was barely a year old.

The restored movie earned more than $2 million during its theatrical return, and Cineverse says the anniversary edition had the biggest physical preorder launch in the company’s history. The Amazon-exclusive Steelbook sold out, but collectors have another shot at one on December 1, when a 4K + Blu-ray Steelbook with different cover art arrives at retailers nationwide alongside the Blu-ray Collector’s Edition. A separate 4K + Blu-ray Collector’s Edition follows on December 29.

Pan’s Labyrinth 20th Anniversary Edition is available to own on digital now and to rent beginning December 8. Stay tuned at Collider for more.
