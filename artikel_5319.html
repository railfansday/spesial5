Pulsar added USDT deposits through TRON's TRC-20 network on November 19.

Supported stablecoin deposits carry no Pulsar fees, but network and third-party charges may apply.

TRON reported $2.21 trillion in Q3 stablecoin settlement volume, not Pulsar-specific activity.

Pulsar USDT deposits are giving users another route to spend stablecoins without first converting them through a traditional bank transfer. The November 19 integration supports USDT on TRON, letting eligible users fund their accounts and pay through Pulsar’s virtual card.

It’s a practical addition. But don’t confuse a new deposit option with proof of a sudden stablecoin spending boom.

Users can deposit USDT through the TRON network and use supported balances for online purchases, subscriptions and other everyday expenses.

The process requires selecting USDT and the TRON (TRC-20) network in the Pulsar app, then sending funds to the deposit address provided for that network.

Pulsar says supported stablecoin deposits carry no fees across its plans. However, network and third-party charges may still apply. Availability can also vary by region and service provider.

The fee distinction matters. A free deposit doesn’t automatically make the entire payment process free.

Users may still face blockchain network charges or fees from third-party providers. Pulsar’s announcement doesn’t provide a complete comparison of total transaction costs against other networks or payment methods.

The company also describes Pulsar as a money app, not a bank.

The launch comes as TRON reports substantial stablecoin activity. Its Q3 2026 settlement volume reached $2.21 trillion, averaging approximately $24 billion daily, according to the announcement.

TRON DAO also reported more than $94 billion in circulating USDT on the network.

Those figures show the scale of the underlying ecosystem, not Pulsar’s adoption. The announcement disclosed no user numbers, deposit totals or completed card-payment volumes.

For now, Pulsar USDT deposits expand access to stablecoin-funded payments. Whether users actually spend more through the virtual card remains an unanswered question.
