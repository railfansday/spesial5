Anthropic prices IPO at $83 per share, giving it a $1.5 trillion valuation

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Anthropic priced shares for its initial public offering on Wednesday at $83 per share, above its initially guided ranged of $70 to $78 per share, and likely valuing the AI company at $1.5 trillion.

Anthropic’s stock will begin trading Thursday on the Nasdaq exchange under the ticker ANT, marking one of the most highly anticipated IPOs in years as well as one of the largest ever.

The San Francisco-based AI company increased the number of shares sold in the offering to 980 million, instead of the 825 million shares it initially planned to sell.

Seven strategic partners, including Google and Nvidia, have also agreed to buy $26 billion of non-voting stock at the IPO price.

The IPO will make instant billionaires out of numerous Anthropic employees and early investors, particularly the seven cofounders who created Anthropic in San Francisco just five years ago. At a $1.5 trillion valuation, Anthropic would be the second most valuable IPO of all time, behind SpaceX’s $1.77 trillion IPO in June.

At $83 a share, Anthropic has raised at least $68.5 billion in the offering (the exact sum will depend on how many shares it sold)—a critical infusion of capital as it seeks to stay ahead in the AI race and to invest in the computing infrastructure to train its models.

Anthropic is the first pure-play maker of frontier AI models to go public (SpaceX also does rockets, and Google and Meta both have massive businesses in search and social media), and its Wall Street debut is being closely watched as a barometer for the health of the broader AI industry. The IPO comes as the AI boom is in a period of uncertainty following a leadership shake up at AI chipmaker Nvidia and growing public backlash to AI data centers.

Anthropic’s revenue has soared thanks to strong demand from business customers. In the nine months ending September 30, Anthropic generated $33.2 billion in revenue, up from $2.5 billion in the same period a year ago. The company posted a $104 billion net loss in its most recent nine-month period, though the bulk of the red ink is because of the way it accounted for its convertible notes.
