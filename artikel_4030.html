PS5 Exclusive Destruction AllStars Shuts Down On November 25

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

PlayStation embarked on a live service push a few years ago, and there’s a trail of bodies lying in its wake, most of which never even saw the light of day. Destruction AllStars did make it out alive, but only just. The PS5 exclusive hasn’t been available to buy for a while, and from November 25, it will no longer be playable at all, barring one unsupported feature.

In May of 2026, PlayStation delisted Destruction AllStars along with the game’s virtual currency. It gave those who still play the game a little more time to keep playing offline, with the promise that it will end service on November 25, 2026.

At the time of writing this, that date is tomorrow, which means if you have continued to play Destruction AllStars offline, your fun is about to forcibly come to an end. Server support will officially be shut down at 3 pm UTC on November 25. That’s 10 am EST, so technically you will still be able to play for a little while tomorrow too.

You might actually still be able to play after that time has passed, as PlayStation’s shutdown statement clarifies that the game’s single-player arcade challenges might still be available. Emphasis on the “might”, as with support for the game being pulled entirely, if something were to immediately break, which seems likely, there will be no one on the other end to fix it.

If you have any of that aforementioned virtual currency left, now is the time to spend it. Once Destruction AllStars is shut down, that money will simply disappear. If you do still have any left over, you have effectively already lost it since anything you buy today will be gone by tomorrow.

Destruction AllStars managed to avoid the live service grim reaper for five years, although that is giving the game a lot of credit it definitely does not deserve. When PlayStation announced it would be shutting down the game and ended multiplayer service effective immediately, it had basically been broken for two years.

The same statement that confirmed the game’s shutdown noted that multiplayer services would remain offline indefinitely due to “ongoing technical issues”. It remains unclear, and may stay that way forever, whether PlayStation was actively trying to fix the problems for all that time and couldn’t figure it out, or it simply didn’t care due to lack of player interest and eventually let everyone know that it wasn’t even trying to fix things behind the scenes.

Destruction AllStars will be a relatively small footnote when gaming historians inevitably write about PlayStation’s infamously poor live service push. Concord’s cancelation and Naughty Dog being forced to waste time and resources on a game that would never be released, The Last of Us Online, will be the focus. It’s a footnote all the same, though, and had it been a more notable release, its immediate failure might have forced PlayStation to rethink this whole live service thing before it led to layoffs and studio closures.
