Mini Review: Tanuki: Pon's Summer (PS5) - Easily One of 2026's Most Charming Adventures

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Tanuki: Pon's Summer is a delight — and a finely crafted delight at that.

This is one of the most downright lovable indies I've played in a long time; it's a consistently charming concoction of semi-open world exploration, character stories, and minigames.

You play as the titular Pon, a lazy little tanuki who's tasked with repairing his god's shrine for the upcoming festival. But this is the kind of work that requires cash, and so our unlikely protagonist somehow snags a job at the local post office.

What follows is a day-to-day adventure across a rural Japanese island, where Pon must deliver packages to its inhabitants, and often lend a paw to their predicaments.

Oh, and Pon's also a BMX enthusiast, which means that he can take advantage of the landscape's many bike ramps and grinding rails to get around more (or less) efficiently.

There's a whole trick system that you can master, and while far from mandatory, pulling off sick moves as you go about your business nets you extra money at the end of your shift. Additional moolah lets you purchase more decorations for your shrine — which you're free to customise when you're not at work.

The relaxed process of cycling around the island, meeting quirky new characters, and taking part in an impressive variety of minigames — like fishing, bug catching, pint pouring, sushi stacking, and many others — is surprisingly moreish.

Indeed, developer Denkiworks has created something quite special in terms of structure. It feels like the game's always coming up with clever new ways to keep you entertained, which makes for a very nicely paced package.

That said, a few of the minigames can come across as slightly tedious. Picking up trash or sanding the bark off logs, for example, can't compare with landing a big fish, or partaking in a spot of sumo. Still, as alluded, it's hard to fault the variety that's on offer.

Lovingly put together and full of heart, Tanuki: Pon's Summer is a fantastic little slice of relaxation.
