A Man Just Ran 3,015 Miles From San Francisco to New York in 41 Days, Setting New Record

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

When Cameron Balser left San Francisco at 4:59 a.m. on October 10, he set out to run the 3,015-mile Trans America route across the United States in under 42 days in pursuit of a new transcontinental record. About six weeks later, he reached New York City Hall in lower Manhattan at 11:45 p.m. on Friday, November 20, finishing in 41 days, 16 hours, 46 minutes, and 45 seconds.

His crossing beat the previous transcontinental record by more than seven hours and cleared the 42-day mark with just over seven hours to spare. For the final stretch down Manhattan’s West Side Drive, a large group of supporters ran alongside him to the finish.

The final push in New York. Photo via Bernie Bernstein

Data collected during the run shows how Balser broke the record due to consistency. Over the 42 days, he averaged 72.1 miles per day with a median pace of 74.5 miles per day. He hit at least 70 miles on 33 of his 42 days and crossed the 75-mile threshold 18 times.

On his third day, Balser logged his biggest climb of the entire crossing, gaining 14,210 feet of elevation over 69 miles. By the time he reached Manhattan, he had accumulated 148,546 feet of total vertical gain. Balser ran 500 miles in 6.4 days, 1,000 miles in 13 days, and 2,000 miles in 27.2 days.

Balser’s only notable slowdown came in early November, when he recorded his shortest day of the crossing at 42.2 miles in 10 hours and 10 minutes, followed by 45.5 miles the next day and 59.4 miles on November 9. However, by November 12, he had returned to a 75-mile day. In the following week, which would mark the end of his attempt, Balser covered 542.5 miles, averaging 77.5 miles per day. By comparison, his opening week totaled 504.7 miles at a 72.1-mile daily average.

Cameron Balser in New York City. Photo via Bernie Bernstein

His final day into New York City was the biggest push of the entire run. On that day, Balser covered 91.8 miles in 18 hours and 48 minutes, which also was his only day surpassing 80 miles.

At the end of the run, Balser focused on the motivation behind the miles rather than the clock.

“It was very humbling in a lot of ways. I wanted people to know how beautiful they already are and how loved they are,” Balser said. “Getting that message out by running 75+ miles a day is more important than the record. That’s why I did this, to show people how beautiful they are.”

Looking at the crowd that turned out for his late-night arrival in New York, he added: “I did not expect that many people to come out, it was a surreal moment… I went back to the memories of the people who helped us. I just really appreciated everyone’s help.”
