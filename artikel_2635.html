How the Sixers can advance to the NBA Cup knockout round

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The Joel Embiid-less Sixers got pasted Monday by the Portland Trail Blazers, but they have a golden opportunity to wash that bad taste out of their mouths. Their next two games are their final two games in the NBA Cup's group-play stage, and they still aren't eliminated from earning a spot in the knockout round.

It might look like the Heat are sitting pretty at 2-0 with a plus-32 point differential, but they face the Sixers on Wednesday and the New York Knicks on Friday. There's a non-zero chance they're 2-2 by the end of that stretch, which would likely end their chances of winning Group B.

So, what do the Sixers need to emerge as the winners of Group B? First, they have to sweep the Heat and Pacers this week. Anything short of a 3-1 record will all but guarantee their elimination.

Even if they do win both games, the Cleveland Cavaliers are lurking as a roadblock since the Cavs have the head-to-head tiebreaker over them. The Sixers would need the Knicks to beat the Cavs on Wednesday, which would push them down to 2-2.

If the Knicks do beat the Cavs and the Sixers beat both the Heat and Pacers, they will win Group B and advance to the knockout round. If the Sixers beat both the Heat and Pacers but the Cavs beat the Knicks, the Cavs will win Group B, and the Sixers will have to hope to sneak into the knockout round with a wild-card spot.

The Sixers' minus-19 point differential won't help them in the wild-card race, though. They'll need to run up the score against both the Heat and Pacers to give themselves a realistic chance of securing a wild-card bid.

If the Sixers fall short of the knockout round, it once and for all proves that they're frauds this year, obviously. They'll also have two games added to their schedule between Dec. 6-10 based on a "formulaic approach" that uses the group-play standings in each conference, according to the NBA.

"Two of the 22 games will be scheduled cross-conference since there will be an odd number of teams in each conference that do not advance to the quarterfinals. These cross-conference games will be scheduled between bottom- finishing teams in the Group Play stage subject to travel constraints, and no team will play more than one of its two games cross-conference. The other 20 games will be scheduled within conference featuring teams that are otherwise scheduled to play each other three times over the course of the season wherever possible."

The Sixers have never advanced to the knockout round of the NBA Cup yet, and they're facing an uphill battle to get there this year thanks to their blowout loss to the Cavs. But they aren't dead yet, especially if they beat the Heat on Wednesday and the Cavs lose to the Knicks.

So, as you gather round your family for the holidays this week, be sure to tell them what really matters: Sweating NBA Cup games on Thanksgiving Eve.
