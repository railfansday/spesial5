After his Pittsburgh exit, what's next for Aaron Rodgers?

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

As Aaron Rodgers enters waivers, the first question is whether any team will claim the balance of his one-year contract.

Obviously, no one will do it without ensuring that Rodgers actually wants to play for that team. Unless, of course, a team or two hopes to troll Rodgers by forcing him to show up to play where he doesn’t want to play. Like Marty Schottenheimer did to Deion Sanders in 2002.

So, yes, it would be hilarious if the Jets decide to put in the proper paperwork.

The more likely outcome is that he’ll clear waivers and become a free agent. It will give him the ability to sign with any team, at any time.

No clear-cut contender currently needs a starter. And it would make no sense for Rodgers to sidle up to a quarterback-needy also-ran, like the Cardinals.

Some have suggested that Rodgers could join the Patriots, who will be missing Drake Maye while they try to parlay 5-5 into playoff position. His August comments about the Mike Vrabel-Diana Russini situation would potentially be an impediment to that outcome.

There’s a spicy possibility of Rodgers aligning with the Ravens, who still play the Steelers twice. Is he that petty? At this point, it’s impossible to say he’s not.

Rodgers’s best play could be to wait. Let fate decide. Last year, Broncos quarterback Bo Nix broke his ankle late in a playoff win over the Bills. If Denver’s instant desperation caused them to consider dusting off Drew Brees (despite the fact that he can’t throw a football with his dominant arm), they would have surely called Rodgers, if he’d been available.

Something like that can happen again. Of course, a team that loses its starter to injury between now and the Super Bowl would have to want to do business with him. Which means accepting the very strong possibility that, in time, Rodgers will have grievances to air about his experience.

Because if it goes poorly, it won’t be his fault. There will be other reasons for his struggles. Reasons that he has shown he has no qualms about sharing in his national-media safe space.

During his 22-year career, Rodgers has played for three NFL teams. He ended up disgruntled in each location. If he adds a fourth franchise to the list, he’ll likely find a way to be disgruntled there, too.

Unless it’s a very short-term arrangement. As short as one or two playoff games.
