St. John’s faces daunting rebounding test against Louisville on Thanksgiving

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

LAS VEGAS — The top priority is pretty obvious for St. John's on Thanksgiving night: rebounding, rebounding and more rebounding.

That was coach Rick Pitino's biggest concern this week in Sin City, and the 13th-ranked Johnnies will have to deal with one of the best offensive rebounding teams in the country in No. 25 Louisville.

Led by twin towers Flory Bidunga and Obinna Ekezie Jr., the Cardinals are seventh nationally in offensive rebounding percentage at 44.6. They hammered No. 5 Texas Tech on the glass, 41-28, in their Tuesday night opener of the Players Era Festival.

"It's a big focus for sure. They get [almost] 50 percent of their misses back," Red Storm big man Ruben Prey said Wednesday. "We have to make that number lower."

Red Storm forward Ruben Prey (17) and Saints guard Tim Ibukunoluwa (13) fight for a loose ball during the St. John's blowout win over Siena on Nov. 18, 2026, at Carnesecca Arena. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

St. John's is familiar with the 6-foot-9 Bidunga. The Red Storm faced him in the second round of the NCAA Tournament last March while he was with Kansas.

The Red Storm did a strong job in the rebounding department Tuesday against Oregon. They were plus-4 on the boards and limited Oregon big man Malique Ewin to seven points and four rebounds. Prey outplayed him, finishing with 15 points, four rebounds and two assists. The guards rebounded well, as Quinn Ellis, Ian Jackson and Tounde Yessoufou combined for 18 boards.

But Louisville is a different animal. The Cardinals will play Bidunga and the 7-foot-2 Ekezie Jr. together at times. The onus will be on the 6-foot-11 Prey to protect the interior.

"I feel like it can still get some improvement," Prey said of his rebounding. "I'm not totally happy with it. But I also am happy with my improvement during the season. I've been getting better, and I will continue to improve."

Babacar Sane is still spending more time watching than playing. The athletically gifted forward from Senegal remains a work in progress. Pitino has had a short leash with the 6-foot-7 Sane after turnovers or missed defensive assignments. He was limited to six minutes Tuesday.

"I think he's handling it like a professional," Jackson said. "He's been great. Obviously, it's not the easiest thing to do. He's approached it well. He's coming out looking out for ways to get better, looking to understand what he's doing wrong, looking to fix it. It's really nice to watch: an older guy being able to handle something like that."
