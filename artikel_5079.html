‘Give It Up’: Saylor Fires Back at Infamous Bitcoin Critic

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

When American entrepreneur Jeffrey Tucker told Bitcoiners to stop dreaming about Bitcoin becoming the world's most valuable asset, Strategy co-founder Michael Saylor responded with the example of a gold-backed banknote.

"Give it up, Bitcoiners. There is no such thing as money that is not used in exchange," Tucker wrote on X.

Tucker argued that Bitcoiners had brought the criticism upon themselves. They made the mistake of thinking they could simply declare Bitcoin a store of value and that would somehow make it one. That is not how it works, according to Tucker. He argued that nothing in the history of money supports this idea.

In his response, Saylor started with an example involving a bakery. Suppose someone buys bread at a bakery. They pay for the bread with a gold-backed banknote. The baker can use that banknote to pay the miller.  The miller can use it to pay the farmer. All three transactions could be completed using the same banknote, while the gold stays in the vault.

@jeffreytucker Jeffrey, a gold-backed banknote could pay the baker, the miller, and the farmer while the gold stayed in the vault. Today people pay in dollars and save in gold, stocks, and real estate. Bitcoin is competing for those savings.

In the second part of his response, Saylor made an argument familiar to anyone who gets paid in dollars.

Sure, you can use dollars to buy groceries. However, you can still decide to keep some of your wealth in stocks, gold, or real estate.

"Today people pay in dollars and save in gold, stocks, and real estate. Bitcoin is competing for those savings," Saylor wrote.

This last point was less ambitious than the claim Tucker was pushing back against.

Tucker was talking about Bitcoin becoming the world's most valuable asset.

Saylor, meanwhile, was making a simpler argument about where people choose to keep their savings.

However, Saylor's gold analogy raises an interesting question for Tucker. In Saylor's example, gold is still relevant to the purchase of bread because it backs the banknote the baker accepts.

However, in his modern examples, someone who chooses to save in stocks isn't necessarily concerned about whether their local bakery accepts stocks as payment.

For Tucker, it's all about how the customer pays for a loaf of bread. For Saylor, it's about what the baker does with the money after selling it.
