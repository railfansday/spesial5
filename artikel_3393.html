Zane Lowe shares Apple Music Radio audience numbers, recalls ‘crickets’ in the early days

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

In a new interview with Billboard’s On the Record with Kristin Robinson, Apple Music’s Zane Lowe discussed the early days of Apple Music Radio and revealed just how many people tune in each month. Here are the details.

Apple is rarely terribly specific about how many people use its services, including how many tune in to its radio stations.

In 2019, for instance, Eddy Cue said Beats 1 (now Apple Music 1) had ‘tens of millions of listeners,’ but Apple has rarely provided more specific figures since.

Apple Music’s Zane Lowe, however, has now offered a more concrete figure, revealing that the service reaches over 50 million unique listeners every month:

We were never, you know, delusional enough to assume we were going to get a huge audience overnight. We’d have to build it like anyone else would have to build anything. But we’ve just got to a point in our 11 years on where we now, overall, reach over 50 million unique listeners a month, which is a really large number for us, and it’s growing exponentially and really fast.

The revelation came in a wide-ranging interview published today with Billboard’s On the Record with Kristin Robinson, where Lowe discussed Apple Music Radio’s early struggles, its approach to supporting emerging artists, and why human curation remains central to the service.

When discussing the early days at Apple Music, Lowe recalled:

When we started the radio here, I didn’t have access or opportunity with a lot of the artists that I built relationships with from the BBC because we were unproven. And I came over here thinking, “Well, I’ve got Jimmy Iovine over here, and I’ve got Apple over here and I feel unstoppable, and who’s not going to want to come and launch this thing with us?” But it was kind of crickets… As upsetting as that was at the time, two good things came out of it. Number one, it snapped me back into the reality force field of building something from scratch. And number two, it made us focus really clearly and in a big way on new and emerging artists.

He also mentioned some of the first artists Apple Music Radio supported, including Billie Eilish with “Ocean Eyes,” Khalid with American Teen, and Bryson Tiller with his first song.

At the time, Lowe noted, these artists were just getting started, and supporting emerging talent became a defining part of Apple Music Radio’s approach, one that continues to this day:

The really cool number is on our radio network. We played — this year alone — over 6,500 individual artists, which is not a commercial radio model. [Other radio networks’] playlists are very small, and the amount of plays is very great. Our playlist is gigantic. We play a lot of different artists, and over half of those 6,500 artists we’ve played in one year are new or emerging. So, think about 3,300 artists, roughly, getting played, who may be on their first song, their first EP, their first album…It’s proof of concept. It’s great.

Still on the subject of the early days at Apple Music, Lowe said “it was very clear [from the start] that that was very important to everybody who was responsible for bringing Apple Music to life,” adding that “serving artists and the artistic community in a way that is of most value and of most service” still drives “a lot of the decision-making” at the platform.

When the conversation turned to Apple Music’s recent rebranding, which introduced a new logo physically created using sound waves from a composition by Fred again.., Lowe said he couldn’t speak to the decisions behind it, but stressed the human element and the creative process that went into it:

We could have created that in a way that could utilize all of the tech available. We could type that into a prompt. But at the end of the day, seeing how it’s made is so much more additionally inspiring than just seeing it. It speaks to, I think, what we try to do here, which is, we love the process of what we do, and the result is a byproduct of getting to do it and loving to do it.

The issue of algorithms also came up when Lowe discussed Apple Music’s approach to music discovery, with the executive emphasizing the importance of human curation over automated recommendations:

Well, we don’t want to say that too loudly, because someone could be like, “Wait a minute, we could cut costs here? Man, how did we not think of that!” Look, I mean, jokes aside, we’ve been incredibly fortunate, and I’ve been incredibly fortunate to take that step from the BBC into a company and to work with people that value exactly what it is you’re describing: this idea of place and this experience of sharing things with each other, and how meaningful it is when someone shares a record with you and it comes with context. It’s a gift…
