'Suits' Meets 'The Lincoln Lawyer' in Paramount+'s Near-Perfect Procedural

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Few shows have had as exciting a journey as USA's Suits. The series, which aired nine seasons from 2011 to 2019, was a success from the very beginning. With twisty storylines and lovable, messy characters, the legal drama attracted legions of fans. Suits' popularity grew even further after the show's original conclusion when it became available to stream on Netflix, earning millions of obsessed viewers. Since then, audiences have had streaming series like The Lincoln Lawyer to fill the Suits-sized hole in their hearts, but were missing a network legal drama practically designed to keep them tuning in — until now.

Robert and Michelle King have been crushing the procedural format. With acclaimed series such as The Good Wife, Evil, and Elsbeth, the husband-and-wife creative team has delivered some of the best television in recent years. The duo's new CBS show, Cupertino, which premiered on October 8, is a fast-paced legal drama about a pair of underdog lawyers who decide to take on some of Silicon Valley's biggest tech companies. The series is set in the titular Cupertino, California, where tech bros have created a new class of entitled, wealthy foes whose power is currently going unchecked.

Michael Price (past Evil collaborator Mike Colter) has been dismissed by his employer, but gets the idea to start a brand-new firm alongside gullible attorney Olivia Siffre (Rachel Keller). Even though the two make a good team, their journey to success is far from easy. Each episode features a new case for the lawyers to take on, with overarching storylines that carry throughout the season. With a 100% Rotten Tomatoes score, Cupertino is a must-watch.

Every legal drama runs the risk of falling into the same patterns of generic predictability. From the very first episode, it's clear that Cupertino is in a class of its own. The Kings and the show's writing team have crafted storylines that feel almost terrifyingly prescient. By covering wildly timely issues like the far-reaching impacts of AI chatbots, voice cloning, military drone catastrophes, and an AGI deepfake that seems to have a mind of its own, Cupertino tackles problems that are already becoming embedded in our lives. It's not just that the writing feels like it's diving into real-time issues; it's also somehow predicting near-future crises, as well as delivering action-packed storylines. There are plenty of twists and turns to keep things interesting, and the characters are written so smartly that you can't help but be sucked into their stories.

Cupertino is already entertaining because of the writing, but the actors are really where the show thrives. Colter has developed Michael into a perfect protagonist, with intelligence, integrity, and smoldering good looks. Keller also holds her own as the naive and inexperienced Olivia, and their platonic chemistry helps to create a partnership that actually feels believable.

As strong as its leads are, Cupertino also features talented supporting actors that help the show flourish. Ella Stiller, who plays the firm's quirky yet devoted assistant, is able to build on her comedic timing from HBO's The Comeback. The always-game Busy Philipps is reunited with her Girls5Eva co-star Renée Elise Goldsberry, and both actors bring extra charisma and wry humor to the series. Joe Morton also pops up as Michael's father in a role that will expand in the future, and Broadway veteran Rob McClure appears as an eerily realistic character.

Cupertino is already doing well in the ratings, ensuring that the era of network television is far from over. There will surely be many more intriguing cases to follow as Michael and Olivia pursue their David vs. Goliath battles. The Kings are continuing to demonstrate their genius when it comes to crafting stories that feel inherently captivating, and we can't wait to see what else Cupertino tackles in the courtroom.

Cupertino airs Thursday nights on CBS and is available to stream on Paramount+ in the U.S.
