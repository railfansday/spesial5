Repaying a Bitcoin-backed loan on Sui can leave you waiting to withdraw native BTC

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Fluid borrowers reclaim hBTC after repayment. Redeeming that token for native Bitcoin goes through Hashi, which limits the pace and size of BTC withdrawals.

Once a Fluid borrower has repaid their USDC loan and reclaimed eligible hBTC collateral, they hold a Bitcoin-backed token on Sui. To get native Bitcoin into their wallet, they need a separate redemption from Hashi, the protocol that issues hBTC. Hashi’s payout limit can hold up an approved withdrawal even when the pool has enough BTC to cover it.

Fluid announced its hBTC lending markets on Nov. 20, a day after the Sui Foundation announced the market was live. Bitcoin holders can deposit BTC with Hashi, receive hBTC on the Sui network and pledge that token to borrow USDC, the first stablecoin available through the market.

The underlying coins remain on Bitcoin at an address derived for the depositor’s Sui account. Hashi’s deposit guide describes who can spend them in the normal withdrawal process. Validators in Hashi’s committee provide one authorization collectively, and a separate signer called the Guardian provides the other. Once the borrower has the hBTC back on Sui, those signers still have to authorize the Bitcoin payout.

Once a borrower has the hBTC back, they can request a withdrawal on Sui by submitting the amount and a compatible Bitcoin address. Hashi sets the hBTC aside and queues the request while it waits for a quorum of committee members to approve the request. The committee leader gathers approved requests into a batch so several users can receive Bitcoin in a single transaction.

Hashi burns the hBTC when the leader commits the batch. The committee jointly produces one signature for each Bitcoin input the batch spends. The Guardian checks the batch against its limit and supplies a second signature for each input. Once the transaction is fully signed, the leader broadcasts it, with each user’s share of the miner fee deducted from their payout. Hashi marks the withdrawal confirmed after the transaction reaches its required number of Bitcoin confirmations.

The Guardian’s withdrawal limit has two settings. One sets the maximum amount the system can release at once. The other determines how quickly capacity refills after a payout batch uses it. The pool balance measures the Bitcoin held by Hashi, while the limit governs how much of that Bitcoin can move at a given time.

If an approved request is within the maximum but is too large for the capacity available at that moment, it waits for room to open up. Requests behind it can wait too. Hashi skips requests above the maximum until governance raises the limit. The borrower can cancel and recover their hBTC after a cooldown, up until the leader commits the request to a batch.

Hashi describes roughly five minutes from submission as one trigger for building a batch. The full withdrawal also depends on approval, available payout capacity, signatures and Bitcoin confirmations. Hashi’s withdrawal FAQ describes how to check current capacity. The deployed maximum and refill rate remain unverified, as does the time from repayment to a Bitcoin payout for a Fluid borrower.

On Nov. 21, Merkl’s Fluid lending campaign displayed $862.26K in total value locked in its Sui fUSDC rewards opportunity. The figure captures activity on the lender side of the market. The amount of USDC borrowers have drawn against hBTC remains unverified.

Fluid says Anchorage Digital’s institutional clients can lend USDC or borrow it against hBTC on the protocol. Anchorage Digital Bank N.A. provides custody and settlement services. The Sui Foundation says institutions can already use Anchorage’s self-custody wallet, Porto, to access hBTC and select Fluid strategies. It expects tri-party settlement in early 2027. Anchorage identifies Atlas as its tri-party route for institutions that keep assets in qualified custody.
