Senior Infrastructure Engineer II at Kit

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Kit is the email marketing platform experts choose to grow their audience, deepen their relationships, and build a business around the work they love. For coaches, YouTubers, authors, podcasters, and other creatives, there isn't a better marketing hub to rely on to grow audiences, automate email marketing, and sell digital products — all within one platform.More importantly, there isn't a team more committed to helping creators earn a living. We're on a mission to help creators earn $1 billion using our creator marketing platform. We have always been 100% independent and 100% remote.We are proud to have built a product that our customers love, and we look for people who have enthusiasm and belief in our mission, vision, and values to join our team. We're also embracing AI thoughtfully — both in how we build and how we hire to ensure our team is adaptable, innovative, and ready for what's next.

We're looking for a Senior Infrastructure Engineer II to take ownership of a large share of Kit's core infrastructure: databases, Kubernetes, version upgrades, platform tooling, and day-to-day support. You'll also build the paved paths that let engineers, non-engineers, and AI agents test and ship changes safely.

The infrastructure supports creators who depend on Kit to reach their audiences and earn a living. When it's healthy, nobody notices. That's the job.

This is a full-time Individual Contributor role for an experienced infrastructure engineer who likes owning production systems end to end, takes on-call seriously, leans into automation and enjoys making other people's work easier.

You'll report to Mojtaba Hosseini, VP of Engineering at Kit. Day to day, you'll work most closely with Kris Hamoud and Ivan Mora Perez.

You'll also partner with product engineers across Kit, and with teams outside engineering who rely on infrastructure to get their work done. Once you're ramped up, you'll join a three-person on-call rotation, which works out to roughly one week in three.

First Week: Get access to our AWS accounts, Kubernetes clusters, monitoring, and CI/CD pipelines. Read through our runbooks and architecture docs. Take part in Get To Know Yous (GTKYs) with Kris, Ivan, and the engineering teams you'll support.

First Month: Pick up day-to-day infrastructure support requests and start working through them with Kris and Ivan as backup. Ship your first improvement, such as a version upgrade or a fix to a gap in monitoring. Shadow on-call shifts. Write down what you learn along the way, especially the parts that only lived in someone's head or those that can be automated or improved.

First Six Months: Own core infrastructure areas end to end: databases, Kubernetes, version upgrades, and platform tooling. Take your full share of on-call, which you'll start by the end of your first quarter. Become the go-to person for day-to-day infrastructure questions. Find one recurring source of pages and make it go away. Ship a first paved path, such as a sandbox environment where AI agents or non-engineers can safely build and test changes.

First Year: You're the primary owner of a large part of Kit's core infrastructure, and you lead projects in it without needing direction. Uptime stays at 99.9% or better, page volume is down, and you've found real cost savings. Your paved paths are in regular use by engineers, non-engineers, and AI agents. You've built observability good enough for AI agents to handle some on-call load. You've documented your decisions so others can build on your work.

Running production infrastructure on AWS at scale, including managed relational databases, networking, and load balancing

Operating Kubernetes in production: upgrades, capacity planning, and troubleshooting

Managing infrastructure as code and maintaining CI/CD pipelines that engineers trust

Building monitoring and alerting that catches real problems early and doesn't page people for noise

Diagnosing complex, multi-service incidents calmly, then fixing the underlying cause

Cutting cloud costs through right-sizing and reserved capacity without hurting reliability

Writing clear runbooks, docs, and decision write-ups

Nice to have: experience with Cloudflare, ephemeral or sandbox environments, or email sending infrastructure

Around 7 or more years in infrastructure, platform, DevOps, or SRE roles

Owning production infrastructure for a product where downtime costs customers money

Carrying a pager for systems you owned, and making that on-call rotation quieter over time

Leading a major upgrade or migration, such as a database or Kubernetes version upgrade, with little or no customer impact

Building self-service tooling or paved paths that other engineers actually adopted

Working on a small infrastructure team where you couldn't hand problems off to a specialist

You own what you run. You spot infrastructure debt and fix it without waiting to be asked, and you follow through until it's done.

You build quality into the work. Upgrades have rollback plans, maintenance windows are planned around customers, and "good enough" is a decision, not a default.

You leverage AI effectively. AI helps you increase quality and velocity where it matters most. You know when to delegate and when to dive in, while holding accountability.

You make work easier for everyone else. You'd rather build a tool or write a guide once than answer the same question ten times.

You teach what you know. You write things down so the next person, or the next AI agent, can build on your work.

You work in public. You share context and tradeoffs openly, including during incidents and when things go wrong.

You use AI thoughtfully. It's part of how you work, and you help others adopt it without creating new risks.

You remember who's on the other end. An outage means a creator's launch email doesn't go out, and that shapes how you prioritize.

Wanting a well-defined backlog handed to you each week. Here, you'll find much of the work yourself and set priorities with Kris.

Preferring not to be on call. On-call is a core part of this role, shared across a team of three.

You are skeptical of AI’s capabilities, or conversely use AI without good judgement and accountability.

Wanting mostly greenfield architecture. A lot of this work is running, upgrading, and improving systems that already exist.

Needing layers of approval before acting.
