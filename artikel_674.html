SpaceX is counting down to make history with the launch of its Starship Flight 15 mission on Friday, Nov. 13, with liftoff scheduled for 5:45 p.m. EDT (2245 GMT) at Starbase, Texas. Starship Flight 15 will attempt to land both Starship Ship and Super Heavy Booster. It will be the first-ever full recovery of an orbital rocket and spacecraft, if successful.

SpaceX is now less than 24 hours from the planned launch of its Starship Flight 15 mission, with liftoff on track for Friday, Nov. 13, at 5:45 p.m. EST (2245 GMT). The test flight will attempt land both the Starship Super Heavy Booster 22 and its Ship 42 upper stage.

Because the mission will last 22.5 hours, Starship will likely be visible in the night sky to stargazers hoping to see the giant spacecraft in orbit. Space.com's Skywatching Writer Anthony Wood has a great guide on how to see Starship in the night sky.

"When it passes overhead, it will look like a bright, fast-moving star crossing the sky — similar to the International Space Station, but potentially even brighter depending on its orientation," our skywatching columnist Joe Rao told Wood.

STARBASE, Texas — We are one day until launch of SpaceX's Starship Flight 15, and Space.com is onsite here in Texas prepping cameras and coverage.

Liftoff is currently targeted for tomorrow (Nov. 13), during a 75-minute window opening at 5:45 p.m. EST (2245 GMT).

This flight could be a historic one for SpaceX and the space industry. For the first time, SpaceX will attempt to catch both stages of its massive Starship rocket — Super Heavy just minutes after launch, and the Ship upper stage roughly 22 hours later, following several orbits around Earth.

I'll be covering the action from here in Texas, so stay tuned to Space.com for updates as we count down to liftoff!
