How Hershey strategized its brand film to be more than a 90-minute ad

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Filmmakers worked with the company, gaining access to archives and historians and collaborating on IP, brand and creative considerations.

“Hershey,” a biopic of Hershey Company founder Milton S. Hershey, hits theaters on Wednesday. The film, along with the marketer’s celebration of America’s 250th anniversary, are expected to contribute almost one percentage point to company sales in 2026, CEO Kirk Tanner said on a recent earnings call. “Hershey” has so far earned strong customer and retail response.

“It's relevant for our time, so we’ve seen a lot of support and that's going to show up in-store with inventory in displays and a connection back to the movie ... That will be an uplift. That will be a tailwind for the portfolio and especially Hershey, which has already had a terrific year,” Tanner said. “We like the momentum that will build going into 2027.”

What sets “Hershey” apart from other branded films is the way that the filmmakers worked with the company, gaining access to Hershey archives and historians and collaborating on intellectual property, brand and creative considerations.

“We wanted a real story told, which means, as a marketer, you need to gulp a little bit and say, ‘Hey, it's not a story if there's no conflict.’ You only get a tiny bit of that [conflict] when you create advertisements,” said Suzanne Jones, vice president of the Hershey Experience at The Hershey Company. “We wanted to make sure this was not in any way, shape or form a 90-minute commercial.”

"Hershey” is the product of a collaboration between the Hershey entities — the combination of The Hershey Company, Milton Hershey School, Hershey Entertainment & Resorts, The M.S. Hershey Foundation and Hershey Trust Company — with production company Dandelion Media. The film was produced in association with Aloe Entertainment, Peachtree Group and RCM3 with UTA Independent Film Group.

“This is not just a story about something that happened in the past, but it is a living legacy that exists today, and not just in the candy bar, but in the Milton Hershey School,” Jones said. “There are kids across town sitting in algebra class because of what Milton and Catherine Hershey did. It's way more than the story of a founder or the story of a brand.”

The content of the “Hershey” film sets it apart from a rash of recent branded films from E.l.f., Garage Beer and Texas Pete that mostly used humor to market products and value propositions. The film focuses on the story of Milton Hershey, whose grit and perseverance led not only to a $35 billion snack company, but also a series of charitable organizations.

“Consumers today are really looking for authenticity and what a brand stands for in the products that it delivers to the marketplace, but its role in the community and in society today, as well,” Jones said.

For Hershey, the film is at the heart of an ecosystem that spans channels and its portfolio, which includes Reese's, Twizzlers and salty snacks like Dot's Pretzels. The brand on Wednesday will sell Hershey bars via Amazon and Walmart at their original 5-cent price, and will release limited-edition, vintage-inspired packaging featuring QR codes that link to more information about the Hershey story.

“As we've looked at activating against this, we've been able to … collaborate with our retail partners across the country to bring the film to life,” Jones said. “It wasn't this insurmountable lift to marry the Hershey's brand to the equity of the film because it is so authentically intertwined.”

Beyond retail, Hershey will utilize social and creator content, as well as some surprises for consumers during the holiday week. The company has been working to develop its muscle around social media, including through posts tied to cultural moments like the recent Microsoft shutdown and the release of “Grand Theft Auto VI.”

Hershey kicked off the year with its first major creative effort in eight years, launching the “It’s Your Happy Place” platform ahead of the 2026 Olympic and Paralympic Winter Games. The brand subsequently partnered with girl group Katseye on a fully integrated campaign around its new Creme Bars. The Hershey Company reported net sales growth of 3% in Q3, with North American confectionery business sales growth of 1.6%.
