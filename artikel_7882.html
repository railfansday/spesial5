Short Circuit: A roundup of recent federal court decisions

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

Geek therapy, crack felony, and some Scooby Snacks.

Please enjoy the latest edition of Short Circuit, a weekly feature written by a bunch of people at the Institute for Justice.

New on the Short Circuit podcast: We look at 100 years of zoning and chances of zoning reform, in court and in city councils, with Harvard's Molly Brady and Vice Mayor of Cambridge, Mass., Burhan Azeem.

And on the latest episode of the Law Office History podcast, Christian Burset of Penn Law explains how it was hard to be a textualist in Olde Timey England because Parliament wasn't very clear when writing its statutes and it was hard to find copies of them anyway.

Protectionism alert! This week, IJ sent a letter to officials in Clewiston, Florida, warning that the city's food truck ordinance violates the Florida Constitution. Food trucks in Clewiston may only operate on Mondays and Tuesdays and must stay at least 200 feet away from any open restaurant unless that restaurant gives written permission. Those two days are not a coincidence. Mondays and Tuesdays are when most brick-and-mortar restaurants in Clewiston are closed, meaning the ordinance permits food trucks only when they are unlikely to compete with established businesses. The 200-foot buffer zone fulfills a similar protectionist function, giving existing restaurants veto power over whether a competitor can set up nearby.
