Every Unmade & Cancelled Justice League Movie (It's Not Just Snyder's Sequels)

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The cinematic future of the Justice League hasn’t really been discussed a lot since the end of the DCEU, with DC’s most iconic team noticeably absent from James Gunn and Peter Safran’s Gods and Monsters DCU slate. But it seems that’s now about to change.

With regime change for DC on the cards again and Kevin Feige’s former protegee Jeremy Latcham replacing the outgoing Gunn and Safran, rumor has it, the new supremo has been told by the Ellisons that the new DCU priority is to get big names like Batman, Wonder Woman, and Aquaman back in business. And quickly. That would inevitably lead to a new Justice League project.

Ironically, it’s also been reported that James Gunn was also planning to build to a Justice League movie, as confirmed by Ben Fritz, who says the DCU will introduce Wonder Woman in Man of Tomorrow, Batman would then follow in The Brave and the Bold, and additional Superman sequels in 2029 and 2031 would have given way to a Justice League movie. Little else is known, but now, Gunn’s plan joins the pile of unrealized Justice League movies that have peppered DC history. And here’s the other failed attempts…

Until the Snyderverse was canceled, George Miller’s lost Justice League: Mortal was the most notorious unmade DC movie, probably alongside Tim Burton’s Superman Lives and Joel Schumacher’s Batman Unchained. That is, at least in terms of the mythology built around each. Like Superman Lives, Justice League: Mortal actually got very close to being made, before everything unraveled dramatically.

Kicking off in 2007, George Miller was signed to adapt Kieran and Michelle Mulroney’s script, and the central cast of Armie Hammer as Batman, D.J. Cotrona as Superman, Megan Gale as Wonder Woman, rapper Common as Green Lantern/John Stewart, Adam Brody as The Flash/Barry Allen, Hugh Keays-Byrne as Martian Manhunter, and Santiago Cabrera as Aquaman was set after an extensive auditions process. The main villain, Maxwell Lord, was to be played by Jay Baruchel, and Teresa Palmer was signed to play his ally Talia Al Ghul.

The plan was for filming to take place in Australia through the summer of 2008, ahead of release in 2009, but a last-minute change in tax relief put a halt to things, and supposed delays were eventually confirmed to be outright cancellation. Mortal would have brought the heroes together to form the iconic team, drawing partly from both Crisis on Infinite Earths and Infinite Crisis, and it’s always remained one of the most fascinating lost DC movies of all.

Thanks to his role in creating the iconic Batman: The Animated Series, Bruce Timm holds a special place in DC fans’ appreciation, and had he gotten his way, we would have seen three further expansions of that hugely popular world.

Just over 20 years ago, Timm’s Justice League and Justice League Unlimited were fan favorites (despite both ending before their time), and the plan originally was for the series to be bridged by a Worlds Collide movie. It would have featured the Crime Syndicate as the villains, and also would have revealed the origin of Wonder Woman’s invisible jet, but it was canceled before fully coming together. Eventually, it was retooled and became the stand-alone Justice League: Crisis on Two Earths in 2010, but it wasn’t in Timm’s continuity.

Mark this one down as wishful thinking, but in 2009, in an interview at New York Comic Con, Timm was asked which non-DC character he'd like to make part of his universe and revealed his desire to make a Marvel and DC crossover movie:

"I’m not sure bringing them into the DC Universe is necessarily something I’m dying to do, although I would like to do one DC/Marvel crossover movie. I think that would be fun. I’m sure it’s, like, absolutely impossible how tricky the rights issues are, but that would be fun to do, like the JLA vs. the Avengers or something."

In the same interview at NYCC 2009, Timm was asked about the possibility of making a Crisis on Infinity Earths movie and offered the intriguing response, "Maybe. That’s all I can say about that," suggesting there may have been talks already. Ultimately, sadly, nothing came of it, and instead, 2024 saw a Crisis on Infinite Earths movie trilogy released to end DC's animated Tomorrowverse.

Another project including the work of, and thus confirmed by, James Tucker, this direct-to-DVD animated Justice League movie was in the works around 2008 and 2009. No title was ever released, and very little is known of the project, beyond a sketch of Wonder Woman by Tucker that found its way online. the project didn't make it past the early development phase, but the sketch remains...

Before the DCEU introduced Gal Gadot as Wonder Woman, Warner Bros was apparently unconvinced that the iconic hero could carry her own movie, which feels unthinkable now. After 2009’s animated Wonder Woman, which was written by Gail Simone, failed to make a profit, WB drew up plans to give her a leading role in a movie that would avoid outright calling itself a Wonder Woman movie.

Veteran DC animator and producer James Tucker revealed to ToonZone (via THR) that they planned to use the Justice League banner to stealth release solo movies for characters deemed unable to draw on their own:

“We’ll probably do a Wonder Woman-centered Justice League movie. We’ll use the Justice League as an umbrella to focus on characters who might not be able to support DVDs of their own. That’s not my judgment, that’s based on sales. We’ll handle Wonder Woman in our own way, regardless of whether they’ll do a [live action] movie or not. I like her as a character and want to feature her as much as we can. But it will probably be in the context of a Justice League movie.”

That project never happened, as the New 52 chapter of the DC Animated Movie Universe only released an Aquaman-centric Justice League movie - Throne of Atlantis - in 2013, and then went back to more traditional ensembles until the Tomorrowverse took over in 2020. And somewhere along the line, WB executives changed their thinking on Diana, as Wonder Woman: Bloodlines was released in 2019.

At the end of 2012, early rumors began to appear that Guillermo del Toro was attached to make a Justice League Dark movie, in which he intended to feature Deadman, the Spectre, the Swamp Thing, John Constantine, the Phantom Stranger, Zatanna, Zatara, Sargon the Sorcerer, and Etrigan. By March 2013, the core team had tightened and del Toro revealed the story would have seen Constantine recruiting the Swamp Thing, Etrigan, Deadman, the Spectre, and Zatanna (with other heroes potentially involved).

As the DCEU kicked off, the director said Justice League Dark - or Dark Universe as it was then known - would have remained separate, but that too seemed to change by December of 2014. Ultimately, despite the script being completed, del Toro dropped out, Doug Liman came in and then also dropped out, and the newly titled Justice League Dark limped along for a few more years with little progress before it was confirmed to be canceled in 2023.

During the fascinating and frustrating period of DC movie history that saw Zack Snyder’s leadership of the DCEU end in tragedy and his vision for Justice League replaced by Joss Whedon’s Frankenstein’s monster of a movie, we learned of his sprawling plans for multiple Justice League movies. Those plans became central to the fan campaign to resurrect the Snyderverse, as Snyder’s original five movie plan was romanticized. When his version of Justice League finally released, the campaign grew in confidence, but ultimately, hope for the two Justice League sequels has never been satisfied. But we do know a fair bit about them...

Snyder’s vision for Justice League 2, which was originally announced with Snyder returning, would have seen Lex Luthor side with Darkseid. It would also have seen Lois Lane’s death thanks to Batman failing in his mission to protect her, and Superman’s corruption by the Anti-Life equation, and the arrival of the Knightmare timeline first shown in Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice.

With the Knightmare timeline having enveloped Earth, the trilogy ender would have been set five years after Lois’ death, and charged the surviving Justice League heroes - including a newly introduced Green Lantern - with sending The Flash back in time to warn Bruce Wayne about Lois’ death. That would have prompted Bruce to redeem his failure to save her by sacrificing himself, before a final battle with Darkseid led by the now-uncorrupted Superman.

Some day, we may get to see these sequels released as comics - or a miracle in which they’re released as Elseworlds movies - but for now all we have is Snyder’s artwork and interviews about the lost movies.

After the end of Snyder's oversight of the DCEU, Henry Cavill’s tenure as Superman looked to be complete. There was no sequel, and until Dwayne Johnson strong-armed Cavill back into play for his Black Adam cameo and the embarrassment of being publucally unhired shortly after, it looked over. In actual fact, when Walter Hamada had taken over as President of DC Films, he was developing his own plans for Cavill’s future with a project based on Crisis on Infinite Worlds.

Like the unfortunate sequence in The Flash, the movie would have brought together multiverse versions of the main Justice Leaguers. Cavill would have served as the Nick Fury-like connecting character, appearing in other DC projects to set up the event, which would have been the culmination of the DCEU. Ultimately, though, the merger of Warner Bros. and Discovery and David Zaslav’s arrival spelled the end for Hamada and the project was permanently shelved.

As James Gunn and Peter Safran's tenure in charge of DC kicked off, the Tomorrowverse was still active, with Justice League: Warworld released in 2023 and the Crisis on Infinite Earths trilogy following in 2024. Has the Warworld creative team of producers Jim Krieg, Jeff Wamester, and Butch had their way, they would have made another movie in the timeline, but Gunn and Safran seemingly nixed it. They told ScreenRant exclusively: "We originally talked about 'Kingdom Come' after this, but changing regimes, we couldn’t go forward with anything like that."
