Stacy Keach, ‘Fat City’ and ‘Mike Hammer’ Star, Dead at 85

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Stacy Keach, the highly prolific screen and stage actor known to audiences for his television role as the private detective Mike Hammer and other tough guy characters, died of heart failure on Tuesday at his family residence in Magdalenka, Poland. He was 85.

Keach’s death was confirmed by his wife, Malgosia, in a statement sent by representation at Guttman Associates.

“It is with profound sadness and a broken heart that I announce the passing of my darling husband, Stacy Keach, who passed away on November 10, 2026, at our family home in Magdalenka, Poland,” reads the release. “After a long and difficult battle with a serious heart condition and heart failure, Stacy peacefully departed this world. He fought for so long, with extraordinary courage, determination and strength. She continues: “Stacy was not only a legendary actor, admired and loved by audiences the world over, but also my beloved husband, my companion and the heart of our family. His passing leaves an immeasurable emptiness in our lives. But his extraordinary spirit, his unforgettable voice and the love he gave so generously will remain with us forever.”

Keach rose to the stature of one of the preeminent classical stage actors in the United States through the ’60s and 70s, venturing into avant garde productions and playing roles like Falstaff and Hamlet in his early career. He first appeared on Broadway in 1969, playing Buffalo Bill in Arthur Kopit’s “Indians,” for which he received a Tony nomination. He was also a four-time Drama Desk award winner.

It was after establishing his onstage bona fides that Keach was tapped to play the detective Mike Hammer for CBS’ television film “Murder Me, Murder You,” adapting the character of Mickey Spillane novels that had previously been brought to the screen in noirs like “Kiss Me Deadly.” The film spawned a three-season run of “Mickey Spillane’s Mike Hammer,” with nearly 50 episodes and two more TV movies featuring Keach as the tough PI. A revival, “Mike Hammer, Private Eye,” was produced in the late ’90s.

In film, Keach established his star in 1972 with early featured roles in John Huston’s classic boxing drama “Fat City” and Richard Fleischer’s intense cop thriller “The New Centurions,” along with a starring role of Doc Holliday in Frank Perry’s 1971 Western “Doc.” Though he never quite rose to marquee stardom, Keach’s character actor skills were called upon regularly by Hollywood, oftentimes playing military authorities in titles ranging from “Up in Smoke” to “Escape from L.A.” to “Truth.” In 1980, he starred in, co-wrote and executive produced the Walter Hill Western “The Long Riders,” alongside his brother James Keach.

Other notable credits for Keach include the lead of “Exorcist” creator William Peter Blatty’s horror oddity “The Ninth Configuration” (1980), the voice of the mysterious villain Phantasm in the classic superhero animated feature “Batman: Mask of the Phantasm” (1993), and the Bakersfield-loving father to George Clooney’s movie star in “Jay Kelly” (2025).

Born June 2, 1941 in Savannah, Ga., Walter Stacy Keach Jr.’s family moved to Pasadena after his drama teacher father signed a day-player contract with Universal. Keach attended University of Calfornia, Berkeley and later studied theater at Yale School of Drama and the London Academy of Music and Dramatic Art.

Along with Malogisa, Keach is also survived by his their children, Karolina and Shannon; their grandchildren; their spouses; and his brother, James.
