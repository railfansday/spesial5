The Berkshires Are Made for a Soft-Life Getaway

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Two hours from New York City, this corner of Massachusetts pairs farm-fresh finds and open-air beauty with restorative retreats

Every Saturday from May to November, Great Barrington has a popular farmers’ market. Lines of local Berkshirites, visitors, little kids, and friendly dogs on leashes gather under packed canopies to buy a vibrant mix of fresh fruits, vegetables, flowers, and more. An older woman overheard me talking to my husband—in town for our end-of-summer vacation—about honeycrisps and insisted we go to the opposite end of the market to Maynard Farms’s booth. The family-owned orchard carries a diverse range of apples and “amazing stone fruits” she swore our excitable toddlers would love. She was right—the boys devoured nearly all of the produce before we left the market.

Last August, in the final warm days of summer, we drove winding highways through Hudson River mountains to the westernmost area of Massachusetts: a lush and culturally rich destination composed of 30 towns and two cities, collectively known as the Berkshires and located just two hours outside of New York City.

Lenox, the town we stayed in, is a quaint enclave that boasts multimillion-dollar Cape Cods, luxury day resorts, golfing, country clubs, and farm-to-table restaurants. It’s an ideal place for a weekend getaway with friends, a romantic escape, or a family trip with your littles to touch well-landscaped grass.

We stayed in a newly built rental home, directly across the street from the Boston Symphony Orchestra at Tanglewood. In the evenings, if you’re within three miles of the parking lot, you can hear concerts from world-renowned artists. “Whether you're sitting in the front row enjoying James Taylor in the Koussevitzky Music Shed or sharing a candlelit picnic under the stars on the lawn, there is something magical about being here,” says Amy Aldrich, senior director of patron experience at Tanglewood. “We take great pride in creating a welcoming atmosphere where world-class music and the beauty of nature come together to create something truly special.”

Well-to-do families have long escaped the hustle of metropolitan New England cities to decompress in the Berkshires because of its natural beauty. Author Edith Wharton, who chronicled the sordid lives of Gilded Age elites, built her 16,000-square-foot mansion and expansive garden estate in Lenox in 1902.

Back in the day, when affluent housewives needed a respite, they’d go to the “spa.” I never fully understood the older meaning of the term until I visited Miraval Berkshires, a 380-acre resort that boasts ancient Eastern treatments, healing equine therapy sessions, meditation classes, macronutrient dining, sound baths, astrological readings, and much more. It’s an adults-only, device-free space, so disconnecting from stress is mandatory.

Equestrian activities were a common theme for us during the trip. HomeFarm at Undermountain, run by executive director Lori Pestana, is a gem of a property. The animal sanctuary is dedicated to caring for and maintaining the health of horses—but also houses goats, sheep, and donkeys. “It’s a place where life slows down, where people reconnect with nature, with animals, and often with themselves,” Pestana says. “Whether visitors come to ride, explore, learn, or simply experience the peaceful rhythm of farm life, they leave with something they didn’t necessarily know they were looking for—a sense of belonging, joy, and connection.”

Pestana’s small but dedicated staff offers riding lessons and a holistic view of what restoration can look like with a lot of care.

For dining, expect most restaurants to serve farm-to-table dishes that are sourced from the seasonal produce and livestock being raised in the area. You couldn’t possibly get a closer farm-to-table experience than Highlawn Farms, which has delicious cheeses, ice cream, and milk made from the same cows grazing its property. It’s a general rule of thumb in my family that we never pass up the opportunity to have a wood-fired pizza—Pizzeria Boema has a chic Scandinavian interior and New England quaint courtyard, serving the crispy, flavor-filled pies made with locally sourced mozzarella. And if you’re more interested in a fine-dining experience, Cello, run by couple Ryan and Kim Boya, takes a contemporary approach to comfort food. The 29-seat intimate restaurant has a thoughtfully curated menu of autochthonous plates like oyster mushroom pappardelle and a honey-nut squash served with goat cheese, pickled poblanos, granola, and a drizzle of hot honey.

Wildlife is ever-present in the Berkshires, from the calming transparently clear waters of Lenox Beach to the newly renovated aquarium wing of the Berkshire Museum in Pittsfield. Out the window of our car, driving from town to town, we saw peaceful creeks, grazing deer, and chirping birds. I soaked it all in, even stopping at the side of Glendale Road in Stockbridge to take in the beauty of the Housatonic River.

In recent years, because of the chaos of flying with toddlers, I’ve come to really appreciate the convenience of going to places just outside of New York City. I just want a soft life—or one as soft as humanly possible. With a good playlist on deck (we lean toward Afrojazz or Bad Bunny), we strap the kids into their car seats and roll. Open space and calm have to be on the itinerary.

There are many places we could go, but this experience in the Berkshires really stuck with me because it offered a quiet luxury that doesn’t position itself as high-maintenance or arrogant. There’s an ease and openness to this place, and if nothing else, an amazing peck of apples I’ll be looking forward to on my next visit.
