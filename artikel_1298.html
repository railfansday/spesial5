Former Man United star Darron Gibson breaks down in tears as wife Danielle recalls horrifying moment she feared he'd died amid his nine-year battle with sleeping tablet addiction: 'He had blue lips'

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Published: 05:50 EST, 26 November 2026 | Updated: 08:38 EST, 26 November 2026

Former Man United midfielder Darron Gibson has revealed the harrowing extent of his sleeping pill addiction and broke down in tears as his wife recalled the moment she thought he had died.

Gibson, who played for United 31 times between 2005-2012, secretly battled against anxiety during his career and turned to sleeping pills to help switch his mind off.

He began taking the pills after suffering a knee injury for the Republic of Ireland in 2013 and developed an addiction that led to him consuming 14 every night and on one occasion led to wife Danielle thinking he'd died.

Opening up on RTE’s Howler podcast, Danielle remembered her husband suffering a seizure in 2021, saying: 'When the ambulance staff came in, he had blue lips, I thought he had gone. I was being treated for shock. I just thought: "At least he’s not struggling anymore." Next minute (sharp intake of breath), he's back.'

Gibson replied: 'Ultimately, if that hadn’t have happened, I wouldn’t be here now.'

Danielle then said: 'I think you’d be dead', prompting Gibson to break down in tears, admitting 'probably'.

Darron Gibson (left) spoke about suffering from sleeping pill addiction and his wife Danielle (right) opened up about thinking he'd died in 2021 after a seizure

Gibson broke down in tears at the painful memory but is now far happier and in recovery

Gibson was convicted of drink driving in 2015 during his downward spiral and was playing for Everton at the time

He added: 'Looking back, who takes five sleeping tablets at 10 in the morning and doesn’t go to sleep? It’s ridiculous. But it’s where I was. It’s what I was doing.

'I think Everton thought they were looking after me by not punishing me, which I really appreciated at the time.

'And I don’t blame them for any of this, but I think if they’d punished me, maybe I would have changed. But I didn’t and kept going.'

Three years later at Sunderland, he consumed a mix of pills and vodka before crashing into parked cars. He was spared prison time but had a 40-month driving ban and the Black Cats ripped up his contract.

He recalled: 'Took a few sleeping tablets, thought I was going to go to sleep. Next thing I know, I’m in handcuffs in the back of a police van.'

Danielle, who worked as a specialist in addiction services while Gibson was at Sunderland, says Gibson was one of many with similar issues in the professional game.

She went on: 'I understand that football is a business. I just think they should have had a duty of care.

'He’s not the only one that was struggling, there were so many lads, whether it be sleeping tablets or mental health or depression or anxiety or booze.

'There were so many that were almost denied access to services that I feel should have been available to all of them.'

Gibson is now in recovery and says he is in a far happier state of mind than in his playing days. He ended his career at Salford City five years ago following a brief spell at Wigan.
