British doubles No 1 criticises ATP bosses at trophy presentation absence

British doubles No 1 Henry Patten has taken a swipe at ATP bosses for their absence from a trophy presentation in Turin on Monday.

Upon receiving the year-end doubles No 1 trophy from chief tour officer Andre Silva, alongside his Finnish partner Harri Heliovaara, Patten called out the ATP chairman Andrea Gaudenzi and CEO Eno Polo.

"For us, it's a really big deal. It's a real shame that the ATP chairman and CEO don't think the same." Patten said.

"It would have been nice to have them on court with us today, but here we are by ourselves."

Sky Sports News has contacted the ATP Tour for comment.

Patten and Heliovaara enjoyed a sensational season, winning Wimbledon for the second time in their careers and lifting trophies in Adelaide, Doha, Dubai, and Madrid.

The duo have repeatedly criticised the ATP's approach to doubles, including their proposals that would include smaller draws and reduce prize money for the 2028 season.

Patten and Dutch player Sem Verbeek spearheaded an open letter, signed by over a hundred other players - including former champions Bob and Mike Bryan - calling for the reform project to be scrapped.

Speaking to Sky Sports Tennis, Tim Henman said: "He [Patten] made his thoughts pretty clear about the lack of representation from the chair and the CEO on the tour.

"There is the conversation around massive reform in the doubles world, potentially cutting the doubles draw in half and that would save prize money that could go into the singles, so those doubles players are feeling like their jobs are in jeopardy.

"This is a great platform as the doubles is getting incredible support."

Halving draw sizes. ATP Masters 1000 doubles draws would be cut in half to 16 teams, while smaller ATP 500 and 250 tournaments would drop to just eight teams Cutting prize money. The share of tournament prize money allocated to doubles would drop from 20% to 10%, shifting roughly $20 million (£15 million) away from doubles specialists to fund early-round singles payouts

Heliovaara also addressed the crowd, thanking Patten and their team, saying: "I'm going to say that Henry is the best partner in the world for doubles. I've been lucky enough to play with him for two and a half years.

"We shared a lot of wins, a lot of losses, but most of all, a lot of time everywhere in the world, really. On the court, off court, it's been a very nice, amazing trip together. But it's not just the two of us, I think it's also our team there in the box, in the spotlight today.

"I would like that we also give a big hand to every one of them. It's a lot of sacrifices from us, from the team, and obviously our families at home. Everybody who has helped us to achieve something like this. And to have a trophy like that at home, we will be very proud for a very long time."

Patten and Heliovaara will now turn their attention to the task at hand in Turin, where they are 1-0 in round-robin play. They defeated Orlando Luz and Rafael Matos in a match tie-break on Sunday.
