The referral source real estate agents overlook is sitting in the next office

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

A durable client pipeline can come from agent referral partners. Start with colleagues in your brokerage, then expand through events, committees and cross market relationships built on follow through.  AI Summary

I’ve spent more than 25 years in this business, and one pattern has held strong in every market I’ve worked in: the agents who build lasting careers rarely do it alone. They have a network of fellow agents who think of them first when a client needs help. At The Agency, we were built on that idea. We encourage our agents to reach across the aisle, even to competitors, because when agents collaborate, more deals get done and clients are better served.

Referrals between agents get less attention than they deserve. We talk constantly about our sphere of influence, past clients and social media. Yet a colleague who’s relocating a client to your market, or who’s overbooked and can’t take on a new listing, might hand you a deal worth far more than a month of lead generation. Those opportunities only come to agents who’ve earned a place in someone’s mind.

Your brokerage is the most natural place to begin, and it’s often the most overlooked. Most of us share an office, a leadership team and a set of tools with dozens or even hundreds of agents, and many of us barely know their names.

Introduce yourself. Show up at the sales meeting, the training and the broker open, and stay for the conversation afterward. Ask people what they specialize in, what kind of client they love working with, and what they’d happily hand off. You’ll learn who’s the go-to for condos, who knows the hillside neighborhoods best, and who’s the strongest negotiator in the building.

That knowledge pays off quickly. When a client mentions a need outside your expertise, you’ll know exactly who to call. When a colleague gets a request that fits your strengths, they’ll know to call you. Trust inside a brokerage builds slowly, but once it’s there, it makes everyone’s business stronger.

Competition is part of this industry, and I won’t pretend otherwise. But the agent across the street at another firm is also a potential partner, and the best ones treat them that way.

Think about how much of real estate is geographic. Clients move across the country, across the state, sometimes across town into a different market. Your client in Los Angeles who’s buying in Aspen needs someone they can trust on the ground, and you can’t be that person. A thoughtful agent in Aspen, whom you’ve met and vetted, can. The same goes in reverse. That’s a referral built on mutual respect, and it often leads to repeat business flowing both ways for years.

Industry events, local board committees and MLS involvement are all excellent places to meet these people. You see how people handle disagreement, how they treat staff, and how they follow through. That’s the kind of insight no online profile will give you.

If you want referrals, you have to be worth referring. When one agent sends a client to another, they’re putting their own reputation on the line. They want to know that their client will be taken care of, that communication will be prompt, and that the deal will be handled with integrity.

So treat every interaction with another agent as an audition, whether or not it’s a referral situation. Return calls quickly. Show up prepared. Be the person who makes a tough negotiation easier instead of harder. Agents remember who was gracious under pressure, and they remember who wasn’t.

Close the loop, too. If someone sends you a client, keep them updated and thank them, even when the deal takes months. It sounds small, but plenty of agents forget, and the ones who don’t stand out.

I’d encourage agents to build  a simple structure around this. Set a goal to connect with a few new agents each month, one or two inside your brokerage and a few outside it. Grab coffee or hop on a quick call. Send a note when you see a colleague’s listing go under contract. Ask how you can be useful before you ask for anything.

It doesn’t take much time, just consistency and a willingness to see other agents as allies. Your next client might be working with an agent down the hall right now, or with one in a brokerage across the country. Sooner or later, that client will ask for a recommendation. Make sure the agent who gets asked already knows you.

Rainy Hake Austin is a brokerage leader at The Agency.
