Steelers' Max Iheanachor in spotlight in matchup with Eagles' Makai Lemon

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Pittsburgh Steelers will travel across Pennsylvania to take on the Philadelphia Eagles in what they hope will be a bounce-back NFL Week 11 matchup on Nov. 22 at Lincoln Financial Field.

First-year coach Mike McCarthy's team struggled on both sides of the ball in a Week 10 blowout loss to the AFC North rival Cincinnati Bengals. Pittsburgh now trails the division race by a game.

Now the Steelers will look to stay close in the standings by beating a typically elite Eagles team that has gotten off to an uncharacteristically slow start to 2026 with a 2-7 record.

Here's USA TODAY's look at storylines to watch as the action unfolds.

The Steelers infamously called Lemon to tell him they were selecting him with the No. 21 overall pick in the NFL draft in Pittsburgh this past April. That was before the Eagles swooped in to take him at No. 20 via trade.

Optically, it was a major embarrassment for general manager Omar Khan's front office, but his pivot to selecting his now starting right tackle out of Arizona State instead has not been.

Iheanachor earned his spot in the lineup by Week 3, and his arrival coincided with quarterback Aaron Rodgers, running back Jaylen Warren and the offense having a lot more success than they did in the first two games, when they scored just a single offensive touchdown.

The weeks since have included their growing pains. Iheanachor has allowed five sacks, four QB hits and 21 total pressures in seven games, per Pro Football Focus data. Evaluators with that scouting website give him a 66.7 grade for the season, however. That makes him already a substantial upgrade over Broderick Jones, the 2023 first-round pick who started the past three years but never posted a grade above 58.7 before being traded to Dallas in the preseason.

Lemon is Philadelphia's leading receiver with 37 receptions for 312 yards and a touchdown. That puts him only slightly ahead of Steelers second-round pick Germie Bernard statistically. The latter has 32 catches for 284 yards and a score.

Now we'll get to see both on the same field in South Philly, so the subplot of which player performs better will be an intriguing one.

Receivers Roman Wilson and Michael Pittman Jr. were both designated to return from injured reserve, opening a 21-day window in which they can practice and eventually be added back to the active roster.

The passing game needs them back. Top receiver DK Metcalf has been mostly neutralized in the past two games. His two catches against the Bengals came after the game was well out of reach. He also has among the most drops in the NFL. Bernard, meanwhile, has just 30 yards to his name in that same span.

Pittman was acquired in an offseason trade to help draw coverage away from Metcalf while contributing reliable No. 2 production of his own, but he's only seen the field in three games this season.

Wilson is the speedy separator quarterback Aaron Rodgers could use for some of the downfield throws that simply haven't been hitting lately, save for DK Metcalf's long catch in the win against New Orleans in Paris.

If either or both play, keep an eye on how quickly they're able to get up to speed and change the calculus for Rodgers.

The first-year defensive coordinator's unit failed the test of facing Bengals quarterback Joe Burrow and dangerous receivers Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins on pretty much all fronts.

The Steelers had previously done a nice job of taking away explosive pass plays and forcing quarterbacks to beat them with consistency underneath. Burrow did the latter while also beating Asante Samuel Jr. for long passes to Chase on multiple occasions to make matters much worse.

The concern for the Steelers now is whether that was a product of Burrow's individual brilliance, or whether the scheme may be getting exposed a bit now that the league has had some time to react to it.

The Eagles present an interesting challenge in that regard. Super Bowl-winning quarterback Jalen Hurts is on injured reserves because of blood clots. Joe Milton will make the start in this game instead with little NFL experience to draw from.

He has useful targets to work with, though, in the form of Lemon, fellow receiver Devonta Smith, tight end Dallas Goedert and, of course, the incomparable running back Saquon Barkley.

The Steelers can't take this team lightly two years removed from winning a Super Bowl. And not with the playbook for success against them that they put on tape last week.
