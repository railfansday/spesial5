CGI: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Acquisitions and managed services are reshaping the business, but the article leaves open whether its growth can keep pace with its buying spree.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 26, 2026 at 6:07PM EST

On its fourth-quarter fiscal 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 11, 2026, CGI (GIB +1.40%) announced sequential revenue expansion driven by business acquisitions and managed services contract wins. For long-term investors, the company's expanding backlog of $32.1 billion in contracted revenue provides steady multiyear cash flow visibility. Its operational strategy balances organic systems development with selective corporate acquisitions. Three key takeaways from management's comments highlight where the business is heading.

Long-term managed services, where clients outsource technology operations to CGI, provide predictable revenue streams that insulate the enterprise from short-term spending slowdowns. CGI reached an annual peak in managed services revenue as contracts signed earlier in the year transitioned from initial setup into full operational delivery.

Public-sector demand is a primary growth engine for U.S. federal and state agencies. Government contracts accounted for a majority of new orders during the quarter, supported by high customer interest in CGI's specialized public sector intellectual property (IP), which refers to proprietary software solutions built for specific agency workflows.

To complement systems integration and consulting (SI&C), in which consultants integrate third-party software and build custom IT infrastructure, CGI relies on niche acquisitions to expand its technical capabilities and regional presence. The company completed five mergers during fiscal 2026 and plans to maintain an active buying cadence backed by low debt leverage.

Client demand across core technology service lines continues to increase, supported by expanding global alliance partnerships. CGI relies on industry expertise and multiyear enterprise relationships to capture digital transformation projects while maintaining strict project pricing discipline.

Strong operational cash flow and low debt leverage give CGI significant flexibility to pursue long-term shareholder returns. Management is balancing ongoing business acquisitions with share repurchasing programs and regular cash dividend payouts.
