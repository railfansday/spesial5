Giants have golden chance to validate their record against ‘really good’ Jaguars

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

That depends on how you interpreted John Harbaugh's rapid-fire response to what seemed like a fairly innocuous question: What have you seen from the Jaguars that helped them become a top team?

"I don't care,'' Harbaugh said. "I could care less.''

What at first sounded like a dismissive retort was actually Harbaugh alerting all who were paying attention that he was not keen on delving into how the Jaguars were built or analyzing how good a team they've become.

"That sort of analysis about why they're where, or one of our opponents, we don't even look at that,'' Harbaugh said. "All you do is you look at the team they are right now, the opponent that we're facing.''

It was Harbaugh's way of putting a pin in the notion that what comes next for the Giants represents some comprehensive litmus test, a barometer for what the Giants are and what they might become. If Harbaugh wanted to send a message about what Sunday's game with the Jaguars at MetLife Stadium means to the Giants, it is this: They are a quality team, we can be a quality team, and whoever plays better will come out with the victory.

Giants head coach John Harbaugh speaks at a press conference in East Rutherford, N.J., Nov. 18, 2026. Robert Sabo for NY Post

"They're good,'' Harbaugh said before quickly listing the Jaguars' "good stuff" on offense, defense and special teams.

"I mean, we could go on here for a while,'' Harbaugh said, clearly having some fun with this.

Then, Harbaugh made all Giants fans of a certain age happy by bringing up the famous Bill Parcells quote: You are what your record says you are.

"So the Jaguars are really good,'' Harbaugh said. "Their record says so.''

There is no debate about the ascension of the Jaguars under Liam Coen. He took over a team that went 4-13 in 2024 and 13-4 last season. The Jaguars thus far have outscored their opponents by 125 points, far and away the largest point differential in the league — the Bills are a distant second at plus-88. The last time the Giants took the field they blew several opportunities to close out a 21-17 victory and lost to the Commanders 24-21, failing to add to their first-place lead in the NFC East. They go into the weekend at 5-3-1 — ahead of the Cowboys (4-6), Commanders (3-5-1) and Eagles (2-8).

Jacksonville Jaguars quarterback Trevor Lawrence, right, celebrates a touchdown with Parker Washington (11) on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026, in Nashville, Tenn. AP Photo/George Walker IV

It is possible the Giants fail to beat a team with a winning record the entire season, finish 8-8-1 and win their division. That could happen. So, losing to this AFC power would not be a goal-crusher.

If the Giants on defense are unable to contain quarterback Trevor Lawrence, and if the offensive thrust deployed by J.J. McCarthy — featuring Malik Nabers and Isaiah Likely in the passing attack and Cam Skattebo on the ground — cannot put a dent into the Jaguars' strong and trade-deadline fortified defense, it will be a long afternoon-into-evening experience.

This game was flexed from 1 p.m. to a 4:25 p.m. kickoff to put the Giants into the more exclusive TV window. Now they need to show they belong there and are worthy of all the extra eyeballs peering in at them.

"Obviously, you want to give credit where credit is due to teams, but also you want to make sure your stuff is up to date,'' Likely told The Post. "We want to make sure they're playing against us and not us against ourselves.''

That is a more succinct message. Do not make this more than what it is.

"We do recognize this team's talent and how well they've been playing and the type of points they've been putting up,'' linebacker Micah McFadden told The Post. "For us, it's just as much about us as it is about them.''

McFadden did acknowledge that the Jaguars must be praised for one particular characteristic.

New York Giants tight end Isaiah Likely (9) runs 66 yards for a touchdown against the Washington Commanders on Thursday, Nov. 12, 2026, at MetLife Stadium. Charles Wenzelberg/NY Post

"I think as a team they play hard,'' he said. "A lot of times you find players who play hard, but as a team, these guys play hard. A lot of times in the league, you respect teams that play like that.''

The Giants know they blew it 10 days ago in a loss that should not have been. The Jaguars roll in, compiling a three-game winning streak by a combined 115-40. Harbaugh probably doesn't care about that either.
