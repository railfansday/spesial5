The 2026 National League Rookie of the Year Award presents an interesting philosophical debate

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

The 2026 Rookie of the Year Award is one of the more fascinating award races this year (at least it is in the National League, since Kevin McGonigle should run away with the American League variant). New York Mets outfielder Carson Benge is one of the three finalists, alongside St. Louis Cardinals shortstop JJ Wetherholt, and Cincinnati Reds corner infielder (though mostly first baseman) Sal Stewart. Each of the three players have an interesting and varied case to bring home the hardware in what ended up being a hotly contested race (one of which that saw a few players snubbed as finalists, namely Mets starter Nolan McLean and Colorado Rockies first baseman TJ Rumfield, both of which would place easily in my personal top three).

JJ Wetherholt represents the season-long interpretation of the award. He looked like the runaway favorite to win it for months, as his exceptional defense at second base (+25 DRS) and league-average offense (101 wRC+) gave him a huge head start, especially when Carson Benge was hitting like a pitcher for about a month or so. Wetherholt, despite only playing 139 games, led all National League rookies in fWAR with 4.6 — and trailed only Kevin McGonigle in fWAR among all rookies. He was a steady player for the Cardinals from the word go. He did miss a few weeks with wrist tendonitis, but he represents the safer vote.

Sal Stewart represents a vote with an older school flair. Stewart was a mediocre defender (-2 DRS at first), and his wRC+ sat at 112, so it is fair to wonder how he ended up a finalist, especially when he finished with a rather tepid 2.0 fWAR, good for fifth among NL rookies, and 17th among all rookies. How he got here was his counting stats. Stewart hit 32 home runs, easily leading all NL rookies, and finishing only behind Munetaka Murakami and Kazuma Okamodo. His 112 RBI are twenty more than any other rookie, and were third in all of baseball. Those counting stats pop off the page, and his .263/.330/.457 slash line makes it look even better. Twenty or so years ago, Stewart wins this in a landslide and no one is batting an eye.

Carson Benge represents a little from Column A and a little from Column B. His 4.3 fWAR is second among NL rookies and third among all rookies, pairing a solidly above average bat (118 wRC+) with solid defense (+1 DRS). He went 20/20 on the season, hitting exactly 20 home runs and swiping 22 bags. He also has the benefit of a narrative behind him. Benge was, for lack of a better term, horrific to start his rookie season, finishing April with a 48 wRC+. He turned it on from there, finishing May with a 128 wRC+. He came back down to Earth a little in June with a 108 wRC+ before finishing the season on fire, notching a 126 wRC+, 143 wRC+ and 147 wRC+ in July, August and September, respectively. His ability to put a horrid month behind him and play good and even excellent baseball for the rest of the season, putting him at the very top among all rookies, was a fascinating development.

Any of these players can win this award and it should not come as a surprise. However, Benge has a unique combination of both Wetherholt's and Stewart's resumes that is perhaps being underrated in this conversation. You can make the analytical argument for Benge, seeing as he placed within the top three in fWAR amongst rookies, was fine defensively, had good offensive season (with an absolutely torrid second half of his year). You can also make a less analytical, more traditional argument for him, seeing as he went 20/20. According to research done by MLB.com's Anthony DiComo, Benge is the eighteenth player in MLB history to achieve that milestone as a rookie (and eight of those eighteen did it in their debut season, Benge included). That is a deeply impressive and underrated landmark for a rookie to hit, and something that does not come along often. He also as an interesting narrative case, if you want to look at it that way (and, frankly, I believe these awards are somewhat narrative based), considering he went from a 48 wRC+ in April to being in a finalist for the award, and being an offensive start for the entire summer.

If you would make me guess a winner as I type this out, I would guess Stewart gets the nod, simply because a 32 home run, 112 RBI season is not going to be ignored by voters. Despite my skepticism he wins the award, Benge represents a unique blend of the analytical argument, traditional argument, and narrative argument.
