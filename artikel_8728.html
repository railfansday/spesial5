Our Editors Found the Best Early Black Friday E-Bike Deals, Including 45% Off a Cannondale and $499 Budget Picks.

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Save on editor-tested e-bikes for commuting, cargo hauling, trail riding, and more.

Gear-obsessed editors choose every product we review. We may earn commission if you buy from a link. How we test gear.

Fall riding season is winding down, but Black Friday is right around the corner. Several e-bike companies and retailers have already rolled out great deals and special promotions on some of our favorite and best-tested electric bikes. Now’s the time to score big savings on an e-bike if you’re shopping for a holiday gift or for yourself.

There isn’t a single type of electric bike or e-bike rider. These zippy, pedal-assist bikes are perfect for enjoying your local bike paths, riding rail trails with your family, or tackling singletrack adventures. E-bikes are also great for your daily commute, basic transportation, and running errands.

With big savings from brands, now is a great time to score a deal to get some rides in before the snow starts falling!

No matter your budget or riding needs—from commuters to mountain bikers, experienced cyclists and brand-new riders—there is an e-bike for everyone and every age. Our test team rides dozens of electric bikes each year, and these are some of our favorites and the best deals out there right now.

The Aventon Level is our benchmark electric bike. It’s a great e-bike for commuting, running errands, or leisure riding. The Level platform has proven to be reliable, low-maintenance, and fun to ride—and it doesn’t break the bank.

The REC features a 750W hub motor with 80 Nm of torque. The bike also has regenerative braking, upping the range to 75 miles. For commutes and running errands, the Level comes standard with fenders, a rear rack, lights, and suspension.

Riders will also find the Level’s connected features very useful. The Level 4’s ACU (Aventon Control Unit) adds several smart features to the already well-equipped platform. The wheel lock and motion sensor alarm are passive theft deterrents when you stop for a mid-ride coffee or run into the post office. A 4G chip in the ACU enables GPS tracking in Aventon’s mobile app, so you can locate your bike if you forget where you locked it. 4G connectivity also allows you to set geofence restrictions on the bike for teens or housemates.

Compared with earlier Aventure versions, the third-generation model rides smoother and feels more like a premium bike (without the high price tag). Aventon’s new torque sensor allows more control over the acceleration of Aventure’s 750W rear hub motor. Lighter-weight testers found previous models had too much torque, but Aventure fixed that issue. Additional features include a new head-unit interface, integrated turn signals, a front light, a rear rack, and fenders.

The Aventure 3 is an excellent fat-tire e-bike for commuters—especially on snowy days and gravel paths. Off-road, it excels on doubletrack trails and cinder paths. However, the bike is burly; its weight and components limit its fun on technical singletrack and more aggressive mountain trails.

Aventon also offers a mid-drive version of the Aventure. It keeps the same big, burly character but adds a more premium feel, a smoother, more powerful motor, and an upgraded drivetrain—along with a higher price.

Aventon’s Pace 5 REC is a zippy e-bike that’s perfect for weekend cruises around town, the bike path, or even occasional commutes. The brand refined the Pace with a slew of updates, making it one of the best bang-for-your-buck step-throughs on the market. These include connected features (such as GPS tracking and theft deterrence), a powerful 750 Watt motor, regenerative braking, and an impressive 75-mile range. It’s offered in five colors (grey, mint, pink, orange, and teal) and two frame sizes.

The Pace 5 does not come with a rack, fenders, or a suspension fork. If you commute by bike frequently or use your e-bike for running errands, consider paying $200 more for the Level 4.

Buying an e-bike on Amazon can be risky, especially if you don’t know much about bikes or have much mechanical experience. However, sometimes you can find a good deal on a decent bike. At $500, the Heybike Cityscape isn’t the pinnacle of e-bike tech, but it helps you get out and enjoy some rides without busting your budget.

The Cityscape stands out for its combination of low price, practical features (like a rack and fenders), and a UL-certified battery. Unlike some Amazon brands, Heybike offers a U.S. support line for warranty service or product questions.

Includes FREE accessories: Fast Charger, Running Boards, Frame Bag, Cushions, Elite Headlight, Seatpost, Comfort Seat, and Orbitor.

Many riders are attracted to the XPedition’s very appealing price. Discounted to $1,699, the dual-battery model has up to 120 miles of range and a 28 mph top speed. A single-battery version is also available for $1,399; it boasts an impressive 60-mile range. Need even more battery power? The $1,999 Long Range model goes up to 170 miles from a single charge.

These prices make the XPedition one of the most affordable electric cargo bikes on the market. And they’re a solid option for riders seeking an excellent deal on an e-cargo bike. “The XPedition’s capability surprised me”, said Test Editor Dan Chabanov. “Combined with the Lectric XL panniers and the Essentials package, I comfortably carried several days of groceries for a family of three or one adult passenger.”

Includes FREE accessories: Rear Rack, Water-Resistant Panniers, Lock, Elite Headlight, Seatpost, Comfort Saddle, Phone Mount, and Mirrors.

If you want power and range in a small package, the XP4 750 is right for you. It’s a fast, zippy Class 3 e-bike (pedal assist up to 28 mph) with a 330-pound payload capacity. To increase the XP4’s function and fun, the 750 has a 1310-watt peak motor output (200 watts more than the standard model) and an 85-mile range.

Whether you’re riding city streets on your commute or out for a weekend ride, this bike is fast, easy to ride, and a blast to pedal.

Includes FREE accessories: Rear Rack, Fenders, Headlight, and Seatpost.

The XP Lite 2.0 scales back the XP4 750’s top speed and power, with pedal assist topping out at 20 mph, a peak motor output of 819 watts, and a reduced payload capacity of 275 pounds. It also drops the suspension fork found on the XP4, but still delivers an impressive range of up to 80 miles.

However, the XP Lite isn’t simply a stripped-down version of the XP4; it has some major advantages. At 49 pounds (without accessories and options), it’s 26 pounds lighter than the higher-powered model. This makes the Lite much easier to lift and transport. Plus, instead of an 8-speed drivetrain, the XP Lite features a low-maintenance Gates belt drive. That means no greasy chain, no finicky gear adjustments, and a quieter, cleaner ride. Yes, it’s a singlespeed, but the pedal-assist motor easily compensates for all but the steepest hills or heaviest loads.

Includes FREE accessories: Support Seat, Elite Headlight, Suspension Seatpost, and Cargo Package.

Electric trikes are a great choice for many riders, including seniors, people with mobility limitations, and those who need to carry heavy payloads. At $1,699, the Lectric XP Trike2 is an easy-to-ride, stable three-wheeler.

A 750-watt motor with 1,310 peak watts powers the XP Trike up to 17 mph and up to 70 miles of range on a charge. The Trike features a wide, soft seat with back support. Plus, it has a suspension seatpost and suspension fork for added comfort.

With a top speed of 28 mph, 81 miles of range, a 100mm-travel suspension fork, 27.5”x2.6”, a rack, and fenders, the Adventure Neo Allroad Speed’s name fits it to a T. This bike is built for adventures large and small, from commutes to work in the snow or rain, to gravel trails at your local park.

To power the Adventure, Cannondale uses a Bafang 750W rear hub motor and an easy-access 750 Wh removable battery. With its power and wide-range 8-speed drivetrain, you will get up every hill, while Tektro hydraulic disc brakes will control your speed on descents. Plus, it’s available in flashy red or more subdued matte black.

At $1,200, this Cannondale is 45% off its original price and is equipped with better parts than many lesser-known brands’ bikes priced hundreds more.

Many “step-through” e-bikes lack a truly low-step design, making them difficult for some riders to mount or dismount. However, the CTY e2.1, from REI’s Co-op house brand, has a fully step-through front triangle. This makes the CTY a great choice for older riders, those with mobility limitations, or others who seek an easy-to-ride e-bike.

REI equips the CTY e2.1 with a 250-watt hub-drive motor; it provides smooth pedal assistance up to 20 mph. It also features a suspension fork to smooth out bumps and potholes, an adjustable stem to custom-tune your riding position, and hydraulic disc brakes for improved stopping power. For less than the cost of many unknown e-bikes online, you can get a quality electric bike backed up by REI’s warranty and service.

Salsa’s Moraine 145 costs less than many hardtail E-MTBs. But don’t let the price fool you; this bike is value-packed. This dual 29-inch-wheeled trail bike comes equipped with Fazua’s Ride 60 motor (60 Newton-meters of torque) and a 430-Watt-hour battery. While it has less torque and a smaller-capacity battery than higher-priced competitors, it has enough power for some serious fun in the woods and on singletrack.

The frame uses a Split Pivot suspension system designed by Dave Weagle (the mastermind behind the dw-link suspension system on Pivot bikes), for a balance of performance and pedalability. A 10-speed Shimano Cues drivetrain, four-piston Shimano brakes, a dropper post, and Terravail tubeless tires round out the components of this feature-packed full-suspension E-MTB.

Electric bikes are great for commuting and city life. However, their added weight (sometimes as much as 90 or 100 pounds) can make e-bikes a pain to carry up flights of stairs, load onto a bus or train, or even move around in your garage.

Weighing only about 40 pounds, the Roadster v3 cuts weight without sacrificing much performance or adding cost. Ride1Up uses a 500 watt/50Nm rear hub motor with an internal battery for a clean, stealthy look. 700x45c gravel tires carry speed on pavement or hardpack dirt roads, and metal fenders keep the road spray off your clothes.

Electric mountain bikes are a ton of fun and help you ride more of your favorite trails. We recommend full-suspension E-MTBs for aggressive and technical trails. But hardtails often work fine on easier trails and mellower terrain, and they cost a lot less than dual-suspension models.

Velotric keeps costs low on the Summit 1 by using a rear hub motor instead of a pricier mid-drive unit. The Summit also features an 8-speed Shimano drivetrain rather than more expensive 11- or 12-speed systems. However, Velotric gives the bike a 120mm thru-axle fork to soak up bumps, Shimano hydraulic disc brakes, and a full-color screen with Bluetooth connectivity.

Electric bikes are a great way for older riders to enjoy cycling and stay active. However, many commuter e-bikes have high top tubes, and even many step-through-style frames still require riders to swing their leg over the frame. The Velotric Discover has a Euro-style, low-step-through frame that makes it easier to get on and off.

Additional comfort features include an adjustable-rise stem, wide seat, and suspension seatpost. The Discover has a 28mph top speed and about 75 miles of range. At 63 pounds, it’s not super light, but it weighs less than many similarly priced e-bikes.
