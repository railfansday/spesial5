This Variation of Sun Salutation Is a Beginner-Friendly Energy Boost

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

In Yoga Journal’s Archives series, we share a curated collection of articles originally published in past issues beginning in 1975. This article about Sun Salutations for beginners first appeared in the September-October 1990 issue of Yoga Journal. Find more of our Archives here.

Practicing yoga for beginners is all about mastering the foundations of your flow. And at any level, a Sun Salutation is a welcome means of honoring the Earth’s many cycles—and getting your body and mind moving.

This simple variation of Sun Salutation allows you to align breath and movement with ease—and minus any strain on your body—while boosting your circulation and energy levels. It’s also a great option for those still working on their arm and abdominal strength, as well as anyone with lower back problems.

Move through this Sun Salutation as many times as you’d like, making modifications (like gently bending your knees) when necessary.

Feel your feet solidly grounded beneath you. Exhale and bend the knees slightly. As you inhale, press the feet down into the ground so the legs straighten. As you feel the downward force of the legs rebound up through the body, let the arms lift out to the sides and up over the head in response. With the palms facing each other and the hands shoulder-width apart, look up to the sky, greeting the sky gods and goddesses with an open heart.

If you have a weak abdomen and lower back, be sure to keep your arms close to your body as you bend forward into Standing Forward Bend, to decrease the stress on your back muscles. As you exhale, bend your knees and bring the hands in Namaste (Prayer Position) down past your face and chest to the floor. Keep the knees bent and let the spine hang between the thighs. Use the exhalation like an anchor, feeling the weight of it drop down through the spine and out through the head. In more challenging variations of Sun Salutation, Standing Forward Bend will be one of the only resting positions in the cycle. Learn to let go completely and relax your head and neck to take full advantage of this moment.

Surrender yourself to the pull of gravity and to the Earth in Low Lunge. On an inhalation, step the right leg back behind you. (The leg with which you step back will be the leg that you later bring forward to complete the cycle. With each cycle, start with a different leg.) Allow the right knee to bend slightly and make sure that the left knee is directly over the left ankle. If this pose puts too much strain on your lower back or groin, rest your hands on two blocks placed on either side of your forward foot.

As you exhale, bring the left leg back to meet the right. To make the position a little easier on the back of your legs, place the feet slightly wider than hip-width. Press the ground away with your hands and reach back through your sitting bones. Stay on the balls of your feet with the heels lifted to make the position easier on your lower back and hamstrings. Stay in this position as you take your next inhalation.

As you exhale, press the ground away from you with your feet, driving the spine forward until the shoulders are directly over the wrists, and the ears, shoulders, hips, knees, and ankles are in one line in Plank Pose. Lift the abdomen up into the shell of the pelvis so that both the front and back of the spinal column are equally supported. If you let the abdomen hang down from the lumbar spine, the hips will sink, causing the back muscles to overwork.

Hold this position as you take an inhalation.

Return to Downward-Facing Dog on an exhalation; Lunge on an inhalation; Standing Forward Bend on an exhalation; Mountain Pose. Bring your arms out to the side and over your head as you lift up on an inhalation. As you exhale, release the arms down by your sides.
