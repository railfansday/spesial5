An Up-Close Look at <i><em>The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power</em></i>’s Twisted Love Triangle

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Morfydd Clark, Jamie Campbell Bower, and Charlie Vickers unpack that tree nest scene.

This story contains spoilers for The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power.

After sleeping alone, it takes some getting used to sharing a bed with someone. This is a challenging proposition in the third season of The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power for the recently reunited Galadriel (Morfydd Clark) and her husband, Celeborn (Jamie Campbell Bower), when they use a tree nest as a makeshift bedchamber, as the Dark Lord Sauron (Charlie Vickers) hunts them down. But Elves are not mere mortals, and after centuries apart, they slip back into slumbering beside each other as if not a day has passed. On set, however, things were much more awkward: Galadriel’s sword gets in Clark’s way, she struggles to roll over in chainmail, and the couple’s moss pillow is too small.

It is mid-September 2025 at the legendary Shepperton Studios just outside London, and there has been another request for more real moss to elevate Bower’s head. (He likens this grassy pillow to the mattresses in The Princess and the Pea when we talk a year later.) The mood up in the tree nest, as Clark and Bower rehearse a scene from the fifth episode, is much lighter than what plays out on screen. Under the watchful eyes of director Sanaa Hamri, the actors figure out the logistics of spooning in this confined space, mimicking sleep so the camera can see their faces, and allowing Clark to shift to her side, then quickly back away when Galadriel imagines Sauron lying beside her. Later in the day, Vickers will switch in. The shock sends Galadriel falling backward out of the tree and straight into danger. This is not your run-of-the-mill love triangle or burgeoning throuple.

To put it mildly, Galadriel has been going through it in the adaptation of J.R.R. Tolkien’s history of Middle-earth. After she rejected Sauron’s offer to be his queen (again) at the end of season 2, he has since taken much of Middle-earth, and his quest to create the One Ring—and thereby rule over all—continues. In the previous episode, he attempted to locate an elf being held prisoner in Gundabad who knew the secrets of forging the One Ring. It turns out the last Elven-smith was already dead, but Galadriel’s long-lost husband, Celeborn, was locked up in a neighboring cell. Celeborn likely knows those same secrets that Sauron needs, making him invaluable. During their escape, Arondir (Ismael Cruz Córdova) is slain, and Sauron comes face-to-face with Galadriel for the first time this season. Well, other than popping into her subconscious.

When Sauron appears in Galadriel’s dreams again in the fifth episode, “The Lady of Lórien,” the safety of the tree canopy is immediately shattered as Galadriel’s sleeping mind pictures Sauron lying inches away where she expects to see Celeborn. When I catch up with Clark on Zoom a year later in September 2026, her face lights up when I mention this scene. “I loved being up there [in the tree nest],” she says. “It was so cozy.” While the episode ends with Celeborn being captured by Sauron—separating the couple once again—there is a brief respite from the horrors of this war-torn land. “There’s a real unabashed lean into the gorgeousness of fantasy,” Clark says of this season, including the foray into swoon-worthy romance in the cocooned foliage.

While the Elves have only just reunited, Clark and Bower had already established the foundation of Galadriel and Celeborn’s relationship during a dance sequence in the season 3 premiere episode, revealing a far lighter Galadriel than we have seen for some time. This also gave the actors an opportunity to get to know each other. “There is a magic about him,” Clark says of Bower. “We did get to rehearse that together, which also took away a certain type of magic in that learning a dance with someone is pretty vulnerable. We got to know each other doing that, which was really nice. He was so lovely, and he is elf-like.”

As we learned in season 1, Celeborn left to fight in the war during the First Age and never came home—Middle-earth is now in its Second Age. Galadriel and Celeborn share battle stories, but it is Sauron informing Celeborn that they have previously met (Celeborn claims he has no memory of this) that throws more fire onto this already sizzling dynamic. But before Sauron storms in like a jealous ex, Celeborn and Galadriel manage some K-I-S-S-I-N-G in the tree. “It was a tight space,” Bower laughs over the phone earlier this month. “Towards the end of that scene, there’s that beautiful moment of physical reconnection, and our intimacy coordinator [Sophie Cooch] was so helpful in finding a beauty and almost like a dance and a grace within that physical reconnection into the falling down [together], and being able to lie side by side,” Bower says.

Having just played Stranger Things’ big bad Vecna, Bower (who is a huge fan of Tolkien’s work) said he was looking for a character who represented love, and Celeborn radiates light even in the darkest circumstances. In the episode, Celeborn senses Galadriel is holding back, but doesn’t press too hard about what. “She definitely feels like she’s lost a sparkle, and he’s a mirror to her,” says Clark. “She can’t ignore the parts of it that have changed.” Clark recalls discussing with Bower that there should be zero “feeling of ownership between them.” When I mention this to Bower, he references the line from Breakfast at Tiffany’s to sum up this dynamic: “We belong to nobody, and nobody belongs to us. We don’t even belong to each other.”

Tolkien’s marriage and experience in World War I provided inspiration for this union, but it was The Magnetic Fields song “Abigail, Belle of Kilronan” that Clark says she became obsessed with while filming. “It’s about someone going off to war, and it’s like, ‘And when I come home, if I come home, you’ll be a grown woman. And when I come home, if I come home, don’t be alone, Abigail, Belle of Kilronan,’” Clark says. “That’s almost been her biggest betrayal of him that she allowed herself to be lonely for so long, which I found really beautiful.”

One person Galadriel was close to before knowing his real identity was Sauron. Although she vocalizes that she doesn’t want Sauron present in this moment—not even uttering his name—the image of him lying next to her is a jolt back to reality, even if he is a figment of her imagination. “Even though in many ways her dreams are coming true with being reunited with Celeborn,” Clark says, “they can’t live in a simple vacuum of happiness. The world is still out there, and she’s part of the world.” Unfortunately for Galadriel, as she quickly backs away, she falls from the tree, alerting the nearby orcs to their presence.

The vision seems to be Galadriel’s own doing; Sauron is living rent-free in her mind instead of intentionally popping into her dreams. Vickers discussed this approach with Clark. “The way I play it was determined by what she [Clark] had in her mind—because it’s Galadriel’s experience, it’s not Sauron’s experience—and that was to be the scariest I could be in Galadriel’s mind,” Vickers says.

When I met Vickers on set, he was in costume, grabbing a juice from craft services before joining Clark up in the tree nest. Having spent a lot of time on a nearby soundstage, where Sauron’s bleak Barad-dûr lair is located, the Australian actor was happy to experience this change of scenery. “Barad-dûr this season is incredibly intense. It was 35 degrees [95°F] on the days we were filming there,” Vickers says over Zoom. “There was a lot of sweat. It was wonderful to go into the forest on a soundstage.” Still, there were some logistical issues for Vickers up in the nest, first finding the same eyeline to match Bower’s sleeping position and then trying not to stab Clark with Sauron’s pointy gloves. “Lying down in all that stuff is incredibly challenging,” Vickers says.

Of course, you don’t see this human behavior in the final cut. “It’s a very particular thing to film something fantasy in this way because the look is so important,” Clark says. “It does matter that their hair looks absolutely lovely in that light, and they move like they’re artists with their bodies.” This includes scenes that might require a stunt double, like the one I observed. “Your stunt double is always a huge part of creating the character, but I think more so than ever with an elf, because she’s got to be excellent,” Clark says. “We do as much as we can with me being as excellent as I can be.” For everything else, Clark’s stunt double Hannah Grant (who has a ballet and martial arts background) steps in.

While the version of Sauron in this scene is a figment of Galadriel’s imagination, the real Dark Lord is traveling to the Eryn Galen woodland. Celeborn sacrifices himself to stop his wife (who is lying unconscious nearby) from being discovered. What comes next will likely be incredibly unpleasant, but Bower enjoyed going head-to-head with Vickers as the twisted trio enters its next phase.

“It’s where sparks can fly,” Bower says. “You need to build a rapport, have a friendship, a comfortability, and a trust in one another. Charlie made that so easy from day one.” However, Sauron won’t be as accommodating. “Celeborn has something that Sauron really needs,” Vickers says. “It’s the key to his whole existence up to this point, so you have all the emotional interplay, but then beneath that you have this real psychological drive, which is, ‘I will do anything. I will not stop. I will do all I can to get this secret out of you.’” It seems unlikely that Celeborn, Galadriel, or Sauron will get any sleep anytime soon.
