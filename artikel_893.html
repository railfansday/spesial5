Nicholls breaks record for Jumps wins with Warwick triumph

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Ditcheat handler Paul Nicholls became the winning-most trainer in British National Hunt history, surpassing the great Martin Pipe's total of 3,390 when Knappers Hill crossed the line in front at Lingfield, donning the colours of Paul Barber

Paul Nicholls reflected on a “fantastic journey” after rewriting the record books by surpassing Martin Pipe’s all-time National Hunt tally for winners by a British trainer.

The 14-time champion eclipsed Pipe's record of 3,930 wins at Warwick on Thursday after Knappers Hill toughed out victory in the Denis O'Connell Memorial Veterans' Handicap Chase under Sam Twiston-Davies, carrying the colours of the trainer's long-time friend and mentor Paul Barber.

Nicholls, who has been training at Manor Farm Stables in Somerset since 1991 after first renting the yard from Barber, was thrilled to etch his name into the history of a sport in which he has long been a master of his craft.

"I never thought when I started training we'd ever train that many winners, but it's a nice milestone," said Nicholls, whose tally includes famous victories by all-time great horses such as Kauto Star, Big Buck's and Denman.

"We're not far off 4,000 winners that have been sent out across all codes, but they'll get beaten again in the future because records are always there to be broken.

"You'd probably have thought Martin would never have been beaten. What a fantastic trainer he was.

"Hopefully we can head on with the British winners and end up with getting near the 4,000. It's exciting."

Nicholls added: "As you keep racking up these winners, you're getting closer and closer.

"A year ago, I wouldn't have known the exact number, but then, of course, everyone fills you in and now you know.

"It's just a target, isn't it? Dan [Skelton] is targeting beating Martin's record of winners in a season. We all like beating records.

"It's nice to be the winning-most National Hunt trainer in Britain, even if it's not forever, it's nice to do that. It just reflects well on the whole team at Ditcheat and the whole set-up really and what we've done here."

Olveston has more meaning than most for Nicholls as it is not only the village where he was raised, but it is also the name of his first winner.

What made it even more special was his father Brian was a part-owner and family has been a running theme throughout his 35 years at Manor Farm, with daughters Megan and Olive and nephew Harry Derham all playing their part along the way.

He added: "I remember my first winner. It was December 20, 1991 at Hereford. Dad was involved in Olveston with some mates. It was brilliant.

"Megan's had a lot of winners, Olive has, Harry Derham was here and dad's still here every day.

"I've been incredibly lucky, I've had some great people around me, some fantastic horses. We keep trying to work hard and do the best we can.

"Paul Barber was a big part of my life and lots of people along the way. Lots of people have gone on to be good jockeys, lots of people have been gone on to be great trainers.

"It's just been a fantastic journey. I pinch myself to think I've been here 35 years now, it just seems like it was yesterday. I don't know where time goes."

As he targets his next landmark, what remaining ambitions does Nicholls have for a trainer who has won it all?

"Keep training winners, still loving the game. I love the game. It's just awesome," said Nicholls, who is still one short of Pipe's 15 champion trainer titles.

"I've invested quite heavily in some new facilities this year, I've got some nice young horses and a good team around me. I love it, just training big winners."
