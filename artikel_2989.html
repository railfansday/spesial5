Crypto Market Today, Nov. 17: A Warning Sign Not Seen Since 2022 Is Flashing Across Cryptocurrency Markets

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Accelerating stablecoin outflows, mounting interest rate concerns, and deteriorating risk appetite threaten to derail the cryptocurrency market's recovery.

Cryptocurrency markets remained under pressure on Tuesday, November 17, as attempts to recover from last weekend's brutal sell-off continued to falter. As of early evening, Bitcoin (BTC +0.35%) was down 1.29% to $63,990.47, while Ethereum (ETH +0.27%) fell 1.22% to $1,796.92, slipping below the psychologically important $1,800 level. Solana (SOL +4.68%) bucked the broader trend, rising 2.15% to $73.04. Despite pockets of strength, deteriorating liquidity conditions remain a significant concern for cryptocurrency investors.

Several headwinds are converging that threaten to derail a cryptocurrency market recovery. Weakness in equity markets, elevated Treasury yields, and uncertainty surrounding Nvidia's highly anticipated earnings report continue to weigh on investor risk appetite. Although the recent wave of forced liquidations appears to have subsided, buying pressure remains subdued.

Beneath the surface, however, a more concerning trend is developing. According to CoinDesk, capital is exiting the cryptocurrency market through stablecoins at the fastest pace since 2022. Stablecoins serve as a primary source of liquidity within digital asset markets, and a contraction in their supply suggests investors are withdrawing capital rather than simply rotating between cryptocurrencies. This erosion of liquidity could leave the market increasingly vulnerable to further sell-offs.

Spot Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs were lower on the day, with iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF (IBIT -1.77%) down 1.77%, Fidelity Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund (FBTC -1.68%) down 1.66%, and iShares Ethereum Trust ETF (ETHA -1.17%) down 1.17%.

With Bitcoin struggling to reclaim $65,000 and Ethereum falling below $1,800, cryptocurrency investors should be paying particularly close attention to whether capital flows begin to stabilize. Although the recent streak of spot Bitcoin ETF outflows has reportedly ended, a sustained reversal in institutional demand would provide a more convincing indication that the market is establishing a bottom.

Solana's relative strength on Tuesday is encouraging, but it remains too early to determine whether this represents renewed investor conviction or simply a temporary rebound following substantial losses.

Looking ahead, the combination of contracting stablecoin supply and growing expectations of another Federal Reserve interest rate hike leaves cryptocurrency markets in a vulnerable position. Unlike during periods of expanding liquidity, there is increasingly less capital available to absorb selling pressure. Should macroeconomic conditions deteriorate further, Bitcoin could find itself testing $60,000, while several other major cryptocurrencies risk setting fresh bear market lows.
