Rubin brings the universe into focus

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

After more than 20 years of planning, construction, and testing, astronomers have officially started the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST). The survey will snap about 1,000 images per night with a 3.2-gigapixel camera — the largest digital camera in the world — attached to the 8.4-meter telescope at the Vera C. Rubin Observatory in Chile. The LSST will observe the entire southern sky every three to four nights, on average, across its 10-year lifetime. The goal: build a high-definition, time-lapse record of the cosmos from the solar system to distant galaxies. This part of the initial image covers about 1.7° by 1.7° centered in northeastern Centaurus, though the left side bleeds into Lupus. The wispy swirls are tenuous clouds of gas and dust known as galactic cirrus, illuminated by the diffuse glow of the Milky Way.

Richard Talcott is a former senior editor and current contributing editor of Astronomy.
