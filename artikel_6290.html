This convenient appliance works better than your average cooler and offers versatile power options. Here are the ones we recommend.

Even the best coolers we’ve tested from names like Yeti, Coleman, and RTIC with superior insulation eventually get too warm for ice to stay frozen. That’s where portable refrigerators shine, offering cooling capability that you just can’t get from a regular cooler. They’re great for long camping trips where you need to keep meat and other food at safe temperatures and come in plenty of sizes to suit hardcore camper rigs and RVs or fit in a standard sedan.

Portable refrigerators—often called car refrigerators—plug into your car’s 12-volt outlet, an AC outlet, or a portable power station. They’re pricier and heavier than the standard cooler, but they're more efficient at cooling. And you get to skip the expense and hassle of dealing with bags of ice. They can also be helpful around the house if your fridge breaks or there’s a power outage.

We used our expertise on coolers, power stations, and camping gear to find the best portable refrigerators. We checked camping and overlanding gear reviews and forums to find models that hold up on long treks in remote areas and are viable alternatives for people who don't want to deal with bags of ice for a cooler.

We chose options in different capacities and across a range of prices and prioritized models that offer convenient features like USB chargers, wheels, and LED lights. If you want some extra cooling power on-the-go, consider one of the portable refrigerators for your next trek.

Portable refrigerators run on a compressor just like your kitchen fridge and freezer and keep food fresher a lot longer than a cooler and ice. Most reach an average minimum temperature of -4 to -7 degrees Fahrenheit to freeze and refrigerate food, though some are refrigerators only, reaching a minimum temperature of 32 degrees Fahrenheit.

Although portable refrigerators don't rely solely on insulation for cooling power, they're still well insulated enough to take some of the load off the compressor when it comes to temperature regulation.

Don’t confuse portable refrigerators with electric coolers, as that’s another product category entirely. Electric coolers rely on thermoelectric cooling, which isn’t as efficient because it's impacted by ambient temperatures. As such, they can only cool to about 40 degrees Fahrenheit. Unlike portable refrigerators, electric coolers don’t have a compressor and don’t have the power to achieve freezing temperatures.

Portable refrigerators typically draw an average of about 45 watts of power, which is less than a standard mini fridge. This won’t blow out any car fuses or drain a car battery unless the unit is kept on for days without the car running. We chose portable refrigerators that have features like eco modes and automatic shutoff so there are precautions in place to prevent it from draining a car or portable battery.

While portable refrigerators offer more storage than coolers because they don’t need ice, their built-in compressor makes them relatively heavy at about 30 pounds or more. Still, some are more portable than others thanks to built-in handles and wheels. They don’t have shoulder straps and aren’t meant to be carried over a far distance. Also, most don't have drain plugs like standard coolers because condensation is minimal and can easily be wiped out with a small towel.
