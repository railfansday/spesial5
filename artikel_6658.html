Is Social Media Really the Great Equalizer? Here’s What Hulu’s ‘The Circle’ Host Tinx Has to Say (Exclusive)

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

I sat down with Christina Najjar to talk alliances, authenticity, and the responsibility of being the “internet’s big sister.”

After a seven-season run on Netflix, The Circle has found a new home on Hulu, and things look very, very different. Rather than “normies,” the cast is now made up of familiar faces from the Walt Disney Company reality TV ecosystem and micro-influencers disguised as civilians. (Even the player with the most modest initial following, Cyrus Irani, had over 10,000 followers at the time his casting was announced. Is it a problem? Not necessarily. But it’s certainly a switch-up from season 1’s social media skeptic Shubham Goel.)

The revival series is also airing in near-real time, with new episodes dropping Tuesday through Thursday and audience voting being used to shape the process. And perhaps the biggest change: stepping into hosting duties in place of comedian and actor Michelle Buteau is SiriusXM’s Christina “Tinx” Najjar, who rose to fame on social media during the COVID-19 pandemic.

As a longtime Circle devotee, I was...curious about what these changes might mean for my favorite competition series. So when I got the chance to fly to Atlanta to visit the set (and meet Tinx) myself, I took it. There I chatted with her about the power of social media, the potential problem of having celebs in the Circle, and the player she thinks might win it all.

I truly couldn’t believe it. I know how many loyal fans The Circle has, so when I got the call that I got it—I mean, I’m still pinching myself every day. It’s a really big moment for me, and I know I have big shoes to fill. And I really just want to make everybody proud.

I remember watching the very first episode and thinking, “Wow, this is such a fresh concept, and it’s truly a social experiment for the modern age.” So the fact that I’m here all these years later is truly a pinch-me moment.

I relate to it so much, because in COVID, I was living in a studio apartment in L.A. I had just moved. I didn’t have a lot of friends, and all I had was my phone. That’s why I initially picked up TikTok and thought, “Who knows what’s going to happen in the world? I might as well make a few videos and try to make friends online.” And as soon as I started creating content, I just realized, “Wow, I love connecting with people. I love entertaining people.” It’s been a wild six years ever since.

I grew up doing theater in high school, but honestly, no. I truthfully had a lot of limiting beliefs about myself. I just thought there was no way I could ever be an influencer. I didn’t feel pretty enough. I didn’t think I was put together enough. And I think one thing COVID taught us is that anybody who has a dream can go for it. Social media is really the great equalizer in that sense, because if you are funny, if you have something to say, you can get online and you can connect with people.

It’s such an honor, and I love that people look up to me for advice. It’s the honor of my life that people trust me enough to call me on my radio show for advice or write to me, and I just try to be a voice of reason and remind women to know their worth. I feel like this is a journey I was meant to be on, and all of the opportunities that being online has afforded me, because it really has given me so much. The fact that I’m sitting here as the host of The Circle is—again, I can’t believe it.

It’s so different because I’m used to just holding up my phone, recording, cutting quickly, and pressing post. This is a totally different operation, but the crew is so cool, production is so cool, and the writers that I work with are just so smart and so funny. I was actually shocked when they explained to me the process of how close to the wire we are. It is as close to real time as you could ever think.

Because I have lived online for six years, I post in real time myself. I’m someone who wakes up and I’m like, “Hey guys, I’m going to the gym. Here’s what I’m eating for breakfast.” I don’t know if I would know how to do it any other way, because I’m just, like, an oversharer, so I think I would just be myself and see what happened. But I can be strategic. I feel like I would make a lot of alliances. I’m good at that.

I honestly think maybe the celebrities are at a slight disadvantage because, as you say, people have perceptions about them. I feel like they also have more of a target on their backs.

He got got by Justin [Hinsley], if I’m being honest, who has very strong strategy. I think Justin is incredibly strategic and knows how to play the game.

I mean, Hannah [Brown], obviously. I don’t think she knows how to play any other way. She’s so genuine, and she’s definitely playing with her heart. So we’ll see how that fares and whether it’s strategy or heart that wins.

I think it’s totally fine, because there’s a lot on the line here. This is a popularity contest, but there’s a big prize at the end. So I think it’s fun when people are ruthless. Why not? It’s a game, and games are meant to be won.
