Grand Theft Auto VI Review: Better Than The Hype

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

There are moments in GTA 6 that’ll have you levitating off your sofa. Unprecedented in its scope and production, it was created by a team of almost 5,000 developers over nearly 10 years. Its budget is rumored to rival the construction of Dubai’s Burj Khalifa skyscraper; its first trailer set records with 93 million views in a day. Every social media brand account, from the White House to BMW, has jumped on the hype, while economists predict a national sick day as millions play hooky to game. When you do, you see the time, talent and flair on the screen. But, at its heart, Grand Theft Auto VI follows in series tradition: an epic American crime story, set in a stunning parody of the US that riffs on film, music, politics, style and satire.

Fronting the first franchise entry in more than 13 years are Jason and Lucia. Developer Rockstar attempted a multi-character arc in Grand Theft Auto V, but those three leads learned to loathe one another. Instead, VI tackles love, a game design challenge most developers have sworn off with good reason. Not a parent/child relationship like The Last of Us or God of War, but a mature, romantic, adult one. You can call or text each other at pretty much any point—to do missions together, commit robberies, explore, meditate, work out, fly, or just to coexist under the Vice City sunshine.

This isn’t some kind of ChatGPT fantasy to indulge in. Neither Jason or Lucia exists at your beck and call as some kind of hyper-realistic plaything. You have a role in their relationship, both in the way they speak to each other and where the story takes them. You can cruise down the Vice City waterfront together in your souped-up ’70s muscle wagon with one of you driving, the other scrolling through your Snapmatic feed (the in-game social media channel), before grabbing a drink and carving up the sidewalk with an assault rifle five minutes later. You just don’t get these detours in Call of Duty.

More than a car: in GTA VI, your vehicle is a mobile base of operations to stash your guns, fits, money, and drugs.

It’s a different pace from previous Grand Theft Autos too. Rockstar changed its dynamics with 2018’s Red Dead Redemption 2—a more thoughtful take on the outlaw tale that added friction to the action and a conscience to the narrative. For a developer that once experienced the spotlight of US Congress during 2005’s infamous “Hot Coffee" controversy (when a sexually explicit mini-game meant as an inside joke between developers was figured out by savvy users), this kind of nuance was revelatory. With VI, Rockstar takes Red Dead 2’s ethos and applies it to a modern city with 1,000 x the population. The result is staggering, but still an absolute blast to play. That is to say, the greatest hits of GTA—driving cars, shooting guns, and stealing money, drugs and more (all to spend on a better Beretta, G-Wagon replica, and impeccably modeled Air Jordan dupes)—but with added depth to how you play. Weapon slots are finite; you can no longer run amok with an entire arsenal stashed in your jacket pocket. Money is vulnerable; get killed or caught with cash in your possession, and you’ll lose it. And if you jack a car that’s fitted with a tracker, the police will find you quickly.

Should you get into a bust-up with the law after a jewelry store smash-and-grab gone wrong, the game’s action is frenetic. Roads are much narrower, traffic is denser, and you can’t see police on your minimap. That $40,000 in cash sitting on the back seat had better be worth the hassle. As such, the game supports your decision to play chaotically, but truly immerses you in playing methodically. Jason and Lucia each have a “criminal profile”—be unnecessarily violent and you’ll get a “psycho” rating—that adds replayability and roleplay. Still, I had the most fun when planning missions: using a silenced pistol, shooting to maim not kill, and wiping security footage to leave no trace. It’s the closest GTA has honed in on the art of crime. The feds will surround the store you’re robbing. “Come out right now with your hands above your head!” Do things right, and you’ll have prepped your partner with a getaway car out back. You’ll leave the scene anonymously as backup blares past you on the other side of the road.

Every club, pub and dingy dive bar in GTA 6’s Leonida has a distinct vibe—from the music to the decor to the sweaty Floridians inside.

The game kicks off with an electrifying setup accompanied by an original song from composers Justice. It perfectly sets the tone: VI has the ’80s flamboyance of Vice City made new and fresh. It takes its opening hours steadily, sending you on odd jobs to make a little money here and there. Gradually—then suddenly—you’re thrust into the most high-octane campaign in Grand Theft Auto history. Tense hospital escapes, daring bank heists, cross-country chases, and more. Its set pieces are balls-to-the-wall bravado, rivaling what you’d see in Nolan’s The Odyssey or Villeneuve’s Dune, upping in intensity through its chapters. Tonally, it’s the most “of the now” Grand Theft Auto game yet, as your two in-love criminals race to find a better life in an unfair world. No previous GTA has given you a savings account—it’s like playing the millennial curse. The only dings are some pacing in the game’s second chapter as the game wrestles with its story while still introducing mechanics. Think of it as VI’s version of RDR2’s Guarma, albeit with bargain bin stores and grimy strip malls instead of exotic Caribbean fauna.

And then to Leonida, with which Rockstar has created the most technically and artistically impressive game world ever made. The pre-release trailers promised beaches bustling with vacationers and nightclubs packed with dancers. That is the reality. From sea to sand to sky, the world is alive. Vice City alone could be an entire game in its own right, complete with distinct districts, an explorable international airport, and more. Yet, Rockstar spoils us with a massive expanse of varied towns, high-class beach resorts, lush green national parks, murky swamplands and sun-kissed island keys. All packed with people that range from useful, to funny, to completely unsavory (shout-out to the old woman who took one look at my Jason and said “I’mma take my teeth out and suck the skin off that dick”). The density and fidelity of it all often stretches the limits of even Sony’s PlayStation 5 Pro. Frame rate dips don’t spoil gameplay, but the slowdown is noticeable when you’ve punted a couple of grenades into passing cars to set off multiple explosions at once.

NPCs don’t just feel like backdrop mannequins anymore, but real people with an incredible range of faces, body types and outfits.

Every frame a photograph. Stop anywhere in GTA 6, and you’ll likely find something enticing to see, hear or interact with.

As with Red Dead Redemption 2, the most impressive of the game’s many achievements is how everything seems to live on without you. All it took to make this world was Rockstar founding an entire studio to work solely on Leonida’s population. I’ve seen a woman throw herself out of a cop car window as I scooted along Ocean Beach, and car thieves burst through the glass windows of a car dealership while the alarms howl. I saw a furry taken down by armed police, staged perfectly with a helicopter spotlight. During one 3 a.m. wander through the rough neighborhood of Crosstown, a man approached me to warn me of the local gang and tells me they have a nearby base. I accidentally tapped the button that brings up your in-game phone. “Ohhhh! You don’t just believe what I say” the man says, responding to my actions. “You do your own research!” In more than 70 hours of play, I have seen only one moment of repetition.

There is so much more besides. A cast of interesting, funny and believable characters; off-the-beaten-path secrets that can lead to side quests both big and small; perhaps the best radio track list of any GTA spanning from Travis Scott to Lana Del Rey; podcast episodes; golf to play; gators to hunt; dozens of supercars to steal and customize; weapons that feel punchy and satisfying; multiple racing series; a litany of actually interesting shops and bars and clubs to explore and buy things in. I’ve seen brilliant horror vignettes, startling science fiction, and laugh-out-loud comedy just by exploring. Rockstar has created a world that rewards you for spending time in it. Expect to spend 100 hours if you want to see everything.

As you’ll likely have seen and heard—in everything from Miami Heat takeovers, to Netflix launches, to Selfridges pop-ups—that this is the most-wanted entertainment property in the world. In a way, all it had to be was Grand Theft Auto V: The Second. But, on the largest and most scrutinized stage imaginable, Rockstar takes creative risks with its story, protagonists, and mechanics, and makes big leaps in what games can be. It’s unlikely other developers will catch up soon. In 2002, Grand Theft Auto: Vice City reshaped and refashioned pop culture to give us a playable version of a place and a time we already knew. A quarter of a century later, VI shows Rockstar at the peak of entertainment in its very own right.
