The US DOJ is planning to seize $1.1B of the $6.3B drained from Binance by the DPRK about a month ago.

Binance is now waiting on bureaucratic procedures that will enable it to recover the stolen value.

The rest of the funds remain frozen, about $900 million is lost to slippage, while $247 million has run through a mixer.

The US Department of Justice (DOJ) has filed a civil forfeiture complaint in a Washington, D.C., federal court, with the aim of recovering $1.1 billion of the $6.3 billion lost to North Korean state-sponsored hackers (TraderTraitor) from Binance on October 29, 2026.

According to a statement on its website, the agency seeks to permanently forfeit over $1.1 billion of the Circle-issued stablecoin USDC.

Notably, Circle voluntarily froze said funds an hour after the hack, while a US court issued a formal restraining order on the same funds two weeks later.

The latest action is now part of ongoing efforts to track down more of the stolen funds, with current recovered and frozen funds sitting at over $2.9 billion. Since Circle is headquartered in New York, the case is under the joint investigation of the FBI, the US Secret Service and the IRS Criminal Investigation.

Two weeks after the hack, Binance announced that it had fully reimbursed users using its own corporate treasury, company profits, and the Secure Asset Fund for Users (SAFU) emergency reserve.

Following the DOJâs filing, Binance is now waiting on the government to publish public notice of the lawsuit. After this, the crypto exchange will have 30-60 days to file a formal claim in court.

Once a judge issues a Final Order of Forfeiture, Circle will mint an equivalent amount of tokens to the frozen ones, and then transfer them to the US government. Thereafter, Binance can petition for remission to reclaim the assets.

Since Binance users were made whole, the exchange now stands as the primary eligible victim.

USDT-issuer Tether continues to freeze about $1.77 billion of the stolen funds. These will likely undergo a similar recovery process to the USDC, but the process will require international coordination since the company is headquartered in El Salvador.

As for the rest of the funds, blockchain analytics firm Elliptic says the hackers have routed at least $247 million to Bitcoin mixing transactions, specifically coinjoins. Deposits into the pool are taking place at fixed intervals, implying possible coordination with fiat-converting launderers.
