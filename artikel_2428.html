Renée Slegers leaves Arsenal by mutual consent as Clarke-Reed takes interim charge

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

To help deliver timely coverage of women's football, we may use AI tools to support content creation and editing. While we strive for accuracy and editorial quality, readers should independently verify important information.

Arsenal Women are looking for a new head coach after Renée Slegers left the club by mutual consent on Friday, a little over a year and a half after she guided the Gunners to the Women’s Champions League title.

The club confirmed the departure on its official website, pointing to a disappointing opening to the 2026/27 campaign. Long-serving assistant Aaron D’Antino, part of the backroom team since August 2018, has also gone. Melissa Clarke-Reed, previously an assistant coach, steps in as interim head coach while Arsenal search for a permanent appointment.

Director of women’s football Clare Wheatley called it “an extremely difficult decision to part ways with Renée” and added: “After careful reflection, it is clear that results have been below the expected level, and change is necessary.”

The 37-year-old Dutch coach first arrived as an assistant to Jonas Eidevall, took over on an interim basis when he resigned in October 2024 and was handed the job permanently in January 2025. Her first months in charge were remarkable. Arsenal lifted their second Women’s Champions League crown in May 2025, and in February 2026 they won the first edition of the FIFA Women’s Champions Cup.

That success earned her a fresh contract in January 2026 that was due to run until 2029, but she leaves around ten months into that deal.

The problem has been the Women’s Super League. Arsenal sit sixth after 11 games, with Chelsea already 12 points clear at the top and London City Lionesses holding third, the last Champions League qualification place, by a margin of eight points. According to BBC Sport, they have managed only 11 league goals so far, fewer than half of what Chelsea, Manchester City and London City have each scored, and they have gone six games without a win at the Emirates Stadium.

The latest home setback came on Sunday with a draw against West Ham, after which Slegers accepted the blame. “I take my responsibilities, because the results haven’t been good enough,” she said. A 2-0 home loss to Lyon in the Champions League had already led former Arsenal and England forward Lianne Sanderson to say it was time for a change, and Sky Sports notes that Clarke-Reed, a former Brighton manager, had been working as the club’s head of analytics.

Chief executive Richard Garlick described Slegers as “a wonderful person” and said the club’s recent results had convinced the board that a change was needed to compete for major trophies on a consistent basis.

Whoever takes over inherits a squad with plenty of quality but a lot of ground to make up, both in the title race and in the fight for a top three finish.
