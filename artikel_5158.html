Banks Are Adopting Stablecoins: Here Is Why Crypto-Native Dollars Still Win on User Freedom

As traditional giants rush into digital dollars, USDD’s Yvonne Chia explains why 24/7 access, self-custody, and transparent yield give decentralized stablecoins an edge banks can’t replicate.

20 November – Road Town, Totorla, British Virgin Islands – Decentralized stablecoin protocol USDD is accelerating its multichain utility across TRON, Ethereum, and BNB Chain—combining automated sUSDD yield via its Smart Allocator with GasFree zero-TRX transfers—as traditional financial giants race to build competing stablecoin rails.

In a recent industry interview with instant exchange partner ChangeNOW, USDD Head of Communications Yvonne Chia outlined why self-custody, 24/7 settlement, and transparent onchain reserves give decentralized digital dollars a structural advantage over bank-issued alternatives.

For billions of people globally, opening and maintaining a traditional bank account still involves paperwork, minimum balance requirements, geographic restrictions, and approval delays. Even when banks integrate stablecoin rails behind the scenes, those services remain gated behind legacy compliance walls and intermediary control.

Crypto-native stablecoins flip that model on its head. As Yvonne Chia highlighted during the interview:

“Compare this to a stablecoin like USDD, where all you need is a phone, a working internet connection, you can immediately start a wallet and you have access to your funds right away.”

With USDD, users can access and manage their digital dollars directly through their own wallets, without depending on traditional financial intermediaries.

This represents a fundamental shift in financial access: users are no longer limited to the services, operating hours, or account structures of a single institution. Instead, they have the freedom to hold, manage, and transfer their assets on their own terms.

Self-custody also gives users greater autonomy over their financial decisions. They can choose which wallets to use, where to transfer their assets, and which supported decentralized applications to interact with, without needing to route every transaction through a centralized financial institution.

With USDD, users’ digital dollars can move with them across an open blockchain ecosystem, rather than remaining tied to a particular bank or service provider.

Legacy payment networks still operate on decades-old schedules: weekend cutoffs, bank holidays, and multi-day cross-border settlement delays accompanied by hefty wire fees.

Blockchain networks never close. Whether it is a Sunday night or a public holiday, USDD moves globally without depending on traditional banking operating hours or settlement windows.

“We are able to have transfers 24/7 and we’re not limited to any traditional banking hours. So I think that in some ways, stablecoins are not just becoming banks, but exceeding what traditional banks can offer to users.”

To make everyday transfers even smoother than fintech apps, USDD introduced GasFree integration on TRON, allowing users on supported wallets to pay network fees directly in USDD without needing to hold TRX.

This removes another practical barrier to stablecoin adoption: having to acquire and maintain a separate gas token just to transfer digital dollars. With GasFree, eligible users can pay approximately 0.5 USDD per transaction, compared with around 1.5 USDT for a standard USDT transfer under the referenced fee conditions.

These improvements matter because the usefulness of a stablecoin is not determined by its peg alone. Low-cost transfers, straightforward wallet experiences, and around-the-clock availability are what make digital dollars practical for everyday payments and global value transfers.

Perhaps the sharpest contrast between banks and decentralized stablecoins lies in how yield is distributed.

In traditional banking, institutions take customer deposits, deploy them into yield-generating markets, and retain a significant portion of the returns. Everyday depositors may receive relatively low interest rates, while higher-yielding products often come with eligibility requirements, minimum deposits, or fixed maturity periods.

Stablecoins introduce another way to participate in digital-dollar opportunities. Rather than keeping capital entirely within a traditional deposit account, users can access onchain yield opportunities through decentralized protocols and transparent investment mechanisms.

USDD was built to return that value directly to holders. Through USDD Earn, users can deposit USDD to receive sUSDD, earning automated, competitive yield powered by our Smart Allocator system, with no fixed lock-up periods restricting access to their capital.

For users, the distinction is important: USDD Earn provides access to a productive stablecoin while maintaining flexibility, rather than requiring users to commit their capital to a conventional fixed-term product.

Traditional banks ask customers to trust closed-door balance sheets and periodic regulatory filings—until a crisis proves otherwise.

USDD takes a different approach through its “Don’t trust, verify” ethos, emphasizing publicly accessible onchain information and independent verification.

Transparency does not eliminate financial or technical risks, but it changes how users can evaluate them. Instead of relying exclusively on an institution’s assurances, users have access to information that can be independently examined and verified.

The bigger picture is an interconnected financial ecosystem in which users can move between holding, transferring, exchanging, and deploying their stablecoins according to their own needs. These are not isolated functions. Together, they make stablecoins more useful as everyday financial infrastructure.

Banks will undoubtedly continue using stablecoin rails to modernize their back-end settlement. However, true financial freedom comes from open networks where assets flow freely across protocols without intermediaries.

For USDD, the opportunity goes beyond offering a dollar-pegged asset. It is about making digital dollars accessible, transferable, and productive across an interconnected financial ecosystem. From self-custody and 24/7 transfers to flexible yield opportunities and multichain DeFi access, these capabilities give users more ways to put their money to work.

Banks may be bringing stablecoins into traditional finance. Crypto-native protocols are showing what stablecoins can make possible beyond it.
