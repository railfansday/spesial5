Hut 8 Expands AI Campus Commitments While Funding Buybacks With Bitcoin Sales

TL;DR: Hut 8 says it fully commercialised its Beacon Point AI campus with a second 352 MW lease, taking expected aggregate base-term contract value across its AI data-centre portfolio to about $26.6 billion. The company also sold Bitcoin to fund share repurchases, showing how mining-era assets are being redeployed.

Hut 8’s third-quarter update is less about how many coins it mined and more about how much power, construction capacity and long-term tenant demand it can assemble. That represents a significant shift for a company long associated with Bitcoin infrastructure.

In its November 4 results, Hut 8 said a second 352-megawatt IT lease at Beacon Point fully commercialised the campus. It placed expected aggregate base-term contract value across its AI data-centre portfolio at about $26.6 billion and reported 1,330 MW of utility capacity under construction across its major AI projects.

The numbers describe commitments over years, not cash that has already appeared in the bank. Hut 8’s timetable includes an initial River Bend data-hall delivery target in the second quarter of 2027, followed by Beacon Point Phase 1 in the third quarter of 2027 and Phase 2 in the second quarter of 2028.

Those future dates matter. A contracted tenant can improve the business case for a development, but execution still depends on power availability, construction, testing, financing and customer readiness. The risk profile is not identical to Bitcoin mining, where machines may be deployed in stages and production responds directly to network conditions.

Hut 8 also reported 1,300 MW of additional energy capacity entering exclusivity, bringing that category to 3,180 MW. Exclusivity is a development milestone; it should not be interpreted as an operating facility or a guaranteed utility connection. Investors need to track the movement from a pipeline into actual delivered infrastructure.

The company said it bought back $74.7 million of shares during the quarter, funded with proceeds from selling approximately 954 Bitcoin. It later repurchased another $175.3 million of shares, funded through the sale of roughly 2,148 additional Bitcoin. Subsequent to the quarter, the repurchase authorisation increased to $1 billion.

That is a revealing capital-allocation decision. Selling Bitcoin to repurchase shares reduces treasury exposure to the coin while potentially increasing each remaining shareholder’s proportional interest in the company. Whether that creates value depends on the repurchase price and long-term performance of the business.

Hut 8 also closed a $1.07 billion senior secured revolving credit facility. The existence of a facility adds flexibility, but readers should distinguish committed borrowing capacity from cash revenue or money actually drawn. NewsBTC has previously examined the financing pressure created by miners’ AI expansion, and Hut 8 offers a clear example of how those choices reach the balance sheet.

Hut 8 has a substantial development pipeline and named delivery targets. The crucial tests will be whether those targets are met, whether project-level financing stays on acceptable terms and how much profit flows through when sites become operational.

The update captures a broader change in listed Bitcoin infrastructure businesses. Their market stories increasingly revolve around electricity access and long-term AI customers rather than the number of Bitcoin retained. That can produce a different revenue profile, but it does not remove the need for disciplined spending or independently verifiable execution.
