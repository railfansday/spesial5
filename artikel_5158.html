Solana heads into breakpoint with $5B in RWAs – Why is SOL below THIS?

Solana’s Breakpoint 2026 conference couldn’t be coming at a better time.

This year’s event focuses on “The Token Supercycle”, looking at how Solana is becoming a key player in the stablecoin, RWA tokenization, and AI space. With the broader market in a risk-off mode, improving fundamentals and weak price action could strengthen SOL’s undervaluation narrative.

Interestingly, this trend may be starting to take off. Solana has seen an increase in its tokenization ecosystem with RWA value reaching a record high of $5 billion in October. Meanwhile, Ondo’s tokenized assets on Solana have also crossed $500 million, pointing to growing institutional interest in the network.

Notably, the momentum has continued into November.

The key takeaway? This growth adds weight to SOL’s undervaluation narrative. As one analyst pointed out, SOL was trading at around $140 on the back of the 2024 and 2025 Breakpoint conferences, but this time it’s trading at just $80, despite Solana making significant progress.

They went on to explain that Solana has had a lot of positive developments, such as block times of roughly 150ms, over $1.5 billion in cumulative ETF inflows and top DEX volume and active addresses. In short, Solana’s ecosystem has grown considerably, but SOL’s price has yet to catch up.

Does this make Solana’s [SOL] current dip a buying opportunity?

Solana is clearly emerging as one of the worst-performing high-cap assets in Q4 so far.

The altcoin is down by close to 40%, which is the lowest it has been since Q4 2025, when it finished the quarter with a 40.37% correction. Essentially, Solana has taken a bigger hit than most other high-cap assets during major market sell-offs, suggesting that each market downturn has had a deeper impact on SOL.

Notably, this is compounded by poor whale positioning. As seen below, a SOL whale has dumped an additional $33 million worth of SOL into Binance as SOL prices fall below $70. The wallet has just dumped 162,560 SOL (worth around $11.56 million) onto the exchange. This brings their total amount deposited over the last five days to 435,000 SOL, worth almost $32.96 million. The key takeaway? The whale still has 180,950 SOL left to dump.

Taken together, weak whale positioning and SOL’s relative underperformance suggest that the market is not fully convinced by its undervaluation narrative yet.

In this context, Breakpoint optimism, a new all-time low in RSI and seven red candles in a row will not be enough to validate that this is a bottom. Even though SOL has oversold conditions and great fundamental growth, there still needs to be more buying pressure to push it higher, leaving the $70 level exposed.

Solana’s growing RWA ecosystem and strong network activity support its undervaluation case.

Weak whale buying and a nearly 40% quarterly drop leave SOL’s $70 support at risk.
