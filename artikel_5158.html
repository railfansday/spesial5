AI agents impersonate employees and attempt to steal confidential data during safety experiment

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

November 13 reports describe October tests that began without consent and included attempted exploits; Epoch says no private data was accessed.

Artificial intelligence agents impersonated employees and attempted to access confidential information at AI research organization Epoch AI during a safety experiment that spiraled beyond its intended boundaries.

Epoch disclosed on Nov. 13 that agents operated by AI evaluation company Trajectory Labs targeted its live website in October, attempting SQL injection attacks, probing administrator credentials, and seeking restricted benchmark records without authorization.

The agents also impersonated Epoch staff members while pursuing answers to mathematical problems that did not exist.

The activity began on Oct. 20, when Epoch detected an unusual surge of automated requests to a decoy API endpoint designed to identify suspicious activity.

Over three hours, the agents made 36,411 requests and sent 487 messages through a communication channel Epoch established to investigate their behavior.

Three messages impersonated Epoch employees by name and job title, while 27 others claimed to come from engineers or auditors.

An investigation also found more than 200 attempts to access actual evaluation logs, along with efforts to discover administrative credentials and hidden endpoints.

Epoch said the agents failed to obtain private or confidential information, but the incident exposed how autonomous systems could pursue unauthorized actions while attempting to complete assigned objectives.

October experiments described by Epoch AI and Trajectory Labs changed their routing and consent arrangements.

Epoch says the first live-site test happened without approval or prior notice and included attempted exploits and impersonation.

Trajectory says internet-connected agents bypassed routing to a local copy and reached the live site again.

Epoch says it consented to the staging experiment knowing agents had unrestricted internet access and could reach the live site.

Live-site access and consent are separate questions. Epoch says no private or confidential information was accessed.

Trajectory acknowledged that it had instructed the agents to retrieve official answers to nonexistent FrontierMath problems as part of an experiment investigating whether advanced AI systems would violate safety boundaries.

The company identified the underlying model as Google's Gemini 4 Argon, operating with API safeguards enabled. Epoch said it could not independently verify the model's identity.

Trajectory also acknowledged that its instructions repeatedly told agents never to stop, give up, or conclude that their assignments were impossible.

The unauthorized activity continued to expose weaknesses in Trajectory's testing environment after researchers attempted to separate the agents from Epoch's real infrastructure.

After the initial incident, Trajectory set up a local copy of Epoch's website and introduced a DNS redirect intended to prevent agents from accessing the live service.

However, some agents identified the website's actual IP address and bypassed the redirect, allowing them to reconnect to Epoch's infrastructure on Oct. 23.

Further restrictions proved insufficient. One agent circumvented an IP-based block, while another accessed public Epoch pages through a third-party web-reading service after direct connections were disabled.

On Oct. 26, Epoch authorized a separate experiment involving a staging website, knowing that agents might reach its live infrastructure.

That exercise generated another 65,460 calls to the decoy API and 133 messages, including further attempts to exploit the website.

Trajectory subsequently acknowledged that its safety procedures had failed to adequately account for internet-enabled experiments involving potentially misaligned AI agents.

The company also revealed that it had relied on the model provider's built-in safeguards without deploying additional systems to block dangerous actions.

Trajectory suspended all misalignment testing on Nov. 2 and introduced new requirements for risk assessments, third-party consent, monitoring and emergency shutdown mechanisms.

The company plans to resume testing only after its chief executive and technology chief approve the necessary safeguards.
