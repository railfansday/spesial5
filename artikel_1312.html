Southampton Must Axe Mubama in January With Nmecha the Perfect Replacement

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

With gameweek 14 now complete in the Championship, Southampton know that they would be top of the table were it not for their four-point deduction.

Southampton have started the 2026-27 season where they left off last term, and while they look formidable at home, Tonda Eckert will know that there is an improvement to be made on the road with both of their league defeats coming away at Watford and Wrexham.

Only six points separates top-of-the-table Middlesbrough and 10th-placed Portsmouth, in what looks set to be another exciting campaign in England's second tier, where teams know that an eighth-place finish is now enough to guarantee a spot in the playoff positions.

On paper, Saints don't look to have too many weaknesses given they have the joint-best goal difference (15) in the league alongside West Ham, with only the Hammers and Boro scoring more than their total of 29.

Meanwhile, only Birmingham City have conceded fewer goals than their 14, with their defensive record improving significantly since the last international break.

The south coast outfit clearly have one of the strongest first-choice starting XIs in the division, but there are concerns about the strength in depth behind them in certain positions.

Defensively they largely look well covered, although there are question marks over the back-up to James Bree, while they are well stocked in central midfield, especially when all four of James Ward-Prowse, Caspar Jander, Flynn Downes and Cameron Bragg are fit.

The front four, when fit, picks itself, in Leo Scienza, Finn Azaz, Kuryu Matsuki and Cyle Larin, but the latter has missed the club's previous three matches through injury.

Southampton have dealt well without their top goalscorer with eight goals during that period, but there is an increasing feeling that Divin Mubama isn't at the level required when Larin is absent.

Divin Mubama for Southampton (Championship)

Eckert handed the Manchester City loanee is his first league start of the campaign in Tuesday's 1-1 draw with West Bromwich Albion, but he just isn't making the same impact that Larin, and more recently Lewis Dobbin, have in the centre-forward position.

Saints' German manager clearly doesn't rate him too highly either given most of his appearances have come as a late substitute, playing just 205 Championship minutes in total.

One of the reasons Southampton were so strong in the first half of 2026 is that Eckert was often bringing the likes of Shea Charles and Ross Stewart off the bench, but there is a feeling that their depth isn't quite as strong this time around.

Mubama isn't showing he is anywhere near the quality that Stewart was last term, and Saints need to put that right in the January transfer window.

The young striker surely won't be happy with his lack of minutes, and neither will Manchester City as they look for him to develop, so the best course of action unless things improve in the next two months would be to terminate the loan early and bring in someone who can have a bigger influence on the promotion push.

Sporting director Johannes Spors may have already identified the perfect replacement in Leeds United's Lukas Nmecha, with TEAMtalk reporting last month that the club is looking at a move for the seven-cap Germany international.

Nmecha joined Leeds on a free transfer in the summer of 2025, but with his contract due to expire at the end of this season, there is every chance he could be moving for nothing again.

The 27-year-old has seen his playing time in the Premier League this season limited, despite the fact he has scored seven goals in 47 outings for the Yorkshire outfit, having started just 10 top flight games.

Nmecha wants to play regularly and he will have a point to prove at his next club. The fact that he has played for Germany, and was a prolific scorer for Wolfsburg in the Bundesliga previously, makes him an attractive option for a club like Southampton, who want to be a Premier League club next term.

Convincing the German to play second tier football for six months could be a challenge, but if he can help them achieve promotion, then he could be a top-flight player again anyway.
