Martin Odegaard critics are wrong - Arsenal must give him what he deserves

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Your weekly briefing on Arsenal, featuring team news and expert analysis from Matt Verri

A quick glance at two contrasting stats sums up exactly where Arsenal’s shortcomings lie at the moment.

Mikel Arteta’s side are still in fine shape to progress straight through to the last 16 as one of the top-eight sides in the Champions League. Only the Gunners, holders Paris Saint-Germain and Real Madrid remain unbeaten in this season’s competition.

However, it could have been so much better, after an extraordinarily wasteful display in the draw with Borussia Dortmund on Tuesday night. Arsenal registered 30 shots, but only four on target and they scored just once.

It should have been so much more for Martin Odegaard, too. He has registered only one assist in Europe this season, ranking him 45th in the competition for that metric.

And yet for expected assists, per FotMob, Odegaard ranks top across the entire Champions League. Above Nico Paz, who has five assists to his name, and Lamine Yamal, who has three.

Arsenal have had no issue creating chances. Against Dortmund, Odegaard laid on nine of them, the most by any player in a Champions League match this season.

That was just the second time this decade that an Arsenal player has created more than five chances in a Champions League match. The other example? Odegaard against Napoli in September.

The discourse around Odegaard is often hypercritical. That, to an extent, is understandable. He has been the captain and creative heartbeat of the team in a period in which, for all it has been successful, there have been ongoing questions surrounding the attack.

It is perhaps also a hangover from the last two seasons, when injuries hampered Odegaard and he was well off his best. Frustration towards him built and some minds appear to have been made up.

However, the criticism that he does not deliver consistently enough just does not hold up to scrutiny this season.

From the moment Odegaard returned to the club in the summer after a strong World Cup with Norway, he has looked back to his best. That started with his sublime goal against Manchester City in the Community Shield and has continued since.

For all there have been some notable exceptions, Arsenal have on the whole played much better football this season. They have been far more free-flowing and fluid in attack.

Odegaard has been central to that. Along with his chance creation stats in Europe, Odegaard is also second for expected assists in the Premier League.

He is taking more responsibility in front of goal, too. Odegaard is Arsenal’s top scorer this season and is only one league goal shy of his combined tally from the last two seasons.

If there has been a dip in Arsenal’s attack from the start of this season, that is not down to Odegaard. Bukayo Saka, who was displaying far better goalscoring instincts, suffering a quad injury, and Kai Havertz’s performance levels dropping significantly are far more relevant explanations.

Arsenal do have a problem in attack - but it is not Odegaard

Odegaard has been the most consistent forward player. It is not just the chances created or even the goals, but his influence in all facets of Arsenal’s game as almost an extension of Arteta on the pitch. He is the leader of the press, and that led to the winning goal against Liverpool at Anfield earlier this month.

Arsenal do have a problem in attack - but it is not Odegaard. It is not an exaggeration to say that Viktor Gyokeres should have scored another three goals against Dortmund from chances created by Odegaard alone.

The Gunners are not clinical enough but that is not the fault of the player creating the chances. Nor is it a coaching issue. Arteta could not have done more to deliver a win against Dortmund.

The problem is a personnel one. The quality of Arsenal’s attackers does not come close to matching the level in every other position in the squad. At least one top-class winger signing is required, along with a new striker.

It was clear at the end of last season that significant upgrades were needed. Instead, Arsenal sold three forwards and signed only Christos Tzolis.

That is the big failure and where the fingers should be pointing. Not at Odegaard for occasionally taking too many touches or making a wrong decision on the counter.

Odegaard’s contract situation adds another factor to the debate over his role in the team. He is nearing the final 18 months of his current deal.

Talks have not yet begun over an extension, Odegaard revealed during the international break, but that should be a no-brainer for Arsenal.

That will require the club to make another significant financial commitment, following on from several other new contracts for key players, but it is still a far cheaper alternative to trying to sign a replacement of Odegaard’s quality.

Securing Odegaard’s future should be the club’s next top priority, swiftly followed by then delivering him the forwards he deserves.
