The Newcastle United defence has been the subject of much focus this season.

Not a lot of the debate on it has been positive.

So has the criticism that’s come the way of the Newcastle United defence been fair?

In the ten Premier League matches so far, this has been the story of the Newcastle United defence.

What I think should be acknowledged first of all, is that we have lacked a settled Newcastle United defence. Injuries playing havoc.

Secondly, there has been undeniable improvement. The Newcastle United defence leaked 13 goals in the opening six Premier League matches, only three goals conceded in the last four. In the last six and half hours (including added time) of Premier League action, United conceding just those three goals, an average of around a goal conceded each 130 minutes following the defeat at Coventry.

This is not to say the United defence were almost faultless, more a case of they are improving.

When you look at those three goals that were conceded most recently in the Premier League, it gives added information. The Villa goal came when a cross from the right eluded Lewis Miley, a midfielder playing out of position at right-back. The Everton one was pure and simply a poor error from Lukas Hornicek who allowed a low standard cross to squirm under. Then the one at Fulham came via deflections off both Thiaw (in the build up) and Burn (to turn it beyond Hornicek.

Yes the opposition had other chances in these games but not loads.

Reality is as well that if Newcastle United had managed at the other end to score a couple of goals in each of these last four matches then it would have been 12 points out of 12 in the last quartet of Premier League games.

That is very different to the opening half dozen EPL matches, where Newcastle United scored six goals in the four games against Liverpool, Coventry, Leeds and Bournemouth but that only brought two points from a possible 12. The Newcastle United defending a lot better against Tottenham and Hull for the two victories early on, with only a single goal conceded in more than three hours of football against that pair.

Now though I think things could go to another level again with this Newcastle United defence, if a bit of fair luck starts going our way on the injury and fitness front.

An international level Newcastle United defence.

Lewis Hall is brilliant, for England and Newcastle United. Surely no doubters amongst you on that one.

Dan Burn has been a huge miss in the opening seven Premier League matches this season. No coincidence only two goals conceded in the three EPL matches he has started on his return after a length injury spell. He has now backed that up with how he has performed for England in recent days.

We saw last season how good Malick Thiaw can be and I think the Fulham match definitely suggested than alongside Dan Burn we could at last have the best available partnership in place finally, of the current NUFC squad. Thiaw has started the last three Germany matches under Jurgen Klopp and they have only conceded one goal.

Tino Livramento was back in the Premier League matchday squad at Fulham and these couple of weeks should have brought him along further. For me there is no doubt about Tino’s quality, the problem has been getting him injury free and back to full fitness on a consistent basis.

Lukas Hornicek has had an up and down start with Newcastle United but taking everything into consideration, I think he has shown plenty to suggest he can do well, especially if he has a regular back four in front of him who have the capacity to give him decent protection week in week out.

Having the three England internationals and our German international making up a Newcastle United back four has huge potential to build the kind of solid platform NUFC need this season.

If Tino Livramento continues to have injury and fitness issues, I believe Amar Dedić is capable of doing a very good job in his absence. I think the Bosnian defender has done okay overall so far, having made the huge step up to the Premier League and walking into what was a chaotic NUFC situation.

If we can have the Newcastle United defence performing consistently well, that then gives us far more chance of improving things further up the pitch, helping United to have far more control of matches and giving a much more positive platform for midfielders and attacking players to prosper.
