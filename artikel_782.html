'A bit strange' - Leclerc seeking outlier to catch Mercedes in Qatar

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Mercedes dominated Friday's practice sessions at the Qatar Grand Prix and Charles Leclerc suggested Ferrari, McLaren and Red Bull are missing something; watch final practice at 2.30pm ahead of Qualifying at 6pm on Saturday live on Sky Sports F1

Sports Journalist @NigelCJourno

Charles Leclerc believes an “outlier” has given Mercedes a “massive” advantage at the Qatar Grand Prix, after Kimi Antonelli and George Russell dominated Friday’s practice sessions.

Mercedes were nearly half a second quicker than the rest of the field at the Lusail International Circuit as Antonelli topped both sessions from Russell.

Their four-tenths advantage over closest challenger Lando Norris in second practice could have been even greater, with neither Antonelli nor Russell completing a full low-fuel lap on the soft tyres. It was an ominous sign for the rest of the field ahead of Saturday's qualifying, which gets under way at 6pm, with coverage live on Sky Sports F1 from 5.15pm.

Mercedes' dominance was similar to their pace advantage earlier in the season but the second half of 2026 has largely been very close between F1's top four teams - Mercedes, Ferrari, Red Bull and McLaren.

Ferrari were over one second behind Antonelli on a dismal Friday as Leclerc spun in second practice and Lewis Hamilton suffered an engine problem which brought his Ferrari to a halt.

"Honestly it's been a very difficult day. It's not that we didn't try on the softs, we couldn't improve our lap time on the mediums," said Leclerc.

"I lost the car and had some damage so we had to repair the car but that was the first lap of FP2 and I couldn't improve and even on the new soft later on I couldn't improve.

"A very, very difficult day. The feeling is just very difficult to get right. Not even on high fuel we were strong, so for now, it looks like a very difficult weekend but we are working hard to try and understand something.

"For some reason, Mercedes are so much faster than everybody expected, so the gaps are just a little bit strange, so there's something that's an outlier and we just need to find that."

Ferrari were not expected to be strong at the Lusail International Circuit due to the long flat out zones and high-speed corners but Leclerc suggested his team, along with McLaren and Red Bull were missing a trick that will give them more pace.

"I don't think we know yet what's the outlier. I think other teams probably didn't find that because Mercedes still have a massive advantage," he said.

"We are going to work towards that and analyse the data to understand what's going on."

McLaren were predicted to be very competitive in Qatar due to their strength in the high-speed corners but Norris and Piastri were never close to matching the pace of Mercedes.

On Saturday, McLaren are expected to put the new floor they ran in Las Vegas on the car but Norris does not think it will make a big enouch difference to catch Mercedes who are in "a different category".

"I don't know how much that will help around here compared to Vegas," said last year's world champion.

"But obviously tonight [Friday] we will see how much we can gain from that. It's not going to be the gap we need.

"We are probably seven tenths off the Mercedes a lap so it's not even worth thinking about them. But relative to the rest we seem to be in a good place. The fact we can have the new floor on tomorrow will add a little bit and a little bit is better than nothing. We seem in a relatively decent place."

Both Antonelli and Russell pointed out that Verstappen will always be a threat and the four-time world champion is coy about his chances of challenging the Mercedes in qualifying.

"A bit of a difficult day. The balance of the car is a bit too disconnected at the moment to go fast, so that's what we need to fix," said the Red Bull driver.

"The all-round balance is not there at the moment and is costing us quite a bit of lap time. We will keep trying and see how good we can get it."

"Mercedes look quite far ahead on the short run and long run. Let's see how much we can close the gap."
