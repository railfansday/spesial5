Hasan Piker’s Lawyer Says Suing Scott Jennings is Just the Beginning

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The clash between left-wing political commentator Hasan Piker and his media detractors is moving from the chatroom to the courtroom. One week after Piker filed a $72.5 million defamation suit against conservative commentator and CNN personality Scott Jennings, his attorney, defamation lawyer Daniel Watkins, tells Vanity Fair Piker is “just getting started.”

“There are other people and other entities that will likely receive outreach from my law firm in the coming days,” says Watkins. “They know they have a reckoning coming.”

The Jennings lawsuit, which accuses the former Republican strategist of falsely telling listeners of his Salem Media podcast that Piker advocated for lynchings, comes at the end of a busy, and tumultuous election cycle for Piker, who—after years of online politicking—became a popular surrogate for far-left insurgents, and a popular boogeyman for virtually everyone else. It’s an apparent payback for the mainstream media, whose obsession with villainizing Piker has meant guilt-by-association for everyone Piker champions (and has far outpaced Piker’s actual name-recognition and influence).

On the podcast, which aired on August 11, Jennings shared a spliced montage of the Twitch streamer appearing to make racist and incendiary remarks, some of them about lynching. It’s packaged as an attempt to dissuade Michigan voters from voting for Senatorial candidate Abdul El-Sayed, Piker’s “best pal and campaign partner,” Jennings said.

I watched the October 2024 stream from which the most inflammatory snippets in Jennings’s sizzle reel were spliced together. On the stream, Piker is live-reacting to a YouTube video from the popular channel Jubilee, in which a Black conservative debates a Black liberal.

At one point, an incredulous Piker pauses the video and very clearly mocks the Black conservative by saying, “Can we go back to a better time when lynchings were happening?” At another point, after a commenter asks why he’s “always doing too much with black people” Piker responds sarcastically, “Yes dude, it’s because I’m racist, that’s why. It’s because I’m fucking racist dude.” Both of these clips are included in the Jennings segment without context.

At the end of August Watkins sent a retraction demand and spoke to Christopher Henderson, Salem Media’s chief legal officer. Absent a reply, they proceeded with a lawsuit. (At the time of publication Henderson and Jennings have not responded to a request for comment.)

Piker’s suit against Jennings is seeking $27.5 million in compensatory damages, and $45 million in punitive damages. Piker says he left it to the lawyers to crunch the numbers. “I don't know what I'm worth, if we're being real,” Piker told Vanity Fair in an interview this week.

“I have been very deliberately anti-litigation for a very long time,” Piker said, “but something shifted that’s been bubbling up where…people like Scott Jennings, and there are others as well,” he claims, “started trafficking in outright lies, defamation, and slander.”

“It is particularly pernicious to misattribute a quote to someone,” Watkins said. He explained that for Piker, “who has built a career at a platform based on advocating for marginalized people and taking stances that are not necessarily popular,” such misattributions are “disgusting and vile and dangerous.”

For Piker, the turn to legal action is partly a pro-truth crusade against “pundits who’ve gotten far too comfortable thinking that they can just get away with making ridiculous claims about people.” He pointed out what he claims is a pattern where outlets circulate clips that omit key words, fundamentally transforming the sentiments of their subjects. “I’ve observed this with the likes of James Talarico, for example. He’ll say something and then they’ll cut it right before you can associate with the full context of the statement,” Piker said.

He also admitted that it’s personal. “First and foremost, [the lawsuit is] for me.” He cited what he alleges is “reputational harm,” “emotional harm,” and “financial damages,” sharing that the lynching comment had spurred someone to “stalk a public event [he] was at.”

“When is the last time you experienced eight consistent months of [mainstream media] having one primary villain?” Piker asked. “It’s not fun at all. It sucks.”

Discussing the Michigan race where the controversy with Jennings began, Piker noted El-Sayed’s primary opponent Haley Stevens’s surprising Sunday endorsement of Republican nominee Mike Rogers. Explaining her party defection, Stevens made specific reference to Piker, criticizing El-Sayed for associating with him.

Piker believes Stevens’s statements will backfire in El-Sayed’s favor as the votes are tallied in Michigan.

“Okay, a Twitch streamer is the reason why you’re endorsing ICE raids in every city?” Piker said, referencing Rogers’s platform. “That’s ridiculous.”

Piker maintains, as he has since July, that his political interventions do more good than harm—for everyone except him. In the case of his surrogacy for El-Sayed, he said, “The only person who got attacked in the process was me. I was the one who had his reputation destroyed in the process.”

As for Piker’s own relationship with El-Sayed, he admits it’s not quite as buddy-buddy as Jennings suggested. Piker stumped for the Michigander in the primaries before receding somewhat. “My relationship with him has always been one of…a transactional nature,” he said.

Piker will spend election night streaming live from his home in LA and checking in periodically with candidates. When the midterms are over, he seems prepared to continue delivering comeuppance to those he feels have unfairly represented him during this election cycle.

So who else can expect to get hit with a lawsuit?

Piker didn’t say, exactly, but he did note that Ronna McDaniel, the former chair of the RNC, who, on C-Span last week, repeated the claim that Piker supports reinstating lynchings. He also pointed to Sen. Ted Cruz, who re-shared the clip from Jennings on X. Last week, Piker responded to the X post with a link to the news of his lawsuit against Jennings and wrote, “hey ted you seen this ?” (Cruz and McDaniel did not respond to a request for comment.)

When asked if Piker’s post on X was a clue as to a future lawsuit against Cruz, Watkins answered: “Buckle up.”

Piker did not disclose other specifics regarding future litigation, saying only this: “if you felt as though you could outright fabricate quotes and make up totally ridiculous claims about me, you should probably work on your retractions immediately.”
