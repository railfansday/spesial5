Nurse Broke Kid's Rib? Doc Accused of Illegal Pics; Quest's $13M Giants Tickets

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

— A weekly roundup of healthcare's encounters with the courts

Daniel Keate, a pediatric nurse in Kentucky, was arrested on domestic violence charges, including an allegation that he squeezed a 5-year-old family member so hard it broke her rib. (The News-Enterprise)

Families of 13 children sued Michigan urologist Bryan Sack, MD, and two hospitals alleging Sack took pictures of the kids' genitals outside of legitimate medical purposes, and uploaded them to a Dropbox account. (Click On Detroit)

Police investigated a bomb threat at Boston Children's Hospital, and ultimately gave the all-clear. (Boston 25 News)

Brian Monaco, MD, of Louisiana, was arrested on charges of sexual battery, domestic abuse battery by strangulation, and three counts of domestic abuse battery with child endangerment. (KSLA)

A former Eli Lilly sales rep filed a lawsuit alleging she was fired for complaining that the company was marketing tirzepatide (Mounjaro) for weight loss before its regulatory approval for that indication. (Seeking Alpha)

A second patient alleged that a podiatrist at a Kaiser Permanente facility in Oregon injected her with rubbing alcohol instead of a painkiller before tearing off her toenail. (OregonLive)

CEO impersonators allegedly stole nearly $8 million from two hospital systems by tricking employees into transferring the money into fraudulent bank accounts. (Becker's Hospital Review)

Quest Diagnostics paid more than $13 million to settle allegations that it paid kickbacks to doctors and healthcare executives, including free tickets to suites at Met Life Stadium for New York Giants games, to induce referrals, federal prosecutors said.

A jail nurse in Arizona was arrested after law enforcement officials found drug paraphernalia during a car search. (12 News)

Five states have joined a Federal Trade Commission lawsuit alleging telehealth provider Hims and Hers shared sensitive health information with third-party advertisers, the agency announced.

A California lab will pay $1.25 million to settle allegations that it paid kickbacks to marketers, the Justice Department said.
