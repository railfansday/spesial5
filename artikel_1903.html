20 Best Soccer Players of the 2010s Ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Correspondent AUTHORITY Football journalist with experience at Reach PLC and PA Media; University of Central Lancashire graduate. FOCUS Fan-engagement content and match analysis across the Premier League and Championship. THE INSIGHT Oliver utilises a network of club and industry contacts to deliver verified reporting and fan-first content. He provides the human stories behind the results to ensure fans get the full picture as it happens.

The 2010s delivered one of the greatest golden eras in soccer history, defined by unforgettable Champions League nights and World Cup drama.

At the center of it all stood Lionel Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo, pushing each other to historic Ballon d’Or totals while dominating El Clásico for Barcelona and Real Madrid.

Alongside them, endless stars produced peak performances that captivated fans from Europe to the United States.

Football Insider has ranked the 20 best soccer players of the 2010s strictly on their output, trophies, and individual brilliance between 2010 and 2019.

Scroll down to see who makes our top five, and let us know in the comments who we left off the list.

Benzema sneaks into the top 20 but cannot go any higher because he actually reached the peak of his powers in this current decade, when he won the 2022 Ballon d'Or. The French striker was the ultimate foil in one of the most iconic forward lines in soccer history throughout the 2010s, and had huge pedigree to boot.

He was key to Madrid winning an unprecedented four Champions League titles between 2014 and 2018, was named in the La Liga Team of the Season twice and scored over 200 goals for the club in the alloted timeframe. He did lack international pedigree, though, after spending five years away from Le Bleus from 2015 onwards.

Ibrahimovic's career started in 1999, but he arguably dominated the 2010s more than any other decade while playing for four different clubs in as many countries. The charismatic ponytailed forward recovered from a tough period at Barcelona to further his reputation as one of the great players of his generation.

He scored over 20 goals in every full season during the decade and won five league titles across spells at AC Milan and PSG. The Swede dominated everywhere he went, even with Man United, and also had time to become one of the most individually decorated MLS players ever in a successful stint at LA Galaxy, but international glory always eluded him.

Marcelo may not be that highly ranked, but he can take pride in being the only left-back to make this list. That is simply because he consistently outperformed his peers every year, and did it while playing for one of the most successful club teams of all time. The eccentric Brazilian was a cornerstone in Madrid's aforementioned superteam.

He truly established himself as one of the best in his position of all time in this decade, winning heaps of club and personal honours and eventually making both the IFFHS and France Football Team of the Decades.

Ribery may not be revered worldwide as one of the all-time greatest, but he was certainly a force to be reckoned with in the 2010s as he maintained a consistently high level rarely matched by other wingers of the era. He utilised every bit of his pace and trickery to help Bayern dominate.

The French forward enjoyed a superb peak, as he made the final three-man shortlist for the 2013 Ballon d'Or following a season in which he helped fire them to Champions League glory and was the top assister in the Bundesliga. He wound down towards the end of the decade but was a true great in his prime for the German giants and a key man at international level.

Right-back Alves, much like his compatriot and rival Marcelo, became a standout in his own position as Barcelona ran riot in Spain and the rest of Europe for much of the 2010s.

He was the first choice in the position for Barca between 2010 and 2016, and with Brazil until 2019, becoming one of the most decorated players in soccer history with over 30 trophies won throughout the decade. He also tasted success as a mainstay for Juventus and PSG, proving that he could also dominate in other countries.

Kroos also formed part of a dominant Madrid side and became known as one of the greatest soccer midfielders ever in this era. He was calmness personified and had an unbelievable eye for a pass which made him a mainstay at the Santiago Bernabeu.

He helped Germany win the World Cup in 2014 as the tournament's top assister, while also lifting countless league and continental trophies at Bayern Munich and then Madrid, making a switch to Spain after winning on the world stage. Kroos will always be remembered as a midfielder who helped define the decade after others had moved past their peak.

The first player to primarily feature in the Premier League to make this list, Aguero was one of the faces of English football for so many years as Man City began to dominate and overtake the traditional top four sides. His 2011 signing truly kickstarted their glory era, particularly as he went on to score the most iconic goal in the league's history to win their first title since 1968.

Aguero won countless trophies in his time at the Etihad, both team and personal, scoring over 220 goals for the club before 2020 came around. The only thing stopping him coming any higher on this list was injuries, as his impact was somewhat restricted by recurring absences at City.

Hazard was an unplayable genius of a winger who could win matches all by himself when on song. He became a Premier League great at Chelsea and scored countless key goals in their pursuit of success throughout the decade.

He started the 2010s firing Lille to an unprecedented Ligue 1 title, then lifted plenty of silverware in a seven-year Stamford Bridge stint that saw him rip defences apart and also shine internationally in Belgium's Golden Generation. Hazard made the Premier League Team of the Year in four different seasons and was later named in the Blues' All-Time XI.

Robben was another who shone at Bayern throughout the 2010s, offering different qualities to left-wing teammate Ribery and arguably outshining him through key moments and better consistency. He was absolutely deadly when cutting onto his strong foot from the right flank and even made the bald head an iconic part of his look.

The Dutch star registered over 120 goals and over 90 assists for Bayern in the decade, scoring the winning goal in their 2013 Champions League success. He was a main player for the Netherlands as they just fell short in the 2010 and 2014 World Cups.

Neuer was not only the best goalkeeper of his generation, but he will also go down as one of the greatest to play between the sticks in soccer history. He redefined what it meant to be a keeper in the decade, pioneering the sweeper role and maintaining outstanding consistency for club and country.

The 2014 World Cup winner also lifted two Champions Leagues in the 2010s as captain of Bayern, becoming the only goalkeeper ever to achieve two European trebles. He made the IFFHS World Team of the Decade and firmly established himself as an all-time great.

Bale was an international superstar at his peak, figuring regularly at the highest level for the biggest and most dominant club in the world during the previous decade. He had a knack of scoring in the biggest moments and his overhead kick in the 2018 Champions League final may be the most iconic strike in the history of the competition.

He powered Wales to an unprecedented semi-final berth at the 2016 European Championships and was key to Los Blancos' huge successes alongside a plethora of greats. Those achievements came after he was arguably the best player in the Premier League between 2010 and 2013 at Tottenham.

Lewandowski became one of the greatest pure goalscorers in the history of soccer in this decade. His elite finishing, exceptional anticipation and underrated physicality combined to make him a serious force and certainly one of the finest strikers in this century so far.

Only three players scored more than him in Europe’s top five leagues in the 2010s, and the Polish superstar also matured into his country’s greatest ever player by some distance in the same timeframe. He helped Borussia Dortmund rise again and then dominated alongside others on this list at Bayern, winning countless honours and making IFFHS’ World Team of the Decade.

Neymar’s amazing natural ability saw him become one of the world’s greatest and most famous athletes in the previous decade. He was maybe the closest of anyone to becoming a consistent top-two player in the world at the time, and also remains the most expensive player ever after joining PSG for £200m in 2017.

Selecao soccer royalty in his own right, even though the World Cup eluded him despite his outstanding career, he formed part of what many believe is the best front three of all time at Barcelona and came third in the Ballon d’or voting twice. He arguably peaked too soon in the middle of the 2010s, and has failed to reach such heights in this decade.

Xavi oozed class and elegance throughout his playing days, building on an already excellent career in the 2010s as Barca went from strength to strength with their star-studded side. He was the constant in that team, undeterred by the competition and part of one of the best midfield threes ever known.

He may not rank as high as expected because his career at the top level effectively ended in 2015, but the Spaniard was key to 2010 World Cup success and barely put a foot wrong in those five years to help the Catalan machine keep going full steam ahead. Xavi finished third in Ballon d’Or voting in 2010 and 2011.

Ramos was another star of that iconic Real Madrid side, and he established himself as one of the greatest centre-backs in soccer history during this era. The Sevilla academy product matured into an absolute warrior at the back who led his team into battle with undying passion and determination, as well as fantastic quality in every facet of his game.

He was named in the UEFA Team of the Season in all four of their Champions League-winning campaigns, while also popping up with huge goals and displaying impressive offensive abilities on a regular basis. Ramos not only impressed for his club, he was also a regular in Spain’s 2010 World Cup and 2012 Euro-winning tournaments too.

Now comes the man who scored that memorable winner in the 2010 World Cup final. Iniesta’s effortless class made him the perfect partner for Xavi, as he mostly took on a more attacking role for club and country, creating chances at will with a deft touch and unbelievable close control.

He was named as the Euro 2012 Player of the Tournament, and La Liga’s Best Midfielder for four years in a row between 2011 and 2014. Iniesta ranks above his former midfield teammate on moments alone, and because he remained a regular for the Camp Nou side until 2018. Sir Alex Ferguson famously confessed that he felt Iniesta was the main “danger” of the iconic Barcelona side of the early 2010s, rather than Lionel Messi.

Few forwards in modern soccer have terrorized defenders quite like Luis Suarez at the height of his powers. After joining Liverpool in 2011, "El Pistolero" capped his Anfield spell with one of the greatest individual seasons in Premier League history in 2013/14—scoring an astonishing 31 goals in just 33 league appearances without taking a single penalty. That breathtaking campaign nearly carried Liverpool to a long-awaited title, sweeping England’s Player of the Year awards and earning him a share of the European Golden Shoe. Controversy was never too far away as his will to win too often crossed the line, but his technical skill and ability to wriggle away from defences in tight spaces was thrilling to watch.

Suarez reached another stratosphere after moving to Barcelona in 2014, forming the iconic "MSN" trident alongside Lionel Messi and Neymar. He fired Barca to a historic treble in his debut season before exploding for 40 La Liga goals in 2015/16 to outscore both Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo for a second European Golden Shoe, cementing his spot as the deadliest pure striker of the decade.

Modric’s peak was higher than any player below him on this list during the 2010s, and this is why he slots surefootedly into the top three. The silky Croatian could change a game within seconds and had everything a midfielder needed to dominate opponents at the highest level throughout his prime. He became the best player ever from his country during this decade.

That is because he was the one to upset the established order, being crowned the first Ballon d’Or winner not called Messi or Ronaldo since 2007 after an outstanding 2018 that saw Croatia reach the World Cup final, as he picked up the Golden Ball, and Madrid lift a third successive Champions League crown. His victory was hugely significant as it ended an era of dominance - and cemented the unassuming midfield kingpin as an all-time great.

Ronaldo shaped the 2010s as a global superstar both on and off the pitch, as the decade transformed him from a newly-established great of his time to one of the most revered sportspeople to ever walk the earth. His unmistakable charm and aura, coupled with extraordinary athleticism and consistency in front of goal, all combined to see him challenge for the biggest prizes every season and year without fail.

He won four Ballon d’Or awards, four Champions League titles and helped fire Portugal to Euro success in 2016. CR7 also adapted to his strengths and went from being a tricky winger to a devastatingly prolific centre-forward by 2019. He broke club and competition records galore in a decade of relentless brilliance as one half of an iconic rivalry that will never be seen again.

No player has ever dominated a single decade of soccer quite like Lionel Messi did during the 2010s. Operating at his absolute peak for Barcelona, the Argentine magician turned the impossible into a weekly routine—shattering Gerd Müller’s single-year scoring record with an astonishing 91 goals in 2012 and finishing the decade with 522 club goals in just 521 appearances.

His numbers between 2010 and 2019 border on video-game fiction, leading Europe’s top five leagues in both goals and assists while powering Barcelona to seven La Liga titles, two Champions League crowns, and the 2015 continental treble. Messi claimed five Ballon d’Or trophies across the decade (2010, 2011, 2012, 2015, and 2019) to reach six overall, cementing his place at the top of the era—and sparking the fiercest GOAT debate in sports.
