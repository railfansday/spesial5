Conte: ‘Chelsea did not want Terry, so my wife made him pasta’

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Antonio Conte reveals how his wife made pasta for John Terry and changed the course of his Chelsea career. ‘The club had no intention of renewing his contract, he wasn’t even mentioned.’

The Italian tactician sat down with podcast Cronache di Spogliatoio and looked back over his long experience in Italy and England, including the Premier League title and FA Cup at Stamford Bridge from 2016 to 2018.

He explained that Terry’s playing career could’ve been so very different, as before he became a legend for the London club, he was about to be pushed out of the door.

“Terry was a fundamental figure in our Premier League title victory,” said Conte.

“A solid guy, he flew with a private jet along with his agent from London to see me in Turin. His contract was running down, he decided to pay for the flight to visit me.

“He basically said I love Chelsea so much that I want to keep going. Until that day, we had never talked about Terry. Marina Abramovich said he wanted to come and talk to me, but they had no intention of renewing his contract.

“My wife ended up making pasta for John Terry and his agent, who I had not met before. I assured him, I don’t care about how old you are, but if you prove that you deserve to play in training, then I will play you.

“The only promise you have to make is that even if you don’t play, you are still added value in the locker room, because you represent the history of this club.”

At that time, Terry was 35 years old and Conte confirms Chelsea had not the slightest intention of keeping him in the squad, ready to let him leave as a free agent.

“He simply wasn’t mentioned, that was the season when they finished 10th. I’d met all the Chelsea players a month earlier, but they didn’t let me meet Terry, because he just wasn’t on their radar,” added the coach.

Conte also reveals how Terry represented the Chelsea squad prompting a complaint for his very Italian way of working, which at the time seemed unusual to Premier League players.

“Terry came to me in training one day and said the players felt we were doing too much tactical work, too many videos. When my captain comes and tells me that, I have to speak to the staff and move towards them.

“It might be a coincidence, but after that we drew with Swansea, lost to Arsenal, so I thought we’re going back to my approach. If we have to die, we die with my ideas,” insisted Conte.

“The dressing room was bubbling up, ready to revolt, but then after another break, Terry comes to me. ‘Hey gaffer, we’ve got to keep doing the tactical stuff, because these things actually happened during the game!’

“They realised he’s not just telling us things for no reason, they are situations you see in the match, and that changed everything,” concluded Conte.
