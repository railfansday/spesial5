Carrick weighs up stripping Bruno Fernandes of Man Utd captaincy

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

A defiant Michael Carrick refused to rule out stripping Bruno Fernandes of the captaincy as the Manchester United manager told his players to cut out their public sniping and start doing their talking on the pitch.

Carrick struck a bullish tone as he came out fighting and moved to reassert his authority after a tumultuous past fortnight that has left him battling to save his job ahead of Sunday’s must-win game against Bournemouth at Old Trafford.

The United manager began his press conference on Friday afternoon with a stirring five-and-a-half-minute monologue in which he issued an emotional call to arms to his players to demonstrate the values of hard work, sacrifice, humility and resilience that define the club.

Referencing Sir Matt Busby, Sir Alex Ferguson, Sir Bobby Charlton, Ryan Giggs and Wayne Rooney, Carrick warned there could be no “entitlement” culture in echoes of complaints from his predecessor Ruben Amorim.

United are actively considering their options in the event Carrick cannot quickly arrest a shocking start to the season that hit a new low with Wednesday’s 4-1 humiliation by Como. The likes of Julian Nagelsmann, Eddie Howe, Unai Emery, Fabian Hürzeler and Antonio Conte are in the frame.

But Carrick insisted he was “100 per cent” convinced he was the right man to take United forward and, while he claimed the dressing room was “not divided”, he has had to contend with first defender Lisandro Martínez and then Fernandes venting in public.

Martínez was fined and publicly apologised for an extraordinary tirade at Jason Wilcox, the director of football, and the Old Trafford board last week before Fernandes appeared to call out Carrick’s tactics in the wake of the Como debacle.

Carrick responded at the time to having his set-up and approach questioned by suggesting tactics count for little if players are unprepared to run.

Asked if he would consider taking the captaincy off Fernandes, Carrick said: “I’m just saying I’ll always – whatever the situation is – do what’s best for the club.”

Pressed on whether Fernandes’s comments were all the more insidious and divisive at a time when the manager was trying to pull the squad together so soon after Martínez’s outburst, Carrick added: “Yeah, the most important thing for me is the team.

“First and foremost is the squad, the dynamics in the squad. And again, it’s about the club. And I’ll always do what’s best for the club.”

Carrick, Wilcox and United chief executive Omar Berrada held a meeting with Martínez in the wake of his criticism but the manager would not be drawn on whether he had spoken with Fernandes and it remains to be seen if he will be captain or start against Bournemouth.

“If I’ve spoken to him or haven’t spoken to him, I don’t think this is the time to go into too much detail,” he said.

Carrick agreed that United collectively needed to stop turning on each other in public. “We need to move forward in the right direction. We need to do things that enable us to do that. That’s pretty clear,” he said.

“It [the dressing room] is not divided, no. We just need to get back to doing what we do best and we will.”

Separately, Carrick expressed sympathy for Fernandes over the “awful” burglary at his home on Wednesday night when he was in Italy, and said the club “are doing a lot of work to support Bruno and his family”.

Carrick is under huge pressure heading into Bournemouth but said he was happy to take the heat if it enabled his players to perform.

“It is my responsibility whatever goes on the pitch, and what we are as a group, and what we look like, and what we feel like,” he said. “I’ll hold that umbrella for me to get wet and for everyone else to stay dry.”

Nonetheless, Carrick admitted he was “hurting an awful lot” and that the team had to get back to showing the values and qualities that have underpinned the club’s historic success after a “really unacceptable” performance against Como and wider struggles. United have won two of 11 matches this season.

“I’m not entitled to be here,” Carrick said. “We’re not entitled to win. We’ve won in the past, we’ve got an incredible history, but that doesn’t mean you’re entitled to just win.

“I’m fully aware of that. It takes hard work, a lot of sacrifice. You’ve got to invest yourself in the club and you’ve got to do a lot of things that maybe aren’t so nice, but you’ve got to do it for each other, and you’ve got to win.

“It’s important we live up to that, and it’s important for me to push those values, and for our supporters to feel that moving forward.

“We’ve kind of lost our way a bit if I’m honest, and we will get it back. I’m totally confident we’ll get it back. Respect this club, respect what it takes, because it’s very important to me.”

Carrick added that he was inspired by the values of “humility, hard work, resilience, sacrifice and pride” embodied by the likes of Busby, Ferguson, Charlton, Giggs and Rooney.

“We can never lose that, and I won’t let us lose that,” he said. “We’ve had it for a spell, I feel very strongly about that. It inspires me a lot to live up to that and keep driving that with the players.

“There’s a resilience about this club that’s been shown for a long time. Whether that’s bouncing back from adversity, comebacks, late goals, success, sustaining success, there’s a resilience in this club that runs very deep.”

Carrick was trailed by a camera crew from Amazon Prime as he entered the media auditorium at Manchester United’s Carrington training base at 1.30pm on Friday.

There have been plenty of dark moments at Old Trafford in the post-Sir Alex Ferguson era. But the difference this season is that any internal drama will soon be available for public consumption after the decision to allow cameras into a once-sacrosanct dressing room.

Whether Carrick will see out the next week, let alone a season being captured by filmmakers from Amazon’s All Or Nothing series, who probably cannot believe their luck at the unfolding crisis, remains to be seen. He certainly needs a big reaction against Bournemouth at Old Trafford at the very least.

But it became clear as he began his press conference with an emotional 5½-minute monologue that he would not go down without a fight.

Michael Carrick is not one of the Premier League’s more loquacious managers but supporters will be interested in what he has to say at his pre-Bournemouth press conference this afternoon.

The Manchester United manager is under severe pressure after a run of no wins in seven games across all competitions, and senior players are talking out of school in post-game interviews.

Just when it appeared a line had been drawn under Lisandro Martinez taking on technical director Jason Wilcox, Bruno Fernandes reached for a petrol can and poured more fuel on the flames.

Speaking after the dismal 4-1 Champions League defeat by Como, Fernandes called out Carrick and his coaching staff for their tactical approach, specifically a reluctance to press opponents aggressively in a man-to-man fashion.

Carrick, by his own very measured standards, delivered a pointed response.

“We actually were half-pressed man to man in the second half and then conceded a goal from that,” he said. “And there’s a little bit on whatever system you play, whatever tactics you play, if you don’t run, and you don’t run forward, and you don’t run back, you ain’t going to win.”

Their opponents on Sunday are Bournemouth, who after a lean run of results under Marco Rose have just dispatched Sunderland for their first league victory of the season, and the mighty AC Milan in the Europa League.

Things do not get easier for United, who go to Stamford Bridge next weekend before a home game against Aston Villa before the November international break.

Telegraph Sport reported on Thursday that former Bayern Munich and Germany manager Julian Nagelsmann features prominently and is understood to want the job – as does Antonio Conte. United are also tracking Fabian Hurzeler while there is interest in Eddie Howe.
