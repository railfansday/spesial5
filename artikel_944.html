Danny Kelly obituary

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Ebullient journalist, magazine editor and broadcaster whose passion for music and football shone through all his work

Danny Kelly, who has died aged 69, influenced music journalism and sports broadcasting for more than four decades. Humour, enthusiasm and encyclopedic knowledge were the hallmarks of his work as a writer and editor at the New Musical Express (NME) and Q magazine, and throughout his online, TV and audio career, most prominently around his passion for football.

In 1997 Kelly helped set up Football 365, one of the earliest football websites, and still one of the most popular in the UK. He hosted radio shows on BBC 5 Live, TalkSport, BT Sport and Times Radio, as well as many podcasts including The View from the Lane, about the team he had supported since childhood, Tottenham Hotspur.

Kelly’s deputy at the NME, Stuart Maconie, described how he championed “the best of everything … roots, rock, reggae, the Smiths, Tamla Motown, the Clash, T.Rex, prog”. His close friend Danny Baker, his regular co-host since 1992, said that they shared “music, football, life … an absurd private universe of turmoil” together and turned to humour “to forge another path for football punditry, based in hoopla, voodoo and toasters”.

Kelly’s much-shared 2008 commentary of an Arsenal and Spurs match, alongside the retired footballer Stan Collymore, exemplified his relatable passion: “I love football, Stan! I love it!” he shouted when Spurs equalised, adding, seconds later, “How unprofessional.” Collymore described how Kelly brought him to TalkSport after he struggled with chronic mental health issues; Kelly gave many others support during their own physical and psychological struggles.

Born in London, into a family that had recently emigrated from County Carlow in Ireland to Islington, Danny was the oldest of the six children of Josie (nee Martin), a school dinner lady, and Andy, a railway worker.

Despite growing up “literally in the shadow of Highbury Stadium”, and in a family of Arsenal supporters, Danny supported their near-rivals. Speaking to FourFourTwo magazine in 2006, he said that Spurs represented “the true spirit of rock’n’roll”.

A pupil at Sacred Heart primary school in Holloway and later at St Aloysius’ College in Highgate, Kelly loved history (“but I don’t suppose anyone really cares about my opinions about the Anglo-Prussian agreements of the 17th century,” he told the New York Times’ sports website The Athletic in 2022).

In a 2020 interview with the Behind the Lines podcast, he described himself as “teenage music fanatic”, calling the NME in the mid-1970s his “bible”.

After failing to complete law and combined arts studies at Leicester University through “lack of effort, and punk rock”, he worked at British Rail on the complaints counter and as a station announcer. As a shop steward with the Transport Salaried Staffs’ Association, he wrote about music in the union magazine. A copy picked up by the NME jazz critic Richard Cook at a TUC conference in 1983 led to freelance work for Kelly, who then returned to college to pursue media studies at the Polytechnic of Central London (now the University of Westminster).

His writing was ebullient and vivid: Marvin Gaye possessed “a combination of gifts seldom stuffed into one body, one psyche”; the Smiths made “a Catherine wheel of inspired language” against “shifting, sometimes unnervingly evocative and beautiful guitar music”. Joining the NME in 1985 as a writer, he became deputy editor in 1987 and editor in 1990, before taking over Q in 1992.

There, he commanded audiences in the office and at after-work pub sessions regaling his stories of meeting musicians, and presented serious cover stars with characteristic irreverence: Sting, formerly of the Police, in handcuffs; Kate Bush under the headline “Booze, fags, blokes and me”.

In 1995, he launched Total Sport magazine, which ran for four years, and from 1997 to 1998 presented the late-night Channel 4 sports and entertainment show Under the Moon. Simultaneously, he ran editorial operations for Football 365, which became popular during the 1998 World Cup.

His subsequent radio career was prolific and popular. On TalkSport’s This Sporting Life, his interviewees included Virginia Wade, Frank Bruno and Eric Bristow. Trans Euro Express combined his love of European football and music, while his expertise in other sports, including cycling and American football, was in regular demand.

A year-long marriage ended in 1998; in 2005, the literary journalist and editor Alex Clark became his partner. He married her, wearing shoes from a range by his favourite rapper, MF Doom, in 2021. They moved to Kilkenny in 2018 to be near his mother, who had returned to Ireland.

Kelly broadcast remotely throughout the pandemic, up until his diagnosis with oesophageal cancer in March. He posted a diary of his treatment, the Kelly Cancer Kronikles, to his 106,000 followers on X until August, its title referencing one of his literary heroes, the fictional schoolboy Nigel Molesworth.

“I hope that what I’ve done over the years is to be honest and open about football, life, culture, everything else,” he said to the Athletic, and added the only two pieces of advice he ever gave to aspiring broadcasters. “Be a version of yourself, but add an extra 15% of personality. And don’t be shit. Try not to be shit and you’ll be OK.”

Alex, his mother, Josie, and his siblings, Margaret, Michael, Andrew, Joanna and John, survive him.

Daniel John Kelly, journalist and broadcaster, born 22 December 1956; died 10 November 2026
