Winners and losers from Ravens’ Week 9 loss to the Jaguars

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The Baltimore Ravens' 33-10 loss to the Jacksonville Jaguars was their third consecutive game decided by at least 22 points. There was more bad than good in this one for the Ravens, as you would expect in such a lopsided contest.

RB Justice Hill: Hill was one of the few bright spots for Baltimore on Thursday night. The seventh-year veteran showed he still had burst on a 65-yard touchdown run in the fourth quarter. Hill's 85 rushing yards were the most allowed to a single player by the Jaguars in their last 24 games.

C Cam Jurgens: Jurgens rebounded from a poor game last week with a solid outing against Jacksonville. His 72.7 PFF grade was the highest of any offensive player for the Ravens.

RG Vega Ioane: Ioane's return to the lineup after missing the previous game with a knee injury was a welcome sight. The rookie's 66.7 PFF grade was only behind Jurgens among offensive players.

RT Roger Rosengarten: Rosengarten allowed zero pressures against the Jaguars and was third among offensive players with a 66.4 PFF grade.

QB Joe Fagnano: The rookie undrafted quarterback making his first start on a short week with an injured ankle played how you would expect. Fagnano struggled to move the ball through the air, racking up just 60 yards on 18 attempts, while throwing two interceptions.

LG John Simpson: Simpson has been perhaps the most criticized starter on Baltimore's offensive line after signing a three-year, $30 million contract this offseason. His 4.5 pass-blocking grade against Jacksonville should only stoke the flames.

TE Mark Andrews: Highly-paid veterans continue to let the Ravens down this season, and no player exemplifies that more than their once Pro Bowl tight end. Andrews and Ronnie Stanley appear to be in competition at this point for who can have the most false starts, as the duo committed the penalty on consecutive plays to move the ball back 10 yards on second down. Andrews later fumbled the handoff on a fourth down, turning the ball over on downs.

CB Marlon Humphrey: Humphrey continues to draw the ire of fans with comments made off the field, and his play on it only strengthens the criticism. Humphrey allowed a 52-yard completion to a tight end on the game's opening drive when he appeared to get stuck watching Trevor Lawrence on a scramble, allowing the rookie Nate Boerkircher to get behind him down the field.

WR LaJohntay Wester: Wester has failed to make much of an impact in the return game this season, and his lost fumble on a kickoff against the Jaguars could put his job security in question.
