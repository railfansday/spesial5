How 'sleeping giant' Everton are exceeding expectations

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

After a difficult summer, Everton are proving greater than the sum of their parts with another European push and some battling performances; watch Brentford vs Everton live on Sky Sports Premier League and Main Event from 6.30pm on Monday Night Football; kick-off 8pm

Everything felt a bit gloomy at Everton after the closure of the summer transfer window. 'Disappointment' was the buzzword used by David Moyes - especially after a Deadline Day move for Folarin Balogun fell through.

Moyes has said for some time that the Toffees need more bodies, but for all of his public declarations, Everton have been left with just 18 senior players and one recognised striker until January.

Coming so soon after a late-season collapse saw them miss out on European football, it is understandable why Moyes was left frustrated and some tipped them for relegation form.

"My expectations changed during the window," the Scotsman said recently. "I don't think Everton are ever going to spend hundreds and hundreds of millions to improve so all I can do is take a small progression, get a bit better every year and that felt it was going to be much harder, but the players are doing a brilliant job with their league position."

Any expectations have been exceeded. Everton are mounting another European push, currently sixth in the Premier League - just two points from the top four. They are also into quarter-finals of the Carabao Cup, facing Fleetwood in the final eight next month.

"It's a massive club, a sleeping giant of the English game," Everton midfielder Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall told Sky Sports. "I really want to do something with this club or win some silverware and give the fans something to scream about."

While it remains early in the season, the belief and confidence is clear. The Toffees are proving greater than the sum of their parts as their upward trajectory under Moyes continues.

It hasn't all been easy points for Everton - they have had to work hard for them. In two draws against Bournemouth and Manchester United, they did not score before the 83rd minute. They needed an 80th minute goal from Charly Alcaraz to beat Wolves in the third round of the Carabao Cup.

The Toffees also went behind to Coventry in their last Premier League match before the international break, going on to win 3-1. Admittedly, that game came with its own dose of luck for Everton, but fans might argue that it is about time they were on the right end of things.

After that game on Friday Night Football, Dewsbury-Hall said: "Our mentality this year has gone from strength to strength... we just never know when we're out of the game. Every time we have gone behind this season, we've reacted in a positive manner."

And just as importantly, they have seen out games when needed. There have been wins and clean sheets against Crystal Palace, Ipswich and Newcastle. They also swept the Magpies aside in the Carabao Cup fourth round with a convincing 3-1 win.

They have only lost twice - at home to Chelsea and away to Premier League champions, Arsenal. Neither result was disastrous.

After beating Newcastle in the Premier League, Moyes said: "We're creating chances. That's the big thing for me. We want to be a side that creates more chances. If we could be more offensive, that's the way I'd like it to be."

And Moyes is getting his wish. Everton are largely matching or exceeding their numbers from last season. While the number of goals scored per games is roughly the same, they are registering more shots and have a higher xG.

A big part of that is down to the form of their only recognised striker Thierno Barry. "He's come back off the summer like a different player," team-mate Dewsbury-Hall said.

He is playing far more centrally than last year - where he spent more time on the right wing - and is close to surpassing most of his attacking numbers from the entirety of last season with only ten games played. He has also scored in three successive games for Everton for the first time. Suddenly, the Frenchman is discovering a taste for goals that even the most optimistic Evertonian would have struggled to predict.

However, he has missed more big chances than any other Premier League player this season (16) - twice as many as the player in second, Bournemouth's Rayan (8). It suggests he has actually been anything but clinical, and if he had a better finishing touch, he could be an early frontrunner for the Golden Boot. His continued improvement in that part of his game is going to be vital for Everton.

Thierno Barry’s six goals are the most by a player in Everton’s first 10 Premier League games of a season since Dominic Calvert-Lewin in 2020-21 (10), and most by a non-English player for the club since Romelu Lukaku in 2016-17 (7). Barry has scored (5) or assisted (1) six of the Toffees’ last seven league goals.

Another in-form playing having a huge influence is Dewsbury-Hall himself.

Sky Sports pundit Jamie Redknapp was full of praise for the midfielder after his display against Coventry, especially in the No 10 role, running forward with and without the ball to push Everton up the pitch.

"I'm really enjoying it," the former Chelsea and Leicester man said of his role in that game. "I've got a really good hybrid role in this team where the gaffer has given me license to pop up all over the pitch.

"I feel like I'm getting a good relationship with Thierno now and I'm buzzing he keeps getting the goals because he deserves it so much... as long as he keeps performing, I'll keep trying to help him as much as I can."

Moyes is the Premier League's most experienced manager. Monday's game at Brentford will see him overtake Harry Catterick's record of the most Everton games managed, moving on to 493.

Across his career, he has stabilised clubs, won silverware, and worked with fewer resources than may be available at some other Premier League clubs - elements he has had to contend with in his second spell at Everton.

When he took over for a second time in January 2025, Everton were staring down another relegation battle, sitting 16th in the table, a point above the relegation places. They eventually finished in 13th, 23 points clear of the drop.

And last season, the Toffees were rarely spoken about as relegation contenders. While it was a disappointing finish, Moyes had once again stabilised a club who are one of six ever-presents in the Premier League.

But the Everton boss is a realist and while they are exceeding expectations at the moment, Moyes is keen to keep feet on the ground. "I don't mind building expectations but somewhere along the lines someone will turn around and say, 'have you actually had a look at the size of their squad and the players they've got?'," he said.

"Maybe when you get to that point, you start saying, 'wait a minute, calm yourself down a little bit'. I'm not using it as an excuse, I'm just telling you around the corner there could be things that don't go for us."

Of course, a lot of that will stem from last season's disappointment at missing out on Europe and an underlying concern that Everton could drop off once again.

They failed to win any of their last seven Premier League games in the 2025/26 campaign, which ultimately saw them slip out of European contention, finishing 13th.

That will still be fresh in the mind and Moyes will want to increase his squad size in January to ensure history does not repeat itself. Sky Sports News reported this week that a striker to support Barry and right-back are key for the upcoming window.

We also have to keep in mind that there have only been 10 Premier League games played - not even a third of the season - and anything can happen between now and May.

But so far, Everton are heading in the right direction and will have learnt from previous failures. Good players and good managers - of which they have both - always do.

"There's a feel-good factor about Everton right now. David Moyes has done a great job," Redknapp said a few weeks ago. The Toffees will be hoping that lasts for the next seven months and beyond.
