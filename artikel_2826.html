Giants-Colts matchup: When the Giants have the ball

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The New York Giants are coming off of a fantastic win at home in Week 11, but now need to turn the page to focus on the Indiana Colts.

This is not the Colts team that many were expecting when the schedule was released. The Colts looked like one of the best teams in the AFC for the first half of the 2025 season, and it was widely expected that they'd return to form with Jones' return from injury. That, however, didn't happen and the 3-8 Colts are well out of the AFC's playoff picture.

The Giants' 23-18 win over the Jacksonville Jaguars preserved their lead at the top of the NFC East, and this game is being looked at as a brief reprieve before the toughest stretch of the Giants' schedule.

Can the Giants maintain their level of play and not overlook Colts?

The Colts' defense is designed and called by a familiar face in defensive coordinator Lou Anarumo. He was the Giants' defensive backs coach in 2018 before being hired as the Cincinnati Bengals defensive coordinator in 2019 and then becoming the Colts' DC in 2025.

Anaroumo is a well-regarded defensive mind who has been in consideration for the Giants' head coaching job. His scheme is built around a "multiple" 4-3 front and he primarily calls zone coverages on the back end, though their rate of zone coverage is one of the lowest in the NFL, while their man coverage rate is among the highest. The Colts call more coverages with single-high safeties, usually mixing Cover-1 and Cover-3 looks. When they use two-deep safety looks it's usually Cover-4, with some Cover-6 mixed in.

Recall, Cover-6 is a hybrid coverage that blends Cover-4 and Cover-2 shells, or "Quarter-Quarter-Half". The Cover-4 is called to the passing strength, matching more DBs to the side of the offense with more receiving threats.

The Colts primarily rely on a 4-man rush, though it remains to be seen how Anaroumo will adapt now that DeForest Buckner has been traded.

The Colts' defense hasn't been good this year, but they've been better against the pass than the run. Their passing EPA allowed is 0.082 per play (19th in the NFL), and their passing success rate allowed is 42.5 percent (8th best). Their rushing EPA allowed, however, is -0.010 per play, which is 29th in the NFL, with a rushing success rate allowed of 43.9 percent (23rd).

The centerpiece of their defense is now CB Sauce Gardner, acquired from the Jets last year. Gardner has rare athletic traits for the position with exceptional length and athleticism, and is one of the best corners in the NFL at forcing tight window throws. Gardner is allowing just 2.1 yards of separation as the closest defender (per NextGenStats). For comparison, that's less than the Giants' own Deonte Banks, who's allowing 2.3 yards of separation on average. That said, Banks has been much better at the catch point, forcing -4.7% catch rate below expected, while Gardner's opponents are catching passes at a rate of 11.1% higher than expected.

Gardner's presence allows the Colts to play more man coverage than they have in the past, but he can be had in coverage.

The Giants' offense has struggled against the blitz over the last two weeks. Both the Washington Commanders and Jaguars dialed up the blitz on key downs, and they were generally successful in disrupting the Giants' offense when they did so. Whether by getting a free runner in run defense or sending pressure against J.J. McCarthy on passing downs, the Giants' opponents were able to generate pressure and disrupt the Giants' offense.

The good news is that the Colts don't blitz all that often. They're 31st in the NFL in blitz rate at 16.7%. Their four-man rush had been adequate, generating pressure and hitting the quarterback at (roughly) league-average rates. But that was, for the most part, with DeForest Buckner. He was one of the Colts to go in their trade deadline fire sale. Now, their best pass rusher is DL Adetomiwa Adebawore, who leads the team in sacks (5.0) and QB hits(12). Edge defender Laiatu Latu is a potential problem as well, but it remains to be seen just how much pressure the Colts will be able to generate with just their front four.

It's possible that they could attempt to play a more blitz-heavy style of defense in light of how the Commanders' and Jaguars' defenses were able to disrupt J.J. McCarthy. However, that isn't really in their defensive DNA, and playing outside of their scheme and preferred philosophy could create opportunities for the Giants.

The Giants' running backs are all capable receivers, and it might be a good idea to ask them to chip before releasing into routes as opposed to purely blocking. If McCarthy can quickly recognize the blitz, slowing a pass rusher and immediately giving him a quick answer could help the offense be more consistent.

The Giants will almost certainly look to get their rushing attack on track and lean on their running game against the Colts.

Not only have the Colts fielded one of the worst run defenses in the NFL this year, ranking 29th in yards per carry allowed (4.7 per carry), but they also rank 30th in rushing EPA allowed (16.47 EPA allowed). That was before the Colts traded away their best defensive lineman in DeForest Buckner, and the Colts gave up 167 yards on the ground to the Texans without Buckner.

When the Giants aren't running the ball, they can attack the vulnerable flats and seams inherent in the Colts' favored Cover-3 looks. J.J. McCarthy has plenty of arm strength to hit square-outs in the flat to either sideline, and those routes would use Malik Nabers and Darnell Mooney's quickness to generate separation. Likewise, the Giants have been trying to hit on explosive plays down the middle of the field. The seams in Cover-3 defenses could be good targets for deep passes to tight ends Isaiah Likely or Theo Johnson, or wide receivers Mooney, Nabers, and Jalin Hyatt.

Finally, the Giants could also look to their 11-personnel package more often. Malik Nabers has proven to be an absolutely devastating slot receiver, and the Giants should look for opportunities to use him in that capacity. Darnell Mooney might not be an ideal outside receiver, however using him as an outside receiver not only adds another speed threat to stretch the defense but allows Nabers to move into the slot.

The Giants have flashed a dangerous offense that's equally capable of being explosive and suffocating — as well as ineffective and inefficient. The Colts' defense presents them with an opportunity to truly put it all together for the first time this season.
