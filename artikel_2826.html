Bears at Lions Q&A: Their Biggest matchup of the Super Bowl era

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The Bears and Lions face off on Thanksgiving with first place on the line. It's the first time the Bears and Lions have played with both teams 6 or more games over .500 since 1956! It's been 70 years! To set you up for this epic tilt, we sat down with Ryan Mathews from Pride of Detroit (SB Nation's Detroit Lions site) to talk about what could be one of the games of the year. Here's that Q&A (and here is a link to my answers with Pride of Detroit):

1. I'll admit, Dan Campbell's opening press conference with the biting of kneecaps and whatnot, I thought this guy was a clown. Then I thought that most of his success was thanks to Ben Johnson and Aaron Glenn. But watching how he operates and adapts, I'm becoming a big fan of his. I think he's a darn good coach, and I'll be honest: he seems like a fantastic person and the kind of guy you would want leading your team. Is the fan base 100% behind him or are there some stragglers who still want a different head coach?

The fan base is, without a doubt, in support of Dan Campbell. Anyone who isn't can't be trusted.

For the third time in four years, the Lions have started a season with an 8-2 record or better. You know how many times they started a season 8-2 in their previous 60 seasons as a franchise? Zero. None. Not one. Even after a down season that saw the Lions finish 9-8, with people questioning whether Detroit made the right decision in sticking with Campbell and letting Ben Johnson walk, the Lions have resoundingly answered the bell. All the talk about the brain drain on offense was silenced with the performances coordinated by Drew Petzing. Campbell's faith in defensive coordinator Kelvin Sheppard has been rewarded with the defense righting the ship after an abysmal September. It's clear that Campbell's message is still resonating with his players, and he's the right leader–the right kind of man–to guide this team through all this adversity they've faced so far this season.

2. Short and sweet: Is there any way to stop Jahmyr Gibbs? How would you try to set up a defense to defend against him?

Not sure if you're a God-fearing man, Bill, but prayer wouldn't hurt if you're looking for a way to stop Jahmyr Gibbs. Nobody will be Barry Sanders, but that's a great thing for Gibbs because there's no way you'd want him to be anything other than himself. It's alarming to watch Gibbs run because he has this startling combination of power, strength, and balance for a guy his size, and it makes it tough for any defender to bring him down–even when the defense has him exactly where they want him. The problem is that it's hard to keep tackling this guy who doesn't shy away from contact for four quarters, so just as soon as you're bringing your hard hat and lunch pail to meet him in the hole, he's zigged when he's supposed to have zagged, and he's giving your free safety a run for his money to the goal line.

Coincidentally enough, the Buffalo Bills and Carolina Panthers chose not to stack the box against Gibbs and the Lions' rushing attack. They played a lot of zone coverage and spent over a quarter of their defensive snaps in dime personnel, and these are the two games where Gibbs struggled the most this season, running for just 98 total yards on 31 combined carries across both games (3.1 YPC). In my estimation, a defense needs some willing tacklers in their secondary to limit the big plays, but more importantly, it's going to be any opposing defensive line's responsibility to win the line of scrimmage. The latter has proven to be a difficult challenge as the year's gone on and this offensive line has grown and developed, but closing run lanes (which leads to negative plays as Gibbs refuses to give up on a play; it's the most Sanders thing about him) and having disciplined run defenders work as a unit have kept Gibbs in check a couple of times this season.

3. Drew Petzing seems to have done an excellent job as offensive coordinator. Last year it seemed like Detroit really missed Ben Johnson, but they have recalibrated nicely. What has he done to get the offense back on track?

On the surface, it looks like Drew Petzing simply rewound the clock back to Detroit's 2024 offensive blueprint, but some context is important to understand why 2025 looked the way it did. Sam LaPorta's season-ending back injury midseason, in combination with Detroit losing their No. 2 tight end in Brock Wright, really had a pervasive effect on the offense in every respect–most notably the way Detroit's run game fell off a cliff in the back half of 2025. So it's important to consider the health of that group, along with upgrades to the offensive line, in an effort to understand how the pieces Petzing has had to work with are yielding such impressive results.

Under his guidance, the Lions have leaned back into heavier looks—dropping 11 personnel usage from 58.0% in 2025 down to 47.5% while bumping 12 personnel up to 29.6%—and put Jared Goff back under center on 55.0% of his snaps, almost mirroring 2024's rate of 54.6% and 43.8% shotgun rates. They've also pushed play-action back up to 34.6%, a huge increase from 2025's rate (28.3%) but still not quite up to where it was last under Johnson (37.6%).

Here's the catch, though: while the structural return to 2024's heavier, under-center identity is clear, the actual efficiency surge isn't coming from where you'd expect, especially since you guys know how integral play-action passing is to Ben Johnson. Detroit's play-action EPA per play has actually dipped to 0.164 (down from 0.296 last year and 0.344 in 2024), whereas non-play-action passing has exploded from 0.086 EPA per play in 2025 to 0.245 this season. Petzing's scheming and individual player execution (hello, Jameson Williams) are directly unlocking that straight-dropback success, but what's clear is Detroit didn't just copy-paste 2024—they're winning in the dropback game in a way they haven't before thanks to everyone involved.

4. Is the Lions defense legitimately good or is it a little smoke and mirrors? I know they've played well this year, but I still think this is the unit that can hold them back from winning a title, especially with no Jack Campbell and no Aidan Hutchinson.

As is the case with most things in life, the truth lies somewhere in between. Have the Lions had the benefit of playing some lesser quarterbacks over this stretch of football where they've allowed less than 275 net yards of offense over four straight games? Sure, absolutely, and that's part of the smoke and mirrors aspect of their performances, but to me, there's something to be said about taking care of business in a historical way, even against the lesser opponents. Also, from the first month of the season, when they were legitimately one of the worst defenses over a four-week stretch in modern football history to where they are now, there's enough data there to suggest the schematic changes have played a pivotal part in Detroit's turnaround.

In Weeks 1-4, Detroit's defense ranked 31st in EPA/play, 29th in EPA/pass, and 27th in pass-success rate, but what was most uncharacteristic of their defense over that stretch was the run defense: 26th in EPA/rush, 24th in rush-success rate, and 22nd in yards per carry (4.4). If there's anything Campbell prides himself on when it comes to the defensive side of the ball, it's the effort and mentality that's required to stop the run, so that was the underlying aspect that was so worrisome about Detroit's disastrous start on defense.

But two things have happened since Week 5: the return of Brian Branch and Kelvin Sheppard getting back to the kind of defense they want to run. Over that first month of the season, Detroit was running man coverage less than 20% of the time, but since then, Sheppard has used man coverage on 33.3% of snaps–the fourth-highest rate of any team over this stretch. Detroit ranks 12th in EPA/play, eighth in success rate–12th in pass-success rate–and their run defense has legitimized itself: first in EPA/rush and third in rush-success rate.

One thing that will be interesting to watch for in this game is how Detroit chooses to utilize three of their best players in the secondary. Kerby Joseph finally returned to the lineup last week, and that resulted in kicking Branch into more of a slot/box role. Last week, Chuck Clark, the veteran safety, was allowed to play more of a roaming role while Joseph played the middle deep. Detroit loves lining up in base personnel (45.1%, fourth-highest rate in the NFL), but in order to get all three of those players on the field, expect to see their subpackage rates increase as they did in Week 11: Detroit lined up in base just 26.2% of snaps.

5. This is a huge game; first place is on the line. I think this is probably the biggest game in Bears-Lions history, at least of the Super Bowl era. How do you see this one playing out? What's your final score prediction?

It doesn't get much bigger than this: Thanksgiving Day, first place in the NFC North on the line, and only one of these red-hot teams can come away victorious. Well, suppose both could come away with ties, but there isn't a football-watching person who wants that.

Ever since the Lions game ended last week, it's been hard not to think about this upcoming game. It's Ben Johnson, and there has to be a measured amount of focus and intention he's bringing to this game specifically. It's a chance for him to get his first win against the Lions, and I could see him throwing the kitchen sink at this Lions defense, stress-testing the areas impacted by injury over the past few weeks. There's a reality where the Bears are hungry, they get out to an early lead, and hang 35+ points on this relatively untested Lions' defense. We've seen it be difficult for the Lions to play complementary football at times, and if the Bears get out to an early two-score lead, it could be tough for the Lions offense and defense to work together and pull themselves out of a hole.

But on the other hand, Campbell has segmented this stretch of the season where Detroit plays three times in 11 days into… something. He's been constantly referring to it as such since the international game in Munich against the New England Patriots. He's done it in front of the media; he's talked about it in the locker room after the game. You can tell this is a challenge unlike anything he's been through, and he seems hell-bent on finishing the job on Thanksgiving.
