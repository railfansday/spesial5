Raiders Week 12: Schwesinger, Browns defense an active and aggressive group

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

What's the next step for Las Vegas Raiders rookie quarterback Fernando Mendoza?

"Winning," head coach Klint Kubiak was quick to say when asked that very question during his media availability earlier this week.

A question later, however, the rookie head coach added much-needed context when it comes to his prized No. 1 overall pick in the 2026 NFL Draft, when asked about Mendoza's performance in his first two initial starts.

"I think we just conveyed it to the whole team. And how did he respond? He's played two solid football games, and he's got to keep improving," Kubiak said. "But we're not putting it all on him. It's a team game and he'll play better when the whole team plays better."

A collective effort from the the 4-7 Raiders where complementary football is present throughout and not fleeting is a must for Las Vegas this coming Sunday against the 6-4 Cleveland Browns. The host Browns are unbeaten at home (4-0) and are trying to keep pace of the AFC North-leading Cincinnati Bengals (7-3 overall) and staying ahead of the ragga muffin Pittsburgh Steelers (also 6-4).

While head coach Todd Monken's Cleveland offense is near the bottom of the league rankings — 27th in points scored at 188 total; 21st in yards gained at 3,149 — his chosen defensive coordinator, Mike Rutenberg, has the Browns defense in the middle ranked 14th in both points allowed (220) and yards yielded (3,274). While that may be a far cry from the Seattle Seahawks and Denver Broncos defense Mendoza faced in his two initial starts, Rutenberg's Dawg Pound defense is an aggressive man-based group, which will test the Raiders rookie.

The heart of Cleveland's defense is Carson Schwesinger, a 23-year-old linebacker who arrived on the NFL scene as a second-round pick (33rd overall) out of UCLA. A special teams maniac that transformed into a true starter with the Bruins, the 6-foot-3 and 242-pounder from California won the 2025 AP NFL Defensive Rookie of the Year award last season and is once again playing with his hair on fire in Year 2.

Able to do it all — stop the run, blitz, tackle, and cover — Schwesinger was one of my popular mock draft picks to the Raiders and it's easy to see why. He's racked up 102 total tackles with six stops for loss, three sacks, four quarterback hits, a pass deflection, and three forced fumbles so far this season.

With Schwesinger in the middle of the defense with the green dot helmet, he relays the play calls from Rutenberg and ensures the Cleveland defense is lined up and makes adjustments. And the middle linebackers athleticism and high football IQ allowed the defensive coordinator to go from a heavy zone defense to a heavy man-press scheme that'd make legendary Raiders owner Al Davis proud.

According to ESPN Next Gen stats, the Browns ran man coverage concepts on about 45 percent of its defensive snaps (one of highest marks in the league) with a 64 percent press rate and 74 percent single-high safety alignment.

This has allowed defensive tackle Mason Graham and defensive end Isaiah McGuire to each rack up four sacks alongside nine and six tackles for loss, respectively. And behind them are defensive tackles Maliek Collins and Adin Hunington racking up 3.5 sacks, each. Cornerbacks Denzel Ward and Tyson Campbell, often left on an island in the press-man scheme, are stifling cover men with Ward leading the team with three interceptions and 12 pass deflections while Campbell has one pick and six pass deflections.

Thus, it'd be wise for Kubiak and his coaching staff to hone in on man-beater concepts and Mendoza executing the offense while combating Rutenberg's aggressive ways.

Brock Bowers' availability will certainly help Las Vegas, in that regard. The elite tight is hobbled by an ankle injury and his presence is a supreme headache for any and all defensive coordinators. While Mendoza has done an admirable job without his elite pass catcher available, Bowers playing would truly stress Cleveland's defense and provide an outstanding matchup with the tight end and Schwesinger.

Going back to the collective comment from Kubiak at the beginning of this piece, it'll take the offensive line keeping Mendoza protected, pass catchers separating and getting open from the man-press coverage, and the rookie quarterback smartly distributing the ball.

One of the Browns main defensive weaknesses is something the Raiders haven't done well consistently enough: Running the ball.

The aggressive press scheme with single-high safety looks leaves little margin for error against the run and Cleveland is susceptible to the ground and pound allowing 1,263 rushing yards (23rd most amongst the 32 NFL teams).

Las Vegas' run game came to life in Week 11 producing a season-high 143 total rushing yards (previous high was 130 in Week 1). The Silver & Black having that kind of production against the Browns likely goes a long way into determining the team's success (or lack thereof) this Sunday night.

Sunday night's clash between the Raiders and Browns marks the 30th meeting between the franchises. The Silver & Black lead the overall series 18-11 (16-11 in regular season play and 2-0 in playoff action) but the Browns won the most recent outing, a 24-10 victory in November of 2025. Before that, however, the Raiders won five-straight meetings dating from 2015 to 2024. That was streak was the second-longest consecutive victories in the overall series by the Raiders. The team won nine-straight from 1974 to 1986 including the postseason clashes in 1981 and 1983.

"Yeah, it's a really good question. It's a really fair question. You want to win right now. You want to win every game, but you know that part of the future is those guys having success. So, obviously you go into every game, and you put a game plan together to go win, and you know that some young guys are going to make mistakes. That's part of their growth. So, that's just part of it. But you need to give them the reps they need to see it live for us to have success moving forward."

—Las Vegas Raiders head coach Klint Kubiak on balancing the importance of letting younger players develop while also trying to win games

I'm just looking forward to keep on doing my 1/11th and getting better at that to help assist the run game."
