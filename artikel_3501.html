John Ternus talks plans to improve Siri AI

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

During Apple’s quarterly earnings call, CEO John Ternus was asked repeatedly about Siri AI’s future. Here’s what he said about Apple’s plans for improving and expanding Siri AI.

Sometimes when Apple launches a new feature or product, the company sees its work as finished, and whatever ships goes unchanged for far too long. Many might characterize Siri this way, arguing the assistant went years without meaningful improvement or updates.

But according to Apple CEO John Ternus, the Siri AI that just shipped is only the beginning of what’s planned.

Yesterday on Apple’s quarterly earnings call, Ternus gave a rundown of Siri AI’s capabilities. He concluded: “We’re really excited to have rolled all these innovations out, and there’s still so much more we plan to do.”

This signals that Apple doesn’t see Siri AI as a feature that can now be left alone for a while. Instead, as we already saw in iOS 27.2 last week, Apple is focused on rapidly improving it.

Now that Siri AI is out, I would like to see it iterate as follows:

Support for a wider range of file types, such as PDF.

Accessing it from Search (especially on the Mac) should be instantaneous and reliable.

Proactively suggesting and reminding the user based on what it knows.

What are those future plans? It sounds like agentic capabilities may be among them. Ternus said:

With Siri AI, we really have the kind of—the Siri we always wanted. It’s primarily request-based today, but as we talked about at WWDC, it’s built on a modern, orchestrator-driven architecture that gives us real room to expand capabilities over time.

It’s telling that Ternus says Siri is “primarily request-based today” and mentions “room to expand capabilities” before pivoting to mention agentic features. It sounds like Apple is already, behind-the-scenes, envisioning and working toward a version of Siri that won’t need to be “primarily request-based.”

Finally, later in the call when discussing opportunities for services growth, including potentially launching new services, Ternus says: “We think intelligence is going to be another opportunity as well.” This refers back to an earlier discussion of Apple’s intent to charge a fee for expanded usage limits on certain Siri AI and Apple Intelligence capabilities. The idea is that as Siri evolves and improves, Apple will have opportunities to charge for certain features.
