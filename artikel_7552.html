From YouTube views to crypto hacks, subscription cyclones to Czech hats: Have you kept up with this week’s media news?

It’s been yet another busy week. Recap it with the fourth edition of the Nieman Lab News-About-News Quiz, your weekly incentive to keep up with the latest happenings in the world of digital journalism. All questions are based on the week’s Nieman Lab stories or articles elsewhere that we’ve linked to in What We’re Reading. This is the final edition of the quiz for 2026; if people seem to like it, we’ll keep it going in 2027. Good luck!

The Chicago Sun-Times announced a new slogan this week. What is it?

“Chicago’s Paper” replaces “The Hardest-Working Paper in America,” the paper’s slogan since 2018.

An “overwhelming majority” of American journalists employed by this company voted this week to authorize a strike.

“The union has been negotiating a new contract for more than a year, and leaders say disagreements over wages, artificial intelligence and manager overreach have forced them to take this step.”

A “digital experience platform” company whose software runs on many news sites was hacked this week, sending malware to readers of the BBC, CNN, and Fox News. What’s the company’s name?

The hack stole cryptocurrency and credit card numbers from an unknown number of news site visitors.

A major journalism school announced this week that its master’s degree programs would now be tuition-free for in-state residents. Which school?

“Newmark J-School’s campaign for free tuition is rooted in CUNY’s decades-long commitment to access and opportunity for New Yorkers. At a time of profound transformation in the news industry, removing the cost of tuition makes it easier for more people to take the risk of entering the field.”

At this week’s summit in China, Bloomberg TV reporter Annmarie Hordern asked Donald Trump whether he felt Nvidia founder Jensen Huang had been disloyal to the United States. What happened next?

“It was the latest example of China’s strict controls on news coverage and how tightly Beijing tries to stage-manage major international events.”

Paid digital subscriptions at McClatchy-owned publications — a group that includes the Miami Herald, the Kansas City Star, and the Charlotte Observer — have declined how much in the past year, according to an internal corporate presentation?

Unique visitors have also dropped by 39%. McClatchy’s response? Doubling down on AI.

This is the per capita annual funding for public media across a set of five rich countries: $110, $81, $53, $27, $3. Name the five countries, in order.

With prospects for public media brighter with a Democratic Congress, some advocates are calling for a big increase in the United States’ historically puny funding levels.

YouTube has found a new way to inflate the number of “views” a video on its platform gets. What is it?

“YouTube, which has become even more popular on TVs than phones, also says the change will help it align with ‘TV industry standards,’ which similarly record views by estimating the number of people watching shows.”

In Czechia, mass rallies in support of public broadcasting have featured protestors wearing a very specific hat. Whose hat is it?

Since 1965, Večerníček (a diminutive of večerník, meaning evening newspaper) has appeared on Czech Television every night at 6:45 p.m. to wish children goodnight. Demonstrators wore his hat during the country’s largest protests since 1989 to oppose government plans to scrap public media license fees.
