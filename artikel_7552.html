Government contractors accused of ripping off software company for over $1 billion

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The software company, 4DD Holdings, claims government contracting giants Lockheed Martin and Leidos used its full-stack data platform to win health data contracts with the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs.

ALEXANDRIA, Va. (CN) â A software company sued two government contracting giants in federal court this week, accusing the pair of benefiting from a clone of its data-sorting technology.

4DD Holdings filed federal claims against Lockheed Martin and Leidos under the Defend Trade Secrets Act, the Lanham Act and the Copyright Act. 4DD also filed state claims of interference with business relations, misappropriation of trade secrets, unjust enrichment and fraudulent inducement.

The software company hopes to recoup the profits the contractors gained through the purported unauthorized use of its TETRA software. 4DD claims the former services firm Systems Made Simple, or SMS, later purchased by Lockheed Martin and Leidos, took advantage of limited access to TETRA when it subcontracted with 4DD in 2013 to consolidate Department of Veterans Affairs and Department of Defense health records.

According to 4DD, SMS initially sought to purchase 4DD for $40 million while evaluating potential subcontractors, an amount 4DD felt was well below market value. 4DD and SMS then entered into a partnership agreement. SMS convinced 4DD the government would later select it for a much more lucrative contract modernizing the Pentagon’s legacy health data systems.

The agreement prohibited copying TETRA to create a competing product.

“SMS signed written agreements that prohibited it from using 4DD’s technology to build a competing product,” 4DD says in its complaint. “SMS broke that promise and others.”

According to 4DD, SMS cloned the software rather than downloading and copying it to avoid producing a license key for each installation. SMS created a competing platform called eHIIP.

“Each clone was a complete, functioning copy of TETRA that carried the original license key, so a single licensed installation could be multiplied across any number of servers and environments without 4DD’s knowledge and without payment,” 4DD said. “In effect, SMS’ cloning circumvented TETRA’s built-in license enforcement mechanism.”

4DD relied on SMS to self-report any instances of unauthorized TETRA use to the government. 4DD claims the government went along with SMS’ underreporting so it wouldn’t have to reimburse 4DD for excess use under the Anti-Deficiency Act, which bars federal agencies from incurring obligations or spending funds in excess of appropriated amounts.

“It purposefully circumvented 4DD’s license enforcement mechanism, made thousands of TETRA copies beyond what the government had licensed and knowingly underreported the copies to the government and 4DD,” 4DD says in the complaint. “The unpaid copies grew into a liability that the government had not budgeted for.”

4DD won over $12 million from the government in the U.S. Court of Federal Claims for copyright infringement.

“The true motivation for the government turning against TETRA was that accumulating liability, not any judgment about the quality of the product itself,” 4DD said.

The government chose not to award 4DD the larger modernization contract. Lockheed Martin purchased SMS in 2014, knowing it would also obtain eHIIP. In 2016, Lockheed Martin agreed to make SMS a Leidos subsidiary in a deal valued at $4.6 billion.

4DD accuses Lockheed Martin of using eHIIP to obtain a $624 million contract from the VA. Leidos benefited from over $760 million worth of federal contracts that 4DD claims relied on its software.

Attorneys representing 4DD, and representatives from Lockheed Martin and Leidos, did not respond to a request for comment. The court assigned U.S. District Judge Rossie D. Alston, a Donald Trump appointee, to oversee the case.
