Barnsley FC defender Sam Gale takes accountability - now others must as autumn turns to winter

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Many in his shoes wouldn’t have done. When you are in a survival fight, which Daniel Stendel's side plainly are - even though it is still November - fronting up and being brutally honest is at least a starting point.

Gale said there was a bit of that in the away dressing room at Valley Parade on an evening when Barnsley’s winless league streak extended to 11 matches.

Those at the top of the club must do the same this winter and take accountability if the beleaguered Oakwell outfit are to have any chance of getting out of the mess they are in and avoid playing fourth-tier football next season for the first time since 1978-79.

On the pitch at Valley Parade, it was a rinse-and-repeat performance from the Reds, who outperformed Bradford in attacking metrics, just as they did to Sheffield Wednesday on Friday. In both boxes, they remain wholly deficient and soft, even.

Reflecting on events on the night at Bradford, Gale didn't shy away from that damning word. Soft.

"I think if you watched that, I don't think you can dispute that”, the former Gillingham man told The Yorkshire Post.

"It hurts to admit (that), I'm a centre-half and pride myself on defending.

"Bradford were a team of men who defended their box and blocked and headed everything and made sure no-one had an opportunity and ultimately we gave up really poor goals.

"We're coming up to the Christmas period and games are coming thick and fast with plenty of points to play for. Seasons can be won and lost.

"You can really turn your season around or it can go the other way. We've got to be around each other, address what we have to and it probably means a lot of home truths with each other and we've probably got to all look at ourselves.

"There were some home truths in there at full-time (at Bradford). It was necessary and needed.

"Ultimately, the management team could do nothing to affect the goals. The goals were poor and had nothing to do with tactics, shape or formations.

"There were three long balls we didn't deal with. And we haven't put the finishing touch in their box. That's the difference.”

Gale was culpable for the first goal from ex-Reds striker Stephen Humphrys when he made a hash of coping with a goal-kick from Bantams keeper Jon McCracken. It was compounded by meek defending from Arley Kay, who was shrugged off too easily by the goalscorer.

How Gale, 22 - in his first season in League One - and Kay, who turns 19 in February, would benefit from having a hardened defensive lieutenant alongside them who knows the level to learn from.

Gale added: "It was poor from me for the first goal and it sets the tone.

"This was a perfect example of a game being won in both boxes.

"By a country mile, we were the best side between both boxes. But it's irrelevant, they defended their box and were savvy with blocks, pushes, nudges and not letting you have anything.

"In our box, they were also savvy with the same thing and they got opportunities from it. That was the story.

"After the Sheffield Wednesday game, we talked about set-pieces and how we needed to defend them better and we did that. We still conceded three poor goals which were very similar - probably a cut and paste of each other. It's not one simple solution unfortunately.

"All we can do is put ourselves first and come together as a team because it has to get better."

Between now and the close of play on January 1, Barnsley, who have taken a pitiful two points from the last available 33 - face several sides in the bottom half of the table, starting with AFC Wimbledon on Saturday.

Their points haul could well decide whether they have a realistic chance of survival before addressing things in a January window they simply cannot afford to get wrong.

In the circumstances, Barnsley's support was admirable and stoic at Valley Parade. Their patience did snap at the end when a late penalty from Jake Beesley made it 3-0, but it was understandable.

"They are within their rights to be unhappy. Barnsley is a big football club with a big history and we're bottom of League One," Gale said.

"I'd be negative too if I was a fan - I'm a football fan myself - and paying my hard-earned money and travelling up and down the country to watch my team not win in 11 games, I'd have something to say as well.

"We've just got to take it and wear it. They are the reasons we are playing for this football club. Going into Saturday, we've got to block out the noise and give them something to cheer about."
