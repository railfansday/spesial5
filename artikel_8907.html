Victorian hospital conversion in Edinburgh named Scotland’s best new building

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

An old Victorian infirmary transformed into an educational space by UK studio Bennetts Associates has won the Andrew Doolan Best Building in Scotland for 2026.

Named the Edinburgh Futures Institute, the project involved refurbishing the Category A-listed structure, originally designed by Scottish architect David Bryce, and integrating new teaching and work spaces for the University of Edinburgh.

The Royal Incorporation of Architects in Scotland (RIAS) said the award jury praised the project for its “modern, welcoming, clever and stimulating” design that successfully blends “Scottish Baronial architecture with modern innovation”.

“The Edinburgh Futures Institute stood out for its civic generosity, scale and ambition,” said RIAS president Libby Heathcote, who is associate director at Reiach and Hall Architects.

“Balancing loyalty to David Bryce’s diagram with creativity in introducing bold new interventions is a remarkable achievement, blending historic Scottish Baronial architecture with modern innovation,” she continued.

“The result is a modern, welcoming, clever, and stimulating place that people want to work in and return to.”

Central to Bennetts Associates‘ redevelopment was the restoration of the building’s existing fabric, which was at risk of dereliction after being vacated in 2003.

Updates to the existing structure included reopening an original clock tower and public plaza, as well as the restoration of original wards and 150-metre-long corridors.

Across its five floors, flexible teaching and work zones were added alongside new public areas such as exhibition space and a 450-seat event hall below ground.

The Andrew Doolan Best Building in Scotland Award is an annual prize named after Scottish architect Andrew Doolan, who passed away in 2004. It recognises buildings of all typologies.

The Edinburgh Futures Institute was chosen as this year’s winner ahead of two other adaptive reuse projects. These have each been featured in a series of videos produced by Jim Stephenson and published by Dezeen and RIAS to showcase the shortlist.

The other two projects shortlisted were the Tarlair Outdoor Pool Pavilion by London-based Studio Octopi and the Paisley Central Library by Collective Architecture.

Previous winners of the RIAS Andrew Doolan Best Building in Scotland award include the Union Terrace Gardens by Stallan-Brand and LDA Design and the Burrell Collection museum by John McAslan + Partners.
