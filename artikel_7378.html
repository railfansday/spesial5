Meta removed ‘train’ from its crawl clause, kept ‘develop’, then paused everything. The pushback worked once. Do not expect it to work twice.

Meta backed down this time. On Nov. 5, it paused new business terms that would have let it crawl the website of every business using Facebook or Instagram, after publishers, a court, and a regulator pushed back. Pushback can work. But a business that gets its customers through Meta should plan as if Meta will never get better for it.

In October 2026, Meta previewed new business terms, the rules a business accepts when it uses Facebook or Instagram for business: a Page, ads, a shop. The first version let Meta use automated software to read the websites tied to a business, to “provide, develop, improve, research, train, and maintain” its products. Accepting was the price of staying, and the only way to say no was to stop using Meta for business.

On Oct. 16, Meta took the word “train” out and limited the crawl to its advertising and business products. “Develop” stayed in, and so did the crawl. On Oct. 23, Meta moved the start date from Oct. 30 to Nov. 20.

Meta doesn’t say why it paused. Paused is not withdrawn. Nieman Lab reports Meta’s notice says “our existing terms remain in effect”, and nothing in it says what comes back, or when.

Meta has done things that work for Meta since forever, with no regard for anyone. This is what Meta does. Hollywood has made two films about Facebook, and neither is about how well it treats its users.

Meta’s pattern feels like a slow death for a business in the Meta ecosystem, with nothing it can do about what Meta changes. Reaching for a business’s website is new. The attitude isn’t new: Meta not caring about its business users, and trying to give them no options.

So start investing in other acquisition channels, or at least planning for them.

Meta backed down this time. Do not wait for it to do it again.
