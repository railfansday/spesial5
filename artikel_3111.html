Huntington Ingalls: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

With 90% of 2030 shipbuilding revenue locked in, execution and workforce scaling become the true profit drivers.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 29, 2026, Huntington Ingalls (HII -1.79%) raised its full-year shipbuilding revenue forecast to between $10.5 billion and $10.7 billion. Long-term investors care because expanding order backlogs give defense shipbuilders clear revenue visibility for years to come.

The company secured major Navy contracts for nuclear-powered submarines and aircraft carriers during the third quarter. That surge brought its backlog, or total value of contracted orders not yet filled, to $86.2 billion and guarantees work for years.

High-tech military hardware expands beyond traditional capital ships into unmanned surface vehicles, which are robotic ships operating without human crews. The Mission Technologies division enlarged its production facility to meet growing demand for autonomous ocean platforms.

Increasing throughput, or the speed at which shipbuilders complete work, requires steady craft labor. Adding net workers across its shipyards lets the business build hull modules faster and expand its overall production capacity.

A swelling order backlog and steady labor additions drive predictable operational momentum. High entry barriers and specialized nuclear shipbuilding infrastructure protect the company's competitive position while management focuses on supply chain stability and shipyard throughput discipline.

Multiyear Navy contracts shield the business from economic downturns. That long runway gives the shipbuilder a durable foundation to compound earnings over time.
