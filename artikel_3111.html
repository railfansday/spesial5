Siemens is building an AI robot to unpack parts at its Erlangen plant

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The robot is one of several AI tools Siemens showed at the SPS fair, alongside agents that plan production.

A robot arm stands over an opened delivery box in a test cell at Siemens’ Electronics Factory in Erlangen.

Siemens is developing a robot that uses AI foundation models to unpack component deliveries at its Erlangen electronics plant, a job fixed robot programs cannot handle. At SPS 2026 it also showed an AI planner that it expects to cut manual planning work by 70% and costs by up to 20%.

Siemens is developing an AI-powered robot to unpack incoming parts at its electronics factory in Erlangen, Germany, the company said on Tuesday. It was one of several AI tools Siemens showed at the SPS automation fair in Nuremberg.

Deliveries vary so much in box type and size that fixed robot programs cannot handle them, so unpacking is still mostly done by hand. In Siemens’ system, cameras watch the workspace and robot foundation models, AI trained for physical tasks, decide the next steps. The robot opens cartons and takes out reels of components.

The plant receives 80 to 200 packages a day, trade site All About Industries reported. The AI learned from people steering the robot remotely, and it runs on Siemens industrial PCs with Nvidia chips.

Siemens has not said when the robot will be ready. The same plant ran an eight-hour humanoid robot trial with Nvidia in April. Similar AI is already in paying customers’ warehouses: Warsaw’s Nomagic says it halved how often its robots need human help.

Siemens also showed AI agents for planning. Its Autonomous Production Planner uses several agents to draw up production schedules, then tests each one in a digital copy of the factory. Siemens expects it to cut manual planning work by 70% and costs by up to 20%.

“The next productivity leap will come from automating tasks that were impossible to automate before,” said Rainer Brehm, president of automation at Siemens.

Rivals are pushing into factory AI too. In June, Schneider Electric agreed to buy industrial-AI firm Cognite for $3.1B.
