Iran’s Overland Scramble Is a Turning Point

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

A new U.S. strategic focus could mark the end of Tehran’s evasion architecture.

An Iranian truck driver waits to cross back into Iran in the Penjwen district, near the border with Iran in Iraq’s autonomous Kurdistan region, September 21, 2026.

Earlier this month, U.S. Central Command announced Task Force Iron Gate, a unit charged with disrupting the overland and Caspian Sea routes that Iran has leaned on since the U.S. naval blockade sealed its southern ports on the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman. The announcement landed amid news reports that had made the American blockade look leaky: hundreds of trucks lining up at Iran’s border crossing with Turkey, along with reports of Iran rerouting trade to the north through the Caspian Sea.

But that reading gets the story backward. Iran’s overland scramble provides a map of the regime’s remaining economic arteries — and Washington has now built the tool to cut them.

Miad Maleki served as a senior sanctions official at the Treasury Department from 2017-25 and is now a senior fellow at the Foundation for Defense of Democracies (FDD).

Behnam Ben Taleblu is Iran Program senior director and a senior fellow at the Foundation for Defense of Democracies (FDD).
