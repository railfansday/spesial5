From dramatic lightning strikes to foreboding storm clouds, these stunning photographs showcase the beauty, power and unpredictability of our planet's weather.

Every year, the Royal Meteorological Society's Weather Photographer of the Year competition offers a reminder that some of the world's most compelling images emerge from the unpredictable forces of nature. The 2026 short list includes 30 photographs selected from more than 5,500 entries submitted by photographers around the world. Each offers a different perspective into the relationship among weather, landscapes and the people who experience these natural phenomena.

The short-listed images span a range of settings and subjects, from lightning strikes, to cloud swirls, to rainbows. You can explore the full short list and vote for your favorite photo before the competition ends on Thursday (Nov. 5).

Live Science rounded up seven images that stood out for their composition, atmosphere and ability to tell a story. Photographer Lisa K. Kuhn captured a lenticular cloud above Mount Shasta in California.

Watching from Bayonne, New Jersey, photographer Dan Martland captured this triple lightning strike above New York City.

"Mother Nature was kind enough to give us a supercell and lightning at night," said photographer Laura Hedien about seeing this storm in Nebraska.

During a walk in England's Lake District, photographer Mat Robinson waited in the pouring rain and eventually took this image of a beautiful rainbow.

While walking through a wintery landscape in Bachledova dolina, Slovakia, photographer Dominika Koszowska noticed ice forming on wire mesh, creating unique heart shapes.

Photographer Erfan Samanfar snapped a haunting image of a farmer trying to protect his farmland from a raging fire in Marvdasht, Iran.

Photographer Andres Miraglia captured the moment a floating iceberg was seen in the sunset off of Antarctica's Paradise Harbour.
