Improvements in education, lifestyle and health during early life are slowing rates of cognitive decline in older people.Credit: Laura Olivas/Getty

People entering their golden years now have better memory and experience slower cognitive decline than previous generations. A study of people born between 1910 and 1964 found that a ten-year jump in birth date was associated with a 10% higher memory score at the age of 60. Researchers speculate that improvements in memory might be thanks to better education, health care and nutrition in childhood, while the slower decline in cognitive function might be influenced by factors later in life, such as improved treatment for cardiovascular and metabolic disease.

The large language models that underlie chatbots such as ChatGPT are quickly overcoming some of their weaknesses, say computer scientists Owen Queen and James Zou. They analysed flaws — such as hallucinating references — that had been flagged by researchers in academic papers. Most flaws were no longer a problem within roughly 16 months of a paper’s publication — for example, the latest models hardly ever attribute information to sources that don’t exist. On the other hand, newer models are getting more sycophantic and worse at generating random numbers.

Reference: Owen Queen & James Zou blog post

Undergraduate students were consistently the first group to start spreading emerging variants of the SARS-CoV-2 virus between 2021 and 2023, according to a massive genetic analysis. Researchers found that viral transmission during that period was highest among school students between the ages of 15 and 18, university undergrads and nursing-home residents. However, staff at all three types of institution had about the same risk of contracting COVID-19 as did those in their surrounding communities. The study’s scale “is truly impressive”, says infectious-disease researcher Bill Hanage.

A report by the US National Academies of Sciences, Engineering and Medicine (NASEM) suggests that science publishers take too long to retract or correct research papers, and standards for doing so are applied inconsistently across journals. NASEM recommends that the editorial review process be completed within 12 weeks for most cases and should rarely stretch beyond 6 months. The report was due to be published on 19 November, but was delayed after journalists at Science noted that it contained references with fabricated author names, which could suggest that artificial intelligence was used to draft or edit it.

Earlier this week we tried our hands at a game that tests our ability to decode ‘tortured phrases’ — ridiculous synonyms for common terms, which are intended to obscure plagiarism. For example, instead of ‘big data’, a study might use ‘colossal information’. Bees are the well-deserved superstars of the pollination world, and the reputations of birds, bats and even wasps are on the rise. Make room, now, for flies. Diptera — especially hoverflies (Syrphidae) — are undervalued pollinators. For example, on strawberry farms in England, hoverflies can make up more than half of the pollinating insects. Meanwhile, our reliance on managed honeybees can actually damage biodiversity and crop quality.

For the first time, astrophysicists have directly observed a giant star — one that’s swelling in size toward the end of its life cycle — engulfing a nearby ‘companion’ star in its gas envelope. Now they wait to learn what comes next. The companion star could cause the giant star to shed its gas envelope, after which the two could settle into a tight orbit; or it could plunge into the giant star’s core to its doom. “To conclusively rule out either case, we need to continue observing the system,” says astrophysicist Viraj Karambelkar.
