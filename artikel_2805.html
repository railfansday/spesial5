Toronto faces struggling Bucks

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Toronto Raptors kick off a three-game homestand tonight, facing off against the Milwaukee Bucks in their second matchup of the season. In their last contest, Toronto won handily, 121-107 despite missing Immanuel Quickley, Scottie Barnes, and Kawhi Leonard. That shouldn't be surprising though, as Milwaukee has come out of the gate slowly this season, holding a brutal 1-16 record.

Despite it being close in the first quarter of that game, Toronto was eventually able to run away with it due, in big part, to the contributions of Jamal Shead and Alijah Martin. Shead started the game and set a career high 16 assists. His facilitation was integral throughout, boasting a +23 point differential in his minutes on the floor. Alijah Martin's contribution was scoring, going 6-7 from long range to finish with 27 points. He had the second highest point differential, +22 from the floor. Without him, the team was a combined 3-25 from long range, one of their less than savoury shooting performances of the season. Although they struggled shooting, their rebounding, ball movement, and defence allowed them to stay ahead.

Milwaukee was carried by rookie Brayden Burries and Tyler Herro, who both had 20-point performances. It wasn't enough though, especially as they shot a brutal 10-40 from the field. That said it's only marginally worse than their previous performances this season, averaging a brutal 34% from long range as a team. This is a rebuilding year for them, especially after the loss of Giannis in the offseason and they haven't really found their identity yet.

To be successful again, they should focus on their defence and ball movement. Milwaukee commits a lot of turnovers, usually about 16 per game. Last time, Toronto managed to get them to turn the ball over 20 times, along with generating 12 steals and 4 blocks. Toronto continues to be in the top-half of the league defensively and despite some late game execution, they've made it tough for teams to score, especially given some excellent individual performances from Barnes, CMB, and Shead to name a few. They also play better when they're not forcing isolation possessions, looking for the open man, and finding open shots. Sometimes they panic, and usually the result is an empty possession. Toronto is also young and learning though, so the more they can work on their offensive system and ball movement the better.

They've gotten past their biggest hurdle in beating a winning team on Friday against the Pistons. That game also provided an opportunity for rookie Allen Graves to have a breakout 20 point, 10 rebound performance that should earn him another starting spot tonight.

Being at home and playing a couple weaker teams will hopefully give Toronto the opportunity to develop some momentum and work their way back towards 0.500. And what better chance to start then facing the Bucks again?

Toronto: Scottie Barnes, Jakob Poeltl, Immanuel Quickley, Ja'Kobe Walter, Allen Graves

Milwaukee: Tyler Herro, Kyle Kuzma, Myles Turner, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Brayden Burries

Toronto: RJ Barrett, Nate Bittle, Kawhi Leonard, Collin Murray-Boyles (Probable)

Milwaukee: Kam Jones, Bogoljub Markovic, Ousmane Dieng (Questionable)
