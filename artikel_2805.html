McCutcheon girls basketball wins third straight Hoops Classic

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

LAFAYETTE — For a third consecutive year, McCutcheon girls basketball was the team that cut the net off the basket to commemorate an IU Health Hoops Classic tournament win.

The Mavericks picked up a 52-32 win over Rensselaer Central on Saturday, Nov. 21, on their home floor, capping off a run that featured wins over Benton Central and Harrison.

The difference in this win than the last couple? McCutcheon has stayed calm, cool and collected all tournament. That might be because seniors Keara Lipscomb-Allen and Lillie Graves have been here for all those wins, even with a roster around them filled with newcomers.

The Mavericks opened the game with a 13-0 scoring run, led by a quick 10 points from Lipscomb-Allen, mostly in transition from quick passes from her teammates as she went to the lane aggressively. McCutcheon wanted to put some doubt in the mind of the Bombers quickly.

Although Rensselaer Central matched the Mavericks with 13 points each in the second quarter, it was outscored 24-15 in the second half. Even when it pushed them in stretches of the game, McCutcheon didn't panic.

"I think really throughout the whole game, and even throughout the whole tournament, I feel like we stay poised," Graves said. "So kind of let whenever the other team goes on a run to not let us put our head down or anything like that. Everyone's kind of been calm … I think that helps us keep the leads that we've been able to get in this tournament."

Graves was awarded the MVP award for the Hoops Classic, pouring in 26 points, six rebounds, three assists and four steals on 11-of-18 shooting from the field and 2-of-6 from behind the arc. Most of her work was done in the second half, while most of Larry Crabb Sportsmanship Award winner Lipscomb-Allen's 20 points, three rebounds, two assists and three steals on 9-of-21 shooting came in the first.

The nice thing about them both, according to coach Jeff Kony, is that they're equally unselfish. Neither are actively thinking about who is scoring at any given point, but they're both able to take control of the game at any point.

It's about playing and feeding off each other, and that was on display throughout Saturday's final.

"I would say that we know when someone is hot, so when they're hot, we give them the ball," Lipscomb-Allen said. "I think it's just really whoever is getting the most points in that moment, and we'll give them the ball."

Both of the two seniors played nearly 32 minutes — nothing unusual so far this season. Especially in a tournament setting and playing in back-to-back games on Friday and Saturday, conserving energy is crucial.

But at this point, it's become second nature. They know they're expected to, and the off the court preparation and catching their breaths on dead periods have helped them be at full strength in all four quarters.

"It's definitely tiring, especially playing in a tournament like this," Graves said. "I feel like now I'm kind of getting used to it. The games are kind of my conditioning at this point, so that's definitely helping."

Rensselaer Central was led by senior Courtney Mathew, who dropped 17 points and five rebounds on 8-of-17 shooting from the field. Whenever it found some momentum, she was spearheading it.

The Bombers (5-1) dropped their first game of the season after earning wins against West Lafayette and Central Catholic in the tournament. While it fell short against McCutcheon, they've gotten off to a strong start and will be a dangerous team in Class 2A.

"They did a really good job," Knoy said of Rensselaer Central. "Courtney's a really good player. She battles hard. Coach (Ed Cain) does a good job, so they're a good team. They're going to win a bunch of games, but I thought our kids handled that well."

It certainly won't be the last tournament the Mavericks (5-1) play this season nor the last look at any of their opponents, but getting another Hoops Classic win gives it early momentum. Especially for the youth on the roster still finding their roles, it'll be valuable experience moving forward.

Whereas nerves and chemistry weren't all put together in their season-opening loss to Plainfield, it's slowly starting to be realized for McCutcheon now. Patience is still key, but it's seeing growth.

"From the first game we were just all timid, but now that we've worked with each other more and going harder in practice, I think it really helped them get more comfortable with the varsity level," Lipscomb-Allen said.

The Mavericks will at least get an extensive break to rest up. Their next game will be against Zionsville (2-3) at 1:30 p.m. Dec. 5 at home.

After the run Graves and Lipscomb-Allen just had, that'll be useful.

Third-place game: Harrison 47, Central Catholic 33

Harrison's Aleah Szymanski scored 19 points on 6-of-20 shooting from the field, as well as 6-of-7 from the free-throw line.

Central Catholic was led by Brooke Thompson, who finished with 17 points and four rebounds on 4-of-13 shooting from the field and 7-of-7 on free throws.

Fifth-place game: Benton Central 56, West Lafayette 51

McKenna Yadon led Benton Central with 22 points and five rebounds on 8-of-17 shooting from the field.

Amaya St. Hilaire scored 17 points and grabbed 12 rebounds for West Lafayette, while Emma Garringer and Cora Loveless added 15 and 13 points, respectively.

Seventh-place game: Twin Lakes 32, Jeff 31

Kassi Nickerson and Elaine Need scored 11 and 10 points to lead Twin Lakes.

Jeff's Cambria Marcus finished with 14 points, two rebounds and four steals on 4-of-11 shooting from the field.
