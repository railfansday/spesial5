5 things we learned from the Chiefs choking in the freezing rain

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

It was a God-awful game in God-awful weather with a God-awful ending.

On a frigid, rainy night at New Highmark Stadium, the Kansas City Chiefs went toe-to-toe with the Buffalo Bills in a clash of supremacy between two of the best teams in the AFC.

The margin of error in this game was slim, and in the end, the Chiefs' undoing was by their own hand foot.

Here are five things we learned from the Chiefs' 13-10 victory over the Bills.

This is an unfortunate truth we need to accept.

The Chiefs lost this game because their special teams are horrible. They've been horrible all year, and they show no signs of improvement. The fact that kicker Harrison Butker is still on the roster and allowed to go out there and miss what would have been a field goal that sent this game into overtime is inexcusable.

But it's not just that Butker stinks. The entire unit is mistake-ridden and undisciplined. There was the muffed snap by punter Matt Ariza. Josh Downs decided to let the ball bounce and end up being downed inside the five-yard line.

And after all of it, during his press conference, all we get from the head coach and leader of the franchise is a canned answer where he refuses to call out the people responsible for the loss.

Listen, in principle I get it and even agree with defending your guys publicly—but if you're going to do that, then you have to confront them behind closed doors and make it very clear that this terrible performance is unacceptable and demand that changes are made.

Maybe he has confronted Toub and Butker privately; we'll never know. But what we do know is that nothing has been done that noticeably improves the situation.

Andy Reid is more loyal to his friends and players than he is to winning football games. And because of that, this loss is just as much on him as it is on anyone else.

The idea that replay and official review on plays is supposed to prevent bad calls and ensure that officials are getting the call right. Instead, we've reached a point of overanalysis, where instead of deciding if a player made a catch or not, they are litigating every single iota of ball movement to try and debate whether it qualifies as loss of control.

With 1:51 left in the game, the Chiefs had the ball at their own nine-yard line. Quarterback Patrick Mahomes fired a laser beam of a ball across the field to an awaiting Rashee Rice, who was sandwiched between two people. The play was originally ruled a catch, and the Chiefs were granted a first down. After review, the officials deemed that when Rice got hit and went to the ground, he lost control of the ball, and the call was overturned and ruled an incomplete pass.

This call set off a firestorm of tweets by fans and former players, and they all pretty much said the same thing. The officials got it wrong, and the NFL has no consistency regarding what is and is not a catcj.

The image of Andy Reid standing hunched over his call sheet, watching the game stoically like a big walrus, is one of the most iconic images in the NFL.

The issue with looking stoic on the sidelines is that you're not doing anything.

With 17 seconds left to go in the first half. Mahomes completed a 21-yard pass to tight end Travis Kelce to give the Chiefs first-and-goal at the Bills' eight-yard line. Mahomes called a timeout with nine seconds left on the clock, but the referees failed to stop the clock, and it ran down to 4 seconds before they stopped it. Mahomes complained and petitioned for more time. Andy stood there stoically.

The Chiefs had two timeouts left at the end of the game when the Bills kicked a game-winning 57-yard field goal in the rain. Why Reid didn't call a timeout and attempt to ice Bills kicker Tyler Bass with the game on the line is inexcusable.

Despite the conditions. The Chiefs outplayed the Bills in two out of the three phases of the game. The Chiefs had the better quarterback and better offense, and most importantly, their defense shut down Buffalo's high-powered offense. This game was a wasted masterclass by defensive coordinator Steve Spagnuolo and his defense, which held quarterback Josh Allen to just 81 passing yards on 21 attempts. They also created two turnovers by intercepting the Bills' quarterback.

It should have been enough to win. But it wasn't, thanks to special-teams misfires. But in the end, you're only as good as your record, and right now the Chiefs are two games behind the Bills.

What's worse. Barring a meltdown by the Denver Broncos in the second half of the season, the Chiefs are most likely going to be a wildcard team heading into the playoffs.

The Denver Broncos currently hold a half-game lead over the Chiefs in the AFC West, playing the Aaron Rodgers-less Pittsburgh Steelers on Black Friday. It may not seem like much, but it's a lead that will be hard for the Chiefs to surmount.

The Chiefs' margin for error moving forward is very small. They can ill afford another special teams catastrophe.
