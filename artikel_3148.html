Insanely popular on Amazon, the Novilla Bliss Memory Foam Mattress is a cheap and cheerful all-foam offering that I freely admit I wasn’t expecting much from. However, there were lots of things about the Novilla Bliss that surprised me in a good way and overall, I was quite impressed by the support considering the mattress’ very reasonable pricing.

Name: Jo PlumridgeAge bracket: 45-55Weight bracket: 100-130lbsPredominant sleep position: Combination sleeper who spends slightly more time on her sideSleep issues: Suffers with back pain which can easily be exacerbated by an unsupportive mattress

It’s not going to rival the very best mattresses on the market but if you’re on a budget, this should definitely be on your radar. With a queen mattress currently priced at $269.99 in the Black Friday mattress deals, I think this is one of the best budget beds currently on the market.

In terms of firmness, I’d suggest that most sleepers will find it at the firmer end of the medium scale. There is sinkage and this is an all-foam mattress, but there’s still some support underneath. I can’t think of any other all-foam mattresses I’ve slept on that are as firm, except for the Nectar Classic Memory Foam. However, I must stress that I’m a lightweight sleeper and my husband, who is lightweight to average weight for a man, found it softer than I did.

Still, we both enjoyed it for side sleeping particularly and, as someone who is ultra fussy about lumbar support, I found it more comfortable for back sleeping than I expected. Motion isolation on the Novilla Bliss is exceptional, and I couldn’t feel my husband moving around at all, but edge support is lacking both when you lie or sit on the edge. I didn’t feel as if I’d actually fall off the bed, but there was some definite tilting.

The mattress' one big failing, though, is temperature regulation. I sleep cold so this wasn't an issue, but my husband found it stuffy and, while he didn't wake up sweaty, he didn’t find it massively comfortable either. Hot sleepers on a budget might be happier on the Siena 10" Signature mattress, which kept our testers comfortably cool. But value for money is the Novilla Bliss' big selling point and considering its price, I was really impressed overall with the mattress.

Of course, November is one of the best times of the year for mattress sales and we’re seeing some Black Friday deals — a twin size Novilla 8" hybrid mattress is down to $90, for example, and on Amazon the queen size 10" Novilla Bliss has dropped below $210 a couple of times in 2026. So, it’s definitely worth keeping an eye on prices but do remember that, at $269.99 for a queen, the Novilla Bliss is already incredibly well priced.

Benefits differ very slightly depending on where you buy the mattress. On the brand’s own site, you’ll bet a 100-night trial, 10-year warranty and free shipping and returns. Amazon, on the other hand, offers a 90-night easy returns system, manufacturer’s warranty and free shipping and returns.

An extremely affordable all-foam mattress that offers far more support than you’d expect for its price tag, a queen size Novilla Bliss is currently priced at $269.99 on both the brand’s own website and on Amazon. Keep an eye on prices as Black Friday approaches.

I tested the 10" version, but as detailed above, you can buy the Novilla Bliss in a variety of heights. But for this review, I’m concentrating on the middle ground of the 10” version. Starting at the top, there’s a breathable cover with OEKO-TEX certified fabric. This is helpfully removeable and washable — mattress covers that can’t be removed for freshening up are a real bugbear of mine, although I’d obviously recommend that you use a mattress protector anyway.

Underneath this is a layer of cooling memory foam that’s CertiPUR-US certified and contains the brand’s NanoGEL cooling particles, which Novilla says are designed to ‘capture and redirect excess body heat away from the surface’.

Next up is a layer of adaptive cushioning foam that provides comfort and a transition layer to the high-density support foam layer underneath it. This layer provides most of the support and prevents the mattress from sagging.

Underneath this you’ll find the zip to remove the top cover, plus a non-slip fabric to stop the mattress from sliding about on your bed base. Somewhat annoyingly, the mattress doesn’t have handles to help move it about but as it’s all-foam, it’s not impossible to rotate it without them.

As a lightweight sleeper, I rated the Novilla Bliss as a medium-firm mattress but my husband (and additional testers of a heavier build that I asked to try out the mattress) felt it was a medium, albeit a firmer medium. Averaging out scores, I’d probably place the mattress at a 6 out of 10 on the firmness scale.

There’s definitely some ‘squidge’ when you first lie on the mattress, but this is backed up by a decent amount of support from the lower levels. This combination makes it particularly suited for side sleepers. My husband in particular found the mattress instantly comfortable in this sleeping position, but it did take a couple of days for the foams to soften up a little at the shoulders for me — after this, it was extremely comfortable.

Pressure relief around the pressure points was also really good and I didn’t wake up with any symptoms of a dead hip (something that I can be prone to if the mattress is too unyielding).

I wasn’t expecting great things from this mattress when it came to sleeping on my back, as I’ve historically found all-foam mattresses can lack the firmer lumbar support that I find essential. And, when I first lay down on the bed, I did notice a tiny amount of pulling through my lower back. However, this eased quickly and as a lightweight sleeper, I actually found the mattress supportive enough to keep my spine aligned.

My husband, who boarders the lightweight and average weight category also found it pretty supportive. It’s not going to rival the very best mattresses for back pain but, overall, I thought it did an excellent job on pressure relief for both side and back sleeping.

If you’re of a heavier weight though, I don’t think you’ll find the Novilla Bliss supportive enough and even average weight sleepers towards the higher end of that weight bracket may find it lacks support for back sleeping. I asked a couple of friends who are larger than my husband and I to try the mattress out and while they both thought they could just about get away with it for side sleeping, they felt that support was lacking for both back and stomach sleeping.

In fact, strict stomach sleepers of any weight probably won’t get on with the Novilla Bliss as it’s just lacking in enough support to keep the hips lifted, leading to the spine sinking out of alignment. If you’re a stomach sleeper, have a look at our recommended mattresses for stomach sleepers instead.

I’m in the UK so the mattress was shipped over the Atlantic and, despite its long journey, it arrived none the worse for its voyage. Order on the Novilla website and you’ll receive a tracking number within two business days, with delivery within three to seven business days. Novilla uses FedEx, UPS and GOFO Express for delivery. If you order through Amazon, you can expect delivery in around five days and there’s also the option to pay for the delivery drivers to unpack the mattress and take away packaging.

I recruited my long-suffering husband to help get the mattress upstairs although, being all-foam, it was considerably lighter than the hybrid and pocket sprung mattresses I’ve tested in the past.

Once released from its vacuum seal, the Novilla Bliss did take longer than a hybrid mattress to inflate and I left it for a good 10 hours before sleeping on it. It did definitely continue to expand minutely over the next few days, so I’d say it was around 48 hours before it was fully expanded. The brand suggests that it will take 24-72 hours to fully expand, so this was bang on the money. Some Amazon customers have reported issues with the mattress not expanding fully but in fairness, I didn’t have any issues on this front.

Like most foam mattresses, there was some off-gassing from the Novilla Bliss. It wasn’t the worst I’ve come across but it did last for a couple of days.

My husband and I slept on the mattress for four weeks to get a clear overview of how it performed in key areas. In addition, I carried out some objective tests in an approximation of our comprehensive sleep lab testing methodology.

Through both objective and subjective testing, I’ve assessed the mattress in three key areas. I’ve looked at motion isolation (will a partner moving about at night disturb you?), temperature regulation (does the mattress keep a hot sleeper comfortable through the night?) and edge support (how solid does the edge of the bed feel when you lie right on it or sit on the mattress?). These are the results.

If you share your bed with a restless partner or are a light sleeper, a mattress with good motion isolation is a must. Good motion isolation stops movement from one side of the bed travelling over and waking up another sleeper. I can report back that the Novilla Bliss is particularly strong in this department.

My husband does tend to flail about in bed when he’s rolling over, channeling a walrus trying to leave the ocean and I’m a pretty light sleeper, so even a little motion transfer can be enough to wake me up. I’m pleased to report that I wasn’t disturbed by him at all during our testing period, which was an unusual and pleasing thing.

This was backed up by my objective tests. Firstly, I performed a series of drop tests. I placed a wine glass on the left-hand side of the mattress and dropped a weight from 24" above the bed surface on the right-hand side. This is a similar sensation to a person getting into bed.

I dropped the weight five times consecutively 30" away from the wine glass. Each time, the wine glass gave a very slight wobble but otherwise stayed upright, which was impressive. Next up, I got my husband to perform some subjective tests, starting with low force movements and then increasing to moderate and high force movements. I rated the mattress 10 out of 10 at low force and nine out of 10 at both moderate and high force. Overall, the Novilla Bliss really excelled when it came to motion isolation.

When I talk about temperature regulation, I’m looking at how well the mattress manages heat build-up throughout the night and whether it leaves a sleeper feeling stuffy. I’m afraid this was one area where the Novilla Bliss didn’t perform brilliantly, despite claims of a breathable cover and gel-infused memory foam.

My husband sleeps hot and he found the mattress stuffy after only 15 minutes of lying on it. And while he didn’t wake up seriously overheating, he did feel slightly clammy and uncomfortable. It was not a particularly breathable mattress and, on some hotter nights during testing, he did actually decamp to our spare room.

I’m something of a reptile at the best of times but even I noticed some heat building up around my lower back while lying on it. However, thanks to my cold nature, I wasn’t ever uncomfortable on it.

We tested through the tail end of an unusually warm period in the UK and through to some slightly cooler nights, so it gave me a good overall view of the mattress. For context, temperatures in the room ranged from around 75F down to 60F. However, I should point out that I wouldn’t expect an all-foam mattress to perform as well as a hybrid for temperature regulation and, if you are a hot sleeper, you’d be better off investing in one of our best cooling mattress picks.

A mattress with good edge support allows sleepers to use the whole width of the bed, as well as providing a stable surface to sit on while getting in and out of bed. My husband and I foster cats for a local charity and, as any cat servant will tell you, these little furry creatures have the ability to take up 90% of the bed overnight, leaving human subjects very much clinging to the edges.

Unfortunately, the Novilla Bliss wasn’t the strongest candidate when it came to edge support. There was some definite tilting when we lay on the edges, although I didn’t feel as if I’d actually fall off. It was a similar story when sitting on the edge of the bed — there was some definite tilting, but I didn’t feel as if I’d actually slip off.

To back up my experiences, I placed a 53lb weight midway across and 3" in from the foot and sides of the mattress. The weight sunk in by 5" immediately and dropped further to 6.5" after five minutes. And I had to get my husband to hold the weight the whole time (he only took his hands off very briefly for a photograph).

I repeated this test midway down and 3" in from the side of the mattress and got exactly the same results, with the weight needing to be held the whole time. Just for context, the same weight placed in the center of the mattress compressed by 5" immediately but then didn’t drop any further.

The Novilla Bliss receives overwhelmingly positive reviews from sleepers on both Amazon and the brand’s website. There’s lots of praise for the “exceptional” price and many sleepers praise it for its good quality. Sleepers also praise the mattress for pressure relief, with one customer describing it as the “perfect balance of softness and support…I woke up feeling much more rested without the usual aches and stiffness.” Overall, this is a recurrent theme, along with frequent praise for the motion isolation.

There are nearly 45,000 reviews for the mattress on Amazon so, as you might expect, you will find some complaints in there. The most common one seems to be poor expansion of the mattress, so it would seem that this might be something you’ll need to look out for. And, while many people praise the mattress for soothing aches and pains, stomach sleepers and those of a heavier build complain that the mattress is nowhere near supportive enough for their sleeping style or body weight.

There are plenty of positives about the Novilla Bliss, but the price is the overwhelming winner. At under $300 for a queen, it’s a bit of a bargain and I was impressed by how well it performed considering that price tag. It certainly beats out quite a few more expensive mattresses when it comes to side sleeping in particular. The soft foams at the top and some decent support underneath mean that this is a great option for side sleepers, except for those of a heavier weight.

It also boasts some of the best motion isolation I’ve come across, and it will be a winner for co-sleepers thanks to this. But, as mentioned, I don’t think it’s a great fit for hot sleepers and I would like to see better edge support.

But overall, I was hugely impressed with this mattress, and I think that most lightweight and average side and back sleepers will be comfortable on this mattress.
