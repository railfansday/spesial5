Target’s Shoppers Are Coming Back as Store Traffic Jumps 5% After Falling 2.2% a Year Ago. Can the Momentum Last?

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Target stock is up 74% this year. Can it keep rallying?

By Jeremy Bowman – Nov 18, 2026 at 5:09PM EST

You may have missed it from all the attention on AI stocks this year, but Target (TGT +4.01%) is one of the best-performing stocks in the S&P 500.

The retail giant has jumped 74% year-to-date as it's put its earlier struggles behind it and returned to growth under new CEO Michael Fiddelke.

Target kept that momentum going in the third quarter as the stock jumped 6.7% Wednesday on another strong earnings report.

The retailer easily beat estimates on the top and bottom lines, as comparable sales jumped 5.6% on a 5% increase in traffic, showing shoppers are returning to its stores. That drove revenue up 7.1% to $27.07 billion, which topped the consensus at $26.54 billion.

The company's Fun 101 category, which includes products like toys, electronics, sports, and entertainment, delivered 27% growth. Beauty and food and beverage were also strong, up 9% and 7%, respectively. Target's non-retail business, including subscriptions like Target Circle 360, the Target+ marketplace, and its Roundel ad business, grew nearly 20%, showing the playbook that it's borrowed from Amazon and Walmart is paying off.

Further down the income statement, Target's gross margin rose 120 basis points to 29.4%, excluding a benefit from tariff refunds, as operating leverage from the increase in comparable sales helped drive gross margin improvement. Adjusted operating margin rose from 4.4% to 5.2%, excluding the tariff benefit, and adjusted earnings per share jumped from $1.78 to $2.65, or $2.15 excluding a $0.50 benefit from related tariff refunds, representing a 21% gain. That beat estimates at $2.07.

Target also raised its full-year revenue growth from 5% to 6%, and its adjusted EPS guidance from $9.90-$10.90 to $10.65-$11.40, which helps explain today's pop as well.

With Target stock nearly doubling this year, investors are clearly betting on a long-term recovery, but is the momentum sustainable? Let's take a look at what's going well for the company.

On some level, Target is benefiting from the low-hanging fruit in front of it after several years of underperforming its industry. The company is reclaiming lost market share with basic improvements like reducing out-of-stocks and investing more in labor and training to improve the sales experience. The strong comparable sales in part reflect the previous downturn in the business as the company recoups lost sales.

The retailer is also getting back to its roots with collaborations with brands like Pokémon that have helped drive its success in Fun 101, and price cuts have helped drive traffic without hurting margins.

Unlike in the second quarter, Target did not report growth in all of its core merchandising categories, as sales of apparel and accessories fell, and home furnishings sales were nearly flat, reflecting broader pressure in the housing sector. Fiddelke said he was not satisfied with the company's performance.

Target's implied fourth-quarter guidance should be encouraging to investors coming during the crucial holiday season.

While the third-quarter results weren't perfect given the misses in some core merchandising categories, Target is still performing well in a challenging retail environment, and the company is finding its way back to its historical formula of success. Meanwhile, the growth in the non-retail business is encouraging and should continue.

Trading at a price-to-earnings ratio of roughly 15.5 based on its updated guidance for the year, Target looks well-priced. While there's still some risk in the stock as the company picks through the low-hanging fruit, the risk/reward looks attractive at the current price given its momentum and the potential to further claw back lost sales and margin.
