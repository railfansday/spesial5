Rocket Lab Missed on Q3 Earnings -- and That's Great News

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Rocket Lab isn't profitable yet -- but it's on the right track.

By Rich Smith – Nov 20, 2026 at 7:27AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Rocket Lab ›

Rocket Lab (RKLB -3.50%) stock "missed earnings" last week, reporting losses nearly twice as bad as Wall Street had predicted. And then the stock shot up -- and kept going up. By the close of trading last Friday, Rocket Lab stock was up nearly 16% from where it began the week.

Heading into the earnings report, analysts forecast Rocket Lab would lose $0.07 per share on sales of $257.2 million. Actual losses were $0.12 per share, however. Rocket Lab reported $268 million in Q3 sales, beating on that metric -- and it looks like there may be more gains in store.

Revenue hit a new quarterly record, rising 73% year over year to $268 million. Backlog of work to be done, however (i.e., future revenue), grew by nearly 6x that number -- $1.2 billion -- bringing the total backlog to $3.6 billion.

As fast as Rocket Lab grew in Q3, this massive backlog implies it's going to grow even faster in the future.

Also important is CEO Sir Peter Beck's observation that the company signed $470 million in Space Systems contracts and "nearly $1 billion" in new launch contracts in the quarter.

Why is this important? Consider that, according to data from S&P Global Market Intelligence, Rocket Lab's Launch Services division earned 43% gross profit margins over the last 12 months, while Space Systems earned closer to 33.6%. That's nearly a 10-percentage-point difference in profitability between Rocket Lab's two main divisions, with the advantage currently going to Launch Services -- which is outselling Space Systems 2-to-1!

So what does that mean for Rocket Lab? Not only will revenue growth accelerate, but the profits Rocket Lab earns on that revenue will accelerate even more (thanks to a higher profit margin).

And the good news doesn't end there. Rocket Lab generated positive operating cash flow in Q3 2026 -- $7.2 million. It's not a big number. It's not positive free cash flow. But this is the first time Rocket Lab has generated positive operating cash flow for a full quarter. Once it grows beyond capital investment costs, Rocket Lab will finally become FCF-positive.

I have publicly predicted that Rocket Lab will turn generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) profitable and FCF positive in 2027 after it completes its acquisition of Iridium Communications and adds Iridium's revenue and profit streams to its own.

In the meantime, Rocket Lab's Q4 guidance will show steady progress toward its goal. Turning to guidance, management predicts quarterly revenue between $270 million and $290 million, with gross margins from Launch Services and Space Systems blending to average 34%-36%.

This implies gross profit of about $98 million for the quarter. If operating costs reach $168 million -- as management also predicts -- this will prevent Rocket Lab from turning profitable in Q4. I'd similarly expect operating cash flow to be eclipsed by capital spending costs, which are averaging more than $30 million per quarter right now -- so you can expect no FCF in Q4, either.

All that said, Rocket Lab is on the right trajectory for profit. Sales are rising, profit margins are rising too, and Rocket Lab has demonstrated that it can generate positive operating cash flow. All it needs now is to scale that up past capital requirements. Once Iridium Communications is brought in-house, I expect that mission to be accomplished.
