AMD Stock Just Plunged on a Short-Seller Report. Is This a Golden Buying Opportunity?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

AMD’s fundamentals are more than just a whisper of “buy the dip.”

By Prosper Junior Bakiny – Nov 22, 2026 at 3:30AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Advanced Micro Devices ›

Chip stocks can't seem to catch a break. Recent developments, including a scandal and a major leadership change at Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%), the most important semiconductor company, have weighed on the industry.

Another market leader is now facing a headwind. Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -13.25%) was the target of a short-seller report published on Nov. 19, which sent the company's shares down significantly, extending the losses the chipmaker had experienced over the past few weeks. Is AMD worth investing in on the dip?

Hunterbrook Media, an investigative journalism outlet, published the report in question. It is affiliated with Hunterbrook Capital, a hedge fund that trades based on reporting from its journalism arm.

In the report, Hunterbrook claims that some of AMD's firmware ended up in the hands of a Chinese server manufacturer that the U.S. government has blacklisted. That means AMD cannot sell its software to this company, at least not without the appropriate license or authorization, and may be in violation of export restrictions if it does so.

If AMD is found to have broken the law, it could face penalties, including a hefty fine, more stringent export restrictions, or, worse, criminal investigations involving its executives. This could disrupt the company's business for a while.

But that's if the allegations are true. Hunterbrook itself acknowledges that it doesn't know how this Chinese company got hold of AMD's firmware. It could have obtained the firmware in various ways that do not involve the company that made it.

There's also the possibility that AMD does have the appropriate export license. At any rate, there is limited direct evidence that demonstrates wrongdoing on the part of AMD, at least in my view.

It's worth noting that AMD has denied these allegations. That's what the company would do, of course. It wouldn't simply acknowledge wrongdoing publicly. However, Hunterbrook Capital itself stands to gain from a decline in AMD's stock price.

That's how short-sellers make money. So there is also a financial incentive. Meanwhile, Reuters, a highly respected journalism outlet, said that it could not independently verify Hunterbrook's claims. That doesn't mean the claims are false.

But the evidence for them is looking slim, at best. In my view, investors should ignore this whole debacle and continue focusing on AMD's fundamentals and business outlook. And once we do that, there is a strong case for investing in AMD.

Consider the company's third-quarter results. AMD's revenue increased by 45% year over year to $13.4 billion. The company's non-GAAP gross margin was 56%, up from 54% in the year-ago period, while it posted non-GAAP earnings per share of $2.09, up 74% from the prior-year quarter.

AMD's data center segment was once again the star of the show, with its sales nearly doubling to a record $8.5 billion. Business is booming for AMD, and there is more where that came from. The company's leadership in CPUs (central processing units) positions it well to capitalize on the growing adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) agents.

Agentic AI runs on CPUs, and this market is driving up AMD's sales. As management noted, data center revenue growth has accelerated every quarter this year, and that should continue in the fourth quarter, thanks in part to the ongoing agentic AI boom. AMD expects revenue growth of more than 80% in 2027, well above the growth it posted in 2026.

And over the next five years, we could see the semiconductor leader continue to tap into its large server CPU total addressable market (TAM), which should reach $220 billion by 2030, according to the company's projections -- and AMD has increased its mid-term TAM estimates several times over the past year as demand for its products continues to expand.

The opportunity across the entire range of its business is even larger. Lastly, AMD is trading at 31.4x forward earnings, a fair valuation, in my view, considering the opportunities ahead. The stock may not stay down for too long. Investors should buy the dip.
