How GKIDS Home Releases Are Creating the Fandom They Want to See in the World

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Though the days when every film gets a massive DVD release are gone, and all kinds of logistics shape how companies like GKIDS plan home releases, the core requirement remains the same. Someone on the GKIDS team has to be a fan.

Director of Home Entertainment Alison Kozberg is a fan of all the titles the company is releasing this winter, from the incredibly personal and warm “Linda Linda Linda” to the more wrenching (though you would expect nothing less from Hideaki Anno) “Love & Pop.” But crafting a personal fan experience dictates the presentation and bonus features behind each release, and that can look wildly different for each title GKIDS puts out. “ Every project is really different. Every project has different access. Some films have creators that are living; some films don’t. Some films have a rich archive; others don’t. So you’re always navigating different conditions of production,” Kozberg told IndieWire. “[We think about] what is emotionally resonant for us. And we really have conversations about that. What do we feel about this film? What artwork and images impact us? What is particularly interesting? Like, when thinking about bonus features, you also think about what captures your imagination.”

When it came to putting out a box set for the Netflix series “Arcane,” for instance, the team was guided by the incredibly rich tradition of cosplay and fanworks in the “League of Legends” community online. They imagined fun art that would be right at home in an artists’ alley at a convention expo hall, and that guided everything from the art on the discs to the set of dice that came in the box set. But with this year’s collector’s edition of “Angel’s Egg,” the incredible collaboration between director Mamoru Oshii (“Ghost in the Shell”) and legendary artist Yoshitaka Amano, the home entertainment team wanted to cultivate a completely different experience.

“We were really, really, really passionate about being able to bring ‘Angel’s Egg’ to people in North America, both theatrically and on disc,” Kozberg said. “We really, really wanted to create a forum to extend access to the creators, but also to create access to Amano’s artwork, which we know fans are so passionate about, and which is so exquisite. So when putting the package together, we tried to be really thoughtful, and really restrained, and really platform these beautiful illustrations.”

It was also important for the set to do what great home releases have been doing since we all went hunting for behind-the-scenes content hidden as Easter Eggs inside DVD menus. It was important to teach curious audiences about how filmmaking works and the context that makes a particular story such a miracle to have gotten made. The interviews and essay booklet were designed to build the historical context for a film as dense and thematically rich as “Angel’s Egg” and to give fans a platform to extend their conversations about it.

“We’re always thinking about ways to additionally kind of facilitate and support other people’s creative inspiration,” Kozberg said. “And  there is a real genuine joy to having objects connected to films you love in your life. I look at the ‘Angel’s Egg’ art cards. I think it feels really special and joyful to be able to have those displayed in your home, to think about ‘Angel’s Egg’ in the day-to-day, to see the artwork.”

While GKIDS releases a mix of live-action and animated titles, the overall theme of their work is facilitating fandom. “We talk a lot about kind of like seeing people’s fandom and reflecting it back to them. So [our goal] is understanding what about a film is emotionally resonant, and then creating opportunities to reflect and engage with that emotion,” Kozberg said.
