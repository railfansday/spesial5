Catholic Girls’ School Thriller ‘On Top of the Cliff’ Wins Top Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival Award

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

'When She Hears the Bells,' 'Pop Pop,' 'The Matriarch,' 'One Sweet Hour' and 'How to Divorce During the War' are also among the winners of the 30th anniversary edition of the Estonian festival.

Peruvian filmmaker Enrica Pérez’s Catholic girls’ school thriller On Top of the Cliff won the top award, the Grand Prix, in the main competition of the 30th edition of Estonia’s Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival (PÖFF) and also earned Bianca Bucelli the best actress award for her performance in the movie.

In the atmospheric suspense drama, a worldly teen challenges a strict boarding school’s resident “holy” girl.

The best director honor went to Swiss writer-director Fred Baillif for his cinematic blend of fiction and documentary, A Job Well Done, which dives into the complexities and moral ambiguity that social workers working with immigrants face. In the performance categories of the main competition, Bucelli was joined by Mathisse De Sutter, who won the best actor honor for his screen debut in Patrice Toye’s The Assignment. The jury lauded “the tenderness and emotional depth” he brought to the cinematic exploration of masculinity in the aftermath of war.

The jury, led by two-time Oscar-winning screenwriter and director Christopher Hampton (Dangerous Liaisons, Atonement), also featured Lebanese producer Myriam Sassine, U.S. cinematographer and visual effects specialist David Stump, French producer Julie Billy and Estonian actress Tiina Tauraite.

The jury lauded On Top of the Cliff for its strengths across the board. “Among a number of extremely accomplished entries, the jury was especially impressed by the high quality of every department of this film – direction, script, acting, cinematography, design and music – combining to create a hermetic universe, intense, powerful and visually eloquent,” it said.

In PÖFF’s First Feature Competition, Chabname Zariab’s When She Hears the Bells won the best film award. The film stars Golshifteh Farahani as a widow in Kabul, Afghanistan, who is fighting the exploitation of kids as the Taliban nears the city.

Young U.S. filmmaker Dylan Stephen Levers’ debut feature Pop Pop, which explores the manosphere and radicalization in the digital age, took the top prize in Tallinn’s Rebels With a Cause section.

Meanwhile, Maya Vitkova-Kosev’s One Month won the best film honor in PÖFF’s Critics’ Picks Competition. The coming-of-middle-age film tells the story of artist Sarah, 45, whose parents surprise her with a shocking ultimatum: After a lifetime of being protected and supported, she has one month to leave the family home.

And in the Baltic Film Competition, Andrius Blaževičius’ Lithuanian Oscar entry How to Divorce During the War won the best feature film award. The jury for the section highlighted the film’s ability to find humor amid difficult circumstances and “weave the personal and political into everyday details.” The honor makes Blaževičius the first two-time winner of the award after his 2021 win for his feature Runner.

Of course, the inaugural Free World Award, unveiled by PÖFF and the Odesa International Film Festival earlier this week to recognize “cinema that champions freedom, human dignity and human rights,” went to Halkawt Mustafa’s No Paradise If You Are Killed by a Woman. The award is made from melted weapons metal.

And the 2026 Audience Award went to Louis Paxton’s The Incomer.

Among the other honorees and special mention recipients on Friday night were also such features highlighted in THR‘s Tallinn coverage as The Matriarch, One Sweet Hour, Exotic, Arrow for A Stone Heart, Night Vision, Father’s House, and Princess.
