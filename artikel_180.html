Spotify’s new tool aims to stop deepfakes from being attributed to real artists

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

As AI-generated slop continues to flood music streaming platforms, Spotify is rolling out an “Artist Profile Protection” tool to all artists, giving them control over the music appearing under their name. The official launch comes eight months after the company began beta testing the feature with select artists.

Spotify says the tool is designed to address a longstanding issue across streaming services, where music can end up on the wrong artist profile due to deliberate impersonation, metadata errors, or shared artist names.

The update comes as Billboard reported last month that Sony Music had requested the removal of more than 260,000 deepfake songs from digital platforms, nearly double the number the label disclosed in March.

With this new feature, Spotify is aiming to give artists more control over their digital identities as the volume of music uploaded to streaming services continues to grow.

Artists can enable the feature through Spotify for Artists to receive alerts when new music is submitted under their name. If a release doesn’t belong to them, they can remove their association with it to stop it from appearing on their profile.

By default, releases go live as scheduled unless an artist intervenes. If artists want greater control, they can choose to switch on an advanced setting that requires them to manually approve or reject releases before they appear on their profile.

“Artists should never have to find someone else’s music under their name, said Sam Duboff. Spotify’s Global Head of Marketing and Policy, in an email, stated to TechCrunch. “Their artist profile should be in their control. Starting today, every artist on Spotify can use Artist Profile Protection to review what shows up on their profile and prevent spammy and incorrect releases from ever reaching their page or their fans. We’re the first and only streaming service with a solution to the industry-wide problem of content mismatch, and we hope it sets a new standard for streaming platforms.”

The company says it worked with thousands of artists during the beta testing phase to refine the tool and make it easier to protect their profiles without disrupting release schedules.
