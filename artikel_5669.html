Novel ADC Could Be New Option in Recurrent Ovarian Cancer

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

— A phase III trial will test sofetabart mipitecan in platinum-sensitive and -resistant disease

Promising data from a phase I study presented at the European Society for Medical Oncology congress suggested encouraging activity with the investigational antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) sofetabart mipitecan (Sofe-M) combined with bevacizumab in patients with recurrent platinum-sensitive ovarian cancer.

In this exclusive MedPage Today video, Roisin O'Cearbhaill, MD, of Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center in New York City, discusses the early findings and how the three-part FRAmework-01 study could expand treatment options for patients with platinum-sensitive and platinum-resistant ovarian cancer.

Following is a transcript of her remarks:

It was a very exciting phase I study. It was looking at a folate-targeting antibody-drug conjugate, and they were looking at the platinum-sensitive population, patients whose cancer had recurred more than 6 months after prior platinum-based therapy. The majority of patients had measurable disease and they treated the patients with Sofe-M plus bevacizumab. So it was phenomenal to see the waterfall plots with such great response rates, objective response rates, in the vast majority of patients and disease control rates in 100% of the patients.

Not only the efficacy data, but also the toxicity data was very exciting in terms of the thing that we always worry about with antibody-drug conjugates or eye toxicity or pneumonitis. So it was great to see that those really weren't a concern with this novel ADC.

And so based on this and other clinical data, we're actually participating in a global phase III study. There are three different subprotocols in that.

In part A, we're looking at the platinum-resistant population, and that is going to be looking at standard-of-care, single-agent chemotherapy versus the Sofe-M. And then in the other two cohorts, they're for patients who have platinum-sensitive disease. In part B, patients have received a prior PARP inhibitor. And in part C, patients have not received a prior PARP inhibitor. In both part B and C, patients are randomized to the standard of care, which would be carboplatin plus another chemotherapy agent with bevacizumab. And then the investigational arm is looking at Sofe-M in combination with bevacizumab.

I hope that we'll have a new treatment option for our patients with recurrent platinum-resistant and platinum-sensitive disease. It would be really great if we could move away from empiric platinum-based chemotherapy and have other options to offer our patients in the platinum-sensitive setting. And for the platinum-resistant, it would be great to have yet another effective treatment option.
