Permitting Reform Bill Passes Senate as Industry Awaits House Decision

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

A bipartisan bill bringing reform to the permitting process for building homes and apartments has officially passed the Senate, with industry organizations applauding the effort.

The Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026 (S.5653, also known as BAAJA)—originally proposed back on September 30—passed the Senate on Thursday, Nov. 19 by a vote of 62 to 33, and is now off to the House for consideration. The bill includes several provisions to roll back some regulatory requirements from the National Environmental Policy Act, the Clean Water Act, energy infrastructure and more in order to streamline the permitting process and fast track production of more housing.

National Association of Home Builders Chairman (NAHB) Bill Owens lauded the Senate’s passing of the “landmark permitting reform legislation that will help builders deliver the affordable homes families need.”

“By cutting the regulatory costs and delays that drive up the price of construction, BAAJA will expand housing supply, lower costs for buyers and renters, and increase access to affordable rental housing,” he continued. “With so many families priced out of the market, the House must pass this bill without delay.”

Owens and NAHB have addressed the need for regulatory reform many times as of late, as several data reports have shown that the housing construction industry continues to struggle under the weight of affordability and other issues.

Taking a closer look, the bill “limits the scope” of reviews for the National Environmental Policy Act by limiting the type of actions that trigger environmental review, narrowing the alternatives that are considered in such reviews, and exempting some types of building projects from reviews. The bill would also impose deadlines for issuing permits once environmental reviews are complete.

National Association of Realtors® (NAR) Executive Vice President and Chief Advocacy Officer Shannon McGahn noted that the housing shortage “demands solutions that make it easier and more affordable to build,” some of which are provided in this bill. She added that NAR “supports streamlining permitting processes and reducing unnecessary regulatory burdens that drive up development costs, delay construction, and limit housing supply.”

“These barriers affect everything from starter homes to multifamily rental housing, making it harder to meet the needs of buyers and renters alike,” she continued. “With the median age of first-time homebuyers now at a record high of 40, the urgency is clear. Reducing these obstacles is critical to expanding housing options, improving affordability, and making the American Dream of homeownership attainable for more Americans.”

The bill lays out similar provisions for the Clean Water Act: a narrower review scope, extended terms for permits under the National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System and a limited review period for permits for material discharges.

Energy regulations are also addressed, first and foremost by expanding the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (FERC) authority to issue permits for interstate electricity transmission projects. Additionally, the bill would allow FERC to establish ratepayer protections related to data centers or other “large computational loads,” would remove permit requirements for onshore oil and gas drilling on nonfederal land, and would accelerate offshore energy projects such as renewable energy, geothermal or hydropower.

Claudia Larsen is a senior editor for RISMedia.
