Chainguard goes self-serve with free Starter plan and $500-a-month Team plan

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Software supply chain security company Chainguard Inc. is now offering its products on a self-serve basis, with the launch today of a free plan called Chainguard Starter and a paid Team plan priced at $500 a month.

The free plan covers nearly all of Chainguard’s products, with usage limits set at a level that fits a personal project or a small team. According to Chainguard, users can sign up, pull an image, connect their package manager to its screened libraries and place coding agents in a sandbox in one sitting, with no credit card or sales call required.

The launch comes after Black October, the name given to a run of supply chain attacks and critical vulnerability disclosures in the third week of October. With incidents like those, a team may have only until the end of the day to deal with a bad dependency, according to the blog post in which Chainguard announced the plans. In such cases, the company wrote, “nobody is going to wait on a purchase order.”

Until now, access to Chainguard’s products has typically required a conversation with its sales team first. One exception was Catalog Starter, a free beta plan offered earlier this year that was limited to five container images. Chainguard says the sales-led model, designed around large companies with procurement processes, works far less well for a team that needs a hardened base image ahead of its next deployment.

In most cases, moving a service onto Chainguard requires changing only a single line in the file that defines its container image. Security engineers can also put Chainguard’s images and libraries through their own scanners before signing anything, according to the company.

Starter includes five container images from Chainguard’s catalog of more than 3,000 minimal images, which are claimed to have near-zero known vulnerabilities, with downloads of up to 100 gigabytes a month. A software bill of materials and signed build provenance are provided for every image.

Also part of the plan is Chainguard Libraries, which provides malware- and greyware-scanned dependencies for JavaScript, Python, Java, Go and .NET with a limit of 50,000 pulls a month. Under a fixed seven-day cooldown, newly published versions are held back for a week, which the company says allows a compromised release to be caught before it reaches a build.

Starter also comes with 500 hours a month of virtual CPU time on each of Chainguard Checks and Chainguard Workspaces, which launched in beta at the company’s Assemble London event in October. Checks is designed to provide hardened runners for continuous integration, with Workspaces providing hardened sandboxes for coding agents.

Other features on the free plan include hardened replacements for GitHub Actions, more than 1,000 hardened agent skills, community support and 100 gigabytes of private artifact storage. Starter users also get 1 million tokens a month for Guardener, Chainguard’s artificial intelligence agent, which inventories the GitHub Actions and container images in a customer’s repositories and opens pull requests to swap in the company’s hardened versions. Each Starter account is limited to five users.

The paid Team plan, which is billed to a credit card, includes everything in Starter with higher limits, such as unlimited users, 1 terabyte of image downloads and 1 million dependency pulls a month. Team customers also get base, application and AI images at their latest versions on top of their five chosen images, along with 1,000 hours a month each of Checks and Workspaces, 5 million Guardener tokens and 500 gigabytes of private storage.

Features reserved for Team include backported fixes for known vulnerabilities across five remediated versions, zero-day patches on the day of disclosure, ticketed support and managed policies that set rules for what is pulled and what is blocked. Chainguard says the usual reasons for upgrading include a sixth person needing access, a project outgrowing the usage limits and a team needing a patch for a version it cannot yet move off.

According to Chainguard, the upgrade takes a few seconds from the console that flags which limit a user has hit. Nothing has to be migrated, since the account, images and session all carry over. Customers can cancel at any time from the same console. Storage beyond Team’s included 500 gigabytes will be metered starting Jan. 1, 2027, according to Chainguard’s documentation.

The Enterprise offering is unchanged and still starts with a conversation with the company’s sales team. Enterprise provides companywide use of Chainguard with no usage limits, along with service-level agreements, enterprise compliance features and integrations. Starter is available now.

Chainguard says it has raised $892 million since its founding in 2021. Its most recent round, $280 million from General Catalyst’s Customer Value Fund, came in October 2025.
