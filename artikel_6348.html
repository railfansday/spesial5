The 8 Best Handheld Vacuums for Cleaning Small Messes

Cleaning is soothing for some, but for others, it’s simply another task to add to an overwhelming to-do list. Hauling your clunky upright vacuum out of the closet every time you need to tidy up a minor spill or dust bunny makes the task of cleaning even more inconvenient. That’s why handheld vacuums are such a practical companion to your regular vacuum. They help you reach narrow spaces such as stairs, corners, under car seats, and around furniture and cut down on how much large, clunky equipment you need to carry around.

But don't retire your go-to vacuum just yet. Handheld vacs are better suited for tackling small messes in between your deeper cleans. Since they have lower dustbin capacities and less suction power, they aren’t equipped to handle whole rooms or entire floors and can’t penetrate deep enough into your carpet fibers. However, if you’re looking for a machine that can supplement your usual cleaning tools, then a handheld vacuum might be right for you.

We evaluated a host of handheld vacuums from top brands like Black+Decker, Dyson, and Shark. We looked at their capability, features, and capacities to find the best devices to help you maintain cleanliness. These are the eight models we recommend, along with expert insight and valuable tips before you shop.

For handheld vacuums that we tested ourselves, we evaluated their performance around the house on a mix of surfaces and different types of furniture, paying attention to how well they handled everyday messes and how easy they were to maneuver. We also considered some of the more practical details like emptying the dustbin, maintaining the filter, and overall ease of use.

For models we haven’t tested firsthand, we drew on our extensive vacuum-testing knowledge, researching leading contenders and focusing on key features like battery life, filtration, suction performance, and attachments.

We prioritized cordless, rechargeable handheld vacuums that work well for a range of messes, such as pet hair and crumbs. This list reflects our top selections for handling everyday messes and more demanding cleanup jobs.

The dustbin holds about half a liter of debris at a time, and it empties with a quick press of a button. The Dustbuster comes with a wall mount for quick and convenient storage.

What we appreciate about this handheld vacuum is that Shark has packed in similar features to some of the pet hair vacuums we love, like a paddled roller brush for scooping up deeply embedded hair and making continuous contact with surfaces. The Dirt Devil Scorpion has a 15-foot power cable so you don’t have to always be within range of an outlet, along with a 2.5-foot detachable hose and built-in crevice tool for picking up dirt and debris in hard-to-reach areas.

Use this alongside your corded upright, and you’ll have the suction power you need to tackle tough household messes. We just wish it came with some more attachments to really take it to the next level.

A quality handheld vac should be ready to use whenever you need it, which means having a dependable battery with a run time that lasts long enough so you don’t lose power mid-clean. Many of our selections use a lithium-ion battery that can last anywhere from around 10 to 50 minutes between charges. Just be prepared that some models take several hours to go from fully dead to fully charged, so keeping the vacuum docked and hooked up to power consistently is important.

Handheld vacuums have a smaller dustbin capacity and fill up faster than full-size, upright versions, but that’s because they handle light spills, not whole-home cleaning. If you regularly reach for your handheld vacuum, look for models that hold up to half a liter or more of debris, so you don’t have to make as many trips to the garbage can.

A handheld vacuum with multiple attachments may be a worthwhile investment if you have kids that are prone to dropping crumbs or furry friends that shed profusely. Crevice tools get into the nooks and crannies of the couch, whereas bristled or motorized brushes pick up stubborn pet hair.

Additional hose pieces can also be beneficial if you need extended reach. These types of attachments focus airflow and suction to a designated area, and may be used on different surfaces like upholstery, carpet, and hardwood floors.

Handheld vacuums often come with cloth, foam, or pleated filters that trap dust, hair, grime, and allergen particles—and most of these versions are washable to avoid buying replacements all the time.

Some handheld vacuums include an additional filter, called the pre-filter, which traps larger particles before they reach the main filter. Others might have a HEPA filter that removes up to 99.7 percent of airborne particles and is useful for allergy sufferers.

We recommend shopping for a handheld vacuum with at least a two-step filtration system or a HEPA filter if you’re trying to keep your home or car clear of common allergens like pollen and pet dander. Otherwise, a washable filter will do just fine for general cleaning tasks.

What is a good suction power rating for a handheld vacuum?

First, it’s important to note that brands don’t all advertise suction power the same way. Some list air watts (AW), some use pascals (Pa), and some don’t provide a suction rating at all and focus more on the motor.

These measurements aren’t directly interchangeable, but for everyday messes and light debris I would look for a handheld vacuum rated around 20 to 40AW or between 5,000 to 10,000Pa.

How often should I clean or replace the filter?

A light cleaning should be done every few weeks or whenever you notice reduced suction power. Foam and felt filters can be washed every 1 to 3 months, and should be replaced every 3 to 6 months.

HEPA filters are not washable unless specified by the vacuum manufacturer and should be replaced between 6 to 12 months. You can adjust the frequency of maintenance based on your cleaning needs and how much debris you typically pick up

There’s no one-size-fits all for how long a handheld vacuum lasts because it largely depends on build quality, frequency of use, battery health, and maintenance. Cleaning the filter and dustbin regularly helps preserve the battery and motor, too, but if the vacuum is losing suction or the battery can’t hold a charge, it might be time for a replacement.
