He Played For Leeds at 18, Then He Was Sent to Prison For 9 Years

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Michael Owen and Frank Lampard are two of the most recognisable names in English football history, racking up 935 Premier League appearances between them, and the pair have naturally had a number of incredibly talented teammates in their time at the very top of the game.

Owen famously won the Ballon d'Or in 2001, after enjoying a prolific season at Liverpool. The pacey striker netted 158 goals in 297 appearances for the Reds, before going on to enjoy stints at Real Madrid, Newcastle United and Manchester United. He finished his career with a Premier League title, an FA Cup and two League Cups to his name.

Meanwhile, Lampard's illustrious career saw him become the most prolific goalscoring midfielder in Premier League history. He hung up his boots having scored 177 goals in the English top flight, whilst he also has a number of team accolades to his name, including three League titles and a Champions League triumph.

While the pair played alongside an array of exceptional, world-class players, there are always teammates that fall by the wayside — not everyone makes it to the very top and stays there. Former Leeds United star Paul Shepherd is among those who played with both ex-England stars and very much falls into this category.

Twenty-seven years after making his first team debut for Leeds at the age of 18, Shepherd was sentenced to nine years in prison.

Born in West Yorkshire, Shepherd joined the Whites' academy at a young age. He steadily progressed through the various youth ranks before going on to make a solitary appearance for the senior side under George Graham.

After uninspiring loan spells at Ayr United and Tranmere Rovers, Leeds parted ways with the full-back, allowing him to leave for the former club in 1999. He'd go on to enjoy stints at Scunthorpe United, Luton Town and Scarborough, as well as spending time in the lower leagues with various semi-professional Yorkshire clubs.

16 years after hanging up his boots, Shepherd's post-retirement life came crashing down when he was arrested and subsequently found guilty of conspiracy to supply Class A drugs.

A defensively astute full-back at youth level, there was some level of excitement over what Shepherd could develop into as he approached the first team. His performances in the Leeds academy were garnering attention, and a month after taking over at Elland Road, Graham chose to acknowledge the defender by calling him up to a matchday squad.

In October 1996, the Whites travelled to North London to take on Graham's former club Arsenal at Highbury. Leeds may have lost 3-0, but the game marked a memorable occasion for Shepherd, who made his senior debut. Completing the entire 90 minutes, the right-back was strangely deployed as a forward, alongside legendary number nine Ian Rush.

Many thought that this game might serve as a launchpad for Shepherd's career at Elland Road, but it proved to be anything but. Graham inexplicably opted to leave the youngster out of the squad for the rest of the 1996/97 season, despite the player being properly integrated into the senior set-up at this point.

“George came into the club, by then I was a professional, and he literally never said two words to me,” Shepherd told Planet Football in 2020.

"As a young kid, you just think ‘he doesn’t like me’. It was really hard to deal with. He [Graham] gave me my debut at Arsenal, but he never spoke to me once during the time I was there, before, during or after that game. Literally nothing. As an 18-year-old kid, I had no idea how to handle it, to be honest. It was a real shock to the system.”

While Graham evidently wasn't fond of Shepherd, this lack of senior minutes at club level didn't prevent the Leeds academy graduate from making waves in the England youth set-up. In the summer of 1997, Shepherd was named in the Three Lions' squad for the FIFA World Youth Championship, held in Malaysia.

The England squad consisted of the likes of Jamie Carragher, Danny Murphy and, of course, Owen. A year or so before that, Shepherd had been mixing it with Lampard in England's under-18s squad.

Despite being on a level playing field with these future stars, the Leeds man didn't develop in the same way some of his peers did. After Graham froze him out, Shepherd attempted to reignite his career in West Yorkshire by twice going on loan. Neither proved successful, and he eventually left his boyhood club to sign for Scottish outfit Ayr United.

His exit from Leeds sparked the beginning of a six-year spell which saw Shepherd play for eight different clubs. After failing to make an appearance at Oldham, he spent time in non-league, at Scarborough, Leigh RMI, Harrogate Town and finally, Stalybridge Celtic.

Shepherd retired in 2005 after struggling to settle at a number of different clubs. In 2023, the ex-footballer was arrested for dealing large amounts of cocaine.

According to BBC News, Shepherd was found guilty of being a trusted courier for the drugs, involved in collecting and delivering cash for linchpin Carl O'Flaherty all over the UK.

The former Leeds star was jailed alongside three other men, with the criminal network uncovered after police stopped Shepherd and seized cocaine from his car. It was unearthed that O'Flaherty had led a wholesale drugs supply business for a number of years, and he was ultimately sentenced to 17 years and 10 months in prison.

Shepherd's sentence was less severe, although he was still jailed for nine years and six months after pleading guilty.

A former Arsenal player, who played alongside Jack Wilshere and Cesc Fabregas, was sentenced to four years in prison back in 2025.
