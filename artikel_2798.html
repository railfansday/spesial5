Premier playmaker: Parkston's Mason Jervik named 2026 Mitchell Republic football player of the year

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Nov. 27—PARKSTON, S.D. — Barely three years ago, before his high school career had even begun, Mason Jervik considered giving up football.

The Parkston Trojans are sure glad he didn't.

During four years in blue and orange, Jervik evolved from a speedy secret weapon to a complete player and a multi-year captain. Along the way, he established a reputation as a top-tier playmaker and a winner within one of South Dakota's top nine-man football programs.

"I wanted to leave my legacy at Parkston because this program has been so good to me," Jervik said. "I wanted the younger kids to look up to me so I could show them that you don't have to be the biggest guy or the strongest guy to make an impact."

Jervik's lasting imprint includes exceptional contributions to maintaining the Trojans' team culture and winning ways, along with a long list of personal accolades.

For spearheading a perfect 12-0 state championship season in Class 9AA, Parkston senior Mason Jervik earned the Mitchell Republic's 2026 football player of the year award. Jervik, who was named the most outstanding back of the state championship contest, also received postseason honors as a Class 9AA all-state running back for the second-straight year and an all-Missouri Valley Conference selection for the title-winning Trojans.

As a senior, Jervik totaled 1,370 yards from scrimmage, leading the Trojans in both rushing with 875 yards and receiving with another 496 yards, and scored 28 total touchdowns on the way to a Class 9AA championship. Defensively, he offered 50 tackles with three pass breakups and a forced fumble.

Since 1994, the Mitchell Republic football player of the year has been selected by the newspaper's sports staff, with voting conducted via a point-based system that awards five points to the top player on the ballot, four points to the second player and so on. Jervik received two first-place votes and 14 total points to become the second Parkston player to win the award, joining Luke Bormann from 2023.

Other players receiving consideration for the 2026 player of the year honor were Freeman's Tayden Kerrigan, Platte-Geddes' Landon Keiser, Hanson's Jason Porth, Parkston's Brycen Bruening, Kimball/White Lake's Carter Konechne and Avon's Grayson Ratzlaff.

Brayden Jervik remembers sitting at the dinner table when his younger brother said he didn't want to play football anymore. Plagued by injuries as a youth player, Mason Jervik was entering his freshman year and wanted to focus on his first love of sports: basketball.

But Brayden, a rising senior, wasn't having it.

"I was basically like, 'You've just got to hang in there one year, and I promise we'll make it worth it. If you hold on one more year, we'll get you a (state championship) ring," Brayden recalled. "That talked him back into it. Obviously, it worked out pretty well."

With Brayden, a two-time Class 9AA all-state performer at linebacker in his own right, as one of the central figures, Parkston ran the table to go 12-0 and win the program's first Class 9AA football title in the fall of 2023.

That season changed everything for Mason, igniting a flame for football that eventually grew into a fiery passion for the sport he nearly left behind.

"You could just see this spark went off inside of him," said Parkston head coach Matt Grave. "He took a couple hard hits as a freshman but popped back up. He started gaining confidence, and his body was getting stronger. As a coach, you just prayed that he continued to stay healthy so that everybody had the opportunity to see what you saw."

Though still an underclassman, Mason resolved to uphold the championship standard his brother and the 2023 Trojans had established.

"I've just loved football from then on. Those guys were so influential, and they helped me so much. I can't thank them enough," Mason said. "The biggest thing they always told me was don't do too much. Go out there, be yourself and just go win."

As a sophomore, Mason Jervik was a starter at defensive back and a key contributor on offense and special teams, as Parkston made another run to the state title game. However, Hamlin proved too much, and the Trojans had to settle for being state runner-up with an 11-1 record.

Jervik's junior season ended short of another trip to Vermillion, as Parkston was clipped during the state semifinals in Elkton, ending at 9-2.

With one year left to add another piece of championship hardware, Jervik helped make sure the Trojans left little to chance.

"He is a great leader, and he realized, 'Hey, now it's my time,'" Grave said of Jervik. "Not in that he's trying to do it all himself, but it's his opportunity to go lead like his brother and those classes before him did. He just took ownership of it. As coaches, we couldn't have asked for anything more."

With Jervik leading the charge, the 2026 Trojans dominated all challengers, matching their predecessors from 2023 with a perfect 12-0 Class 9AA championship campaign. Parkston won every game by at least 23 points, with an average score of 47-8. The Trojans handed conference rival and state runner-up Platte-Geddes its only two losses of the season, a 30-6 final during the regular season and a 36-6 rout in the title game.

During Jervik's four varsity seasons, Parkston's overall record was 44-3.

"We wanted to go get our own (championship)," Jervik said. "We put in all the extra work, and we got it done."

During Mason Jervik's freshman year, Grave put him in the middle of a Homecoming night rout to play a few snaps with his older brother and the rest of the Trojans' offensive starters.

"He only touched the ball three times in that game, but he had two touchdowns and almost had a third. And it wasn't against other freshmen," Grave said. "Even with starters out there, everybody had to play to his speed. You could tell that he's different and special."

Those trips to the end zone were the first of many.

By the time he was finished, Jervik stood alone atop the Parkston charts for career touchdowns. No matter the game, the lasting image of Jervik in a Trojan uniform is likely No. 2 sprinting in the open field on the way to another score. If there is a defender in the frame, they're undoubtedly fading fast.

And yet Jervik, who runs a high-4.5-second 40-yard dash, an 11.5-second 100-meter time, maintains that he's "not that fast," instead pointing to his short-area quickness.

"I feel like what separates me on the football field is I'm more shifty, more quick than just blazing speed," Jervik said. "Right away when I get going, I'm not too fast, but then I can find another gear."

When it came to shaping his style on the football field, Jervik said the former Trojan he most wanted to play like was another No. 2: Cole Prunty, who played both running back and quarterback during his career. Jervik still remembers Prunty, whose final high school football season was in 2021, racing 99 yards to set the school record for longest touchdown.

"I'd love to say that it's a great comparison between us, but I think he looks a little faster than me. He's just phenomenal," Prunty said with a laugh. "I wouldn't want anyone else to wear No. 2. It's the number I wore, his brother (Brayden) wore, and Mason wears it well.

"Just watching from afar, I see how he handles himself. He has such humility, but he's also so passionate," Prunty added. "I've seen him make all these amazing plays, and it's just nothing new. I'm just really proud of him."

After Prunty, in the lineage of standout Parkston ball carriers with game-breaking speed and elusiveness, came Luke Bormann, another player Jervik looked up to as a classmate of his older brother.

Jervik, at 6-foot and 165 pounds, won't be mistaken for a power back. However, in Bormann's eyes, learning to be a physical runner was what allowed Jervik to make the leap and become an elite player.

"When I was an underclassman, we had a coach who said, 'Running backs have to turn 0 (yards) into 3, 3 into 10 and 10 into touchdowns,' Bormann said. "Mason has always been very good at turning 10 into touchdowns, but where he really grew as a runner is when he started being able to turn 3 into 10, putting his shoulder down to get a few more."

All in all, Jervik is as explosive of a playmaker as any in the state.

This season, Jervik scored 26 offensive touchdowns on 129 total touches, effectively good for six points every fifth time he got the ball. He was particularly potent in an expanded Parkston passing attack with fellow senior Brycen Bruening at quarterback. Jervik had 496 receiving yards and seven scores on just 14 receptions, an eye-popping 35.4 yards per reception.

Further, he had seven punt return opportunities all season, as opponents knew better than to punt in his direction. Of the seven, he returned two for touchdowns and had three more scores called back. He was not credited with a single fumble as a senior.

"Opponents needed to know where he was at all times," Grave said. "He is very fundamental, but so explosive. His football speed is among the fastest I've ever seen, with such great change of direction and burst. And I think he really just started tapping into his potential."

Bringing his prep football career full circle, Mason Jervik ended his career in the DakotaDome, a building that helped set him on his current path.

In his final flourish as a Trojan, Jervik scored three touchdowns, hauling in scoring strikes of 47 and 67 yards, and capping the effort with an 8-yard score in the closing moments.

"He showed the whole state how dynamic and explosive he can be on any given play," Grave said of Jervik's championship performance. "He was very special."

As Jervik looks to the future, the kid who once was content to step away from the game altogether doesn't want to leave it.

From former teammates to current coaches, all who know Jervik believe he has traits that will make him successful in his future endeavors within the sport.

"He seems like a sponge; whether it's good information or good habits to build, it feels like he kind of absorbs all of that from his coach, from his older brother, from his teachers," Bormann said. "He's just a one-of-a-kind kind dude, and I think he's going to do a lot of good things."

"I think he's a student of life and can talk and relate with anyone about anything," Grave added. "He adapts very well and is calm in the way he handles things."

Jervik is currently weighing his options for where to play college football. He plans to study physical education and hopes there's a future in coaching ahead of him.

"I feel like I'd miss football too much," Jervik said. "I wouldn't say it's my life, but I love football."
