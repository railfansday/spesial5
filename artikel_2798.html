Warriors are ‘putting too much pressure’ on rookie Yaxel Lendeborg, ‘It’s unfair’

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

NEW ORLEANS — Yaxel Lendeborg, the typically talkative Warriors rookie, was given the night off Monday from his usual postgame media obligations.

He didn't need to say a word to convey his feelings in Golden State's 115-94 loss to the Pelicans. His body language came with as much candor as anything to escape his mouth.

Lendeborg poured in a team-high 28 points, but almost all of them came after the Warriors had dug themselves into a 31-point hole to a team that had lost its previous six games.

Golden State Warriors forward Yaxel Lendeborg (1) reacts during the fourth quarter of an NBA game against the New Orleans Pelicans on Monday, Nov. 16, 2026. Getty Images

He spent the final minutes of the loss sitting on the bench, head hanging, with a towel covering his newly buzzed head of hair. Draymond Green picked up the slack with reporters after the game, and said that's exactly what the Warriors need to do to help the rookie on the court.

"[We need to] depend on him, but lower the expectations," Green said.

Fourteen games into his NBA career, the 24-year-old Michigan product is being asked to be the primary scorer in an offense with precious few options without Stephen Curry.

"It's unfair to him to try to make him step into Steph's role 13 games into his career. So depend on him like we need to, but lower what we expect of him to allow things to just flow into him.

"I think, right now, we're putting too much pressure on him."

Golden State Warriors' Draymond Green, left, is fouled by New Orleans Pelicans' Jordan Poole on Monday, Nov. 16, 2026, at Smoothie King Center in New Orleans. Getty Images

All eyes were already on Lendeborg as soon as he stepped into Smoothie King Center. Taking the court for warm-ups, he unraveled a durag on his head and revealed his brand-new, chopped-off hair.

Lendeborg's decision to lop off his lengthy locks came in response to an even more frustrating loss two nights earlier to the Spurs. He said afterward that the 48-point rout left him "f—ked up mentally." Kerr emphasized the importance of quickly moving on to the next one of 82.

After a 1-of-13 shooting performance Saturday night, Lendeborg came out tepid, passive and generally a mess on both sides of the ball. He turned the ball over as many times as he shot it in the first quarter and racked up a minus-25 differential by halftime in a 61-36 game.

Saddiq Bey #41 of the New Orleans Pelicans blocks the shot of Yaxel Lendeborg #1 of the Golden State Warriors in New Orleans, Monday, Nov. 16, 2026. Getty Images

He was visibly frustrated at times, just like two nights earlier, but coach Steve Kerr didn't think it was a product of Lendeborg's emotions bleeding over from the San Antonio loss.

"I thought he was frustrated because we were getting killed again," Kerr said. "He came over at one point and talked to me about some strategic stuff and made some really good adjustments at halftime and found his angles to attack."

Golden State Warriors forward Yaxel Lendeborg (1) dribbles against New Orleans Pelicans guard Jeremiah Fears (0) on Monday, Nov. 16, 2026, in New Orleans. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

Lendeborg scored 20 on 7-of-14 shooting after halftime, but that was too little, too late.

He knew it. And so did the world. Because he showed as much.

"It's his first year, and we're putting an enormous responsibility on his shoulders," coach Steve Kerr said. "He has to go through this. There's no way around it. You can't cheat your way through that. You have to feel the highs and the lows. You heard his comments the other night. He cares about all this stuff, which is a great sign. But you have to learn how to manage that."

Golden State Warriors' Yaxel Lendeborg (1) plays against New Orleans Pelicans guard Jaron Pierre Jr. (10) in New Orleans, Monday, Nov. 16, 2026. AP Photo/Morgan Simmons

Green, who also doesn't often lack for words, kept it concise when asked about Lendeborg's night. Multiple times, he went up to the rookie with apparent words of advice.

"Just about how he's got to play with Steph not out there," he said.

Golden State Warriors forward Draymond Green (23) sits on the bench against the New Orleans Pelicans on Monday, Nov. 16, 2026, in New Orleans. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

It seemed to be a concerted effort on Green's part to tamp down the attention on Lendeborg, which he has rightfully earned with his Rookie-of-the-Year worthy play but also comes with certain expectations. The pressure only rises without Curry, his 30 points per game and gravity.

The spotlight is going to continue to follow him.

Internally, at least, the Warriors are in control of the dimmer switch.

"We can control what we can control," Green said. "We can't control the [outside] world."
