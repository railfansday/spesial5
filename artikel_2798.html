Warning to NFL general managers: Howie Roseman is about to fleece you

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Warning to NFL general managers: Howie Roseman is about to fleece you originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

A broken-down receiver, a washed-up guard and an underachieving defensive tackle walk into a bar. And Howie Roseman walks out with two 1st-round picks and three 2nd-round picks.

If I were an NFL general manager, I wouldn't even answer the phone when Howie calls. Because you're going to get fleeced.

And if there's one thing to feel good about during this miserable season to nowhere, it's the way Howie has stockpiled premium draft picks with a series of what sure seem like lopsided trades.

It all started in June when Roseman shipped disgruntled and divisive A.J. Brown to the Patriots for a 1st-round pick in 2027 and a 5th-round pick in 2027.

Brown was one of the best in the league not too long ago. He owns the two-best receiving seasons in Eagles history – 88-for-1,496 and 11 TDs in 2022 and 106-for-1,456-7 in 2023.

But it sure seems like his knees won't allow him to be that kind of player again. The A.J. Brown we saw hobbling around and dropping passes in his final game in an Eagles uniform, the playoff loss to the 49ers in January, seems to be closer to the player he is now than the Pro Bowler of a few years ago.

The Patriots got a WR whose balky knees have allowed him to play only three games so far this year, with just nine catches for 101 yards, three drops and a fumble.

Of 95 WRs who've been targetted at least 20 times so far this year, Brown's 15 percent drop percentage is highest. And his catch percentage is 89th. Maybe he gets healthy the second half of the season and reverts back to that three-time Pro Bowler. Or maybe this is just who A.J. Brown is right now. Either way the Eagles got a 1st-round pick for a guy who had no future in Philadelphia, didn't want to be here and may be a shot fighter at 29.

Then came the Cam Jurgens trade, and it's still hard to believe the Ravens donated the Eagles a 2nd-round pick for a guy who just hasn't been the same player as he was in 2024. Jurgens was a tremendous interior lineman before his career was derailed by injuries. Although he made a second straight Pro Bowl last year, he was only a shell of the player he was in 2023 and 2024. This year, he struggled both at center and guard before Howie someone finagled a 2nd-round pick out of the Ravens for him.

How has it gone in Baltimore for Jurgens? You always take Pro Football Focus grades with a grain of salt, but out of 89 interior linemen who've played at least 300 snaps his 43.9 combined grade with the Eagles and Ravens is 4th-worst.

In Jurgens' three games in Baltimore, the Ravens – with both Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley hurt – have scored 33 points, gone 0-3, losing 35-13 to the Bengals, 36-10 to the Bills and 33-10 to the Jaguars after starting the season 4-1.

Then there's Jordan Davis. He was a good player here and at times a very good player. He probably should have made the Pro Bowl last year. But overall? As the 13th pick in the 2022 draft and the 10th-highest paid interior lineman in the league? He's underachieved, in particular this year. He was a key starter on two Super Bowl teams but he just never dominated the way you wanted him to, the way he's being paid to. And he played just 52 percent of the snaps in his nine games this year, which ranks 27th among starting interior linemen so far this year.

Great guy, natural leader, inspiring backstory, solid lineman. But hardly indispensable. Not when you also have Jalen Carter under contract and Moro Ojomo now in line for an offseason extension. The Eagles don't really have a third high-level rotational defensive tackle now – Ty Robinson, a 4th-round pick last year, will likely take over the role. But if you can get 1st- and 2nd-round draft picks for essentially a part-time player that's a no-brainer.

Add it up and Roseman got 1st-round picks in 2027 and 2028, 2nd-round picks in 2027, 2028 and 2029 and a couple 2027 5th-round picks to boot for a beat-up wide receiver, a fading interior lineman and a defensive tackle who hasn't lived up to expectations.

The way Roseman drafts, most of those picks are going to turn into important pieces as the Eagles build back toward respectability.

There hasn't been a whole lot to celebrate on the field this year, and everything points to more of the same over these last eight games.

But up on the second floor of the Jefferson Health Training Complex, Roseman is doing all he can to make sure it doesn't happen again.
