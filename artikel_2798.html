Game Recap: Suns run out of fuel in 116-104 loss to Rockets

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Phoenix Suns fought valiantly, but couldn't finish the road trip undefeated as the Houston Rockets held off a late push by the Suns and earned a 116-104 victory. Phoenix clawed within two at 99-97, but the Rockets finished on a 17-7 run.

Alperen Sengun finished the game with 23 points, 11 rebounds and 11 assists, recording his third triple-double of the season. Seven of those rebounds were on the offensive glass. Steven Adams also snatched six offensive boards, and the Rockets won the overall rebounding battle 51-38.

Oso Ighodaro had 13 points on 6-for-8 shooting, but managed just two rebounds in 20 minutes before fouling out. Khaman Maluach added 12 points of his own and made several outstanding plays, but is still a little too raw to compete with the burly Adams and All-Star Sengun.

The Suns were an abysmal 5-for-32 from three-point range in the game. Jordan Goodwin was 2-for-4, which made the rest of the team 3-for-28. That looks like a typo. Posting a -13 on the boards and shooting 15.6% from the three-point range makes it hard to win a basketball game. A bad shooting night is one thing, but the rebounding situation is starting to look like a fatal flaw for this squad.

Miles Bridges struggled through his worst offensive game of the season, recording just two points while shooting 0-6 from three-point range and 1-8 overall. Devin Booker had a game-high 25 points and Jalen Green chipped in 16, but they were 6-18 and 8-21 from the field, respectively. Booker did knock down 11-of-12 free throws to continue his impressive performance from the line this season.

The Suns' bench group played well overall. In addition to Maluach's performance, Goodwin scored 11 points and grabbed five rebounds, but played just 17 minutes. Kevin Durant recorded a double-double with 20 points and 10 rebounds, while Amen Thompson recorded his own with 18 and 10. That is two more than the Suns had.

The Rockets' starters were just too much for Phoenix to contend with tonight. Plain and simple.

The Suns' aspirations of advancing to the next round in the NBA Cup have been quashed with this loss.

Phoenix will try to get back in the win column when they host the Dallas Mavericks and Cooper Flagg at 7:00pm Friday.

The Rockets raced out to an 11-4 lead with Kevin Durant getting five quick points. The Suns recovered to tie it up at 11, and for a long stretch in the quarter it looked like we might be watching a defense-optional game.

KD kept pouring it in and already had 11 points when the Rockets went up 22-18. At that time, the Rockets held a 10-2 free-throw disparity over the Suns.

The Suns were trying to play physical, but the referees didn't let them impose their will, opting to let Durant and Adams flop to get calls. Maluach ended up with a fairly weak flagrant one as he somehow managed to plant Adams face first on the ground. Or maybe Adams just laid down that way on purpose.

Jordan Goodwin entered and hit a quick three-pointer to pull the Suns within one at 23-22. Goodwin is shooting a career-high 38.6% this season from three-point range.

A dunk and damage for a three-point play from Maluach tied the game at 25. Khaman was playing very well overall but still displayed hand problems during the game. Goodwin hit his second three-pointer to give the Suns a 32-27 lead courtesy of a 10-2 run. The first quarter ended 33-29 in the Suns' favor. Booker finished the quarter with 13 points and survived a massive block by Amen Thompson.

Goodwin was fouled on a three-point attempt after making his first two. He sank all three free throws to give the Suns their biggest lead of the game at 38-31. The Oso/Kennard/Gillespie/Brooks/Goodwin lineup meshed well together, but the Rockets started punishing them inside.

Steven Adams grabbed four quick offensive rebounds. There just isn't a player on the Suns with the size and strength of Adams.

He helped the Rockets go on a 13-7 run to cut the Suns' lead to 45-44. Maluach was still making his presence felt. A few plays after a block on Sengun, he nailed a jumper as the shot clock expired. In between that, he mishandled the ball on offense for a turnover. Such are the highs and lows with the 20-year-old.

Sengun was still winning the matchup overall. After another offensive rebound, Sengun hit a three to cap an 11-2 run that put Houston up 57-51. That turned into a 20-7 run by the time the quarter was over, and Houston led 66-56 at the half. The Suns had squandered their early lead and would trail the remainder of the game.

Booker finished the first half with 15 points and four assists, while Goodwin chipped in nine points.

The Rockets starters had a well-balanced attack. Durant scored 14, Sengun had 13 along with seven rebounds and six assists, Jabari Smith Jr. poured in 12, and Amen just missed double figures with nine points. Houston led the battle of the boards 27-15, including 12-4 on the offensive end. The Rockets shot 26 free throws, but made just 14, while the Suns were 11-14 from the charity stripe.

Sengun, Durant and Amen Thompson were back to mangling the Suns as Sengun inched closer to a triple-double. The Rockets pushed their lead to a game-high 14 as they took a 79-65 lead. Phoenix fought back to cut it to six at 79-73, but a three-pointer by Tari Eason pushed it right back to 12.

The Suns were 0-6 from three-point range in the third. They hung around but couldn't get closer than six points. Lapses like the shot clock violation on their last possession in the quarter seemed to pop up and prevent them from completely catching up.

Maluach and Goodwin finished the quarter with 11 points each. Houston led 92-81.

Luke Kennard was awarded a technical free throw to start the 4th quarter on a positive note. Oso followed that up with a layup and was fouled but missed the free throw. Then it was Gillespie with the bump and the bucket, but he converted his three-point play. Suddenly it was 92-87, and the Suns were right back in it.

The teams traded a few buckets before Kennard cut the lead to four at 99-95. Green blew past a defender for a layup. 99-97. VanVleet drained a dagger three to make it 104-98 after Maluach went 1-for-2 from the line.

Sengun dished out an assist with 3:49 left to notch his third triple-double of the season. Booker made a layup to help the Suns keep pace at 106-100, but after a defensive stop both Booker and Brooks missed wide-open looks. Jabari Smith Jr. missed the second of two free throws, but Sengun scored off another offensive rebound to push the lead to 109-100. A VanVleet three-pointer made it 112-102.

The game ended with Brooks and KD in each other's face. Dillon got to start his shower 23.8 seconds early. I was frustrated too. The Rockets held on for a 116-104 victory, evening the season series 1-1.
