The Mammoth become the NHL’s last team to do this — and it’s why they’ve been so successful

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

On Oct. 29, the Ottawa Senators lost to the Los Angeles Kings in an overtime thriller. They were the NHL's 31st team to suffer two consecutive losses this season — everyone but the Utah Mammoth.

The Mammoth wouldn't concede their first pair of games until nine days later. The Senators happened to be the team to hand them that second loss, a 4-2 decision Saturday night in Ottawa.

"I think it was a good hockey game. We did a lot of good things. Could have gone either way, just kind of a bad way to end it," said Utah captain Clayton Keller, who had points on both of his team's goals in the game.

One-off losses happen to everyone, but you tend to stay out of trouble if you can limit your losing streaks to a game or two. The key now is to make sure the Mammoth don't let it get to three consecutive losses.

Utah's loss broke its win/loss rotation, which had held for 10 games. The Mammoth would win one and lose the next, over and over again. While there are details to clean up, that pattern has helped them maintain a top-three spot in the mighty Central Division.

It's also worth mentioning that no team has played fewer home games to this point in the season than Utah (several have played the same amount). The Mammoth are now five games into their second six-game road trip of the year, and they've already knocked out some of their longest-distance away games.

Head coach André Tourigny admitted after the game that fatigue played a role in Saturday's loss.

"You're on the road, end of a long road trip, like I said, the team's a little bit tired. Our back end is banged up," he said.

After describing a couple of the details that led to goals against, he punctuated it with four simple words: "We're better than that."

Many of Utah's "easiest" games should still be ahead of it. The Mammoth now return home for a few days before shipping out to Las Vegas, where they'll meet their first-ever playoff opponent for the first time since May in the Golden Knights.

After that, they get to sleep in their own beds for a week and a half — and play three of the bottom 10 teams in the league.

If some of those "banged up" bodies, namely their entire defense pairing of Nate Schmidt and John Marino, can get healthy in that span, the team will be in a good position.

The only thing more back-and-forth than Utah's game in Ottawa Saturday was an Olympic ping-pong match.

Ottawa would take the lead, only for the Mammoth to get it back within the next five minutes. But when Nick Cousins tipped a puck past Karel Vejmelka with 2:18 left in the third period, Utah didn't have five minutes to tie it up.

The Senators would score an empty-netter before the night's end to seal it off at 4-2.

As Keller noted after the game, "one shot was the difference."

If there's one thing the Mammoth should be glad to see, it's the way Vejmelka settled in. It's well-documented that the team's longtime starting goalie hasn't been himself so far this season. When he allowed a highly save-able goal five and a half minutes into the game, Utah fans thought they were in for a long night.

On his best save of the night, and one of his best of the season, he went left-to-right to stop a Michael Amadio one-timer midway through the second period.

The two goals Vejmelka allowed after that can hardly be placed on him: A 3-on-1 due to an ill-timed pinch and a back-door tip-in.

The Mammoth are tied for sixth in the league in total goals and allow the sixth-least shots against per game. If Vejmelka and his goalie partner, Sebastian Cossa, can have more games like the latter 54 minutes of this one, there won't be much that can stop Utah.

"We were a minute and a half away from having at least one point, so we're not happy. We're frustrated," Tourigny said. "But we need to make sure we're not overreacting here."
