The One Thing Daisy Edgar-Jones Wishes She Had Stolen from the <i><em>Sense and Sensibility </em></i>Set

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

In honor of the Jane Austen adaptation’s release, the actress, who plays Elinor Dashwood, chats Norland, Barton Cottage, and her own design style.

A crucial plot point in Jane Austen’s Sense and Sensibility revolves around moving. The three Dashwood sisters—the sensible Elinor, the passionate Marianne, and the youngest Margaret—and their widowed mother spend much of the novel concerned about the move, preparing for it, and then coping with the change, as they depart their grand family estate, Norland Park, for Barton Cottage, a disheveled, small house in faraway Devon. The new film version of Austen’s debut novel, out today, brings these spaces—and the Dashwoods’ story—to life.

Daisy Edgar-Jones who, stars as Elinor in the adaptation, and got particular joy working within the film’s intricate sets. In honor of the movie’s release, and this new interpretation of Austen’s spaces, we decided to ask Edgar-Jones some rapid-fire questions about design (“my favorite subject,” she told us).

Below, Edgar-Jones reveals her favorite Dashwood house and what she wishes she had stolen from the Sense and Sensibility set.

ELLE Decor: What house in Sense and Sensibility would you want to live in?

Daisy Edgar-Jones: I love Barton Cottage, and I love what the girls and the women do with it. But it's very, very in the middle of nowhere. It's in the middle of Dartmoor, which is this beautiful moorland in Devon. And I think I would probably ultimately want to live in Norland, which is their first home, because it's so, so beautiful and so homely. And it's in Dorset, which is so beautiful. But, it's a little bit more accessible.

If you could live in any historic or famous house, which would it be?

Is your own decorating style more sense or sensibility?

My taste probably leans more sensibility. I'm not necessarily a minimalist. In a very Marianne-esque way, I love a decorative pillow. I love ribbons. I love lace. I love lots of color and texture.

If you could steal one thing from the Sense and Sensibility set, what would it be?

I wish I could steal more than one thing because I was obsessed with the interiors of this film. Just everything—the vintage pillows, and they sourced the most gorgeous furniture. I did love Marianne's pianoforte. I don't play the pianoforte, but I would like to learn, and an instrument can always make a room feel lived in.

The Dashwood family’s transition from Norland to Barton Cottage is a key part of Sense and Sensibility. What was the biggest move you’ve ever made?

My biggest move was when I was probably 22, and I moved into a flat all on my own, and it was my first time living on my own. It was the first time I really discovered my style and interior aesthetic and what, for me, made a house a home. That felt really formative because it was the first time that I had a space that was completely mine.
