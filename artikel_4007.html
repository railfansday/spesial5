Talking Point: What Made The Xbox 360 Era So Incredible In Your Opinion?

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

As we continue celebrating the past 25 years of Xbox throughout the course of the month, we thought we'd use this opportunity to look at the Xbox 360 - a console that's consistently rated as the best system in the history of the brand.

The Xbox 360 will be turning 21 years old on November 22nd, and its impact is still being felt today. This is the console that gave us such features as Party Chat, modern-style Xbox dashboards, the ability to download mainstream games digitally, and a ridiculous number of incredible franchises that have won over the hearts and minds of many.

For what it's worth, we did actually talk about some of this stuff for the 20th anniversary last year:

With that said though, every era of Xbox has treated us to innovative new features and amazing games that have stood the test of time. The PS3 existed at the same time as the Xbox 360, and yet somehow, Microsoft's console felt like the more popular of the two, despite selling slightly less units overall in the end.

It even managed to navigate a devastating technical issue in the form of the Red Ring of Death, and somehow still convince the masses that it was one of the greatest consoles of all time. That's some feat.

So, what made the Xbox 360 era so incredible in your opinion? Was it just a brilliantly-designed system, RROD aside? Did it innovate in ways that no other system did? Were the games simply that good? Or has its popularity become overblown since the horribly botched launch of the Xbox One in 2013?

The Xbox 360 featured an exclusive title called Lost Odyssey, arguably the greatest JRPG ever created. While most JRPGs are aimed at a younger audience and tend to feature straightforward, simple narratives, Lost Odyssey takes a different approach. Its core, titled "A Thousand Years of Dreams, A Thousand Years of Memories"—which explores the protagonist’s past—was penned by Kiyoshi Shigematsu, a recipient of the prestigious Naoki Prize. These stories delve into universal themes: the experience of loss and separation, a profound sense of emptiness, and the sorrow of having neither a place to return to nor a destination to head toward. Much of this content is written with those in the twilight of their lives in mind.The main storyline is also rooted in the personal tragedies experienced by the project’s executive producer, Hironobu Sakaguchi.The game boasts a top-tier creative team, including character designs by Takehiko Inoue (Slam Dunk, Vagabond) and a musical score by Nobuo Uematsu—some of the finest talent to ever work on an Xbox title.Even today, many fans continue to hope for a remake or a sequel.
