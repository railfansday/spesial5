Microsoft Thinks Xbox 'Pioneered' Dual Analogue Controllers

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

It’s a big month for Xbox as it celebrates its 25th anniversary, with lots of entertaining initiatives underway – including a pretty cool website where it tracks your entire history with the brand.

But in a big – and otherwise interesting – celebratory blog post, boss Asha Sharma appears to have mixed up some of the facts.

Writing about the original Xbox, which launched 15th November 2001, she claims the console “pioneered dual analogue controllers”.

It’s obviously not a big deal, but this is blatantly not true.

While the Xbox innovated in a number ways – namely including a hard drive and ethernet port, which paved the way for the industry changing Xbox Live – dual analogue controllers had already been introduced four years earlier with the PS1.

While it’s true the DualShock wasn’t originally included with Sony’s console as standard, that changed with the PS2’s release in 2000, over a year before the original Xbox arrived.

I wondered whether Sharma may have meant to write “dual analogue triggers” here, but that’s also not true; the Dreamcast’s controller, for example, introduced pressure-sensitive triggers as early as 1999.

So, I’m not exactly sure what Microsoft thinks it invented here, but it sure wasn’t dual analogue controllers!

Yes, this relates to the symmetrical design. The origins of that design stem from the early days on PS1, where most games used digital direction control, and only a few supported analogue.

Hence Sony "tacked on" the Left-Stick in the secondary position and retained the D-Pad in the optimum ergonomic position for primary directional control, just as it was located on the original digital PS1 controller.

Everyone else (Nintendo/Microsoft) later swapped the D-Pad to the secondary position and the directional analogue stick to the primary position when it became clear that analogue control was now the majority control method for almost all games.

In that regard Microsoft were likely the first to release a fully prioritised, dedicated, dual-analogue design.

Technically speaking Sega were the first to place the directional stick in the primary position and shift the D-Pad to secondary (I don't count N64's weird design), but of course the DC only had the one stick....
