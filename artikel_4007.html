"Add to cart status" has been achieved, which is the most Valve way of saying you can buy one now

Valve has issued an update on its Steam Machine stock levels, waiting lists, and purchasing process, saying that the 512GB model has now achieved "add to cart status". After months of joining a lottery-based waiting list to secure the brand's alternative to a gaming PC, you can now grab the entry-level model and its bundled controller with no waiting or reservation constraints.

Unfortunately, the same can't be said of the 2TB model, which could even see further price hikes, let alone wait lists. Valve posted an update to its hardware blog yesterday evening, confirming that despite the doom and gloom of the 2TB model, "We've finished getting purchase invites out to all reservation holders."

The blog post says, "We've been able to fulfill all current reservations for the 512GB model with no waitlist remaining, and have wrapped up sending purchase invites out to all remaining 2TB reservation holders.

"However, we are continuing to encounter supply constraints for hardware components, which impacts our ability to source parts."

The Steam Machine, along with the Steam Controller and Steam Frame, were all meant to launch in "early 2026", but the very same supply constraints Valve is mentioning this week caused massive delays and (presumably) big price hikes on the company's new generation of hardware.

On the 2TB model's future availability, Valve says: "We expect to have more details to share in January about additional supply."

But given the RAM shortages that are ongoing, and that SSD prices just keep increasing, it's highly likely that Valve will need to jump the 2TB model's price to even higher ranges in the new year, because the current RAM and SSD situation shows no sign of stopping.

"We are continuing to build more Steam Machine units, but memory and storage costs continue to increase at unprecedented levels; as a result we don't yet know the number we are able to make, and at what price we'll be able to offer them," says Valve.

With more updates in January, it seems as though Valve is issuing a fairly general and open-ended statement here in order to leave a lot of wiggle room. But the above quote in particular leaves the possibility open for a temporary discontinuation of the 2TB model, or a price hike on new stock.

Thankfully, if people are still on the waiting list for a 2TB model, Valve is allowing them to choose a 512GB model instead while supplies last, while opting out of the queue for the 2TB version.

That may be the best alternative when stock levels are this uncertain, as Valve says, "the number of customers on the waitlist exceeds our current production; even when we are able to build more it will be at smaller quantities than we planned."
