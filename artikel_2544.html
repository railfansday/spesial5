Maikel Garcia trade grades: Mariners land Gold Glove infielder; Royals get pitching help, but with huge risks

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

The Kansas City Royals and Seattle Mariners have struck the offseason's first blockbuster trade. The Mariners have acquired third baseman Maikel Garcia in a six-player trade, the teams announced on Wednesday. The Royals receive righties Logan Evans and Bryce Miller and lefty Jose A. Ferrer in the deal.

Garcia, 26, battled hand and hamstring injuries in 2026, but still managed a 1.8 WAR season in 88 games thanks to league-average offense and spectacular third base defense. He is a year removed from a 5.6 WAR season and receiving MVP votes. Garcia is signed long-term as well. He's owed a very reasonable $52.5 million through 2030, with a club option for 2031.

The 28-year-old Miller headlines the return for Kansas City. An oblique strain delayed the start to his 2026 season, but once healthy, he threw 118 ⅔ innings with a 4.17 ERA and under-the-hood numbers that were hit (3.70 expected ERA) or miss (4.41 FIP). Miller threw 180 ⅓ innings with a 2.94 ERA in 2024, and will remain under team control through 2029.

Ferrer, 26, is a workhorse ground ball reliever with three years of control remaining. McArthur (fracture) and Evans (Tommy John) both missed 2026 with elbow surgery. McArthur pitched in four minor-league rehab games in September and should be ready for spring training. Evans has surgery in January and is expected back at midseason.

Here now are our grades for this three-for-three swap between 2027 American League postseason hopefuls.

The Mariners came into the offseason with an opening at third base, a glaring need to improve their defense, and more starters than rotation spots. That all came to a head in this trade. They used Miller, their least effective starter the last two seasons, and two spare parts to net a long-term solution at the hot corner in Garcia, who plays Gold Glove-caliber defense.

One player alone won't fix Seattle's defense, of course, but Garcia is a very big step toward improving a unit that ranked 26th in defensive runs saved and 30th (by a lot) in outs above average. As for the lineup, new manager Jason Kanzler can run out something like this most days:

Donovan was limited to 39 games by two groin strains and a concussion this past season. He's very versatile and needn't be locked into DH. The Mariners can play Donovan in the outfield to open up DH at-bats for slugger Lazaro Montes, who hit some titanic home runs during his MLB debut in 2026. They could also move Donovan around to give others DH days.

The lineup and the defense are improved, and the Mariners still have a formidable rotation that includes Kade Anderson, Logan Gilbert, Emerson Hancock, George Kirby, and Bryan Woo. Right-hander Ryan Sloan, arguably the top pitching prospect in baseball (it's either Sloan or Anderson), is knocking on the door and figures to debut at some point in 2027.

Seattle will miss Ferrer in their bullpen but also have close to an entire offseason (minus a lockout) to replace him. Assuming the Mariners won't break the bank for Adrian Morejon, the free agent market still offers solid second- and third-tier lefty reliever options in A.J. Minter, Sean Newcomb, JoJo Romero, Gregory Soto, and Alex Vesia. Ferrer is a quality reliever but not irreplaceable.

Before his elbow fracture, which ultimately required three surgeries, McArthur showed a very lively curveball and strong ground ball ability. He's a middle reliever more than a high-leverage option, but is not merely a throw-in. Langevin struck out 106 batters in 55 ⅓ Single-A relief innings thanks to his mid-90s fastball in 2026. He also walked 39 batters, however.

Garcia received MVP votes a year ago thanks to a .286/.351/.449 line with 16 home runs and 23 stolen bases. He also runs some of the game's best contact rates. That's his upside and maybe 2025 was a career year, but Garcia has a reasonably high floor thanks to his defense. Getting even league-average offense moving forward makes this a win for Seattle.

The free agent market is flush with No. 3-4 type starters and light on position players, specifically non-first base infielders. Couldn't the Royals keep Garcia and just sign a pitcher or two? There is something to be said for Miller's cost control as an arbitration-eligible player, of course, but he hasn't been healthy and effective at the same time since 2024. That's awfully risky.

Evans is a quality second piece as long as he bounces back well from Tommy John surgery. He made his MLB debut with the Mariners in 2025 and underwhelmed (4.32 ERA with iffy underlying numbers in 81 ⅓ innings), though he was regarded as a strong prospect before that, someone who should hold down a rotation spot for a long time.

Here's what FanGraphs wrote about Evans in June 2025, the last time he was prospect-eligible:

(His) sweeper is easily his best pitch, spins in at 2,800 rpm, and has hellacious lateral wipe. His reliance on it robs it of some bat-missing ability, and the last couple of years, the miss rate on Evans' slider has been pretty average because he's so often trying to throw it for strikes and hitters anticipate its arrival. But because Evans' fastball is fairly vulnerable, he's forced to lean on his breaking stuff ... Again, it's really only the sweeper that's plus, but Evans has a super deep repertoire, he throws a good rate of strikes and gets groundballs, and in 2024, he proved he could sustain this kind of stuff across more than 100 innings. Without a single pitch generating plus miss, he's probably not going to be a star, but he does enough to be a useful member of a big league rotation in a no. 4/5 capacity.

The Royals will have to wait for Evans to make an impact in their uniform because the timing of his Tommy John surgery means his rehab will extend into next spring and summer, but he is a quality second piece even if he is only a No. 4-5 starter. Cheap back-end starters are incredibly valuable and Evans should be that once his elbow is ready to go.

Ferrer could very well wind up closing games for the Royals this season and is a quality add to the bullpen. For Kansas City, the trade boils down to three things. One, can they keep Miller healthy and get him back to his 2024 form? He's the headliner in the trade and his success is imperative. Two, will Evans turn into a reliable rotation member once his elbow is sound?

And three, was Nick Loftin's 2026 for real? The Royals believe it was, hence trading Garcia. Loftin, 28, was a Gold Glove finalist this past season and he authored a .240/.322/.388 batting line with eight home runs in 118 games. If you're sold on Loftin as a league-average hitter and Gold Glove-caliber defender, well, that's what Garcia is, just without the name value.

The Royals badly needed rotation help -- there's no arguing that -- but they also ranked 22nd in runs this year and were middle of the pack defensively despite Loftin's (and Garcia's) glovework. There's a chance Kansas City made its position player core worse with only unspectacular pitching upgrades to show for it. I understand what they did. I just don't love it.
