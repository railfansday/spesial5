Even if the Nikon D850 Is Discontinued, It Won’t Be Hard To Buy

The Nikon D850 may be on its last legs, but that doesn’t mean photographers can no longer get their hands on one.

Nikon Rumors reports that Nikon’s Sales Support team in the United Kingdom communicated that the Nikon D850 is “now end of life,” which effectively means it is discontinued.

“We would like to inform you that the D850 model is now end of life. Unfortunately, we are not expecting any further stock, and as a result, we are not accepting new orders. Please update your systems accordingly. We apologize for any inconvenience. If you have any questions, please let us know,” Nikon’s UK Sales Team said in an email.

This aligns with the UK retail situation, where stock varies by retailer. For example, Wex, a major UK photo retailer, no longer lists the D850. However, as Digicame-Info reports, the D850 is still available in Japan, and it remains easy to find in the United States as well.

There are a couple of caveats here. First, as PetaPixel has seen plenty of times, cameras and lenses are often discontinued in one region before another. Second, nothing may have actually changed regarding Nikon’s manufacturing of the D850. The company could have halted production a while ago, and local stock has only just run dry in the UK, prompting the new notice.

PetaPixel has reached out to Nikon for clarification and will update this story when more information becomes available.

In the meantime, photographers can still acquire the Nikon D850. Even if new stock runs out, this prolific DSLR is widely available on the used market.

As for brand-new units, they remain readily available in the United States. B&H still carries the Nikon D850, for instance, and has it discounted by $300 through November 29 for the holidays. Nikon USA also still sells the D850 directly.

The Nikon D850 remains a fantastic camera that photographers still love, and it will stay easy to buy for the foreseeable future. The only real question is how much longer it will be available brand-new in the box—a status that will eventually end everywhere, not just in the UK.
