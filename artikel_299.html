Apple adds new disk controls to prevent AI agents from running amok

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Mac users will be warned of the risks of granting AI full disk access.

After sounding an alarm about the issue last month, Apple is taking steps to limit Mac disk access to AI agents, the company announced on its developer blog. In its latest macOS beta, Apple introduced controls to ensure that users will need to take "very explicit" actions to grant apps like ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini "this extraordinary level of [full disk] access."

The issue rose to prominence in September, when Inc. columnist Jason Aten noticed that Meta's Muse Mac app was able to access his messages even though he thought he had denied that permission. Meta argued that Aten must have granted access, saying the feature is entirely opt-in, but the incident caught Apple's eye. "Some developers are using Full Disk Access in ways that could put users at risk, exposing everything on their systems — including files, mail, messages, and even browsing history — without users' full knowledge and understanding," Apple wrote.

Apple vowed to address the issue with new disk controls. As screen grabs from X user @madeby_ldt show, Apple's Full Disk Access setting now requires explicit permissions to be granted from a scary-looking pop-up screen. It states, "Your Mac will not be able to protect your data from [your desired AI app]," and forces you to review and OK the actions it will be able to take. Those include "Read, Change, Delete and Export all your Data," "View Hidden and Recently Deleted Photos," and "View History and Browser as You."

The new controls won't stop users from granting AI agents access to your files, but it should give them pause. If one of those AI agents goes rogue — which is something we've seen before — they could potentially leak or misuse your private data. Stopping this is key for Mac models like the Studio that Apple is specifically marketing for local AI models and agents.
