This week we launched a series celebrating the top architects and designers of the next 20 years

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

This week on Dezeen, we kicked off our 20×20 project, in partnership with Kohler, to celebrate 20 years of Dezeen.

Over the next month, we will reveal the architects and designers who will have the biggest impacts over the next 20 years, as selected by some of the biggest names in architecture and design from the past 20 years.

We started the series with an interview with Harry Nuriev, who was selected by Bjarke Ingels. We then interviewed Lanza Atelier, Freya Tangelder and Swedish Girls, who were selected by Frida Escobedo, Nendo and Sabine Marcelis, respectively.

In a big week for Dezeen, we also announced the winners of the 2026 Dezeen Awards winners at a ceremony in London.

We we revealed the architecture, design and interior project winners, including the Silent River Tree Sauna by Daichi (pictured), along with the six designers of the year.

In architecture news, recently revealed images of the White House ballroom drew attention to the huge scale of the project, which many said “dwarfs” the central residence.

“It says everything about Donald Trump that his gilded ballroom dwarfs the size of the People’s House—and it says everything about the American people that they repudiated him loudly and clearly last Tuesday,” wrote Hilary Clinton on X.

A group of Harvard GSD students also aimed to draw attention to another Donald Trump-backed scheme in Washington DC, by proposing alternative designs to his planned arch.

The two proposals for the planned site of the arch replaced the proposed structure with an expansive pool of water.

Also in the US, plans by SHoP Architects for a supertall skyscraper in Miami, which is backed by Serbian tennis player Novak Djokovic, were revealed.

The 320-metre-tall skyscraper will have a ribbed design and include amenity facilities developed by the tennis player.

In less serious news, American fast-food chain Chipotle launched a range of hoodies all with a hidden, insulated pocket for sneaking burritos into cinemas.

The tongue-in-cheek clothing was designed to keep concealed burritos warm to allow for illicit snacking at the movies.

Popular projects on Dezeen this week include a “surreal” house in rural China, a pink commercial building in Mexico and a Cotswolds barn renovation.
