Couples who married in 2025 spent an average of $2,800 on wedding flowers, according to The Knot’s Real Weddings Study. Much of that cost comes from choices you control, starting with which stems fill each arrangement and how many arrangements you order.

A stem of flowers almost always costs more than a stem of greenery, so an arrangement filled out with greenery such as eucalyptus costs less than one packed with blooms. Flowers in season near you can trim the bill too. And every arrangement you skip saves its stems, its container, and the time to build it.

Say a florist quotes $3,200 for a design built on imported peonies, with two arrangements for the ceremony. If local garden roses and eucalyptus take $800 off, and moving those two to the reception saves $500 in reception arrangements, the quote drops to $1,900.

To get there, give your florist a color palette instead of a list of must-have blooms, and ask what local growers will have in season the month of your wedding.

Then use each arrangement twice. Ask a friend to carry the ceremony arrangements to the reception during cocktail hour, and set the bridesmaids’ bouquets in vases of water on the head table.

Leave the bouquets and boutonnieres to the florist, and make simple centerpieces yourself from bulk flowers and greenery, which Costco and Sam’s Club sell online. On tables that don’t need flowers, set out candles or potted plants. Doing all the flowers yourself means taking on the florist’s prep, from stripping thorns to giving each stem a fresh cut. Try it only with helpers and refrigerated space lined up for the day before the wedding.

Before you sign with a florist, ask for a second quote with in-season flowers and the ceremony arrangements reused, then compare the two line by line.
