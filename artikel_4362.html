Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered Review: Absolute Kaiju Chaos

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered delivers chaotic kaiju battles and couch co-op fun, even if its story and side features are thin.

72 years ago, the world was first introduced to the King of the Monsters, Godzilla, in the revolutionary 1954 kaiju film simply titled Godzilla, and since then the reptilian monster has gone on to appear in dozens of movies, shows, comics, and even video games.

While most of Godzilla’s video game appearances debuted in the 1980s and 1990s, the radioactive monster has made a few notable gaming manifestations in the early 2000s and 2010s, with a fan-favorite being 2002’s Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee.

Godzilla: DAMM turned out to be such a popular sixth generation console title that it not only spawned two sequels but a complete Unreal Engine 5 remaster for modern platforms called Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered.

To give a little bit of a background on myself with this title, I have never actually played the original Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee before nor any Godzilla game for that matter. I didn’t become a fan of or really understand Godzilla’s cultural importance until high school, around the time when Legendary Pictures’ Godzilla film was coming out coinciding with the franchise’s 60th anniversary.

The success of that film and its later Monsterverse sequels opened the door for me and a whole new generation of fans to become invested in Godzilla and his related kaiju, and seemingly to help capitalize on this new fan base, Atari and Pipeworks have decided to remaster DAMM for modern platforms.

Unlike other remasters, Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered isn’t a simple HD port of the original 2002 game but is rather a sort of remake of the original, with Pipeworks having rebuilt the game in Unreal Engine 5 complete with enhanced visuals and modern quality of life improvements.

Pipeworks strived to keep Remastered’s core premise and gameplay as close to the original 2002 game as possible, with the remaster still featuring up to four-player local co-op, Adventure Mode, Destruction Mode, Survival Mode, and Practice Mode.

For the first time ever, Remastered allows DAMM players to fight and play together across great distances thanks to the introduction of online multiplayer.

The game even features updated cutscenes primarily seen in Adventure Mode, with the cutscenes featuring the alien Vortaak being fairly reminiscent of the updated cutscenes in the 2020 Destroy All Humans! remake, especially with how both utilized 1950s-inspired sci-fi movie music.

The premise of Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered is the alien Vortaak have come to Earth and mind-controlled most of the planet’s kaiju to wreck the planet and prepare it for a complete Vortaak invasion. One monster is somehow able to break free of the Vortaak’s control and sets out to defeat all of the other monsters and ultimately the Vortaak themselves.

All of these monsters previously appeared in the original Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee save for the first version of Mechagodzilla who replaced Mechagodzilla 2 in the remaster.

1990s Godzilla, Anguirus, Megalon, and Gigan are all available to play as from the get-go while every other monster has to be unlocked by progressing through G-Force Research Rumors and Projects (not to be confused with the 2009 guinea pig spy movie G-Force).

At the game’s core, Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered is primarily centered around sandbox fights between two, three, or four monsters, with each monster featuring their own unique moveset that they can use to fight other monsters, destroy cities, and wipe out military units.

These fighting games' story modes allow people to play as villains instead of traditional heroes, often with destructive results.

For example, Megalon can use his drill-like front appendages to not only strike at enemies and buildings but also to burrow underground to pop back up at enemies and unleash a devastating blow sending monsters hurling through the air.

In this regard, Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered is a ton of fun to play and unlock each monster, with learning each monster’s moveset to use in local or online multiplayer being particularly fun.

However, aside from unlocking every monster and playing through Adventure Mode with each monster, there’s not much incentive to keep on playing the game for long. Sure, players can unlock G-Force sighting video clips, monster photos but there’s no system to customize the monsters and most maps play far too similar to each other.

I can barely tell the difference between most cities aside from a few notable landmarks like Seattle’s space needle. That's okay though as the remaster is clearly not trying to reinvent the wheel or even include features from DAMM’s sequels since this is a remaster first and foremost, not a proper reimagining or a true sequel.

While I didn’t play Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee growing up, I can immediately feel the sixth console generation charm of the game from its remasters right from the start. It’s a fighting game that people can instantly have a ton of fun playing through as they wreak havoc throughout some of the planet's most famous cities and fight against some of cinema’s most famous monsters in single-player or comedic local or online matches.

With how uncommon it is to find brand-new local co-op games these days, Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered feels like a breath of fresh air and it’s a game that young Godzilla fans will certainly want to pick up before the year’s end. Plus, with how affordable the remaster is both in terms of its price and its remarkably small file size, DAMMR is a game that any fan of Godzilla or fun couch co-op games in general should pick up soon as it will likely make for fantastic holiday gift. DAMMR might not have all that of an engaging story or even a ton of side features but it more than makes up for those flaws by being a simple fun game to create kaiju chaos solo or with friends.
