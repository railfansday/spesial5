Dylan Larkin says he's 'proud of time' with Red Wings, happy to be in Montreal

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The last time Dylan Larkin skated in Montreal, he was greeted by chants of "We want Lar-kin" by fans at Bell Centre.

He was back skating on that ice on Monday, Nov. 23, the day after a blockbuster trade in which the Detroit Red Wings sent Larkin, a second-round pick in the 2028 NHL draft, and a third-round pick in the 2027 draft to the Canadiens for forward prospect Michael Hage, NHL forward Kirby Dach, NHL goalie Sam Montembeault, a conditional first-round pick in the 2027 draft, a conditional first-round pick in the 2029 draft, a second-round pick in the 2027 draft, and a fourth-round pick in the 2028 NHL Draft.

Larkin is scheduled to suit up for the Canadiens in their evening game against the Los Angeles Kings.

The Canadiens were not on Larkin's first three-team list, nor his expanded 10-team list, but they won out in the end − and here's Larkin's reason.

"I'm honored to be here," he told reporters in Montreal while wearing a white Canadiens hoodie with the No. 89 on it. "Thanks to the Montreal Canadiens for believing in me. I'm so excited and honored to be here. The process wasn't easy but the outcome, being here today − my answer is this team, this unbelievable group that's building towards something special, and being able to work with Marty St. Louis. I have a lot of respect for him as a player and what he's doing as a coach, what he's building is pretty cool.

The trade ended Larkin's career with the Wings, the team that drafted the Waterford native at No. 15 in 2014 and of which he was captain from January 2021 through last season. He requested a trade shortly after last season ended, but his restrictive list limited the Wings' options − until now.

Larkin has declined to explain his reasons for the trade request.

"I think there's a lot there, but I'm so happy to be here," he said. "I think there will be a time where I get into that. I'm a kid from Michigan. I grew up there, grew up playing hockey there. I think tonight will probably be my first home game outside of Michigan. It was a great story and I'm so proud of that. And I'm proud of my time in Detroit. I can't thank the organization enough. Maybe didn't end the way anyone thought it would, but I'm here now, and I'm excited to be a Montreal Canadian and, you know, be a part of what's going on here."
