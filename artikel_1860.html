Newcastle and Liverpool serve up thrilling tribute to Kevin Keegan

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

It had to end that way, didn’t it? A last-minute goal, a sense of what might have been and a helter-skelter contest that will take days to decipher.

Newcastle versus Liverpool became the Premier League’s most dramatic fixture when Kevin Keegan was manager here and, on the afternoon they flocked to the “cathedral on the hill” to pay tribute to the late, great superstar, we were treated to a contest that was in keeping with his narrative.

Liverpool operating with the discipline of a basketball team undoubtedly helped, and we can examine those gaping chasms soon enough, but the poignancy of seeing Matthias Jaissle looking as bereft as Keegan at Anfield 30 years ago at the final whistle was impossible to ignore.

Really, the German’s first match in charge of Newcastle should have ended with three points. His team are a work in progress – rich in youthful exuberance though prone to lapses – but how they fought, ran and worked to give the locals the result they wanted.

They were almost at the finish line, seconds away from beating Liverpool here for the first time since December 2015, but then Lewis Hall ran into Victor Muñoz, sending the Spanish substitute sprawling, and Stuart Attwell pointed to the penalty spot. Dominik Szoboszlai inflicted maximum damage.

Dominik Szoboszlai equalises from the spot in the 99th minute! 🤯 pic.twitter.com/0Ag9WitqHt

“I have clear thoughts,” Jaissle said quietly, as he was asked to reflect on the day-wrecking decision. “I guess you call it a soft penalty here. If you give it, you need to give a lot during this match and a lot during the season. I don’t know if it that is good for football.”

Here was the third time in four seasons Liverpool had scored in injury time in front of the Leazes Stand, after Darwin Núñez (2023) and Rio Ngumoha (2025), and their eighth goal beyond the 90th minute in collisions with Newcastle is a Premier League record against the same opponents.

No wonder he was stunned. Jaissle had spent idle moments in the build-up thinking about what might happen if Newcastle scored an early goal and, much to his delight, fantasy became reality in the most thrilling fashion. On this emotional afternoon, it was fitting the hosts scored first.

One of Keegan’s great early wins as Newcastle manager was in November 1993, when his team blew away Liverpool in the snow and Andy Cole scored a first-half hat-trick. His first goal that day came after five minutes and, by happy coincidence, the clock stopped at that moment for Anthony Elanga, too.

How Liverpool went from having a chance of their own to conceding 20 seconds later is something Andoni Iraola will have to address quickly, but that is what happened: Ryan Gravenberch should have shot, instead he passed and Amar Dedic intervened, his interception finding Will Osula.

Off Osula galloped, Liverpool’s defenders looking leaden-footed as they gave pursuit, and he ushered Elanga into open space. Once he was in the clear, he was not for catching and his shot was perfectly placed beyond Alisson Becker.

An electric start! Anthony Elanga gives Newcastle the lead inside 5 minutes! ⚡️ pic.twitter.com/8DYYFJ9i6F

As Elanga was mobbed, the recriminations started. Fingers pointed at each other, Iraola spun around in dismay. Liverpool were easy to play against last season – Iraola’s Bournemouth had scored two identical goals against them on the opening day 12 months ago – and old habits die hard.

“We should finish the play,” Iraola conceded. “We created a great chance. We are on the edge of the box and have a clear opening to shoot and we decide not to. We don’t finish it properly and then you have to give credit to Elanga, who wins the race.”

What could Liverpool offer in response? The answer before the interval was nothing, other than a shot from Florian Wirtz that was well saved by Lukas Hornicek. The first half was wretched and it was difficult to see what had changed from the final throes of Arne Slot.

This, to be clear, is no criticism of Iraola, more a spotlight on players who need to step up. They did that momentarily in the second period, when Cody Gakpo equalised. He was afforded too much time by Newcastle’s defence but, still, his finish from 20 yards was super, zipping beyond Hornicek.

But parity was secured for less than two minutes before Newcastle scored a near-carbon-copy second goal, another counter-attack – this was one led by the outstanding Yoane Wissa – ending with substitute Joe Willock finding a corner of Alisson’s net. Again the inquests followed.

“We had the numbers to defend it better,” Iraola said. “It’s not like they caught us by surprise, but we didn’t defend it properly. We should compete for the first ball and probably make a foul, be a little more aggressive as we had the numbers to defend it.

“You don’t want to concede transitions and we’d been preparing them in the week for transitions.”

Why they didn’t follow the plan that had been set is something that Iraola must address, but he will be relieved that the days spent this week are not dissecting a defeat, as Wissa should have wrapped up the contest, only for Alisson to make a brilliant save to thwart the Congolese striker.

Those missed chances should not have mattered but in the final throes, Hall misjudged the angle Muñoz attacked him from, he got his challenge wrong and, after a pause, Attwell pointed to the spot. Given the agony Liverpool have inflicted, the outcome was as clear as black and white.

“We scored at the end, so you have a better taste,” Iraola said. “Before the game, we wouldn’t have signed for this result. It’s a reward. It’s only a point, but at least it’s something that’s deserved.”

Plenty would contest his conclusion. Liverpool got away with it – and Iraola is about to find the hard work is only just beginning.

Alexander Isak is going to be in for the sort of hostile afternoon unseen in English football for years.

Not since Michael Owen has a player left Newcastle and been so universally despised. Owen was accused of ducking out of a relegation battle to ensure he secured a lucrative free transfer move after an injury-ravaged spell in the North East.

If anything, Isak was even worse, He went on strike, refusing to play for the club again, despite having three years left on his contract, in order to force a move to Liverpool. It felt like the ultimate betrayal and crossed a line with Geordies. They will love you and worship players as good as Isak, but if you behave like the Sweden international did you become the ultimate villain.

Isak was a superb player in black and white stripes, arguably their best since the takeover by Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund in 2021. He was top goalscorer two years running and was the focal point of Eddie Hower’s attack as they qualified for the Champions League twice and won the Carabao Cup.

He could have been a Geordie legend, but he left in such an ugly and acrimonious fashion. Isak forced Newcastle to buckle from their not for sale stance, rejecting a pay rise and a new contract, insisting he would never play for the club again even if they refused to sell him, that he will be tarnished forever.

It is fitting that Newcastle United vs Liverpool is a Premier League opening weekend fixture following the death of Kevin Keegan at the age of 75 last month.

All corners of St James’ Park will pay tribute and show their appreciation for the former player and manager who means a great deal to both sets of supporters.

Fans will find a printed message from the Keegan family on the back of black and white cards on their seat, which they will hold up to display a mural before kick-off.

As well as playing for both clubs, Keegan was Newcastle’s manager when they were beaten by a last-gasp Stan Collymore goal at Anfield in April 1996, a 4-3 defeat remembered as one of the great Premier League games.

This fixture usually delivers entertainment, and with new managers Matthias Jaissle and Andoni Iraola promising front-footed, ‘vertical’ football, there should be goals. That was certainly the case last August, when Newcastle fought back from two down with 10 men, before Rio Ngumoha scored a memorable winner.

It has been a summer of tumult for Newcastle, losing manager Eddie Howe, as well as key players Anthony Gordon, Sandro Tonali and captain Bruno Guimaraes. There has been bullish talk of “Newcastle 2.0” from David Hopkinson, the club’s chief executive, and the club believe they have found a promising coach, but fans have understandably revised expectations downwards.

Liverpool are a difficult team to read; champions only 18 months ago but brittle last season, which resulted in Arne Slot’s dismissal. Iraola’s appointment is a shift back towards Jurgen Klopp’s style. That will be music to the ears of Anfield regulars, but holes remain in the squad. Central midfield has not been replenished; they need a Mohamed Salah replacement, and their strength at full-back is questionable.
