Celtic are facing an uphill battle to sign Chelsea winger Kendry Paez on loan in January, sources have told Football Insider.

The Bhoys have shown an interest in the Ecuaddorian of late, but face competition from several European and English Championship sides.

However, with Jota now fully fit again after returning from injury, Martin O'Neill's side is well-stocked for wide players.

With that in mind, sources say that Paez will not be a priority target for Celtic in January, and they will only move for him if they pick up more injuries.

However, such is the interest in the 19-year-old at the moment, that the option of a loan may not remain on the table for much longer.

Paez has been playing U21 football in PL 2 so far this term, and has notched one assist from five games so far.

Celtic already have two players on loan from Chelsea in Shumaira Mheuka and Landon Emenalo, but they are unlikely to add a third in Paez.

As things stand, O'Neill is happy with the depth his squad has in the wide areas, with Yang Hyun-jun impressing of late as well.

Camilo Duran has often ben used on the right flank, and Jota's return to action has limited the minutes for both Sebastian Tounekti and Haissem Hassan in recent weeks.

The addition of another winger is not something that is on Celtic's radar as a result, although that could yet change if there is another injury.

Chelsea are currently assessing theur options for Paez this winter, and are prepared to loan him out to the most suitable club.

Former Aberdeen chief executive Keith Wyness recently told Football Insider that Celtic have made another PR blunder by exploiting their "glorious past" when releasing another range of watches.

The Bhoys recently annnounced another round of their Lisbon Lions watches, with only 1,967 up for sale, and selling at £299 each.

Wyness pointed out that the Glasgow giants will only make around "£100,000" profit from the scheme, which he feels it won't be worth it given the trouble it could cause.

Celtic are locked in a constant battle with their fans at the moment, and Wyness believes the supporters won't like to see their history exploited for monetary gain.
