Hawks’ second unit stars in comeback victory over Pistons

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Atlanta Hawks rallied in the fourth quarter to dispatch the Detroit Pistons 107-97 at State Farm Arena on Sunday afternoon to win their fourth consecutive game. Nickeil Alexander-Walker led the Hawks with 27 points while Corey Kispert added 18 points off the bench. For the Pistons, Isaiah Joe scored 19 points while former Hawk John Collins added 18 points.

Heading into the game, it was unclear whether Pistons star Cade Cunningham would suit up, and while he would start this game it didn't take long for the Hawks to draw him into foul trouble as the Hawks started this game on the front foot behind Jalen Johnson's eight first quarter points as the hosts got themselves out on the run often in the first half, scoring 18 of their 23 fastbreak points in the first half.

The Hawks led for the majority of the first half behind strong bench play (with Dyson Daniels leading the bench unit), with Kispert in particular looking lively with 10 points. After the Hawks took a double-digit lead in the second quarter, the Pistons went on a 16-4 run to take their first lead of the game.

While a quiet first half from Alexander-Walker (eight points) quickly burst into life in the third quarter, the Pistons tightened the screw defensively, and behind Collins and Ausar Thompson (combining for 18 points in the third) began applying pressure on the Hawks offensively — Collins from deep, Thompson on his aggressive drives.

With the Pistons finding their groove, they established a double-digit lead of their own, and an 11-point lead heading into the fourth quarter. It seemed unlikely at the time that the Hawks were going to be able to pull a result here, with the Pistons defensively clicking, and with Cunningham emerging into the game as he struggled with foul trouble and injury. …And that's when it happened.

Behind a lineup of Daniels, Aaron Wiggins, Kispert, Lu Dort, and Zuby Ejiofor, the Hawks began the fourth quarter and contributed to an 18-0 run — holding the Pistons scoreless in the opening seven minutes of the fourth quarter. The Pistons finally scored their first points of the fourth quarter with 4:52 remaining, off of which Hawks head coach Quin Snyder called an immediate timeout and re-inserted Alexander-Walker, Onyeka Okongwu, and Johnson. Alexander-Walker took over, with Dort also continuing his strong fourth quarter, and the Hawks would re-take a double-digit lead after a dagger three from Alexander-Walker put the Hawks up by 12 points with just over a minute remaining. While the Hawks held the Pistons to single-digits for much of the quarter, in the end, the Hawks outscored Detroit 33-12 in the final frame, overturning a double-digit deficit heading into the fourth quarter after a strong third quarter from the visitors.

"Rather than try to compare and contrast, I think you just look at that group of five guys that were out there just competing," began Snyder when asked about the difference between the third and fourth quarter. "Corey, Wiggs, and Lu, Zuby, obviously — I'd be remiss if I left out Dyson — but that five-man line-up, just the level of tenacity, defensively was unique. You could feel it in the way they competed on the offensive boards. They took threes and they crashed, and tried to rebound and defend, and that's what we were trying to accomplish, trying to do, and they actually went out there and did it."

How did the Hawks' bench achieve what Snyder and the Hawks wanted them to accomplish?

"Focus," said Snyder on how the second unit remained connected. "Commitment to that, to each other. I think it begins with our spacing coming down the floor. If we don't have something, passing the ball. Where we're attacking the defense, not on the front side where they're shifted, not holding the ball where the defense can shift again, and being willing to create shots for each other, and taking them, and crashing (the offensive glass). It's kind of formulaic. Those guys, when you're coming into the game, you have that focus. Particularly when your team's behind, there's an opportunity there. Those guys have done that and seized it."

Whether the Hawks have a lead or are trailing, the bench's approach is the same, according to Corey Kispert, who led the bench scoring with 18 points.

"That's just kind of the mindset that we have going into each game," said Kispert of the bench connection. "Either we turn the game around or we carry what the starters have already started. Either way, that doesn't affect what we do. We try to come in and change the game and then hand it back. Nights like tonight really help us carry that on, carry that forward. We're a super connected group, especially on that bench."

While the margin of bench scoring was narrowly in the Hawks' favor (47-43), their production was still a significant contributor to winning this game and I think their plus/minus tells a more accurate and fitting story as to their contribution to this game. The lowest plus/minus from a bench player was Aaron Wiggins' plus-18 (!!) — that's the lowest from the bench unit, and considering he shot 4-of-11 from the field and 1-of-8 from three, that's probably fair.

In the absence of Jock Landale, Zuby Ejiofor continues to see rotation minutes and, again, was impactful with his energy. He got an opportunity to post a few more shots, shooting 2-of-9 from the field and missing four three-pointers, but as a screener and as a threat on the offensive/defensive glass he was certainly effective, once again. As briefly highlighted, Lu Dort was excellent, particularly in the fourth quarter where he scored 10 of his 16 points, playing the entirety of the fourth quarter. Offensively, Dort was crashing the offensive glass, buying extra possessions, and defensively was a large factor on Cade Cunningham's difficult evening shooting the ball. Dort was fantastic in the initial comeback to erase the Pistons' lead and chipped in along with Alexander-Walker to finish the job.

Finally, there's the play of Corey Kispert. Kispert has been a DNP-CD when the Hawks were fully healthy, but with an injury to CJ McCollum, Kispert has seen an increase in opportunities and he is taking them — a season-high 18 points on 7-of-13 from the field and 4-of-10 from three.

Snyder was full of praise for not just Kispert's level of play but also his mental fortitude and the energy he brings off the bench.

"It's hard to overstate it, and it's something I've had direct conversations about," said Snyder about Kispert. "He's played well, even up to the point where he's got to get on the floor to have more minutes. You want to play everybody, and the game doesn't always present itself that way, but we are — and I've said this before too — a work in progress. The thing that's most impressive about what Corey's done is obviously his play, but his mental toughness and his commitment to the team has been unwavering through all that. Keep throwing yourself in the team, and that isn't something I even need to say, but in my mind, the way that he's been able to lose himself in the group is pretty unique. It's really unique, and that's one of the reasons that I think he's been so ready to impact and to play. His minutes certainly have been impactful, but I think his energy, as much as anything, you can feed off that. He's doing that in every situation. You guys have covered enough basketball. It doesn't happen. It's pretty unique, he's a pretty unique player."

What's been most impressive about Kispert is not just his ability to shoot the ball (48% from three so far this season) but his ability to open up his game by putting the ball on the floor and driving to the rim.

"That is the skill," said Kispert on his shooting and driving. "That's what has made me an NBA player. You don't either shoot it or drive it, and obviously shooting it is my main thing, that's my main skill, that's my NBA-level skill, but it's really easy to guard somebody that can't drive as well. So, being able to read whether to drive it, whether to shoot it, or pass it and move it, that's the skill that I've gotten to get really good at over the course of my career to stick around. When you can do both and finish the rim and also shoot threes at a really high level, you become really, really hard to guard."

Kispert was especially a threat when the Hawks grabbed offensive rebounds; 24 of them to be exact, as the Pistons are still without Jalen Duren and forced to play smaller. When the Hawks snatched second chances, Kispert was never far away.

"There's points when we've just been so focused collectively and sometimes that focus drifts," said Snyder on the defensive effort. "Over the course of a 48-minute game, rarely do you get it for 48 — especially when you're playing a team as good as Detroit — where you have it and they score. Good defense, better offense. I think if that connectivity that we have on defense, being disciplined, crushing the ball, but then also in help situations. One of the key things for us is to finish possessions, to rebound. You don't want those efforts on certain possessions to go wasted. We've talked about that being a big part of our identity. The challenge for us is when we don't get to play defense, meaning if we take a tough early shot after not making a pass, or we turn the ball over early, it's really hard to defend in those situations. You're not going to be perfect on offense, but that's something for us to keep focusing on so we get a chance to play defense."

"The way that you get threes against a really good defensive team like Detroit is offensive rebounds," said Kispert. "It's really hard for them to track the ball, track it off the rim, and then go find me, Aaron, whoever to go guard. That's where you need to get your most open looks. I don't know, it's something about the ball coming to you from the basket, it makes it easier to shoot if it's like practice versus having to catch it coming sideways off of a pick and roll, or off the dribble, or anything like that. It feels more like practice when you get it off of an offensive rebound and shoot. Statistically, those are the ones that go on the most. those are the highest value threes that we can get. You definitely want to hunt them and shoot them to get those chances."

The Hawks scored 27 second chance points, and having been on the other side where teams will grab offensive rebounds and score, the Hawks know exactly how deflating it can be and were acutely aware of that fact as they ran riot on the glass.

"It was the most important," said Kispert of the Hawks' rebounding edge. "Lu tore it up, Zuby tore it up, Dyson tore it up on the glass. Plays like that allows more chances to score and deflates the other team. We talk about that all of time as a team … when it's rebound after rebound after rebound and a big three, that flattens the other team."

The key, according to Snyder, is committing to the offensive glass even if you may not come up with the rebound as it may lead to a tip, or another avenue to keep the ball alive. This is something Dyson Daniels has had a particular nose for recently, grabbing 14 rebounds in this game, including seven offensive rebounds.

"I think we have to commit to going," said Snyder of the offensive rebounding. "Even if we don't have somebody that individually is—there's guys in this league that are getting four or five a game. I think we have to send numbers to the glass. Dyson's been pretty unusual, he's been able to do the rebound the ball. He can also keep the balls alive. I think that's part of it, we have to see it as a collective effort. Even if you don't get it, you may tip it. We may get it off the floor because someone has to account for somebody in the glass. The transition defense is much easier to play when you're offensive rebounding, particularly if there's less transition opportunities. Again, that's why it's so important for us to take our shots because we get a chance to do that as opposed to defending against something more difficult. It's just something that we know we have to do. Our guys committing to that the way they have is just really important to our efficiency on offense."

Daniels' fingerprints were all over last night's game. While he scored just five points on 2-of-5 shooting, he came up with 14 rebounds, seven offensive rebounds, eight assists, five steals, and two blocks in a game-high 38 minutes. Even with the return of Kingston Flemings to the lineup, Daniels is an instrumental element of the Hawks' playmaking, and this was even more so the case as, incredibly, Jalen Johnson registered zero assists in this game, struggling to make an impact beyond the first quarter.

"He was really the guy that kind of turned the pace for us towards the end of the third quarter," said Daniels of Flemings. "He was able to get downhill, got a few buckets, create some opportunities for us and change the momentum of the game … he did a really good job tonight."

The star of the second half was Alexander-Walker, who scored 27 points on 8-of-18 shooting from the field and 6-of-10 from three — 19 of those points and five of those threes coming in the second half alone. It's an odd one, because the boxscore would indicate that Alexander-Walker dominated proceedings here, but his impact on this game was understated, and I mean that positively. Yes, he had a that initial third quarter blitz, but he didn't jack up shot after shot as can happen in other games, allowing the game to come to him and taking over from the bench unit excellently in the fourth quarter to finish what they started.

Between the execution of the bench and the comeback nature of a game that felt as though it was slipping away in the second half, this is arguably the Hawks' highest quality victory of the season, and one that lifts them to second in the Eastern Conference.
