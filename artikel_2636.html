Panthers vs Ravens: Jaelan Phillips, Mike Jackson among storylines to watch

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Panthers managed to get out of New Orleans last week with a win, which snapped a 3 game losing skid and once again placed them atop the NFC South with a one and a half game lead. While their division is widely criticized for its inability to post a winning record or just win games in general, any team in the division could get hot and win a few games to take the lead. The Panthers have the Ravens, and the newly traded for QB Jacoby Brissett, on deck this week. And while its a winnable game, the Panthers have made many of their games difficult this season with penalties and mistakes. So this is by no means a gimme game, and there's a lot the Panthers have to both continue to progress in and make right for them to have any realistic possibility at both the playoffs themselves and any chance of actually making any waves short of a first round exit. Don't get me wrong, I know this Panthers team has had a ton of adversity injury wise and game script wise, but you can only handle those things as they come and to your best to overcome them. Let's dive in to some important storylines for this week.

The Panthers started the year with many pundits wondering if the combination of WRs Tetairoa McMillan and Jalen Coker being one of the best combos in the league. They have since not proven they are, and this is coming from someone who very much believes in them both. T Mac continues to play at the level you'd expect, but with6 targets and only 4 catches against the Broncos for 56 yards and a TD, and only 3 catches on 7 targets against the Saints last week, that's not quite good enough. For Jalen Coker, he caught 4 passes on 6 targets for 29 yards against the Broncos and didn't log a single catch on 4 targets against the Saints. These two were (for the most part) the catalyst behind the Panthers having one of the more productive passing attacks in the NFL to start the season, specifically on throws down the field. Coker's unfortunately missed a couple that could have been big, but the reality is close doesn't count in the NFL. And trust me when I say this, I know it isn't necessarily their fault. The offensive line was battered and bruised, Bryce Young hasn't been consistent ever since the losing skid had started, and Brad Idzik likely needs to make adjustments as the offensive coordinator now that defenses have more of a read on what the Panthers passing attack is trying to do. But winning games by basically feeding Chuba Hubbard every touch and the two tight ends in Tommy Tremble and Darren Waller around him is not going to be a sustainable formula. The Panthers employed the run-pass option at times this year, mostly with T Mac, to diversify their offense. Jalen Coker tends to be most effective when he's gotten a few catches early on in games. This might be the time to try and get these two DAWGS involved early. They also may have to lean on these guys since both Darren Waller and Tommy Tremble are dealing with knee injuries that kept them out of practice Wednesday.

The Panthers have basically simplified their defense since Todd Wash took over for Ejiro Evero, who is hanging out on Sean Payton's couch in Denver somewhere I'm sure. While making a whole defensive transition in the middle of a season will have its own growing pains, mainly in the form of penalties, I am seeing the vision with what Wash is trying to accomplish. Jaelan Phillips won NFC defensive player of the week, Derrick Brown was productive, Lee Hunter continues to excel in limited snaps, and the Panthers cornerbacks being more aggressive and in the face of the Saints offense paid huge dividends. They are going to face more polished offenses as the season goes on, but this formula seems to be working. And it starts with stopping the run. They held the Broncos to 75 yards rushing by their running backs, and 24 yards by the Saints as QB Tyler Shough led the team with 25 yards, more than their running backs combined. This week could be a major test because this Ravens team should lean on Derrick Henry out of the backfield. I say "should", because despite Brissett making his first start for the team, they threw the ball 49 times in a game where they were down by 10 points at worst throughout. Regardless, putting pressure on this offense with aggression in both aspects of football should help them win this game. Hopefully the offense can capitalize on more short yardage fields this time if they generate turnovers.

The Panthers are most likely getting CB Mike Jackson back this week from injury, after losing both Mike and Jaycee Horn a few weeks ago. The Panthers have mostly operated on a skeleton crew, especially after Will Lee III got injured himself. It looks like they'll get one of their top corners back this week, and he excelled at times last season when he needed to be the CB1 in the absence of Horn due to injury/suspension. They also signed Marco Wilson to the active roster, who showed out when needed in his last three games called upon as an active roster escalation from the practice squad. But in theory, if Mike Jackson can play and Will Lee doesn't suffer any setbacks, they'll have two of their top three CBs healthy for this week.

On the offensive side, Carolina is still waiting to fully clear OL Brady Christensen, but he would be a very important piece for an offensive line that is thin. He would start anywhere if they needed him to due to injury at this point. And Luke Fortner hasn't been good at center, so they could even opt to make a switch with Christensen playing center, he's done that in the past and been more than passible.

What are you looking for this week, Panthers fans?
