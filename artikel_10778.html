Valorant team disqualified for cheating after knocking out Masters champion Derke

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Riot Games has disqualified BONK from the VCT EMEA Kickoff Europe Qualifier after confirming one of its players used cheating software, just days after the team knocked out Derke’s Aurora Gaming.

BONK beat Aurora 2-1 in the Round of 32 of Qualifier 1 on November 6, before going on to reach the top four of the bracket.

In a statement on November 8, Riot said its anti-cheat team had been reviewing matches throughout the qualifiers, and that suspicious behavior involving BONK had been reported.

Competition Update: BONK, Kickoff Europe Qualifier #1 pic.twitter.com/gqnNRxOhiu

“Further investigation by our team confirmed that a participant on Team BONK was using unauthorised cheating software,” Riot wrote.

All BONK players and registered team members have been provisionally suspended from Riot-sanctioned competitions while the investigation into individual involvement continues, with final sanctions to be decided for each person.

Leo Faria, Riot’s global head of Valorant esports, didn’t hold back on X.

Imagine cheating in OQs and thinking we wouldn’t catch you. If you’re involved, don’t expect a slap on the wrist. https://t.co/s7nTEgOTm6

“Imagine cheating in OQs and thinking we wouldn’t catch you. If you’re involved, don’t expect a slap on the wrist,” he wrote.

Riot has decided to replay the two rounds that followed BONK’s win over Aurora, calling it “an exceptional measure” that “does not establish a precedent” for future cases.

Aurora will face Project 100K in the Round of 16 on November 8, with the winner taking on YCATABLE in the quarter-finals for a place in the Qualifier playoffs.

@BnkBonk were officially banned today.After our playoff qualifier last night, their cheating became so blatant that we, together with the guys from @Aurora_Valorant couldn’t ignore it. They’ve been cheating for 1.5-2 years 🤡 pic.twitter.com/K8424kVkLc

In a Discord message to YCATABLE, Riot’s player management team confirmed the disqualification came after the team filed a dispute, thanking them for “using the official channels to report this.”

The semi-final against Pixel Lumina and the final have been pushed back to November 10, so no team has to play three or more series in one day.

Not everyone was convinced, though. JTayy, assistant coach for G2 Gozen, G2’s Game Changers team, pointed out that BONK’s level hadn’t suddenly changed.

BONK have been playing together for a year, have played at LAN events, played in Challengers, and have been playing at a similar level the whole timeDid someone… just start Cheating? Have they been cheating that whole time? If so, why is it only now we find out? https://t.co/MippHGbQmQ

“BONK have been playing together for a year, have played at LAN events, played in Challengers, and have been playing at a similar level the whole time,” he wrote.

However, former Team Vitality player nataNk, who now plays for Pixel Lumina, claimed his team had suspected BONK a month earlier, after losing to them 2-1 in the semi-finals of EVC: The Surge 14 on October 11. BONK went on to win the tournament and its $1,500 prize.

A month ago we lost EVC against them 1-2. 1st map was so easy, they activated and won the Bo3 and the tournament (1.5k$)I reported them to GamerDoc 1month ago but didn’t take me seriously maybe, or didn’t see the message. https://t.co/CX0jCzNxKy pic.twitter.com/QJjfZYiVvi

“1st map was so easy, they activated and won the Bo3 and the tournament,” he wrote, suggesting BONK only turned their cheats on after the first map.

nataNk said he reported the team to GamerDoc, a principal anti-cheat analyst at Riot Vanguard, at the time, sharing a screenshot of his message. However, he added that “[he] didn’t take me seriously maybe, or didn’t see the message.”

Pixel Lumina were set to face BONK again in the semi-finals of this qualifier before the disqualification.
