Though they aren’t quite as easy to install as solar lanterns, most solar gutter lights are generally easy to put up, typically attaching via clips to a home’s gutters. Many lights also include additional mounting hardware so they can be used above doorways, along fence posts, and as deck lights, making them both solar security lights and architectural features on a home’s exterior.

Before shopping for the best solar lights, there are some points to consider. Below are some of the factors that go into choosing the best solar gutter lights to help you decide which type is best for your specific application.

Note: These kinds of lights run independently of solar roof lights, a solar power system, or solar generator, so they don’t require any hardwiring or complicated installation.

Similar to solar garden path lights, the best solar gutter lights come in a wide range of brightness levels. Your lighting needs will determine which product is best for you. Many small clip-on lights offer just enough accent lighting to add a tasteful glow to your property. These lights provide between 20 and 50 lumens each, which is enough to illuminate a garden bed or a bit of a walkway relatively well.

For more light, there are models available with outputs over 500 lumens. These lights are excellent for lighting a driveway, fence line, or other areas you’d prefer to keep brightly lit.

Since most solar gutter lights attach directly to a home’s gutters, the materials chosen are an important factor. A light made with bulky materials, such as heavy-duty metal, will eventually bend and deform a gutter, so heft is worth considering.

By and large, the best solar gutter lights are made of plastic. These lights are lightweight but durable enough to withstand the elements. However, the brackets used to clip them to the gutter are usually steel, which gives these units the strength they need to withstand wind and heavy rains. Some of the lights that attach directly to the home’s structure might be aluminum, which is a more corrosion-resistant material than steel, and it’s also lighter weight.

Another important consideration is the IP rating. An IP rating (or Ingress Protection rating) indicates how well a light can stand up to elements like dust and water. The first number indicates how dustproof a light is on a scale of 1 to 5. The second number is water resistance. A number between 1 and 6 indicates a product is water-resistant, and 7 or 8 means it’s waterproof. Most of the lights on this list are either a 5 or 6 for being dustproof, and although they are all water-resistant, none are waterproof. Because these lights are charged by the rays of the sun, keeping them dust-free and keeping your gutters clean will directly influence the effectiveness of these lights.

It’s also important to note that while many of the lights claim to work at least 8 hours after a day of charging, cold weather can affect how well they stay charged. Therefore, these lights may perform for shorter periods, closer to 6 hours, when used during the winter or in cooler climates.

Most of the best solar gutter lights will automatically turn on when their built-in photosensors recognize that it’s getting dark. The length of time they run for, or their operating time, will vary depending on battery size, light output, and how much sun each light receives during a given day. Most of the lights we tested required 6 to 8 hours of charging time to last an average of 8 hours in the night, with some able to run as long as 10 to 12 hours.

Solar gutter lights have onboard batteries that charge using energy from the sun, similar to those found on solar chargers. The light absorbs solar energy via a built-in photovoltaic cell. The cell then converts the energy into electricity and stores it in the battery. For some of the lights, the battery is a built-in component, but others take a rechargeable AA battery. This battery is most often included with the lights and may need to be replaced every 12 months to 3 years, depending on manufacturer’s recommendation and how much weather the lights have endured.

Battery power output can vary between 800 and up to 3,000 milliamp hours, and the larger the battery, the more energy it can produce. The panel’s size relative to the battery’s size determines how efficiently the light will charge. Too large a cell and the battery will fill too quickly, wasting sunlight. Too small and a battery may never reach its full potential. For this reason, most manufacturers try to size their photovoltaic cells to match their output ratings.

Choosing the best solar gutter lights for your home might come down to ease of installation. Many of these models simply attach to the lip of a home’s gutters, tightening down onto the edge. All that’s required to secure them is a ladder and a pair of pliers or a screwdriver, depending on the model.

Other models might require the user to attach them directly to the home’s structure with brackets and screws. While this could seem like an inconvenience, these models typically have the greatest light output and battery life, so there is a trade-off. Installing these lights typically requires a ladder, a power drill, and a screwdriver.

In general, most solar lights are function first, meaning they may not add to a home’s curb appeal. But, there are models available that aren’t as jarring to the eye. For the most part, gutters on homes are white, so most gutter lights are available in white plastic. Homes with gutters in other colors might look better with black solar gutter lights, which are readily available.

If you’re unable to find a light that matches well with your home’s aesthetic, you can still achieve attractive outdoor lighting by finding a model that attaches directly to the home’s structure. This allows them to be hidden under the eave somewhat, but it’s important that they get at least some sunlight to function as expected.

Some additional features help increase the convenience and functionality of the best solar gutter lights. For example, a motion sensor might activate when it senses someone or something walking underneath it, which is excellent for security.

Many models also come with automatic switches that activate the light once a sensor determines it’s getting dark. And should the battery still have enough charge by morning, the controller will turn the light off when the sun comes up. There are also models with remote controls, which allow you to operate the lights remotely and adjust their output without climbing a ladder.
