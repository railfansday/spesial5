Peter Storey: Arsenal double winner dies aged 81

Arsenal have announced the death of their former midfielder Peter Storey at the age of 81.

Having joined the Gunners as an apprentice in 1961, Storey made his first-team debut for the club in October 1965, and went on to stay at the club for a further 12 years.

A club statement on Tuesday read: "Everyone at Arsenal is deeply saddened by the passing of 1970-71 Double Winner, Peter Storey.

"All our thoughts are with his family and loved ones at this incredibly sad time. Rest in peace, Peter."

Arsenal also confirmed that the club will pay tribute to Storey during their Champions League fixture against Borussia Dortmund, which takes place on 24 November.

Storey initially started his Arsenal career at full-back before moving into midfield and winning the Fairs Cup in 1970.

During the 1970-71 campaign, Storey featured prominently in the Arsenal team that completed the double, playing an important role in their FA Cup triumph.

He scored a stoppage-time penalty against Stoke in the semi-finals to force a replay, before the Gunners went on to lift the trophy.

With 501 total appearances for Arsenal, Storey ranks as their eighth highest appearance maker of all time. Departing north London in 1977, he joined Fulham prior to retiring in 1978.

At international level, Storey received an England call-up in 1971 and was capped on 19 occasions.

Arsenal are due to play their first game after Storey’s death on Saturday when they face Newcastle at St James’ Park.

The Gunners headed into the international break with a five-point cushion at the top of the table after winning nine of their opening 10 Premier League matches.

Their nearest challengers are currently Brighton, who are also the only team to beat them this season.

Manchester City are a further point adrift after losing three matches in a row prior to the international break.
