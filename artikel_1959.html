The Celtic 'rollercoaster' Mika Baur is getting used to & how he spends his days off

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

It is easy to forget that Mika Baur only turned 22 over the summer. The German midfielder displayed maturity beyond his years in Celtic's captivating Europa League victory over Besiktas on Thursday evening, chasing lost causes, contributing defensively, and progressing play forward when the opportunity arrived.

It was his finest performance since joining the club for an initial fee of £4.5 million from Paderborn in August.

Baur compared his first few months in Glasgow to riding a rollercoaster, with downtime away from the football pitch proving rare.

"There’s no time to think about it! We are playing every three days and I really try to fight and bring my heart on the field all the time," Baur said. "Sometimes it works, sometimes it doesn’t, but I hope people understand as a young player it can be tough to be comfortable in a new country, a new culture and new club.

"But I really try my best. I have felt good in my first few months but it is a bit of a rollercoaster.

"Playing in this unbelievable stadium in front of these fans, getting the call-up to the national team was a big honour. You feel the pressure with some of the games we have lost but I am getting used to it."

This is Baur's first time living away from home. He credits his partner for making the transition to Scotland smoother, even if chances to play some golf are limited.

"I’m grateful to my girlfriend as she does most things and makes it comfortable at home," Baur continued. "The club also have people who have helped us a lot to settle.

"But you don’t get much time – you get home and lie on the couch!

"It’s not all stressful though. It’s also nice to go somewhere new and learn the city, get to coffee shops and restaurants.

"We’ve been to the beach, we’ve been to Edinburgh, we try to see a lot on our day off.

"I also play golf – so I’ve come to the home of golf! I’ve only managed to get to the driving range though. When you are playing every three days it’s a bit much to play nine or 18 holes.

"My father and brother play so hopefully the weather will be okay around Christmas to maybe manage a few holes."

Thursday's win over Besiktas wasn't quite a hole-in-one, but it marked a pivotal forward step in Celtic's Europa League campaign.

Finally up and running after consecutive defeats to Ferencvaros, Benfica and Celta Vigo, Baur gave credit to a raucous home support.

"The fans helped a lot," he explained. "You could feel the energy. It was not just us players on the field who deserved the win – it was also everyone around the stadium and they brought the energy as well.

"It’s an unbelievable feeling as a player to have everyone pushing you and being there for you. It gives you that last percentage of energy you need at the end.

"There is always pressure to win here, to win every three days. That’s what we all want. In the last two games it wasn’t possible for us. We knew it wasn’t good enough but here we played well and deserved it."

"The win was important after some tough weeks," Baur continued. "But the belief and the way we work together as a team has always been good.

"There was never any problem with that because we always believed in ourselves. We showed how we can leave our hearts on the field and put in a shift.

"It was important to get those three points and we deserved it. Hopefully we can go forward with confidence from this game."

Despite his stellar showing on Thursday, Baur missed out on Jurgen Klopp's latest Germany squad. He made his senior international debut against Serbia last month as the former Liverpool manager had named two separate squads for four fixtures.

Klopp's group was reduced this time around, though Baur will return to the under-21s.

"I can understand," Baur said of the snub. "There are so many unbelievable players in the squad, especially in my position.

"We’re talking about guys from Bayern Munich and Borussia Dortmund. I just do my best and hopefully they can see I am developing and improving.

"I’m still with the under-21s for the games against Belgium and Croatia, so that will be good. European matches like Thursday can only help and I have to hope they see.

"I’m still young but I’ll keep doing my best and we’ll see what happens next time."
