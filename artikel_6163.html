JetBlue Airways is set to debut its latest transcontinental service as the airline begins flying from San Diego International Airport (SAN) to Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International Airport (FLL) on November 19. The airline's expansion of its transcontinental footprint will see the launch of a daily service between Southern California and South Florida.

The flight will operate with a block time of almost six hours. The daily service will be operated onboard the airline's Airbus A321ceo and marks the return of the airline, which previously served the route periodically between June 2016 and January 2025.

The corridor between San Diego and Fort Lauderdale is tight, with three competitors all operating multiple weekly flights across the year. Alaska Airlines provides nonstop flights on the route, while Southwest Airlines adds a seasonal service between November and January.

JetBlue's resumption on the route marks the third to begin out of Fort Lauderdale, adding to the new flights to Indianapolis International Airport (IND) and Columbus (CMH).

As already mentioned, JetBlue will fly the route daily with the schedule below, departing in the early morning from Fort Lauderdale, with aircraft pushing back at 7:00 AM, for a mid-morning arrival into San Diego. Return flights depart late morning for an early evening arrival into Florida.

JetBlue has expanded aggressively at Fort Lauderdale since Spirit Airlines' collapse in May. The airline is now the largest airline at the South Florida airport, and the airport has even been suggested to be a highly likely location for the airline's third BlueHouse lounge.

From Fort Lauderdale, JetBlue has a network of 62 total destinations, with the most popular routes from the airport being to New York (JFK), Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), New York (LGA), Boston Logan International Airport (BOS), and San Juan (SJU).

As already mentioned, JetBlue has operated the route from San Diego to Fort Lauderdale before, having operated it up until January 2025. During the eight and a half years the airline operated the service, it had an average load factor of 82.5%, according to data from the US Department of Transportation. In the final months before the route was axed, 85% of passengers traveled on the route point to point, whereas a further 15% connected onto another JetBlue flight.

JetBlue is doubling down at FLL just as Spirit Airlines is forced to pull back.

This coming winter season, JetBlue's major routes from Fort Lauderdale are to New York JFK and Los Angeles LAX, which, from November through the summer of 2027, will see in excess of 1,900 flights up until May.
