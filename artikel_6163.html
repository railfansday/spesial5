Thailand wants events to offset falling arrivals. Early data shows Eurovision Asia riding Bangkok's seasonal upswing, not driving it. One night won't move the needle, but a steady calendar of events just might.

Bangkok hosts the first Eurovision Song Contest Asia on Nov. 14, with 11 countries competing at One Bangkok Forum. It is an early test of Thailand's new tourism playbook: earn more from each visitor as arrivals stall.

"Hosting the first-ever Eurovision Song Contest Asia reflects Thailand's Value over Volume direction, where global events are leveraged to deliver quality tourism, meaningful experiences and long-term benefits for local communities," Tourism Authority of Thailand (TAT) Governor Thapanee Kiatphaibool said in April.

At least at first, the payoff is likely to be publicity rather than spending. "For this first edition, I expect visibility and discovery to be the main benefits," said Nat Srinara, a Singapore-based hospitality consultant and lecturer at EHL Hospitality Business School.

Thailand hosts the first Eurovision Song Contest Asia in Bangkok on November 14, an early test of its "Value over Volume" tourism strategy as arrivals fall. Foreign visitors dropped 7.2% to 32.9 million in 2025, and the Tourism Authority of Thailand now expects just over 31 million this year, below the government's earlier 33 million forecast — yet the draft tourism plan targets at least 4 trillion baht ($118 billion) in annual revenue by 2032, roughly 48% above 2025, with no budget, arrivals assumption, or spending-per-visitor goal attached. TAT has published no revenue estimate for Eurovision Asia and told Skift it does not oversee the contest, in contrast with Tomorrowland's Pattaya edition in December, which TAT projects will generate about 5.3 billion baht ($158 million) and which carries a disclosed 2 billion baht ($60 million) state commitment over five years. The early data points in two directions: Agoda searches by Asian travelers for Bangkok stays over the contest window rose 14% year over year and Amadeus shows air arrivals up 11% for November 12 to 14, but hotel occupancy of 60% and ADR of $143.20 show similar gains before and after the contest, which Amadeus said suggests a broader trend rather than a festival-specific spike. Bangkok could use the help: third-quarter RevPAR fell 5.3% from the previous quarter, and Kasikorn Research Center counted 148 hotel and accommodation closures nationwide through September, up 42%.
