Ethereum’s Next Upgrade Could Be Harder Than The Merge, Foundation Warns

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The Ethereum Foundation is planning a security upgrade to protect the network against artificial intelligence (AI) and quantum computing risks. This year, the wider sector has faced hacks that drained billions from the market, prompting devs to roll out security updates.

In a recent community note, Ethereum Foundation researcher Kev Wedderburn said plans have been in the works for years to mitigate quantum risks, but recent AI changes will prompt changes. It has doubled its security protocol team to beef up defenses on multiple fronts.

A major issue is the limited timeframe and amount of work required for a comprehensive upgrade. The Foundation projects it will be harder than Merge, though it has more advanced tools at its disposal. Ethereum Merge saw the network move to a Proof-of-Stake consensus mechanism following heated debates over energy consumption.

The network build covers three areas: consensus, users, and data. First, validators sign with hash-based keys, while wallets let users seamlessly pivot to hash-based signatures.

“Moving fast makes it easier to ship bugs, so formal verification and security work will have to keep up with the pace of protocol development; cutting corners here would defeat the whole purpose of the upgrade. In response to this, the EF has doubled the size of its protocol security team.”

According to the note, every layer will adapt, even if it means delaying current features. This includes tools being built, as they will be monitored for overall network safety.

Recently, Justin Drake, an Ethereum researcher, tipped their team to complete a post-quantum upgrade by 2028. Quantum fears are dominating blockchain security conversations because supercomputers are developing so quickly.

If these computers break the cryptography, bad actors could drain billions in Bitcoin and other assets. While Ethereum has made significant progress to prevent Q-Day attacks, Bitcoin is largely behind due to governance setbacks.

The largest crypto by market cap plunged below $60,000 amid crushing low sentiment from whales. Ethereum is down 2.4%, trading at $1,657 after a negative few months.
