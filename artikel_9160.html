derrick adams invites miami art week visitors to build a giant LEGO unicorn pool float

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

during miami art week, visitors can help artist derrick adams build a nine-foot LEGO unicorn at 1111 lincoln road.

During Miami Art Week this December, artist Derrick Adams and the LEGO Group will bring a nine-foot unicorn pool float to Miami Beach. Built from LEGO bricks, the oversized figure will sit at the center of Pool Party, a colorful installation at Avant Gallery inside 1111 Lincoln Road. The project takes Adams’ long-running Floaters series, with its images of Black people at leisure, into an entirely different medium.

On December 3rd and 4th, visitors will have a hand in creating the artwork themselves. Adams and the LEGO team will invite the public to help assemble parts of the installation, ahead of its completed unveiling on December 5th. The two-day community build is part of The Art of Building, a participatory experience organized during Miami Art Week.

Surrounding the unicorn, a poolside landscape will feature around 3,000 flowers from the LEGO Botanicals collection. More than 500 hours of building work will go into the installation, which translates Adams’ distinct planes of color and playful pool imagery into a space people can move around. ‘This is the first time the unicorn will take on a scale this monumental,‘ Adams tells designboom.

Adams began developing Floaters in the mid-2010s, inspired in part by photographs of Martin Luther King Jr. vacationing with his family. His paintings depict Black people relaxing on inflatable pool toys, their angular figures set against expanses of bright blue. The scenes celebrate leisure and relaxation, and have become some of the artist’s most recognizable works.

The unicorn has made its way beyond the paintings before. In 2022, Adams brought Funtime Unicorns, a series of interactive playground sculptures, to Rockefeller Center in New York. The new Miami installation returns to the familiar character at a scale that changes how people can experience it.

‘The unicorn feels like a beacon of optimism and mystery,‘ the artist tells us. ‘There’s something about that combination that continues to interest me.‘ For Adams, the familiar pool toy leaves plenty of room for imagination. He sees it as an opportunity to give people permission to step outside their usual routines and enjoy themselves.

At nine feet tall, the LEGO unicorn called for a different approach to construction. Adams worked with the LEGO Model Shop engineering team to translate the strong outlines and geometric color fields of his paintings into a large brick-built form. Each piece becomes part of the design up close while, from farther away, they accumulate to become a recognizable figure.

‘In my paintings, color, form and composition create a world that viewers experience visually,‘ Adams explains. ‘With LEGO bricks, those same elements become physical and architectural.‘ The change in material also alters the relationship between the work and its audience. People can now walk around the figure and discover it from different perspectives.

Adams says the engineering process opened up new questions about scale and surface. ‘Working with The Model Shop engineering team has been eye-opening regarding how scale transforms what the figure does in space,‘ he says. The large unicorn will sit within an imagined pool scene, where thousands of colorful LEGO flowers extend the composition into its surrounding environment.

While Adams and the design team have developed the overall composition, the final installation will include contributions from the public. During the December 3rd and 4th sessions, participants will select and assemble LEGO bricks with guidance so that each contribution fits into the larger artwork. The process is designed to be approachable, even for people who have never considered themselves particularly creative.

‘What excites me most about Pool Party is the opportunity to invite people into the creative process,‘ Adams says. He sees a certain freedom in having enough direction to get started, without the pressure of inventing something from scratch. ‘It removes the pressure of needing to know where to start or feeling like you need to be an artist to participate,‘ he continues.

‘Hopefully, guests have their inner child activated,‘ he adds. The artist hopes the experience will encourage adults to make more time for creativity, and to rediscover the simple pleasure of making something with their hands.

The Art of Building takes place at Avant Gallery, 1111 Lincoln Road, Miami Beach, on December 3rd and 4th, from 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. daily. Walk-ins are welcome, although advance registration is strongly encouraged.
