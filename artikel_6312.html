United Airlines Pushes Back Airbus A321neo Coastliner Debut To “Early 2027”

United Airlines' Airbus A321neo Coastliner is set to enter regular scheduled service early next year. The airline had hoped to debut this new aircraft by the end of this year; however, after facing delivery delays from European aircraft manufacturer Airbus, the planes will not take flight until next year.

United's first Coastliner was delivered on November 5 and bears registration N74751. The jet departed from Hamburg before being delivered to the airline later that day. The aircraft will now undergo testing and crew training before entering commercial scheduled service in 2027.

Before you board, you will easily recognize this aircraft with its unique United livery and the words 'Coastliner' depicted on the fuselage. Onboard passengers will be treated to lie-flat United Polaris business class seats, all offering the premium passenger all-aisle access. Further back down the plane, United Premium Plus is available with retracted dividers.

For economy class passengers, the largest economy seatback screens in the world are fitted, ensuring travelers can sit back and relax with the latest blockbuster movies or television shows at the touch of a button on transcontinental services. Fitted with Starlink, the flights will offer some of the fastest WiFi in the sky, which is free for all MileagePlus members.

Beginning to fly in early 2027, the airline is expected to debut the aircraft on services from Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR) to San Francisco International Airport (SFO) and Los Angeles International Airport (LAX). These jets are set to replace the older Boeing 757-200 and free up larger widebody planes to be redeployed on other international high-demand services. These planes are set to service premier routes and align with the airline's broader United Next strategy.

For Chicago-based United, the Coastliner is a custom-designed aircraft that has been built for premium heavy transcontinental routes that otherwise do not demand the use of the carrier's widebody aircraft. The key features onboard include 20 lie-flat Polaris suites, 12 Premium Plus recliners (which is rare for domestic narrowbody services), and then 129 seats in Economy.

A walk-up economy snack bar is on offer for economy passengers, which saw the airline remove one of the last rows to install the self-service snack bar. With the aircraft also equipped with Starlink and large entertainment screens, its going to put United at the top of its game for US domestic flying.

With United also edging closer to its return to flying at John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK), the airline is likely to also operate the Coastliner on transcontinental services from the major New York airport.

Simple Flying reached out to United for comment on the Coastliner on Friday; however, an airline spokesperson could not be reached.

The latest additions to the fleet just rolled off the line.

For United, it joins a number of other major US carriers that continue to upgrade the transcontinental hard product. American Airlines has already launched its own coast-to-coast service with the Flagship Suites on the Airbus A321XLR, while Alaska Airlines plans to introduce the lie-flat suite and premium economy on its subfleet of 737 MAX 10.

Atlanta-based Delta Air Lines has also debuted its own Airbus A321neo, which includes 44 domestic first-class reclining seats (not lie-flat). This comes as more airlines look to increase transcontinental flight frequencies without needing larger aircraft that would otherwise offer the improved hard product. Instead, airlines are looking to smaller jets and improving the onboard experience for key domestic transcontinental services.

For United, the first delivery of the Coastliner marks the latest chapter in the airline's fleet development. The airline has a total of 50 Coastliners on order with Airbus, alongside its existing order of 50 A321XLRs. The new Coastliner is set to play a pivotal role in the airline's lucrative domestic transcontinental flights for decades to come.
