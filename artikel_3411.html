Black Friday is still a couple of weeks away, with the big shopping event falling on November 27, but Amazon's early Black Friday sale is already bringing some worthwhile discounts on Soundcore's Nebula projectors. Prices start at $299 for the compact Nebula P1i and go up to $2,799 for the premium X1S 4K laser projector, with savings of up to $700 across the models we've rounded up. There's quite a bit of variety here, too, from battery-powered projectors you can take camping or move between rooms to more powerful 4K models designed for a dedicated movie-night setup.

The Soundcore Nebula P1i is down to $299 from $399, while the Nebula P1 is $699.99, down from $799. Both offer 1080p resolution and Google TV, but their speaker setups are what make them interesting. The P1i has two 10W speakers that flip out and rotate to direct sound toward your seating area, while the P1's speakers detach completely and can run wirelessly for up to 20 hours. The P1 is also brighter at 650 ANSI lumens versus the P1i's 380, though you'll need a power outlet for either projector since neither has a built-in battery.

Small enough to slip into a bag, the Nebula Capsule 3 and Capsule 3 Laser are worth considering if you want something you can easily take on trips or move around the house. The standard Capsule 3 is down to $399 from $529, saving you $130, while the Laser version is $649.99, down from $749.99. Both offer 1080p resolution, automatic image adjustments, and up to 2.5 hours of battery life, although the Laser model is a little brighter at 300 ANSI lumens compared with the standard model's 200.

Taking movie night outdoors is a little easier with a battery-powered projector, and both the Nebula Mars 3 Air and Mars 3 are designed for just that. The smaller Mars 3 Air is down to $519.99 from $599.99, offering 1080p resolution, 400 ANSI lumens of brightness, and up to 2.5 hours of playback. The larger Mars 3, now $899.99 from $999.99, steps things up with 1,000 ANSI lumens, up to five hours of battery life in Eco mode, and a more powerful 40W speaker. It also has a weather-resistant design that's useful for camping or backyard screenings, though its extra bulk might make it less convenient to carry around.

If you're ready to move beyond 1080p, the Nebula Cosmos 4K SE at $1,099.99 (originally $1,299.99) offers a more affordable way to get 4K resolution. Its hybrid LED and laser light source delivers up to 1,800 ANSI lumens, while its Dolby Vision support helps bring out more detail in compatible movies and shows. You also get Google TV and automatic image adjustments for easier setup, though it doesn't have a built-in battery, so you'll need a power outlet nearby.

At the premium end of Nebula's lineup, the X1 and newer X1S are designed for a more serious home theater setup. The X1 is down to $2,299 from $2,999, saving you $700, while the X1S is $2,799 from $3,299. Both use triple-laser technology with 4K resolution, Dolby Vision, and optical zoom, but the X1S has a considerable advantage in brightness, reaching up to 6,000 ANSI lumens compared with the X1's 3,500. That extra brightness is useful in rooms with some ambient light, but the tradeoff is louder fan noise.
