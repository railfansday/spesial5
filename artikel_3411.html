Amazon’s all-in-one cheap soundbar has a built-in subwoofer for under $250 — there's just one thing holding it back

All-in-one soundbars are a dangerous game. How does one balance center channels for dialogue with a subwoofer for the best blockbuster movie action? In the case of the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar Plus, with semi-difficulty.

Don’t get me wrong, the price is incredibly appealing — $249 for a 3.1-channel soundbar is super affordable — and the minimalist design won’t cause any controversy. But is this enough to make it one of the best soundbars?

Yes and no. Unfortunately, the bass tends to overpower dialogue in action-heavy scenes. But could this still be the soundbar for you if you only watch TV and YouTube? Find out in this Amazon Fire TV Soundbar Plus review.

If you’re looking for a reason to buy the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar Plus, that sweet, sweet MSRP is the biggest one.

At $249 at Amazon U.S. ($139 on sale) and £249 at Amazon U.K., the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar Plus is incredibly cheap for what you get. It’s an all-in-one “Dolby Atmos” soundbar — yes, that means with a built-in subwoofer. The basic model, Amazon Fire TV Soundbar, is $119, so you’re essentially paying $130 for a built-in subwoofer.

At this price point, there’s very little competition. Of course, there’s the Samsung B750F ($297, 2025 model), which has a separate subwoofer and 5.1 channels (more upfiring speakers for better surround sound). Even so, this is $50 pricier, which can be a dealbreaker if you’re on a rigid budget.

I’ve also tested the Samsung Q600F — MSRP of $499 but often on sale in the $300s — which is 2025’s model but has a 3.1.2 channel setup, which is the minimum you need for genuine Dolby Atmos. I’ll get into this more below, but that’s the long and short of it.

The Sony Bravia Theater Bar 5 is $349 with a 3.1 channel — so the same as the Amazon Plus and without the surround channels present on the Samsung Q600F — which I would recommend over the Plus if you have a little more to spare. Its separate subwoofer provides a stronger bass response without overpowering dialogue, unlike the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar Plus.

Like the $119 Amazon Fire TV Soundbar, the Plus model also has EQ modes. These are as follows: “Music”, “Movie”, “Sports”, and “Night”. There’s also individual bass level adjustment if you want to get really particular with your listening experience.

There's a dialogue mode, but this didn't isolate speech as well as I wanted it to, especially in louder, action-heavy scenes.

Music mode (connected via Bluetooth) is fine, although the bass was a little boomy and the vocals a little too sharp. I would recommend grabbing one of the best speakers, like the JBL Flip 7, to listen to music at home.

So let’s get one thing straight: the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar Plus does not have “true” Dolby Atmos. If a soundbar doesn’t have side-firing or up-firing channel speakers, it physically cannot project soundwaves in that way.

In Top Gun: Maverick's dogfighting scene, I could feel the soundtrack whoosh around me, and the radioed dialogue sounded as if it were coming from beside the TV

However, the Plus has “Dolby Atmos”, which means it can technically decode Atmos signals. While this obviously doesn’t produce a genuinely immersive experience, it does have its moments. In the dogfighting scene in "Top Gun: Maverick," I could feel the soundtrack whoosh around me, and the radioed dialogue sounded as if it were coming from beside the TV. As I’ll get into later, this pushed the dialogue to the back of the soundstage, resulting in me straining to hear speech.

Dolby Atmos was better in “K-Pop Demon Hunters, which is really surprising. Usually, “Top Gun: Maverick” blows other movies out of the water in terms of Atmos. Cheering crowds were louder and more immersive in “K-Pop Demon Hunters," and some dialogue was positioned well in the up-firing speakers. One character's voice was particularly crisp and immersive — the lowest female voice — which makes me think the soundbar is better at isolating lower mids than sub-bass.

Despite having a range of EQ modes and a palatable price, unfortunately, the Plus gets a 3.5-star rating from me for a reason. There's one majorly important thing holding it back.

Unlike the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar, the Plus really struggles to differentiate between dialogue and bass layers in action-heavy scenes. “Top Gun: Maverick” was particularly egregious here: Bass often overpowered dialogue, especially when the soundtrack was relatively heavy in flying scenes. I could barely hear Maverick's radioed dialogue to the characters in the control room over the string-heavy soundtrack and the flying airplane.

This was incredibly surprising, as the cheaper non-Plus model excelled in the very same scene. Bass volume was set to the minimum, and I watched on “Movie” mode.

While not as egregious in “K-Pop Demon Hunters," I still struggled to hear some dialogue. Surprisingly, the music sounded excellent in this movie, but dialogue is arguably the most important part. If you can’t hear the actors, what’s the point?

The consequences are really enormous: I struggled to make out dialogue in action-heavy scenes across the three movies I watched, which isn’t really what you want when using a soundbar. The whole purpose of a soundbar is to make dialogue more intelligible.

However, I also watched "Gilmore Girls" on basic Netflix, which sounded great. If you predominantly watch TV or YouTube, I don't think you'll notice this issue. It's just a concern for action movie buffs. In that case, I'd recommend checking out the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar Plus with Subwoofer ($375).

At $249, the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar is incredibly affordable. There are very few soundbars that can beat this in terms of penny-per-performance.

The aforementioned Sony Bravia Theater Bar 5 is $349, so $100 more than the Amazon model, but has a dedicated subwoofer and a center channel for dialogue. I would personally save up more and buy the Sony model over the Amazon.

But if I were to save more money, I'd choose the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar (non-Plus) over the Plus version. Sure, there's no subwoofer, but it's just $119 and genuinely sounds fantastic. It's the best budget soundbar I've ever tested.

Hmm. This is a tricky one. I loved the basic Amazon Fire TV Soundbar — if you want seriously great sound at a low price, get that one. The extra $130 for the Plus model? Not worth it, if you ask me. I would either spend an extra $50 on the Samsung B750F or an extra $130 on the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar Plus with Subwoofer.

Unfortunately, due to a lack of communication between the built-in subwoofer and the main channel speakers, dialogue can be overpowered during action-heavy scenes. Consequently, I can’t recommend this model over the Samung B750F. Personally, I would either get the cheaper Amazon Fire TV Soundbar or the aforementioned Samsung model. Or, you could save up a little extra for the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar Plus with Subwoofer ($375) or the with Rears version ($489).

However, if you have an Amazon Fire TV and want something capable of integrating into the ecosystem seamlessly (even though you'll need a separate Fire stick or Fire TV), without having to spend an extra $50 on the B750F, the Plus is a great deal.
