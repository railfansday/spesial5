Five lighting designs listed by Marset on Dezeen Showroom

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Spanish lighting brand Marset has showcased five of its products on Dezeen Showroom, including a mid-century reissue and a ceiling light that appears like a block of ice.

Marset‘s selection includes the Gambosa table lamp, created by designer Mathias Hahn to reinterpret the classic shade, stem and base as one smooth, glossy sculpture in steel and opal polycarbonate.

Gambosa is defined by its elegant composition, with a round-ended body that looks as if precariously balanced. It also features a considered cable design that sweeps down one side of the lamp like a signature.

It is distinguished by its select palette of colours, with combinations of orange, moss grey, black, pale pink and opal that evoke mid-century style combined with contemporary curation.

With the Lauro collection, Marset has revived a genuine 1970s design classic from its own catalogue, featuring chunky tubes of bent steel.

Created by designers P Aragay and J Pérez Mateo, the Lauro lighting comes in table, floor and wall variants. According to Marset, the range represents the technical achievements of a time when bending steel was a huge challenge.

Another of the highlights from Marset is the Ringo lighting, crafted by designer Joan Gaspar from transparent polycarbonate that is rotomoulded, giving it a dimpled texture reminiscent of a melting block of ice.

Available in flush-mounted or pendant styles, the design has a simple cylindrical shape, with scale and materiality bringing fresh life and visual impact.

Another of Gaspar’s designs is Domus, an angular bollard-style outdoor light that, through simple gesture, recalls a warmly lit house at night.

The design is available in three heights. Units can be optionally combined to create a sculptural look and the illusion of multiple windows.

Geometric glass volumes, metal detailing and glowing bar-shaped lighting elements meet in the Fragile collection, designed for the brand by Jaume Ramírez.

The lights come in pendant, wall and table formats, each made up of spherical and conical shades that lend them a playful aesthetic.

The roots of the Marset brand stretch back to the 1940s, when Francisco “Paco” Marset established a small metal foundry in Barcelona.

His grandchildren Javier and Carlos Marset helped reposition the company as the global design brand it is today when they took over in the 1990s, bringing on board designers such as Gaspar, who is a frequent collaborator.
