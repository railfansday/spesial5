Find the conditions that waive your monthly checking fee and meet the easiest one, which may only mean sending part of your paycheck to the account. If none fits, ask the bank to move you to its basic account, or switch to a free checking account elsewhere.

A $15 monthly maintenance fee on checking costs $180 a year, for the kind of account many banks offer free.

Your account’s page on the bank’s website and its fee schedule list the ways to avoid the fee, and you may be close to meeting one already. Chase Total Checking, for example, charges $15 a month but nothing for a statement period with $500 in qualifying electronic deposits, such as a paycheck or government benefits.

A balance of at least $1,500 at the start of every day also waives the fee, and so does a $5,000 daily average, counting linked Chase savings and investments. The $1,500 balance is the easiest of the three to miss, since one day that starts below $1,500 rules that option out for the whole period.

Say your paycheck goes to a credit union and your Chase account charges $15 a month. If your employer lets you split your direct deposit, send a fixed $500 of each paycheck to Chase and the rest to the credit union, and you save $180 a year. A Zelle transfer, cash, or a check won’t count toward the $500.

If no condition fits, call the bank and ask to move to its basic account. Chase Secure Banking, for one, charges $4.95 a month but nothing for a statement period with $250 in qualifying electronic deposits, though it has no paper checks or outgoing wire transfers. On the same call, ask for the last few months’ fees back, along with any other fee you find by adding up a year of bank fees.

If you’d still pay a fee, open a free checking account at an online bank or credit union.
