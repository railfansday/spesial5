Chewy jumps on stop-motion animation trend lighting up holiday ads

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Let Marketing Dive's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

The brand’s research shows nearly 80% of pet owners plan to buy their furry friends at least one present this year.

In an effort to cut through generative artificial intelligence slop this holiday season, several brands have turned to what is considered to be one of the most labor-intensive forms of filmmaking: stop-motion animation. From Levi’s heartwarming claymation spot to Prada’s holiday effort which combines live actors with traditional 2D animation à la “Who Framed Roger Rabbit,” the technique has found itself the medium for many a holiday advertisement this year.

Pet supply company Chewy is no exception. Its holiday campaign marks the first time the brand has used the medium in a national TV spot. Stop-motion animation is used to portray the rich inner lives of pets during the holidays, where a Chewy box transports pets to a world of toys, treats and fun in the snow.

The idea was born out of the long-running Chewy Claus initiative, where owners can write to the company on behalf of their pets. As part of the effort, Chewy will help fund adoptions for shelter animals and send surprises to select letter-writers throughout the month of December.

“It didn't start with stop motion,” said Melissa Bell, Chewy’s chief brand officer, who joined the company in July. “We think about [the holidays] from our point of view, but given the importance of pets and animals for our company in particular, we were like,’ let's start with what our pets and animals think,’ and it naturally blossomed from there.”

Despite ongoing economic concerns potentially putting a damper on gift-giving this year, holiday pet spending continues to be a priority for many, especially for younger consumers. Nearly 80% of pet owners plan on buying a present for their animal this holiday season, with 21% of Gen Z and millennials planning to spend $100 or more, according to data from the brand. Chewy’s efforts go beyond the standard pet food and medication orders to encourage surprising the animals in one’s life and including them in the festive season.

The campaign marks the first time Chewy Claus is getting a national TV spot as part of the holiday campaign, according to Bell.

“The love and following of Chewy Claus is really inspirational, and for us, it was about connecting to…a bigger purpose because it's about helping shelter pets find homes. We wanted to bring that message to a larger audience, and this campaign gives us a way to bring that tradition to more people,” said Bell.

No AI was used in the creation of the spots, according to Bell. The puppets featured in the campaign were all crafted by hand and modeled after real-life animals. In the 30-second spot “A Holiday Dream,” a cat and dog are treated to a world of holiday whimsy created entirely in stop-motion animation. In the 15-second “A Holiday of Treats,” a dog is thrown a treat by a snowman dog toy, which he catches and crashes into a Chewy box filled with toys, before it ends with the dog asleep on the living room floor. Chewy’s in-house brands, such as Frisco, make frequent appearances.

Chewy’s holiday campaign expands beyond the national spot into a social media effort. Influencers, which the brand refers to as “Chewpanions” are central to the strategy and will be promoting the Chewy Claus effort, along with the brand more broadly.

In-person activations are also a key component of the effort and are largely centered around pet adoption. Chewy will host an event in Santa Monica, California, from Dec. 5-6, where attendees can write letters to Chewy Claus, explore a holiday market and meet adoptable pets. A similar event was held in Miami from Nov. 21-22 to kick off the season.

Chewy’s net sales totaled$3.33 billion, a 7.3% year-over-year increase, in its fiscal second quarter ending Aug. 2, per an earnings release. In February, Chewy acquired SmartPak, a horse supply and supplements brand. In May, it expanded its veterinary practice footprint through the acquisition of Modern Animal, bringing its approximate number of locations to 60.
