A dated tub and tile are often sound under a worn finish. Before you pay to tear them out, check for wide cracks, leaks, and soft spots in the floor around them. Replace only what fails that check, and have the rest reglazed with a fresh coating for a fraction of what replacing them costs.

Say a contractor quotes $5,800 to tear out a 1990s tub and its tile surround and put in new ones. If neither one is badly cracked, leaking, or soft underneath, most of that money replaces parts that still work.

Reglazing coats the old tub and tile with a new finish instead. HomeGuide puts the price at $800 to $1,200 for a tub and the shower tile around it, so a $900 job would save $4,900 against that quote.

The trade-off is lifespan. A reglaze lasts about 10 to 15 years, by HomeGuide’s estimate, and a second one when it wears out would bring your total to about $1,800, under a third of the quote.

Check the tub and walls first. Small cracks in a tub can be repaired for $100 to $300, by HomeGuide’s figures, but a wide crack, or a leak a plumber can’t fix, usually means a new tub. A soft spot in the floor or wall, or tiles that are loose or sound hollow when you tap them, point to water damage behind the surface. That section has to be torn out and repaired.

A reglazed surface needs gentle care. Wait at least 24 hours before using it, then clean it with a non-abrasive liquid cleaner instead of scouring powder or bleach, and use a bath mat without suction cups.

Get a reglazing quote for the same tub and tile before you sign a replacement contract, and put the difference toward whatever fails those checks.
