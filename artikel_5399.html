Judge Upholds Mamdani’s NYC Rent Freeze, Tosses Landlords’ Lawsuit

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A Manhattan judge has upheld New York City's rent freeze for roughly 1 million rent-stabilized apartments, dismissing a lawsuit brought by landlords who argued that the decision was politically predetermined.

In a 20-page ruling issued on Wednesday, New York Supreme Court Justice Brendan T. Lantry found that the city's Rent Guidelines Board had acted within its authority when it voted this summer to freeze rents on both one- and two-year leases.

The landlords, led by Kenilworth Holdings LLC, argued that Mayor Zohran Mamdani had effectively dictated the outcome after campaigning on a promise to freeze rents and appointing board members who supported the policy.

Despite the favorable ruling, Lantry wasn't entirely sympathetic to the mayor. In his final decision, he called Mamdani's campaign promise "reckless and irresponsible." Even so, he concluded that the board had independently considered the evidence before reaching its decision.

The freeze passed in June after a landmark 7-1 vote, with only Arpit Gupta, an economist who was appointed to the board in 2022 by Mayor Eric Adams, voting no.

But it was owner-representative Maksim Wynn's statement that stoked controversy at the meeting, after he raised concerns about the financial health of the city's buildings. Wynn's full statement was drowned out by boos from the crowd before he ultimately voted in favor of the freeze.

Those pressures were central to the landlords' suit, which argued that a "rent freeze is impossible to reconcile" with such rising costs, pointing to mounting expenses for insurance, maintenance, utilities, and property taxes.

Operating and maintenance expenses consumed roughly 71 cents of every dollar of revenue in 2024, according to the board's 2026 Income and Expense Study. And as Realtor.com® previously reported, those figures exclude mortgage payments and major capital expenses, which landlords argue can leave otherwise profitable-looking buildings struggling financially.

“Freezing rent sounds like a sure-fire way to solve housing affordability, but what I'm worried about is the unintended consequences on the rent-stabilized stock and market-rate prices,” Jake Krimmel, senior economist at Realtor.com, said in June.

Lantry acknowledged that potential fallout, writing that the freeze could have "detrimental impacts on the New York City residential real estate industry." But he emphasized that weighing the wisdom of the policy was not the court's role, writing that the freeze "has a rational basis and must therefore be upheld as valid."

The ruling leaves the freeze intact; however, the landlords could seek to appeal.
