Estate Planning Attorney Jennifer McKenna Addresses Whether You Need a Trust or if a Will Is Enough

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Jennifer McKenna explains how trusts and wills work together to support an estate planning strategy based on individual needs.

LEE'S SUMMIT, Mo., Nov. 27, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Do you need a trust or is a will enough? A HelloNation article featuring insights from Estate Planning Attorney Jennifer McKenna of the Jennifer Ward McKenna, Attorney at Law in Lees Summit, Missouri, explains how trusts and wills serve different purposes within an estate planning strategy.

The article explains that many people begin estate planning by asking whether they need a trust or a will. The answer depends on factors such as family circumstances, financial goals, the types of assets they own, and how they want those assets managed after death. The article notes that both legal tools play important roles and understanding their differences helps individuals make informed decisions.

According to the article, a will is a legal document that explains how a person's property should be distributed after death. It also allows someone to name an executor to manage the estate and nominate guardians for minor children when necessary. The article describes a will as the foundation of many estate planning plans because it provides clear instructions and guidance for loved ones during a difficult time.

The HelloNation article also explains that a will always goes through the probate process after death. During probate, the court validates the will, oversees the payment of debts, and authorizes the distribution of assets. While probate is a routine legal process, the article notes that the timeline depends on the amount of assets administered through the will, the complexity of the estate, and state law. Some estates move through probate relatively quickly, while most require several months to resolve legal or administrative matters.

A trust serves a different purpose by allowing assets to be managed by a trustee for the benefit of designated beneficiaries. Unlike a will, many assets held in a properly funded trust can pass directly to beneficiaries without going through probate. The article explains that avoiding probate is one of the primary reasons some individuals include a trust as part of an overall estate planning strategy. It also notes that a trust can provide instructions for managing assets if the person creating the trust becomes unable to manage financial matters during their lifetime or when a beneficiary is unable to manage an inheritance effectively, such as a young child or an individual with special needs.

The article emphasizes that a trust is not automatically the right choice for every family. Establishing and maintaining a trust requires additional planning, and assets must be properly transferred into the trust for it to function as intended. If assets remain outside the trust, they may still be subject to probate. The article explains that understanding how a trust works is just as important as deciding whether to create one.

Rather than viewing these documents as alternatives, the article explains that a will and a trust often work together. A trust may be used to manage significant assets, while a will can address property that was not transferred into the trust and nominate guardians for minor children. According to the article, this combination creates a more complete estate planning approach that reflects a family's specific circumstances and long-term goals.

The article also notes that every estate is different, and no single solution works for everyone. Some individuals focus on protecting young children, while others are more concerned with managing business interests, real estate, or investment accounts. Estate planning allows legal documents to reflect those priorities instead of relying on general assumptions.

The article concludes that delaying estate planning because of uncertainty about choosing a trust or a will can leave important questions unanswered. Learning how a trust, a will, and probate work allows individuals to make informed decisions that fit their own circumstances. Whether someone ultimately chooses a will, a trust, or both, taking time to understand these legal tools can provide greater clarity and help protect loved ones in the future.
