Gray Zone Warfare Update Addresses Biggest Rogue Ops Concerns

Gray Zone Warfare stands out for its immersion as a PvE open-world tactical shooter set on a quarantined Southeast Asian island with plenty of narrative elements to get stuck into. Rogue Ops allows for a fresh experience by building your very own Hideout and growing your reputation in new ways.

The ambitious mode has a lot to love, but it has faced its fair share of player complaints, as major issues have been interrupting the fun in the gameplay loop. A Halloween Dev Stream teased significant improvements to enemy AI and making the Hideout economy more rewarding.

This is the detailed all purpose hub for Gray Zone Warfare guides and content.

Now that Gray Zone Warfare Patch 0.5.1.0 is live, we can dive into all the key changes you can expect to see when you embark on your next mission.

First of all, huge changes have been made to the way enemy AI behaves. One of the biggest criticisms players had of the AI enemies was their inconsistency and the feeling of them being extremely overpowered.

Following this update, enemies should no longer know your exact position after shooting. Sound now only gives them an approximate location, meaning the farther away you are, the more difficult it is for them to detect you. Rather than the AI instantly sharing your exact position, they'll rely on information they can gather, which ultimately makes their reactions more natural.

Stealth has also undergone some work to make it more consistent. Moving slowly should make you harder to detect, and the AI exhibits reactions to light that you’d expect. These changes should make stealth, flanking enemies, and repositioning more rewarding and allow you to take a greater advantage over your surroundings.

Improvements have also been made to how enemies engage in combat. Trained soldiers bring more precision to their combat abilities and make better use of cover, but untrained enemies will be more careless in their actions.

Combined with fixing pesky problems with hit boxes, grenades, and enemies getting stuck behind doors, this series of changes aims to make combat more varied and test your tactical performance.

Sound now only gives them an approximate location, meaning the farther away you are, the more difficult it is for them to detect you.

Another major change has been made to the economy in Hideout. A range of rewards have been increased, including raising the rewards from smuggling tasks and increasing the sales prices of smuggling backpacks, and improving rewards from hideout handovers and increasing the sale prices of hideout products.

Production and disassembly times have even been reduced across the board, and the size and weight of selected building materials and disassembly have been reduced for a better looting experience.

The Gray Zone Warfare update arrives with a slew of bug fixes to gameplay, animations, audio, tasks, and so much more. This includes fixing a bug where the health panel could show 100% damage on an organ that was not destroyed and repairing an issue that caused some tasks to not be offered due to a nearby task blocking them. The full patch notes can be viewed here.

In the meantime, the team will continue to address further feedback, like AI enemies seemingly taking too many shots to kill, provided as an example, with further fixes planned for upcoming patches. The developers are already working on their next major update, Patch 0.5.2.0.
