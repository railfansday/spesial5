REO Runabout T4X Is a Simple Truck Promising a $22K Starting Price

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The single-cab T4X will be joined later by a crew-cab T4C and an S4C SUV, all promising the same blend of affordability and simplicity.

Affordable cars are gradually fading from the market as automakers chase profits over volume. Michigan-based Slate Auto wants to plug that gap with an electric truck set to enter production by the end of this year, but with a mere 205-mile range, the Slate Truck won’t fit everyone’s needs. That’s where REO Trucks aims to step in, promising a low-cost gas-powered pickup by late 2028.

REO Trucks revives the name of the company founded in 1905 by Ransom Eli Olds, after departing his first venture, Oldsmobile. The original REO, which went defunct in 1967, is best known for the Speed Wagon, an early ancestor to the modern pickup truck.

So, it’s no surprise that the revived REO is launching with a pickup truck of its own. The Texas-based startup’s pitch is straightforward: pickup trucks have grown in size and cost, leaving the market without a compact, practical, affordable, and simple option. REO’s Runabout lineup is designed to fill that hole.

Three variants will be available, all sharing a common steel ladder-frame platform. The T4X is a two-door work truck, the T4C gains a crew cab, while the S4C is a family SUV. The bodies of all three models will be made from stamped steel, skipping aluminum or composites in an effort to reduce manufacturing and repair costs.

The clean, basic look was honed by frankstephenson design, the eponymous design house led by the man responsible for the McLaren P1 and 2001 Mini Cooper. REO says the confident stance was inspired by the American bison, with black plastic wheel arches protruding from the bodywork that REO says can be easily removed if customers want to fit larger tires.

The wide grille is flanked by rectangular LED headlights stacked one atop the other; below, there’s a chunky bumper with integrated tow hooks. The Runabout models have an upright cabin and flat, unadorned doors, with the handles mounted vertically on the pillars above. All three models are identical up to the B-pillar and have the same 119.6-inch wheelbase, and they all feature a rear bumper that integrates the taillights and a step.

The T4X will launch first, pairing a two-seat cab with a steel drop-side flatbed measuring 79.1 inches long and 70.6 inches wide. The tailgate includes a built-in ruler, cupholders, a bottle opener, and slots to hold phones and tablets. The T4C adds a pair of doors and a rear bench seat, with a 54.1-inch bed length. The S4C SUV, meanwhile, measures 182.7 inches long, about two inches shy of a Honda CR-V, and can seat six. It also features a one-piece, side-hinged tailgate and a rear window that can retract into the door.

REO has yet to show the interior, which it says will be revealed in 2027, but REO promises plenty of physical controls, with buttons and switches designed to be operated while wearing gloves. Still, a digital gauge cluster will be present to display navigation and media. Unlike Slate, which cut costs with manual crank windows, REO is sticking with power windows. The Runabout will also get manually adjustable bucket seats, 120-volt power sockets, and a bit set in the glovebox that works for every fastener on the vehicle.

Under the hood will be a naturally aspirated four-cylinder engine, hooked up to a standard manual transmission. An automatic will be available, and all REOs will feature part-time four-wheel drive, with a mechanical transfer case with low-range ratios and a locking rear differential. REO says it’s targeting a powertrain life of 500,000 miles, avoiding turbochargers, direct injection, and a stop-start system in the name of longevity. The company didn’t reveal the engine’s capacity, nor where it will be sourced from.

REO keeps things simple with solid front and rear axles, suspended by coil springs up front and leaf springs at the rear. Braking is handled by ventilated front discs and rear drums, and the trucks feature hydraulic power steering. The steel wheels are wrapped in 245/70R-17 all-season tires.

A planned Ransom package will improve off-road capability, with 265/70R-17 all-terrain tires, upgraded dampers, and a 2.5-inch lift. The increased ride height raises the approach angle from 42.5 to 48.3 degrees and the breakover angle from 16.3 to 21.2 degrees. Ransom-equipped vehicles will be distinguished by orange dampers up front and upgraded leaf springs and dampers at the rear.

REO also wants to shake up the ownership experience, starting with a direct-to-consumer sales approach. REO promises its vehicles will be affordable and easy to maintain, making “right-to-repair” a core part of its mantra. REO will etch a service diagram under the hood, and REO says the bumpers, door panels, headliner, and trim are designed to be removable in under five minutes using common tools. The company also wants to provide open-source parts availability, with manufacturers that meet REO’s standard given factory mounting patterns and the ability to sell directly to customers via REO’s online marketplace.

The first T4X prototypes are set to be built in 2027, before full production starts in 2028, with deliveries kicking off later that year or early 2029. The T4X is supposed to start at just $21,500, with the T4C requiring $25,000 and the S4C coming in at $28,500. REO says production will come from an unnamed assembly plant in Texas and claims it already has 40,000 reservations, although that only requires paying a refundable $25 fee.
