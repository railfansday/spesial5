The world of Calcio reacts to GTA VI: Corriere dello Sport, Gattuso, De Zerbi & more

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Grand Theft Auto VI (or, GTA VI) has finally arrived after 13 years of waiting, and Italian football has joined in on the hype, from the newspaper front pages to the coaches’ press conferences.

As noted by Il Fatto Quotidiano, the game launched at midnight on 19 November. Football quickly caught on. Milan, Inter, Juventus and Lazio all marked the release on social media, Corriere dello Sport turned its Sunday front page into a GTA tribute, and two of Italy’s best-known coaches were asked whether their players would be up all night with a controller in their hands.

Corriere dello Sport’s Sunday front page was the standout. “GTA ttuso 32” was how the paper opened the day after Gattuso’s win over Lecce, playing on the Lazio coach’s name. The 32 refers to Lazio’s points tally, with the Biancocelesti top on their own, “+2 on Gasp and +4 on Chivu”.

Lazio’s 3-0 win was summed up as “Divertimento Lazio”, and “Meraviglia Noslin” was drawn in the style of the video game everyone has been talking about for days. Tijjani Noslin is the main figure in the illustration, with Davide Frattesi and Gattuso embracing behind him.

Milan posted a carousel that turns their players into characters inspired by the look of Grand Theft Auto, captioned “The characters we want in GTA VI”. One image showed Adrien Rabiot driving through Vice City, and another had Strahinja Pavlović at a petrol station. The Rossoneri followed up with “Growing up takes time. So did GTA VI”, a nod to the 13 years since GTA V.

Inter went for a debate instead. Curtis Jones and Djed Spence discussed the series on the way to training: Jones named San Andreas and GTA V as his favourites, while Spence went for Vice City. Juventus, meanwhile, used the game’s graphic style to present Kenan Yildiz and some of his teammates.

If the social media teams were excited, Gattuso clearly wasn’t. Asked in his pre-match press conference before Lecce whether the game might distract his players, the Lazio coach seemed to have no idea what it was.

“I don’t even know what you’re talking about. Is it a video game? I didn’t know. With that name, I thought it was a car,” he said.

“If they play at night, that’s their business. I’m not going to start checking up on them.

“This job requires self-respect. At breakfast together, I’ll try to work out if anyone has been staying up late.”

Asked about Fisayo Dele-Bashiru, who had said he would be playing the game after training, Gattuso added: “That’s part of being a professional and caring about your job. I would never tell an individual player not to play.”

Despite all that, Gattuso still ended up in the world of Vice City in a video published by Lazio.

Roberto De Zerbi was more direct. The Tottenham coach was asked the same question at his pre-match press conference before facing Ipswich Town.

“Listen, I am not a policeman, I am not a father, I am a coach,” he said. “I think in my dressing room are good people, mature people, so the PlayStation or other things, the right time they can close, but otherwise I go in their room and I destroy the GTA and the other things.”
