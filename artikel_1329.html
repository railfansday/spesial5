How EitC Helped Sean Realise He's Not Alone After His Wife's Tragic Passing

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

This Christmas, a lifelong Evertonian is sharing how Everton in the Community helped him survive the loneliest time of his life after his wife died just 12 hours after the birth of their second son.

Sean Foster, from Wigan, lost his wife Taylor in the early hours of 1 July 2020. Taylor was just 25 years old and passed away due to a splenic artery aneurysm, after being found unresponsive by staff on the mother and baby ward. 

Sean faced the devastating reality of raising two young children without his wife, while navigating grief, anxiety and suicidal thoughts.

He said: “I’ve been through grief before, but this was something else.

“You’re not just grieving for the person you’ve lost; it’s the future you had with them, it’s what they’ll miss out on with the kids and what the kids will miss out on with having their mum.

“With grief also came overwhelming struggles with PTSD, depression, anxiety and suicidal ideation. One thing that accompanies grief, which I have found the hardest and most persistent to cope with, is loneliness.”

Just six days after Taylor's death, Sean reached out to Everton in the Community on Twitter (now X) in the middle of the night – a decision he described as his “hail Mary” and the first step towards seeking help. 

“My newborn baby and his two-year-old brother were asleep, and I found myself feeling completely alone, drowning in grief and crying uncontrollably.

“It was a hail Mary. I just needed to reach out to someone and, as an Evertonian, I knew about Everton in the Community – but I didn’t expect the level of help I did get. It was a lifesaver.” 

A donation to Everton in the Community this Christmas will help the official charity of Everton Football Club to support more people like Sean and to ensure no one has to face loneliness alone.

Although Sean had immense support from friends and family, he didn’t want to worry them.

He continued: “By lunchtime, I had a response from Everton in the Community and just a couple of hours after that I was on the phone with them.

“The support I got from them was second to none and I know I would have found that period of my life so much harder if I hadn’t reached out.”

A member of Everton in the Community’s team visited Sean, providing essential items to help him care for his two young sons. 

 The charity's wellbeing team also signposted him to additional organisations for grief support, while regular phone calls gave Sean someone to turn to during some of his most difficult moments.  

He also received support in navigating practical challenges, including his finances and helping his children understand what had happened. Sean received a call from Everton legend Duncan Ferguson on the day Taylor's cause of death was confirmed.  

Taking the time to ask Sean about his wife and what she was like, the conversation marked an important moment in his journey, helping him take the first steps towards talking about Taylor again. 

 “Taylor was a force to be reckoned with,” Sean said. “She was genuinely one of a kind. A lot of people will say it about her, she was so thoughtful and she always looked out for others before herself.

“Even the gifts she’d give on birthdays and Christmas, she’d know exactly what each person wanted and needed, make a care package, wrap it nicely and put a bow on it.

“She was really into her arts and crafts and my eldest, he had two years with her, one of the things he always remembers is colouring with his mum.” 

Reflecting on the importance of having someone to turn to, Sean says he dreads to think about what might have happened had he not reached out to EitC. 

“I was in a really dark place and considering suicide," he admitted. "It’s not that I wanted to die. I wanted the kids to have a full family, a mum and dad figure and a safe, stable home. At that point in time I didn’t think I was giving them that. If I hadn’t reached out or got a response, I really struggle to think where I’d be.”

Sean has since become an advocate for men’s mental health, including volunteering for Andy’s Man Club and starting his own podcast about grief. He has received therapy and attends group support sessions where he passionately talks about issues such as loneliness. 

He said: “The loneliest I have ever felt was at Christmas, the year Taylor died. All my family were crammed into my mum and dad's house, as we do every year, all 20-plus of us in the living room, scrambling to find our pile of presents as we did every year.  

“Only this year, Taylor wasn’t there laughing at the Christmas chaos. To this day I have never felt as lonely as I did in that moment.”

Although he still experiences moments of loneliness, Sean says the support he has received has helped him understand that he doesn't have to face them alone.

He hopes that by sharing his experience, he can encourage others who may be struggling to reach out, particularly during a time of year when feelings of loneliness can become more difficult.

Sean said: “Loneliness isn’t the same as being alone.

“We can have people around us, we can see friends, family, colleagues daily, but we can still feel lonely. 

“What I found about loneliness, particularly as a man, is that there's a stigma around talking about it, there's an expectation that we should just be strong, just get on with it and deal with it ourselves.   

“Most of the time that is the expectation we put on ourselves, when the fact is the real strength is in asking for help, admitting we might need a hand every now and then. 

“I still experience moments of loneliness, but they aren't as all-consuming. Thanks to Everton in the Community, I've learned I'm never truly alone.” 

A donation to Everton in the Community this Christmas could help someone facing loneliness find the support they need – someone to listen and somewhere to turn when things feel impossible.
