Jose Mourinho’s 2018 Comments About Mauricio Pochettino Now Look Very Interesting

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Jose Mourinho's comments about Mauricio Pochettino back in 2018, while the Special One was in charge of Manchester United, make for very interesting reading now that the Argentine has agreed to become the latest manager to take on a poisoned chalice that is widely considered 'football's most stressful job'.

Pochettino, who managed Chelsea and Tottenham in the Premier League before his latest move, is the eighth permanent manager the United hierarchy have appointed since Sir Alex Ferguson's retirement in 2013. It's safe to say he couldn't be arriving at a worse time, with United sitting 18th and in the relegation zone, having won just three of their 15 matches across all competitions.

While the hierarchy have seemingly tried every avenue and every type of manager to get the club back on an even keel, hope and belief among supporters are quickly running thin.

Pochettino is inheriting something of a shipwreck, having never won a major trophy outside of his spell at PSG, which will hardly calm the nerves of yet another new era.

To this day, Mourinho still claims one of his biggest managerial successes was guiding a Red Devils side featuring the likes of Paul Pogba, Anthony Martial, and Alexis Sanchez to a second-placed finish in the 2017/18 season.

His claim is a nod to just how difficult it has been to manage United in recent times, and the current side is hardly any better, giving Pochettino an almighty job to restore some stability. But when asked about the Argentine's ability to take on a big European club back in 2019, the current Real Madrid boss said, as per The Independent:

“I think he has [what it takes to manage a big European club]. I think he is already at a big club as a start. I look at Spurs' history and I don't see a small club at all. I see a big club with big expectations and dreams to achieve.

“I think he's having that experience already at the highest level, Premier League and Tottenham Hotspur, we are speaking about high level football club, high level competition. So yes, I see him at Spurs for many years. I also see him leaving and having a very good career at other clubs.”

Mourinho has come up against Pochettino on 16 separate occasions throughout his career, during his spells at Madrid, Chelsea and United - recording 11 wins, one draw and suffering just four defeats.

With Pochettino having had eight years to nurture his craft - having managed PSG, Chelsea, and USMNT since leaving north London - his latest switch back to England is certainly his biggest leap of faith yet.

Based on his success elsewhere - which includes gradually transforming an underperforming Tottenham side and leading them all the way to a Champions League final - it would be easy to assume Pochettino is the right man to steady a United side desperately short on confidence.

His outstanding record of developing individual talent and his history of building excellent team cultures make him an ideal candidate to lead this group forward. But perhaps the bigger question these days is whether United themselves are prepared to give their next boss the tools he needs to work a few miracles.

From Mikel Arteta to David Moyes, all current Premier League managers have been ranked from best to worst.

One of the biggest issues during Michael Carrick's reign - as was the case with the likes of Ole Gunnar Solskjaer and Erik ten Hag - is that recruitment and the club's hierarchy have seemingly had a different vision for the team to the man tasked with managing it. It's hardly a recipe for success when everyone isn't singing from the same hymn sheet.

Some world-class managers have come and gone through Old Trafford's revolving doors, yet the problems have remained remarkably familiar. So what can Pochettino really do differently to Mourinho to turn United's fortunes around, not just for the remainder of the season, but in the long run too?
