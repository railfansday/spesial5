Warriors vs. Grizzlies player grades: Charles Bassey posts a double-double

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Well, that was yet another dud. The Golden State Warriors completed a winless five-game road trip by losing 126-98 to the Memphis Grizzlies, suffering their fourth consecutive defeat of 20 or more points. How fun!

I thought the Warriors played better than the score would suggest, and just couldn't overcome a 5-for-29 three-point shooting night, but still, one thing is painfully clear: this team will need a miracle just to tread water while Steph Curry is sidelined.

With that, let's speed run some grades. As always, grades are based on my expectations for each player, with a "B" grade representing the average performance for that player.

Note: True-shooting percentage (TS) is a scoring efficiency metric that accounts for threes and free throws. Entering Thursday's games, league-average TS was 57.5%.

33 minutes, 11 points, 8 rebounds, 7 assists, 1 steal, 2 blocks, 3 turnovers, 2 fouls, 3-for-11 shooting, 1-for-4 threes, 4-for-4 free throws, 43.1% TS, -31

It was unfair to expect Lendeborg to carry the Warriors in Curry's absence and, unfortunately, we're seeing why. He's been the team's first option, and the opponent's focal point, in all four games since Curry was injured … and he's shot just 19-for-67 in those games.

I did love the way that he stayed committed to other elements of the game in this one. He was so active on the glass, and had some of his best passes. It was honestly a solid all-around game for Lendeborg, save for the lack of scoring (which really hurts his grade), and he didn't have his best defensive game, either.

Bassey as easily one of the best players on the court for the Warriors, and one of the only ones who could score reliably. He shot 6-for-7, while his teammates combined to go 27-for-73. He got into foul trouble early, as fouls and turnovers continue to be the thing he is struggling with the most as he steps into a bigger role. But can't argue too much with someone having a highly-efficient 16 and 13.

The Warriors had one of the worst shooting performances from deep that you'll ever see and, unfortunately, Podz was the biggest culprit. He just couldn't buy a jumper, though it didn't stop him from trying. He had a few brilliant passes in this game, but otherwise it was just a really rough outing from him.

25 minutes, 9 points, 1 rebound, 5 assists, 2 steals, 2 blocks, 2 turnovers, 5 fouls, 3-for-10 shooting, 1-for-4 threes, 2-for-2 free throws, 41.4% TS, -14

Melton slid back into the starting lineup with very mixed results. He was once again extremely disruptive on the defensive side of things, as he has been on so many occasions this year. And he did a good job setting up his teammates. But he was a bit out of control for far too many possessions: he swiped a little recklessly on defense, drawing five fouls; he had some careless passes, some of which went for turnovers and others that he was lucky didn't; and he took some ill-advised shots early in the shot clock.

Melton showed a lot of talent in this game, but not as much veteran poise as the Warriors need.

27 minutes, 15 points, 5 rebounds, 2 turnovers, 1 foul, 5-for-8 shooting, 1-for-3 threes, 4-for-4 free throws, 76.8% TS, -13

Another game where Richard was both an absolute terror on defense and way more impactful on offense than we expect him to be. He didn't technically register any steals in this game, but his defensive excellence was the primary reason behind multiple Grizzlies turnovers. And for the second game in a row, he was one of the only Warriors who could do anything on offense.

He's endearing himself to his teammates and coaches by playing through the UCL tear in his thumb, but even more importantly, he's proving that he's a player who deserves a big workload even when the team is healthy.
