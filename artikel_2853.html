Steelers Coach Fires Back at Browns Over Controversial Body Slam on Defensive End

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

The Pittsburgh Steelers and Cleveland Browns continue to disagree over Mason McCormick's takedown of Jared Verse during their November 1 game. The incident drew criticism from Cleveland and prompted a response from Pittsburgh special teams coordinator Danny Crossman.

Crossman rejected the Browns' claims, while Verse expressed frustration over the play and his teammates' response.

Pittsburgh Steelers special teams coordinator Danny Crossman has rejected the Cleveland Browns' accusation that guard Mason McCormick intentionally hit Jared Verse during a special teams play. The comments followed criticism from Browns special teams coordinator Byron Storer after the teams' November 1 meeting.

Oct 18, 2026; Tampa, Florida, USA; Pittsburgh Steelers guard Mason McCormick (66) looks on before the game against the Tampa Bay Buccaneers at Raymond James Stadium. Mandatory Credit: Kim Klement Neitzel-Imagn Images

Speaking to reporters Monday, Crossman said, "There is never, ever going to be an intent to hurt in the National Football League… So I think (the suggestion that that was what that was) is nonsense."

The incident occurred during the first quarter of Pittsburgh's 27–6 win over Cleveland. On Chris Boswell's extra-point attempt, Verse jumped to block the kick, but McCormick grabbed him in midair and brought him to the ground. Officials penalized McCormick 15 yards for unnecessary roughness. Verse recorded one assisted tackle and no sacks in the game, while McCormick started at guard for the Steelers and played the entire contest.

Verse later expressed his frustration with McCormick's actions, saying, "To do something like that, I just can't have respect for that."

He was also unhappy with how quickly his teammates responded after McCormick brought him down. On November 5, Verse said he had spoken with his teammates about the incident and made his feelings clear.

McCormick, meanwhile, said he did not know who he had brought to the ground. Explaining his actions, he said, "I really had no idea who it was. I just felt somebody jump and we're told to get jumpers' hands down, and that's really it."

Storer criticized McCormick's actions, saying an attempt to stop a kick block did not justify taking an opponent's legs out and bringing him down. He viewed the play as different from the usual effort to prevent an opponent from blocking a kick.

Crossman rejected that interpretation, maintaining that players do not take the field intending to hurt opponents.

Although McCormick received a penalty during the game, a November 7 report confirmed that the NFL did not issue a fine.
