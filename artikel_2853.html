SpaceX Stock Is Down 39% From Its High. Here's What Has to Happen Before I'd Buy.

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

SpaceX's business is thriving -- but something needs to happen before it becomes a buy in my book.

By Steven Porrello – Nov 23, 2026 at 10:00PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Space Exploration Technologies ›

It's a bit of a riddle: A stock loses nearly 40% of its value, yet still looks expensive by traditional metrics. If that rings a bell, you're likely thinking of the predicament facing investors interested in Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +4.35%), better known as SpaceX.

SpaceX shares have dropped to $136, a roughly 39% decline from an all-time high of about $225. Even after that considerable decline, SpaceX still carries a sizable valuation -- about $1.9 trillion at this writing.

What would it take for me to buy SpaceX stock? Has its recent decline brought it any closer to that point?

SpaceX is a behemoth of a business. Three-headed, fast-growing, and formidable, it operates across three segments: rockets and spacecraft (Space), a global communications network (Starlink), and artificial intelligence (Super Intelligence).

Most people would recognize the first category. Rockets and spacecraft are, in fact, SpaceX's bread and butter, but they're not the business's breadwinner -- at least, not by revenue. That distinction belongs to its AI division, which it calls Super Intelligence. The business generated an impressive $7.6 billion in revenue in the third quarter alone. Connectivity comes second, with $5.2 billion, while rockets and spacecraft contributed about $1.8 billion.

Revenue growth is rarely a thumbs-down, but it's even sweeter when the company keeps more of those extra dollars. Here, too, SpaceX gets a gold star: It reported $4.4 billion in net income for Q3, up substantially from a $1.1 billion loss the previous year. That's a great turnaround for a company investing so heavily in its future -- so why am I not investing in SpaceX today?

Previously, my concern about SpaceX stock was its valuation. That is still my concern; the stock trades at roughly 60 times trailing sales, more than 16 times the S&P 500 (^GSPC +4.73%). Now there's another thing holding me back -- the cost of its growth.

During the first nine months of 2026, SpaceX generated about $13 billion in operating cash flow but spent a staggering $48 billion on capital expenditures, roughly $40 billion of which was on its AI business. That works out to about $35 billion in negative free cash flow for the year -- so far. Management is expecting to spend another $35 billion to $40 billion in the fourth quarter alone.

Obviously, it isn't a bad thing to spend aggressively to grow a business -- and SpaceX has three of them. What worries me is the risks that come with spending billions on AI infrastructure, where the company faces some pretty deep-pocketed competitors. They include Microsoft (MSFT +1.65%), Amazon (AMZN +3.47%), and Alphabet (GOOGL +6.34%) (GOOG +5.88%), just to name the big ones. True, SpaceX has shown that it can compete in this market, but whether it can create the same strong competitive advantages that have made it dominant in rockets and satellite communications remains to be seen.

With tens of billions being thrown at AI, I want some proof that the investment will pay off. Indeed, at a lower valuation, I might be willing to take that risk, but at 60 times trailing sales -- no thanks. I'm fine letting this one play out for now.
