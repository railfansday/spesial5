Wake Forest Takes Apart Georgia Tech, 50-29

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

It wasn't pretty or artistic. While it was a blowout, it certainly was not the same type of route Wake Forest has been delivering of late; it was good enough to get the job done. Wake Forest takes apart Georgia Tech, 50-29, and gets one game closer to a spot in the ACC championship game.

The weather was dark, gloomy, and sometimes wet during the game. The mood on the field matched. The game was chippy from the outset. Before the game, Wake players were headed to gather in their end zone. It just so happened to be at the same time Georgia Tech was coming out of its tunnel. Words were exchanged, but nothing else.

And then Wake scored early and often, and some of the aesthetic beauty of the game went by the wayside. By the end, penalty flags became the common sight throughout much of the second half. Wake ended the game with 12 penalties for 111 yards. The conference office can count on hearing from Wake head coach Jake Dickert on a few of them.

Wake scored on its first drive of the game, which has become the norm lately. Ty Clark III pushed his way up the middle from four yards out for the touchdown, capping a 12-play, 78-yard drive. The drive featured everything that has been Gio Lopez this season. The drive was a mix of completed passes to three different receivers, some of his own runs (both by design and scrambling), and managing the offense.

Georgia Tech, knowing it was going to have to empty the bag of tricks to stay with Wake, went to the flea flicker on its next series. Dalen Penson had picked up 24 yards to the Wake 32 after making the catch, but he was hit by Deuce Blades and fumbled. It was Blades who also recovered the ball.

The ensuing drive was another mix of run and pass plays, with Clark picking up 16 of the 68 total yards on the drive. It took 10 plays before Lopez ran a quarterback keeper to the front left corner of the end zone from two yards out. Wake ran a two-point conversion, as it does at least once a game, and the lead was up to 15-0.

The Deacs run game was again decisive. Out of the 607 yards of total offense, 261 were on the ground. Deuce Lawrence led the running backs with 105 yards and a touchdown on 13 carries. Clark finished the game with 98 yards and two touchdowns on 18 carries.

The lead grew in the second quarter. Lopez threw a pass out in the flat to Jack Foley. The defender dove at the wideout and missed. That cleared a path for Foley to weave his way through traffic for a 66-yard touchdown. The 50-second scoring drive put the score at 22-0 early in the second quarter.

Justice Haynes fumbled away GA Tech's next possession at the Yellowjackets' 47-yard line. Aiden Hall recovered the ball, and the Deacs were on the offensive attack again.

Lopez threw deep to Carlos Hernandez, who made a spinning catch in the air at the Georgia Tech eight-yard line. Clark needed just two carries to cover that remaining eight yards. It was the second consecutive Wake Forest drive that took only 50 seconds, and the Deacs lead was now up to 29-0.

Then the unthinkable happened. Georgia Tech put together a scoring drive. Wake Forest had outscored its last five opponents by a total of 181-7 in the first half. But the chippy tone of the game worked against Wake late in the second quarter. Thirty yards of penalties (roughing the passer against Langston Hardy and an unsportsmanlike penalty against Aiden Hall), gave Georgia Tech nearly half of the yards it needed in its 75-yard scoring drive.

Haynes pushed his way across the goal line from a yard out, and Wake had given up first-half points since the North Carolina State game in mid-October.

Wake added one more touchdown before the end of the first half, when Lopez ran a quarterback draw from two yards out to cap a drive that went all of 49 yards in five plays and 58 seconds. The lead was now 36-7, and the game was over for all intents and purposes.

And while that was still true throughout the second half, getting there was not as easy as it had been in recent weeks for Wake. Dickert left many of his starters on defense in for the second half. The Yellowjackets put up 245 yards of offense compared to 236 for Wake Forest. They outscored the Deacs 22-14 in the second half, including 7-0 in the fourth quarter.

That's not to say there were not high points in the second half for Wake. Lopez engineered a nine-play, 75-yard scoring drive. From the 14-yard line, Lopez stepped up in the pocket to avoid the rush, and when he did, he saw a wide-open Dom Barry in the middle of the end zone for the touchdown and the 43-7 lead.

Wake's last points came thanks to the running clinic from Lawrence. He had runs of six, 12, nine, six, and three yards as part of an eight-play 70-yard drive to make it 50-15 at the time.

Dickert made an interesting point after the game about what happened in the second half. He called it a muted celebration in the locker room after the game. "It's the first time we've lost a half of football in a long time."

Dickert gave due credit to Georgia Tech for its second-half game plan. "I thought they used good schematics. I thought they used their personnel well and combatted some of the things that we were doing," Dickert said. "We've got to adjust a little bit because now we are going to see that play [the wildcat] the rest of the season."

Dickert said the next 24 hours will be a mix of celebrating and taking a critical look at the penalties and mistakes. "We'll celebrate the positive things. But we'll look really critically at the things we have to do that once again will get you beat in a four-quarter game."

Wake hasn't had one of those in several weeks. The regular season finale is Saturday at 3:30 pm against a Duke team that has one of the best running backs in the country, but got beat at home by Clemson on Friday night.
