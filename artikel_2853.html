Jim Cramer says Costco's hefty special dividend signals ‘more gains ahead’

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Costco is rewarding shareholders with a $30-per-share special dividend, and CNBC's Jim Cramer says history suggests that investors should stick around despite the catalyst moving into the rearview mirror.

The warehouse retailer announced its sixth special dividend last week, alongside a $4 billion share repurchase program. Shareholders of record as of Nov. 24 will receive the payout on Dec. 4. The company also pays a regular quarterly dividend of $1.47 that yields just 0.5% annually.

"With all due respect to the buyback program, which represents less than 1% of Costco's $490 billion market cap, it's the special dividend that I'm excited about," the "Mad Money" host said. "The sporadic special dividend is one of the unique privileges of owning Costco shares."

Costco issued its first special dividend in 2012 and has announced five more since then, with payouts arriving roughly every three years over the past decade. Together, those six payments have totaled $74 per share, on top of the company's regular quarterly dividend.

To be sure, given Costco's track record of these periodic extra payouts, some investors may view last week's announcement as a "buy the rumor, sell the news" situation. However, Cramer said in each of the past three instances — 2023, 2020 and 2017 — Costco shares have outperformed the S&P 500 over the next year.

"That's a very good verdict for those who wonder what to do with the stock now that the good news is out," Cramer said. "It says hold on, don't sell. More gains ahead."

There's also compelling math suggesting that investors should look to reinvest those dividends back into Costco's stock.

Cramer looked at investor returns over the past 15 years. A $1,000 investment in Costco's stock in November 2011 would have grown to approximately $13,321 through Friday, reflecting a 1,232% gain. But reinvesting all dividends into additional shares would have increased that investment's value to roughly $18,411, representing a 1,741% total return.

That's more than $5,000 in additional gains from reinvesting dividends rather than collecting the cash. Special dividends played an outsized role, accounting for roughly 64% of the $116 per share Costco paid in total dividends over that period. "That's an incredible case made for reinvesting of dividends," Cramer said.

"The sporadic special dividends have boosted this stock's total shareholder return meaningfully over the past decade-and-a-half, and they help keep you invested in the stock when it has its occasional lulls," he said.

Beyond boosting returns, the prospect of another special dividend gives investors an incentive to hold Costco through periods of weakness. That patience paid off this year, when shares retreated from nearly $1,100 in May into the high $800s before rebounding sharply this fall. The stock is now up 30% for the year.

"Sometimes I think that the stock's too expensive and it might be a good time to take profits," he said. "But then I remind myself that there's a good chance of a special dividend right around the corner, and it's so much easier to stick it out."
