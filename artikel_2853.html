As Cowboys strip DC Christian Parker of play-calling, Brian Schottenheimer asserts stakes: 'If we don't make the playoffs, it's a failure'

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

In Houston and Denver, the decisions took just four games this season.

Head coaches DeMeco Ryans and Sean Payton stripped their defensive and offensive coordinator of play-calling responsibilities in early October. An 0-4 start for Houston and a 2-2 start in Denver convinced each man at the helm to send his unit in a different direction.

Against that backdrop, the Dallas Cowboys stripping defensive coordinator Christian Parker of play-calling this week seems less rash. But Parker was still just 10 games into his play-calling career when Dallas transferred that responsibility to senior defensive assistant Mike Pettine, whose experience includes eight seasons as a defensive coordinator and two as head coach.

Pettine will call defensive plays beginning this week against the Tennessee Titans, Cowboys head coach Brian Schottenheimer confirmed Wednesday.

"I plan on him being the play-caller for the year," Schottenheimer added.

Dallas' Christian Parker won't be making the primary defensive calls on gameday.

It's a drastic shift from the confidence and excitement that surrounded the 34-year-old Parker coming out of training camp, when a highly vocal and schematically multiple group of defenders had Dallas brass wondering whether they could solve multi-year defensive woes.

Parker has not fixed the Cowboys' defense as the unit ranks third-worst in points allowed per game (28.2) and worst in yards (393.8). But consider that last season's Cowboys ranked worst in points allowed and third-worst in yards a year after the Cowboys ranked second worst in points allowed and bottom-five in yards.

The issues predate Parker. The fix remains elusive.

Still, after ceding 46 points and a Cowboys home record-worst 584 yards to the San Francisco 49ers, Parker's role has been reduced. He will continue to run meetings and lead walkthroughs during the week even as his play-calling input will shrink to secondary.

Schottenheimer acknowledged that the urgency is high for the 4-6 group that holds just a 29% chance of making the playoffs, per Next Gen Stats projections.

"If we don't make the playoffs, it's a failure," Schottenheimer said. "The season's a failure. That's what we play for. We play to win and we play to make the playoffs, and we've got seven games left. "We need to put together a run of success."

In aggregate, the Cowboys have produced on offense. Their 28.6 points per game rank fifth-best in the league and their 368.3 yards rank eighth.

But Dallas has not beaten a team on track to make the playoffs.

The Cowboys' run game has jelled inconsistently, and their ability to finish games deserves questions on both sides of the ball.

Even as Dallas shifts from Parker to Pettine this week, early competition will only test improvement to a degree. The 1-8 Tennessee Titans visit AT&T Stadium this week, followed by the 2-7 Philadelphia Eagles on Thanksgiving.

Then Dallas' tougher stretch will come: the Seattle Seahawks before the Cowboys' bye, followed by the Los Angeles Rams, Jacksonville Jaguars and New York Giants.

How intentional was shifting defensive play-callers before a lighter schedule stretch?

"Not at all," Schottenheimer said. "Because again, we live in the moment. This decision was based off of the last few weeks and just looking at what was best for the team and not about who you're playing."

The last few weeks, for Dallas, have featured an overtime loss against the Arizona Cardinals despite a 14-point lead early in the fourth quarter followed by a game against the trade deadline-selling Indianapolis Colts that required a fourth-quarter comeback to eke out a win.

Technically, the Cowboys lost by only 6 points on Sunday to the 49ers. But San Francisco led 38-19 early in the fourth quarter and never trailed in the second half.

At least as damaging: The 49ers scored touchdowns on plays of 74, 70 and 66 yards.

The Cowboys have allowed nine plays of 50+ yards this season and 47 of 20+, per Pro Football Reference data.

Schottenheimer met with Parker on Monday afternoon to discuss the challenges.

"It was very honest," Schottenheimer said. "It was very forthright. We have that type of relationship that we can do that. And so, slept on it, picked [it] up again Tuesday morning, having really good conversations, talking about different things. And then the decision was made mid-morning on Tuesday."

Schottenheimer alerted the Cowboys' staff on Tuesday followed by his players on Wednesday. He continued to preach support of Parker even as he demoted him.

Does that mean Parker could get his play-calling job back?

"I mean again, my focus right now is just trying to beat the Titans," Schottenheimer said. "But like I said, I think CP has a very, very bright future as a coach, but more specifically a very bright future here as our defense coordinator. …

"He's a critical piece of what we're trying to do as we search for answers and solutions."

The remainder of the Cowboys' season will influence not only Parker's coaching future but also Schottenheimer's.

The Cowboys have not won consecutive games since last November. In two seasons, Schottenheimer has led the team to an 11-15-1 overall record.

An offense featuring Dak Prescott, CeeDee Lamb and George Pickens has flashed high ceilings but inconsistency. A defense that struggled under one-and-done coordinator Matt Eberflus last year has flailed for solutions.

Dallas shifted its defensive structure from a 4-3 to a 3-4 base for the first time in more than a decade this offseason. Personnel has turned over significantly, from trades for interior defensive lineman Quinnen Williams at last year's trade deadline and cornerback Joey Porter Jr. this October, to draft investments that included trading up for first-round safety Caleb Downs and doubling up in the first round with edge rusher Malachi Lawrence.

Missed tackles and communication errors are among the areas Schottenheimer said the team is emphasizing with the message of "doing simple better."

The Cowboys have allowed a league-high 1,350 yards after the catch, per Next Gen Stats. Pro Football Focus, an advanced site that does not know team assignments but does evaluate each team's game film, credits Dallas with 93 missed tackles through 10 games.

For context: PFF credits its top-graded tackling team this season, the Detroit Lions, with 52 missed tackles. PFF's lowest-graded tackling team, the Denver Broncos, have missed 123, per the site. The Cowboys rank 25th in tackling, per PFF.

Can Pettine, who hasn't called defensive plays since 2020, improve the unit?

Schottenheimer credited the 60-year-old as valuable for his perspective on how to use personnel and "really big on affecting the quarterback and pressuring the quarterback and he wants to get the offense unsettled."

Schottenheimer acknowledged his own accountability for the defensive woes, while saying he believes the staff change is a "delicate matter" but ultimately "fair."

When asked if Parker was akin to a young quarterback who could learn from a step back, Schottenheimer said he saw some merit in the comparison but "I don't think Christian Parker's being put on the bench."

Without improvement, Schottenheimer knows questions will grow about whether he will be.

"When you're in this situation and you're the head coach of a 4-6 football team, I mean, you're aware there's always going to be noise," Schottenheimer said. "But I don't focus on that. I really don't. I focus on trying to get these guys ready, trying to get them prepared, and that's my job and my sole responsibility."
