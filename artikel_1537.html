Aston Villa's Champions League clash with Galatasaray plunged into chaos as Turkish police hunt for a fugitive LION spotted 10 miles from the stadium

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Published: 12:24 EST, 23 November 2026 | Updated: 18:09 EST, 23 November 2026

A lion has been spotted about ten miles from the stadium where Aston Villa are due to face Galatasaray in the Champions League on Tuesday.

In a bizarre development, Turkish security and wildlife personnel were on Monday searching for the animal​, believed to be roaming loose ‌in woodland north of Istanbul.

Around 70 people, including police and other emergency personnel, have been involved in the search, with seven drones deployed. The animal was first sighted in Istanbul's Sariyer district, which is also where Galatasaray's RAMS Park is located.

According to local reports, personnel at a police station in ​the Zekeriyakoy area, north of the ​European side of Istanbul, reported seeing 'an animal ⁠resembling a lion' on surveillance cameras at ​around 9pm local time on Sunday (6pm GMT).

Extensive security measures have been put in place around the forested area where the animal is believed to be located and nearby villas. Access to and from the area is being controlled, while residents have been warned to remain cautious.

'The area where the lion is located has been identified, and every effort is being made to capture it alive,' the Istanbul Governor's Office said in a statement.

Reaction to the lion's escape bordered on the surreal, with one outlet interviewing a person in a lion suit, wearing a Galatasaray shirt, about it. Galatasaray are nicknamed the Lions while Villa have a lion on their badge.

Gala defender Abdulkerim Bardakci was even asked about the matter, and replied: 'I want to say something because the whole country is talking about this right now. There's only one lion I keep at home, and that's my son.'

Daily Mail Sport has contacted UEFA for an update.

There were even suggestions in Turkey that Villa had cancelled their training session at RAMS Park because of the lion, but Villa did their preparation at Bodymoor Heath for a very different reason – the state of the RAMS Park pitch.

European football chiefs are confident the playing surface will pose no risk to Aston Villa after conducting thorough inspections.

The Turkish giants have commissioned intensive work on the pitch and moved their most recent league game, against Samsunspor, to the stadium of rivals Istanbul Basaksehir.

Yet it is thought conditions will be some way from perfect and though Villa boss Unai Emery played it down, he will be anxious about injuries. 'We have to think that if there was a risk of injury, we wouldn't be playing on it,' he said. 'The pitch will be better or worse, for both teams.'

Villa defender Ian Maatsen said: 'We don't know anything about the pitch. They say it's in a little bit of a bad condition but that's no excuse. We have to see how the pitch is. We are ready.'

Galatasaray coach Okan Buruk added: 'It was dug up again, another hybrid treatment was applied, and seeds were planted. There is also sand mixed into the pitch to help.'

Villa, who have been followed abroad by Prince William this season, will take on Galatasaray

Aston Villa shocked Europe by marching straight into the last 16 in the Champions League two seasons ago but a win over Galatasaray will put them on course to repeat it.

Unai Emery’s side have nine points from a possible 12 in the league phase and victory in Istanbul will give them one foot in the knockout rounds.

Villa are back in their happy place. This city hosted their 3-0 win over Freiburg in the Europa League Final last May, though that game happened at Tupras Stadium, home of Besiktas.

Repeat that result here and Villa will be eyeing a top-eight finish – which they also achieved two years ago. That would allow them to skip the play-off round early next year and head straight into the last 16.

Five months into his loan spell from Chelsea, Alejandro Garnacho is still waiting for his first competitive start for Aston Villa.

When the Argentine moved to Villa Park, it was suggested that turning the temporary move into a £43million permanent deal would be a relative formality. Right now, that feels nearly impossible. Garnacho has featured for only 95 minutes this term and if Emery really did want the 22-year-old last summer, he has a funny way of showing it.

Indeed, Daily Mail Sport understands that even during Garnacho’s early days at the club, some Villa players were starting to wonder whether he would ever play regularly for Emery.

What next? There is believed to be no recall clause in the loan deal with Chelsea yet as Garnacho has only represented Villa this term, the clubs could agree to cancel the arrangement in January.

Born in Madrid, Garnacho was part of the youth system at Atletico before joining Manchester United. With four Argentines in the Atletico squad, plus another in boss Diego Simeone, that might be worth keeping an eye on.

Yet it is thought that neither Galatasaray nor Fenerbahce, who were monitoring Garnacho last summer, are in the market for a high-wage signing in January. That would close off one potential escape route.

Thanks to the sales of Morgan Rogers, Ezri Konsa and Youri Tielemans that brought in nearly £200million, Villa were able to spend heavily in the summer.

To invest again in the winter, they will probably need to attract offers for some fringe players. As well as Garnacho, Tammy Abraham and Lamare Bogarde have been overlooked by Emery recently. Abraham has fallen behind Brian Madjo in the pecking order, though the 17-year-old was not included in the Champions League squad, while Bogarde’s only starts have been in the Carabao Cup, which is always low on Emery’s priority list. Selling Bogarde would also deliver pure profit – crucial for a club forever fighting to stay in line with Premier League and UEFA spending rules.

There were always going to be bumps in the road this season for Villa, and there will likely be more. In last Saturday’s dispiriting 0-0 draw with Sunderland, there were only two players – Emi Buendia and Pau Torres – in the starting XI who were also in the line-up for the Europa League Final. Replacing the six who departed will take time.

In their commendable attempts to bring down the average age of the squad, Villa find themselves with a surplus of attackers but no midfield controller since the departure of Tielemans, and they also look vulnerable in central defence.

Tyrone Mings turns 34 in March and Victor Lindelof was 32 in July while Taylor Harwood-Bellis must find his feet quickly in one of the most demanding competitions in the world. It is tough without Konsa.

Elsewhere, there are possibly too many options. George Hemmings and then Alysson have forced their way into the side at different times this season, meaning fewer opportunities for Ibrahim Mbaye, who cost nearly £50million from Paris Saint-Germain last summer. Johan Manzambi has played regularly and with John McGinn and Buendia also in the mix, a player like Garnacho has a near-impossible task to convince Emery.

Scouts from across Europe have been watching Galatasaray’s matches this season and Aleksey Batrakov is usually the reason why.

The 21-year-old Russian playmaker has six goals – including four in the Champions League – and four assists since his £21million move from Lokomotiv Moscow and is one of the breakout stars of the world game.

He caught the eye of Paris Saint-Germain while playing for Lokomotiv and Barcelona boss Hansi Flick has praised him.

The last Russian to play in the Premier League was Pavel Pogrebnyak, whose last game for Reading was against West Ham in May 2013.

Given the current geopolitical situation, Pogrebnyak will surely hold that record for some time to come.
