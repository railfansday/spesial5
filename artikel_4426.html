HoYoverse is one such studio, never shy about trying something new with both original titles and new entries in their larger Honkai franchise. The newest title in that franchise, Honkai: Nexus Anima, is approaching its final closed beta, implementing full playable versions of its creature acquiring, base building, and auto-battler combat, a far cry from the action RPGs and turn-based trades the franchise is now known for.

At this point it’s becoming a little difficult to define the “Honkai style” when it comes to actual gameplay, a fact HoYoverse doesn’t seem all that afraid of. While the original heavy hitter for the studio, Honkai: Third Impact, was an action RPG, the success of Honkai: Star Rail has made it clear that what fans really expect from the name Honkai is the sci-fi trappings and HoYoverse's seal of quality the studio is known for.

Continuing the trend of constant change, Honkai: Nexus Anima feels like a mix of creature catcher and auto-chess, a wild fusion of Pokémon and Teamfight Tactics that feels strangely perfect. The game’s final closed beta, which is currently open for registration and starts on November 26th, will be as close to launch-feature complete as possible, showcased in a new gameplay video that goes through all the major mechanics.

Unlike other HoYoverse titles that ask players to pull for both characters and their weapons, Nexus Anima focuses on both larger characters with unique abilities, known as Eidos, and the creatures used to make up teams, called Anima.

Obtaining an Eidos will grant an immediate copy of their matching Anima, offering a series power boost and making pulling for Eidos directly more outwardly profitable. Alongside pulling for some banner Anima, many can be found and caught directly in the wild, providing the basis for your team to be empowered by higher-tiered creatures.

Alongside the auto-battler combat, Nexus Anima puts a major focus on exploration and personal base building, allowing you to spend time with your Anima and potentially attract extra. Anima also provide a variety of exploration benefits as you catch them, serving as mounts for faster movement or mobility tools to open up otherwise inaccessible areas.

HoYoverse is seriously skilled at making beautiful, out-of-this-world settings that fuse fantasy and technology to great effect. Nexus Anima seems to be their answer to other exploration-focused titles that have released recently, with the extra spin of creature collection doing wonders to incentivize that exploration.

While it may not always work, with proper care, these titles can shift from trend-chasers to iterations with proper value, introducing fans, new and old, to all-new ways to play.

Despite how much HoYoverse has done to define the baseline of the modern Gacha space, it’s great to see them really shake things up, offering a truly different vibe than the Action RPG hybrids that dominate the space. While the rest of the genre is trying to play catch-up, HoYoverse is giving the people what they never knew they wanted.

Competing with HoYoverse polish is a struggle for their biggest competitors, and while an auto-battler may seem like a small market, the success of Teamfight Tactics across the globe makes it clear there’s a market. Nexus Anima’s cozy side offerings make it the perfect choice for the gamer who just wants something low-stress to log into after a long day, a niche that’s only grown over the years.
