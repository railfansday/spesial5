Wolves sent Marshall Munetsi to France - what happened next changed everything

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Bethany Patrick is a football writer based in Manchester, UK.

Her work focuses on the EFL, with particular interest in football governance, supporter culture, and fan experience and access.

Bethany is a director of the award-winning Sheffield Wednesday Women’s Supporters Group, where she works on fan engagement, strategy and campaigns for greater inclusion across the game.

She regularly represents supporters in governance spaces across Europe, including government panels and discussions around football regulation and the supporter's voice.

She has previously worked as a researcher for The Stretford Paddock and is a regular contributor to The Wednesday Week.

Wolves’ £16m signing of Marshall Munetsi had threatened to become an expensive false start.

Having arrived from Reims in February 2025, the Zimbabwe international initially established himself under Vítor Pereira, making 12 Premier League starts during the second half of the campaign.

But his influence diminished following the Portuguese manager’s departure, with Rob Edwards ultimately sanctioning a loan move to Paris FC in January.

It was a decision that offered Munetsi the chance to rediscover his confidence and regular playing time.

Now back at Molineux, he has become an important fixture in Cesar Peixoto’s side.

Munetsi arrived at Wolves from Stade de Reims for a reported £16m in February 2025, becoming the most expensive Zimbabwean footballer in history in the process.

His physical presence and ability to contribute at both ends of the pitch made him an intriguing addition - and he wasted little time making an impression.

He scored his first goal for the club against Everton in March and became a regular under Pereira.

However, the managerial change that followed proved less favourable for the midfielder.

After Edwards took charge of Wolves, Munetsi found opportunities increasingly difficult to come by, making just one start before being allowed to return to France on loan on 16 January 2026.

For a player who had arrived with considerable expectations, it was a disappointing turn of events.

Yet, rather than allowing his confidence to suffer further on the fringes of the squad, the move to Paris offered a valuable opportunity to get his career moving again.

Wolves should try and sign Chelsea's Ryan Kavuma-McQueen, who is also on the radar at Sheffield United due to his performances at Portsmouth.

The change of scenery appeared to suit Munetsi.

He made 15 Ligue 1 appearances for Paris FC during the second half of the season, scoring four goals and reminding Wolves of the qualities that had prompted them to invest so heavily in him.

His performances also ensured that his future remained an interesting talking point heading into the summer.

With Wolves preparing for their first season outside the Premier League in nine years, Munetsi returned to Molineux facing another opportunity to establish himself. Under Peixoto, he has taken it.

The 30-year-old has featured in all 15 of Wolves' Championship fixtures so far, starting 14, and has become a consistent presence in midfield alongside Andre.

His experience and willingness to do the less glamorous work have proved valuable in a division where the demands are considerably different.

Munetsi's resurgence is part of a wider pattern at Molineux, with several players returning from loan spells.

Fer Lopez has arguably been the most prominent, emerging as Wolves' leading Championship goalscorer following his return from Celta Vigo.

Nasser Djiga and Tommy Doyle have also added further options following spells at Rangers and Birmingham City, respectively.

Wolves have responded to the disappointment of relegation with an impressive start to the Championship season, sitting fourth during the November international break and just one point off the summit.

Munetsi believes the returning players have helped change the atmosphere around the club after a difficult period.

"With the difficulties we had last year, it was tough for everybody at the club, but us coming back, we came with a bit of freshness," he explained.

Wolves' decision to send him to France may initially have looked like an admission that the £16m investment had not worked out.

Instead, however, it has helped give them an important player for the challenge ahead.
