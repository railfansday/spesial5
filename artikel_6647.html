The Best Christmas Rom-Coms of the 2000s

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Name a better feeling than this: you’ve got a plate of picky bits piled high, a glass of fizz in hand, a fire on, your phone Bricked, and a film you’ve seen one hundred times lined up. Winter gets a bad rap, for many reasons, but this Christmas scene? A true flow state.

For some reason, Christmas is the only time of year when we’re not just willing to watch films we could recite by heart while half-asleep—it seems to be a centerpiece of the holiday.

For many of us, those comfort watches simply have to be Christmas rom-coms (perhaps with a bit of Home Alone thrown in). There’s an unrivaled comfort to a love story that plays out to a stripped-back piano version of “Have Yourself a Merry Little Christmas,” some fake snow, and a will-they-won’t-they New Year’s countdown. Maybe it’s the champs talking, but you truly can’t beat it.

To that end, here are some of the best Christmas rom-coms of all time.

Based on the newspaper column and subsequent book by Helen Fielding, the story is told through diary entries by the titular Bridget Jones, played by Renée Zellweger (as you probably well know). Of course, Bridget is the blueprint for the frazzled Englishwoman trope—with her laddered tights, cooking mishaps, and pretty much always saying and doing slightly the wrong thing—but who could fail to fall for her? Certainly not Daniel Cleaver (Hugh Grant) or Mark Darcy (Colin Firth), whose rivalry culminates in one of the greatest slapstick brawls in cinematic history. So genuinely funny, heartwarming, and perfectly cast, we can even begrudgingly overlook the problematic fact that Bridget’s weight was a subplot of its own.

Starring Will Ferrell as an enormous yet child-like elf who was born human but grew up in the North Pole with Santa and his crew, this John Favreau-directed film became an instant classic upon release in 2003. With anyone else but Ferrell in the lead, this movie could have flopped, but he plays Buddy to perfection. (Hands up if you still quote, “Daddy, Daddy, I’m in love and I don’t care who knows it!”) Buddy travels to New York to find his real father, played by James Caan, who turns out to be a grump who has absolutely zero festive cheer. Luckily, his son is an elf who can help him discover the true meaning of Christmas—and turn his failing children’s book company around in the process. The rom part of this rom-com is courtesy of Zooey Deschanel, who plays the department store worker who falls for Buddy.

Christmas doesn’t officially start until you’ve done your annual rewatch of Richard Curtis’s Love, Actually (we don’t make the rules!). The ultimate ensemble movie, with its various subplots culminates in a grande finale involving a school nativity play, this film is like seven little films in one. There’s Martine McCutcheon as Downing Street staffer Natalie; Hugh Grant as the prime minister we’d all like in real life; Colin Firth as the stern, broken-hearted one who flees to France; Liam Neeson as the widower; Keira Knightley and Chiwetel Ejiofor as the newlyweds and Andrew Lincoln as their borderline-creepy best pal; Bill Nighy as a washed-up rocker; and Laura Linney nabbing the hottest office co-worker to ever live. Emma Thompson, meanwhile, delivers one of the most devastating scenes you’ll ever see (Joni Mitchell CDs were never the same again). Also featured: an ideal soundtrack (Dido, Gabrielle, Texas) that your mom would have cleaned to in the Noughties.

A highly-strung New Yorker, Meredith Morton, played by Sarah Jessica Parker, goes to Connecticut to meet her boyfriend Everett Stone’s bohemian, eccentric, and extremely close-knit family at their home for the holidays. Everett (Dermot Mulroney) plans to propose with a family heirloom (ahh! The family stone!), but after a rocky start, his family is going to need some persuading that Meredith is right for him…and them. It’s not your straightforward warm and fuzzy movie, but it’s far more unique than anything made today, and the cast really is stellar (the late Diane Keaton, Claire Danes, Rachel McAdams, and Luke Wilson all appear). One to rewatch before the sequel, The Families Stone (yes, really), drops in November 2027.

Nancy Meyers outdid herself with this one. The set-up is simple: Amanda (Cameron Diaz) and Iris (Kate Winslet) are suffering the most dreadful romantic woes thanks to the men in their lives, played by Edward Burns and Rufus Sewell. And so they find themselves on an early sort of Airbnb to initiate a house swap, with Amanda staying in Iris’s chocolate box Cotswolds home while Iris jets to Amanda’s Los Angeles mansion. They are on a mission to “find themselves” and, by golly, that involves some snogging. Iris falls for musician Miles, played by Jack Black, Amanda gets Graham, a widowed father-of-two played by Jude Law. It’s all so lovely, there’s rarely been a film more capable of calming one’s nervous system.

Not to be confused with Nancy Meyers’s bicontinental confection—nor with 2019’s Last Christmas, starring Emilia Clarke and Henry Golding and co-written by Emma Thompson—Last Holiday, directed by Wayne Wang (Joy Luck Club, Maid in Manhattan), is loosely based on a 1950 British film of the same name. This version stars Queen Latifah as Georgia Byrd, a selfless saleswoman from New Orleans who, upon learning she is terminally ill, books a lavish Christmastime visit to the Grandhotel Pupp in the Czech Republic, where she intends to squeeze every last indulgent moment out of the weeks she has left. If all that sounds a bit maudlin, trust that Queen Latifah makes an incredibly winning (and glamorous) leading lady, Timothy Hutton is delectable as a big-business villain, and LL Cool J is the love interest (!). (Yes, Gérard Depardieu is in there too, but don’t let him scare you off!)

I long for the weird days when Vince Vaughan starred in rom-coms. Sure, he’s a sort of unlikely leading man, but his ability to gruffly subvert a genre? No one does it like Vaughan. Four Christmases sees him star opposite fellow rom-com royalty Reese Witherspoon; they play Brad and Kate, a couple who avoid Christmas with their divorced parents each year by claiming to do charity work abroad (when they’re really going on a tropical holiday). One year, their flights get canceled because of widespread fog, their lie is outed by a news reporter on live TV, and they find themselves having to visit all four parents in a single day for four different Christmas celebrations.
