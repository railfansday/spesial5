‘Sentimental Value’ Casting Guru Recalls Meta Stellan Skarsgard and Elle Fanning Experience

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Yngvill Haga also discussed her rapport with Joachim Trier and Fanning's Oscar nomination during an appearance in the Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival's market program.

You may not know Yngvill Haga by name. But the likes of Stellan Skarsgard and her regular collaborator Joachim Trier do. And you very likely know the Norwegian’s work as a casting director on the likes of Trier’s Oscar winner Sentimental Value, The Worst Person in the World and Thelma.

On Wednesday, she took the spotlight, of sorts, herself in an appearance at the Industry@Tallinn & Baltic Event (ITBE), the market of the Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival (PÖFF) in Estonia, whose 30th edition runs through this weekend. Asked about how she first met Trier, Haga shared: “His producer at that time texted or called me. ‘Do you want to have a coffee with him?’ I didn’t know him, and I always want to meet directors that I don’t know, so yeah, sure. I don’t know who has seen Sentimental Value, but we met at Lorry’s, the restaurant where the father and daughter have this talk [in the film]. Joachim loves that place, and he pitched me for a film called Thelma.”

The casting expert not only liked the film but also their connection. “I immediately liked his pitch and also our way of communicating, talking together,” Haga said. “He hadn’t worked with a casting director before; he had put people together to do his casting, but now he wanted to try out something else, and so it started from there.”

Of course, Trier wrote Sentimental Value with Renate Reinsve in mind. “When I first met Joachim [about the film], he pitched the story again, and he said, in a kind of nervous way, ‘I’m starting to think about Stellan Skarsgard.’ He didn’t know him and had never worked with him, but had such huge respect.” Trier went to Stockholm and met the star, who then came on board.

Had Haga known the Swedish actor personally before Sentimental Value? “No, I met him during this process,” she noted.

The casting director also shared insight into how Elle Fanning ended up in the film and took her first trip to Oslo. “We worked with an American casting director, Avy Kaufman, and Joachim has an agent in the U.S. who, of course, knows all the big acting agents,” Haga explained why she wasn’t directly involved.

“I was talking to Joachim and saying who I liked, and I was super happy that it ended up being Elle Fanning — for many reasons,” she continued. The facts that she is a bit younger than the sisters and is blonde, while the sisters are dark-haired, already helped. “It’s just a very visually cool contrast,” Haga argued. “And I know that some of the actresses [Trier] was talking to didn’t like the role and didn’t want it because it wasn’t the lead. I personally really like her character, so I thought that was very strange. And Elle Fanning was like nothing like that. She saw the importance of the role and liked the role. Also, she got Oscar-nominated, so she was very grateful and moved.”

Haga also recalled Fanning’s first visit to Oslo to do fittings and rehearsals. “The house that’s in the film is a house in Oslo that they had rented for half a year or so,” she said. “The rehearsals in the film are happening in the house where he’s filming, and we also did the rehearsals in the house, and they asked me to read the other female parts of the scenes. So that was cool for me to be part of a scene with Stellan and Elle, and that was the first time I met her. But it was so meta because we were rehearsing a rehearsal scene and doing it on the location, and she had come over from the U.S., which was her first time in Oslo — like her character. So it was super weird, but very fun.”
