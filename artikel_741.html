Beyoncé Makes Grammys History as First Artist With 100 Nominations

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Beyoncé has once again made Grammy history in becoming the first artist with 100 nominations, as “Morning Dew (Donk)” secures a nod for best R&B solo performance.

The pop singer dropped the long-unreleased “Morning Dew (Donk)” on July 4 ahead of the 20th anniversary of her sophomore album “B’Day.” The track was originally written by Beyoncé, Pharrell Williams, The-Dream and Darius Dixon, and produced by Beyoncé and Williams. “Morning Dew (Donk)” will compete in the best R&B solo performance category against Kehlani’s “Out the Window,” Victoria Monét’s “Let Me,” Jill Scott’s “Pressha” and Tems’ “What You Need.” Related Stories 'Cher Adores Michael Douglas,' Icon's Rep Says After Douglas Disses Her Oscar Win for 'Moonstruck' AFM Dealmakers, Talent Agents and Hollywood Stars Left Stranded by Nationwide Flight Stoppage

Beyoncé is no stranger to making Grammy history, breaking the record for most wins in 2023 with 32, after earning four awards from the Recording Academy that year. At the time, the award that put her over was best dance/electronic album for “Renaissance.” Before the 2023 Grammys, the record for most Grammy wins was held by the 31 claimed by classical music figure Georg Solti — a benchmark set all the way back in 1997 when he earned his final prize, for best opera recording. Solti died that same year.

In 2024, Beyoncé became the most-nominated artist of all time after securing 11 nods tied to her last album “Cowboy Carter,” surpassing her husband Jay-Z after they had been tied at 88 nominations each. She added three more trophies to her Grammy collection in 2025, upping her overall tally to 35 career wins and making her the most decorated Grammy winner among all artists and female musicians.

With her first album of the year win in February 2025 for “Cowboy Carter,” she became the fourth Black woman to take home that top honor as a lead artist following Natalie Cole, Lauryn Hill and Whitney Houston. “Cowboy Carter” also picked up the award for best country album, while “II Most Wanted” with Miley Cyrus won best country duo/group performance.

Beyoncé has been nominated for Grammys almost every year since her group Destiny’s Child picked up a pair of nods in 2000 with “Bills, Bills, Bills.” That song was included in the categories for best R&B song and best R&B performance by a duo or group with vocals.
