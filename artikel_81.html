A gay New South Wales MP has backtracked on his request for the deeply conservative politician, the Rev Fred Nile, to be honoured with a state funeral, after blowback from the LGBTQ+ community, AAP reports.

Nile, an ordained Christian minister who was a member of NSW parliament for more than 40 years, died aged 92 on Saturday.

He was known for his staunch opposition to drug use, gay marriage and abortion.

Alex Greenwich, the member for Sydney, had praised the controversial politician on Sunday and suggested a state funeral.

But Greenwich, whose electorate takes in Australia’s gay heartland of Sydney’s Oxford St, backpedalled on Monday, saying that:

double quotation markI’m sorry. I’ve listened, read your comments and emails, and recognise that my requests for a state service for Rev Fred Nile caused hurt and distress, particularly within the LGBTQ community.

While we worked constructively together towards the end of his life, and he apologised for past harm and supported LGBTQ reforms, this does not erase the pain and discrimination caused by his earlier campaigns.

Alastair Campbell, former Blair spin doctor, challenges Australian media to be braver

Australians shouldn’t sit easy with Pauline Hanson receiving millions in donations, a sweetheart real-estate deal and a private jet from the country’s richest person, according to one of the world’s most popular political pundits, AAP reports.

Alastair Campbell, the arch spin doctor for former UK Labour prime minister Tony Blair turned co-host of the Rest is Politics podcast, has challenged the Australian media to be braver and improve their offering for young people.

Speaking to an audience of reporters at Canberra’s National Press Club earlier today, Campbell said:

double quotation markI’m overwhelmingly critical of the media and what it’s become over my lifetime but ... my criticism comes from a place of love.

Campbell criticised what he said was journalists’ failure to properly scrutinise populists, and convince younger generations there was anything to gain from political involvement.

double quotation markWhen the politicians are driven by the populism of my definition – politics as problem exploitation, not problem solving – then the media should expose and challenge the populists, not ventriloquise.

Is it too much to expect journalists to stand up for each other once in a while in defence of the legitimate role they play?

NSW Greens ask candidates not to interrupt opponents’ press conferences

The NSW Greens have issued a statement reminding their candidates not to interrupt their political opponents’ press conferences after heckling by upper house candidate Shane Bazzi led to the premier, Chris Minns, cancelling an event this morning.

Bazzi, who is fourth placed on the Greens ticket in the March ballot, crashed a media appearance in the marginal electorate of East Hills, shouting at Minns about making “captain’s calls” on Labor preselections, the war in Gaza and the government’s treatment of protesters.

In a statement, a spokesperson for the NSW Greens said:

double quotation markAs we enter the election period we will remind candidates that while we may sometimes strongly disagree with the views of our political opponents, we generally don’t interrupt their press conferences.

They said the party had “a long and proud history of campaigning against the genocide in Gaza, for climate action, for First Nations justice and for the right to protest in NSW”, including against anti-protest laws struck down as unconstitutional.

In a post on social media following the incident, Bazzi said he grew up in East Hills, where his family still lived, saying Minns had “fled when I asked him legitimate and important questions and held him accountable for his appalling draconian crackdown on peaceful anti-genocide protestors”.

At a reconvened press conference this morning, Minns said there was a difference between “a candidate and a regular punter, a member of the public”, saying:

double quotation markWhen you’re an MP and someone comes along in a media conference or on a street walk and has their say, you’ve got to take your hits, and that is part of the political process ... I just think there’s a distinction [with] political candidates getting their two cents in.

The treasurer is making a ministerial address to the House now.

‘It takes two to tango’: Coalition won’t rush gas reservation scheme negotiations

As deliberations between Labor and the Coalition continue on a gas reservation policy, Nationals leader, Matt Canavan, says the opposition won’t be pressured by “some artificial timeline”.

double quotation markIt takes two to tango. Here we’ve been very constructive.

We put specific amendments to the government. We’ve written and drafted those. If the government doesn’t support them, we’ll move those and have the debate in the chamber.

The two major parties remain locked in talks over a proposal to set up a federal scheme that would require Australia’s largest gas exporters to set aside up to 20% of their product for domestic use.

With only three parliamentary sitting days left, time is running out to pass legislation this year.

The opposition remains the party of choice for Labor to negotiate a deal with, as the Greens demand a 25% tax on gas exports in return for their support.

Question time ends early after fiery dixer

The PM just called time on QT after a particularly heated dixer to Tanya Plibersek.

She was asked how the digital duty of care bill will “help tackle family, domestic and sexual violence”

She took the opportunity to accuse the Coalition of allowing online violence to continue because they won’t support the digital reforms.

It had the Coalition in an absolute uproar.

double quotation markWhile we know that rates of sexual violence are exploding, we’ve got the Liberals, the Nationals and One Nation not prepared to vote to support a digital duty of care – particularly for our children. They’re continuing to say that young Australians should be exposed to things like violent pornography, including depictions of incest and strangulation, or content on how to get away with grooming.

She’s told to “reflect on her language” by the speaker, but ends saying, “members opposite should be ashamed” for not supporting the bill.

Independent Helen Haines, who lost both her legs and is in rehabilitation after she was allegedly struck by a vehicle, isn’t in parliament today.

The crossbench have put an orange cockatoo on her seat to honour her.

Wilkie calls for action to clear patients from hospital beds waiting for aged care and NDIS

Back to the crossbench, independent Andrew Wilkie asks the health, NDIS and aged care minister, Mark Butler, about the number of people in hospital beds who are waiting for an aged care or NDIS place.

He says in his state of Tasmania, nearly 10% of public hospital beds are occupied by people awaiting residential aged care placements or appropriate NDIS supports, and asks what the government will do to fix this.

Butler starts by outlining the scope of the problem:

double quotation mark15 years ago, about 15,000 Australians turned 80 every year. Next year, it will be 90,000 – an increase of 500%.

Butler lists the $25bn of funding the federal government has provided the states to beef up the primary care system, as well as increased funding for aged care support at home places. He says that’s helping the situation but it’s still not ideal.

double quotation markI think even the minister in Tasmania would accept that the work we’ve done – which started under Minister [Bill] Shorten – to put in place NDIS planners in hospitals have dramatically reduced the wait time for people ready for discharge into an NDIS place.

I accept the challenge really is in aged care. The numbers are increasing dramatically. Our ability to build aged care facilities not improving as quickly as I would like. I want to continue working with state governments, including Tasmania’s, to improve that.

Albanese on the back foot on golf club grant

We’re back to the Marrickville golf club grant, folks.

The plot thickened recently after it was revealed by the general manager of the club that the prime minister’s honorary membership was not worth $5 as Anthony Albanese claimed, but $1,100, and that the $6m grant was not for sewerage works.

Deputy Nationals leader, Darren Chester asks: “Isn’t this just another reason why Australians don’t trust this prime minister? I give the call to the prime minister.”

Albanese doesn’t answer the question, but instead defends his other prior claim that he had no idea about the honorary membership.

double quotation markHe said this: ‘I went through it extensively. It wasn’t the practice at the time, and in the board minutes, to name the new members. There’s no mention of it in the board minutes. There was a club newsletter, The Ibis and, in that newsletter, they did name all the new members who had joined in the previous month. In the new month, there’s no mention of an honorary membership being offered to Mr Albanese. There’s nothing in the social committee comments of the time. I couldn’t find any emails on the subject. Essentially, there’s no paper trail to suggest, I can say in all those years before the committee, he was a member, there’s not a single round of golf entered against that golf number’.

‘I won’t be taking lectures from those opposite’: Chalmers rejects calls to resign

I’m sensing a bit of a theme here: Liberal backbencher Leon Rebello is next and he asks Jim Chalmers to “admit he’s run out of steam” and calls for him to resign.

He says Chalmers’ “out of control spending” is driving up inflation, interest rates, and accuses the government of tanking the housing market and killing small business.

Chalmers comes back with a quick quip against his counterpart, Tim Wilson:

double quotation markIt should have rung alarm bells for the honourable member when not even a shadow Treasurer would ask that question, Mr Speaker.

double quotation markWhen it comes to the trillion dollars of debt in the budget, let’s be really clear. Two-thirds of the debt in the budget was racked up by Coalition governments.

“Rubbish!” Shouts a Coalition backbencher. Chalmers continues:

double quotation markSo of all of the people that I will be taking lectures from when it comes to responsible economic management, I won’t be taking lectures from those opposite. They are precisely the last place that I would go for advice on managing the budget.

Tim Wilson continues Coalition attack on treasurer

Shadow treasurer, Tim Wilson, is up next and has his go attacking Jim Chalmers.

He has a long preamble citing job losses at large and small businesses and asks Anthony Albanese: “How many more Australians will lose their job before the treasurer loses his?”

The prime minister says the Australian economy is, in fact, pretty good compared with other nations.

double quotation markAnnual economic growth is under pressure, but it’s still stronger than every major advanced economy except for the United States. Inflation is lower than what we inherited and global inflation is having an enormous impact.

I’m [told] by those opposite ‘have a bit of honesty’. Here’s a bit of honesty. More than 1.2m jobs have been created on our watch. We still have the lowest average unemployment of any government in the last 50 years.

Crossbencher says EVs being dumped while companies farm carbon credits

Over to the crossbench, where Rebekha Sharkie asks the government why carbon credits are given to companies for EVs at the point of importation to Australia, rather than when they’re sold.

She says this risks companies dumping EVs for financial incentive and leaves them sitting unsold.

double quotation markThousands of Chinese-made EVs are sitting unsold around Australia, dumped at our ports and misused water parks. Many will likely never be sold. Yet many have attracted lucrative carbon credits for their maker.

It’s not often we get a question taken on notice in question time, but Pat Conroy, representing Chris Bowen, says broadly that the government’s EV policy has been critical for decarbonisation:

double quotation mark[Our policy] has led to a very strong take-up of electric vehicles in this country, which is critical to de-carbonising our economy.
