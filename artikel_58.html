South Korea’s high-stakes university exams upended by AI cheating scandal

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

South Korea has been gripped by revelations of AI-aided cheating, just days before more than half a million students are to sit high-stakes entrance exams

The integrity of South Korea’s career-defining university entrance exam has been thrown into doubt after the top scorer in a recent professional exam admitted using a concealed camera and AI to cheat.

On Thursday, more than half a million people, mostly school students, are due to sit the exam, known as the suneung, at 1,312 centres across the country. The exam runs for a gruelling eight hours over a single day and determines which university a candidate can attend and, in turn, their future prospects.

The country goes to great lengths to ensure the smooth running of the suneung. Aircraft take-offs and landings are rescheduled during the 25-minute English listening paper to minimise noise, while artillery fire and tank movements are suspended.

The stock market opens an hour late and cars are stopped 200 metres from every centre. Extra trains run from 6am, police drive latecomers to their exam halls, and tourist attractions open late to ease traffic. Offices are asked to open at 10am.

But in recent days, exam organisers have faced a renewed threat to the validity of the entire exercise, after the top scorer in this year’s tax accountancy exam- who got just one question wrong out of 160– admitted on Sunday he had cheated by using AI.

Prime minister Han Seong-sook told a cabinet meeting in Seoul on Tuesday that AI-assisted cheating was a “social evil” that the government would “root out”.

“We will block outright the carrying of smart devices during the exam,” she said. The education ministry and other agencies should approach it “as if staking their jobs on it”, she added.

Han said smart devices must be turned off and handed in, while bags left in corridors would be checked for any devices that were still on. Exam officials would also inspect all candidates’ glasses and watches, as well as their collars, sleeves and buttons for concealed cameras.

The government will also send emergency alerts to every phone in the country, which sound at full volume and cannot be silenced, in the hope of catching out anyone who has not handed in their device.

More invigilators will be deployed, and the government said it was preparing to switch off school wifi inside exam centres and public wifi nearby, and that personal hotspots, which cannot be blocked, would be traced through connection records.

The weekend’s cheating revelation captured national headlines in South Korea, stoking concern about the security of the suneung.

In a video posted to his lawyer’s YouTube channel, the exam cheat said he had photographed the questions with a concealed microcamera, sent them to an AI system and read the answers from a smartphone. “I cheated in the tax accountant exam,” he said. “My top placing is not the result of legitimate effort.”

His lawyer handed police a camera, a laptop and a phone. No charges have been brought, and the prime minister has ordered procedures to begin that would void his result and bar him for five years.

The education minister, Choi Kyo-jin, told parliament on Monday that while authorities could screen out the smart glasses already on sale at Thursday’s exam, newer variants could not be dealt with perfectly.

Choi has ruled out postponing the exam, which has been delayed several times before, including in 2017, after an earthquake struck the south-eastern city of Pohang, and in 2020 due to the Covid pandemic.

Smart glasses have been banned in exams since the end of June. In October, several candidates were caught with them while sitting university-run admissions exams. At the suneung, wearing a pair counts as using one even when switched off.

The education ministry looked at using radio-frequency equipment to detect the glasses in exam halls, but concluded conditions varied too much between venues for deployment this year. Checks will instead rely on visual inspection, with at least one dedicated official placed in each exam centre.

Teachers’ unions have criticised the government response as belated. A survey last month of 2,716 secondary school teachers by the Korean Teachers and Education Workers Union showed that 82.3% said they could not tell whether a candidate was wearing smart glasses under the current procedure, with only 3.5% saying they could.

To avoid facing scrutiny on exam day, some candidates are reportedly switching to contact lenses. An optician in Suwon, south of Seoul, said students who had never worn contacts were coming in to ask how to put them in.

Han said on X on Monday that the checks would be handled carefully “so that candidates acting in good faith do not suffer unnecessary anxiety or inconvenience”.

In his video confession, the accountancy test cheat addressed suneung candidates directly. “The suneung is coming up soon,” he said.

“To anyone thinking of cheating as I did: I am going to tell the investigators everything, so please don’t think, ‘I won’t get caught.’”
