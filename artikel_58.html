EU leaders fail to bridge budget splits as talks stall

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Emmanuel Macron said it would be a "strategic mistake" to reduce EU spending

European Union (EU) leaders have deferred negotiations on its long-term budget after tensions surfaced over the bloc's spending priorities and aid for Ukraine.

Tense talks on the 2028-34 budget ended after 12 hours on Thursday at a summit in Brussels, with some members walking out of negotiations that will resume in December.

Divisions were largely split along the lines of countries, including France and Italy, wanting to approve a higher-spending proposal against those advocating for financial restraint, with Germany chief among them.

Many members want the budget locked in ahead of several high-stakes elections held across Europe in 2027, including in France.

Slovakia, the Czech Republic and Hungary have objected to the loan, but German Chancellor Frederich Merz said uniform EU agreement was a condition for approving the broader budget.

"I stand by my view that aid for Ukraine must be supported by the European Union as a whole," Merz told reporters.

"The European Union comprises of 27 member states, not 23 or 24."

Bulgaria, which previously opposed the plan, said it would agree to fund part of the loan that commits non-military financial support to Kyiv, marking a small sign of progress emerging from the special summit of the European Council.

Those advocating higher spending want the funds to future-proof industries such as farming and invest in regional development. Fiscally cautious countries want to avoid amassing ballooning debt.

Four countries wanting to rein in the budget, including Denmark and Austria, walked out of negotiations after several hours of meetings.

Merz said there was "new movement" towards reducing the size of the EU budget, though he added "we are not there yet" when he confirmed that a new round of negotiations would not take place in Brussels on Friday.

European budgets requires approval from all 27 countries, with each country effectively holding veto power.

French President Emmanuel Macron said it would be a "strategic mistake" to lower long-term spending.

France's forthcoming presidential election came into focus during the EU budget talks, with frontrunner Marine Le Pen calling the talks an "underhanded political manoeuvring to thwart the democratic will of the French people".

Last month, Le Pen announced she would significantly reduce French aid to Ukraine and EU contributions if she were to win the presidency.

Macron addressed Le Pen's remarks on the sidelines of the Brussels summit, saying: "It is an odd kind of arrogance to behave as if she is already in charge of France's destiny because, as things stand, she has not been elected."

Once the talks concluded, the European Council announced a two-day summit on 17 and 18 December would revive budget negotiations.
