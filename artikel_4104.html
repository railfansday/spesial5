Opinion: A Pokémon Rave Just Might Have Been The Strangest Night Of My Life

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

It's a strange feeling, finding yourself in a dark room with thousands of Pokémon fans while a DJ blasts EDM so loud you can feel it in your chest.

Yet that's how I found myself yesterday evening, bobbing my head along to tunes far from my usual playlist and cheering whenever a Pokémon appeared on the giant screen that encompassed the performer. To add to the fever dream, a glance at my watch informed me that it wasn't even 8pm yet.

This was the Pokémon Night Out, a live Electronic Dance Music event organised by The Pokémon Company that came to London's O2 Arena last night after an LA show in October. Artists Marshmellow (the guy from Fortnite) and Alison Wonderland (not from Fortnite) headlined, and I can honestly say that it was unlike anything I have ever experienced before.

Not being a particular fan of either large crowds or loud music, it may come as a surprise to hear that I'm not a natural raver. I am, however, a big old Pokémon fan.

So armed with my finest earplugs and the most outrageously expensive beverage money can buy, I made my way to the seated area just in time for the first set.

The above images do a pretty good job of illustrating the experience, but let me paint you a picture anyway. Alison Wonderland stood behind her decks on the main stage, with one of the largest screens I've ever seen extending up behind her. Throughout the set, this screen would be adorned with all manner of flashes and alternating colours, occasionally with a Pokémon popping into view.

And yes, I use 'occasionally' on purpose there. There were a lot of Pokémon visuals, no doubt, but I was surprised at the number of times the screen was 'mon-less.

This was doubly true of the music, which was hardly packed with tunes from the series. There was a particularly jarring EDM mix of the Lavender Town theme at one point, but I struggle to remember many other motifs entering the setlist.

Giant DJ-ing Pikachu and Mimikyu helped keep everything suitably Poké-themed, mind you, both receiving a huge cheer when they wobbled on stage and 'played' a track of their own.

Marshmellow's set was more of the same, though his elevated position in the backing screen meant that the visuals could really sing. As out of my depth as I was, I fully cheered watching a giant battle between Groudon and Kyogre play out behind Mr. Mellow, sat with a cheesy grin as huge Greninja and Lucario animations showcased their kung fu moves, and I'll even admit to shedding a little tear at seeing the OG starters round a grassy knoll as Lana Del Rey's Summertime Sadness blared in the background.

The finale featured giant Eevees, confetti, and those light-up wristbands you see at a Coldplay gig. I still wasn't fully convinced that the worlds of Pokémon and EDM are a match made in heaven, but I was kind of swept up in the strangeness of it all. 'The first in my bloodline to see this,' and all that.

It will take a massive change of heart (and perhaps a blow to the head) to convert me into a full-bodied raver, but as I looked out into the crowd of those standing and witnessed three Ash Ketchums dancing with a Pikachu and a rather sweaty Bulbasaur, I knew that I had just experienced a night out unlike any other.
