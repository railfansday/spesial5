Bitcoin's (BTC) Worst Is Behind After $55,000 Low, 4-Year Cycle Theory Suggests

Bitcoin (BTC) fell as low as $55,081 on Nov. 21, its lowest price since September 2024, according to TradingView data. The cryptocurrency then bounced back and was trading at $60,583 on Sunday, after it opened November at $77,425.

Bitcoin has lost 21.6% so far in November 2026, according to CryptoRank. The last time Bitcoin had a worse month was June 2022, when the cryptocurrency lost 37.9%, according to the same data.

The four-year cycle theory ties Bitcoin's market cycles to halvings, and its supporters point to 2014, 2018 and 2022 as the years when each bear market went through its final sell-off. In this context, 2026 is the next year in that row, and the next Bitcoin halving is expected in April 2028. The previous three halvings took place in July 2016, May 2020 and April 2024.

Bitcoin set its record above $1,100 in late 2013. In February 2014, the Japanese exchange Mt. Gox, which handled most Bitcoin trading in 2013, filed for bankruptcy after it lost about 850,000 BTC. In November 2014, Bitcoin gained 11.6%, according to CryptoRank, but December of that year brought a 15.3% drop, and the bottom below $200 came in January 2015.

In December 2017, Bitcoin reached a record near $20,000 on the back of the ICO boom, and during 2018, the ICO market dried up. In November 2018, the crypto market crashed after the Bitcoin Cash hard fork and the so-called hash war between the two camps of miners, and Bitcoin lost 36.4% that month. The cryptocurrency then lost another 6.4% in December 2018 and set its cycle low at about $3,200 in the middle of that month.

The 2021 record near $69,000 came in November 2021. In May 2022, the Terra ecosystem collapsed, and in July of that year, Celsius and Three Arrows Capital filed for bankruptcy. In November of that year, the FTX exchange went bankrupt. Bitcoin lost 16.1% in November 2022 and set its bear market bottom at around $15,500 on Nov. 21, 2022, four years to the day before the latest low. December of that year brought a 3.7% loss, and in January 2023, Bitcoin gained 39.9%.

In the current cycle, Bitcoin set its record above $126,000 in October 2025. In 2026, Bitcoin has lost value in six of the 11 months so far, according to CryptoRank. On Nov. 20, the cryptocurrency fell below $60,000 for the first time since July, and the decline continued the next day.

The latest drop comes amid a broad sell-off on Wall Street, where technology and AI stocks came under pressure, and amid expectations of further interest-rate hikes by the Federal Reserve.
