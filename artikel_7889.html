All year, I save up bubble wrap to use as a cheap DIY solution to see my garden through the winter. My trick is simple: I sandwich bubble wrap between a folded rectangle of burlap and wrap it around patio pots. Those little pockets of air act as excellent insulation, but my burlap and bubble-wrap pot-cozy does so much more than help to keep borderline-tender plants alive through winter.

If you're a fan of container gardening, you'll know that the temperature of soil in planters is so much more erratic than in the ground, leaving roots riding a right rollercoaster of conditions. By insulating pots, I save my plants a whole heap of stress, which means they can use their precious energy for far more exciting things. Winter-flowering pansies and violas in pots can stall in a cold snap, but stop that soil from freezing solid around their shallow roots and the plants will carry on pushing out their cheery petals. Winter-flowering heather in planters, too, will bloom far more steadily if their root balls are protected.

A DIY pot-wrap also helps to stop plants from drying out. When soil freezes – and it’ll freeze far sooner and deeper in a planter than in the ground – roots can’t access its moisture. If the plant keeps losing water through its leaves (and remember the wind strips moisture from foliage just as readily as the sun), it’s no different to drought conditions. And when a plant is losing more water than it can replace, it goes into survival mode. For early-flowering shrubs like Daphne odora and ornamental camellias, that can mean dropped buds and zero blooms.

Wrapping also helps to stop pots from cracking in the continual freeze-thaw of winter, especially if they're made from porous terracotta. And let’s not forget those borderline-hardy plants either: with their roots already safely insulated in bubble wrap and burlap in late fall, it’s a super-quick job to zip up shrubs in frost jackets when a cold spell is forecast.

It’s best to combine bubble wrap with good-quality, tightly-woven burlap on a roll such as this from Walmart to insulate pots. Cut a rectangle of burlap that’s twice the height of the pot and long enough to wrap all the way around with a generous overlap. Then fold the burlap in two and lay bubble wrap in between the two layers. Tie this around the pot with heavy-duty twine or rope, being careful not to cover the soil surface.
