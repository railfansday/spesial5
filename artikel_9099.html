Rare Hilma af Klint Work Heads to Auction

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Christie's withdraws a Frida Kahlo painting following protests, Sotheby's says heirs of contested Van Gogh agreed to a deal, and more in today's art news.

Welcome to our roundup of daily art news, featuring top stories and timely updates handpicked by Hyperallergic's writers and editors. Have a news tip? Get in touch with us.

Works by the Swedish spiritualist and abstract art pioneer, who asked that her paintings remain unseen for 20 years after her death in 1944, have not come up for auction in more than three decades. That's about to change with the emergence of “Ser III No. 5” (1920) at the Stockholm-based house Bukowskis, which estimates the oil and graphite on canvas will fetch up to 8 million SEK (~$791,000) in its upcoming November 18–19 sale. Controversy has long followed the legacy of the late artist, with some arguing that her works should be kept together rather than sold and commercialized.

After the heirs of the Jewish publisher Samuel Fischer requested the withdrawal of Van Gogh's “Châtaigniers en fleurs” (1890) from a sale at Sotheby's, claiming that the work had been sold under duress amid Nazi persecution in Europe, the family has reached a “mutually satisfactory resolution” with the work's consignors, said a Sotheby's statement shared with Hyperallergic. But will the dispute put a damper on sale results? We'll find out tonight when the painting, valued at $180 million, goes under the hammer.
