Royce Lewis traded; Twins move on from Bailey Ober, Trevor Larnach

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

After making a pair of additions to the roster earlier in the week, bringing back both catcher Ryan Jeffers and starting pitcher Sonny Gray, the Twins made a number of notable subtractions on Friday.

The Twins non-tendered veterans Bailey Ober and Trevor Larnach and then traded Royce Lewis to the Los Angeles Dodgers, sources confirmed, as they continued to remake their roster.

There was question as to whether the Twins would tender Lewis a contract for the upcoming season before Friday's deadline or move on. They answered it by doing both, tendering him a contract before dealing away the former first-overall draft pick.

While Lewis will likely be best remembered for a pair of home runs in Game 1 of the 2023 Wild Card Series, helping the Twins' break a nearly two decade-long playoff losing streak. Along with the immense potential he showed early in his career, the infielder dealt with a number of injuries during his career and hit a combined .232 with a .689 OPS over his previous three seasons.

Amid a tough start to last season, the Twins sent Lewis down to Triple-A, where he had a torrid stretch against minor league pitching before returning to the major leagues. The Twins at that point shifted him from third base to the right side of the infield, where he primarily played first base, learning the position on the fly.

Lewis, asked about his future with the Twins at the end of the season, said he expected to return. Instead, the Twins sent the Southern California-native home and in return acquired right-handed pitcher Bobby Miller and minor league catcher/first baseman Francisco Espinoza.

Like Lewis, Larnach and Ober were two of the Twins' longer-tenured players. Both had one more season of team control. But by non-tendering them on Friday, they became free agents. The Twins also non-tendered Forest Lake-native Matt Wallner, who spent much of the year in Triple-A.

Larnach — who underwent wrist surgery last Friday — is coming off perhaps his best season offensively, but the Twins have a number of young, left-handed hitting outfielders who are major league ready and he was due a raise to around $6.3 million, per MLB Trade Rumors.

Top prospects Walker Jenkins and Emmanuel Rodriguez, both of whom reached the majors near the end of last season, are left-handed hitters, as is Alan Roden, who spent his year between the majors and minors.

With Byron Buxton expected back after hip surgery and Luke Keaschall also shifting to the outfield last year, the six-year veteran outfielder was the victim of a roster squeeze.

So was Ober, a longtime rotation mainstay. While it seemed likely Ober would be the odd man out anyway, the signing of Gray earlier this week made that even more apparent.

Along with Gray, the Twins' rotation depth runs deep with Pablo López, Joe Ryan, Taj Bradley, Dean Kremer, Connor Prielipp, Zebby Matthews and Mick Abel. David Festa — who did not pitch last season due to injury — and Kendry Rojas have also been starters, though both could be bullpen options for the Twins.

Though the Twins could have retained Ober, who pitched for them for six seasons, for one final season as a back-end-of-the-rotation option, they instead decided to move on from the starter, who had a better 2026 than 2025 but had been pitching with diminished velocity and hadn't been as effective as he had been earlier in his career.

In non-tendering the pair of veterans, the Twins saved a projected $13.2 million, per MLB Trade Rumors.

As for Wallner, the right fielder wound up in Triple-A after striking out in 53 of 135 (39%) of his plate appearances and hitting .167 in 34 major league games this season. But unlike 2024, when he returned to the majors after a reset in St. Paul, Wallner never made it back to the Twins after May, and the writing was on the wall.
