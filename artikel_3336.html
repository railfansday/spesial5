Mullin Says Skipping LSU Game Amid FAA Crisis Would Have Caused ‘Confusion’

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin appeared before the House Homeland Security Committee to answer for the chaos of the last week. A full aviation ground stop, widespread tech outages at major American companies, and the news that while Americans scrambled to deal with the fallout of a national transportation crisis, Mullin took a luxury government jet to an LSU football game, and watched it from the private box of a DHS contractor.

Mullin opened his testimony by berating lawmakers for questioning the administration’s handling of Friday’s ground stop — only the third in the nation’s history and the second since the September 11 terror attacks. The secretary’s defense was essentially: What are you even mad about? Do you really expect me to pass up a sweet hookup to a college football game just because the nation is going through what I described a day earlier as a “crisis”?

“We landed over 4,000 planes when the ground stoppage went out because of the communication and didn’t have one single incident. That in itself is a great success,” Mullin ranted during his opening statements. “You want to bring up the fact that I took my family and my great nieces to an LSU game … because I wanted to be a good uncle and take my little niece — who has never been to an LSU game, and she’s a huge LSU fan — to a game.”

“We had deep conversations about it, and it came in that if we canceled something when the airspace was completely open, it would bring confusion,” he added.

The issue was not so much that Mullin attended a football game, but that he did so less than 24 hours after a major national incident, while millions of Americans remained stranded in airports across the country and were out hundreds or even thousands of dollars in missed flights, travel expenses, hotel costs, and other incidentals. Mullin further claimed that he had no idea that the box he had tickets in belonged to Ben Bordelon, the CEO Bollinger Shipyards, which holds several federal contracts from Homeland Security. Mullin claimed that he “didn’t reach out to him for the tickets. I reached out to the state for the tickets, [telling] them we were going to reimburse them. They put us in the suite. I’d never met the company. I’d never met the team or the gentleman. Mullin and Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy have yet to provide a cohesive explanation for what exactly caused the nationwide communications disruption that forced the FAA to pull the emergency brake on all American airspace. They’ve suggested everything from a faulty router upgrade, an A.I. dust-up (or “S.I.” as the administration continues to insist), or — if you ask Trump — connecting glass to copper wire. FAA investigators are still working to determine the true cause of the communications issues that triggered the ground stop.

Instead, the administration is attempting to offload responsibility onto the administration of former President Joe Biden. At one point, Rep. LaMonica McIver (D-N.J.) noted that while the secretary had attempted to shift responsibility onto the previous administration despite having voted against infrastructure funding that would have helped upgrade the very systems responsible for the outages when he was still a senator.
