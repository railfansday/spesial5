Box Office: ‘Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping’ Debuts to Mighty $120 Million, Reviving Lionsgate’s Most Important Franchise

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

“The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping” made quick work of the competition, debuting to an impressive $120 million and easily capturing the top spot on the domestic box office chart.

The sequel represents a massive improvement on the opening of its predecessor, 2023’s “The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes,” which bowed with $44 million, a franchise low. However, “Sunrise on the Reaping” didn’t match the highs of the series. Its opening pales in comparison to 2013’s “Catching Fire” ($158 million debut), 2012’s OG “Hunger Games” ($152.5 million), and 2014’s “Mockingjay Part 1” ($121.8 million debut). Deadline trumpeted the fact that the film has the second highest-grossing opening for a prequel behind “Rogue One,” which seems like a milestone no one really ever thought or cared about before. So extra credit to some enterprising studio publicist or Google for unearthing that gem. But whatever records it holds, “Sunrise on the Reaping” has revived a series that was looking a little long in the tooth. Why did this “Hunger Games” outpace “The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes”? It probably helped that franchise icons Jennifer Lawrence, Josh Hutcherson, and Woody Harrelson were coaxed back for brief appearances. There was also a top-shelf cast that included Oscar winner Kieran Culkin, Oscar nominees Glenn Close, Elle Fanning, Ralph Fiennes, and Jesse Plemons, and rising stars like Iris Apatow, Mckenna Grace, Ben Wang, and Joseph Zada. Internationally, “Sunrise on the Reaping” earned $105 million from 84 territories, pushing its worldwide box office gross to $225 million.

Lionsgate is distributing the $150 million production, as it has the previous films. The studio recently mounted something of a comeback under Adam Fogelson, who replaced Joe Drake as the motion picture chief in 2024. Lionsgate was on its back foot at the time, having released flops like “Borderlands” and the reboot of “The Crow.” In recent months, its fortunes have shifted thanks to blockbusters like “Michael” and hits such as “The Housemaid” and “The Long Walk.” A revived “Hunger Games” franchise is critical, as it gives Lionsgate another pillar for its slate.

But “The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes” success threatened to overshadow “I Play Rocky.” The Amazon MGM Studios release earned $9.3 million after expanding from 29 locations to 2,477 theaters. It’s playing like gangbusters, earning an A+ CinemaScore, a rare distinction that puts it in the company of “Top Gun: Maverick” and “The Avengers.” The word-of-mouth is clearly excellent, which could help it build momentum over the holidays. It’s also worth noting that “I Play Rocky” is catering to older moviegoers who take their time getting around to seeing things. Even if it starts to fade, “I Play Rocky” is a relatively economical bet. It cost in the $30 million range to produce and has earned $10.4 million since it debuted.

Then there’s “Ebenezer.” The Johnny Depp revival isn’t as glorious as his most diehard fans may have hoped. The Christmas story fell roughly 60 percent in its second weekend, earning $7.7 million to bring its domestic total to $32.5 million. But Paramount didn’t spend lavishly to make the film. It only cost $38 million to remind Depp’s Scrooge of the Christmas spirit. “Ebenezer” has also earned $45.9 million internationally, bringing its global total to a respectable $78.4 million.

Among the newcomers, Sumerian Pictures released “Josephine,” a family drama with Gemma Chan and Channing Tatum,” on four screens in New York and Los Angeles this weekend, grossing an estimated $84,200 for a per-screen average of $21,050. The film earned raves at Sundance where it premiered and Sumerian bought it. “Elsinore,” a drama about Ian Charleson’s final stage performance, opened with $240,000 from six locations in New York and Los Angeles for a per-theater average of $40,000. Focus Features is distributing the film, which has earned Oscar buzz for Andrew Scott. Lastly, Janus Films and Criterion debuted Paul Thomas Anderson’s concert film “Cameron Winter at Carnegie Hall” in three locations, where it earned $162,000 for $54,000 per-screen average. The film is only showing in 35mm, which limits the number of screenings available to cinephiles and PTA completists.
