yuri suzuki transforms amusement park rides into acoustic sculptures for onomatopia

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

five colorful dioramas reconfigure classic amusement park rides into structural acoustic instruments using horns, pipes, and funnels in vibrant colors.

Yuri Suzuki edited by christina vergopoulou November 11, 2026 3 min

Staged at Osh Gallery within Pentagram’s London headquarters in Islington, Yuri Suzuki: Onomatopia is a solo exhibition running from November 10th, 2026, to January 7th, 2027. Curated collaboratively by sound artist Yuri Suzuki and Vicky Richardson (founder of Pick Up Architecture and former Head of Architecture at the Royal Academy of Arts), the exhibition investigates the confluence of play, acoustic engineering, and speculative public architecture. Drawing conceptual references from Thomas More’s 1516 text Utopia, mid-century World Expos, and mid-century theme parks such as EPCOT, the project critiques visual dominance in contemporary design by prioritizing acoustic space.

Mechanical Music Wheel | all images ©Chris Kidall Park unless stated otherwise

The exhibition is structured around five physical dioramas that function as prototypes for larger public acoustic interventions: The Roller-Coaster (Coaster of Sounds), The Ferris Wheel, The Dodgems, The Helter-Skelter, and The Sonic Carousel. Reinterpreting classic fairground attractions as kinetic sonic structures, each prototype is assembled from modular networks of pigmented industrial piping, vertical collection funnels, and directional acoustic horns. Individual structures incorporate dedicated sound compositions, expanding upon artist Yuri Suzuki’s Coaster of Sounds (Onomatopoeia Series), a modular installation previously exhibited at the 2025 Royal Academy Summer Exhibition.

The spatial prototypes are displayed alongside process models, recorded audio tracks, archival references, such as a 2001 Walt Disney World map and an Åbäke map of Margate, and a commissioned short film directed by Dylan Hayes documenting the mechanical kinematics and soundscapes of the assemblies. ‘Sound can be a powerful tool for good and bad, but it is often overlooked in a world that is dominated by visual culture. My Onomatopian dioramas use the forms that create sound to build a landscape of escape, from where we can look back at reality and consider how we want the world to be,’ the artist states.
