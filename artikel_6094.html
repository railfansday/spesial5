As one of the world's largest cruise ships, Royal Caribbean's Legend of the Seas promises no shortage of things to see, do and eat. With an expansive lineup of dining and entertainment venues, pools and activities, there's enough onboard to fill an entire vacation without ever stepping off the ship.

But as someone without extensive cruising experience, I boarded the ship with a few preconceived notions about what the rooms, crowds and restaurants would be like.

After sailing on the ship's inaugural voyage, however, I found myself pleasantly surprised by several aspects of the experience. Here are five things I didn't expect.

With thousands of passengers on board, I expected finding a place to lounge by the pool, grab a drink or simply sit and relax to be a bit of a challenge. After all, even the largest cruise ship in the world has a finite amount of space.

Given that the ship's double-occupancy capacity is 5,610, I was surprised by how rarely I felt surrounded by crowds.

I was traveling with a friend, and we could almost always find available lounge chairs; we rarely encountered long lines at restaurants, bars or other public areas. Elevators were easy to navigate, and I never felt like I was constantly waiting behind a crowd of other passengers.

There were a few exceptions. Some of the pools got busy, and popular performances and events filled up quickly, so planning ahead was important if I wanted to see something specific. But even during popular moments, like the rooftop deck fireworks display, there was enough room to enjoy the show without feeling packed in.

The same was true during our visit to Perfect Day at CocoCay, Royal Caribbean's private island in the Bahamas. We spent time at South Beach and Cove Beach, where there were plenty of open spots to settle in and enjoy a peaceful vibe.

For a ship carrying so many passengers — up to 7,600 passengers at maximum capacity — I was surprised by how easy it was to find a little breathing room.

Cruise lines aren't shy about advertising their entertainment, but I wasn't entirely sure what to expect from the actual productions. I figured there would be plenty of activities to keep passengers occupied, but I didn't anticipate the caliber of performances I saw onboard.

The AquaTheater show was one of the highlights of my sailing. The production featured acrobatics, high dives and aerial stunts that had the audience audibly gasping throughout the show.

The ice skating production Fusion was another standout. The performers were incredibly talented, and the show was engaging from beginning to end. There was also a full production of “Charlie and the Chocolate Factory” that I unfortunately didn't have time to see.

Beyond the major productions, there was plenty of entertainment to stumble upon throughout the ship. The live music was consistently good, and we happened upon a silent disco where passengers of all ages were dancing while listening to music through their own headphones.

We also participated in an onboard escape room, which was just as thoughtfully designed and challenging as some of the escape rooms I've experienced on land.

I went into the sailing thinking of entertainment as something to do between meals and pool time. Instead, it became one of the reasons I was excited to explore the ship.

I knew Legend of the Seas would have plenty of dining options, from casual restaurants to more upscale specialty dining. But I wasn't expecting one of my favorite experiences of the entire sailing to be an eight-course tasting menu at the Old Hollywood Supper Club.

The experience felt completely removed from the typical cruise-ship dining environment. The restaurant's glamorous old Hollywood decor, live musical quartet and immersive atmosphere made dinner feel like an evening out in its own right.

The eight-course menu was thoughtfully presented, with each dish accompanied by a corresponding cocktail. Our servers were incredibly knowledgeable about the food and drinks, and there were little surprises throughout the meal, including an amuse-bouche to start and an unexpected dessert at the end.

Some highlights included blinis with caviar and goat cheese, which came paired with what the restaurant called a Sunset Martini, and the wagyu filet, which was paired with the club’s version of an Old Fashioned. Even the palate cleanser — a vodka-infused sorbet presented theatrically under a smoky glass top — was well thought out and delicious.

It was a three-hour dining experience with enough time to enjoy each course, discuss all the flavors with my tablemates, enjoy the live music (which included songs that coincided with each course) and get excited about what was coming next in the story. For a few hours, I almost forgot I was eating aboard a cruise ship.

I assumed this ship would have plenty of casual, approachable, kid-friendly dining options (which it did), but this experience made me realize there are also opportunities for a more sophisticated night out onboard.

With so many restaurants, bars, pools, entertainment venues and distinct neighborhoods, I assumed it would take me a while to get my bearings on Legend of the Seas. But the ship's size never felt overwhelming, and I found it surprisingly easy to navigate. Clear signage made it simple to find my way between venues, elevator displays helped me figure out where I needed to go, and crew members were always nearby if I had a question.

That ease extended beyond the ship itself. The Wi-Fi connection was strong enough that I could research the different beaches and amenities at Perfect Day at CocoCay before we arrived, making it easy to plan how I wanted to spend my day. Once ashore, complimentary trams made it simple to get between different areas of the island without having to walk everywhere.

For a ship with so much happening at once, I appreciated how little effort it took to get where I wanted to go and make the most of my time.

Before this sailing, I had always pictured cruise ship cabins as fairly cramped spaces with just enough room for a bed and a small bathroom. I expected to spend most of my time out exploring the ship and very little time actually enjoying my room.

My cabin on Legend of the Seas changed that perception.

I stayed in a Spacious Infinite Ocean View Balcony Room, which had a large bed, a couch and a sitting area by the floor-to-ceiling window. There wasn't a true balcony, but the sitting area and full floor-to-ceiling window gave a balcony-like feel. Guests in these rooms can open and close the windows using a button on the side of the window.

There was a surprising amount of storage available in the room. Between the full closet, dresser drawers and bathroom shelves, there was plenty of room to unpack and keep everything organized rather than living out of a suitcase. There was also enough space for two people to get ready without constantly maneuvering around each other.

Of course, a cruise ship cabin isn't going to offer the same square footage as a traditional hotel room. But I was impressed by how thoughtfully the space was laid out and how comfortable it felt for two people.
