I've wanted to crawl under a rock and hide - Pressley

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Steven Pressley says there is a "fragility" in the Dundee squad

Head coach Steven Pressley has admitted that "at times, I've wanted to crawl under a rock and hide" after Dundee's 8-0 "humiliation" by Heart of Midlothian.

Two days after the Dens Park debacle, Pressley is still adamant he must shoulder the blame - despite winger Alan Forrest earlier saying the "freak" result was down to the players.

Pressley said after the game he was concerned a poor result was imminent and admits he had sensed some complacency within the squad.

"Our strength has been built on cohesion, unity and aggressiveness," said the head coach, who said he was embarrassed by the result but that at difficult times he "seems to find strength from somewhere".

"I think Saturday highlighted exactly what I've been saying - if we get lazy on the hard yards, we're a very vulnerable team.

"We have to be, as I've been consistent all along, at our absolute best to get results.

"I've sensed a little bit of subconscious complacency and my job is to ensure that doesn't seep into their performance."

Pressley believes "there is a little bit of fragility in the squad - certain players of huge talent but a little bit fragile mentally" and must now lift them to face a Hibernian side on Tuesday who are hurting from a 4-0 home defeat by Dundee United.

"It's been a difficult 24, 48 hours," he admitted. "There's been a lot of hurt, a lot of embarrassment.

"At times, I've wanted to crawl under a rock and hide on the back of such a disappointing result.

"But I will say this, you know I suffer a lot from vulnerability at times, but for some reason during difficult moments, I seem to find strength from somewhere. I do and I've had to show strength.

"But we had a good meeting on Sunday. We chatted a lot about the various aspects and hopefully we can start responding to the disappointment."

Pressley said he knew pre-match "that we're nowhere near as good as people tell us we are" and he had previously tried "various things" to guard against a poor performance.

"I've been trying to introduce a couple of things within their training, within our debriefs on games," he revealed.

"But obviously what I have tried to do hasn't had the right effect and, when you get a performance like Saturday, which lacked that intensity, lacked those hard yards, that's on me because I have to make sure that they're ready for that."

It was the first time the former Scotland centre-half has suffered such a heavy defeat as a player or manager.

"There's been some really disappointing days, disappointing results, but of course when you're the head coach and it's my responsibility then this ranks as one of the worst," he added.

"I've been in football for nearly 36 years and you think what have I not experienced and then this comes along. It's the great thing about football - it just keeps testing you.

"We have to use days like this as a reminder to ourselves - as a reset moment. I just hope we can bring some pride back to the football club with the right type of performance against Hibs."
