Budderfly to invest $500M to finance and operate municipal energy upgrades

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The energy-as-a-service company will recoup its investment from savings over a contract’s life. Such agreements have become more common, especially for institutions like universities, one expert said.

Energy-as-a-service contracts are not new, especially for institutions like universities and hospitals, said Eric Smith, professor of practice and associate director of the Tulane Energy Institute at Tulane University. They can provide resiliency in addition to savings, he said.

He pointed to Tulane’s arrangement with Enfra, announced in 2022, in which the company paid for new energy assets and equipment, including a 1-MW solar generation facility, while the school got guaranteed annual cost savings over the 30-year term of the contract and help achieving its emissions-reduction goals.

The upgrades also make the school less vulnerable to power outages from hurricanes that regularly hit Louisiana, which ultimately helps protect students, he said.

“What you're really operating is a job shop to do that equipment optimization and manage it over some period of time,” Smith said of the services companies like Enfra and Budderfly offer.

“The way you get paid for that is to demonstrate that you're saving the ratepayer money,” he continued. “Now we have enough power on campus. We can island the campus and run the whole campus based on the power that they generate with our existing power plant.”

Budderfly’s Edwards said his company started out offering energy management contracts to small and medium-sized businesses before expanding to local governments.

Although governments have access to lower-cost financing options, municipal budget and staffing constraints are a challenge for long-term investments in energy efficiency, he said.

“There's very little appetite anywhere to increase taxes to pay for energy infrastructure,” Edwards said.

The company has already deployed the financing model in Cadillac, Michigan, where it invested $4.4 million in upgrades. Budderfly will use Schneider Electric’s energy management and automation technologies to integrate and manage the energy infrastructure improvements.

It is offering local governments a 15-year contract at a locked-in rate based on its 12- to 24-month historical use average. Budderfly becomes the utility’s customer of record and pays the customer’s utility bill, taking a cut of the savings achieved from upgraded, more-efficient equipment.

If bill savings extend beyond a 30% goal, those are shared with the municipality 50/50, Edwards said. Some contracts also have opportunities for virtual power plant programs and to share additional revenue from demand-side management programs, he added.
