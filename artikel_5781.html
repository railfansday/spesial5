Earlier this year, Patrick Woodard had to have a talk with one of the hundreds of clinicians that work at Monument Health, where he is chief information officer. The South Dakota and Wyoming-based health system had decided to block access to OpenEvidence, the popular free artificial intelligence tool, and Woodard had gotten a few objections.

One of them, Woodard recounted, was from a super user. This doctor was storing more than a hundred patient records inside OpenEvidence, and feeding them to the artificial intelligence tool for help making medical decisions. That would have to end, Woodard told him.
