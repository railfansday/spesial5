See Cheap Trick Play Last Concert Before Indefinite Hiatus: ‘Thanks for Everything You’ve Given Us’

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

A significant chapter in Cheap Trick history unfolded Sunday evening at the Sound Board Theater in Detroit, when the band played their final concert before kicking off an indefinite break from the road. There were no extended speeches or teary-eyed goodbyes to their fans, bur rather a standard Cheap Trick concert heavy on the favorites from the 1970s and quite a few guitar changes.

Cheap Trick alarmed fans last month when they called off all of their 2027 dates and announced that they’d be taking an extended break after fulfilling all of their commitments this year. A follow-up statement was issued a few days later to quell concerns. “Many thanks to everyone who has expressed concern for us,” they wrote. “To clear things up, we’ve decided to take some time off after decades of touring because we need a break. Not for health reasons. We’ll play our November shows and then take our first break in 50 years. Thank you for all your support!”

It was no exaggeration that Cheap Trick has been on the road for 50 years. Throughout their many ups and downs since the release of their self-titled debut LP in 1977, including many lean years in the 1980s before their 1988 comeback hit “The Flame,” a messy split with drummer Bun E. Carlos in 2010 that grew litigious, and various health issues in recent years that temporarily sidelined guitarist Rick Nielsen and bassist Tom Petersson, Cheap Trick have always gigged at a relentless pace.

In addition to their own headlining gigs at theaters and clubs, Cheap Trick have also opened for everyone from Rod Stewart and Aerosmith to Journey and Mötley Crüe, played countless casinos and state fairs, and even the occasional private gig. “Once, we played a ski lodge in Park City, Utah, for some multibillionaire, to about 10 people at a table drinking wine,” drummer Daxx Nielsen, son of Rick Nielsen, told Rolling Stone in 2016. “We’re just bashing away and going, ‘What the fuck is going on?'” Editor’s picks The 250 Greatest Albums of the 21st Century So Far The 100 Best TV Episodes of All Time The 500 Greatest Albums of All Time 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century

“We do what we do, and we do it good,” Rick Nielsen told Rolling Stone in that same 2016 article. “We’re too dumb to quit.”

But in 2016, Nielsen had to briefly step away from the tour to under a “minor procedure.” Robin Taylor Zander, the son of Cheap Trick frontman Robin Zander, played guitar in his place. And in 2021, the younger Zander took over from Petersson for a few months while the bassist underwent heart surgery. “If you asked me five years ago if all this stuff would happen, I would have said you were crazy,” Robin Taylor Zander told Rolling Stone in 2022. “It’s all just been mind-blowing to me, and it’s shown me what I can do under pressure. It’s opened my eyes to so many things.”

In September, Nielsen left the stage mid-show at Riveredge Park in Aurora, Illinois, due to breathing troubles. But he was back onstage at the next show, and he finished out the tour without any apparent issues. In Detroit, they opened with “Dream Police” and ran through many of their classics, including “Big Eyes,” “Stiff Competition,” “Heaven Tonight,” “I Know What I Want,” “I Want You To Want Me,” and “Surrender.” In standard fashion, they closed with “Auf Wiedersehen” and “Goodnight.” “It’s been many years we’ve come out here and played live,” Robin Zander told the crowd before “Surrender.” “We appreciate you guys. We love you. Thanks for everything you’ve given us. We hope to see you again, so come see us. We’re Cheap Trick.”

Cheap Trick has yet to explain in any detail why they’re taking a break. A post on their Facebook page this weekend listed their two most recent gigs as “the last weekend of live shows for…a while?” That seems to suggest the members of Cheap Trick themselves don’t know when they’ll be back on the road. But Robin Zander’s “we hope to see you again” gives fans at least some reason to think that their saga isn’t quite over.
