Charter is cutting $2 billion in costs as internet subscriber losses worsen

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Charter posted a $2.4 billion third-quarter net loss tied to a $6.3 billion franchise impairment charge, versus a profit a year earlier

Charter Communications announced a new operating model on Friday that aims to trim $2 billion from its yearly cost base, measured on an annualized run-rate basis, within the next 18 months.

The savings are in addition to more than $1 billion in anticipated synergies from the Cox Communications transactions, the company said. The program will target nonessential activities for removal, streamline how the organization is structured and capture efficiencies by working more closely with vendors.

Charter lost 233,000 internet customers on a pro forma basis in the third quarter, compared with a loss of 171,000 in the year-earlier quarter. Legacy Charter lost 156,000 internet customers, versus 109,000 a year earlier, and legacy Cox lost 77,000. Charter served 34.3 million internet customers as of Sept. 30.

"We are fundamentally rethinking how we operate and compete," Chris Winfrey, Charter's president and CEO, said in a statement. Winfrey said Charter intends to bring back growth in its internet customer base while turning into a leaner, quicker and more nimble company.

Charter is also reformulating its go-to-market approach for its core internet offering, marketing and distribution, the company said.

Charter reported a net loss attributable to shareholders of $2.4 billion, or $20.50 a share, for the third quarter. It had net income of $1.1 billion a year earlier. The company attributed the loss to a $6.3 billion franchise impairment charge, offset in part by a $1.4 billion gain on extinguishment of debt.

Revenue rose 9.1% to $14.9 billion, driven by the Cox transactions. Pro forma results treat the Cox deal as if it closed on Jan. 1, 2024. On that basis, revenue fell 1.5% to $16.5 billion, and adjusted EBITDA fell 4.0% to $6.6 billion.

Charter added 508,000 mobile lines on a pro forma basis. Video customers fell by 77,000, compared with a decline of 121,000 a year earlier. Charter stock dropped 7% to $117.04 ahead of the market open Friday.

Competition from rivals offering fixed-wireless and fiber service has eroded the base of households subscribing to Charter's internet, while the shift toward streaming has undercut its cable operations, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Charter completed its acquisition of Cox Communications in August. The deal added more than 11 million passings and over 5 million customer relationships, according to the company. On Sept. 16, Charter launched Spectrum's products, pricing and packaging across the legacy Cox footprint.
