Nationwide Ibuprofen Recall; Trump Open to Single-Payer? U.S. Stroke Rate to Double

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

— Health news and commentary gathered by MedPage Today staff

Note that some links may require registration or subscription. Also note that MedPage Today will be closed Thursday and Friday in observance of the Thanksgiving holiday. Look for Morning Break again on Monday, Nov. 30.

Golden State Medical Supply issued a nationwide recall on some of its ibuprofen tablets due to potential surface contamination with aluminum particles, according to an FDA notice.

Poor-quality generic drugs may have accounted for an estimated $18.4 billion in preventable U.S. healthcare costs in 2024, including costs stemming from hospitalizations and relapses, according to a MedShadow Foundation report.

Just days ahead of Thanksgiving, a Massachusetts turkey farm had its license suspended after inspectors deemed it an "imminent health hazard." (CBS News)

An HHS official advised children, pregnant women, and people with chronic illnesses to evacuate Middletown, Iowa, before lead-containing weapons are detonated there today. (The Hill)

Wisconsin's health department reported a third death in connection with its ongoing measles outbreak as cases this year reached 918.

With measles cases rising, some new parents have decided not to travel during the Thanksgiving holiday break. (New York Times)

Nearly 6 in 10 Americans said the U.S. wouldn't have lost its measles elimination status if government officials had done more to encourage vaccination, according to a new Ipsos poll.

The Social Security Administration notified an estimated 300,000 legal immigrants that they will lose Medicare coverage starting in February 2027 due to new federal restrictions. (Washington Post)

President Trump said he will meet with Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed, MD, DPhil (D-Mich.), to talk about rebranding "Medicare for All" single-payer healthcare legislation as "TrumpCare." (The Hill)

The mortality gap between Black and white Americans narrowed from 1993 to 2023, an analysis of data from the National Center for Health Statistics showed. (Annals of Internal Medicine)

Critics argued that Medicare's annual payment caps for hospice agencies penalize patients whose terminal illnesses progress more slowly than the government's expected timeline. (New York Times)

Florida's attorney general called for the state medical board to revoke an ob/gyn's license after reports alleged he performed at least 149 suspected illegal abortions from 2024 to 2026. (Fox News)

The head of the World Health Organization called for the U.S. to immediately end its Cuban fuel blockade, warning that it threatens children's lives. (Reuters via AOL)

A scientific statement from the American Heart Association predicted the U.S. stroke rate will nearly double by 2050. (Stroke)

Budget cuts and anti-rights campaigns may trigger a resurgence in new HIV infections in 2027, the United Nations AIDS chief warned. (Reuters via AOL)

Former offensive lineman Laurent Duvernay-Tardif, MD, MPH, who earned his medical degree during his NFL career, is back in Kansas City working with the Chiefs' medical staff to gain experience in sports medicine and on-field emergency response. (Pro Football Talk)
