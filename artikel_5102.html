Inside Bitcoin’s $16B short liquidation zone: Could BTC bears get squeezed again?

Bitcoin [BTC] witnessed yet another bout of volatility this week that caught the bulls off guard. On Wednesday, the 25th of November, Bitcoin bounced from a low of $57,300 to a local high of $61,622.

This bounce spurred the rest of the crypto market higher too, resulting in $406.85 million in long liquidations and $238.06 million in short liquidations.

The U.S. personal consumption expenditures (PCE) data was released on Wednesday. It rose 3.8% year-over-year, while core PCE rose 3.3% year-over-year.

Headline PCE in line with consensus estimates reinforced the market expectations of a 25-bps Federal Reserve rate hike in December.

A week ago, there was a 6.5% probability of a 50-point rate hike. At the time of writing, this probability was 0%.

It wasn’t bullish news, but it was a catalyst that allowed the price to jump higher and wipe out the dense cluster of short positions that had built up in recent days. Monday’s rally just past $64k was another short squeeze.

As things stand, demand is too weak and pessimism too intense for Bitcoin to mount an immediate comeback.

Founder and CEO of intelligence platform CryptoQuant, Ki Young Ju, noted that there was $16 billion in potential short liquidations from $60k to $100k, compared to just $5 billion in potential long liquidations.

In other words, a short squeeze was much more attractive in these conditions. Notably, a mini short squeeze occurred on Monday, as well as the recent price bounce back above $61k.

The Bitcoin active value to investor value ratio was back near 0.8 after climbing above 1.0 earlier this year, reported a crypto analyst. Moreno. It measures how far the spot price deviates from the True Market Mean, which is the aggregate on-chain cost basis for active investors.

Readings below 1 indicate active investors are carrying losses. Aggregate profitability has deteriorated since the BTC price rebound earlier this year.

The Bitcoin risk index was above 2 and neared its peak value from earlier this year. The metric shows higher readings during sell-offs, when capital turnover intensifies relative to market value.

The two metrics showed increasing stress on the market and offered a more attractive valuation to long-term buyers.

To make a compelling case for a bullish recovery, the selling pressure needs to be absorbed. The evidence at hand does not support sizeable demand- for example, Bitcoin spot ETF flows have been overwhelmingly bearish in November.
