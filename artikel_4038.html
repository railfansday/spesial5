One Star Trek MTG Card Is Catching The Attention Of Artifact Players With Massive Value Spike

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Players with an affinity for artifact creatures are taking a keen interest in Death Is Irrelevant, as its price skyrockets overnight.

Magic: The Gathering card prices are forever on the move, but given how weak the Star Trek set has been, we didn't expect to see a card from the set randomly spike by over 200% overnight.

Death Is Irrelevant is the card in question, and this eight-mana enchantment is a must-have for artifact creature decks, especially when most builds in the archetype can afford the huge mana cost long before turn eight.

The question, however, is why the price has suddenly spiked. We'd known about the card for some time, and the preview window made it clear it would be huge for artifact creature decks, but its price crashed along with the rest of the Star Trek set upon release.

It's taken almost three weeks for the price to spike, but is it a long-term move, or can we expect it to crash again in a few days?

On Monday, November 23, the average price of the Death Is Irrelevant LCARS variant was $13.99, but on Tuesday, November 24, this shot up to $44.39. The market price hasn't moved with it (yet), but listings across multiple marketplaces, including TCGplayer, show that the average listing price across all variants is rising.

Some listings still remain close to the original price, but many have also been bought up quite quickly, leaving the higher-priced listing as the only alternative.

It's worth noting, however, that a lot of the increased prices across all three variants of this card are being put up by one seller on TCGplayer, which either points to market manipulation attempts to raise the market value of the card and make it appear more desirable and valuable than it is, or they have the inside track on upcoming demand and are preparing for the incoming wave by raising their prices in anticipation.

In short, we wouldn't advise jumping on the card if you find listings online, but if you've opened a copy or two in your Star Trek packs, it's worth setting them aside to see what happens next.

If you were hoping to buy a signed Star Trek card, you'll need a pretty hefty wallet.

While the odd Star Trek card is seeing a spike in value, not nearly enough is happening across the set to change perceptions of this Universes Beyond product.

Excluding the signed headliner cards, which aren't really considered valuable for Magic purposes, the most valuable card in the set appears to be the showcase surge foil variant of Captain Jean-Luc Picard at $201.16. There are only four other cards with a value over $100 in the set, a quite unprecedented scenario for a new Magic set.

It's raised a lot of questions about the viability of Star Trek as a Universes Beyond property and called for greater scrutiny of properties selected for future Universes Beyond sets. Players have put forward numerous reasons why Star Trek didn't work as a Magic set, from its cultural relevance to the lack of focus on just one aspect of the property, but all we can hope now is that Wizards has learned its lesson and future Universes Beyond sets are greatly improved.
