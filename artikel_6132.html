Bilt Seeks An $18 Billion Valuation — The Business Behind Its Jennifer Lopez Concert Goes Far Beyond Rent

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Bilt is reportedly seeking to raise $500 million at an $18 billion valuation. That would be roughly two-thirds higher than the $10.75 billion valuation from its last funding round in 2025. They’re asking investors to back a company that wants to influence where people eat, shop, travel and spend their time, starting with the relationship it built through paying rent. Last week Bilt held a dinner for 400 people in the two thousand year old Egyptian Temple of Dendur inside the Metropolitan Museum of Art, with food led by Thomas Keller and Daniel Boulud and a private Jennifer Lopez concert. Ken Chenault, Roger Goodell and Barry Sternlicht were some of the business leaders speaking. The event launched their ‘NeighborhoodOS’ platform. Kastle’s partnership would let 60,000 business building access customers offer employee memberships combining office access with neighborhood benefits, like a nearby dry cleaning credit or a team happy hour. Marriott, Hyatt, the Phoenix Suns, Houston Rockets, Houston Texans and Sacramento Kings have been announced as membership partners. Broader integrations roll out starting next year. Bilt earns money from housing and resident technology, merchant commissions, its card business and now additional software subscriptions. (The option offering up to 1.25 points per dollar on housing is what most people choose anyway and still leaves a very good card once 4% back in Bilt Cash goes away for new cardmembers.) Still, my experience isn’t the experience of every member. Bilt told Thrifty Traveler that fewer than 10% of members have its credit card. Someone whose landlord uses Bilt may pay rent through it and do little else. Founder Ankur Jain has called the card “less than 11%” of Bilt’s business (without defining the denominator). That’s not surprising. But it means that there are also likely a lot of members who don’t transact with Bilt frequently, outside of paying rent. Daily Rewards launches January 15 and looks designed to develop more of that engagement. An on-time housing payment earns a reward with Bilt or a partner, as does one qualifying neighborhood activity per day. Points, Bilt Cash and collectibles toward larger prizes are available. What they’re attempting hasn’t already been done well because it’s hard. Restaurant software is hard. Housing payments are hard. Banking, travel, sports loyalty and customer service are each businesses with their own difficult problems. Putting them together is even more complicated. Jain’s March letter acknowledged that simultaneous integrations, the card transition and mortgage launch strained then and their support suffered. In my experience they ironed out the kinks. They don’t need every category to succeed. There’s a tension for merchants, too: Bilt can make a customer more loyal to a restaurant, but it can also send that customer to the restaurant down the street. What they’re attempting hasn’t already been done well because it’s hard. Restaurant software is hard. Housing payments are hard. Banking, travel, sports loyalty and customer service are each businesses with their own difficult problems. Putting them together is even more complicated. Jain’s March letter acknowledged that simultaneous integrations, the card transition and mortgage launch strained then and their support suffered. In my experience they ironed out the kinks.

They don’t need every category to succeed. There’s a tension for merchants, too: Bilt can make a customer more loyal to a restaurant, but it can also send that customer to the restaurant down the street. The merchant needs enough additional profit to make sharing the customer relationship worthwhile.

The scale of ambition here is huge, they’re executing along a lot of different fronts simultaneously. It’s all expensive.
