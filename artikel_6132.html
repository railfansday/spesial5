Expedia Group used its third-quarter earnings call to describe AI platforms as a customer acquisition channel still being priced. CEO Ariane Gorin said the economics of partnerships with Meta's Muse and Google's AI Overviews are "being worked out," and pointed to the company's history of acquiring customers through large advertising and tech platforms at attractive economics. She said Expedia is leading in citation share in the U.S. — how often its brands are cited as a source by AI chatbots — and that an integration with Muse was imminent. Gorin said the integrations are a way to learn how agents make decisions and which customers use them, and that travelers using agentic AI for discovery still want to book with a trusted brand. The underlying numbers were strong: revenue rose 12% to $4.9 billion, net income rose 7% to $1.03 billion, and room nights grew 7%, matching Booking Holdings. B2B revenue climbed 23% to $1.7 billion while the consumer brands grew 6%. Expedia raised full-year gross bookings guidance to 10% growth from 8-9%, and revenue to 11-12% from 9-10%. CFO Derek Andersen said the fourth-quarter environment is softer than the third, citing disruptions in the Middle East and unrest in some European cities.
