Aave passes native Bitcoin lending proposal, but hidden liquidation risks remain

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Aave's plan to let users borrow stablecoins against native Bitcoin passed an early governance vote and awaits final approval before launch.

Aave's proposal to let holders borrow stablecoins against native Bitcoin cleared its preliminary ARFC governance vote on Nov. 9, according to Babylon Labs.

The borrowing market still needs final approval before holders can use it on mainnet. Babylon reported 100% support for the plan to connect its Trustless Bitcoin Vaults to Aave V4, and the next step is an Aave Improvement Proposal (AIP) for final on-chain approval.

Babylon reports ARFC passage on Nov. 9. Final AIP approval and a restricted mainnet rollout remain ahead.

Under the proposed change, BTC would remain locked on Bitcoin, while an Ethereum-based accounting record would let Aave recognize it. Borrowers would not need to supply a freely transferable wrapped Bitcoin token.

The governance proposal sets out initial stablecoin credit lines from Aave's Core Hub liquidity pool of $8 million each for USDT and USDC, $1 million for GHO, and $2 million each for USDG and RLUSD.

A separate liquidity pool, the Babylon Hub, would let liquidity providers choose exposure to native Bitcoin collateral, with its own limits.

Risk adviser LlamaRisk recommends a 78% collateral factor for native BTC. The collateral record itself would remain non-borrowable, representing BTC securing a position.

Babylon targets mainnet for late 2026, subject to change and confirmation. It says deployment will start with restrictions on participants and volume, with a schedule and staged rollout plan still to follow. Its current FAQ directs users to a public testnet and gives a fourth-quarter mainnet target.

Keeping the underlying BTC on Bitcoin does not remove liquidation or settlement risk. The Ethereum collateral record would be a transfer-restricted ERC-20 accounting unit, and a borrower could still lose vault collateral through liquidation.

The design also retains a role for Wrapped Bitcoin (WBTC). Under the permissionless liquidation route, BTCVaultSwap would borrow WBTC from an Aave Hub to settle immediately with liquidators.

Permissioned arbitrageurs would later repay that debt and redeem the underlying BTC over several days, as LlamaRisk's assessment describes.

The proposed Core Hub draw cap for that settlement venue is 100 WBTC, separate from the stablecoin lending lines. Permissioned actors can also liquidate and redeem vaults directly without drawing Hub liquidity, although fees or borrower compensation can still require WBTC.

Liquidation seizes whole vaults, even when clearing the debt requires less than a vault's full BTC value, and the borrower receives discounted compensation for that remainder in wrapped BTC.

Prospective borrowers still need final AIP approval and a confirmed rollout schedule before this market moves from governance backing to mainnet access.
