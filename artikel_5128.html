Solana whales unstaked and transferred 2.31 million SOL worth $169.4 million to Coinbase Prime, raising concerns of selling

SOL price rebounded from $67.78, but resistance at $78.13 and $83.23 remains critical, while open interest has contracted nearly 48% from its peak.

Solana has staged a modest rebound after falling to $67.78, with SOL price climbing back towards $75 as buyers attempt to stabilise the price following a sharp sell-off. However, the recovery remains fragile, with the token still facing pressure after losing several key support levels in recent weeks.

The latest price action has also coincided with a significant movement involving two large SOL holders unstaking & moving millions of SOL tokens. The move has raised questions about whether further selling pressure could weigh on the SOL price as traders look for signs of a sustained recovery.

Two large Solana holders reportedly unstaked a combined 2.31 million SOL, worth approximately $169.4 million, before transferring the tokens to Coinbase Prime on November 19, according to blockchain tracker Lookonchain. The movement has drawn attention amid SOL’s recent price weakness, as large transfers to institutional trading platforms can raise questions about potential selling activity.

The transactions involved two wallets, with the tokens reportedly moved after being withdrawn from staking. Unstaking makes the SOL available for transfer, while Coinbase Prime provides institutional custody and trading services. This makes the movement worth monitoring, particularly if the tokens are subsequently transferred to trading-related wallets or if similar deposits continue.

The latest rebound from the lows below $68 has brought the token back towards $75, while the recovery has yet to overcome the resistance levels that could signal a stronger reversal. Meanwhile, the volume profile highlights increased trading activity around the current price range. This suggests that the zone is becoming an area of greater participation, where buyers and sellers are actively interacting as SOL attempts to recover from its recent decline.

The MACD shows a drop in selling pressure, which raises hopes of a bullish crossover, offering a bullish push. The $78.13 level remains the immediate resistance, followed by $83.23. A sustained move above these levels could strengthen the recovery, while failure to break higher may leave SOL vulnerable to another test of $67.78. A breakdown below this support could expose $64.80 and $60.23.

Solana’s price decline has also been accompanied by a significant contraction in open interest, suggesting that the derivatives market has seen a considerable reduction in exposure over the past several weeks. According to the CoinGlass chart, SOL open interest fell from approximately 7.6 billion to 7.7 billion in late September and early October to around $4 billion, a contraction of nearly 48% over the period.

The decline suggests that a substantial amount of leveraged positioning has left the market as SOL prices weakened. However, the reduction in dollar-denominated open interest also reflects the falling token price, so it does not necessarily represent an equivalent decline in the number of contracts.

Solana’s rebound from $67.78 offers some relief after the recent sell-off, but the recovery remains at an early stage. The movement of 2.31 million SOL to Coinbase Prime has added another development for traders to monitor, although the transfers alone do not confirm that the whales are preparing to sell. Meanwhile, the contraction in open interest points to reduced derivatives exposure, while the rising volume activity around the current price range may make this zone important in determining SOL’s next move.

For now, the $78.13 and $83.23 resistance levels remain key hurdles for bulls, while $67.78 is the immediate support to watch. Whether SOL can reclaim resistance or revisit lower support levels may depend on how buyers respond to the current price range and whether selling pressure intensifies.
