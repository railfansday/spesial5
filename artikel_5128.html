Bitcoin dodged one risk from Japan and now faces a Monday reckoning

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The currency move cut the dollar cost of repaying unhedged yen loans. Monday’s two-year Japanese bond auction offers a fresh read on short-term yields.

The yen slipped against the dollar on Friday, cutting the dollar cost of repaying fixed, unhedged yen loans for traders who borrowed the currency to buy Bitcoin. Bitcoin held above 60,000 USDT on Binance during Tokyo trading as two measures of the city's November inflation moved in opposite directions. Monday's two-year government bond auction is the next chance to see whether the yen and short-term yields move higher together.

The Statistics Bureau of Japan said preliminary consumer prices across Tokyo's 23 wards rose 2.9% from a year earlier excluding fresh food, up from 2.6% in October. When energy is also excluded, inflation slowed to 2.5% from 2.6%. The preliminary Tokyo figures offer an early read ahead of the national release.

Electricity and city gas explain much of the split. Electricity prices rose 9.5% from a year earlier, up from 1.7% in October, and city gas prices rose 13.7%, up from 4.9%. Energy's contribution to the headline inflation rate increased by 0.34 percentage points. The overall rate rose more modestly, to 2.8% from 2.7%, as other categories offset part of that increase. The bureau put the increase in electricity's contribution at 0.22 percentage points, and city gas at 0.11.

Both Tokyo gauges were at 2.6% in October. In November the measure that includes energy rose, while the measure that excludes it slowed.

Electricity and city gas accounted for most of the increase in energy’s contribution to Tokyo’s headline inflation rate.

The dollar bought 159.28 to 159.30 yen at 9 a.m. Friday and 159.33 to 159.35 yen at 5 p.m., according to the Bank of Japan's Friday quotes. That compared with 159.16 to 159.18 yen at 5 p.m. Thursday. Both quotes use Tokyo time.

CoinGlass’s daily Binance data showed Bitcoin trading as low as 60,472 USDT on Nov. 27 as of 11:54 a.m. UTC. CryptoSlate's Bitcoin price tracker put it at $61,529 as of 11:57 a.m. UTC on Friday.

Ahead of the release, the risk for yen-funded Bitcoin trades was that hotter inflation could push the currency and short-term yields higher. An investor who borrows yen to buy Bitcoin and leaves the currency risk unhedged has to buy yen back to repay the loan. When the yen strengthens, the investor needs more dollars to repay the same yen balance even if Bitcoin's dollar price stands still. Friday's weaker yen moved that cost in the other direction.

Interest costs move on a different timetable. A trader pays more in interest if a yen loan has to be replaced at a higher rate. The actual rate and the timing depend on the instrument, maturity and any hedge, so traders with similar Bitcoin positions can face different costs.

Japan's Ministry of Finance has scheduled the November 30 two-year bond auction notice for 10:30 a.m. Tokyo time and the public result for 12:35 p.m. At October’s two-year auction, the yield at the weighted-average accepted price was 1.91%, giving traders a benchmark for the new issue.

The auction yield measures the return implied by the price paid for the new bonds. The two-year yield in secondary trading just before and after the result can show whether investors are repricing the path of Japanese rates. If that market yield rises while the yen strengthens against the dollar, borrowers who need to refinance could face dearer loans just as their outstanding yen debt becomes more expensive in dollars. If yields rise and the yen stays weak, the signals would still be split.

Even a move in both markets would leave open how many Bitcoin positions use yen funding. Bank for International Settlements researchers documented how hard it was to size the broader currency carry trade after the August 2024 unwind.
