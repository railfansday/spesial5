Wolves must look to sanction an exit for £10m prospect in the January transfer window: Opinion

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Wolves have long looked overseas for new stars to bring into their ranks. Some go straight into the first team, like current aces Andre, while others develop in the academy or out on loan before coming into the fold at Molineux.

Paraguayan winger Enso Gonzalez has primarily played academy football since joining from Club Libertad in 2023. He broke into the senior game in his native country and caught the eye, but to date, he has made just one appearance for the Old Gold’s first team.

Gonzalez will turn 22 in January, and the fact he has not been involved in a single senior matchday squad this season indicates he isn’t close to breaking into Cesar Peixoto‘s plans. To aid his development, Wolves must explore exit options for the winger in the winter window.

Gonzalez joined Wolves aged 18 after impressing in Paraguay. Upon arrival, former sporting director Matt Hobbs said: “He’s an exciting, young player, who will fit in well with our dressing room and within the DNA we’re trying to create. We wanted a front player who could play across the line and give us something different, and Enso is the name our recruitment team pushed.

“His creativity stood out, playing forward, being aggressive in possession and doing his work out of possession, but for me it was more what he does offensively.”

But, despite the clear hopes that Gonzalez could break through at Molineux, he has not found anywhere near the role he hoped for. He has shown sparks in the U21s with four goals in 12 games this season; however it doesn’t appear to have got him any closer to Peixoto’s side.

This lack of progression through the ranks is stunting his development. Wanderers must take action to chance Gonzalez’s trajectory in January, unless it goes down as £10m wasted by the club.

Gonzalez is playing, so he’s retaining sharpness and staying active. But, in terms of progress, there really isn’t much for a player of his age to gain from playing the likes of Fleetwood Town’s U21s in the Premier League Cup, or Wealdstone in the National League Cup.

If Wolves are to maximise his potential, Gonzalez has to be sent out on loan. He needs to go and play first-team football at a good level so they can gauge whether he has a future at Molineux.

Alternatively, if the club already feel that his chances of a breakthrough are slim, all parties will benefit from a sale. Wanderers can cut their losses, and Gonzalez can take his talents elsewhere in the bid to kick on.

Regardless of the structure of the deal, Gonzalez needs a move away from Wolves.
