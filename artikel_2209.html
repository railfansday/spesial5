Wrexham consider Welshman as potential Phil Parkinson replacement after Championship rival snub

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Wrexham have risen from the National League to the Championship under Phil Parkinson‘s lead. But now, the boss is facing questions over his future, with the Red Dragons sat 18th in the table after only one win in their last five.

And, as the North Welsh side look to come good on their big ambitions, a managerial change may await. A report from talkSPORT states that Rob Edwards is among the contenders to take charge of Wrexham if they decide to part ways with Parkinson.

The Welshman recently rejected the chance to take charge of Burnley, who appointed Fabio Celestini instead. Edwards has been out of the game since he was sacked by Wolves in June, and he is now keen to return to the dugout.

Edwards has become a well-known manager over the past five years or so, first rising to prominence by leading Forest Green Rovers to the League Two title. A short and unsuccessful stint as Watford boss followed, but the 43-year-old succeeded in his next job by taking Luton Town to the Premier League in 2024.

He left Kenilworth Road as the Hatters struggled to get over the hangover of relegation from the top flight. Middlesbrough offered Edwards his return to the dugout in July 2025, and his impressive work with a PPG rate of 1.81 ultimately saw him poached by Wolves after only 16 matches.

Edwards’ return to Molineux – where he spent time as a player and coach – didn’t go as planned. Nevertheless, he remains held in high regard for his strong Championship record, often leading sides who utilise attack-minded wing-backs with intense and direct football.

This style of play will appeal to Wrexham, who have set up in a similar way on their journey through their leagues. Edwards has Championship success and Premier League experience under his belt though, unlike current boss Parkinson.

Moving on from Parkinson will not be easy for Wrexham when that day comes. The 58-year-old has led their rise from the fifth tier to Premier League contention, and he has regularly received the public backing of owners Ryan Reynolds and Rob McElhenney.

However, as far as successors go, Edwards feels like a great fit. He has stronger pedigree to try and take the Red Dragons to the next level, yet his similar success with wing-backs means he might be able to get results with the current squad of players right off the bat.

Furthermore, his Welsh citizenship is sure to please supporters who will be happy to have a compatriot at the helm.

Time will tell if Parkinson can hold onto his position, but as pressure grows, Edwards could be an ideal heir.
