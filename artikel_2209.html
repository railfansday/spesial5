3 managers Blackpool must target as club legend Ian Evatt departs after 12 months

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Blackpool have had a terrible start to the season which has left them just above the relegation zone after 13 games. Club legend Ian Evatt had come in last season and their was plenty of positive signs coming into this campaign but they have now come through.

As a result, Evatt has departed the club, confirmed by Blackpool on Thursday afternoon. Evatt was a crucial player for Blackpool when they went from League One to the Premier League just over a decade ago, making over 250 appearances.

Steve Thompson will take charge of the team for this weekend’s game against Wigan Athletic, along with Stephen Crainey, Stephen Dobbie and Steve Banks. The Tangerines are on the lookout for a new head coach to take them up the table.

With that said, here are 3 managers that Blackpool must consider to replace Evatt…

Gary Rowett brings substantial Championship and League One experience and a proven ability to organise and stabilise sides quickly.

During long spells at Millwall and Birmingham he regularly extracted solid results from squads that were not the strongest in the division, often through compact defensive structures, aggressive pressing and pragmatic game management.

He is tactically flexible rather than wedded to one system and has a track record of improving results mid-season. For a Blackpool side currently near the bottom of League One, his experience of grinding out points and rebuilding confidence would be a clear asset.

Luke Williams’ standout achievement was guiding Notts County to promotion from the National League with a record points total and highly attractive, possession-dominant football. However, he was dismissed by Peterborough United in League One this season.

He builds sides that control games, press effectively and score freely, which could energise the Blackpool support and improve the squad’s technical confidence. He also has experience of the demands of League One and the Championship.

While his recent spells at Swansea and Peterborough were mixed, his proven ability to implement a clear, progressive style and maximise players in a promotion environment makes him a credible candidate to lift Blackpool up the table.

Matt Bloomfield’s strongest case rests on his excellent work at Wycombe, where he turned a mid-table League One side into one of the division’s most competitive teams, regularly challenging near the top while playing energetic, high-intensity football.

He is known for his player-centred approach, clear communication and ability to create a positive culture. Although his subsequent spells at Luton and Oxford were less successful, those were at Championship level.

In League One he has shown he can overachieve with limited resources, making him an attractive option for Blackpool’s current situation. With Blackpool struggling at the moment, his player-management skills could be crucial to get the club back on track.
