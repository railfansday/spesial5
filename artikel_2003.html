How Scotland can qualify for Nations League playoffs, and how third place finish affects Euro 2028 qualifiers

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Scotland end their Nations League campaign with a clash against Switzerland on Monday, with their slim hopes of qualifying for the promotion playoffs still intact.

Placed in Group B1 alongside Slovenia, North Macedonia and the aforementioned Switzerland, new head coach Sebastien Pocognoli kept alive Scotland’s chances of claiming second spot in the group with a narrow 2-1 home win over North Macedonia on Friday, thanks to two goals from John McGinn.

Here’s what Scotland needs to happen to nab a promotion/relegation playoff spot, how to watch the draw and what a third-placed finish would mean for Pocognoli’s men.

First and foremost, Scotland MUST beat Switzerland to stand any chance of finishing in second place in the group. Should they achieve that, they will also have to hope Slovenia fail to beat North Macedonia. If Slovenia beat North Macedonia this evening, it is impossible for Scotland to finish in second place regardless of their result against Switzerland.

In the scenario that Scotland draw with Switzerland, and Slovenia lose to North Macedonia, both sides would be level on eight points. However, Slovenia would take second place following their 2-1 win at Hampden Park last month, with UEFA prioritizing head-to-head results between tied teams rather than overall goal difference. Currently in third place on seven points, they trail Slovenia in second place on eight points.

The draw for the Nations League playoffs will take place on Friday, 20 November. The draw begins at 11am UK time and will take place at UEFA headquarters in Nyon, Switzerland.

You can stream the draw live via your smart TV, laptop, tablet or mobile phone, the entire ceremony will be streamed for free via UEFA.tv and the official UEFA YouTube channel.

If Scotland fail to beat Switzerland in their final Group B1 game, they will be guaranteed to finish in the third place and will remain in League B for the next edition of the tournament. North Macedonia are already guaranteed to finish fourth place will face a promotion/relegation playoff game with a second-placed League C team to remain in League B. Scotland are safe from relegation to League B.

Meanwhile, Switzerland have already won the group and have been promoted to League A. As detailed above, the second place team [currently Slovenia] will face a playoff against a third placed League A side.

A third placed finish will have a direct effect on Scotland’s Euro 2028 qualifying campaign, which begins in March 2027. The tournament, which takes place across the United Kingdom and the Republic of Ireland, will require all four co-hosts - England, Scotland, Wales, and the Republic of Ireland - to compete in the standard qualifying campaign. They are not given a bye.

Where teams finish in their Nations League groups will directly decide how they are seeded for Euro 2028 qualifying, with each nation seeded based on their final group position. If Scotland finish in third, then Pocognoli’s side would drop into pot three for the draw, which could result in them facing high-ranking opponents from Pot 1 and Pot 2.

The Euro 2028 qualifying draw sees teams separated into 12 groups of either four or five teams. Those who win their groups groups qualify automatically for the finals, alongside the eight highest ranked second place finishers. The four lowest ranked second place finishers enter the play-offs. Team who finish third or lower will not qualify.

The one caveat is that the host nations have two spots in the finals reserved if they fail to qualify. If more than two host teams do not qualify via the usual route, these two places will be allocated to the hosts placed highest in the European Qualifiers overall rankings. If only one or neither of the spots be used, the remaining places will be reallocated via the play-offs.
