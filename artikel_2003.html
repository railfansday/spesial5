Newcastle to lose '£75m-per-year' on 'even higher' interest rate - Finance Guru

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Newcastle United's owners could lose hundreds of millions simply on interest when building a new stadium away from St James' Park.

That is according to former Manchester City financial adviser Stefan Borson, who exclusively told Football Insider that interest rates are "even higher" in this day and age.

Heads have turned on Tyneside following the latest news around Newcastle's move away from St James' Park to a new super stadium.

PIF are already in talks with Goldman Sachs and Wall Street money men over a £1.3billion investment to build on Leazes Park.

All signs point towards leaving St James' Park, rather than splashing the cash to develop it, but either way, it's going to cost a lot of money.

And looking at Newcastle's early plans, there are some eye-watering figures being thrown around on Tyneside.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Borson believes that, in addition to the stadium cost, the interest rate would be equally as eye-watering.

"It could be £75m-a-year just on interest, right? And at the end of it, you've still got a £1.5 billion loan," he explained.

"So £75m is just to service the debt. We're not in a situation where if you look at Spurs, when they raised their money that, they were raising money at 2.5 per cent.

"Now it's more than double that and probably even higher. That is a lot of money, and just financing it in the current interest rate environment...

"Let's say they somehow got away at five per cent; you can see very quickly how expensive it's going to be per annum."

Unsurprisingly, fans are more confident now than ever before that PIF are sticking around for the future, and why shouldn't they be?

But Borson certainly hasn't ruled it out, suggesting that, while the plans are still just drawings on a page, there is no pressure to leave or inject cash.

This could just as easily be project for the Magpies' next owners, and anyone coming aboard will know that this will be part of the purchase.

Newcastle don't appear to be desperately in need of a new stadium, but with the owners wanting to compete against the best, they want it.
