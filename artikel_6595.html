Fall Harvest Minestrone

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Recipe by Margaret Knoebel, Milwaukee, Wisconsin

Our fall harvest minestrone trades summer’s vegetables for autumn’s produce. Break out the slow cooker!

You may be familiar with the usual cluster of summer vegetables in classic minestrone, but any Italian will tell you that real minestrone changes with the season. Italians like to prioritize whole, nutritious, in-season produce—especially those living in Italy’s blue zone, where some of the longest-living people eat minestrone every single day.

As we dive into fall, we’re taking our classic minestrone recipe with us and giving it a couple of tweaks. Our fall harvest minestrone ditches the zucchini, green beans, parsley, basil and spinach, and reflects what’s growing in the garden right now: sweet potatoes, onions, kale, sage and rosemary. We included Italian sausage, too, to enrich the broth and add a big boost of protein—enough for this fall soup to officially be considered dinner.

In a large skillet, cook the sausage over medium heat until it’s no longer pink, six to eight minutes, breaking it into crumbles with a wooden spoon. Drain the liquid from the skillet, then transfer the meat to a 6-quart slow cooker.

Stir in the beef broth, sweet potatoes, canned tomatoes, kale, onion, carrot, garlic, sage and rosemary. Cook the minestrone, covered, on low until the vegetables are tender, five to six hours.

Editor’s Tip: The sweet potatoes should be fork-tender before moving on to the next step.

Stir the pasta into the minestrone. Cook everything, covered, on low until the pasta is tender, 20 to 25 minutes longer.

Ladle the soup into bowls and serve. If desired, top the fall harvest minestrone with Parmesan cheese, red pepper flakes or fresh sage or rosemary.

Allow leftover fall harvest minestrone to cool completely to room temperature in under two hours. Transfer the soup to an airtight container and store it in the fridge for up to four days.

Yes, you can freeze fall harvest minestrone. Let the soup come to room temperature in under two hours, then ladle it into freezer-safe, airtight containers. Freeze the soup for up to two months. Thaw it overnight in the fridge before enjoying it again.

Yes, you can make fall harvest minestrone gluten free. Buy a box of good gluten-free pasta and make sure the Italian sausage is gluten free, too. I like to cook the gluten-free pasta separately when I suspect I’ll have leftovers. I add the pasta to individual bowls, then top it with the soup. Gluten-free noodles will absorb too much broth overnight, breaking down and becoming mushy.

The fall harvest minestrone has everything: vegetables, carbs and meat. It doesn’t really need anything else, but if you want to stretch the meal a bit, serve the soup with cornbread, biscuits (I love our garlic bread biscuits) or slices of crusty sourdough bread. Something like a vinegary salad will cut the richness a bit and bring in fresh flavor.
