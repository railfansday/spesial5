Melbourne Cup horse So You Are found dumped in skip after death

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Melbourne Cup horse So You Are has been found dumped in a Sydney skip following his death, leaving fans and his trainer demanding answers over how the racehorse’s remains were handled.

So You Are finished eighth in Australia’s most famous horse race on November 3, but his racing days sadly came to an end when he developed severe colic. Despite emergency treatment, trainer Bjorn Baker announced the beloved racehorse’s death on November 16.

However, a video uploaded to social media appeared to show the body of So You Are lying in a skip. The footage has since gone viral, with both horse fans and trainer Bjorn devastated by the lack of proper burial and the state in which the racehorse was left.

In his initial statement on X, Baker paid tribute to So You Are and those who had cared for him. “Beyond his achievements on the track, So You Are was a much-loved horse, and his loss will be deeply felt by everyone who knew and cared for him,” Baker wrote.

However, following the discovery of the horse’s body in a skip, Baker called for an explanation from the veterinary hospital that treated So You Are before his death.

“Like everyone who has seen the footage, I am deeply distressed by the handling of So You Are’s remains. He was a much-loved horse, and he deserved to be treated with dignity and respect after his death,” he said in his November 17 statement.

“I understand the specialist hospital facility REC Equine Specialists are investigating the circumstances, and I expect a full explanation of what occurred. My thoughts remain with the Ramsey family, and with all my staff, for whom this has compounded an already devastating loss.”

In a statement shared to news.com.au, REC Equine Specialists managing partner Dr Jonathan Lumsden has since apologized for the handling of So You Are’s remains. He explained that the horse had been humanely euthanized under anesthesia after emergency treatment for severe colic, but admitted he should have received a proper burial.

“After his death, So You Are was collected by our contracted service provider and taken to a licensed landfill. He should have been promptly and respectfully buried. What happened was unacceptable and disrespectful to So You Are and to everyone who cared for him. We are deeply sorry,” Lumsden said.

Although the hospital did not carry out the burial itself, Lumsden acknowledged its responsibility for choosing the contractor and making the arrangements.

“We should have known exactly how horses were handled after leaving our hospital, and we did not. That process is not good enough, and it must change.”

REC said it had stopped using the service provider and was reviewing how its contractors handle horses after death, from collection through to burial. It is also working with the facility operators and the state’s horse racing authority to establish what happened.

“No owner should have to go through what So You Are’s connections have experienced this week. We will make sure it does not happen again. Out of respect for So You Are and those who loved him, we ask that the footage not be shared further.”
