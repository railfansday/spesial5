Curtain Bangs Are an Elegant, Low-Maintenance Style for Mature Hair: How to Wear It Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Timeless and face-framing, this is the easiest way to update your haircut

Every product on this page was chosen by a Harper's BAZAAR editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

From Sabrina Carpenter to Dakota Johnson, curtain bangs were among the most fawned-over hairstyles in the past few years. It’s not surprising; they flatter all face shapes and offer the face-framing effect of a fringe without the harshness (or any awkward growing-out stage) of a full, blunt chop.

Curtain bangs are also versatile at any age, and the cut has recently stepped into the spotlight as one of the most flattering styles for mature lengths. Penélope Cruz’s recent red-carpet appearance makes a convincing case for the look.

"Curtain bangs are one of the most flattering fringe options for mature hair because they soften the face rather than dominate it," explains hairdresser David Cassidy from Hove's Palette & Bristle. "They frame the cheekbones, draw attention to the eyes, and create movement around the front of the hair, which instantly feels more youthful."

Unlike a blunt fringe, he adds that curtain bangs blend into the rest of the haircut, creating an elegant, modern look with just the right amount of youthfulness.

Here, he explains how to get the perfect curtain fringe for mature hair this winter, along with key styling pointers.

As with any bangs cut, an expert eye and professional hand are key to achieving a look that suits your face shape.

"The key with curtain bangs on mature hair is softness and proportion," says Cassidy. "You want them to start no shorter than the cheekbone and taper gently into the length. Anything too short or too heavy can feel aging."

He also emphasizes texture; a slightly broken-up finish keeps them from looking flat and gives a more relaxed, lived-in effect.

"Before committing to curtain bangs, think about your natural hair texture and growth pattern," he adds. "Cowlicks, partings, and how your hair falls when it air-dries all matter. A good stylist will tailor the fringe to work with your hair, not against it. Curtain bangs are versatile. When done well, they grow out seamlessly and can be worn parted, swept back, or tucked away."

Before you veer away from your signature everyday style, consider how much extra styling time a new fringe might require.

"Curtain bangs are surprisingly low-maintenance if they’re cut properly," says Cassidy. "A medium round brush or even a large Velcro roller at the front while the hair cools will give that soft sweep away from the face."

He recommends styling your curtain bangs with the hair dryer angled side to side rather than straight down. "It creates lift without stiffness and keeps your bangs looking light and flattering."
