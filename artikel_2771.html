Game Preview #19: Wolves at Thunder Thread

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Well, if it isn't our old friends the Oklahoma City Thunder? They've been suffering through their second straight year of heavy injuries, but the Wolves aren't the shoulder to cry on. One team has managed to reshape their roster and stay afloat despite losing their best player and other rotation to various ailments, the other is barely staying afloat while dealing with their own litany of players sidelined.

Let's see what we got cooking on Thanksgiving eve.

Who: Minnesota Timberwolves (13-5) at Oklahoma City Thunder (10-8)

When: Wednesday, November 25 at 6:30 PM CT

The back-to-back reigning Most Valuable Player, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, is number two league-wide in total free throws attempted. Sixth in total fouls drawn. I'm just listing that here for no particular reason.

SGA is also having his least efficient scoring season since the 2021-22 campaign. His three-point shooting volume is up, but it shouldn't be surprising to see his two-point percentage come down. It's because he hasn't been flanked by three of his starters: Jalen Williams suffered a gruesome leg injury, Cason Wallace tore his shoulder labrum, and Chet Holmgren has a tear in his knee. Many of these are long-term, if not season-ending, ailments.

The Thunder are finally feeling fear for the first time in the regular season in a while.

Shai has been forced to carry his team more than he's ever had to. His .358 usage percentage is second in the league behind just Luka Dončić. He's felt it, quite literally. He had to miss a game on Sunday due to a neck/back issue. Will he be at 100% against the Wolves on Wednesday? The Thunder better hope so.

Oklahoma City has long been known for their incredible depth (and collection of future draft picks). In fact, they were so rich in depth that they were paying to get off of rotation role players like Lu Dort, Isaiah Joe, and Aaron Wiggins. Had they known their starters were going to get bitten by the injury bug so badly, they'd probably undo some of those decisions. They're currently leaning heavily on holdovers like Isaiah Hartenstein and Kenrich Williams.

Until then, they've had some rookies and unfamiliar faces step up. You may recognize the pair of 2024 rookies in Ajay Mitchell and Jared McCain, who are the second and third leading scorers on the team. Additionally, their 2026 rookies have been a pleasant surprise for them. Mara has been force in the paint on both ends of the court, doing his best Holmgren impression. He stands at 7'2" and has a wingspan longer than Rocco Zikarsky (but shorter than Rudy's). Meanwhile, Stirtz has come on as of late, becoming a legitimate perimeter scoring threat.

Thunder general manager Sam Presti know how to pick 'em. Minnesota should be wise to not get caught sleeping on 'em.

This is a very casual observation, but the Thunder currently go only as far as Shai can take them. Their turnover rate is so low because they rely so much on isolation, particularly from SGA. They literally have to hope he can muster up enough points to carry them to a slim margin of victory.

In Oklahoma City's past eight games, they've won just two of them. The opponents were the Sacramento Kings and New Orleans Pelicans. Shai had to score 37 or more to even defeat those bottom-feeders (respectfully).

Minnesota has no shortage of defenders they can throw at him. The Thunder are a fairly small team (they just started Micah Peavy, a 6'7" guard, at power forward) so that should allow for guys Jaden McDaniels and Jonathan Kuminga to get opportunities to stop the Canadian.

Hartenstein between legs assist to McCain 3PM

Kings fans angrily post about Nique Clifford

Kyle Theige tweets about Brooks Barnhizer

Wolves lock up their NBA Cup elimination stage spot, 114-107.
