Diving deeper into Cavs last-second loss to Grizzlies: Progression isn’t always measured in final result

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

CLEVELAND — The Cleveland Cavaliers were two free-throw misses away from pulling out a victory over the Memphis Grizzlies. Instead, they fell 124-121 on a night they simply couldn't knock down a shot when they needed to most.

26 points on 9-of-11 shooting, 7 assists, 1 rebound, 1 block

Spencer couldn't miss from three, going 5-for-6 beyond the arc. The Cavs didn't have an answer for his ability to create space for pull-up threes. And when the defense overplayed him to prevent the three, he got around them to score in the paint himself or find a teammate. That's exactly what you want to see from someone with a lethal three-point shot.

On a night when seemingly everyone had a good offensive night, Spencer had the best.

34 points on 14-of-27 shooting, 3 rebounds, 3 assists, 1 steal

Mitchell has had strong scoring nights this season, but this might've been his most explosive outing yet. The perennial All-NBA guard was able to create so much separation between him and his defender with his first step. This allowed him to get into the paint at will and go 9-for-12 on shots in the restricted area.

Two things kept this from being an outstanding night for Mitchell.

First, the three-ball wasn't falling as he went 2-for-9 from beyond the arc. Creating space off the dribble helped him generate clean looks. They just didn't fall.

Second, Mitchell wasn't able to get a big shot to go down when he needed one most. The Cavs had several opportunities to make this a game that wasn't decided at the free-throw line, but they seemingly missed every one of them. That included a couple of errant shots from Mitchell in the fourth as he went 2-for-7 in the final quarter.

Merrill made a nice drive to the paint in the closing seconds of the game to force a trip to the line with two seconds left in a game the Cavs were trailing by one. Two free throws would've likely given the Cavs the win. One would've at least ensured overtime. Instead, the 89% career free-throw shooter missed both.

Rather than focusing on the missed free throws, Atkinson praised Merrill for getting t to the line afterward.

"I love the process," Atkinson said. " I love that he drove. He was so smart to drive the ball. That's what I look at. That's a great play. This is sports."

Overall, this was a rough game for Merrill, who made his return to the lineup after missing several games with an elbow injury. He went 1-for-4 on outside attempts and fouled out.

16 points, 7 rebounds, 5 assists, 1 steal

Hezonja made the most of his two starts so far this season. Against the Dallas Mavericks, he scored 22 points. He backed that up here with another all-around impressive performance.

Hezonja can create mismatches in a variety of ways, making him such a unique offensive weapon. Being able to stretch the floor, take someone in the post, and recognize when teammates have advantages allows him to pair well with a wide variety of lineups. He's someone who should get more minutes when it's a favorable matchup like this.

You seem to always get the best version of Allen when you make him a point of emphasis in the game plan.

On Wednesday, the Cavs made a concerted effort to attack opposing center Zach Edey. One of the ways they did that was to try to get Edey to guard out in space, which meant they used Allen even more than normal as an on-ball screen and roller, and as a playmaking hub at the top of the key. Allen excelled in both areas.

Allen is a much more versatile player than he gets credit for being. He's a solid passer, can attack off the dribble with his speed, and isn't a bad ball handler. These were all on display in a game where the Cavs made it a point to feature him.

The second block was incredible. I'm not sure if I've seen anything exactly like that.

"We all trust him, you know, and I would do it again tomorrow. I'd do it again the next night. You're not defined by two free throws at the end of the game."

– Donovan Mitchell on Sam Merrill's two free-throw misses

The Cavs were in the 99th percentile for offensive rebounding (20) and rebounding overall.

The Grizzlies came into this game sixth in both offensive rebounding and second-chance points. The Cavs made sure they weren't able to use that against them this evening.

Even though the Cavs didn't get the result they wanted, this was an impressive performance on the glass for a team that has struggled with rebounding.

Results matter — even in the regular season. From that perspective, this was a failure. Cleveland had a chance to win, but didn't.

At the same time, this Cavs team won't be defined by November results. Postseason success or failure will do so. The process is far and away more important this time of year.

There were some good things to take away from this game from a process perspective. Cleveland put an emphasis on rebounding against one of the best rebounding teams in the league, and they succeeded. Their offense flowed better than it has in recent games as well. Part of that could be attributed to James Harden slowly shifting to a less ball-dominant approach. Role players like Hezonja and Meleek Thomas having strong outings once again is another positive sign. You could also point to the defense in the second half as a positive, as they held Memphis to just 53 points and a much more manageable 112.8 offensive rating then. Those are all good things they can build off of.

"I told everyone in the locker room, we want to learn through wins," Mitchell said. "I've told them that a thousand times. But this loss to me — I don't say this often — it was a good loss for us. There's things we can really take away from watching film. Not even just a schematic standpoint, just the effort, resilience."

It's easy to get caught up in wins and losses, especially after defeats like this. However, there's reason to believe this team is heading in the right direction.

"I'm more so proud of everybody in the locker room than anything else tonight because this is what we need to do," Mitchell said. "We'll grow into that. We're getting the feel for each other, but this is something that we got to continue to carry [forward]."
