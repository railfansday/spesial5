Rashan Gary’s inexcusable penalty opened the door to final collapse

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The Cowboys should have had Arizona facing a desperate decision on a fourth down. Either go for a risky first down on 4th-and-10, or take a safe field goal that would still leave Dallas up seven. Instead, Rashan Gary handed them a lifeline.

With Dallas leading 27-17 late in the fourth quarter, Gary's roughing-the-passer penalty wiped out an incompletion and gave the Cardinals a fresh set of downs. One play later, Arizona was in the end zone. Suddenly, a game the Cowboys should have closed out became another nervous finish, ultimately ending in a 33-30 overtime defeat.

Dallas had already weathered one Arizona response. After taking a commanding 24-10 lead early in the fourth quarter, the Cowboys allowed Tyler Allgeier to score again, cutting the margin to just seven. However, the Cowboys' offense then put together a lengthy possession, before Brandon Aubrey's 35-yard field goal upped the lead to 27-17 with 3:54 remaining. With the clock working in their favor, Dallas should have held on to the victory.

Dallas needed the defense to make Arizona earn every play. Instead, on third-and-10 from the Dallas 29 with 2:23 remaining, Gardner Minshew's pass towards the end zone fell incomplete. However, Gary committed an unnecessary helmet-to-helmet hit on the quarterback, giving Arizona 15 yards and an automatic first down at the Dallas 14. This turned fourth-and-10 into another scoring opportunity for Arizona without the Cardinals having to complete a pass.

It was an inexcusable mistake from a veteran defender. The punishment was immediate. Minshew found an uncovered Trey McBride for a 14-yard touchdown on the next snap. The extra point cut Dallas' lead to 27-24, and Arizona had scored before the two-minute warning.

The timing of Gary's mistake made the penalty even more damaging. Arizona still had the two-minute warning to stop the clock during Dallas' next possession. Gary's error had helped the Cardinals quickly get into the end zone, preserving a realistic chance of regaining possession. This desperate comeback now had a viable path forward.

Gary does not carry all the blame for what followed. The coverage failure on McBride's touchdown, the offense's inability to close and the final defensive collapse each deserves scrutiny. But his penalty was the moment Dallas surrendered a major advantage it had already earned. With Arizona running short of chances, the Cowboys supplied another one for free. For a team trying to prove it could win consecutive games, that lack of discipline was painfully costly, and this was the turning point of the game.
