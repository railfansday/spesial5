Pay Less for Meat – The Big Savings Are in Slow-Cooking Cuts, Not Bone-In Chicken

You can keep eating meat and still pay less for it. Choose slow-cooking cuts like chuck roast over steak, and buy bone-in chicken thighs only when they’re under about 60% of the price of boneless breasts. Cook or freeze markdowns the day you buy them, and stock up when your usual cuts go on sale.

USDA’s Food Price Outlook forecasts 2026 price increases of 8.7% for beef and veal, 1.2% for poultry, and 1% for pork.

By USDA’s Food Buying Guide, a pound of boneless breast cooks down to about 12 ounces of meat. Once the bones and skin come off, a pound of bone-in thighs gives about 7 ounces and a pound of whole chicken about 6. So thighs are the cheaper meat only when priced under about 60% of the breast price, and a whole chicken only under about half.

In USDA’s survey of supermarket ads for the week of Nov. 9, boneless skinless breasts averaged $2.95 a pound, boneless thighs $3.51, and bone-in thighs $1.50. Ounce for ounce, the bone-in thigh meat cost about 14% less than breast meat. At $1.57, whole chickens cost about 8% more than breasts per ounce of meat, unless you eat the skin.

For beef and pork, choose the cuts made for slow cooking. In the same week’s beef ads, boneless chuck roast averaged $7.54 a pound against $13.35 for boneless strip steak, and the pork ads had bone-in shoulder at $2.20 against $3.18 for bone-in center-cut chops. Precut stew meat cost $8.59 a pound, so cube a chuck roast yourself or ask the meat counter to.

The sell-by date tells the store when to pull a package, not when the meat turns unsafe, USDA says. FoodSafety.gov’s storage chart gives ground meat and poultry only a day or two in the fridge, so cook or freeze a markdown the day you buy it.

When a cut you use goes on sale, buy several weeks’ worth, split it into meal-size bags, and freeze it. At 0°F, it can be stored indefinitely, though by the same chart, roasts, steaks, and chops are best within 4 to 12 months and ground meat within 3 to 4. Then serve smaller portions of meat with more beans, grains, and vegetables.

Check this week’s store ad for bone-in thighs under about 60% of the breast price or a chuck roast on sale, and buy enough to freeze.
