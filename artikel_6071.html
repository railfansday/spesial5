Alaska Atmos Rewards Business Card Review: 90K Points & Strong Perks

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Atmos™ Rewards Visa Signature® Business Card is the business credit card of Atmos Rewards, which is the loyalty program of Alaska Airlines and Hawaiian Airlines. The card is issued by Bank of America, and is a rebranding of the former Alaska Airlines Visa Business Card.

The card has just undergone a full refresh, and there are many reasons to consider picking up this card, especially if you fly Alaska or Hawaiian with any frequency. This post is my in-depth Atmos Rewards Business Card review, so I’d like to take a look at the card’s value proposition, for anyone who may be considering applying.

Link: Learn more about the Atmos™ Rewards Visa Signature® Business Card

Before I get into my Atmos Rewards Business Card review, here’s a brief summary of the key details of the card:

Below are what I consider to be the pros and cons of the Atmos Rewards Business Card.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card is the only business card in the Atmos Rewards ecosystem. To start, let’s cover the welcome bonus, card eligibility rules, annual fee, and more, since these are important factors that cardmembers consider.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card is offering a limited time welcome bonus where you’ll get 90,000 bonus points after you make $6,000 or more in purchases within the first 90 days of opening your account. This is the best publicly available bonus that we’ve ever seen on the card, and you can find the bonus offer history in this post.

Based on my latest miles & points valuations, Atmos Rewards points are worth 1.5 cents each, given the great uses of these rewards. That means the 90,000 points are worth $1,350 for me.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card is issued by Bank of America. The good news is that eligibility for this card is unrelated to having any other Atmos Rewards card, so you’re eligible for this card (including the bonus) if you have the Atmos™ Rewards Ascent Visa Signature® credit card (learn more) or Atmos™ Rewards Summit Visa Infinite® Credit Card (learn more).

Let me emphasize that you are eligible for this product if you have any of the previously issued personal or business HawaiianMiles Barclays cards, or any of the previously issued personal Alaska Mileage Plan cards.

Beyond that, Bank of America doesn’t have many consistent restrictions when it comes to getting approved for business cards. While Bank of America has the 2/3/4 rule and 3/12 rules, those apply to personal cards, and not business cards. Generally those with a Bank of America banking relationship also have the best odds of approval (though it’s by no means a requirement).

There’s not a consistent credit score you need to have to be approved for this card, though personally I’d hope to have a score in the “good” to “excellent” range, ideally 700+ points. However, people with lower scores have been approved, and people with higher scores have been rejected.

Applying for this card won’t count as a further card toward Chase’s 5/24 limit, in case that’s a limit you’re trying to stay under.

I should also mention the following restriction in the terms:

I also understand that I may be declined for additional credit card accounts if my business entity currently has, or has had, another business card with Bank of America in the preceding 24 month period.

On the surface, this suggests you may be declined for a Bank of America business card if you have another Bank of America business card. However, anecdotally, this doesn’t appear to be enforced much. I think the term “may” is the key here (which is different than “will”).

The Atmos Rewards Business Card has a $195 annual fee. That’s on the steep side, though the card does have some premium perks. The good news is that you can add employee cards at no extra cost, so that annual fee covers the cost for all the cards you’ll have.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card has bonus categories that can help you rack up points faster. Furthermore, spending on the card can help you earn Atmos Rewards elite status. Let’s talk about how rewarding spending on the card can be.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card offers 10x points per dollar spent on flights booked through the Atmos for Business website. If you’re looking to maximize your earning, this is where you want to book your Alaska and Hawaiian flights, as this is an incredible deal.

You do need your own business to participate in the Atmos for Business program, and once registered, the return on spending is really tough to beat. Based on my valuation of points, that’s like an incremental 15% return on eligible airfare spending, on top of what you otherwise earn.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card offers 3x points on all other eligible Alaska and Hawaiian purchases, with no caps. Based on my valuation of Atmos Rewards points, that’s like a 4.5% return on spending. So if you fly either of those Alaska Air Group carriers with any frequency, that’s a solid return on that spending.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card offers 2x points on eligible dining purchases. Based on my valuation of Atmos Rewards points, that’s like a 1.5% return on spending. It’s always nice to earn bonus points, though in fairness, 2x points isn’t exactly the biggest return on spending that we’ve seen.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card offers 1x points on all other eligible spending. Based on my valuation of Atmos Rewards points, that’s like a 1.5% return on spending. As you might expect, there are more exciting cards for everyday spending, as independently, that’s not a terribly exciting return.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card can help you earn elite status in the Atmos Rewards program. Specifically, the card offers one status point for every $3 in eligible spending. There’s no cap on how many status points you can earn with the card.

If you are serious about earning status with spending, I’d recommend instead considering the Atmos™ Rewards Summit Visa Infinite® Credit Card (learn more), which offers one status point for every $2 in eligible spending.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card also offers a Bank of America relationship bonus. If you have an eligible small business Bank of America checking or savings account, you’ll receive a bonus equal to 10% of the points you earn on each dollar spent.

This is a way to earn even more rewards, as you’d earn 11x points on Atmos for Business purchases, 3.3x points on Alaska and Hawaiian purchases, 2.2x points on the other purchases eligible for bonus categories, and 1.1x points on everyday spending.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card has no foreign transaction fees, so it’s a decent card to use for purchases abroad, since you can rack up rewards without paying a fee.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card offers a variety of benefits and credits, which can potentially more than justify the annual fee year after year. These perks are the main reason you want to consider picking up this card, in addition to the great welcome bonus. In no particular order, let’s go over the main benefits.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card offers a free checked bag for you and up to six guests on the same reservation when flying on Alaska or Hawaiian. In order to be eligible for this perk, you need to charge the cost of the ticket to your card. Another great thing is that authorized users on the card can also receive this benefit, as long as their ticket is purchased with the card, so that’s a huge incentive to add authorized users to the card.

This is a great benefit that could save you a lot of money — Alaska and Hawaiian ordinarily charge $30-45 per checked bag, so that’s a savings of up to $90 per person roundtrip, if checking bags. If you fly Alaska or Hawaiian fairly often but don’t have elite status, that alone could quickly more than justify the annual fee.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card offers preferred boarding for you and up to six guests on the same reservation. In order to be eligible for this perk, you need to charge the cost of the ticket to your card. Another great thing is that authorized users on the card can also receive this benefit, as long as their ticket is purchased with the card.

This is a valuable benefit that helps you ensure that there’s space in the overhead bin for your bags. It’s also an awesome authorized user perk, so that you can share this benefit with others.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card offers four Alaska Lounge passes annually. The way this works, the primary cardmember will have one Alaska Lounge pass deposited into their Atmos Rewards account at the beginning of the quarter, valid through the end of the quarter.

Each is valid for access to Alaska Lounges on a single calendar day, in conjunction with a same day flight operated by Alaska, Hawaiian, a oneworld airline, or an Alaska global partner. Each pass is valid for one adult, plus accompanying children. For what it’s worth, a day pass would otherwise cost $60 per person.

If you’re the primary cardmember on the Atmos Rewards Business Card, you can share your Atmos Rewards points with other members at no cost. The way this works, you can link your accounts with up to 10 other Atmos Rewards members (they don’t have to be family — accounts of friends are fine as well), and then you can move points back and forth for free.

I’d argue that this is especially compelling for families, because it’s easier to rack up a sizable balance of points when you can consolidate them into one account.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card offers 20% back on Alaska and Hawaiian inflight purchases when paying with your card. This applies for the purchase of food, beverages, and Wi-Fi onboard flights, and the statement credit should post to your account within seven days. There’s no limit to how often you can take advantage of this.

Let me just mention that Alaska and Hawaiian should have free Starlink Wi-Fi fleetwide by some point in 2027, at which point there won’t be savings anymore with Wi-Fi. That being said, it can still be used for food and beverages.

If you’re the primary cardmember on the Atmos Rewards Business Card, then you’re eligible for a free same day confirmed flight change on Alaska. This ordinarily costs $50 per person, so that’s quite valuable.

With this, you can change to another flight that might be more convenient, subject to capacity controls. This is especially useful in high frequency markets.

If you’re the primary cardmember on the Atmos Rewards Business Card, you’re eligible to receive a $50 voucher if you have an Alaska flight that is delayed by at least two hours, or that’s canceled within 24 hours of departure.

The $50 voucher is valid for 48 hours, and will be delivered to the email address associated with the reservation, and can be used for food, drinks, etc. Travel must have been booked directly with Alaska using the eligible credit card.

Those with the Atmos Rewards Business Card receive up to a $120 airport security credit every four years, in connection with TSA PreCheck or Global Entry. This is a perk offered by many premium credit cards, and there’s lots of value to this.

When talking about an Atmos Rewards credit card, it’s worth acknowledging the general value in earning and redeeming Atmos Rewards points. Alaska & Hawaiian are both in the oneworld alliance, and Atmos Rewards partners not only with oneworld partner airlines, but also with all kinds of other unaligned airlines. I consider Atmos Rewards to be one of the most valuable points currencies out there.

Atmos Rewards has a distance based award chart, which varies a bit by region. For example, below is the one-way award chart within the Americas.

I constantly redeem Atmos Rewards points for travel within the United States, particularly on American. For example, all the time I redeem 4,500 points to fly one-way economy from Miami to Tampa (when cash fares would often be $200+), or 15,000 points to fly one-way business class from Miami to Chicago or New York (when cash fares would often be $500+). You simply can’t beat these redemption rates.

The icing on the cake is that with Atmos Rewards, you earn status points for award flights, so those flights even count toward elite status, and that’s also why I’ve switched my business to Atmos Rewards.

Of course there are lots of other opportunities with Atmos Rewards points as well. For example, I love the ability to redeem 75,000 points for one-way business class from the United States to many parts of Asia on carriers like Japan Airlines or Starlux Airlines — those are both awards that I’ve booked for myself in the past, and they were a great value.

What makes this an even better value is that you can have a free stopover on a one-way award when traveling long haul. One thing worth being aware of is that Atmos Rewards has a $20 per person per direction partner award booking fee. However, that can be waived if you have the Atmos Rewards Summit Card.

The Atmos Rewards Business Card is the only business card in the Atmos Rewards ecosystem, so how valuable is this product? A few thoughts…

First of all, I think there’s a lot of merit to picking up this card for the excellent welcome bonus, which includes a lot of points. The card also has significant ongoing benefits. The card also doesn’t count toward the Chase 5/24 limit, which is nice. Given the value of the Atmos Rewards ecosystem, there’s no reason not to pick up this card, if eligible.

What about the long term value proposition of the card, though? If you fly Alaska or Hawaiian with any frequency and don’t have elite status, the basic perks add up, including a first checked bag free, preferred boarding, a quarterly lounge pass, free points sharing, and more.

However, I’d say there’s also merit to picking up one (or both) of the personal Atmos Rewards cards…

The $395 annual fee Atmos Rewards Summit Card is the most premium Atmos Rewards credit card. While it’s a personal card, I still think it’s worth highlighting, as it’s incredibly lucrative. I’d take it a step further, and say that it’s my favorite airline credit card.

Among other things, the card racks up status points at the fastest rate, the card allows you to earn Global Companion Awards, offers Alaska Lounge passes, enables free points sharing, waives partner award booking fees, offers 3x points on foreign purchases, and much more.

If you fly Alaska or Hawaiian with any frequency, then getting this card is an absolute no-brainer. As I see it, the anniversary 25K-point Global Companion Award alone basically justifies the annual fee, and that says nothing of all the other amazing perks.
