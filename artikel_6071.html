As it uses its airports and surrounding land to build a hotel business, Adani is betting that its planned airport cities can attract enough businesses and visitors to support thousands of hotel rooms.

India’s biggest private airport operator is becoming a hotel developer.

Adani Group has signed deals with Indian Hotels Company and IHG Hotels & Resorts to build about 3,500 rooms across all eight of its airports, using land it already controls and leaving the hotel companies to run them.

The latest agreement, announced Oct. 29, brings together Adani Airport Holdings (AAHL) and Indian Hotels Company (IHCL) to develop eight hotels with more than 2,000 rooms combined, across Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Lucknow and Guwahati. The hotels will operate under IHCL’s Taj, Vivanta, Gateway and Ginger brands.

The deal follows a May announcement by Adani Airport Holdings and IHG Hotels & Resorts to develop five hotels delivering nearly 1,500 rooms across Jaipur, Navi Mumbai, Mangaluru and Thiruvananthapuram. The partnership will also bring IHG’s Kimpton brand to India.

Adani Group, India's biggest private airport operator, is moving into hotel development without building a hotel brand of its own. Deals with Indian Hotels Company (IHCL) and IHG Hotels & Resorts cover about 3,500 rooms across all eight airports Adani Airport Holdings operates, with Adani supplying the land and the hotel companies handling branding and operations. The IHCL agreement, announced Oct. 29, covers eight hotels and more than 2,000 rooms in Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Lucknow, and Guwahati under the Taj, Vivanta, Gateway, and Ginger brands; the May IHG agreement covers five hotels and nearly 1,500 rooms in Jaipur, Navi Mumbai, Mangaluru, and Thiruvananthapuram, and brings Kimpton to India. The hotels sit inside a larger plan: Adani Airport City is developing more than 655 acres across six airports, with a first-phase investment of more than $2.1 billion to build over 2 million square meters of hotels, offices, retail, entertainment, and convention space. Adani is not alone — Omaxe announced in June a roughly $656 million plan for 19 hotels in 13 cities — and ICRA expects premium supply in India's 12 largest markets to grow 5-6% annually through fiscal 2028 against 8-9% demand growth. The commercial test is whether Adani can attract enough office tenants, retailers, and convention business to fill rooms that airport traffic alone won't.
