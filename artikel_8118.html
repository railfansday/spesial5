Calling Washington Backpackers: This $5,000 Grant Could Fund Your PCT Thru-Hike

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Walking may be free, but a thru-hike is decidedly expensive: Some Pacific Crest Trail trekkers run up a bill north of $10,000 during a months-long hike. That cost, plus the need to take off work for half a year, presents massive barriers to many would-be thru-hikers.

Seattleite Geoff Burg knows this—and he wants to help. With fellow Washington hikers Keya Gillenwater and Madelyn Scherrer, Burg launched the Pacific Crest Trail Grant in 2025 to award $5,000 to a Washington-based hiker with dreams of completing the trail.

Burg’s connection to the PCT goes back a decade. The attorney was hiking with his kids near Stevens Pass in 2016 when he came across some hikers in distress. Burg volunteered to drive the duo, a pair of PCT thru-hikers in their 60s, into town for medical supplies, then offered them a place to stay in his family’s home. The couple told Burg all about their thru-hike and ultralight philosophy—and the encounter stuck with him.

Over the next several years, Burg chipped away at a section hike of the Washington segment of the PCT. He fell in love with being on the trail, alone or with his kids, and with the greater hiking community around it. Several years later, a conversation with his massage therapist sparked the idea of a scholarship. Well established in his career, and with experience as an advisor for a youth outdoor leadership program, Burg was in a position to help others achieve their long trail dreams.

“We give scholarships for football and baseball and everything else,” he says. “Why not for hiking?”

So he connected with former mentees Gillenwater and Scherrer, a PCT alum, and formed a committee. The grant would offer financial support to a young Washingtonian hoping to complete the long trail.

Burg especially wanted to support young hikers with a financial need; applicants must be between the ages of 18 and 33, with strong ties to Washington state. They should also be able to demonstrate past backpacking experience, which Burg hopes will improve the recipient’s chances of succeeding on a thru-hike and paying the experience forward through volunteer projects like youth outreach or LNT education. Grant recipients who meet that criteria are chosen via a lottery, and must stay in touch with the grant committee during their hikes. Burg emphasizes that the process is not merit-based.

“We already have a community of people that can do this or who want to do this, but within that broader community, there’s so many people that can’t afford it,” he says.

Thirty-two-year-old Seattleite Kayla Provost was the grant’s first recipient. She completed her trek in September 2026, and says she couldn’t have finished the thru-hike without the financial support.

Provost had previously worked as a naturalist guide and, as a Washington resident, felt deeply connected to the Cascades. After losing her mom a few years prior, Provost felt compelled to change her lifestyle. She felt called to the PCT, but financial hurdles posed challenges. After searching “grants for thru-hiking” online, Provost stumbled across a Reddit post about the PCT Grant.

“The grant was truly a lifeline for me,” she says. “Beyond the financial support, the monthly check-ins with the grant committee gave me a sense of accountability and reminded me I had people cheering me on.”

She says the experience redoubled her desire to facilitate outdoor experiences for other people.

“It is incredibly powerful to make a choice, trust yourself enough to follow through, and learn to be comfortable with the uncomfortable,” she says. “The trail offered me a deep sense of confidence, gratitude, and trust in my own ability to navigate the unknown.”

The grant will fund its second thru-hiker in 2027. Through donations, Burg hopes to grow the program into a self-sustaining fund to support more hikers on more trails.

Applications for prospective 2027 PCT thru-hikers are currently open. Washingtonians who meet the criteria can apply by January 31.
