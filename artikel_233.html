California senator Adam Schiff said “the need for a permitting reform package has only grown in the past decade” but that current bipartisan legislation intended to speed up permitting reviews for data centers and other infrastructure projects “needs further improvement before I can support it.”

In a lengthy social media post, Schiff, a Democrat, said “the transmission and grid reform provisions are very positive” but “that we need to have confidence that any permitting reform legislation is not going to be weaponized by this or any future administration to single out states and provide giveaways to the oil and gas industries.”

Schiff added that the “legislation would jeopardize tribal sovereignty through major changes to the National Historic Preservation Act, impact the State of California’s authorities under the Clean Water Act, and constrict federal agencies’ ability to protect vulnerable wildlife under the Endangered Species Act.”

Chris van Hollen said he is in “listening mode” and hasn’t “shut the door” on a possible bid for Senate majority leader.

He added, “In response to people calling upon me to step up here, what I’ve said on it is. ‘I’m in listening mode and haven’t shut the door.’”

In its first major move since last week’s elections, the Senate has advanced bipartisan legislation to speed up permitting reviews for data centers and other infrastructure projects.

The Senate voted 73-25 to formally take up the bill, which would shorten the timeline for approving both clean and fossil fuel energy projects.

The Supreme Court will allow the Trump administration to resume border wall construction in the Big Bend region of Texas. The majority paused a lower court’s decision as the case plays out. The Trump administration had filed an emergency appeal, saying there are illegal border crossings and drug seizures in the region.

“This horrific decision clears the way for contractors to bulldoze pristine desert and dynamite canyon walls before the courts have even decided whether this destruction is legal,” Laiken Jordahl, national public lands advocate at the Center for Biological Diversity, one of the groups that sued to stop the construction, told the Associated Press.

The man accused of assassinating Charlie Kirk will stand trial in June 2028, a Utah judge ruled today.

District Judge Tony Graf allocated 105 days for the trial, with jury selection slated to begin on 21 June 2028. Graf said the 2028 date balances the defense’s right to prepare with the Kirk family’s desire for a speedy resolution.

Attorneys for defendant Tyler Robinson, 23, had proposed a trial starting in 2029. Robinson could be eligible for the death penalty if convicted.

In a statement, the Kirk family called the hearing date a “delay” and said that “every additional day without justice prolongs our family’s pain.”

A press conference by US attorney general Todd Blanche interrupted efforts to count ballots in Jackson, Mississippi. The press conference comes as the state of Mississippi is in embroiled in a tight Senate race with a razor thin margin. Democrat Scott Colom leads incumbent Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith – if he wins, Colom will be the first Democratic senator Mississippi has sent to the Senate in decades and the first Black senator the state has sent since the Reconstruction era.

Dozens of police officers idled outside buildings in downtown Jackson while Blanche spoke inside the Jackson City Hall. Elsewhere, officers hovered on rooftops, patrolling local streets and circling in police cars. Many streets around the Hinds County Circuit Clerk office, where votes are still being counted, are blocked off. Thomas Massie floated Florida governor Ron DeSantis as a potential replacement for defense secretary Pete Hegseth during a press conference on the eight articles of impeachment he introduced against Hegseth today.

“I will name a name because I suspect he doesn’t want the job,” Massie said, after initially deferring to name a contender because “if I named a name, they would probably never put that person in there.”

In a press conference full of sharp remarks, Massie said he was advocating for the impeachment in his final weeks in Congress because “the time for waiting around and being polite and introducing bills and waiting six years to try to get them inside of some other bill, that time is over for me.”

“I am trying to force a vote here on this issue because I think it’s at the heart and soul of our republic, who has the war powers,” he added.

Republican Congressman Thomas Massie has introduced eight articles of impeachment against Pete Hegseth, refiling a resolution in the hope of ousting the defense secretary. The House has two days to take it up. This could result in a vote on the floor, a formal investigation or the measure could be tabled. All require a simple majority (218 votes) in the House.

Massie, the outgoing representative from Kentucky, accused Hegseth of illegally waging war in Iran without congressional authorization and abusing his powers. The articles also focuses on Hegseth’s move to strike several boats in the Caribbean that have killed over 200 people throughout the campaign. The resolution also points to the defense secretary’s targeted Democratic Senator Mark Kelly, a former Navy captain, for telling troops to resist unlawful orders. Hegseth formally censured Kelly, accused him of seditious statements, and moved to reduce his retired military rank and pension. A federal judge later blocked the Pentagon’s effort.

While Democrats are expected to back the impeachment effort, some Republicans have also expressed some support, even though they hold a narrow majority. A reminder that for the removal of an impeached official, there needs to be 67 votes in the upper chamber. Speaking to reporters, Massie insisted that his articles of impeachment are “not symbolic”. “He is not doing a good job. In addition to breaking the law and violating the constitution, he’s not doing a good job,” Massie said. “If he stays in power, and Republicans reject this – what message do you think he sends to the American public after last week’s election?”
