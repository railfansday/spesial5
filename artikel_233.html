After traveling through the void of space for 49 years, Voyager 1 has hit a momentous milestone: The spacecraft is now officially 1 light-day away from Earth, which is, as NASA put it, “very, very far.” The probe crossed the milepost on Wednesday at 5:16AM ET and is now 16.094 billion miles away from our planet — equal to the distance that light travels in 24 hours.

It’s the farthest that any human-made object has ever traveled, and Voyager 1’s journey isn’t over yet. It’s been heading toward a star called Gliese 445 in the Ursa Minor constellation since completing its primary objectives to conduct flybys of Jupiter and Saturn in 1980. That star is 17 light-years away, meaning Voyager 1 won’t pass by it for around 40,000 years, despite traveling at nearly 38,000 mph.

The light-day milestone is a remarkable feat alone, but especially so when you consider that Voyager 1 is still operational. NASA has done well to keep it functioning, and it can continue to send back readings on plasma waves, magnetic fields, and space-bound particles, though it now takes radio signals an entire day to travel between Earth and the spacecraft. Voyager 2, which launched 16 days before its twin, is also expected to reach a distance of 1 light-day away from Earth, but not until 2035.
