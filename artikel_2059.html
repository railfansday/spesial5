Tom Cannon in, George Hirst out: How Stoke City's dream January transfer window looks?

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Stoke City are dreaming of a return to the Premier League once again after an excellent start to the 2026/27 campaign, and if Mark Robins' side can maintain this level of form for the next couple of months, the January transfer window becomes crucial to their efforts.

Stoke are flying right now in the Championship, sitting level on points with Birmingham City and Middlesbrough in 1st and 2nd, and narrowly ahead of West Bromwich Albion and Wolverhampton Wanderers in 4th and 5th, respectively.

The Potters, after three defeats on the spin to open the season against Swansea City, Southampton, and Wolverhampton, have followed that up with just one defeat in their following 12, winning nine.

If Mark Robins' side can maintain this level of form into the new year, the January window presents an opportunity to go all out and do what they can to secure promotion, as Football League World takes a look at what a dream window in the Potteries may look like.

Stoke have four different options they could start up front right now, namely George Hirst, Robert Bozenik, Lamine Cisse, and Sam Gallagher, though none of them has really been able to nail down a spot in the starting XI this campaign.

Hirst arrived in the summer from Ipswich Town for a potential £10 million, and with just one goal and one assist in 14 Championship appearances thus far, he failed to justify that fee.

Cisse enjoyed a run of games in the side between mid-September and mid-October, but injury has meant that Bozenik has been preferred up top as of late, whilst Gallagher also recovers from a fresh injury.

Despite this bloated list of strikers, there have been cries for another option up top from Stoke supporters as of late, with Sheffield United's Tom Cannon a name that is well-known in the Potteries.

Cannon, 23, scored 11 times in 25 appearances across all competitions on loan for Stoke from Leicester City during the first half of the 2024/25 campaign, before subsequently joining Sheffield United in January 2025 for around £10 million.

Things have not quite worked out at Bramall Lane for Cannon just yet, and given his popularity at the bet365 Stadium, he would be a dream addition for many supporters; whether a deal is likely, however, is doubtful.

If Cannon were to arrive, it would surely mean that one of their current strike force must depart, and following his lack of impact since his arrival, Hirst would likely be many fans' option to make way.

The former Leicester and Blackburn Rovers striker has never been prolific in his career, hitting double digits in goals in a season just twice at 27, despite earning three promotions with Ipswich under Kieran McKenna.

Whilst it is unlikely Hirst will be going anywhere anytime soon, his failure to justify his hefty fee, as well as Stoke supporters' preference for Cisse, Bozenik, and Gallagher, make him the ideal player to depart.

In reality, one of Bozenik or Gallagher is more likely to leave in January; with the latter's contract due to expire in the summer and now aged 31, it's doubtful he will be offered fresh terms, especially with a subpar injury record.

There are numerous players, as a result of Stoke's summer recruitment, that have been pushed out to the fringes of the squad, as the likes of Bae Jun-ho, Million Manhoef, Tomas Rigo, Ben Pearson, and Ben Gibson have seen their minutes slashed.

Jun-ho, 23, is due to enter the final 12 months of his deal in North Staffordshire, and at this stage, a fresh deal appears incredibly unlikely, as the emergence of Justin Devenny and Jack McGlynn has seen the Korean's game time dwindle.

Ben Pearson has managed just 14 minutes of Championship football this season, with Ethan Galbraith, Djibril Soumare, and Svante Ingelsson's arrivals knocking the midfielder down the pecking order.

Manhoef, Rigo, and Gibson have all barely featured as well, though the most likely to leave in the winter are surely Jun-ho and Pearson, as time will tell how Stoke's season unfolds.
