Q4 Isn&#8217;t the Offseason: Rethinking Active Audience Engagement and Brand Loyalty

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

What if Q4 wasn’t the offseason for active consumers, but an opportunity for brands to connect with them in entirely new ways?

As temperatures drop and outdoor activities change, consumer behavior shifts rather than disappears.

For marketers, understanding these seasonal patterns is key to building meaningful engagement, driving product discoverability, and creating brand value that extends well beyond the holiday shopping calendar.

In this episode of Adspeak by ADWEEK, host Jen Starr, director of North America at Strava and head of Strava for Business, sits down with Daniel Rosenberg, senior brand director at Oakley who heads brand activation andpPartnerships for Costa Del Mar.

They explore how brands can rethink their Q4 marketing strategy, engage performance-driven audiences year-round, and build stronger connections through community, partnerships, and consumer insights.

The conversation examines how winter activity trends challenge the assumption that athletic engagement slows down in colder months. Strava data reveals significant increases in yoga uploads, weight training, and virtual rides, highlighting how athletes adapt their routines as outdoor activities move indoors.

The guests discuss how brands can use these behavioral insights to inform seasonal consumer engagement, from fitness campaigns and snow sports marketing to athlete partnerships and marathon sponsorships.

They also explore why brands need to offer more than Black Friday discounts and Cyber Week promotions to remain relevant. By adding genuine value throughout the year, staying meaningfully present in consumers’ lives, and making products easier to discover, marketers can build lasting relationships rather than temporary conversions.

Daniel Rosenberg is a senior brand and marketing leader with more than a decade of experience at the intersection of sport, culture, and consumer behavior. He serves as Senior Brand Director for Oakley and Head of Brand Activations & Partnerships for Costa Del Mar at EssilorLuxottica North America. Previously, Daniel spent over ten years agency-side, developing integrated marketing programs for brands including Nike and Meta. His work focuses on building brand relevance, driving commercial growth, and creating authentic consumer engagement.

Jen Starr is an award-winning sales leader with more than 20 years of experience connecting brands with the communities that matter most. She currently serves as Director, North America, for Strava for Business, helping brands engage with Strava’s global community of 195 million athletes. Previously, she held leadership roles at BuzzFeed and Nextdoor, building and leading high-performing enterprise teams. A passionate culture builder and advocate for diversity and inclusion, Jen has spoken at SXSW, CES, the NYSE, and Cannes.

[01:13] Q4 Is an Opportunity, Not an Off-Season — Consumer activity doesn’t disappear in winter; it simply changes shape. Strava data shows yoga uploads rising 35%, weight training 41%, and virtual rides 150% as athletes move indoors. Jen and Daniel explain why marketers should follow these shifts rather than pull back their Q4 marketing efforts. For brands like Oakley, staying relevant means showing up across running, skiing, and indoor training, wherever consumers choose to stay active.

[04:04] Don’t Let Discounts Define Your Brand — Relying on Black Friday and Cyber Week promotions to attract first-time customers risks reducing a brand to little more than a discount. Daniel explains why lasting consumer relationships need to be built throughout the year, not just during peak shopping seasons. By staying connected to athletes through everyday training and competition, Oakley creates genuine brand value. The goal is to make holiday conversions an extension of existing loyalty, not simply a response to lower prices.

[05:16] The Three Keys to Winning the Holiday Season — Successful holiday marketing requires more than attractive promotions. Daniel outlines three essentials: add value year-round, stay meaningfully present in consumers’ daily lives, and make products easy to discover. He explains how brands can integrate product storytelling into existing routines and interests without making every interaction feel transactional. For marketers, this provides a practical framework for balancing performance marketing and commercial objectives with authentic consumer engagement during Black Friday, Cyber Week, and beyond.

[06:34] Why Community Beats Traditional Advertising — Consumers are more likely to engage with brands that enhance their experiences than those that simply compete for their attention. Daniel explains how community-driven marketing can create stronger connections than conventional advertising placements. Oakley’s first Q4 partnership with Strava brings this thinking to life through challenges and activations that reward participation and performance. The lesson for marketers is that successful platform partnerships should give audiences something meaningful rather than simply deliver another advertising impression.

[08:04] Think Ahead, But Deliver Value Today — Long-term brand building doesn’t have to come at the expense of immediate consumer relevance. Daniel shares Oakley’s guiding philosophy of designing for the future while delivering in the present. He explains how combining data with direct observation of athlete behavior helps inform marketing and creative decisions. The Travis Scott collaboration illustrates how brands can bring future-facing ideas into current cultural conversations, creating meaningful engagement while keeping today’s consumer needs firmly in focus.

[09:20] Follow Consumer Behavior, Not the Marketing Calendar — Legacy brands stay relevant by adapting to how consumers actually behave rather than relying on established seasonal marketing patterns. Daniel explains why understanding moments like New Year’s fitness resolutions creates opportunities to connect with audiences when their motivations naturally change. For Oakley, that means supporting athletes through winter training, skiing, and new fitness goals. The takeaway for marketers is simple: consumer-led strategies build stronger brand relevance than calendar-driven campaigns.
