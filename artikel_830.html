Sarah Michelle Gellar Reveals Surprising Freddie Prinze Jr. Connection

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Sarah Michelle Gellar told E! News that through Ancestry, with whom she partnered for its Gathering the Good initiative, she and Freddie Prinze Jr. learned they have a mutual connection to The Bronx.

Now this is a revelation to wig out over.

Sarah Michelle Gellar and Freddie Prinze Jr.’s paths didn’t officially cross until the late ‘90s on I Know What You Did Last Summer, but as they recently learned they had more in common than they realized. Specifically, a shared family experience that traces back decades before they graced the screen.

“We always joke that the closest connection I had to my husband's side of the family,” Gellar told E! News in an exclusive interview, “was that for a small time I went to the same high school that my husband's dad went to, and that was like sort of our one thing.”

After all, though Gellar—mom to Charlotte, 17, and Rocky, 14, with Prinze—is a native New Yorker, her husband was born in Los Angeles and grew up in New Mexico. (His dad Freddie Prinze, however, hailed from New York.)

She continued, “Then through Ancestry, we found out that our grandparents lived less than two miles away from each other in the Bronx at one point, which they could have potentially known each other. That's kind of crazy.”

For the Buffy the Vampire Slayer star, who teamed up with Ancestry for its Gathering the Good initiative, it was an opportunity to explore her own family’s legacy while also looking at the spirit of giving through history. So, tracing how her loved ones made the journey to America in the 1890s at the same time philanthropist Lena Sittig space was creating what is now considered to be the modern toy drive, really hit home how interconnected the mission is.

“I learned that my grandmother was coming over on a ship at 16 years old, right when Lena was having her idea for this,” she shared. “It’s kind of crazy how the world intertwines, and it just reminds you that you're just such a small part of such a bigger picture.”

It’s especially poignant as diving back into her roots has not always been a focal point for Gellar.

“It's funny, I didn't really spend that much time thinking about my history and my ancestry because I try so hard to live in the moment,” she explained. “And when Ancestry came to me and talked about what they wanted to do, I thought, ‘What a great idea to be able to learn acts of kindness, acts of goodness that your family may have been a part of that you never knew.’”

As the Cruel Intentions alum knows, ‘tis the season to pay it forward. “The holiday season gets so dramatic—presents and all that,” she noted. “And I always look for what are the things that you could give someone that are really meaningful.”

So, the 49-year-old posited, “What's more meaningful than learning about where you came from and your family and things you may not have known?”

To Gellar, it all comes back to Lena Sittig and her Brooklyn Christmas Tree Society, and how her idea is sparking change so many generations later. In fact, the Ringer actress teamed up with Ancestry to fulfill Christmas lists for Children’s Aid.

“So often, I think we think, ‘Well, I'm one person. What can I do?’ And then you see something like that, and you realize, oh wow, one person can do a lot,” she told E!. “But then to take it that step further, and maybe there's people in her family had no idea they were related to her.”

“Can you imagine going back in your history and finding out that one person in your family was responsible for millions of children's gifts all over the world because they had this idea?” Gellar added. “I think it's really special.”

And it’s an experience Gellar wants to send far and wide, teasing what many of her loved ones—not to mention anyone looking to explore the impact their family has made on others—may be getting under the tree this year.

“There's something for me about the how tangible it was,” she admitted. “I saw signatures, I saw writing. it makes it seem just much more real to you than just getting an email that says, ‘You know you came from here’ like, seeing their actual signatures at Ellis Island. It's also something too, growing up in New York City, you forget about it. But this is the gateway.”
