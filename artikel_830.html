Can Thitikul win a major 2027 after LPGA Tour finale three-peat?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Jeeno Thitikul won her third-successive CME Group Tour Championship on Sunday; the remarkable feat saw her overtake Annika Sorenstam in the LPGA Tour's career money list; Thitikul picked up a $4m prize

Sky Sports Golf's Trish Johnson said it is "hard to believe" that Jeeno Thitikul has not won a major championship, as the world No 2 stormed to her third-straight CME Group Tour Championship victory on Sunday.

The win capped off a brilliant LPGA Tour season for the 23-year-old, with this being her fifth title on the US circuit in 2026.

It was also the 12th win of her LPGA Tour career, with Thitikul ending Annika Sorenstam's 25-year reign at the top of the LPGA Tour's career money list, as she picked up a $4m (£3.06m) prize.

Despite her brilliance on the LPGA Tour over the past three years, the world No 2 is remarkably still yet to add a major championship to her CV.

Thitikul's best major performance came in 2025, when she placed as a runner-up at The Amundi Evian Championship, following a play-off defeat to Grace Kim.

She has five top-five finishes and nine top-10s since 2021 at the major championships, with her best placing this year coming at the AIG Women's Open, where she finished three strokes behind winner Shiho Kuwaki, tied-for-fourth.

"It's extraordinary that she hasn't won a major," Sky Sports Golf's Trish Johnson said following Thitikul's win.

"She has been there or thereabouts in a couple and maybe you think she should have got one over the line, but at the AIG Women's Open she's never really been in contention. US Open; not really.

"There's a lot of work for her still to do. The fact that she's got to number one on the career money list and hasn't won a major, I don't think that could be a stat really; you would never believe that."

Thitikul posted five birdies and an eagle as she wrapped up her final round at Tiburon Golf Club, in Florida, with a seven-under 65, to finish two shots clear of Aki Iwai and four ahead of world No 1 Nelly Korda.

"She's won as much in three weeks [at this event] as she's won in her entire career on the LPGA Tour," Johnson said.

Johnson added: "If I were an LPGA Tour player, I'd look at that and get ready for the schedule next year and pray they move this [event]. 'Please don't let it be at Tiburon Golf Club again', because she's just going to be favourite every year.

"There's something that happens to her [there], without a shadow of a doubt. She's been in decent form obviously all year, but it's like she just comes to this tournament a woman possessed."

Thitikul passes Sorenstam to have amassed a total of $24,292,858 (£20.3m) in career earnings on the LPGA Tour. Her three victories at the CME Group Tour Championship have drastically boosted that figure.

After Sunday's win, Thitikul reflected on her 2026 LPGA Tour season and issued a glowing review.

"After like half of the year, I would rate it like five out of 10," Thitikul said.

"But [coming first] in Hawai'i [at the LOTTE Championship], I rate it like ten out of ten. Today, I rate it 15 out of ten. It was so good.

"It [the season] had a certain point of like: 'Golf is going to break me right now'.

"But, you know, coming [through] those days, it's really an insane feeling."

Thitikul had a mixed start to her 2026 campaign, winning the Honda LPGA Thailand in her second start before struggling to break into the top-10 at her next five events, including a missed cut at The Chevron Championship.

She rebounded immediately, with a win at the Mizuho Americas Open in May, before later going on to make three consecutive top-10 finishes at the KPMG Women's PGA Championship, The Amundi Evian Championship and the AIG Women's Open.

She capped off her season winning three times in her last six starts at The Standard Portland Classic in August, the LOTTE Championship in October and finally at the CME Group Tour Championship.

Asked whether she feels the same expectations are placed on her as world No 1 Korda, Thitikul said: "Expectations are always going to be there.

"Even I expect [a lot from] myself; it's going to be there. A lot of fans expected me to win three times.

"But I feel like what's going to take me to the point [where I reach my expectations] is my routine, my preparation, my process, and not thinking about the expectation more than just process."
