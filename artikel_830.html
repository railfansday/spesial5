60 minutes of perfection, 30 minutes of chaos - an England win for the ages

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Harry Kane scored for a record-equalling seventh England away game

Thomas Tuchel went with bold words when reminded about the caution that sabotaged England's World Cup hopes and left them with so much to prove against Spain in the Nations League on Sunday.

In a media theatre at the Metropolitano in Madrid, head coach Tuchel's sixth sense led him to go public with what he was convinced England would produce against the world champions.

Tuchel looked his inquisitors in the eyes and declared: "We are up for something spectacular."

To say Tuchel and England kept their word was a masterpiece of understatement after the finest night of the head coach's reign resulted in a 3-2 victory that was large parts magnificent, some parts madness - and all parts spectacular.

It also ended Spain's 43-game unbeaten run - setting aside their penalty shootout defeat by Portugal in the 2025 Nations League final.

If England and Tuchel could be accused of being cowed by pressure when they led Argentina in the ill-fated World Cup semi-final, they were thrillingly positive in this unforgiving Madrid environment.

The question being asked when England held a one-goal advantage through Harry Kane's penalty at half-time was 'what would theirs and Tuchel's reaction be?' Stick or twist?

Would Tuchel tell England to press their foot on the pedal for acceleration? Or would he take the approach of going into reverse gear, as he did to ill-fated effect against Argentina?

How did England cling on against Spain and what did Tuchel do differently?

The answer was delivered when they added goals from Arsenal duo Ezri Konsa and Ben White in the first 12 minutes of the second half.

This being England, they then created a mess for themselves when Elliot Anderson was sent off for fouling Lamine Yamal in the area after 65 minutes, although the main culprit was Jarell Quansah for initially conceding possession.

Mikel Oyarzabal scored from the spot and, when Yamal set up Nico Williams for Spain's second with 15 minutes left, it looked like more than an hour of faultless England football might be wasted.

But not a bit of it, as goalkeeper James Trafford produced heroics and England defended with every fibre to close out this landmark win.

Was this the statement victory that was almost demanded of Tuchel as recompense for the World Cup disappointment, a performance to convince the doubters?

"We're fully aware what we put out there," said Tuchel afterwards. "What a performance we put out for 65 minutes to be deservedly 3-0 up.

"Of course, the red card and the penalty changed the momentum and the characteristics of the game... but we know, and the players know very, very well, what they put out there. That is the statement - and that is the significance of this victory."

Simple details of the game barely touch the sides of a night filled with quality and glorious chaos, vindication for Tuchel and England's positive approach.

Spain opened with an ostentatious parade of the World Cup in front of England's players as the teams lined up, perhaps a psychological reminder of the superiority that has seen them dominate international football.

What followed, at times literally, reduced Spain's supporters to stunned silence and their players to a shell-shocked shambles chasing the shadows that were overpowering them.

England's white shirts were everywhere but Spain could not get near them. The tormentors had become the tormented.

It was alien territory for a Spain side that has carried all before them, while England's fans tucked away high in a corner of this vast arena were equally unbelieving as a dream unfolded in front of them.

England, for 65 minutes, were in a different class to Spain. And, yes, you have read that right.

From the first whistle, employing a suffocatingly high press, England ran all over Spain.

When White crowned a superb move with the third goal four minutes after Konsa's second, it looked, remarkably, to be a case of how big England's victory margin would be.

In the end, the margin was narrow but the stature and significance of England's victory was huge.

Tuchel labelled this meeting with Spain as "proof of concept at the highest level".

There was no doubt the German manager and England were under fierce scrutiny after the manner of the collapse, mentally and tactically, against Argentina at the World Cup.

Here they delivered the proof they can match the best then beat them.

Win over world champions Spain is a 'statement' - Tuchel

If the vital signs were being forensically examined for evidence Tuchel has learned lessons from a chastening World Cup, that England have improved after that harrowing experience, this game delivered these conclusions.

Of course, England must do it again. Do it when the pressure and expectation in at its highest.

The Nations League is competitive but cannot be compared to a World Cup and European Championship, both titles currently in the possession of the team they beat here.

But England's world-class players delivered - Kane inevitably with his 93rd international goal, but Jude Bellingham was on a level of his own, the outstanding performer on both sides with an individual display simply confirming he is the player with everything.

And Tuchel got his big calls right, especially selecting Trafford, who responded superbly in a frantic closing phase. White, making a strong claim for the right-back berth, not only scored a goal but produced crucial challenges in the face of relentless, albeit belated, Spain pressure.

"This was the highest level in terms of belief, team spirit, togetherness," said Tuchel. "It was on the highest level football wise in all aspects of the game. We did everything that needs to be done together.

"The deep build-up. The high press. The counter-attack. The tracking. The defensive phases. This was an excellent performance and of course a wonderful feeling for us in the dressing room.

"We overcame some difficult moments in the end. That's how it is. But we are very well aware which steps we took now through after the World Cup."

England's win means they top their Nations League group, but the importance spreads much wider than simply a league table.

The first 65 minutes were simply breathtaking. Spain wilting under attack after attack. Spain rattled until they shook as England refused to allow them meaningful possession.

After Anderson's dismissal, bad memories were revisited as England were forced into a rearguard action, but any other result than the one that was eventually delivered would have been a huge injustice.

England survived 29 nervous minutes, including added time, to make a powerful statement about their current status, which is much healthier than when recriminations about Tuchel's approach provided the post-World Cup narrative.

"I'm very impressed with the steps that we took after the World Cup", said Tuchel. "We wanted to get better, better in a deep build-up, get better in defending in a low block and, on top of it, we wanted always to impose our identity and the way we want to play against every opponent.

"I think this was clearly visible throughout the whole group phase. The results followed with an excellent group phase."

Was this the finest performance of Tuchel's time in charge? Most observers felt so, but the head coach was best placed to answer.

"Yes, given the circumstances," he said, "and the pressure, the quality, where we played in this building against the reigning world champions, unbeaten in 43 matches. Putting a performance out there in Madrid, and in this environment, was a very complete performance."

Tuchel will, of course, need repeat performances - but the outstanding ability and character England displayed here to beat the World Cup holders will deliver a huge injection of confidence and self-belief.
