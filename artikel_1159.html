Mauricio Pochettino must fix a club stuck in a constant cycle of failure. The new Man United boss has the personality but the squad is a mish-mash and where he sits as a coach is a great unknown, writes IAN LADYMAN

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Published: 16:51 EST, 20 November 2026 | Updated: 17:07 EST, 20 November 2026

In a soft-focus Sky TV interview that followed Mauricio Pochettino’s opening press conference on Friday, the Manchester United chief executive Omar Berrada revealed there had been a ‘buzz’ about the club since the arrival of their new manager.

Whether he was speaking metaphorically or referring to the sound of drones stealing Pochettino’s training secrets from above only he will know.

As is usually the case these days, much about the modern United remains unclear.

But what we do know after welcoming the eighth full-time manager since the days of Sir Alex Ferguson is that once again England’s biggest football club is relying on a personality lift to drag them back to their feet.

This is what happens when you get things wrong for so long, when you buy the wrong players and install executives who appear to be learning on the job. You end up hiring and firing managers, waiting for the one who finally arrives with the magic dust, the capacity to somehow create something greater than the sum of the rather wonky parts.

Listening to Pochettino here – a talented coach and a good person – was to occasionally think back to words spoken in the weeks of their arrivals on this territory by predecessors such as Ruben Amorim and Erik ten Hag.

For they too were asked about such intangibles as culture and mood and philosophy. They too were asked about more prosaic football matters such as match fitness.

It’s a conversational cycle that tells a story of recurring and systematic football failure.

Amorim – back on a grim, foggy day exactly two years ago – was asked about Marcus Rashford and whether he had been right to spend time off in New York days before the new United manager walked through the door.

Here, Pochettino could justifiably have been asked about another forward Bryan Mbeumo spending the wee hours of Thursday morning playing the newly released Grand Theft Auto video game when he probably should have been asleep.

This is the United that Pochettino is now being asked to fix, a club where names and faces change but problems remain the same.

The Berrada interview was actually more enlightening than Pochettino’s carefully managed introduction to the wider media.

Certainly it was interesting to listen to his assertion that nine defensive players in one squad was enough. In football, as United are discovering yet again, it’s not necessarily the quantity that matters but the quality.

One point did resonate rather more deeply, though, and that was the one Berrada – once of Manchester City – made about United’s players needing a manager capable of dealing with the scrutiny of the club and who could shield some of his players from all that comes with it.

The recently-departed Michael Carrick did look rather lost on the touchline in the final tumultuous weeks of his time as manager. He didn’t look an awful lot more comfortable in front of the TV cameras either.

In the dressing room? Well, he lost the loyalty of his captain Bruno Fernandes towards the end and that is never a good sign.

Pochettino will do rather better at all that. The former Chelsea, PSG and Tottenham manager has a personality and a charisma that could fill an aircraft hangar. His players will arrive at Anfield for his opening game on Sunday feeling better about themselves than they have for some time.

United have hired some confidence and some authority and some experience at a time when they desperately need it, even if Berrada’s claim that they had to call the USA Federation last week to check on his release clause suggested this is an appointment made rather on the hoof.

It is, however, the impact that Pochettino can bring over time on a jumbled squad put together for six different managers over an incredible 12 years that will define him. Here, the 54-year-old undoubtedly faces challenges.

Talking this week to someone associated with Tottenham about Pochettino’s progressive seasons there between 2014 and 2019, I was reminded of the quality that the Argentinian was given to work with.

Pochettino's Spurs side harnessed the attacking power of stars including Harry Kane and Heung-min Son

The French international goalkeeper, Hugo Lloris, the Belgian central defensive partnership of Toby Alderweireld and Jan Vertonghen, England international defenders Kyle Walker, Danny Rose and Kieran Trippier and, further forward, the burgeoning attacking talents of Dele Alli, Christian Eriksen, Heung-min Son and Harry Kane.

It was a prototype Premier League football team, both in talent, age, fitness and physicality. That was why, in 2016, it almost won it.

Pochettino does not have this at Old Trafford. He doesn’t have anything like it. He has some talented players. The fact he inherits a team in the bottom three is down to Carrick’s inadequacies.

But this United squad is a mish-mash and it serves a purpose to ask how many would get in that old Spurs team we talk about.

Fernandes? Maybe. Mbeumo? Probably. That is about it and that, frankly, is where United are right now.

Pochettino has long wanted this job. It’s not a posting of convenience for him. Like others before him – men like Jose Mourinho and Louis van Gaal – he has long since coveted the cachet, the stature and the power of this remarkable football institution.

Mourinho and Van Gaal had better players than Pochettino is being handed and they both still failed, largely because they were shown to be coaches already over the top of that hill that both had worked so desperately hard to climb.

Mbeumo is one of the few United players who might have got into Pochettino's Spurs side

Where does Pochettino sit in terms of his own personal and professional growth? That is such a hard question to answer and will be utterly fundamental to how he travels in the weeks and months to come.

Sitting here fresh from the training ground and strangely seeming to question whether the glass of water in front of him was fit to drink, Pochettino declared himself to be in his coaching prime.

The proof or otherwise of that will only be revealed out on the grass. For all that he can plead valid mitigation, Pochettino was not hugely impressive in Paris or at Stamford Bridge. Before he starts to talk about dysfunctional football clubs, he may wish to take a look at his new surroundings.

Liverpool away feels like something of a free hit. Sporting in Lisbon on Wednesday less so and Brentford at home next Saturday definitely not. And so it begins. A run of 12 games in six and a half weeks that may well see Pochettino crawling into January begging for fresh legs and new players.

The new manager said here that he wishes to be across transfers and little wonder. When he looks at the mistakes made in that regard by a succession of United regimes and executives, he may indeed question what it is they put in the water in the north-west.

United fans will perhaps appreciate that one of the ‘higher ups’ at Old Trafford has finally put his head above the parapet, even if they question some of what Berrada said. That’s been overdue.

In terms of their new manager, they can be forgiven for keeping a lid on any excitement. Many of them have simply been wearied by the cycle.
