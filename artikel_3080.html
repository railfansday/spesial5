Is Netflix a Buy for $1,000?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Trading 57% off its all-time record, the streaming stock has been crushed.

By Neil Patel – Nov 24, 2026 at 5:10PM EST

Sizable down cycles aren't new to the Netflix (NFLX +1.32%) story. The latest dip, with shares down 56% from their peak (as of Nov. 23), is certainly troubling for investors to witness. But it's smaller than the 76% drop in 2022 and the 79% fall before that in 2011.

History says that the streaming stock has always made a comeback. And despite the most recent tumble, the shares have still returned 392% in the past 10 years. Getting in early on a true disruptor usually produces this type of performance.

Is it time to buy Netflix for $1,000? At the current price of $58.10, investors can add about 17 shares to their portfolios.

Netflix's economic moat comes from its scale. With more than 325 million paid memberships, it spreads its content budget across a far bigger audience than any rival can. That's a powerful cost advantage. Netflix is targeting a 31.5% operating margin in 2026. Walt Disney's (DIS +1.86%) non-sports streaming segment, which includes Disney+ and Hulu, posted an 11.5% margin in its latest fiscal year.

And the runway is still long if you ask the leadership team. Chief Financial Officer Spence Neumann said in July that Netflix was "under 45% penetrated into addressable households around the world." It also captures 5% of TV viewing time and only about 7% of its addressable revenue opportunity.

The bottom line keeps improving. Operating margin expanded from 26.7% in 2024 to 29.5% in 2025 and is projected at 31.5% this year, as noted. Free cash flow is also expected to reach roughly $12 billion in 2026, compared to $9.5 billion last year. Growth is translating into serious profits.

So why has Netflix stock gotten clobbered? Growth is slowing. Revenue increased 16.2% year over year in Q1 2026, 13.4% in Q2, and 11.8% in Q3. Wall Street expects 11.6% in the fourth quarter.

Engagement is a worry, too. View hours rose only 2% in the first half of 2026. Investors also didn't like management's decision to report that figure once a year, starting in 2027, instead of twice.

But all this pessimism has reset the valuation. The forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio is now 15.1. That's cheaper than the S&P 500 index (^GSPC -0.50%). Shareholders aren't used to seeing Netflix shares on the discount rack.

At this multiple, Netflix looks like an interesting buy with $1,000. Just don't expect a smooth ride. The stock could move with heightened volatility in the near term. That's why patience is critical.
