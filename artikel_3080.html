Chipotle Mexican Grill vs. Coca-Cola: Which Stock Is a Buy After the Nvidia Sell-Off?

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

The better pick depends on whether you want growth or steady income.

As Nvidia (NVDA -4.34%) stock faces a sell-off, investors may be considering rotating into consumer good stocks for more protection. Two such companies that some may be considering are Chipotle Mexican Grill (CMG -4.01%) and Coca-Cola (KO -0.13%).

Chipotle focuses on rapid domestic expansion to capture market share. Meanwhile, Coca-Cola leverages its vast bottling system to dominate international markets. Both represent significant players in their respective fields, but they offer distinct financial profiles for long-term investors.

Chipotle Mexican Grill operates a fleet of company-owned restaurants focused on a limited menu of fresh ingredients. By operating within the retail stocks segment, the company avoids the complexities of franchising and maintains more control over its brand experience. Third-party delivery services account for over 16% of food and beverage revenue, which highlights the importance of the digital ecosystem to its modern business strategy.

In its latest annual report, filed for FY 2025, revenue reached nearly $11.9 billion, representing growth of approximately 5.4% over the previous year. Net income for the period was close to $1.5 billion, resulting in a net margin of roughly 12.9%. While the company has faced rising input costs, its ability to maintain double-digit net margins indicates a high level of pricing power among its customer base.

Coca-Cola operates as a total beverage company, manufacturing concentrates and syrups that are sold to a global network of bottling partners. Its system reaches multiple countries and territories. The company also maintains a strategic relationship with Monster Beverage (MNST +0.37%) and is expanding its reach into energy drinks and dairy.

In FY 2025, revenue reached close to $47.9 billion, marking an increase of nearly 1.9% year over year. Net income for the fiscal year was approximately $13.1 billion, supporting a strong net margin of 27.3%. This level of profitability is characteristic of its asset-light model, where bottling partners handle much of the capital-intensive manufacturing and distribution work.

Chipotle Mexican Grill faces material risks from food safety incidents, which can rapidly damage brand reputation and sales across its thousands of locations. The company also operates in a highly competitive landscape against rivals like McDonald's (MCD -0.43%) and Starbucks (SBUX -2.39%). Furthermore, its aggressive growth strategy is sensitive to the availability of prime real estate and its ability to recruit and retain thousands of qualified employees.

Coca-Cola is exposed to risks from global economic conditions, including currency exchange rate fluctuations that can impact reported earnings. It must constantly innovate to meet changing consumer preferences regarding health and sugar content while competing with PepsiCo (PEP -1.17%) and Keurig Dr Pepper (KDP -7.40%). Additionally, the company faces ongoing tax litigation with the IRS and regulatory pressure regarding the environmental impact of plastic packaging.

Each stock looks similar with its forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio, while the bigger difference shows up in the price-to-sales (P/S) ratio.

These are both well-run companies, and they each benefit from consumer habits. The difference in the stocks for the longer term, however, comes down to what an investor is looking for in their portfolio.

The Coca-Cola stock price has done exceptionally well in 2026, with shares climbing nearly 40%. That said, the main reason to own Coca-Cola is its status as a consistent dividend payer, having increased that payout for 64 consecutive years. The key to owning the beverage maker as an income stock is whether those dividend payouts are likely to keep increasing, which, at least according to the 2026 third-quarter results, they are. For that third quarter, Coca-Cola shared it generated $11.4 billion in free cash flow for the first nine months of the year, boasting an operating margin of 33.4%. Revenue also increased 7%, with earnings per share growing 8% and operating income climbing 12%, providing plenty of flexibility to keep those dividend payouts and increases rolling in for shareholders.

In comparison, while a mature company in its own right, Chipotle is still more of a growth stock than Coca-Cola. In its third quarter, revenue increased by roughly 10%, customer transactions climbed, and it opened 84 new restaurants. It also has a long-term goal of opening 7,000 locations in North America.

Between the two, it comes down to whether an investor is looking for an income stream or the potential for greater stock price appreciation.
