What Is a Mortgage REIT? AGNC Investment Pays 18% a Year to Own One.

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

AGNC pays a massive dividend, but its total return has been dismal.

10 stocks we like better than AGNC Investment Corp. ›

AGNC (AGNC -0.57%), the second-largest mortgage real estate investment trust (mREIT), pays a massive forward dividend yield of 18.3%. That high yield might seem tempting, but investors should understand what an mREIT is -- and why it isn't for everyone.

Unlike equity real estate investment trusts (REITs), which purchase properties and rent them out, mREITs invest in mortgage-backed securities (MBS) and other mortgage-related products. By borrowing money at short-term rates to buy longer-term mortgage-related assets, it generates a profit from the spread between its borrowing costs and asset yields.

AGNC primarily invests in Agency MBS, which are backed by Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, or Ginnie Mae. That government support makes it less risky than other mREITs, including Annaly Capital (NLY -0.89%), which allocate more of their portfolios to non-Agency MBS. mREITs, like REITs, must distribute at least 90% of their taxable income as dividends for a lower tax rate.

However, mREITs only flourish when interest rates are low. Abrupt rate hikes erode their book value per share and net spread and dollar roll income per share (which funds their dividends).

From 2021 to 2025, AGNC's book value per share dropped from $15.75 to $8.88; its annual dividends stayed unchanged at $1.44 per share, but its net spread and dollar roll income per share fell from $3.11 in 2021 to $1.50 in 2025.

That decline, largely driven by the Fed's 11 rate hikes in 2022 and 2023, caused its stock to plunge 51% over the past five years. With reinvested dividends, it delivered a nearly flat total return. So before you buy AGNC for its massive yield, you should be aware of these long-term risks.
