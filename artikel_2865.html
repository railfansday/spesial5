A quarterback crisis has left Clemson clutching at straws as it makes the trip to Duke on Friday, with third-string Trent Pearman in line to take the reins in Durham, North Carolina. That leaves Tigers head coach Dabo Swinney ransacking his deeper options, though none of those others at his disposal have completed a touchdown pass this season. Neither senior Pearman nor freshman Brock Bradley have completed a score at the college level, though sophomore Chris Denson – who has played only a handful of snaps this season – at least has a TD pass to his name from a 45-10 hammering of Furman last year.

Duke, meanwhile, is targeting a place back in victory lane after coming up short in a 36-25 loss to Miami last week, having won three in a row prior to that. Both of these teams are 4-3 in the Atlantic Coast Conference, though Duke holds the edge in momentum, not to mention its 7-3 record in overall play is one win superior to Clemson's 6-4 record as things stand.

Despite the loss to Miami, Duke's defense has the potential to shut Clemson out given the current quarterback situation, having conceded a combined 24 points across its previous three wins over Virginia, Boston College and NC State. A poor omen for a Tigers offense averaging just 21 points per game (115th in the FBS) and facing uncertainty as it looks down the pecking order for a solution in the pocket.
