CNBC's The China Connection newsletter: As Nike and Lululemon struggle in China, Xiaohongshu is helping local brands take off

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Hi, this is Evelyn, writing to you from Beijing. Welcome to the latest edition of The China Connection — a snapshot of what I'm seeing and hearing from local businesses.

While the world watched the Nvidia saga around Jensen Huang unfold, Chinese retailers quietly wrapped up the local version of Black Friday, known as Singles Day. What can foreign brands learn?

As Lululemon, On Running and other Western consumer brands have sought to expand in China, so have a crop of local brands with English names.

Monday Sleeping Club, Crying Center, AW Project, an action a day, Liegee. The list of trendy new Chinese brands has grown rapidly, despite flatlining retail sales nationwide.

"Their success rests on one shared formula," said Cynthia Zhang, analyst, Euromonitor International.

"Build a non-product asset first — credible fabric quality, an emotion, an aesthetic reputation, or proprietary fit data — then monetize it through founder-led content, Xiaohongshu-driven discovery, tight supply chains, and the quiet-luxury and Guochao [national pride] mood that prizes craftsmanship over conspicuous logos," she said.

For Liegee, one of its three founders, Cocoa Lin, told me its sales surged by roughly 160% during the latest Singles Day period to the tens of millions of yuan, equivalent to a few million U.S. dollars.

The brand launched in 2022 in Hangzhou to meet a perceived gap in clothes for early career women looking to express their personality, Lin said. She said the brand, which has over 100,000 fans for its store on Alibaba's Taobao e-commerce site, largely uses internet influencers and celebrity product seeding to generate awareness.

The brand uses Xiaohongshu, on which it has over 22,000 followers, for catalog-style photos and marketing videos. It opened a "Liegee Motel" pop-up in Shenzhen last month.

"We believe online and offline will have a closer connection in the future," Lin said in Mandarin via translation, adding that "as lifestyles become more experience-oriented, this energy can be transmitted to online."

It's a dynamic that Western brands have struggled to crack.

Nike reported a 22% sales drop in Greater China in the quarter ended Aug. 31, while Lululemon reported a 2% drop in mainland China comparable sales in the quarter ended Aug. 2.

Adidas runs at least six official Xiaohongshu accounts, with two notching well over 200,000 followers each. Nike and Lululemon each operate one official account, with over 300,000 followers each.

For a country of around 1.4 billion people, Xiaohongshu has captured a key consumer audience.

The social media app, whose layout is a mix of Instagram and Pinterest, has focused on women and young people in China's largest cities, and recent expanded into less developed parts of the country, according to Questmobile. Xiaohongshu recorded average daily users of 120 million in October, a fraction of the 422 million on Taobao and 717 million on Douyin, the report showed.

The app has also attempted to build up its own e-commerce business in order to monetize.

"RedNote [or Xiaohongshu] has been a key platform for product discovery and research, especially for female consumers shopping in the beauty, skincare, lifestyle, fashion, and health categories. Now, we're seeing more transactions within RedNote itself," said Jacob Cooke, co-founder and CEO of WPIC Marketing + Technologies. The company helps foreign brands sell in China and other parts of Asia.

Alibaba, JD.com and ByteDance's Douyin still dominated this year's Singles Day sales with a combined 1.68 trillion yuan in sales over the Oct. 7 to Nov. 11 period, according to Syntun.

What's changed more fundamentally is the mood of the Chinese consumer, including more affluent ones.

In the era of double-digit annual growth in real estate prices, consumers pursued conspicuous consumption to meet "aspirational" desires, Christine Peng, head of Greater China consumer research, UBS, told reporters last week.

"Now those needs no longer exist," she said in Mandarin, translated by CNBC.

"The rise of these trendy brands reflects how consumers now seek to define their own identities and pursue personal enjoyment, while still remaining mindful of price and quality," Peng said.

She said wealthy consumers are also embracing domestic brands, and no longer just buying traditional luxury brands.

Alice Huang tied to Nvidia's biggest Chinese competitor

Alice Huang has owned stakes in companies that have benefited Huawei, private intelligence firm Tesari shared exclusively with CNBC. Some in Beijing worry the Jensen Huang affair will negatively impact U.S.-China relations.

Exports in October jumped by 35.2%, while imports soared 41.6%, both beating Reuters' estimates. But industrial output, retail sales and investment figures for October all missed estimates. China and the U.S. discussed tariffs and agricultural trade ahead of Xi-Trump meeting, the Chinese Commerce Ministry said Saturday.

Alibaba announces plans to list T-Head, its Nvidia chip rival

The Chinese tech company disclosed the Hong Kong Stock Exchange will allow it to spin off its semiconductor subsidiary T-Head.
