Warriors get blown out faster than the British in 1815 in disaster trip to New Orleans

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

In the Battle of New Orleans, Sir Edward Pakenham took the British army on a road trip to Louisiana to face Andrew Jackson's American troops. In less than 30 minutes, the British army had suffered over 2,000 casualties and Pakenham was dead. However, that's longer than the Golden State Warriors lasted Monday night in a 115-94 loss to the New Orleans Pelicans.

One game after the San Antonio Spurs raced out to an 18-0 lead on the Warriors, the Pelicans started the game on a 23-2 run. Coach Steve Kerr used two timeouts in the first six minutes of the game, a reaction to the team starting 0-for-11 from the field and 0-for-9 from three-point range. By the time De'Anthony Melton made the Warriors' first shot from the floor with 4:08 remaining in the first quarter, Saddiq Bey already had 13 points.

The first quarter ended with the Pelicans up 35-11. The Warriors shot 2-for-18 for the quarter, committed six turnovers, and allowed the Pelicans to grab four offensive rebounds. By the time the second quarter began, Joe Lacob was desperately trying to negotiate terms of surrender with Pelicans owner Gayle Benson, through delegates in the Netherlands.

LJ Cryer was particularly rough in this one, bricking a number of wide-open looks from behind the arc and causing our site's commenters to scour the ranks of G League players eligible for two-way contracts. The outside shooting was so bad that Bob Fitzgerald wasn't able to bust out a call of, "From Lake Pontchartrain!" on a make, dragging Kelenna Azubuike into extended discussions of biegnets instead.

The deficit got as bad as 45-18 by four minutes into the second quarter, while the Pelicans' lead would max out at 31 points late in the third, right around when Jeremiah Fears locked up his first career triple-double. The second-year guard finished with 19 points, 10 rebounds, and 10 assists, plus his first career flagrant foul for slamming into Charles Bassey. Fortunately for Bassey, Fears weighs about 185 pounds.

Bassey continued to be a magnet for both offensive rebounds and flagrant fouls in this one. Five days after Marcus Smart drew an ejection and a three-game suspension for punching Bassey, he drew a flagrant foul from the pint-sized Fears. It sparked a 10-2 run where the Warriors continued to miss shots, but also crash the boards and draw fouls, getting into the bonus early. They were 17-for-18 from the line in the first half, while going 8-for-39 overall.

In one of the game's lowest lowlights, newly-shorn Yaxel Lendeborg tried to hit Bassey under the basket, but hit the rim with his pass. Bassey managed to grab the loose ball, which was passed back to Lendeborg, who proceeded to fire a pass three feet over Georges Niang's head for a turnover. Look, the Minivan doesn't have hydraulics.

On another possession, the Pelicans grabbed two offensive rebounds, missed three shots, then stole the ball and got a layup when the Warriors finally did corral the ball.

The NBC Sports Bay Area telecast cut out in the middle of the second quarter, perhaps because the production truck itself was sick of watching the game. However, Secretary of Transportation Sean Duffy announced at a press conference that the delay was actually former President Joe Biden's fault.

How bad was the first half? When Zion Williamson scored through contact and missed the subsequent free throw, the Warriors committed a lane violation that gave him a second chance at the and-one and a 29-point Pelicans lead.

As a (semi)final indignity, they fouled Fears with 0.5 seconds to play to make the halftime score 61-36.

Lendeborg cut off most of his hair over the weekend, perhaps trying to get sleeker after the blowout loss to the Spurs. He did come through with a game-high 28 points, doing most of his damage in the second half by drawing a lot of fouls. Lendeborg also logged a -30 plus/minus, which could make the other Warriors hesitant to bring out the hair clippers on the remainder of the road trip.

The final box score doesn't reveal the extent of the Pelicans' dominance Monday, mainly because the Warriors' two-way players dominated the Pelicans' two-way players in garbage time. Yves Missi may have owned the glass and grabbed 14 rebounds in 23 minutes, but Julian Reese and Malik Dia were no match for Graham Ike and Malevy Leons, baby! Cryer scored five of his seven points and all three of his assists in the final 4:13 of the game.

What were the positives? Will Richard gutted out what looked like a painful re-injury to his wounded thumb, finishing with 11 points and two steals, along with a couple of deflections. Gui Santos had 12 points and seven rebounds in 19 minutes and consistently attacked the hoop, going 7-for-8 from the foul line. Draymond Green was mostly invisible, but finished with eight rebounds and five assists and a Dennis Rodman-like two points. He seemed checked out near the end of the game, presumably thinking about the best places to buy beard dye on the road.

Also, as per the terms of the peace treaty, the Pelicans and Warriors will return all prisoners of war and Steph Curry retains his popcorn rights at the Smoothie King Arena, though Mike Dunleavy, Jr. did have to recognize the sovereignty of the King Cake Baby.

The Pelicans had lost six straight but seems to have righted the ship with two big moves: Sending Dejounte Murray home, and playing the Warriors without Steph Curry. The Warriors have now lost three straight following their eight-game win streak, with this game serving as a poor omen for the upcoming road games in Dallas and Memphis.

Maybe it's time to get more aggressive with Kristaps Porzingis trade talks? After all, Podz already cut his hair, Al Horford is already bald, and it would be a shame for Santos to lose his beautiful locks. It's too bad Curry is going to be ineligible for postseason awards, because the last two games are a compelling argument that he's the most valuable player in the NBA.
