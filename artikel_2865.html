Packers Bye Weekend Rooting Guide: Who to cheer for in Week 11

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The playoff picture around the NFL is starting to take shape, and the Green Bay Packers head into the weekend of Week 11 with a few extra days off due to their bye week. The Packers are 6-4 and still sit in the NFC's 7th and final playoff spot, but plenty of games that are taking place this week will impact the team's chances of holding on to that postseason slot.

Of course, the most impactful games are those that involve the three other teams in the NFC North. The Chicago Bears, Detroit Lions, and Minnesota Vikings are all 7-2 on the season, 1.5 games up on the Packers. Determining which teams Packers fans need to cheer for is easy when those teams are involved, but that's not quite as obvious for some of the other games around the league this week.

As a result, it's time to resuscitate the Packers Rooting Guide this week, breaking down each game on this week's schedule and which result will provide the most positive impact on the Green and Gold. Here's the breakdown for Week 11.

The Packers play the Texans later this season. A Houston win helps their record and therefore indirectly improves the Packers' strength of schedule. It makes very little difference in the long run, but the game isn't completely irrelevant to the Packers.

In every NFC vs. AFC game, Packers fans should probably cheer for the AFC team. In this case, it would be pretty funny to see the Chiefs lose at home to a 3-7 Cardinals team. But ultimately, it probably doesn't much matter, unless you're worried about the younger LaFleur leading his team on a late-season charge to threaten for a Wild Card spot (which I'm not).

Verdict: Whatever, but I'm pulling for Arizona for the laughs.

There are former Packers and Wisconsin connections on both sides of the battle for Pennsylvania, but there really aren't any impacts on the Packers here since they have neither of these teams on their schedule. Would you like to see Aaron Rodgers and Mike McCarthy win or lose here? That's basically what should be your deciding factor since the Eagles are in the basement of the NFC at 2-7.

The Packers will play Buffalo and Miami back-to-back in weeks 14 and 15. They're both in the AFC East. This really has no bearing on the Packers at all.

The Cowboys are two games back of the Packers, who beat Dallas in Week 6. It would probably take some unlikely situations for Dallas to claw back ahead of the Packers for a Wild Card spot, but let's not take those chances, even if the Titans are significant underdogs this week.

The Packers are 6-4, 1.5 games behind the three other teams in the division who are all tied at 7-2. A Bears loss gets Green Bay back to a single game behind Chicago, a lead that the Packers could erase (and clinch a head-to-head tiebreaker) by beating the Bears on Christmas Day. Every loss by another NFC North team is a good thing for the Packers down the stretch, regardless of who it comes against (with in-division games requiring a bit more thought, of course).

Carolina currently leads the NFC South at 4-5, and the Packers have a head-to-head win over them already this season. A Panthers win would help Green Bay's strength of schedule and strength of victory. But there is an unlikely event that multiple NFC South teams go on hot streaks and get into the Wild Card race, so we're pulling for the Ravens here to knock the NFC team down a bit here.

Verdict: Nevermore, Panthers, nevermore. Cheer for the Ravens here.

Obviously, as with the Bears above, the Packers need every other NFC North team to lose every possible game. Like with the Bears, it's also another in-conference game for Detroit, too, so a loss there would have some potential tiebreaker considerations as well.

Here's another nearly irrelevant game. The only impact on Green Bay is the fact that they beat the Jets earlier this year, so a win for Gang Green would give a slight SOS and SOV boost to the Packers.

Who'd have thought that this would be a premium game at this point in the year, especially after the Giants have gone through multiple QBs and now are stuck with J.J. McCarthy?

Here's another AFC divisional matchup that has no impact on the Packers. In scenarios where the Broncos are involved and there's no impact on the Packers, I'll always root against them. Fernando Mendoza gets his second start in this one too, and I can't bring myself to dislike the little goofball.

Verdict: I'm pulling for the dweeby rookie QB.

This one should be obvious. The Packers need the Vikings to lose as many games as possible down the stretch to catch up to them in the NFC North race. San Francisco is all but assured of a playoff spot already, leading the NFC West by 2.5 games. Let them run away with the division and give the Vikings another loss, since they need to lose three games more than the Packers drop over the last 8 weeks of the season.

In yet another AFC vs. NFC game, it's probably worth cheering against the NFC team. In this one, however, we have an MVP candidate who Jordan Love will probably have to compete with in that narrative. If anything, Burrow looks like a strong leader in that race at this point, as he leads the league in completion percentage, passer rating, and yards per game. Personally, I'd love to see Burrow knocked down a peg, even if that seems unlikely this week, to balance the scales out with Love.

Verdict: Hope for a Bengals win, but a low-scoring game with Joe Burrow looking bad.
