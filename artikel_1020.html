Parkinson’s: beyond the brain

Parkinson’s research has focused mostly on the brain, and the processes that lead to the death of dopamine neurons, which eventually causes the varied symptoms of the neurodegenerative disease. But given that research has not uncovered a genetic component for most people, what triggers the disease has remained elusive. Some researchers have begun to investigate how environmental factors — including exposure to herbicides, pesticides and air pollution — could be an important factor in the increasing number of cases. Others, meanwhile, have found that the clumping of proteins that drives the disease might begin in the gut rather than in the brain.

This film investigates these avenues of research, and shares what it could mean in preventing and treating the disease.
