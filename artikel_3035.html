Ontario finds a way to win in regional quarterfinal victory over Columbian

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

ONTARIO — It's one of the oldest clichés in football.

But just because it is, doesn't mean it isn't true. The Ontario Warriors proved it right in a 22-19 win over Tiffin Columbian in the Division III Region 10 quarterfinals on Friday, Nov. 6.

"We stuck together in the second half and found a little life," Ontario coach Aaron Eckert said. "The defense won that game for us. They only scored once on a long play, but we shut them down other than that one play. We came up with a big two-point stop and then found a groove offensively to ice the game at the end. Good teams find a way to win and we found one tonight."

The Warriors (9-2) advance to host St. Marys Memorial (9-3) in the regional semifinals on Nov. 13 despite not playing their best game by a long shot. And yet, they won a key playoff game and will continue their quest to a regional championship.

Ontario got on the board first when Trindon Flenoury caught a backwards pass and fired a strike to Cardae Brown for a 71-yard trick-play touchdown on the second offensive play of the game. Brown finished with four catches for 146 yards and a TD for the game.

"We haven't been a team that has thrown a lot of trick plays out there and we just felt like the moment was right and we got a good look," Eckert said. "We decided to let it fly and see what happens and it worked out and got us on the board early. It is always great when stuff like that works for you."

But, the Warriors stalled out from there. Columbian scored 19 unanswered points to take a 19-7 lead with 5:03 remaining in the third quarter before the Warriors handed the ball over to senior Gavin Miller to take over at quarterback.

"We had a lot of three and outs and I just asked to let me handle this," Miller said. "I'm a senior, I want the ball in my hands as much as possible and I wanted to do everything I could to get it done. I'm glad it worked out."

Miller, who finished 4-for-8 for 94 yards passing and ran for 29 yards and a TD on 11 carries, provided the spark after he finished off a drive with a five-yard touchdown run with 2:42 left in the third to make it a 19-14 deficit and got the Warriors back on track offensively.

"Sean (Tanner) has had a great year for us. The first time we played these guys, Gavin really hurt them running the football," Eckert said. "Both of these guys, to me, are starters and do a great job, but we felt like we needed a bit of a spark in the second half and Gavin was able to provide that for us. I think we have two really great quarterbacks and it is a good problem to have."

But the play of the game came after the Warriors pinned Columbian back at their own one-yard line off of a Miller punt. Three plays later, junior safety Zane Curry picked off a pass and returned it 27 yards for the momentum-shifting score, putting the Warriors ahead 22-19 thanks to a successful 2-point conversion run by Miller.

"Our coaches noticed on film that they like to throw those screens to running backs and they created a great gameplan," Curry said. "Before the play, they called Superman and that is where I take up the running back and I noticed the quarterback's eyes and jumped the play.

"I felt like it was my chance to shine. I needed to come out and provide for my team. I wasn't going to let us lose this, especially when it could be these seniors' last game. I grew up with them and I couldn't let them go out like that. I got in the end zone and went blank."

It ended up being the game-winning play and one that helped the Warriors move on in the postseason.

"I didn't even know he picked the ball off," Miller said. "Before that play, I just felt like we really needed to make a big play as a defense to get something going and create some momentum and he made that play for us. I just found someone to block."

It was a play Eckert knew Curry was capable of making and one he was proud to see.

"He is so dynamic with the ball," Eckert said. "As we look back throughout the season, we think about how we should have utilized him more on offense, but he is such a fantastic player on defense and it's awesome to see him make that play. He is an All-Ohio football player."

From there, the Ontario defense took over. After getting gashed by Columbian's Masen Seitz in the first half, the Warriors held the dynamic running back to just two yards rushing in the second half with four plays of no gain and another for negative-1 yard.

"We just decided to hog our inside guys and create a pile and have Maddix (Smith), who is a dude, to push and pinch everything and it opened up the linebackers to make plays," Miller said. "It worked really well tonight."

The Warriors allowed 281 yards of total offense to Columbian with 211 coming through the air and two of those going for 60-plus yard touchdowns, but it was the tone they set in the second half that made all the difference.

"The seniors came in during halftime and talked us up," Curry said. "The message was to just stay together, play together, play hard and see what happens. We saw what happens when we do that."

What happens is the Warriors tend to win.

"We just had to keep playing," Eckert said. "If you are good enough, you will find a way. If you're not, we would tip our caps, but we found a way. Cardae (Brown) carried us in the third quarter and gave us some life offensively and Zane Curry's interception return and a 2-point conversion really flipped things around. All those things piled up because we stayed together and chipped away."
