Before You Add Another KPI, Ask These 10 Questions First

KPI can give your business insights into their daily activity and performance. It gathers together key metrics such as productivity, operating costs, order fulfillment and customer satisfaction. It is important to think about which KPIs to include. Too many KPIs can cause confusion and if the metrics are not aligned to the objectives of the business then they can be pointless.

Ask the correct questions to ensure that the right KPIs are included in your Operations Dashboard. Asking questions can help organizations understand which KPIs are relevant and gain insights into their business requirements

The first thing to consider is what you want to achieve with your dashboard. Your dashboard should not just be a list of numbers, it should be providing useful information to staff and management.

Think of what you want to achieve. Increase staff productivity? Reduce operating costs? Faster order fulfillment? Improve customer satisfaction? Identify any bottlenecks? It is important to know why you are measuring a KPI. You need to know what you want to achieve so you know how to measure it. Knowing what you want to achieve will help you choose the correct KPIs.

Depending on your industry, what you do and what your business priorities are will affect what KPIs to pick. You should pick KPIs that will give you the most useful insights into your daily activities.

Common KPIs for your Operations Dashboard:

Inventory Turnover: How fast inventory moves or is replaced over a given period.

Labor / Resource Utilization: Percentage of available staff or equipment capacity actively deployed.

Cost per Unit: Total operational expenditure divided by the total number of units produced or processed.

It is also important to know whether you can measure your KPI accurately. If your KPI cannot be measured with accurate data then it will not be useful.

Question: Is the data available? Can it be calculated accurately? How often can you measure? Are the methods of measuring consistent across departments? Is the data reliable?

For example, if you want to measure customer satisfaction you need to ensure you have a consistent method for gathering feedback. They should be easy to understand and measured using consistent methods. If they are hard to calculate or require too much effort then you may need to look for something easier.

If you aren’t setting objectives for your KPI then it can be hard to understand how well you are performing. Tracking a KPI without setting a target makes it difficult to understand whether performance is good or needs improvement. Establishing clear performance expectations for every indicator is important.

These are examples and will depend on your business requirements. Ask yourself whether the objective is achievable. If you set an unrealistic objective then it could create unnecessary pressure.

Ensure you have someone responsible for monitoring your key metrics. If you don’t have clear responsibilities then it could be hard to know who to talk to if you are not achieving the desired results.

Question to ask: Who will be responsible for monitoring? Who will be looking at the results? Who will be responsible for fixing issues? Are staff able to influence the outcome? How will they be kept up to date with performance?

For example, your operations manager may be responsible for monitoring order fulfillment whilst your customer service manager may be responsible for response times and customer satisfaction. Setting responsibilities will help you monitor performance in an organized manner. It will also encourage your staff to take ownership.

Some KPIs will need to be monitored more frequently than others. Depending on your operations some may need to be monitored throughout the day whilst others can be reviewed on a weekly or monthly basis.

Different departments will use dashboards to monitor different aspects of their operations. The important thing is that the dashboard is providing the right information at the right time to help your staff and management.

Your dashboard should help you understand your performance. Consider whether your staff can easily see the key metrics and understand what they mean.

Using simple graphs can help managers understand where they are performing well and where they need to improve. Comparing performance and using color indicators can help.

It is also important to ensure that your KPIs are aligned to the objectives of your business. Sometimes you may find that different departments have different objectives. Your warehouse department may want to reduce stock levels to reduce costs whilst your sales department may want to ensure you have enough stock.

Both are important to consider but you need to balance them. Ask yourself whether the metrics you have chosen will encourage collaboration.

Your business requirements will change over time so what was useful in the past may not be relevant now. Regularly review your dashboard.

Question to ask: Are your KPIs still aligned to your business goals? Have your operations priorities changed? Are some metrics no longer useful? Have new activities been introduced meaning new metrics are needed? re staff using the dashboard effectively?

Regularly reviewing your dashboard will help you remove any outdated metrics and add new ones. It is important to regularly review how your KPIs are performing. This will help you identify areas to improve.

Finally, you should know what actions will be taken if your KPIs are not performing well. If your KPIs are not performing well you need to have a clear process in place.

Your dashboard should help you make decisions. It shouldn’t just be numbers. Using metrics can help business like Heloix identify issues with their workflow and review how they are doing. This can help make more informed decisions.

When choosing KPIs for your Operations Dashboard you need to think about what objectives you have, what metrics are relevant, what can be measured and ensure you have reliable data. Using the right questions will help your organization understand what criteria to use when choosing your KPIs.

This will help create a simple dashboard that gives you accurate and useful information. Regularly reviewing and having clear responsibilities can help you effectively monitor your performance and improve operations.
