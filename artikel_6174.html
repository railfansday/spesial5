You'll need a valid passport to enter the Cayman Islands. U.S. citizens do not need a visa for stays of up to 30 days, though you'll receive a tourist entry stamp on arrival. Make sure your passport is valid for at least 6 months beyond your travel dates to be safe.

Yes, many restaurants automatically add a 15% gratuity to your bill, so check before tipping on top of that for exceptional service. Cash tips are fine, but many places also take cards.

English is the official language and is widely spoken everywhere you'll go as a visitor. You won't need a translation app.

Grand Cayman is compact and manageable by taxi, rental car, or ride-sharing services. Taxis are metered and reliable; rides typically start at  $8–15, depending on distance. If you rent a car, remember to drive on the left side of the road. Uber does not operate here, but other services are available.

The Cayman Islands are generally safe for tourists, with a strong police presence and low rates of violent crime. Standard travel precautions apply: don't leave valuables unattended, be aware of your surroundings at night, and keep a copy of your important documents separate from the originals.

November through April offers the best weather, warm and comfortable. In peak season, expect higher prices and more crowds. May through October is hurricane season, so be aware that even though direct hits are rare. If you're flexible, September and October can offer lower rates and fewer tourists, with the tradeoff of higher humidity and occasional afternoon storms.

Once you land in Grand Cayman, you will need to take a taxi or book private transportation to your resort. Hotels, resorts and condo properties are not permitted to offer complimentary or in-house airport shuttle services to arriving and departing guests to protect the local taxi and licensed public transit trade. When exiting the airport, simply locate the taxi stand directly outside the main arrivals greeting area for a ride to your hotel.

The best part of staying in Grand Cayman is that the island has no shortage of luxury hotel options in the Seven Mile Beach area, the ideal home base for island activities and excursions.

I stayed at the Kimpton Seafire Resort + Spa, which has three on-site restaurants and bars, a Forbes Five-Star spa (trust me, book the massage) and daily yoga and fitness classes overlooking the ocean. The sprawling pool connects directly to Seven Mile Beach, and the hotel regularly hosts events ranging from mixology classes to live Caribbean music, so there is always something to do.

Once you check in and freshen up, head downstairs at Kimpton Seafire to dine at Ave. The Mediterranean-inspired menu has plenty to choose from, and I can say the local snapper ($38) and the Mediterranean orzo salad ($22) were excellent — and don’t skip the creamy mashed potatoes ($9).

The cocktails were as beautiful as the sunset you'll watch from their outdoor patio. I sipped a colorful mocktail called the Butterfly Lemonade ($12), made with butterfly pea flower, ginger, rosemary, orange, cinnamon, lemon and soda, while my husband enjoyed the Ponoma, which features Beefeater gin, Dolin dry, grapefruit and hibiscus cordial, lemon and ginger ale. It was a perfect ending to day one.

Spend day two on one of Cayman Island’s most popular and iconic excursions: a boat tour to Stingray City. The famous shallow sandbars on the North Sound sit in water that can only be described as Gatorade blue, where visitors can stand, swim and interact with dozens of friendly, wild southern stingrays. If you don’t feel like getting up close and personal with the rays, you are welcome to soak up the sun and enjoy the views from the boat.

Chase Sapphire Preferred editor tip: Activities and tours booked through Chase Travel, such as a boat excursion to Stingray City, earn 5 points per dollar when you pay with your Preferred Card.

For a rooftop sunset dinner, make a reservation at Pom Pom at the neighboring Hotel Indigo, where you can enjoy Caribbean small plates and cocktails with some of the best views Grand Cayman has to offer. Since Hotel Indigo and the Kimpton Seafire are IHG sister properties, you can just take a quick three-minute golf cart ride to get there, no taxi required.

After a long day in the sun, I sipped on the Raspberry & Chicha Tereré ($13), an alcohol-free refresher that blends yerba mate, raspberry, and purple corn. For food, our server recommended the Picanha ($39), a 10-oz. sirloin cap with a Brazilian vinaigrette and lava salt, and Pollo Anticuchos ($30), chicken skewers with Peruvian chilies puré and chimichurri that had a little kick without being too spicy. For dessert, do not skip the Quatro Leches ($15) cake; it was sweet, light and absolutely delicious. Needless to say, it was one of my favorite meals of the whole trip.

Wake up bright and early and take a taxi to Cayman Crystal Caves, an underground cave system that's part geology lesson, part adventure, and it’s safe for all ages. The caves are accessible through guided tours that last about an hour and a half, and take you through chambers filled with stalactites and stalagmites that formed over millennia.

Chase Sapphire Preferred editor tip: Transportation expenses such as taxi rides can earn 2 points per dollar with the Chase Sapphire Preferred card, helping you earn bonus points even when you're exploring beyond your hotel or resort.

If you have time, visit the Queen Elizabeth II Botanical Gardens before heading back to the resort. It has 65 acres of maintained grounds with orchid gardens and woodland trails. You'll wind through native plant collections, endemic species and tropical pathways, home to endangered Grand Cayman Blue Iguanas and other Caribbean-native animals. I’d budget about 90 minutes to wander.

For sunset dinner on the beach, head over to Bonny Moon Beach Club. It’s a two-minute walk from the Kimpton, and the restaurant offers seating under their outdoor canopy or directly on the sand. You can’t go wrong with anything on the menu either: flatbreads, ceviches and hummus will all make for delicious accompaniments to another perfect view of the sun setting over the water.

After a few days full of adventure and sightseeing, I prefer to slow it down on my last full day with a little R&R, and that, of course, includes a trip to the spa. Book a morning treatment at The Spa at Seafire, as I did, and enjoy the Artisan’s Touch massage ($250) or a facial that incorporates locally inspired ingredients and techniques to really lean into the Caribbean vibes. (Ask for Donna if she is available!)

And of course, take advantage of the included amenities, such as the spa garden, steam room, cold plunge and hot tub.

In the afternoon, take it easy with a poolside lunch, then head on over to the beach to unwind on a lounger and go for a calming swim in the Caribbean's clear waters.

Chase Sapphire Preferred editor tip: One of my favorite multipliers on the card focuses on dining. You’ll earn 3 points per dollar on dining, whether it’s happy hour at Tillie's or breakfast at Ave.

For your final night, make your last dinner count at Cocoloba. The beachfront Mexican-inspired restaurant is elevated enough to feel like a celebration, but since it's located at the Kimpton Seafire, you can enjoy the meal and easily head back up to your room to pack and get proper rest before your flight home the next day.

After four days spent swimming with stingrays, exploring underground crystal caves and enjoying the Caribbean, leaving was definitely the hardest part. Thankfully, my Chase Sapphire Preferred card helped make the trip home easier. The card's application fee credit for Global Entry helped cover the cost of enrollment, and the program's expedited arrival process meant minimal wait times when we landed. Instead of standing in long lines, we had extra time to enjoy our layover before the final leg of our journey.

Chase Sapphire Preferred editor tip: Back home, my grocery delivery was waiting for me. The Chase Sapphire Preferred can earn 3 points per dollar on online grocery purchases to help make the reset from vacation a little easier.
