The best MacBook to buy in 2026: Heres who should upgrade to the new OLED MacBook Pro

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

MacBooks have been ridiculously easy to recommend since we first started reviewing them in the mid-2010s, but especially since Apple rolled out its first M-series chips in 2020. That custom silicon affords them much faster, quieter performance than most competing Windows PCs. When you factor in their elite build quality, beautiful displays, solid battery life, and polished operating system, it's little wonder that MacBooks are some of the most well-loved laptops.

This fall, Apple launched two new MacBook Pros — including its first with an OLED touchscreen display. After hands-on testing the entire current lineup, these are the best MacBooks we recommend for different kinds of users.

These are the tech, tools, and products — from laptops to e-readers, from earbuds to robovacs, and more — that Mashable ranks best in class.

The 15-inch M5 MacBook Air is the best MacBook for most people. It's a sleek, future-proofed ultraportable with the right amount of premium features and more than enough muscle for everyday multitasking, including occasional content creation. It's the ideal daily driver.

You might also consider the 13-inch M5 MacBook Air, which is slightly cheaper and more portable. We think it's the best MacBook for college students.

The MacBook Neo is our top pick for budget shoppers, casual users, and younger students. It's not nearly as fast or as fancy as a MacBook Air, but it's a good performer for its price point, and it's just as well-made as any other MacBook. If your typical workload involves bopping between some Chrome tabs and an app or two, look no further.

The only users who should splurge on the new touch-enabled MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display are professional creatives who primarily use their computer for intensive visual and design work. We recommend going with the 16-inch model for the maximum screen space — its tandem OLED display is just that good.

Apple raised the prices of all of its MacBooks this summer — thanks, RAMageddon — and the new MacBook Pros are quite expensive. Even the M6 base model costs $2,000. If you're mulling an upgrade, be sure to keep a close eye on Black Friday sales: Most retailers save their most competitive MacBook deals for the year-end holiday season.

(The Neo wasn't around for Black Friday 2025, but it's due for a discount of at least $50 at this point in its lifecycle.)

For a deeper dive into all of our best MacBook picks, read on.

Apple's latest MacBook Air packs the snappy performance of the M5 chip inside a predictably polished and portable design. It's a great daily driver that can do basically anything the average person needs a laptop for, making it the best MacBook for everyday use. Yes, it's several hundred dollars pricier than a MacBook Neo, but it's a much better, more future-proofed computer in every way.

I think most folks will make good use of the larger screen on the 15-inch M5 MacBook Air, which starts at $1,499. It's configurable with up to 32GB of RAM and up to 4TB of storage.

Read Mashable's full review of the 15-inch Apple MacBook Air (M5).

The M5 MacBook Air successfully beats the awkward middle child allegations. It easily handles the workloads of everyday multitaskers, and it has some premium features that won't feel outdated in a few years (without going too overboard on nice-to-haves).

Performance-wise, this is one of the fastest laptops on the market no matter how you stack it. It zips through simpler single-core tasks like web browsing, and isn't intimidated by heavy multi-core workloads like video editing. Just be mindful that too much of the latter can make it warm and cause thermal throttling — it's fanless, after all. The MacBook Pros have fans, so they're better at handling the stress of creative apps and games over longer periods of time.

The M5 MacBook Air lasted 17 hours and 40 minutes in our battery life benchmark, which is a video rundown test. That's a small downgrade from the last generation, but you won't be screwed if you forget your charger at home.

Aesthetically, this is largely the same laptop as the M4 MacBook Air, which isn't a terrible thing, since that laptop slapped. (It just makes for a boring gen-over-gen update.) It has a crisp 12MP Center Stage webcam, a vivid Liquid Retina display, amazing speakers, a luxe haptic trackpad, a snappy backlit Magic Keyboard with Touch ID, and a sleek all-aluminum design. Like all MacBooks, its build quality is class-leading. You can take your pick from four neutral finishes: sky blue, silver, starlight, and midnight.

For its ultraportable design and slightly lower price tag, I deem the 13-inch M5 MacBook Air the best MacBook for college students — particularly liberal arts, humanities, nursing, and social science majors. (Your coursework probably doesn't require Windows.) The budget MacBook Neo can serve you well in the short term if you just need a basic laptop for writing essays and sending emails. But this compact Air is a smarter investment that will last you past graduation.

As a college student, you'll save $100 on the 13-inch MacBook Air with Apple's education discount. It starts at $1,199 with 16GB of RAM and 512GB of storage, which you can bump up to 32GB and 4TB, respectively.

The 13-inch M5 MacBook Air is just as fast and pretty as the 15-inch model, but easier to carry across campus (and a little nicer on your wallet).

There are only a couple minor differences between the two sizes. The 13-incher has two fewer speakers, and its base model has a lesser GPU with two fewer cores (eight versus 10), making it a hair worse for stuff like light video editing and gaming. Otherwise, their performance and specs are identical. It comes in the same four colorways, too.

The MacBook Neo is the best choice for budget buyers and casual users, including K-12 students. It's not as well-rounded as a MacBook Air, but it's an amazing starter laptop with the same great build quality (and livelier color options). It also makes a good secondary laptop for entertainment and online shopping if your daily driver lives at the office.

The MacBook Neo comes in two configurations. There's a $699 base model with 256GB of storage, and a $799 version that doubles your disk space and adds Touch ID.

Read Mashable's full review of the Apple MacBook Neo.

The Neo doesn't have a ton of pizzazz or multitasking brawn, but it's great for what it is: a very cheap MacBook. It outclasses most similarly priced Windows laptops and Chromebooks.

Inside the Neo, you'll find an A18 Pro chip with a six-core CPU and a five-core GPU, which is a lesser version of the processor inside 2024's iPhone 16 Pro. A laptop running on a smartphone chip? How does that work? Very well, actually, per our benchmarking. (Modern smartphones have been holding their own against PCs for some time now.) In basic single-core tasks, the Neo scores on par with an M4 MacBook Air. In other words, it feels just as snappy as a much more expensive MacBook when you're browsing the web, sending emails, and streaming videos.

In heavy multi-core tasks like video editing, the Neo scores about as well as an M1 MacBook Air from 2020 — not too shabby. Mashable tech editor Timothy Beck Werth had no trouble using it for a simple Final Cut Pro edit. But keep in mind that its 8GB of fixed RAM is really going to limit your ability to juggle a bunch of apps or files. The archetypal Neo user will only have a couple of browser tabs open at any given time, maybe with Spotify running in the background. Anything too demanding will make it pokey.

The Neo lasts for around 15 hours per charge. It dies much sooner than an Air or Pro, but it'll definitely get you through a full workday with plenty of juice to spare.

The 13-inch Neo weighs the same as a 13-inch MacBook Air (which technically measures 13.6 inches across), but it's slightly thicker. It's also fanless, just like the Air. It has a pretty Liquid Retina display and excellent speakers with Dolby Atmos and Spatial Audio support. Its 1080p webcam is located in its upper bezel, so unlike a MacBook Air or base Pro, it doesn't have an uggo notch jutting down into its screen. Apple ditched features like keyboard backlighting, True Tone display technology, a haptic touchpad, and fast Thunderbolt ports in making the Neo affordable, but it didn't skimp on build quality. It has an all-aluminum design that makes it just as sturdy and polished as any other MacBook. (Most cheap laptops have plastic components.) It also comes in some fun colors like blush, citrus, and indigo.

The M6 MacBook Pro is our top pick for power users who are willing to pay a little extra for a smoother, brighter display, more ports, better speakers, and more sustained computing power. It's overkill for the average person, but it's worth the investment for most content creators, developers, AI enthusiasts, and other professionals who run demanding creative apps (plus the occasional game) on a regular basis.

The M6 MacBook Pro is available in one 14-inch size starting at $1,999 with 16GB of RAM and 1TB of storage. It maxes out with 32GB of RAM and 4TB of disk space. You can upgrade its display with a nano-texture (matte) finish for $150 extra.

Some users will look at the M5 MacBook Air and find its specs a little lacking — more "adequate" than "amazing." For you, there's the M6 MacBook Pro.

The MacBook Air's M5 chip is no slouch by any means, but this guy's fresher M6 chip is even more powerful, especially in demanding multi-core workloads. And its fans make it better at handling them over a long period of time; thermal throttling is much more likely with an Air.

It's worth mentioning that Mashable's Timothy Beck Werth was able to play Cyberpunk 2077 on the M6 MacBook Pro at 30fps. I wouldn't buy it solely for this reason: Windows laptops are compatible with way more games than MacBooks, and PCs with discrete GPUs, especially, are much better-equipped to run graphically taxing titles. But just know that AAA gaming is possible here if you want to run the occasional mission after work.

Design-wise, the M6 MacBook Pro is slightly less portable than the Air, but not by much. It's less than 0.2 inches thicker than both sizes and 0.1 pounds heavier than the 15-inch model. This affords it enough room for extra fixings like an HDMI port, an SDXC card slot, and a bonus Thunderbolt port, as well as an awesome hi-fi sound system with force-cancelling woofers. I've tested dozens of laptops over the course of my career, and none of them sound better than a MacBook Pro.

The M5 MacBook Pro looks as spectacular as it sounds, as per tradition. (Apple hasn't made any drastic design changes to it since 2021.) Compared to the M5 MacBook Air's standard Liquid Retina screen, its mini-LED "Liquid Retina XDR" display is crisper, brighter, and smoother when displaying visuals in motion. Take your pick from two finishes: space black or silver. Be mindful that the former is a fingerprint magnet.

We haven't run our battery life test on the M6 MacBook Pro yet, but we have high expectations. Last year's M5 model held out for 21 hours and 17 minutes to become the longest-lasting MacBook we've ever tried.

Apple's all-new MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display is geared toward deep-pocketed creative professionals whose work requires a ton of horsepower and a reference-quality display. (And, to a lesser extent, people who've missed MacBooks' glowing Apple logo, which hasn't been a thing since 2015.)

This beastly machine comes in a 14-inch size (starting at $2,899) and a 16-inch size (starting at $3,399) with an M5 Pro or M5 Max chip, 24GB to 128GB of RAM, and 1TB to 16TB of SSD storage. Both can be upgraded with a nano-texture display finish. We prefer the bigger model for the extra screen space.

Read Mashable's full review of the 16-inch Apple MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display (M5 Pro).

Its name is a mouthful, and its screen is an eyeful. Apple's all-new MacBook Pro has an elite tandem OLED display that wowed Mashable Tech Editor Timothy Beck Werth. "Apple hasn't just made an OLED laptop; it's made a genuine reference-quality display, designed to meet industry standards for video editors, photographers, animators, 3D artists, and color graders," he wrote. ("Reference-quality" refers to the fact that its colors are extremely lifelike and accurate.) It's an absolutely luscious display — not to mention super bright. It can hit up to 2,000 nits of peak HDR brightness, which makes it one of the brightest laptop displays ever made.

The new MacBook Pro's OLED display has touch capabilities, too, which is mainly interesting because it brings support for the Dynamic Island instead of a webcam notch. (And Apple execs' history of hating on touchscreen laptops, of course.) You can't use it as a tablet, nor does it support styluses. "If I were a betting man, I would wager that the touchscreen won't stick around forever," Werth said.

The model we tested ran on the M5 Pro, and our benchmarking showed that it's a macho, macho chip. In multi-core tasks, it whizzes past every laptop processor we've ever tried — in any MacBook or Windows PC. Compared to the M6 chip in the new base MacBook Pro, it offers a jaw-dropping 57 percent performance boost when running intensive software.

What's even more impressive is the fact that the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display doesn't heat up easily, despite being thinner and lighter than the standard MacBook Pros. The 16-inch model weighs in at four pounds — a whopping 0.7 pounds less than its non-OLED counterpart, which makes a real difference when you're working on the go.

The other main design differences between this model and the standard MacBook Pro are its glowing Apple logo and upgraded sound system, which "[provides] deeper bass and better clarity," Werth said. Their keyboards, trackpads, color options, webcams, mics, and port setups are the same.

Our battery life test results for the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display aren't finalized yet, but Apple rated it at up to 24 hours of video streaming (same as the regular Pro).

MacBooks tend to go on sale at their deepest discounts during Amazon's summer Prime Day sale and around Black Friday. They also see steep price cuts as soon as they're no longer current-generation models, meaning right after Apple announces their successors (which usually happens every year in March and October.)

Apple hosts a Black Friday sale every year, but it just entails free gift card promotions. For proper price cuts, shop around at third-party retailers like Amazon and Best Buy.

First, a little info about how Apple classifies its older products. Devices that it stopped selling more than five years ago but less than seven are considered "vintage," whereas devices it stopped selling more than seven years ago are "obsolete."

Vintage MacBooks are still eligible for hardware support from Apple, depending on parts availability, but it won't fix obsolete MacBooks. And you can't get parts for them from third-party service providers like Best Buy and Micro Center.

If your obsolete MacBook is still chugging along like normal, is in good condition, and doesn't seem sluggish or scant on storage, by all means, keep using it. However, if any component breaks, deteriorates, or no longer meets your needs, it's time to send it off to the great MacBook farm in the sky. Consider recycling or donating it so it doesn't sit in a landfill.

While MacBooks don't get software updates forever, it's not a dealbreaker if yours can't run the latest version of macOS. You might be able to repurpose it as a streaming device or turn it into a Chromebook. That said, a MacBook you use as your primary laptop for school or work needs to be compatible with the current macOS in order to stay secure and continue running Apple apps.

FYI: The latest version of Apple's Mac operating system, macOS 27 (aka macOS Golden Gate), only works with M-series MacBooks from 2020 or later. That means if your daily driver is an older model with an Intel chip, it's high time to upgrade. It will still get security updates for the next two-ish years but won't support any new features going forward.

Some of the best Windows laptops and Chromebooks we've tested can rival MacBooks on performance and/or build quality. My favorite MacBook alternatives include the Asus Zenbook A14, the Dell XPS 13, and the Dell XPS 14.

If you want to stay within the Apple ecosystem, iPads also make good MacBook alternatives thanks to iPadOS's "windowing" system.

Mashable has been writing about laptops for over a decade, and I've personally been covering them since 2023. I also helped develop the rigorous hands-on testing process we currently use to review them. This methodology revolves around four criteria:

The laptops we review get put to work as our primary computers. This includes trying any unique software or use cases they support. We also subject all of our loaners to a multi-app/tab stress test and Primate Labs' Geekbench, which measures CPU performance in common tasks. Gaming laptops get put through additional graphical benchmarking.

To gauge a laptop's stamina, we conduct a battery rundown test that involves playing a looped 1080p version of "Tears of Steel," a short open-source Blender movie, at 50 percent brightness and 50 percent volume. Ideally, we hope to get at least 19 hours of battery life from MacBooks.

As we're using a laptop, we zero in on certain components to evaluate its build quality. These include the display, keyboard, touchpad, webcam, speakers, and ports. We also assess its overall aesthetic and portability.

We determine the ultimate value of a laptop by comparing its performance, design/build quality, and battery life to other laptops with similar pricing, specs, release dates, and use cases. We consider any accessories it comes with, any upgrades from its predecessor(s), and its future-proofing.
