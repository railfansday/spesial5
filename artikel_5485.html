From chaos to close: Holly Mabery says agents need to quell confusion

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

eXp Realty chief brokerage officer Holly Mabery told NAR NXT attendees to separate headlines from actionable local impacts. She said clearer, story-driven communication and stronger CRM follow-up can help agents win share even if 2027 sales fall below 4 million.  AI Summary

In Holly Mabery’s view, 2026 has been a year filled with chaos. The year started off full of optimism and promise and much like many new year’s resolutions quickly turned. But, Mabery, who serves as eXp Realty’s chief brokerage officer, believes agents can turn the chaos into closed transactions.

“You see a bunch of headlines and does anybody feel like they just want to go back to bed? And then it is like how do I stay ahead? What does that look like? What do I say to people, consumers, team members?” Mabery said during a session at the National Association of Realtors 2026 NXT conference over the weekend.

For agents, brokers and team leaders feeling overwhelmed by it all, Mabery said it’s first important to remember that they are not going through this alone and that other agents and brokers are asking themselves the same questions. From there, the key to navigating the chaos, according to Mabery is figuring out what the most important things are and making them relevant to the person sitting in front of them.

“I can’t teach advanced geometry to a six-year-old. But I can show them the difference  between a square and a triangle,” Mabery, a HousingWire Woman of Influence, Insider and Vanguard award winner, said. “The more you and I make things relevant and connect the dots for people, it becomes easier. But also I find that in connecting the dots, that trust layer opens up and everybody exhales.”

In markets and environments currently experienced by real estate professionals, Mabery said it is easy to allow the chaos to turn in confusion.

“When we have confusion, then there is no action as we lose opportunities. The more people are confused, the more they are afraid to move. It is like being stuck in really deep, thick mud,” she said.

To prevent this from happening with their agents and clients, Mabery said brokers and agents need to recognize what has happened and explain it using no jargon terms and then translate it to their specific market or situation by employing a noise versus action filter. This will allow them to understand what a national headline means for them and what actions they may need to take as a result.

Mabery used an example of the Federal Reserve’s rate hike in September, noting that most people feel this is a buyer problem and would have a conversation with their buy-side clients about how this impacts them and how they may need to change their offer strategy. But, Mabery said in her view it is actually a seller problem.

“This is a seller problem because now, when less buyers feel like they can take action, it falls to the seller to take action,” Mabery said. “What are you willing to do in this market to actually make a difference to get people into your home and writing an offer?”

When you provide consumers or even agents with information like this, Mabery says it enables them to feel empowered and hopeful.

“As you look at 2027, I’m not scared for 3.8 million transactions, I’m just hunting for my piece of it,” Mabery said.

With agents and brokers seriously contending with the possibility of a sub-4 million home sales environment in 2027, Mabery said it is important that agents and brokers find news and data sources they can trust, that they get back in touch with past-clients in their CRM and that they work on their story-telling skills.

For Mabery, keeping client-facing messages short, personal and story-driven will enable agents to better connect with consumers and build trust.

“The relationships you and I have are making all the difference in the world,” Mabery said. “The more confidence you build the more you inspire people to take action. When people are confused they stop taking action and we don’t sell anything and that is what we saw in 2026.”

Despite this, Mabery noted that people still move and buy homes due to life events, inviting real estate professionals into their inner circles during some of the most exciting and trying times, but in order to be invited into these moments agents need to build trust in advance.

“Take away the chaos and talk about what is next,” she said. “I want you to calm the fears as we go forward. The more you share your knowledge, your value goes up. And if I don’t take action, is anybody hurt? No. But if I do, I get to help them.”
