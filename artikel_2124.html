Wolves tied Kieran Trippier down until 2028 – now comes a difficult decision

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Wolves are in a good position as they look to bounce back to the Premier League at the first attempt.

It was a strange summer at Molineux, with Rob Edwards surprisingly dismissed for Cesar Peixoto, and the squad overhaul that many anticipated didn’t come to fruition.

However, to their credit, Wolves did manage to secure two statement signings early in the window, as Raul Jimenez and Kieran Trippier joined the club as free agents.

Both deals were seen as huge coups for the Championship side, as both players featured in the Premier League last season, and they have had excellent careers.

And, especially in the case of Trippier, it saw Wolves addressing what had been a problem position, with Jackson Tchatchoua having endured a miserable first season in English football as the team were relegated last season.

But, with Trippier 36, Peixoto has had to manage the minutes of the former Newcastle man carefully, with the need to do that reinforced after the right-back suffered an injury in the opening day draw against Blackburn that ruled him out for a month.

Since then, it has been noticeable that Trippier only played twice in the three-game weeks that Wanderers have had.

That meant he watched on as Wolves beat Burnley last month, and he was also an unused substitute in the draw at Wrexham last time out, having played 90 minutes against Cardiff and Bristol City prior to that.

Many will see this as sensible management from Peixoto, but it shows the challenges the Portuguese head coach faces, as he will recognise just how important Trippier is to the team.

Despite his age, Trippier remains a decent one-vs-one defender, even if he has struggled at times, with Tommy Conway giving him a tough time in Wolves’ defeat at Middlesbrough.

On the whole, though, even though he lacks pace, Trippier’s intelligence and positional play means he always knows what to do, and he plays a key role in ensuring Wolves are solid at the back.

Then, on the ball, he remains a fantastic player. Whether it’s finding the channels or pinging balls into the feet of attacking players, there are few defenders at this level who have the technical ability of the former England international, who is crucial to the way Wolves build up play.

When you add in his devastating delivery that has already brought rewards, it’s no exaggeration to say that Trippier is one of the most important players in the Wolves squad, even though his best days are behind him - and that's without discussing his leadership qualities.

Yet, as outlined above, Peixoto must pick and choose the games that Trippier plays, so backup right-back Jackson Tchatchoua is going to get a lot of minutes this season.

He has already featured in nine Championship games, and the low point undoubtedly came in the 2-2 draw at Birmingham City, where he inexplicably fired the ball past Jose Sa to score an own goal.

But, since then, Tchatchoua has been solid if unspectacular, with his electric pace meaning he is very difficult to beat.

However, his quality on the ball is incomparable to Trippier, so it certainly restricts the way Wolves can play when the ex-Verona man starts.

That is another reason why Trippier’s minutes will be carefully selected, as a long-term lay-off could have major implications, even if Tchatchoua has been steady when called upon since the mistake at St. Andrew’s.

Trippier’s age and fitness issues mean Peixoto is always having to look ahead, and with the demands of the Championship clear, he has probably mapped out how he wants to use the full-back with the player.

That will be particularly important in the latter part of the campaign, as Peixoto is going to want Trippier, who has captained the team on occasions, to be out there in the biggest games.

Looking beyond this season, though, it shows Wolves face a dilemma in the summer, as they signed Trippier on a two-year contract.

If they remain in the Championship, it wouldn’t be a major surprise if the same situation played out, with Tchatchoua, or a new signing, acting as deputy to Trippier.

But, promotion could change the outlook considerably. Firstly, there aren’t as many games, so, in theory, Trippier could play more regularly as he won’t be required to play three times a week very often.

Nevertheless, there will be doubts about his ability to cope in the Premier League, so Trippier may go from a key player to someone on the fringes, which could impact the dynamic in the dressing room.

Trippier will be integral to their chances of doing that - although his age means that Tchatchoua is going to have a bigger role to play than many perhaps envisaged as well.
