Anti-Arab hate speech surged on social media during Israel’s election, report finds

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Digital monitors recorded 400% rise in posts attacking Arab Israelis, including Netanyahu’s disproven fraud allegations

Anti-Arab hate speech surged on social media in Israel around the country’s general election last month, digital monitors have said, as politicians including Benjamin Netanyahu levelled disproven allegations against Arab Israeli parties of election fraud.

A new report by the Israel-based Brandeis Institute and FakeReporter recorded a deluge of anti-Arab discourse across social media platforms such as X, Facebook, Instagram and TikTok in the days surrounding Israel’s 27 October election.

According to FakeReporter’s analysis, anti-Arab sentiment on X rose by more than 400% from election day on 27 October to 29 October. Similar content also spread across Facebook and other platforms.

Arab-majority parties won 19 seats in the Israeli Knesset in last month’s election, potentially putting them in a kingmaker position. The opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot has been invited to try to put together a coalition to replace Netanyahu’s religious-nationalist government.

The posts mainly pushed three messages, the first of which was the allegation that “the Arabs stole the election” via fraud, vote buying and foreign interference, according to the report. Israel’s electoral commission had already publicly refuted such claims.

Posts also depicted Arab citizens of Israel as a security threat and called for them to have their right to vote restricted.

Some of the most-viewed posts also came from top Israeli officials and Netanyahu’s post “They stole the election in Israel!” was viewed 1.2m times. A post by the far-right national security minister, Itamar Ben-Gvir, calling for heads of Arab political parties was also viewed more than 350,000 times.

FakeReporter said much of the anti-Arab hate speech was fuelled by politicians from Netanyahu’s political bloc. “It can be said that they were a major factor in spreading hate speech against Arab citizens of Israel following the election,” its analysis said.

Arab citizens of Israel, the majority of whom are Palestinians, make up 21% of the population of Israel. Since the Hamas-led 7 October 2023 attack and Israel’s subsequent war in Gaza, Arab political leaders have faced increasingly inflammatory attacks and accusations of dual loyalties.

In the run-up to the election, Israel’s Central Elections Committee voted to bar the country’s two main Arab majority political lists from running – a move later struck down by the Israeli supreme court.

Eisenkot last week condemned what he called a “racist and aggressive campaign of delegitimisation” against the country’s Arab citizens.

The Brandeis Institute and FakeReporter sent letters to Google, Meta, TikTok and X on 4 November calling on them to take steps to stop the proliferation of hate speech against Arab citizens of Israel on their platforms.

Only Google responded, saying that Israel’s election is a “Priority 0” for the company and that it has dedicated resources to monitoring traffic around the election, but that the Central Elections Committee had made no requests for extra moderation procedures.
