Why Firefly Aerospace Stock Dropped This Week

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

There's more than one reason this space stock is falling to Earth.

By Scott Levine – Nov 20, 2026 at 6:03AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Firefly Aerospace ›

An analyst downgrade and market skepticism over a major engine contract sent Firefly Aerospace (FLY -1.46%) sliding 22.6% this week.

Firefly Aerospace stock changed hands at $19.52 as of the close on Thursday, Nov. 19, down 22.6% from Friday, Nov. 13's close through Thursday, Nov. 19's close, according to data provided by S&P Global Market Intelligence. Over the same period, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.45%) dropped 5.1%, and the Nasdaq-100 was down 5.1%.

The primary driver behind the decline was market scrutiny of Firefly's recent contract structure alongside a Wall Street Zen downgrade from hold to sell. Securities filings disclosed that AST SpaceMobile (ASTS -1.45%) issued 10 million shares to Firefly as part of an intellectual property license and rocket engine supply agreement valued at over $1.3 billion. Investors grew uneasy that a substantial portion of the contract relies on customer stock rather than guaranteed cash, while management also pushed back its Alpha Flight 8 rocket launch to early 2027. The sell-off leaves the stock down 68.6% (as of the close on Thursday, Nov. 19) from its 52-week high of $62.17.

Firefly Aerospace builds small- to medium-payload launch rockets, lunar landers, and orbital spacecraft for defense agencies, satellite operators, and commercial customers. In its last reported quarter ending Sept. 30, 2026, the company generated $110.7 million in revenue, up 259.6% year over year, while posting a net loss of $94.2 million. High production costs for its rocket line continue to weigh on profitability, keeping operational execution front and center for shareholders.

Firefly held $534.7 million in cash and short-term investments as of Sept. 30, 2026, providing liquidity to fund facility upgrades. However, taking customer stock to fund engine production exposes the business to secondary market volatility rather than securing immediate balance-sheet cash. Receiving stock instead of cash for its rocket engines shifts the AST deal from a liquidity boost to a bet on another speculative satellite maker.

With trailing price-to-sales (P/S) sitting at 9.4 against a $3.5 billion market capitalization, Firefly carries a valuation that demands steady operational progress. Investors will want to watch the initial flight tests for its Block II engine upgrade, currently scheduled for the first quarter of 2027, as well as the company's next scheduled earnings report on March 18, 2027.

Investors may have soured on Firefly stock this week after a downgrade from Wall Street Zen. Still, it's important to remember that this is solely one firm's opinion -- not an overwhelming consensus among a multitude of analysts. Therefore, investors should take it with a grain of salt.

Secondly, the delay of the Alpha Flight 8 launch until the first quarter of 2027 may also have disappointed investors. However, it stems from a national security customer's request rather than a company misstep.

While Firefly receiving AST stock instead of cash for engine licensing is a valid concern, it should hardly be enough to disqualify Firefly stock as a potential opportunity for exposure to the burgeoning space economy. Rather, investors should continue to monitor the company’s earnings reports carefully to confirm that Firefly continues to grow revenue and narrow its net losses.
