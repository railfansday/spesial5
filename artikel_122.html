‘Spared a lot’: Kessler twins explained assisted dying decision in final letter

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Pop-singing sisters, who died aged 89 last year, described their declining health and desire to depart life together

Alice and Ellen Kessler, the identical pop-singing twins who chose to jointly end their lives through assisted dying last year, said they had “reached rock bottom” due to health problems and had been relieved to be able to die together.

The German sisters, who became huge television stars in the 1960s and 70s, especially in Italy and the US, said a catalogue of health conditions had left them with no desire to carry on living.

In a letter released by a friend, the 89-year-olds wrote: “Dear friends, we’re sorry that we’ve chosen to go this way, but due to our medical conditions we had no quality of life any more.

“Apart from our heart problems, we no longer have our senses of taste and smell. On top of that Ellen has suffered a brain stem stroke, and as a result we’ve reached rock bottom.”

Because they had “entered the world together”, they wished “to depart it together the same way”, they added, concluding: “Don’t be sad; we’re happy that assisted dying exists, because certainly as a result we will have been spared a lot.”

They signed off: “Wishing you all the very best. We’ll see you on Cloud 9.”

The sisters died on 18 November last year , assisted by a doctor and a lawyer, in the villa they shared in the Grünwald district of Munich in Germany, which was divided into two mirrored halves, one for each.

They had planned the process together, working with an assisted dying organisation, but had kept this from their friends.

The letters were posted into the letter boxes of people close to them on the evening before their deaths, with a handwritten instruction on the envelope: “Don’t open until 17 November.” Inside each envelope they had placed the gift of a piece of their jewellery.

Bibi Johns, a 97-year-old actor and the singers’ closest friend, the three having met on the set of the 1959 film La Paloma, said she heard of their deaths on the radio before she had had the chance to open the letter from them.

“It was a shock,” she told the newspaper Bild. “Alice and Ellen were like sisters to me. For me it feels as if they have merely gone on a long journey. I’m still waiting for them to call me up.”

They bequeathed to her a gold pendant depicting the heads of two signs of the zodiac, a gift in 1968 from the French singer Marcel Amont to his then-partner Alice.

The year before their deaths, in an interview with Italy’s Corriere della Sera, the sisters said their wish was “to leave together, on the same day … the idea that one of the two will go first is very difficult to bear”. In their lifetimes they had also made a pact that neither would marry.
