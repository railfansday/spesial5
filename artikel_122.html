Alexander Zverev notified of abuse lawsuit by ex-partner during ATP Finals

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Men’s world No 1 allegedly violated terms of settlement

Lawyers say Zverev will ‘defend himself against lawsuit’

Alexander Zverev was officially notified of a lawsuit filed by his former partner during the middle of the season-ending ATP Finals on Wednesday. The latest legal case against the men’s world No 1 brought by the mother of the former couple’s daughter alleges that Zverev violated the terms of an out-of-court settlement between the pair after the woman accused him of assaulting her.

The lawsuit brought by Brenda Patea in Germany takes issue with Zverev reportedly saying in an interview with the French sports daily L’Equipe earlier this year that “it has been proven that the accusations were false” and “my innocence has been proven”.

Zverev’s Berlin-based lawyers, Christian Schertz and Anna Sophie Heuchemer, confirmed in a statement that they were formally served with Patea’s lawsuit on Wednesday.

“Our client will defend himself against the lawsuit,” Zverev’s lawyers said. “If the statement attributed to him was indeed made in the form alleged, we consider it to be a permissible expression of opinion” – which would be protected by the German constitution. They said there had been no breach of any contractual obligations.

Patea’s lawyer, Michael Scheele, said that Zverev’s claim over his innocence “triggered a veritable storm of criticism against our client”. He said the backlash was damaging to her business as an influencer and that she also felt her honour had been violated.

“Quite apart from the fact that Zverev’s claim is false, he was not permitted to make any public statements whatsoever, in any form,” he said. “Both Brenda Patea and Alexander Zverev had agreed to this in a settlement.”

Patea is seeking that Zverev be ordered not to repeat such comments and that he be fined up to €250,000 (£208,500) every time if he does.

Zverev faced a charge of causing bodily harm to Patea during an argument in Berlin in May 2020. He always denied any wrongdoing. The case ended in a district court in Berlin in 2024 after the settlement. The case officially declared no verdict and the settlement entailed no acknowledgment of guilt on Zverev’s part. Zverev agreed to pay fines to the state and charitable organisations.

The German player this month ascended to the world No 1 ranking for the first time. He also won his first two grand slam titles this year and is competing this week at the ATP Finals in Turin.
