F1 and Manufacturers Unanimously Agree to a Plan to Return to V8 Engines in 2030

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The shrill roar of the V8 engines is set to return to the grid, with the FIA and F1 teams establishing the framework for a 2030 power-unit switch.

The FIA began the Las Vegas Grand Prix weekend by announcing that all manufacturers have agreed to the framework to bring turbocharged V8 engines back to F1 and move away from the current V6 turbo.

This move has long been expected, with hopes that it will make engine building easier and cheaper while bringing back the sound that motorsport fans deeply miss.

The sound of the V8 engines was a rally cry for fans and has been sorely missed by audiences worldwide.

The F1 engine manufacturers agreed to a rough-out overarching plan to move to a 2.1-liter turbocharged V8 from 2030 onwards, with a lower charge of electrical power reducing the hybrid output from 350kW to 200kW, a decrease of nearly 56 percent.

The FIA announced that ‘The plan is to marry the V8 power unit to a chassis which will also be lighter, more agile and placing greater emphasis on driver skill and close racing.”

With everyone in agreement, PU manufacturers will start to jockey between improving the new car entering a sophomore season of new regulations and game planning for the major change coming just three seasons down the line.

It’s been 13 years since manufacturers last built a V8 engine for F1.

“This agreement marks a significant milestone in shaping the future of Formula 1,” FIA President Mohammed Ben Sulayem said. “From the outset, I have been clear in my ambition to deliver a sport that is more competitive and more exciting for our fans, while remaining at the forefront of innovation and sustainability.

“The return of V8 engines represents an opportunity to reconnect with the DNA of this Championship, bringing back the sound, emotion and pure racing that is loved worldwide. Our vision is clear, lighter, simpler and more affordable cars that put driver skill at the heart of the competition.”
