There are approximately 22 million millionaire households in the United States. That is about 17% of all US households — roughly 1 in 6. And the number is growing: the US added over 2 million new millionaire households between 2019 and 2022 alone, even through a global pandemic.

But here is what might surprise you: most millionaires do not look like what you expect. They are not driving Ferraris or living in mansions. The research — most notably from Thomas Stanley and William Danko's landmark study "The Millionaire Next Door" and Chris Hogan's follow-up study of over 10,000 millionaires — consistently shows that the typical American millionaire is a middle-aged person with a modest lifestyle who has been saving and investing for 25-30 years.

This matters for the FI community because it proves that building a million-dollar net worth is not about luck, inheritance, or extraordinary income. It is about consistent behavior applied over time. And if you are on the FI path, you are following the exact same playbook that created 22 million millionaires.

The exact number depends on how you measure it and which data source you use:

The discrepancy between 22 million and 14-15 million comes down to whether you include home equity. If you count total net worth (including home equity), approximately 22 million households qualify. If you count only investable assets (excluding the primary residence), the number drops to about 14-15 million.

For FI purposes, investable assets are the more meaningful measure — your home equity does not fund your retirement spending unless you sell or downsize. But by either measure, the US has more millionaires than any other country in the world.

Chris Hogan's National Study of Millionaires (the largest ever conducted) surveyed over 10,000 millionaires and found: 79% received zero inheritance. The top occupations were engineer, accountant, teacher, management, and attorney — not entrepreneurs or celebrities. 96% said anyone can become a millionaire with discipline and consistent investing. The average millionaire started investing at age 29 and became a millionaire at 49. They are your neighbors, not the people on magazine covers.

The research paints a consistent picture of the typical American millionaire — and it looks very different from what pop culture suggests.

If you are pursuing financial independence, you are on the millionaire path by design. Here is why:

The standard FI formula is 25x your annual spending. If your household spends $40,000 per year, your FI number is $1,000,000 — you are literally targeting millionaire status. If you spend $60,000, your number is $1.5 million. If you spend $80,000, it is $2 million.

Every FI practitioner is building toward a net worth that exceeds $1 million. The difference between the FI community and the general millionaire population is intentionality: FI community members know their number, track their progress monthly, and optimize their savings rate to get there as quickly as possible.

The data from "The Millionaire Next Door" and subsequent research validates the FI approach completely: live below your means, invest the difference in diversified low-cost funds, avoid debt, and let compound growth do the heavy lifting over 15-25 years. That is the millionaire formula. It is also the FI formula. They are the same thing.

Overwhelmingly self-made. Chris Hogan's National Study of Millionaires (10,000+ surveyed) found that 79% received zero inheritance. Only 3% received an inheritance over $1 million. The myth that most wealthy people inherited their money is not supported by the data.

The average millionaire earned a solid but not exceptional income, lived below their means, invested in diversified funds for 25-30 years, and avoided consumer debt. If that description sounds familiar, it should — it is the exact blueprint the FI community follows. You do not need to be born wealthy or earn a six-figure salary to join the millionaire club. You need a savings rate above 20%, a low-cost index fund, and enough time for compound growth to do its work. The 22 million millionaires before you have proven it works.
