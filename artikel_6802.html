Nourishing favorites from brands like Augustinus Bader and ZIIP come expert-recommended

There are plenty of ways to treat signs of aging, but among the most effective is also the simplest: Striking the right moisture balance for your skin. Proper hydration can help reduce the appearance of fine lines and dullness, while also improving skin laxity over time. While almost any face cream can boost hydration, the best moisturizers for mature skin provide targeted, preventative treatments in a jar.

Key ingredients, such as peptides, retinoids, and others, work together to target specific concerns. “Retinoids or plant-based alternative bakuchiol improve and prevent fine lines, increase cellular turnover, improve skin texture and elasticity, and [reduce] discoloration,” dermatologist Morayo Adisa tells Bazaar. “Look for antioxidants such as vitamin C and E to help with collagen production and decrease oxidative stress. Hyaluronic acid, glycerin, and urea to increase skin hydration, softening the appearance of lines and wrinkles. Also, look for an emollient such as squalane or shea butter.”

Several moisturizers feature at least one of these transformative ingredients, so how do you know which one is right for you? To help narrow down your options, we asked Adisa, along with other dermatologists, for their thoughts on some of the best moisturizers for mature skin.

Adisa noted earlier that retinoids or the plant-based alternative, bakuchiol, are ideal for improving and preventing fine lines, increasing cellular turnover, smoothing skin texture, and enhancing elasticity—so check ingredient lists for these. Garshick says to also look for moisturizers like hyaluronic acid and glycerin, along with ingredients to support the skin barrier, such as ceramides and squalane, and peptides or growth factors to help with firmness. “Antioxidants like vitamin C can help to protect against environmental stressors, while niacinamide may be added to help calm and soothe the skin,” Garshick notes.

“As we age, the skin barrier weakens, contributing to increased moisture loss, and there is decreased production of sebum, hyaluronic acid, and ceramides, key ingredients that help to keep the skin moisturized. This makes the skin more prone to dryness and dullness,” Garshick explains.

Johnson adds that skin dries out with age primarily due to a loss of circulation. “As the skin ages, it loses about one percent of circulation each adult year, so at 50, the skin has 25 percent less circulation,” says Johnson. “This leads to fewer nutrients, lipids, and immune cells, which result in a porous, dehydrated barrier.”

Incorporating a moisturizer for mature skin helps hydrate the skin while supporting the skin barrier. “Some moisturizers may also contain ingredients that help to support collagen production, improving the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles while also firming the skin,” notes Garshick.

Johnson advises steering clear of alpha-hydroxy acids because they can dry out and damage the skin. Ceramides, while generally good for sealing in moisture, can also be problematic. “They have a feedback-loop effect on the skin that results in them slowing down your epidermal turnover rate,” the expert says. You should also ease off on harsh exfoliants, which can damage the skin barrier and speed up moisture loss, leaving skin dry and crepey.
