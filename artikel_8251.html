Shah Daulat, a Celebrated Alpinist Who Summited K2, Dies at 31

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Shah Daulat, 31, was raised in the mountains of Shimshal, a remote village in Pakistan’s Hunza Valley that has encouraged and supported generations of the country’s strongest climbers. In his early years of being a professional guide with the Karakoram Mountain Guides Association, Daulat climbed four of the world’s 8,000-meter peaks, cementing his legacy as one of Pakistan’s rising alpinists.

“His journey from a mountain guide to climbing some of the world’s most challenging peaks is something our family is incredibly proud of,” Ibadat said. “He dedicated his life to the mountains and was deeply passionate about climbing.”

Daulat began working as a guide in 2022, and his progression onto the world’s highest peaks came quickly. That year, he summited K2, at 8,611 meters the second-highest mountain in the world. In 2023, he climbed 8,126-meter Nanga Parbat without supplemental oxygen, followed by Gasherbrum II in 2024. In 2025, he summited Gasherbrum I, again without supplemental oxygen, marking his fourth 8,000-meter summit in as many years. That same year, he also climbed Laila Peak, a 6,096-meter mountain in Pakistan’s Hushe Valley.

But his life in the mountains extended far beyond expedition climbing. He was a skier, too, competing at Malam Jabba in northern Pakistan in 2022. And though his career took him to some of the highest vistas in the world, it was on a much smaller objective, close to home, that he passed.

On February 6, 2026, Daulat was ice climbing near Khyber village in Gojal, Hunza, when he fell nearly 400 meters. Local volunteers recovered his body, which was later returned to his home village of Shimshal for burial.

After his death, Daulat’s cousin, pioneering Pakistani mountaineer Samina Baig, remembered both what he had accomplished in a remarkably short climbing career and the person behind those achievements. “Shah Daulat was a true gem in our family—a cherished younger brother (cousin) taken from us far too soon,” Baig wrote in a Facebook tribute. “A young mountaineer, he achieved so much in such a short life, touching many hearts with his courage and gentle spirit.

“May he rest in peace among the mountains he loved,” she continued. “He will forever remain in our hearts, remembered with love and quiet pride.”

In September, the villages of Shimsal and Khyber jointly installed a life-sized memorial sculpture of Daulat near the Khyber Ice Wall, which has been renamed the Shah Daulat Ice Wall.
