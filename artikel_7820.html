Short Circuit: An inexhaustive weekly compendium of rulings from the federal courts of appeal

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

Uncontrolled burns, false claims, and federal highway funds.

Please enjoy the latest edition of Short Circuit, a weekly feature written by a bunch of people at the Institute for Justice.

Victory! Millions of Americans want and need low-FODMAP foods, which are low in certain sugars that can cause debilitating stomach issues, aggravate irritable bowels, and worse. Fortunately, this week (following the Ninth Circuit's revival of our First Amendment challenge), the FDA lifted its ban on producers truthfully labelling their products "Low-FODMAP." Says IJ client Michelle Przybocki, who abides by a strict low-FODMAP diet after nearly dying from digestive complications, "I'm thrilled that the federal government is going to allow companies to start labeling their products as 'Low-FODMAP,' because this makes it easier for me to find foods that I know will not cause me digestive issues." Click here to learn more.

New on the Short Circuit podcast: Fancy some Elevenses? Serving up Eleventh Circuit refreshments in our #12Months12Circuits series. There's excessive bail in Alabama and excessive bar fees in Florida.

Friends, if a federal official violates your constitutional rights, your options for being made whole by money damages have been all but extinguished in recent years, leaving the feds with something akin to absolute immunity for all kinds of misconduct and misdeeds. But there has to be some avenue for accountability. So here at IJ, we're pushing back, not only on the late-lamented Bivens cause of action, but also on the too-narrowly construed Federal Tort Claims Act. And then, of course, there's the traditional source of accountability that the Founders would have recognized (and that the Supreme Court's Bivens skeptics preferred): state law causes of action, like California's traditional tort system and the state's Bane Act. All of which were on the table at oral argument in the Ninth Circuit this week, where we're happy to report we had an engaged, cross-ideological panel. Click here to learn more.
