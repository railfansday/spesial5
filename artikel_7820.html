Justice Department’s Statement on the Closing of Its Investigation of NextEra’s Acquisition of Dominion

The U.S. Department of Justice issued the following statement today in connection with the closing of the Antitrust Division’s investigation into the acquisition of Dominion Energy Inc. (Dominion) by NextEra Energy Inc. (NextEra) (together, “the Parties”):

The Antitrust Division continues to focus on the needs of electricity customers across the country to ensure that they benefit from competition among electricity generators. As demands on electricity grids increase, it is critical that wholesale electricity and capacity markets are competitive and cannot be exploited in a way that could leave American businesses and consumers facing higher electricity bills. The Parties’ commitment to FERC eliminating their unilateral right to deactivate units in New England will protect consumers in the region from potentially hundreds of millions of dollars of increased electricity costs.

On May 18, NextEra and Dominion announced an agreement to combine the two companies in a $66.8 billion transaction. Both firms are electricity generation companies that compete in wholesale electricity and electric capacity auctions run by Regional Transmission Organizations (RTOs) or Independent System Operators (ISOs). RTOs and ISOs are nonprofit organizations responsible for managing and operating electric power grids within specific regions of the United States. These organizations rely on internal or external market monitors, who are responsible for overseeing and evaluating the electricity market’s performance.

NextEra’s acquisition of Dominion combines overlapping electricity generation asset portfolios in two separate RTO/ISOs: PJM Interconnection LLC (which covers portions of the Midwest and Mid-Atlantic) and ISO New England Inc. (ISO-NE).

As required by federal law, NextEra and Dominion have filed an application with the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) for approval of the transaction. During FERC’s ongoing review process, the Internal Market Monitor of ISO-NE submitted comments to FERC regarding the potential impact of the transaction on the electric capacity market. Electric capacity markets ensure the availability of a reserve of capacity that an ISO or RTO can call on to provide network reliability during periods of peak electricity demand. They do this by paying generators to keep plants on-call and available to produce electricity, independent of any payments the generators receive from the sale of electricity generation.

The Internal Market Monitor expressed concerns that the transaction might give the merged firm the incentive and ability to deactivate a plant that still is economically viable with the goal of using its combined portfolio to increase capacity payments in New England. To address this concern, the Internal Market Monitor proposed that the merged firm agree to allow the Internal Market Monitor to investigate any plant that the merged firm proposes to deactivate for a five-year period beginning with the next ISO-NE capacity auction in May 2028. If the Internal Market Monitor determines that the plant continues to have economic value, then NextEra would be prohibited from deactivating it.

Following a detailed investigation — which included review of documents, interviews of industry participants, and sophisticated economic modeling — the Division remained concerned about the potential impact of the transaction on ISO-NE’s capacity market. After the Division informed the Parties of these concerns, the Parties told FERC in a filing on FERC’s docket that they will “commit, without qualification, to comply” with the Internal Market Monitor’s proposal regardless of whether FERC imposes it as a condition for approval of the transaction.

In light of the Division’s review and the Parties’ commitment to FERC, the Antitrust Division decided that its investigation could be closed.
