Lance Broadway, Marvel Actor and Former MLB Pitcher, Dead at 43

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Former MLB pitcher Lance Broadway—who pivoted to acting with roles in Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D., Olympus Has Fallen and more—has unexpectedly died at the age of 43.

Hollywood is mourning a marvelous talent.

Lance Broadway, the former pitcher for the Chicago White Sox and New York Mets, died unexpectedly on Nov. 17, according to the Dallas County Medical Examiner's Office. He was 43.

The Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D. actor died in an apartment complex stairwell in Dallas, Texas, public records confirm, though his cause of death is still pending autopsy results.

Broadway began his career in the spotlight with the Chicago baseball team when he was selected as the 15th overall pick at the 2005 MLB draft.

He spent two years in Triple-A—the highest level in Minor League Baseball—before making his Major League debut in 2007 as the White Sox's third-best prospect.

The Sox ultimately traded him to the New York Mets for Ramón Castro two years later. After a few months back in the minors, he was called up to take over for Óliver Perez. But his time on the field didn't end in NYC. Broadway signed with the Toronto Blue Jays three months later to a minor-league deal, concluding his MLB career after a full season with the Triple-A Las Vegas 51s.

Though the former athlete threw his final pitch in 2010, he wasn't ready to bid farewell to the camera just yet. In fact, he went on to make his acting debut in 2011's Deadly Notes and Food Attack.

He then went on to star in Olympus Has Fallen (2013) as Agent O'Neil and played a TAC Team Leader in Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.

Broadway previously revealed that transitioning to acting after concluding his professional sports career wasn't a total surprise.

"It's been a wild ride, that's for sure," he told Screen Slam in 2013. "2010 was my last season and in every offseason I wanted to get involved with something. And I don't know why, but I just wanted to go take an improv class."

"The improv class I wanted didn't start until January 2011, so I asked a friend who told me to go take an acting class," he recalled. "That way, at least I will be in front of people doing something while they watch. So I got into an acting class in November 2010. I loved it and stuck with it."

Broadway emphasized that his past experience in sports stayed with him in his new career, especially when he worked with Gerard Butler, Aaron Eckhart and Morgan Freeman on Olympus Has Fallen.

"It was the same feeling as walking into a big-league locker room with guys that you knew were established, had earned the right to speak, and you do not have that right," he recalled. "I learned so much from these actors and everyone was so nice, but I was just watching them go about their business.”

“I did the same thing in the major leagues,” he added. “I remember the first time I was called up to Chicago, I just wanted to watch and see how these guys went about doing everything.”
