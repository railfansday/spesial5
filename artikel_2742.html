Eagle River boys, girls swim teams are primed to achieve lofty goals at state meet

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Nov. 5—For the past two years, the Eagle River High School girls swimming team has been the cream of the crop in Alaska, winning back-to-back state championships.

This year, the Wolves boys team hasn't just been on the rise; it's been the gold standard.

Last weekend, both Wolves squads claimed Region IV team titles. While the girls narrowly edged past Service by nine points, the boys team had nearly 200 more points than runner-up Chugiak, cruising to the first region championship in their program's history.

"The boys are really fired up because of their opportunity to be the first boys swim team to win the state title here at Eagle River High School," head coach Patrick Garrity said.

The 2026 ASAA state swimming championships start Friday and conclude Saturday at Bartlett High School.

"They're very fired up to go out and get the first (state title) for the school and for each other," Garrity said.

Despite hailing from one of the smaller schools in terms of student population, this year's boys team has impressive depth.

"We have a really complete team, and we managed to fill every spot on our team except for two, so we're completely filled up, and a lot of us made state," senior Nate Shockley said. "More than I expected, to be honest."

He is the reigning state champion in the 200 individual medley. But this weekend, he is going for gold in the 500 freestyle and 200 freestyle and is on multiple relay race teams.

"I'm super excited," Shockley said. "I've had a really good four years here. I think finishing it now is going to be a lot of fun, but still a little nerve-wracking."

While state brings a new set of opponents and conditions, he believes that they should be able to achieve their goal.

"I think our boys are doing amazing right now," Shockley said. "We are going in hoping for another victory, but we need to be on our A game going into it."

He opted not to try to defend his state title in the 200 IM because he has the overall team's best interest in mind.

"It's more beneficial for the team, I believe, for me to do the 200 (freestyle) instead of 200 IM," Shockley said. "I think overall, by me doing the 200 (freestyle), we're going to score a little more."

In addition to being deep, this year's boys team is well balanced with a healthy mixture of experience and promising young swimmers who Garrity says are "pushing the pace."

One of the Wolves' up-and-comers got the better of Shockley at regions in the finals of the 500 freestyle. Sophomore Colin Russian came from behind to pull off the upset and finish first by five-hundredths of a second.

"He came out of nowhere," Shockley said. "He came into the season with like, a 4:55 in the 500 (freestyle), and he's just dropped five or seven seconds. He's done great, and then we have Zach (Jacobs), he's just dropped a ton of time on the 100 (butterfly), which is great. ... Overall, the boys team and the girls team, both teams alike, have just improved so much throughout the season."

Eagle River girls set sights on establishing dynasty

Winning back-to-back team championships is no small task and pulling off two successful title defenses can be even more daunting, but the Lady Wolves are battle-tested and determined to three-peat at state.

"They've had to overcome some adversity this year," Garrity said. "They lost a few members and a couple of illnesses that sidelined a couple people. But they are in good spirits, and they got a fighting chance to get that third title."

Senior Lelaina Trembath is the latest torch-bearer for a culture that has been building up over the past decade and was passed on to her from her older sister Sophia.

Sophia graduated last spring and will be tuning in to the finals virtually from the University of Oregon, where she is enrolled as a student.

"They grew into that culture, and it proved to have a similar spirit, and now they're teaching the younger swimmers how to work hard and how to succeed and how to pass it along to the next group," Garrity said.

Lelaina Trembath is the reigning state champion in the girls' 200 freestyle and will also be competing in the 100 breaststroke and two relay races.

"I'm really looking forward to it, especially for the 200, because I've done this event all four years," she said. "It's really special to me, and it's really fun. For the 100 breast, I'm also really excited because it's my first year competing this one at state, and it should be a really tough competition. But that makes it more fun."

Extending Eagle River's recent legacy of excellence on the girls' side means a lot to her.

"It's been really special," Trembath said. "Our team is always like a family, so I'm really happy to be a part of it."

The Eagle River girls won seven of 12 events at regions, including all three relay races, which has their confidence soaring heading into state.

"We're pretty pumped and confident," Trembath said. "We're definitely reaching for first, and we're working as hard as we can to get there."

The Wolves got a small glimpse of some non-CIC competition at the Bartlett/East Invitational, but one team that wasn't there that they're expecting to make waves at state as their top competition is Juneau-Douglas.

"Juneau and Service are probably our top competitors," Trembath said. "(A three-peat) would be a dream, and that's what we're reaching for."

Seeing his swimmers develop as athletes and as people is "probably the most edifying" aspect of being a coach, in Garrity's eyes, and the role he's played in their lives hasn't gone unnoticed or underappreciated.

"He's always asking us what he can do better, and always helping us with what we need specifically, with breaking up sets based on what events we do," Trembath said. "He's really been such a helpful person in my swimming career. So it's been really nice having him."
