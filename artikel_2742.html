NBA announces $11 billion investment to build European arenas for new league

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The NBA has unveiled a $11 billion funding initiative aimed at developing and renovating basketball arenas across Europe. This investment is designed to support the launch of the new NBA and FIBA-created European League that could see its inaugural tip-off as soon as next fall.

On Wednesday, Nov. 25, the NBA announced a detailed strategic partnership with a coalition of leading global investment firms, including Apollo Sports Capital, Ares Management, Arctos, Blackstone, HPS Investment Partners, J.P. Morgan and others. These firms will spearhead the funding initiative, collaborating closely with participating clubs, team owners, local developers and a range of service providers. Together, they aim to construct and upgrade world-class basketball arenas.

According to the NBA, the initiative will "provide prospective clubs and ownership groups with access to sustainable capital and the NBA's expertise to develop modern arena facilities and, where appropriate, surrounding districts."

The NBA and FIBA have also emphasized that this initiative represents a significant investment not only in the sport itself, but also in the economic vitality of host cities. New or renovated arenas are expected to drive local growth and create jobs.

"We've seen firsthand how state-of-the-art arenas and the related developments around them can transform basketball teams and their surrounding communities," NBA commissioner Adam Silver said in a statement. "As we prepare to launch a new league in Europe alongside FIBA, we see a significant opportunity to help clubs develop facilities which both build on the unique traditions that define European basketball while also elevating the fan experience and attracting many more live events."

While official host cities have yet to be announced, the NBA-FIBA project is reportedly considering several countries as prime candidates for the new league.

Potential host cities include London and Manchester (Britain), Paris and Lyon (France), Madrid and Barcelona (Spain), Rome and Milan (Italy), Munich and Berlin (Germany), Athens (Greece) and Istanbul (Turkey).
