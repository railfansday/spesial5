Observations after Brown-Maxey duo scores 58 points in Sixers' win without Embiid

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Observations after Brown-Maxey duo scores 58 points in Sixers' win without Embiid originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

The Jaylen Brown-Tyrese Maxey duo starred, LeBron James reached another milestone, and the Sixers earned a win without Joel Embiid on Sunday afternoon.

They improved to 11-3 on the season with a 131-124 victory over the Pacers at Xfinity Mobile Arena.

Brown scored 31 points and Maxey posted 27.

LeBron James had 15 points and 11 assists. He passed Jason Kidd on Sunday for third in NBA history with 12,092 assists.

Pascal Siakam scored 31 points for the 5-9 Pacers.

On the first day of a back-to-back, the Sixers were down Embiid (right foot plantar fascia injury management), Dominick Barlow (lower back stress reaction), Adem Bona (cervical spine sprain) and Ariel Hukporti (right Achilles tendon tear). The Pacers' injured players included Tyrese Haliburton and Andrew Nembhard.

The Sixers will face the Nets on Monday night in Brooklyn. Here are observations on their win over the Pacers:

Jabari Walker started for the first time this season, opening the game at center. He's been in the Sixers' rotation the past five games since Bona's injury.

Walker set up the Sixers' first basket, swiping a steal and then tossing the ball ahead to Maxey for a fast-break dunk. Maxey and Brown both began well and combined to score all of the Sixers' first 15 points.

Though he conceded five inches to 7-foot Pacers center Ivica Zubac, Walker was solid in his first stint. He got an early post stop on Zubac and then snatched a strong defensive rebound.

Dean Wade was next up for the Sixers at the five. He sunk a three-pointer on his first shot to give the team a 20-16 lead.

Walker ended up playing 25 minutes and posting 11 points and seven rebounds. Going back to the preseason, his stock has steadily risen.

"Here we are again after another game saying Jabari played very well," Sixers head coach Nick Nurse said. "He plays hard, he rebounds, he's got another level of confidence with his shooting this year.

"It's kind of wash, rinse and repeat again with him (today). He was great. … Jabari went in there and battled (Zubac). I thought he certainly matched him, if not outplayed him, which is a heck of a job."

Wade had 12 points and seven boards in 35 minutes. Overall, the Sixers' lack of healthy conventional centers is still a clear deficiency. Of course, everything should be simpler once Embiid is back in action.

The Sixers' defense did not have a sparkling start. The Pacers tallied 35 points in the first quarter and shot 62.5 percent from the field.

Brown helped the Sixers avoid a substantial deficit by draining all sorts of jumpers. He rarely appears bothered by close contests. Brown moved to 7 for 9 from the floor when he knocked down a three early in the second quarter. Kentavious Caldwell-Pope's triple knotted the contest up at 37-all.

Nurse needed to stray from his Plan A rotation when VJ Edgecombe picked up his third foul with 9:56 left in the second quarter. Labaron Philon Jr., who got a game with the G League's Delaware Blue Coats on Friday night, subbed in.

For a rookie guard, Philon has looked comfortable and confident on offense in most of his NBA appearances. He swished a long-range jumper in the second quarter and saw a floater rim out.

Brown reached the 20-point mark and put the Sixers up one with a slam assisted by James late in the second quarter.

He ran into serious foul trouble in the third quarter. Brown was whistled for his fourth foul with 9:53 left in the period for an off-arm shove on Aaron Nesmith. Wade entered and Brown exited.

The Sixers' slim lead didn't slip away. Walker nailed threes on consecutive possessions and Maxey jammed in a dunk.

Soon after, Tacko Fall came in for his first meaningful minutes as a Sixer. The 7-foot-6 two-way contract player grabbed an offensive board off a missed Edgecombe free throw, which drew one of the day's loudest cheers. With Fall on the floor, Maxey and James connected on an alley-oop that extended the Sixers' lead to 82-73.

When the Sixers tried to sit in a zone, Fall was called for a defensive three-second violation. Whatever the scheme, most of Fall's defensive work will come around the paint. He certainly has exceptional physical traits as a rim protector and is not nimble on the perimeter.

Fall logged five scoreless third-quarter minutes and recorded three rebounds. The Sixers outscored Indiana by a point with Fall on the court. Given Fall is occupying a (two-way) roster slot and the Sixers are so depleted at center, it made sense to see a bit of what he can do outside of garbage time. While five minutes isn't enough for any grand conclusions, it's very safe to say Fall is a unique player.

"I'm 7-6, so I can change the game in so many ways," Fall said. "Offensively, setting screens, making sure guys get open, being an easy target for lobs. Defensively, I feel like I've always been an elite rim protector and I've worked on my body a lot to be able to move a lot better."

Nurse praised Fall's outing, saying he "gave us some great minutes and a nice lift."

Fall is appreciative of his first NBA opportunity since the 2021-22 season.

"I've worked very hard," he said. "God has brought me back here and I'm thankful. And I'm ready. Whenever my name is called, I'm going to go out there and do my job."

The Sixers built their advantage to 102-87 with a Brown three early in the fourth quarter. They couldn't cruise to a blowout, though.

Siakam kept hurting the Sixers as a driver and had a highly efficient fourth quarter, scoring 19 points on 7-for-9 shooting. Quenton Jackson's layup trimmed Indiana's deficit to 119-115.

The Sixers ultimately held off the Pacers' push.

Caldwell-Pope canned a three, Wade threw down an and-one dunk, and James checked off his milestone assist with a late dish to Edgecombe for a layup.
