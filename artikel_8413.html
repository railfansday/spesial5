Day 30: The All-Nighter With the Full Moon and the Cross

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

At 11 p.m., my Argentinian brother and I walked the silent streets of Astorga, embarking on the night walk to Cruz del Ferro.

Like he read my mind, he immediately brought up the question of how long I’d like to walk together before splitting up and walking solo. We agreed on an hour together and then walking independently under the moon until we met at Cruz del Ferro for sunrise.

The moon was unbelievably bright, shining and casting shadows as if it were daylight.

The conditions were perfect beyond my wildest imagination.

For that first hour, we talked in Spanish and English, opening up about the burdens we wanted to lay down at the cross. It was the most I’ve ever learned about him and his life, and doing it under the full moon felt incredibly special.

Turns out, The Pilgrimage by Paulo Coelho also inspired him to embark on the Camino.

That synchronicity reinforced my feeling that this was exactly the person meant to cross my path for this night.

At 12:30, he went ahead and we started on our independent walks.

As I’d come to be so accustomed to, it was just me, my thoughts, and the stars.

Over an hour in, my foot started to hurt, and my eyes began to get heavy. I pushed on, reminding myself of the challenge this symbolized:

This endeavor was a minuscule fraction of what he endured in that story. With that image motivating me, I continued on.

A bit later, I kept sighing, like there was something in my chest that I needed to release. So I did something I rarely do, and I prayed out loud.

For over an hour and a half, I prayed for the people who had touched my life, crafting a unique message for each one. I’d been surrounded by such amazing souls, and I wanted to acknowledge their impact on the divine.

It became this easy-flowing conversation, one that I hadn’t tapped into in a long time. And eventually, I prayed for myself.

As I spoke to God about what I was about to leave at the cross, I felt an overwhelming understanding to surrender. I couldn’t control the situation and the healing of it, so I gave it to God.

Around 4:15 am, the archetype of the Taurus full moon joined me on the walk, literally.

Out of nowhere, a cow appeared on the path and led me forward for five minutes. It wouldn’t look at me, let me pass, or acknowledge my existence, but it felt like a little gift to encourage me to go finish the final climb strong.

I was exhausted. The shadows from the tree made seeing loose rocks extremely difficult, and I kept tripping. My shoulder hurt so bad from the weight of my pack. And no matter how much I walked, it felt like I never arrived.

Not able to see my Argentinian brother anywhere, I sat against a post and fell asleep.

Less than an hour later, I was abruptly woken up by the cold. I could see faint colors in the sky, like the sunrise was beginning, so I walked around trying to find a better spot.

In that exploration, I finally found my friend. He was tucked against the stone building, wrapped in his sleeping bag. In silence, I went, grabbed mine, and sat next to him. Because it was so cold, we nestled together for body heat.

A choir of cows mooed in the distance as the fire of the sun lit up the sky. There was a unique stillness in the air, and it reminded me of what lay under that cross.

I’d heard that this location may have been the site of a Roman or Celtic tradition before the Christian church moved in. For me, that’s a large reason why I chose to walk to this site in such reverence, to honor what came before.

Other pilgrims showed up and we just observed them from afar as they took pictures and talked loudly.

I recognized a few, but my exhaustion combined with reflection made me want to just sit back and go unnoticed.

When it was fully light though, I knew it was time to leave my rock.

Following the tradition as I’d heard it, I cast all the burdens I’d prayed about during my walk, throwing them backward over my head.

I truly felt like I was leaving it there.

And with a new perspective from this experience, I walked away feeling so much lighter.

My Argentine brother and I walked the last 9 kilometers, and in that time we talked about our experience.

The mountains we passed through had me in awe. As we began descending, a fantastic view of the clouds sunk into the valley looked like a sea of white.

It made every second of the night even more worth it.

And ending with that view, we arrived in the tiny mountain town of El Acebo.

It was a peaceful sanctuary for us to eat, rest, and relax.

We got two bunks at Hostel Mesón El Acebo and immediately showered and went to sleep.

Not wanting to pay for the pilgrim dinner, I dove into time alone and journaling. It felt so good to have the time to be calm and reflect in this little mountain town.

A bag of leftover pasta, tuna, and sweet pepper was the concoction of the night, followed by flan from the albergue bar.

I knew today would be a transformative experience.

It will just take time to unravel it fully.
