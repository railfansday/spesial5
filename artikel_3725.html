Lindsay Clancy Makes Another Bid to Drop Murder Charges Before Retrial

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

UPDATE — 11/19/2026, 6:17 p.m. ET: Massachusetts’ highest court will hear Lindsay Clancy’s petition to dismiss the murder charges against her before her upcoming retrial in March, according to reports.

The state’s Supreme Judicial Court announced on Thursday, November 19, that it will listen to oral arguments after Clancy’s defense team filed a petition calling on the court to intervene on Monday, November 16, according to the New York Times. The court is set to hear arguments in January.

Lindsay Clancy’s defense team has called on Massachusetts’ highest court to intervene ahead of her upcoming retrial in her latest bid to have her murder charges dropped.

In a petition filed Monday, November 16, with the state’s Supreme Judicial Court, Clancy’s lawyers asked to reverse Plymouth County Superior Court Judge William Sullivan’s recent denial of Clancy’s motion to dismiss the charges, per Boston.com. Sullivan’s decision cleared the way for a retrial.

The day of the petition’s filing, Sullivan scheduled Clancy’s second murder trial to take place on March 29, 2027.

Clancy, of Duxbury, sought to have the charges she’s facing in the killings of her three children dismissed on double jeopardy grounds after Sullivan declared a mistrial in her case on September 4.

Clancy’s attorneys argue in their petition that she was “aggrieved” by Sullivan turning down her motion to dismiss on November 9.

“Respectfully, contrary to the trial court’s stated belief at the time, it did have viable choices other than declaring a mistrial,” her attorneys wrote in the filing.

They contend she “should not be ‘forced to run the gantlet’ again” after a deadlocked 11-1 jury couldn’t unanimously agree on whether she was criminally liable for the January 2023 deaths of her children, Cora, 5, Dawson, 3, and Callan, 8 months.

Clancy’s defense team argues Sullivan should’ve investigated the lone holdout juror further after the jury foreperson alleged the man, Michael P. Desronvil, didn’t follow instructions relating to reasonable doubt.

“There is a substantial possibility that the trial court, after inquiry, would have discharged the non-compliant juror,” her attorneys wrote in the petition. “Such possibility, therefore, constitutes a viable alternative to the mistrial, precluding a finding of manifest necessity.”

One justice is reviewing the petition, which requests for oral arguments to be held and for a referral to the entire court, Boston.com reported.

Clancy hasn’t denied killing her children. Her defense team, however, has maintained she shouldn’t be held criminally responsible because she was experiencing postpartum psychosis ahead of their deaths. Prosecutors have alleged she intended to kill her children.

After prosecutors shared on Monday that they planned to retry Clancy, Clancy’s attorney Kevin Reddington told the Daily Mail that his client “knows that at least this case has been and will be able to put a light on the treatment of women with mental health issues.”

Reddington also addressed the possibility of Clancy taking the stand during her retrial.

“That’s one of those issues that you have to wait to find out,” he told the news outlet.
