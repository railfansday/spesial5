Do this: ask an advertising provider for a sample campaign report before you ask for a proposal.

Can you see which audiences converted? Does conversion mean a registration, a wallet connection, or a completed transaction? Can you detect where the ads appeared and spending on placements that aren’t generating results?

These questions will tell you much more than a professionally made slide showing millions of impressions. After all, you are choosing a company to spend your budget, and you need to understand what it intends to buy, how it measures it, and what it changes.

Here is what you should be checking before you commit.

When a provider says it reaches “crypto users,” that’s not enough. Ask what qualifies someone for this label. Is it someone who once read a Bitcoin article, or an active trader who is comparing exchanges right now? Each of these suggests a very different audience.

Here is what a good answer sounds like. Ask Blockchain-Ads, the platform Coinbase, Binance and OKX buy on, and you get a segment with a definition and a recency window: active crypto traders, people who recently compared exchanges or wallets, or your own depositors as a retargeting group. That precision is what a demand-side platform is for, and Blockchain-Ads was the first one built for crypto, iGaming and finance. Hold every network on your shortlist to the same standard.

For example, if you are promoting a lending protocol, your question should be whether you can reach a segment of people interested in DeFi lending, and how recently they showed that interest. Then you should request an audience estimate for your target countries.

A publisher list will tell you where your ads might appear. It won’t necessarily tell you who will see them.

Let’s say you sell a wallet for active DeFi users. A crypto news placement could reach experienced users alongside casual readers. Audience targeting could reach relevant users while they browse other subjects.

Now here is the trick. Neither approach automatically wins. Your task here is to ask the provider what they support and whether you can apply contextual restrictions when you need them.

The most important test for an advertising network that serves crypto exchanges is whether it can connect the advertising spend to funded accounts and trading activity.

For a wallet, the event could be a completed swap.

For a blockchain game, it could be a player returning after a first session.

Give the provider your event definition and ask the following:

The best crypto advertising network for your project needs to measure the actual outcome.

Tip: A public transaction doesn’t automatically reveal which ad acquired the user. Blockchain-Ads’ attribution guide explains that campaign identifiers must be linked to account or wallet activity.

Start with the conversion definition. Then, examine the headline number.

Ask for evidence that matches your situation: similar product, target market, budget, and conversion event. Then check the following:

Minimum deposits, daily budgets, and service eligibility are separate commitments when you pick a crypto advertising network.

Blockchain-Ads, for example, publishes its minimum deposit and daily budget, and offers a self-serve platform or a managed option run by industry experts.

Ask any provider for the same clarity. You need to be able to confirm management fees, creative costs, top-up requirements, and the treatment of unused funds.

Finally, ask what the proposed budget can reasonably test. You need a clear explanation of how the funds will be split across countries, formats, and audiences.

A total CPA can hide expensive placements behind cheaper ones.

Ask for a demonstration of reporting by audience, creative, country, device, and placement. The idea here is to confirm which breakdowns are available together and which require support or an export request.

Try a very practical question during the demo:

“If mobile conversions drop next week, what can I do to investigate this?”

Ideally, you should be able to examine whether the delivery shifted toward a different audience or country.

Tip: Ask how quickly the reports update, and check whether existing customers can be excluded from acquisition campaigns. Otherwise, a campaign may receive credit for people you already acquired. Remember: the best crypto ads in a presentation are less useful than reporting that helps you decide what deserves your money.

Fraud protection needs much more explanation than a badge on a well-designed website.

Ask what gets filtered before buying, what gets checked afterward, and how you can report suspicious activity. Find out whether you can pause delivery during an investigation and what evidence the provider needs.

Blockchain-Ads, for instance, documents independent impression verification, traffic monitoring, allow and block lists, and frequency controls. It also asks advertisers reporting problems to supply the campaign ID and period.

Finally, ask who will review performance and what would trigger a change.

Marko Rendevski, SVP Advertising Services & Partnerships at Blockchain-Ads, describes the principle clearly: “We don’t make decisions based only on CTR or a small number of clicks.”

Have the provider explain how they will assess your campaign against your target conversion cost and normal conversion delay. Someone who deposits several days after registration needs time to appear in the results.

If you are wondering how to choose a crypto ad network, finish it all with this one question:

“What will we know after this first test?”

Choosing a crypto advertising network is not simple. Before you commit, ask the provider to show you how it would build your audience, track your conversions, and report where your money went. Get the costs and the test plan in writing. You should leave that conversation only once you know what the first campaign will test and how you can judge the results.
