Louisiana blocks prediction markets and threatens $370M in revenue from exchanges

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Louisiana’s crackdown on prediction markets targets Robinhood and Coinbase intermediaries as the CFTC challenges state enforcement.

Louisiana's Nov. 12 order related to prediction markets reaches the brokers and clearing firms behind the apps, as well as the exchanges themselves.

Its scope, documented in the CFTC's federal complaint, exposes a shared legal risk for platforms earning income from different kinds of trading.

Robinhood and Coinbase each report prediction-market revenue alongside their crypto businesses. Louisiana's action reaches several layers of the trading chain, including the intermediary serving the customer.

According to the CFTC's complaint, Louisiana obtained an ex parte temporary restraining order on Nov. 12 against entities including registered exchanges, futures commission merchants and derivatives clearing organizations. Ex parte means the initial order was obtained without the other side being heard.

The complaint quotes restrictions on offering, clearing, settling, brokering and otherwise facilitating specified event contracts involving people located in Louisiana without the required state license and permit.

Its description includes sports, elections, and award events within the order's stated statutory scope.

The court-filed copy of the order names Robinhood Derivatives and Coinbase Financial Markets, along with multiple Kalshi entities, ForecastEx and Rothera.

Robinhood's product disclosure identifies Robinhood Derivatives as the intermediary and lists KalshiEX, ForecastEx, Rothera, and Crypto.com Derivatives North America as venues. Coinbase says it offers its contracts through Coinbase Financial Markets and has Kalshi operate them, which determines outcomes.

Robinhood already has several exchange routes. If the same restricted broker remains involved, moving a contract to another venue leaves the intermediary within the order's documented reach. That conditional legal-scope analysis leaves each company's implementation unconfirmed.

Robinhood reported $173 million in event-contract revenue for the quarter ended Sept. 30, more than six times the year-earlier figure. Cryptocurrency revenue was $120 million, down 55%.

Coinbase's Oct. 29 announcement said prediction markets had surpassed $200 million in annualized revenue.

The US and CFTC sued Louisiana and state officials on Nov. 13, arguing that federal derivatives law preempts the state's enforcement. They seek declaratory and injunctive relief, including a bar on enforcing or extending the state order.

The government's motion for a temporary restraining order and preliminary injunction is listed for submission Nov. 17. In Louisiana's enforcement case, which moved to federal court, Robinhood seeks to vacate the state order, and Rothera seeks its dissolution or modification.

In a separate federal case, Kalshi's Nov. 13 oral request concerned withdrawal of its own temporary-restraint application, with its preliminary-injunction hearing scheduled for Nov. 23.

Louisiana-specific treatment of new orders, existing positions, settlement and withdrawals remains unconfirmed.

Prediction markets can add income beyond crypto trading while leaving competing platforms exposed to the same state restrictions. A different exchange partner alone offers no demonstrated protection where the order also reaches the broker.
