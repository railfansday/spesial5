Conte set the ‘Pirlo Law’ at Juventus and made strong Buffon statement

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Antonio Conte reveals how he used Andrea Pirlo and Gianluigi Buffon to send a message at Juventus about the importance of discipline. ‘I even called it the Pirlo Law at every other club.’

The coach sat down with Cronache di Spogliatoio podcast for a lengthy interview, where he looked back over his career in management, which really swung into action at Juventus.

He won the Scudetto in his first two seasons, at a club that had been struggling to live up to those previous heights and went on to win nine Serie A titles in a row.

“Milan let Pirlo leave as a free agent and that was so fortunate for us,” said Conte.

“Allegri had won the title and wanted Mark van Bommel, so Andrea joined us with this incredible motivation. He finished every training session fatigued, but he did it, and seeing him work like that was fundamental for me and my method.”

However, there was a moment when Pirlo set a precedent that Conte would carry with him throughout the rest of his career.

“I decided to substitute him one day, and he went directly into the locker room. The media asked me about it in the press conference, because obviously they couldn’t wait.

“I said that from now on we were imposing the Pirlo Law. From that day on, any player substituted had to go and sit on the bench, unless he was in an ambulance on his way to hospital.

“It was a way of facing up to the problem and not hiding away. This was an extraordinary champion, but I couldn’t treat him any differently.”

The same process was also used at Juventus by Conte when it came to another legend, Italy hero Buffon, and hit home just how seriously the coach took every single step in the summer of 2011.

“I had just joined Juventus and we were in pre-season training, organising two friendly games against a local team. We won the first 18-1, then the second 20-1.

“Buffon played the first half, then Marco Storari came on. Before releasing the squad for two days of free time, I asked the team manager to get everyone there for me to give them a talk, but Buffon had already left and was on his way to Turin.

“I got them to call him, he apologised because he didn’t know this would happen, and turned the car around to come back. I told the players off because even if we won 20-1, we conceded early for two games in a row, and we cannot allow that attitude.

“The squad had to understand that Buffon needed to respect the rules too, it wouldn’t have been as effective if he hadn’t been there too.”
