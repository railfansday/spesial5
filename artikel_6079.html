American Airlines Is Turning Leather From Its Retired MD-80 Seats Into 200,000-Mile Jackets

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Included in this year's Cyber Week drop for AAdvantage® members is the Alpha Industries MA-1 Flight Jacket. This garment is made from reclaimed leather from the airline's most iconic aircraft, celebrating its 100th anniversary of flight. One of the jets included is American Airlines' McDonnell Douglas MD-80s.

The MA-1 Flight Jacket is available exclusively for purchase with AAdvantage® miles on the AAdvantage Exchange℠ marketplace. The jacket is available from November 30 to December 4 and costs 200,000 miles each.

American Airlines provided the clothing brand MA-1 with 650 used airline seats from across its historic fleet. The brand, famous in music, streetwear, and fashion, decided to strip the seats of their leather and use them to create a patchwork material for the jacket's exterior. The effect is a traditional bomber jacket with a unique texture and weight. The garment includes a ribbon stating "remove before flight" on the left sleeve.

In a promotional video for American Airlines, a representative from Alpha Industries said:

"This was really the most complicated product we've ever made. Each jacket required 10 to 12 seats... [For] each jacket, there's hours of labor. Understanding the logistics process behind this was crazy."

This Cyber Week, Black Friday, and Travel Tuesday, American Airlines has put together several exclusive offers for AAdvantage members, including the ability to purchase a bomber jacket built from reclaimed aircraft seats. For example, you can purchase 500,000 or more miles at a 50% discount.

The Instagram post highlights exciting AAdvantage developments launched by American Airlines in recent months, including the ability to combine miles and dollars to access rewards. However, it doesn't seem that AAdvantage members will be able to do this when purchasing the limited-edition bomber if they don't have the required 200,000 miles. The ability to purchase more miles at a discounted rate could be the right pathway to jacket ownership for some members.

American Airlines has also made a range of gifts available for travelers and aviation enthusiasts. This includes heritage-inspired suitcases designed in collaboration with July and limited-edition American Airlines x Volair pickleball paddles. It goes to show that miles can mean merchandise, not just an upgrade or a ticket.

American Airlines has really improved its premium economy product on the 787-9P, but is it actually too much of an improvement?

The jacket celebrates American Airlines heritage by reusing leather from 650 seats from the airline's retired fleet. The most iconic aircraft involved is the MD-80, which the airline stopped flying in 2019. This jet can carry 150 passengers up to 2,550 nautical miles (4,720 km). The carrier originally leased 20 of these jets in 1982, using them to replace its Boeing 727-100 fleet. The MD-80 was also American Airlines' first jet that could fly with just two pilots, rendering the flight engineer role obsolete.

Air Fleets data shows that American Airlines had a total of 384 MD-80s in its fleet at different times. The airline used the jet as a cross-country regional workhorse. It facilitated this type of traffic outside its main networks.

Age was the main reason that American Airlines retired its MD-80s. The series was approaching 40 years old, and some of American Airlines' active MD-80s were approaching 20 years old. As with any airliner, the aging jet was no longer fuel-efficient, reliable, or suited to its role, with modern alternatives like the Airbus A320 series and the Boeing 737 MAX. Retiring the aircraft also allowed American Airlines to achieve a greater degree of fleet commonality, reducing pilot training, maintenance, and certification costs.
