Coach Eugene Bareman shuts down any chance of Carlos Ulberg vs. Navajo Stirling UFC title fight

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Don't get excited about a potential Carlos Ulberg vs. Navajo Stirling UFC light heavyweight title fight, because it'll never happen.

That's according to City Kickboxing (CKB) head coach Eugene Bareman, who told the NZ Herald at a press conference Wednesday that a fight between the two CKB teammates won't be on the table at any point in the future, even after UFC champion Ulberg fully recovers from the torn ACL he suffered in his April title victory over Jiri Prochazka.

Said Bareman, speaking while flanked by both UFC light heavyweights:

"The status at the moment is, and it's been like this forever, is the boys from the same gym don't fight.

"We've had that agreement from the very onset of this gym when it first started, that if you join this team, you've got to commit to not fighting each other.

"Both the boys have known that. Both the boys are in agreement with that. They've been in agreement with the start. They've known, they've known that from the very start."

None of this would be a topic of discussion had Stirling, 29, not launched himself into title contention this past weekend, spectacularly knocking out former champ Prochazka at UFC Qatar.

Ulberg, 36, the reigning 205-pound champion, has been absent since winning the then-vacant belt on one leg this past spring, coincidentally with a knockout of Prochazka. Ulberg tore his ACL in the fight and has been sidelined in recovery ever since.

With each fighter competing in the UFC's 205-pound division, it was a race to see who could reach championship status first. So, Bareman said, as long as Ulberg and Stirling are under the same training roof, there will be no change to the philosophy in place.

"It was never even contemplated. These boys have been through thick and thin together.

"There is no Carlos Ulberg without a Navajo Stirling. There is no Navajo Stirling without a Carlos Ulberg. Carlos Ulberg doesn't win his title without Navajo Stirling, years after years after years coming in and getting in this gym and just trading blood, sweat, and tears with him.

"Likewise, Navajo doesn't get his big win against Jiri, for instance, without Carlos Ulberg coming in here and trading blood, sweat, and tears for years and years and years. You know, that's just history that just means so much."

Stirling rose to contention much faster than many expected after his UFC debut in December 2024. The undefeated Kiwi has already won seven straight fights in the promotion, finishing his last four opponents — the last two being former champions.

Ulberg, likewise, is enjoying a long stretch of success that culminated with his April title win. He lost his UFC debut in March 2021 — the only loss of his 15-fight career — and hasn't looked back since, winning 10 in a row and stopping seven of those opponents.

Ultimately, Bareman said, what separates CKB from past instances in MMA of teammates battling it out in a title fight is the unity of their small nation. Bareman looked at past cases with notably large U.S. gyms, like American Top Team, where it's almost commonplace at times.

"We're a tiny nation and a tiny gym, and our environment's very special. Like, it's very tight-knit. For instance, I might be speaking out of turn here, but you've got like Gilbert Burns and Kamaru Usman who came from the same team and fought each other.

"Now, and again, I could be speaking out of turn, but I guess the difference is like a lot of those American gyms, guys come in, and they do their eight weeks, and then they go back to their home country. And then they come in and do their eight weeks. So you might see a guy two times a year for eight weeks.

"You're not going to form a bond with him. You're not going to get to know his family. You're not going to get to know everything about him. There's going to be any chance for a friendship to be formed.

"We stay here all year, and we have to because we're already up against it because we're a very small team at the bottom of the earth. The only way we can do this is we train all year with each other and make each other better. And when you're in that environment, and you're working so hard to do the impossible and get to the top of the world, you form a very, very, very special bond."

Stirling's win over Prochazka marked his fourth fight of 2026.

Ulberg isn't expected back in action until sometime around or after April 2027.
