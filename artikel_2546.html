Players Era loss triggers schedule change for Michigan basketball

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

LAS VEGAS — Michigan basketball's lopsided loss to Creighton in the Players Era 16 has triggered a change to its nonconference schedule.

With Michigan dropping to the consolation bracket of the 16-team tournament and no longer in the running to reach the championship game, it will host Mississippi Valley State at Crisler Center on Tuesday, Dec. 1.

It's a contingency plan that was put in place by the Wolverines to ensure they play the maximum number of regular-season games. With the Players Era switching to a bracket format this year, all participating teams were guaranteed to play three games. Only the two teams who advanced out of their side of the bracket and qualified for the title game on Saturday, Nov. 28, would play a fourth contest.

"It's part of the way the event had to be structured this year, because there was the fourth game option there," coach Mike Boynton Jr. said last week. "There will be 14 teams that will have a known opponent sometime the following week. I think everybody tried to find somebody that at least in short prep you could try to be confident that you could get your team ready to go against. At least that was our philosophy and that's where we landed."

Michigan, which won last season's Players Era tournament in dominant fashion, will play No. 17 Miami (Fla.) in a consolation game on Thursday, Nov. 26, before playing a third and final game on Friday, Nov. 27.

Instead of competing for another title and more name, image and likeness opportunities against a Power Five program in Sin City, Michigan will pay $95,000 for the game against Mississippi Valley State, according to a signed game contract obtained by The Detroit News through a Freedom of Information Act request.

Mississippi Valley State is off to a 0-5 start, went 3-30 a year ago and hasn't had a winning campaign since 2011-12, which is also the last time it won more than nine games in a season. So why would Michigan even play such an opponent? According to Boynton, it centered around former coach Dusty May's relationship with Mississippi Valley State coach Mike Davis — one that dates to when the two worked together at Indiana three decades ago — and was a scheduling decision made before Boynton succeeded May, who left for the NBA.

This season, the Delta Devils are opening their campaign with 16 consecutive road games, with the majority of those being buy games that will fund the university's athletic department. The visit to Ann Arbor is the seventh stop on Mississippi Valley State's nationwide tour, following trips to Oklahoma, Washington, Oregon, Oregon State, Arkansas and New Mexico.

After the Dec. 1 game, Mississippi Valley State has North Texas, UConn, Mississippi State, Tulane, Ohio State, Texas Tech and Baylor remaining on its nonconference schedule before it opens Southwestern Athletic Conference play with back-to-back road games.

It makes for quite the gauntlet for Davis, who's in his first season and is back in the big seat after leading Detroit Mercy from 2018-24.

For the Wolverines, scheduling Mississippi Valley State as a fallback option guarantees they'll play 32 regular-season games, a total that increased this season. For the past 20 seasons, teams were capped at 31 regular-season games.
