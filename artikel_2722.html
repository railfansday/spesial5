This past Saturday saw seven ranked-on-ranked showdowns, four of which involved SEC teams. Alabama, Georgia, and, Tennessee all made big statements. Notre Dame and Miami-FL muddled things up at top. It was a wild weekend.

Alabama 31 LSU 13 – The Bayou Bengals packed their stadium with the famous and psuedo-famous but forgot to game plan for the Crimson Tide. LSU got big chunks of yardage at times and even outgained Bama 457 to 285 and had twice as many first downs. However, they faltered on all four 4th down conversion attempts and crashed and burned in the red zone, scoring only one touchdown and one field goal on four trips inside the Alabama 20.

Alabama also won the turnover battle 2 to 0. The Crimson Tide proved to be a force to be reckoned with when even close to being fully healthy.

Notre Dame 31 Miami 24 – The Irish had a 24-3 advantage in the 3rd quarter when the Hurricanes mounted a comeback. They would draw to within seven points in the final period. On a controversial 4th down play at the Notre Dame 18, Darian Mensah threw a pass into the end zone for an apparent touchdown (above).

So-called experts have cited this play as clearly a catch and while others have said clearly not a catch. IMHO, this is a reception. He caught the ball, had control, two steps in bounds (even though you only need one), stepped out, and then the ball got knocked away. The way I see it, this play is over once he steps out of bounds. It makes zero sense that a play can continue after that moment. This is utterly ridiculous and needs to be addressed in the off-season.

Utah 28 BYU 20 – Utah raced out to a 14 point advantage on their first two possessions and this one looked like it would a blowout of epic proportions. But the Coogs did not panic and chipped away at the lead and took over 20-14 at the half. Midway through the third quarter, the the Brighams had a 3-and-out followed by a poor punt that left the Utes in good field position. UU would convert that advantage into a touchdown and a one-point lead. After a second consecutive 3-and-out, Utah scored another touchdown. BYU quarterback Bear Bachmeier threw interceptions on the next two series – including one from the 2-yard line – and that was all she wrote.

This was an ugly game. Bachmeier threw four picks. Utah lost two fumbles – including one by media darling du jour Devon Dampier – who also had a turnover on downs.

Texas 34 Missouri 20 – Controversy in this one too. With Mizzou leading 20-13 in the 3rd quarter, Texas QB Arch Manning gets hit as he releases a pass. To any sane person, it was an incompletion. It is seemingly impossible that a replay official could screw this one up. First off, this was an incomplete pass. Then, they are trying to say that there was an immediate recovery. Several seconds passed before the ball was picked up almost simultaneously by a Longhorn and a Tiger who wrestled over it out of bounds. In addition, a Mizzou player was down writhing on the field in pain, the clock stopped at 2:22 before the alleged recovery, and a side judge can be seen signalling incomplete. It is hard to believe that a whistle had not been blown. Despite all these indicators, the replay officials judged this play to be a forward fumble.

Had this been ruled incomplete, it would have meant a Texas 4th & 10 at the 29, which would mean a crazy long field goal, a pooch punt, or having to go for it. Mind you it was still the 3rd quarter. The College Football Playoff and ESPN have agreed on a five-year deal that will expand the field to 24 teams in 2027. Some details:

Power 4 conference championship games will be eliminated. R.I.P.

Four auto bids will be given to P4 champs. The Participation Trophy slot will still be implemented for the one highest-ranked G6, though that does not stop a second G6 from getting an at-large bid.

19 at-large bids will be based on CFP rankings.

The top 8 ranked teams receive a first-round bye.

First and second-round games will take place on the campus of the higher seed. This will absolutely cause the death of several bowl games, possibly including ones in Birmingham, Mobile, and Montgomery.

There will be an open week after the regular season and before the CFP first round (first weekend in December).

No CFP games will conflict with Army-Navy, which many expect to move up to the open week. Heisman Trophy ceremony is moving also to the open week.
