Game Day: Louisville at North Carolina

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Location: Kenan Stadium: Chapel Hill, N.C.

Announcers: Mike Couzens (play-by-play), Tom Luginbill (analyst), and Lauren Sisler (sidelines)

Last Meeting: Louisville won 47-35 on Sept. 9, 2017 in Chapel Hill

Bill Belichick had a poor start to his tenure at UNC last year, with the main issues being on the offensive side of the ball. He remedied that issue this year by going out and hiring none other than Bobby Petrino to be his offensive coordinator. Petrino had been bouncing around at a few spots since being fired by Louisville, but his offensive acumen is still coveted. The Tar Heels have improved on offense, though they still rank toward the bottom of the rankings in some key offensive metrics.

As always, an offense is only as good as its quarterback, and the Tar Heels are average with Billy Edwards under center. The veteran transfer quarterback has played about as well as most expected him to this season. His inconsistent play has been an issue for this offense, and it has contributed to a couple of the losses this year.

As the season has gone on, he has played better, with his last two games being his best. He torched Miami's defense for four touchdowns while completing 82% of his passes. He came back the following weekend to complete 76% of his passes against a solid UConn team. Edwards isn't one of the top quarterbacks Louisville has seen this season, but he has played like one recently.

UNC has three running backs that they can rely on, and they play all three of them. Demon June leads the group in touchdowns with 8 on the season. Benjamin Hall leads the group in yards and yards per carry. Jaylin Mcgill leads the group in yards per play as he is a factor as a receiver out of the backfield. They are all over 220 pounds, and Petrino has done a good job of utilizing them this season. The staff likes to ride the hot hand with this group, so we will have to wait to see which player gets the most touches.

Louisville will face another opponent that has a single key player at the wide receiver position this weekend. Jordan Shipp is one of the more talented players the Cards will face this year, and they will need to have a plan. Shipp has good size at 6-2/200, and he can really move. He is a fluid athlete with good body control which he uses to make contested catches and catches in tight spots. He is not a burner in the open field, but he has plenty of speed to score on a long play if he gets open.

The biggest threat on this offense is at tight end in Jelani Thurman. Lincoln Keinholz' former teammate at Ohio State has had a similar situation as he did not make much of an impact for the Buckeyes, but he has emerged as one of the best tight ends in the country for UNC. He has already set the school record for touchdowns by a tight end with 8, which is the most in the country among tight ends. He also ranks in the top five in just about every receiving category when it comes to tight ends.

This team doesn't create a lot of big passing plays, but when they do it is down the middle of the field to Thurman, or because he pulls defenders with him. Petrino is known for working deep over routes throughout his career, but this team has killed teams down the seams. Louisville's defense has been victimized many times on seam routes this year, so they will need to adjust their plan from their normal reliance on Cover 3.

The UNC offensive line has been pretty average this year, especially from a run blocking standpoint. They don't allow a lot of penetration, but they haven't been opening massive holes for the running backs, either. Where they have struggled is being consistent in pass protection. Billy Edwards is just a big better than being a statue in the pocket, so he is not one to avoid the rush if it gets to him. It has not helped that his line has struggled with speed rushers this year. The Cards should have an opportunity to get pressure this weekend.

Defense is Bill Belichick's forte, and the North Carolina Tar Heels are benefiting from his expertise this year. For the second straight year, Belichick has his defense ranked in the top 50 in yards per play. His group is being led by his son Brian after his son Steve left the team earlier this season. While the defense has taken a step back this year, they have played well enough to keep the Tar Heels in each game, and they've made big plays at big moments.

The most talented group on this team is the defensive line, with a couple of NFL prospects. Melkart Abou Jaoude is the returning ACC leader in sacks, while Jaylen Harvey has been a very good addition from Penn State with 21 pressures this season. The edges of this defense haven't put up crazy numbers from a havoc standpoint, but they will be key players for this defense if Louisville continues to attack the edges of the defense.

The interior of the line is where this defense is at its best. Xavier Lewis is one of the best defensive tackles in the country. He is in the top five in pressures, tackles for loss, and sacks among his position group. The 6-0/260 tackle is extremely undersized, but he is very quick, and he uses his quickness to beat blocks to get into the backfield. He will be a key player in this game and will likely cause some issues for Louisville's offensive line.

Isaiah Johnson and Leroy Jackson are the other top players on the interior of the defense. Jackson has 14 pressures on the season as the main backup for Lewis, but he and Johnson provide really good size on the inside as well. Johnson is big enough to demand a double team, which has been a factor in Lewis' big season. He has been able to get one-on-one matchups often, and that has not gone well for offensive linemen.

UNC's linebackers have benefited more than just about anyone from the strong play of the defensive line. Derek McDonald and Peyton Sheelmann are averaging 19 tackles a game between the two of them. Both players are ranked in the top 25 in tackles nationally, and they are constantly running free when you watch their games. Louisville has adapted their running game over the last few weeks, but they will likely have a hard time running the stretch zone runs that they really like. These two guys can run, and they will have a lot of space to get downhill to make tackles behind the line.

Jaiden Patterson leads a solid cornerback group that has played at an average level this season. UNC has given up a lot of yardage this season, but they are better when it comes to metrics like passer rating. The corners have had some issues against the top players they've seen this year. Umari Hatcher, Jordon Faison, Calvin Russell, and Bryant Wesco all stood out to me when I watched their games. Those receivers were able to get open and make big plays on the outside.

The biggest playmaker outside of the defensive linemen is Greg Smith at free safety. He leads the team with 3 interceptions this year, and he's at his best as a deep safety playing center field. Smith can also come down into the box in run support or cover if needed. He's having one of the best seasons in the ACC at safety. While this secondary isn't great, they do have a couple of players who can make a play on the ball, which is not something I would say about the last few opponents for the Cards.

Give me an SMU win over Wake at noon and a Cards win in Chapel Hill, and the EL is probably back up to where we want it to be for next week's home/conference finale against Pitt.
