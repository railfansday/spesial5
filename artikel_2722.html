Verstappen provides fresh update on F1 future amid talks of V8 return

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Max Verstappen has welcomed the prospect of V8 engines returning to Formula 1, although the Red Bull driver admitted that his ideal regulations would look rather different. The four-time world champion also refused to commit to remaining in the sport beyond 2030.

FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem recently announced that V8 engines are set to return to Formula 1, potentially as early as 2030. The announcement initially appeared on the FIA's social media channels before being deleted around 30 minutes later. However, Ben Sulayem subsequently reiterated his intentions on his personal account, writing: "The people spoke. We listened. V8s. Coming soon."

While the exact timing and technical details remain uncertain, the development represents a significant step towards fulfilling a long-standing wish of Verstappen, who has repeatedly expressed his preference for more traditional Formula 1 engines.

Speaking to GPblog and other media ahead of the Las Vegas Grand Prix, Verstappen was asked whether the potential return of V8 engines could encourage him to extend his Formula 1 career.

"For sure, it's better, but I cannot tell you now what I will do after 2030. But for the sport itself, it's a good choice," the Dutchman explained.

Asked whether the proposed V8 regulations would be exactly what he wanted if he could design the rules himself, Verstappen made it clear that he would prefer an even more radical approach.

"No. But that's also because probably on some tracks, then you're not allowed to race because it will be too loud. Because if it's up to me, of course, I would take the turbo off. Maybe you can use it as a push-to-pass, but not too much of the battery side."

The Red Bull driver acknowledged, however, that removing certain elements of the current power units would create additional challenges, particularly regarding fuel consumption and the practical requirements of racing.

"On some tracks, you're not allowed to race. So fuel consumption, of course, goes up a lot without the turbo, so you would have needed a bigger tank if you don't want to redo refuelling in the races. So I, of course, then understand that you want to have a turbo."

Verstappen went even further when discussing what his ideal Formula 1 engine would sound like, admitting that he would favour a return to V10 power units if there were no practical restrictions: "If it's fully up to me, of course, you go back to a V10 or whatever. That sounds even better, but I know that that's not something realistic."

Despite those preferences, Verstappen remains encouraged by the possibility of V8 engines returning to the championship: "This is good. This is for sure in the right direction. But again, it's not confirmed yet, so there's still a few things, I guess, that they need to agree on."

The Dutchman was also asked whether the discussions surrounding V8 engines, alongside efforts to make Formula 1 cars lighter, suggested that the sport's governing bodies were paying greater attention to feedback from drivers.

Verstappen believes their opinions may have contributed to the discussions, although he suggested that the authorities had also recognised shortcomings in the existing regulations.

"It's probably a combination of maybe listening to our advice, but also realising what we have is not what they want. So it's probably a bit of a combination."
