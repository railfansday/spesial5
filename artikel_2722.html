Why didn't the Colts trade Jonathan Taylor? RB spared in Indy's fire sale

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Indianapolis Colts were the NFL's biggest sellers ahead of the 2026 NFL trade deadline. They offloaded numerous veteran players, including wide receivers Keenan Allen and Josh Downs, defensive lineman DeForest Buckner and long-time, stalwart left guard Quenton Nelson.

But even despite their activity level, the Colts didn't trade their biggest chip. Running back Jonathan Taylor remained in Indianapolis even after being the subject of persistent trade rumors leading up to the deadline.

Taylor has long been a star running back since being selected in the second round of the 2020 NFL Draft. Still, many thought the Colts might cash in and unload the 27-year-old in a blockbuster deal to continue to add to their coffers of draft assets ahead of what figures to be a challenging rebuild.

So, why did the Colts decide to keep Taylor? Here's what to know about their decision as they move forward with the star running back in tow.

The Colts didn't trade Taylor because they view the 27-year-old running back as a part of their future.

Colts coach Shane Steichen spoke glowingly of Taylor when asked what it mean to keep him in the fold during a media availability the day following the 2026 NFL trade deadline.

"He means a ton. He's a hell of a football player. I'm excited that he's here," Steichen told reporters Nov. 11. "It's awesome for the organization to keep, you know one of the best running backs in the league in your franchise moving forward."

Taylor has certainly thrived across his seven seasons to date with the Colts. He has twice led the NFL in total touchdowns during his career, earned an All-Pro first-team nod in 2021, and is pacing for his fourth season with at least 1,500 scrimmage yards in 2026.

Taylor is also under contract through the 2028 NFL season, so the Colts won't have to worry about locking him down to another deal while he remains in the prime of his career.

That may have been part of the reason the Colts were only willing to consider moving Taylor in a "blockbuster offer," per The Ringer's Tom Pelissero. The Colts received packages including a second-round pick and more for Taylor, but he remains under team control and can continue to serve as Indianapolis' offensive engine as the team rebuilds.

It's also worth noting that Steichen expressed no worries about the relationship between Taylor and the franchise even after the persistent trade rumors surrounding the star running back.

"I can't speak for him, but I know that last week, he said he's proud to wear the horseshoe," Steichen said. "And so like I said, I'm excited that he's here. He's a great freaking football player."

And Taylor reiterated in a media availability of his own that he was happy to remain in Indianapolis.

"No," Taylor replied when asked whether he wanted to be traded before the deadline. "Just regular scheduled programing Tuesday. Like it's all water under the bridge now."

While Taylor remained with the Colts, the 27-year-old may not handle his usual workload down the stretch of the 2026 NFL season.

The Colts are sitting at 3-7 entering Week 11 and are highly unlikely to make any sort of playoff push during the second half of the season. As a result, the Colts have seemingly started to limit Taylor's workload, as he has averaged 17.1 touches per game since Week 7 while playing no more than 69% of the offensive snaps in any game.

By comparison, Taylor averaged 22.8 touches per game and never played fewer than 71% of the team's offensive snaps across the first six games of the season.

Taylor's slight downtick in production has coincided with an uptick in production from rookie backup Seth McGowan. The seventh-rounder averaged just four touches per game across the first six games of the season but has seen that number balloon to nine per game in his last four outings.

Against the Dolphins, Taylor saw 18 touches while McGowan had 12 and scored a touchdown. That 60-40 workload split could end up being closer to the norm moving forward as the Colts look to keep Taylor fresh and healthy during what looks like a lost season.
