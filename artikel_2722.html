Attrition finally gets best of Lakers in loss to Jazz

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

LOS ANGELES — The Lakers' league-leading nine-game win streak came to an end in Wednesday's 109-97 loss to the Utah Jazz.

Following a stretch where they seemed unaffected by streaky opponents or a grueling schedule, things finally caught up to them. Legs were heavy, shots came up short, and the Lakers succumbed to an ongoing war of attrition early this season.

Los Angeles had its worst offensive game of the season, scoring just 97 points — the lowest since putting up 95 points in the loss to Golden State that ignited its streak — on 34-for-89 (38.2%) shooting from the field and 9-for-33 (27.3%) from range.

"Frankly, you're going to play 82 games, you're going to have some poor shooting nights," head coach JJ Redick said postgame. "We'll take a look at everything, how we can be better offensively."

Redick and the Lakers refused to make an excuse for fatigue, but it evidently affected the way they played.

"Who in here feels like they played well tonight," Redick said he asked the team postgame. No one raised their hand, including Luka Dončić, who has been bearing the weight of the offense all season, which becomes even heavier when Austin Reaves is out.

Dončić's 11-game streak of 30-point games came to an end as the league's scoring leader scored just 29 points on 28 shot attempts, making just two of his 12 3-point attempts. He also tacked on nine turnovers.

"[Fatigue] can't be an excuse when all NBA teams play 82 games," Dončić said. "I got to do way better. I know that."

It's the balance the Lakers have to live by when they roster two high-usage guards that are critical to their success. Redick pointed to a stretch Reaves endured last season similar to the one Dončić is on now.

In December on the road against Toronto while Dončić was in Slovenia, Reaves scored 44 points and willed the Lakers to a 123-120 win. The next game, in Boston with both Dončić and LeBron James out, Reaves put up another 36 points in a 126-105 loss. Dončić and James returned to the court the following game in Philadelphia, but by then, Reaves was gassed. He scored just 11 points and made 3 of 16 shot attempts.

"The shot creation that is required from [Dončić], it takes its toll," Redick said. "That's part of it when Austin's out, for sure. … That's the luxury of having two really elite creators, and obviously when they're both in the lineup, it makes things a little bit easier."

At the expense of creating the excuse that Redick and Dončić and the rest of the roster wouldn't give the time of day, Wednesday's game against Utah was the Lakers' third in four nights and came the day after an 8 p.m. tip-off in a game that went to overtime. Even Quentin Grimes, who remains the hottest shooter on the team, needed 20 shots to score 20 points the night after scoring 47 on 24 attempts against the Hornets.

Los Angeles' season has been magical. Their 13-3 record is still tied with the 76ers for the best in the NBA. But, finally, for at least one night, the Lakers couldn't avoid the inevitable.
