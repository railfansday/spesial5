Amanda Seyfried Reveals Her Daughter's Reaction to Mean Girls

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Amanda Seyfried shared her daughter Nina's reaction to watching Mean Girls for the first time during an exclusive E! News interview at the Steps world premiere in NYC.

Amanda Seyfried's fifth sense is knowing when to show her kids her work.

Although the Mean Girls alum and ex Thomas Sadoski's kids Nina, 9, and Thomas Jr., 6, aren't fully versed in her filmography, when it comes to what they have seen, they think she's pretty grool.

"They've seen the few animated movies I've done," Seyfried exclusively told E! News at the world premiere of her new Netflix movie Steps on Nov. 8. "My daughter watched Mean Girls for the first time last year. They love it."

Her kids know that she's not a regular mom, she's a cool mom.

"It's normal for them," the 40-year-old said of her career being in the spotlight. "I'm learning more every day. I think as they get older, they're like, 'Huh, what a very unique place to get to be.' I'm like, 'Yeah, it is unique!'"

And when it comes to expressing gratitude, for Seyfried, the limit does not exist.

Along with pressing play on the Lindsay Lohan-led movie—also starring Rachel McAdams, Tina Fey and Jonathan Bennett—her kids have also taken a chance on some of her other biggest hits.

The Dropout star—who announced her breakup from Sadoski in September after nine years of marriage—added, "They loved Mamma Mia."

Now, Nina and Thomas Jr. are watching the Oscar nominee find her happily ever after in Steps—premiering on Netflix Nov. 20—as Cinderella.

Although the animated movie is a reimagining of the fairy tale—in which the princess' stepsisters Lilith (Ali Wong) and Margot (Stephanie Hsu) are misunderstood, rather than evil, as they team up to save the kingdom from childhood bully Priscilla (Nikki Glaser)—it simply expands on the already beloved story.

"I saw her as somebody who was desperately searching for herself in some way," Seyfried said. "That hasn't changed. But I think we've really gone into this book, this fairy tale, this story that we all know, and they've completely upended it."

"It took the complexity of who we are as people in the world and turned it into this unique depiction of sisterhood and friendship," she continued, "and what it takes to fully be able to communicate and bond."

Seyfried will definitely be adding Steps to her kids' movie night queue. "It's beautiful," she beamed. "This is what I want my kids to be watching."
