Alonso heckled as Chelsea are humiliated by Leeds

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

There are two non-negotiables for Chelsea managers. Do not lose at home to Tottenham Hotspur and do not lose at home to Leeds United. But Xabi Alonso has now completed the nightmare double.

Just as Spurs broke their Stamford Bridge hoodoo a few weeks ago, Leeds secured their first win at Chelsea since 1999 by humiliating the hosts after falling a goal behind.

It was 67 long years since Chelsea had last lost at home to Tottenham and Leeds in the same season, and Alonso was left in no doubt about how seriously supporters take that as his team were loudly booed and he was heckled.

Cole Palmer stormed down the tunnel at full time, ignoring the advice of Chelsea’s so-called “cultural architect” Willie Isa to applaud the remaining home fans, and shrugging off the attention of injured striker João Pedro.

Having been left out of the last England squad, Palmer was largely anonymous for Chelsea but he was not alone and Alonso is already facing similar questions to his predecessors about the team’s mentality.

“I felt that probably we are in a fragile moment, mentally,” Alonso said. “When we receive a punch, we didn’t recover that strong, that tough enough and today that was my feeling.

“The fans were disappointed because the expectations from them are high and we feel sorry for them. For sure, it’s not the easiest moment. It’s a fragile moment, but we need to work together and stick together because there is still a long way to go.”

Alonso is yet to name the same starting XI for successive league games and there appears to be no discernible plan or style of play.

Injuries have contributed to Chelsea’s unremarkable start to the season under Alonso – and Moisés Caicedo was forced off early in the first half – but the problems run deeper than the treatment table.

Alonso’s defence has now conceded 20 goals, which has contributed to Chelsea losing five league games already – more than Manchester United. In between three defeats in their last four league games, Chelsea were also knocked out of the Carabao Cup.

The Spaniard had answered questions about Chelsea’s identity, or lack of it, by saying he wanted his team to be competitive. But they were anything but against a Leeds side who had seven more shots than their hosts and five more on target.

It is hard to fathom how, through all the chopping and changing, Wesley Fofana still starts games for Chelsea in defence. It was his pathetic attempted header, which bounced backwards towards his own goal, that contributed to Noah Okafor’s first goal.

Josh Acheampong also got himself into a mess and Okafor was somehow able to squeeze a shot past goalkeeper Emi Martínez despite having three Chelsea players around him.

Chelsea had taken the lead through striker Emmanuel Emegha, on his first start for the club, but from that moment on they were second best with Leeds pair Ethan Ampadu and Ao Tanaka running the middle of the park.

Dominic Calvert-Lewin had hit the crossbar and failed to find the net with three more presentable opportunities in the first half, but he took his chance from the penalty spot eight minutes after the break.

Morgan Rogers had argued that James Justin’s shot hit him too far up his arm for it to be a penalty, but a VAR check upheld referee Michael Oliver’s decision and Calvert-Lewin kept his cool.

That should have been the moment that shook Chelsea into action and prompted a response but, instead, Alonso’s team fell apart and he was unable to provoke a shift in momentum from the touchline.

Just five minutes after Calvert-Lewin had put Leeds ahead, Okafor effectively sealed the victory by firing the ball into the net after Chelsea had failed to deal with a long throw into their penalty area.

John Terry still works with Chelsea’s academy and it might be worth Alonso getting the former captain to speak to the club’s senior players about how to defend and compete. Whatever has been done on the training pitch so far has not worked.

Alonso’s substitutes are yet to register a single goal or assist in the league this season and his changes failed to make any sort of impact here. For the visitors, however, two substitutes combined to score their fourth goal in time added on for stoppages.

Harry Wilson played in Brenden Aaronson and, from a tight angle, he managed to find the back of the net by sending the ball through the legs of Martínez.

Thomas Frank declared “it’s good to be back in the circus” of football management ahead of his first match as Crystal Palace boss.

The 53-year-old Dane admitted he could have easily spent a few more months sipping wine, “eating too well” and collecting passport stamps, but something resonated when he asked his wife why he was tempted to seek out a new job and she told him, “(It’s) because you can’t help yourself”.

Frank hinted that there was more behind the story to his disappointing spell at Tottenham he might one day reveal, but for now he has a team to rescue from a relegation fight, beginning with today’s trip to Coventry.

If you don’t learn from any of your jobs, I don’t think you reflect, I don’t think you improve, and I don’t think you get better.

Of course there’s a lot of things I have been reflecting over. The details, that will be for another time, but I can say I’ve learned, definitely.

I was very aware I didn’t want to do anything in the summer. I put a statement out there, I needed more time. I can actually, honestly say I would have been fine if it was three, four, five, six, seven months more, because it’s good to be back in the circus, but it’s also unbelievably good not to be in, especially if you enjoy life, which I do.

I was spending time with family and friends and doing all that, but I think the club, the way Palace is, how well Steve Parish especially spoke about the club and ideas, Matt Hobbs and Iain Moody and these guys, too, helped convince me it was a good idea.
