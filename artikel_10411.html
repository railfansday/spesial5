‘Sex sells, fellas’: President Trump defends Jensen Huang, says he pushed for him to be reinstated as Nvidia CEO

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

President Donald Trump says he played a role in pushing Nvidia to reinstate company president and CEO Jensen Huang.

On Monday morning, Nvidia announced that Huang would resume his position just 11 days after the tech mogul’s resignation. “I am honored to return to Nvidia and lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a new industrial revolution,” Huang said in a statement on the latest development. 

The decision comes as multiple investigations—including those from the Justice Department and the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission—are still ongoing. And just last week, Nvidia chairman Harvey ​Jones had said that the company would not rehire Huang, given that his conduct did not meet the company’s code of conduct. Huang had stepped down as CEO after allegedly having an extramarital affair with a suspected Chinese spy, as well as an additional affair with a subordinate.

But after Monday’s stunning decision to reinstate Huang, Trump seemingly took some credit for the move, telling the media that he advocated for the briefly jobless boss to come back into power. 

Trump told CNBC that Huang “did nothing wrong,” and said that he spoke to Nvidia’s board “directly and indirectly” ahead of the move, making clear that he argued on behalf of the CEO.

The president also made clear that he told the company that Huang returning to his role was the best decision in terms of national security, and for winning the AI race with adversaries like China. 

“They did ask for assurance on the national security threat,” Trump told the outlet. He added: “People say, ‘Is it a threat to have him?’ No, it’s a threat to lose him.”

When asked about Huang’s extramarital activities, Trump even went so far as to say the ordeal would help, rather than hurt, the CEO’s success. 

“I think he’s going to start off at an almost higher level,” the president said. “He’s apologized for the indiscretions, which were unfortunate. Probably, that’s what brought it to such a level, because sex sells, fellas.” With this remark, Trump seemed to imply that the nature of the news story being tied to affairs is what brought it so much attention.

The president seemed to double down on the sentiment on Truth Social by posting an image of Huang waving a Photoshopped red bra in the air, referencing a famous photo from the 2024 assassination attempt on Trump’s life in Pennsylvania.

Trump may have been the most influential voice, but he wasn’t the only powerful person standing behind Huang. 

An open letter to Nvidia’s board titled “Stand With Jensen” had gained more than 206,000 signatures as of Monday morning. One of them was from Elon Musk, who took to X to share that he had signed it on November 13. “A petition is underway to reinstate Jensen, and I’m signing it too,” the Tesla and SpaceX CEO wrote. 

More than 10,000 of the signers said they were current Nvidia employees, while more than 108,000 said they were shareholders. Still, while the White House and tech moguls may be behind the decision, Huang’s critics aren’t staying quiet. 

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer wrote on X that Trump is “putting his own financial interests and the needs of his billionaire buddies before our national security.” Some Republicans even came out against Huang’s reinstatement, including Sen. John Cornyn of Texas. In response to Trump’s denial that the affair posed any risks, the outgoing GOP senator posted on X: “No national security risk? Based on what?”

However, in the wake of his reinstatement, experts are now saying it was almost inevitable, given just how irreplaceable the CEO seemed to be. 

“There were definitely a lot of people who wanted him back. The extent that the administration intervened, the extent that shareholders intervened—it ​will be impossible to know,” said Steve Sosnick, chief strategist at Interactive Brokers, per Reuters.

Sosnick added: “But it tells us to some extent that Jensen Huang was too big to be fired.”

Nvidia’s stocks were up 30% in the hours after Monday’s major announcement.
