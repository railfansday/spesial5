US to host Copa America again in 2028 as Argentina target three-peat

The United States will host Copa America again in 2028, soccer federations Conmebol and Concacaf announced Tuesday, marking its second consecutive time holding the competition.

Six teams from North America, Central America and the Caribbean will join 10 South American sides in the event, which runs from June 9 to July 9, 2028. Qualification for Concacaf teams will be determined through the 2027 Gold Cup.

The US hosted the last Copa America in 2024, when Argentina beat Colombia in the final. The nation also staged the competition in 2016, finishing fourth.

"After our collaboration in 2024, we are pleased to join forces again to bring the Conmebol Copa America to our region in 2028," Concacaf President Victor Montagliani said in a statement.

"This agreement will bring six Concacaf national teams a new opportunity to compete against some of the best in the world, while further reinforcing the competitive significance of our Concacaf Gold Cup."

The federations noted that host cities and venue details will be announced at a later date.

"The Conmebol Copa America is much more than a tournament: it is part of our identity, our history, and the greatness of South American football," Conmebol President Alejandro Domínguez said in a statement.

"In 2028, we will once again bring that passion to the United States, reaffirming our historic partnership with Concacaf and our shared commitment to continuing to drive the growth of football across the continent."

Argentina have won the last two editions of the tournament, having also beaten Brazil 1-0 in the final back in 2021. However, they will have to defend their title without Lionel Messi in 2028 following his retirement from international football.

Messi called time on his glittering Argentina career after leading his country to the World Cup final in July, where they lost 1-0 to Spain after extra time.

He played one final friendly against Benin last month, scoring a penalty to seal a 3-0 win.
