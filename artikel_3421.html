Best Isekai Books Like Dungeon Crawler Carl

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Dungeon Crawler Carl by Matt Dinniman is not, technically, an isekai. The genre was popularized in Japan, and in the genre's current form, it centers around an ordinary person who's suddenly transported or reborn into another world. Carl isn't relocated to a different world—the madness comes to his world. But it does have all the other makings of an isekai: an ordinary dude has to go up against RPG-like elements such as monsters, leveling up, loot, questing, and so on.

Of course, there are plenty of litRPG books for fans of DCC, but there are also plenty of isekai that fans of Dinniman's books would enjoy. Lots of manga and anime play off the "adventuring in another world" trope, but we'll take a look at the light novels, specifically, which are breezy, fast-paced and fun to read bite-sized novels that are designed to be portable.

Grab one (or all) of these recommendations, because you never know when you might need some light reading when you're out and about on your own epic adventures... or just commuting to work. Plus, quite a few of these have accompanying anime, so you can continue the journey off the page, too!

In So I'm A Spider, So What? (available for purchase here) a freak accident causes an entire Japanese high school to be obliterated and killed. Most are reincarnated into another world as nobles, adventurers, and other characters. But one girl is reincarnated in a dungeon as the lowest and weakest monster of them all: a tiny spider.

Of course, part of the fun of an isekai is watching the underdog go from nothing to being kind of a big deal in the world, just like in Dungeon Crawler Carl. Over her adventures, the spider grows more and more powerful, makes some very useful allies (and, of course, some dangerous enemies), and becomes an influential figure in this strange new world.

The protagonist of Completionist Chronicles (available for purchase here) is an ordinary Joe, literally. Unlike in a traditional isekai, Joe doesn't get run over by a truck or die to get reborn. Instead, he makes a choice to permanently start over his life inside a game.

As soon as he does, Joe realizes that he has some abilities that make him a wanted man in the world. Forced to practice his forbidden magic in secret, he makes it his mission to learn more about the world. Joe won't settle for just a slight glimpse of the game; he's after that 100% completion achievement. He's going to get every item, skill, ability, and every bit of knowledge that this world has to offer, or die trying.

The Rising Of The Shield Hero (available here) follows Naofumi, just an ordinary nerdy guy who's suddenly summoned to another universe. He learns that he's one of the four legendary heroes who are destined to save the world from destruction. Unfortunately, he's the weakest and least impressive of them all: the Shield Hero.

Without a powerful weapon, Naofumi has to rely on his brains, strategies, and loyal allies who he meets along the way. Together, they fight monsters, unravel political plots, and find a way to rise above his weak-seeming shield and do his part to save this fantasy world he's found himself in.

In Dungeon Crawler Carl, Carl's survival depends largely on his use of, essentially, a hidden tool. That's also the case for Solo Leveling (available for purchase here). This series is also technically not an isekai, since Jinwoo Sung isn't transported to another world permanently. Instead, mysterious Gates suddenly appear in his world, which connect to other worlds full of dangerous monsters.

In Jinwoo's world, some people "awaken" to become fighters while others stay as weak non-fighters. Jinwoo is one of the latter. The thing is, once you awaken, you're assigned a rank, and you stay at that rank forever. One day, Jinwoo finds that he can do something no one else can: he can level up.

This changes everything. As the only person in the world who can level up and increase his strength, Jinwoo grows in power by fighting through dungeons. As he does, he uncovers the secrets of these Gates and what's happening in his world.

The idea behind Log Horizon (available for purchase here) is based around an MMORPG—a massively multiplayer online role-playing game. One day, 30,000 players wake up to find themselves trapped inside their beloved multiplayer video game, Elder Tales. The game becomes much more serious when they realize that they might be stuck in this digital world forever, with no way to escape.

A group of adventurers bands together to find a way back to their home world. They travel around the fantasy world, defeat monsters, and search for clues about what's happening. And soon, they discover something even more alarming: They respawn after death, but lose a little more of their memories each time. The fight to survive and return home begins now!

With two such similar genres as litRPG and isekai, there's bound to be some overlap. He Who Fights with Monsters (available for purchase here) sits right at that intersection between the Western litRPG and Japanese isekai.

Jason is just your run-of-the-mill office supplies middle manager until he's transported to another world. On his very first day, he has to fend against monsters, wizards, cultists, and cannibals. Like Carl, Jason has to use his wits and some special powers to stay alive in this wild world. The only problem is that he's a pretty decent guy, but all his powers are evil.

In DCC, Carl's cat Princess Donut captures the spotlight. The young cat girl in Reincarnated as as Sword (available for purchase here) is also at the center of the story, but as the title suggests, she's not the main character here.

Instead, the protagonist is an office worker who's reincarnated in the fantast world as a sentient sword. He remembers his old life, but not his name, so he becomes the girl's Teacher in both name and status. They work together to achieve both their goals: She wants to become stronger, and he wants to know why he's in this world. They learn more about themselves, too, and become an unstoppable team.

In That Time I Got Reincarnated As A Slime (available for purchase here), a guy stuck at a dead-end job meets his untimely end at the hands of a robber. As he's dying, he unknowingly makes a pact with a strange system that resurrects him in another world... as a slime, of course.

Like the spider from So I'm a Spider, So What?, the little slime doesn't stay weak for very long. He uses his special connection with the aforementioned system to acquire some extremely useful skills, then heads out on his adventure.

As the slime meets new characters, he uses his strategic way of thinking to forge some extremely powerful alliances. Just like Dungeon Crawler Carl, the slime's story revolves around a fight for survival and acquiring power. And just like DCC, the slime gets more and more involved in the overall lore and politics of the world, sending him on more epic adventures each time.
