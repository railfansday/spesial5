Star Wars Creator George Lucas’ Rejected Sequels Resurface in New Disney Release

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Whatever your feelings about the Star Wars sequel trilogy, one of the things that would've been particularly fascinating about seeing George Lucas make another three Star Wars movies is finding out what he thought should happen to Luke Skywalker after the events of Return of the Jedi. Lucas had spent much of the prequel trilogy exploring the shortcomings of the Jedi Order, and there was every reason to believe that the man who created Luke would've had something to say about how he went about rebuilding it. Of course, when Disney acquired Lucasfilm for just over $4 billion in 2012, it also acquired Lucas' treatments for another three Star Wars movies, although it turns out that the man responsible for bringing Lucasfilm into the Disney fold wasn't particularly enamored with what Lucas had in mind.

Speaking to Matthew Belloni on The Town podcast, Wall Street Journal reporter Robbie Whelan discussed an anecdote from his upcoming book, The House of Mouse: Bob Iger and the War of Succession at Disney, about the negotiations between Lucas and Bob Iger. As Belloni read from Whelan's book, Iger was sufficiently concerned about what he'd read in Lucas' treatments that he wanted something written into the acquisition agreement that would allow Disney to make an altogether different trilogy.

“After reading the plot outlines, Iger decided Disney had to buy them, but he made sure to write into the contract that Disney had no legal obligation to actually make the movies. Another person who was involved in the negotiation recalls that Iger hated the treatments and said privately, ‘We are not making these movies.’”

What's particularly interesting about the timing of that conversation is that Lucas hadn't actually made a Star Wars movie in seven years at that point, and the reputation of the prequel trilogy was somewhat different in 2012 from what it is today. Whelan explained the rather unusual position in which Iger found himself during those negotiations.

“Imagine you’re an executive who’s never written a movie, never directed a movie, and George Lucas sends you a package of scripts, and you look at these and say, ‘This is terrible. We’re never making this.’”
