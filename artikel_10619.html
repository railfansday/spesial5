The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake review – A masterpiece made better

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time is the most critically acclaimed game ever released, and Nintendo has worked its magic once again, pairing a smorgasbord of improvements with breathtakingly beautiful visuals to make an extraordinary adventure even better.

It’s been nearly three decades since Ocarina of Time was released on the Nintendo 64, and while the 1998 epic famously returned with a remake for the 3DS, Link’s time-travelling quest has never truly received the overhaul it deserves.

While Ocarina of Time famously helped revolutionize gaming with its Z-targeting system and 3D interconnected world, the legendary title feels pretty dated when compared to new adventures like Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom.

Enter the Switch 2 remake, an enhanced version of Nintendo’s masterpiece boasting completely new visuals and game-changing quality-of-life improvements that make Ocarina of Time look and play better than ever before.

Ocarina of Time follows Link, a young boy tasked with saving the kingdom of Hyrule from Ganondorf. He teams up with Hyrule’s Princess Zelda, who tells him that Ganondorf is seeking the Triforce, an ancient relic capable of granting a wish to whoever secures all three pieces.

To stop Ganondorf from plunging Hyrule into darkness, the green-clad hero must claim the legendary Master Sword and embark on a time-hopping quest to awaken the Seven Sages, whose power is needed to defeat him and save the kingdom.

Ocarina of Time remake packs plenty of improvements that elevate the 27-year-old adventure, with many plucked from Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom. The biggest, and most welcome, is the addition of a jump and a run button, which gives you greater control and freedom when exploring Hyrule and its dungeons.

That’s provided 90s muscle memory doesn’t kick in, and you actually remember there’s no auto jump and you don’t plummet off a ledge into the ground with a pained yelp from Link.

Being able to run indefinitely is also a fantastic feature, especially given how time-consuming traversal can be as Young Link in early sections when you don’t have access to Epona or location-warping Ocarina songs. It also avoids your ears from being bombarded with the shrill ‘Hyuuuuup! Hyaaaaa!’ rolling sound effects of the original, always a bonus for anyone who wants to speed things up without losing their sanity.

Threads of Time also helps you keep track of every quest you pick up during your journey, which proved extremely useful when working through all the Happy Mask Salesman requests and trying to remember the steps in the comically long-winded Biggoron’s Sword quest.

One of the most wholesome additions is the ability to play Link’s ocarina by humming, singing, or playing a real ocarina using the Switch 2’s microphone to activate the songs in-game. While not necessary, being rewarded for memorizing Zelda’s Lullaby or Epona’s Song is pretty cute stuff, even if your neighbors may not share the same enthusiasm.

Then there’s the ability to use gyro controls to aim Link’s bow and ranged gadgets like the Hookshot, which really helps with catching Poes on horseback, hitting hidden switches to open secret doors, and whacking bosses where it hurts when chasing the best clear times in the new Hall of Fallen Foes boss rush mode.

Nintendo has also improved combat, with the Master Sword now having a real sense of heft behind it. Striking enemies comes with satisfying feedback from the Switch 2 controller’s improved HD rumble, giving your slashes a weighty feel. This is especially true when using the Megaton Hammer to flatten foes like a pancake or slicing an enemy with the two-handed Biggorn Sword.

While Wolfos and Skulltulas still rely on you waiting for that perfect opportunity to hit their weak points, tougher enemies like Lizalfos and Gerudo Warriors are more reactive, flitting in and out of combat and dashing in for some cheeky damage when you let your guard down.

Ocarina of Time’s bosses are also more of a spectacle now, with upgraded arenas, attacks, and flashier animations. Remake’s Ganon is bigger and badder than his older, blockier brother. In fact, he makes his 1998 counterpart look positively tame. From raining down blood-red lightning strikes from the sky to charging on all fours, his demonic beast form is even more badass than ever before.

While none of these improvements drastically change the adventure you know and love, they do make an already great game even better.

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake isn’t just the best-looking Nintendo Switch 2 game, it’s also the most detailed Zelda ever. Kokiri Forest is filled with lush green foliage, moss-covered rocks, and beautiful babbling brooks.

Hyrule Field is also blanketed with swaying grass and pretty wildflowers, while rugged terrain near Gerudo Valley and rocky formations near Zora’s River give these areas a more distinct look compared to the flatter landscape of the original. Environmental details like Kakariko Village’s signature windmill and houses nestled on the hills beneath Hyrule Castle also help make this smaller-scale adventure feel more lived-in than ever.

Ocarina of Time’s newly added signs of life were especially apparent in populated locations like the sleepy mountainside dwellings of Kakariko Village and the cobbled streets of Hyrule Castle Town. Ducking into alleyways of the latter and having a right good nosy proved particularly good fun, especially now that you can freely explore and interact with the colorful townsfolk.

Nintendo has carried this same level of detail into the remake’s dungeons, where atmospheric fog carpets the stone floors and reacts to Link’s footsteps and sword swings. Dust particles also now hang in the air and glisten as they catch the light, while deeper shadows and improved lighting heighten iconic areas like Kakariko’s nightmarish well and Ganondorf’s Castle.

Even the Hero of Time and his companions have received a graphical glow-up, with hyper-realistic hair and outfits. The most eye-catching is Link’s iconic tunic, which now features woven green straw fibers, intricately designed belts, and enough tassels to have cosplayers curse Nintendo for the level of intricacy in his new, highly detailed drip.

For veteran Zelda players, exploring these well-trodden environments feels like returning to your favorite childhood haunts as an adult. Although each setting has had a significant upgrade, navigating Hyrule and its dungeons feels familiar but fresh. Nintendo has taken so much care recreating its beloved world that it’s hard not to get pulled back into another 20+ hour adventure, gallivanting around Hyrule atop Epona searching for Heart Containers like it’s 1998 all over again.

Just like Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom, everyone barring Link and minor NPC characters is now fully voiced. While this may be a controversial addition for purists hoping to go back to Zelda’s silent era, the new cutscenes help add more life to the Seven Sages and provide more details that will have lore lovers happy to revisit Hyrule’s history.

Characters like the Goron patriarch Darunia are now way more expressive, while Nintendo has done more for Princess Ruto and Link’s one-sided romance than even the most die-hard fanfiction writer. There’s also a greater sense of emotion behind certain events, like when Link leaves Kokiri Forest thanks to some heartfelt voiced lines from Saria. Then there’s Ganondorf himself, whose grand entrance and signature organ playing are elevated further by some particularly infectious evil laughter that I may or may not have tried to imitate.

Whether you’re a returning player or a newcomer, Ocarina of Time remake is the definitive way to play Link’s legendary adventure. Nintendo has successfully breathed new life into its decades-old world of Hyrule, giving it a loving lick of paint that not only elevates it over the original, but also makes it the best-looking Switch 2 game.

Just like Link’s time-travelling epic, Ocarina of Time remake honors its past and carefully combines new improvements borrowed from the series’ present to give this timeless classic the treatment it deserves. Ocarina of Time remake isn’t just a masterpiece, it’s a timeless adventure everyone needs to play.
