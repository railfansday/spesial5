MongoDB snaps up Firebolt to bring transactions and analytics under one roof

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Many have tried to marry analytics with transactions. The document DB posterchild takes another crack at it

MongoDB has acquired analytical database specialist Firebolt in another attempt to bring transactional and analytical workloads onto one platform.

The document database vendor carved out its patch of the database market with flexible, JSON-like documents rather than rigid, predefined table structures. Developers put it to work in web, mobile, ecommerce, and content management applications.

It has since branched out to offer a data platform it says is ready for the age of AI and AI agents working across transactional environments, promising scale, speed and reliability.

Firebolt was founded in Israel in 2019. It built a cloud-native data warehouse based on the notion of automatically and transparently merging and compacting files as they are ingested. Speaking to The Register in 2020, then CTO Eldad Farkash claimed this slashes the amount of data users need to load into the system, accelerating query performance and improving concurrency.

Firebolt raised $100 million in Series C funding in 2022 at a reported valuation of $1.4 billion. MongoDB has not disclosed the acquisition price.

But what will it get, and what does that mean for MongoDB users?

In a blog post, the company said: "By integrating Firebolt into MongoDB's Intelligent Data Platform, we're bringing a high-performance analytics engine to where your operational data already lives. Firebolt's engine will run right alongside your database, and we'll keep it in sync automatically, so there's no pipeline to build or manage. You'll get one platform for operational and real-time analytical workloads, so teams can ship faster, with fewer systems to operate and secure."

The integration isn't available yet. MongoDB says customers will gain access in the coming months, while existing Firebolt customers will retain their current service and support.

MongoDB's pitch is that AI agents will need fresh operational data and fast analytical answers, without customers having to stitch separate platforms together.

MongoDB is hardly the first vendor to chase that combination. Bringing online transaction processing (OLTP) and online analytical processing (OLAP) together promises less infrastructure to manage and fewer data pipelines to maintain. Delivering both without compromises is the harder part.

Earlier this year, Databricks claimed to have cracked the problem, promising "one data, zero compromises, zero copies." It works with Reyden – a new compute engine – and Lakebase, its serverless PostgreSQL on open object storage. Rivals pointed out there were briefly two copies of the data in the system at some level.

SingleStore also tackles the problem with an in-memory row store and an on-disk column store with tiered storage, "meaning transactions hit memory first, and then they roll off to disk storage," allowing analytics and transactions on a single system.

Oracle and SAP have also spent years adding analytical capabilities to transactional platforms to reduce the delay between recording data and analyzing it.

MongoDB promises real-time analytics without a customer-managed pipeline. Whether that also means avoiding separate copies of the data is less clear. Automatic synchronization doesn't explain how the information is stored or moved underneath. The Register has asked MongoDB to clarify the architecture.

MongoDB has been here before. In 2022, it unveiled columnstore indexing designed to help developers build analytical queries into their applications. The idea was to accelerate those queries without changing the document structure or moving data to another system.

Firebolt gives MongoDB another tool for a familiar job: making analytics feel at home beside its operational database. Whether the plumbing lives up to the pitch remains to be seen. ®
