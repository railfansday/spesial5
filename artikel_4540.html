The Honda Avancier SUV Offers a Taste of CR-Vs, Pilots and Accords to Come

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

This three-row crossover won’t be coming to America, but don’t be surprised if future Hondas that do look an awful lot like it.

Before we dive in here: don’t get too attached to the new Honda Avancier you see here, because it ain’t coming to American Honda dealerships. John Cena will not be touting its delights during NFL games. But this three-row crossover is still very much worth discussing here at United States-based Road & Track, because much of what you see here will be finding its way to sedans and SUVs that are, in fact, inbound for the good ol’ U.S. of A.

First up, there’s the design—inside and out. The exterior utilizes Honda’s latest design philosophy, seen previously on a pair of sedan and SUV concepts but rendered here in a much more, shall we say, friendly form. Like many a modern machine, it moves the headlights lower on the front fascia and separates them from LED accent eyebrows above, and adds a strong grille dotted with trapezoidal shapes that boasts the latest version of the “H” front and center. The side and rear views have a hint of Range Rover to them, as well as a surprisingly racy fastback angle to the D-pillar and a spoiler that conceals the rear wiper. Recessed door handles, presumably, aim to reduce drag and boost mileage.

Honda describes the new look as its “Grand-Journey” concept, which emphasizes clean body surfaces to deliver a modern look that hints at a sporty character and pushes owners to embark on a long, delightful drive. I don’t know if I’d go quite that far, but the Avancier certainly looks a good bit fancier than the likes of the Pilot or CR-V found in dealerships today.

The “Grand-Journey” language also extends to the interior of the SUV. The cockpit is clean and elegant, especially in the mint-Oreo tones seen here. In order to free up space, Honda changed the gear shift of past models to a column shifter, and moved as many functions as possible to the central display, creating what the press release describes as “a simple instrument panel free of switches and buttons.” (That said, look close, and you can make out at least what appear to be a couple rockers and tactile switches on the steering wheel, so thank heaven for small favors.)

Perhaps more excitingly, the Avancier marks the first production use of the new, admittely-not-that-interestingly-named “Honda Hybrid” powertrain system. The setup places an even greater emphasis on the electric part of the gas-electric team-up, with the internal-combustion side working directly to move the car during fast cruising but the motor propelling the vehicle the vast majority of the time. Actually, make that motors, plural: this crossover becomes the first in the line to use the company’s “DualPower eAWD,” which has separate electric motors for each axle to deliver improved control during both acceleration and turning. It also gains the S+ Shift function inaugurated on the Prelude, which adds some faux gearing fun to the mix.

And the Avancier’s bones are also built fresh, as this crossover marks the inaugural use of Honda’s newest platform, meant to boost stability and driving fun. This new midsize platform, Honda says, can work for C- and D-segment vehicles in both sedan and SUV body styles—which, roughly, translates to the Civic, Accord, CR-V, Pilot, and Passport. The platform is designed to deliver an ergonomic, comfortable driving position, while also improving tire contact for a smoother and more dialed-in ride.

So while we may never see the Avancier on our streets (unless some odd soul wants to import one come 2051), the bits and pieces that make it up are sure to be found in the next generations of some of our favorite mainstream cars. And with the all-new Honda CR-V set to debut next year and already being teased, we shouldn’t have to wait long.
