Guimaraes will not force Newcastle exit like Isak, says Howe

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Eddie Howe is confident Bruno Guimarães will not demand to leave Newcastle United for Arsenal and echo Alexander Isak’s acrimonious departure to Liverpool last summer.

Howe admitted he could not guarantee that Guimarães will still be at the club in September but gave a strong defence of his captain’s character and insisted the Brazil international will return for pre-season training next week as planned.

Given the huge gap between Newcastle’s valuation of Guimarães and what Arsenal have indicated they would be willing to pay for the 28-year-old, there is a belief on Tyneside that the only way the captain would join the London club is if he behaved as badly as Isak did 12 months ago.

Newcastle value Guimarães at £100m, while Arsenal, through intermediaries, have only indicated they would be willing to pay about £60m including bonuses.

Isak did not travel on the club’s pre-season tour to Asia and then went on strike, refusing to train or play, until Newcastle agreed to sell him to Liverpool for a British record £128m on deadline day.

Asked if he feared a repeat of that with Guimarães this time around, Howe replied: “I’ve spoken to him as I would do with Bruno normally. He is a fantastic person and we have had some really good conversations both before, during and after the World Cup. What we speak about obviously has to remain private but he is a great player and a great person.

“Look, I don’t know what is going to happen with Bruno. That is up to other people to speculate about but also other people to have conversations about which I’m not a part of. I don’t think it is fair to compare situations and what happened previously. You’ve got to look at this in its own right.

“Bruno is captain of our football club. He has been an unbelievable player and person all the time we have worked together. I speak on behalf of everyone at the football club when I say we absolutely love him and of course everyone wants him to stay.

“The plan is for him to come back on July 31, he gets an extended break because of the World Cup and is currently enjoying a holiday with his family... There is nothing to say that Bruno won’t be back training with us.”

Arsenal’s interest in Guimarães is well known but there has been little progress since they began their pursuit more than a month ago.

Whether Arsenal can come up with an offer that tempts Newcastle into negotiations remains to be seen but it is thought the Premier League champions will try to harden up their interest before Guimarães returns for pre-season training.

Newcastle United were held to a 1-1 draw by Gateshead in their second pre-season friendly of the summer. A 79th-minute own-goal saved the Premier League club’s blushes but what did we learn from the match?

Steur did not grab many headlines when he arrived from Ajax for £20m earlier this month because of Sandro Tonali’s move to Tottenham Hotspur. But those familiar with Dutch football would have been excited that Newcastle had signed him.

The 18-year-old has impressed with his performances in training and gave a glimpse of his significant potential in his first public display. He is young and sources have told Telegraph Sport that he must develop physically before he is ready to play every week in the Premier League – but you can already see he has a very high ceiling.

As you would expect from a player described as Ajax’s best midfield prospect for a generation, he is assured on the ball. He is also a progressive passer, always looking to break the lines to get his team moving forward. Eddie Howe will love him.

Whisper it, but there were shades of Bruno Guimarães in his style of play, in the way he took the ball under pressure, in tight spaces, and played on the turn. Patience will be needed but the signs are really encouraging.

Guimarães’s future is all supporters have wanted to speak about for weeks. I was at a wedding on Friday and it felt like everyone asked me about it at some point – including the bride and groom immediately after completing their first dance.

It has already become a summer-long transfer saga that has not progressed since Arsenal’s interest was revealed last month. Telegraph Sport has repeatedly checked whether there have been any fresh developments over the past week and the answer from senior Newcastle sources has remained consistent: there are no talks ongoing and nothing has accelerated, despite claims Arsenal were preparing an official £70m bid.

However, Newcastle need clarity from their captain. He is due back for pre-season next week and is expected to fly to Spain for their training camp on July 31. He has intimated he would like to move to Arsenal but unless Newcastle receive a bid that matches their asking price, he and his representatives have been told he will not be allowed to leave.

Schär has been a brilliant player for Newcastle, but at the age of 34 and coming off the back of a serious ankle injury, he looked understandably rusty against Gateshead.

This will be the former Switzerland international’s ninth season at St James’ Park and he was at fault for giving away a soft free-kick that led to Gateshead scoring first.

Schar accepted a contract extension earlier this summer on reduced terms and although Howe values his quality and experience, he is going to be a back-up centre-back this season.

Neave, 19, started the game as Newcastle’s centre-forward as Will Osula was missing and Nick Woltemade and Yoane Wissa were given additional time off after playing in the World Cup for Germany and DR Congo respectively.

But those players will return to training next week and, although Neave forced a good save in the first half, he is not ready to start in the Premier League. There is little point keeping him around as a fourth-choice striker when he needs to be playing regularly at this stage of his career.

A loan move is on the cards and he could be a really smart signing for a Championship club or a team competing at the top of League One. The most important thing is that he gets regular senior games rather than hangs around at Newcastle on the fringes of the first-team squad.

It has been a difficult and, at times, acrimonious 12 months for Eddie Howe, who must have wondered whether the job was still worth the hassle given the criticism and online vitriol he received at times last season.

Few would have blamed him if he had quit, but Howe has always been fully committed and always knew there would be a period like this to come through.

He has been in the job almost five years and last season was the first time he had been vilified by a section of the fanbase. It left its mark. The boos that followed defeats at St James’ Park hurt and he worried the unity that had powered the team’s success had fractured irreparably.

The apparent collective shrug that greeted another run to the Carabao Cup semi-final, while guiding the team into the knockout rounds of the Champions League for the first time was a source of bewilderment and frustration.

Howe has not complained or moaned. Although he wobbled towards the end of last season and there were some calling for him to be sacked, he was supported and encouraged by those above him. That loyalty mattered.

The problem is, negativity lingers in the fanbase. Even a picture of Howe at pre-season training, posted on social media by the club, led to abusive comments questioning his ability and character. Some of it was toxic. It was rightly called out by other supporters.

Goals from Elliot Anderson (wonder what happened to him?!), Allan Saint-Maximin and Jay Turner-Cooke helped Newcastle overturn a 2-0 half-time deficit when they last visited Gateshead International Stadium in 2023.

Speaking through Gateshead’s club media, Cattermole said of facing Newcastle: “It’s obviously a different test. We have to respect the opposition, they’re a high-quality team and it might not be what we’re getting in our league but we’ll still try to implement what we believe we want to be in and out of possession. It’ll be a great physical challenge for us.

He added: “They [my players] have all shown up great. What a game for them. What a game for them to go and test yourself against some big names and fantastic coaching staff, for us all it’s a great challenge for us. We hope we give them a good test, we hope we give them the challenge they want as well. I played in many of these games as a player as well. We expect it to be competitive.”

Welcome to live coverage of Newcastle United’s pre-season friendly against North-East neighbours Gateshead. Eddie Howe’s side make the three-mile journey south of the River Tyne having beaten Darlington 3-0 in their first outing of the summer last weekend. The Magpies’ National League opponents this afternoon, helmed by former Sunderland captain Lee Cattermole, have already played three times in pre-season, thrashing Blyth 6-0 and drawing against Heaton Stannington and Hebburn Town. The Heed threatened a shock win in their last meeting with Newcastle three summers’ ago, leading 2-0 at the interval before conceding three second-half goals.

This contest at Gateshead International Stadium will be Newcastle’s first match since the death of Kevin Keegan and the club have confirmed that a minute of silence will be observed before kick-off. Newcastle’s men’s, women’s and academy teams will wear black armbands for all pre-season matches this summer.

Newcastle will welcome Liverpool, with whom Keegan enjoyed success in the 1970s, to St James’ Park for their Premier League opener, and the two sides are in talks over a suitable pre-match tribute. Flowers, messages and mementos have been laid outside Newcastle’s ground since Keegan’s death on Monday, including a heartfelt message penned by fellow club legend Alan Shearer, which read: “I hope you realised how much you meant, to me, to this club, to everyone. Thank you does not seem enough. Rest In Peace Boss. Alan Shearer.”

With regards to the football, there is an acceptance among Newcastle supporters that, having finished 12th in the Premier League last season and lost Anthony Gordon, Kieran Trippier and Sandro Tonali, this is a summer of transition. The uncertainty surrounding the future of £100m-rated club captain Bruno Guimarães, who is on the radar of Arsenal, will be a concern; Newcastle will be keen to avoid a repeat of the furore that circled Alexander Isak’s departure last summer. Guimarães will join the team next week following his World Cup exploits with Brazil and is expected to take part in Newcastle’s La Manga training camp, which starts on July 31.

Summer signings Sean Steur and Ewen Jaouen were each handed 45 minutes in the behind-closed-doors victory over Darlington, during which Howe used different XIs for the two halves. Bazoumana Touré – the £50m winger acquired from Hoffenheim – is expected to feature today having returned from his post-World Cup holiday. Yesterday Newcastle announced the arrival of midfielder Aladji Bamba, the France youth international, from Monaco and he was immediately integrated into team training. There is every possibility that he will feature this afternoon. Gateshead this week announced two signings of their own, as Luke Supyk, the New Zealand Under-20 forward, agreed a two-year deal having scored three goals in pre-season as a trialist. Defender Jack Robinson also joined the club.
