Holiday marketing hits inflection point in 2026: What brands need to know

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Consumers’ push-and-pull relationship with buzzy tactics like AI and tried-and-true strategies like creator partnerships is challenging brands to find the right balance.

The 2026 holiday marketing landscape sits at an inflection point as advertisers address the push-and-pull relationship consumers have with buzzy tactics like artificial intelligence and tried-and-true strategies like creator partnerships and deals-driven messaging. For marketers to win the season, balance will be key.

Despite evolving shopping behaviors, consumers remain bullish for the holidays. Average household spend for the season is expected to reach $1,654, a 4% year-over-year increase, according to Deloitte’s annual holiday survey. Affordability remains a chief concern, but traditional holiday values like human connection and togetherness are what will move the needle for brands this season, according to Deb Freeman, head of strategy at Gut New York.

“The holidays in general are being more cherished by people,” Freeman said. “Marketers are really trying to understand how to take their brand DNA and plug it into the engine of this holiday spirit in a way that is going to come out on the other side full of humanity.”

For many brands, this year’s holiday season is particularly high stakes as they contend with year-round sluggish sales. Playbooks have included a focus on handmade ad creative and creator tie-ups, along with strategic ways to help consumers comb through an oversaturation of deals. Brands are also prioritizing younger audiences including Gen Z and millennials, cohorts that for the first time will make up the bulk of holiday sales, per Deloitte.

One of the chief questions to be answered this season is how much — and in what capacity — consumers are interested in AI within the shopping experience. Over one-third of consumers report being very or extremely willing to use generative AI tools to help with holiday shopping, according to Gartner research shared with Marketing Dive. It’s a different story when it comes to AI-powered marketing materials, which consumers have shown resistance to on a broader scale, said Gartner Senior Principal Analyst Emily Weiss.

“If everyone can click and generate a campaign in Copilot and it only takes 20 minutes — or less — then why would they expect that people should spend any time caring about that?” Weiss said.

A variety of brands are placing a greater emphasis on human-made creative. Earlier this month, Levi’s debuted a campaign, “Let’s Get Fuzzy,” featuring stop-motion claymation. The spot, which required four hours of filming for each second of animation, calls back to the 1995 ad “Clayman,” the brand’s first global commercial. The strategy allowed the brand to celebrate human creativity while flexing its legacy status, a move that could help grow loyalty.

“We often reference and pay homage to our past while moving ideas and creativity forward — and it was this same thinking that we applied to our ‘Let’s Get Fuzzy’ campaign,” said Anne Cambria, vice president of global marketing at Levi Strauss & Co, in emailed comments.

Similarly, E.l.f. Brands released “Made to Stand Out,” a campaign that features a spot made entirely with stop-motion animation. Narrated by actor Alan Cumming, the ad tells the story of a trio of friends as they grow to love their unique qualities. The move, which aligns with the beauty purveyor's longtime focus on individuality, represents its first holiday campaign.

“When we looked at the holiday landscape, we saw a sea of sameness and a real opportunity to bring something different to the table,” said CMO Oshiya Savur, in emailed comments.

While consumers have largely rejected AI in marketing, they have expressed interest in the tech for assistance with more mundane tasks like product discovery and price comparisons. Additionally, 13% of shoppers are using AI for gifting inspiration, according to Gartner.

Consumers who use AI for inspiration consult an average of 4.48 additional sources compared to 3.24 among nonusers. The insight suggests that AI isn’t streamlining the shopping journey, but adding another layer to an already fragmented process, Weiss explained. Retailer websites remain the main source for gifting inspiration, while in-store shopping and Amazon are the top planned purchase channels at 77% and 75%, respectively.

“These tools are not replacing the more traditional search channels or the traditional places where people tend to start their shopping journeys, like good old Google Search, Amazon, friends and family wish lists and other people’s real life recommendations — it’s just that we can’t mine real-life recommendations for data,” Weiss said.

The executive recommends that marketers focus on improving discoverability across both traditional and emerging channels. That includes ensuring that brand-based and retail-based data are communicating with each other, as well as keeping product availability and shipping options up to date, moves that directly affect search rankings.

Consumers’ use of AI for price comparisons comes as affordability remains top of mind, with 79% of shoppers expecting higher prices this season, per Deloitte. The tech may also be a way for shoppers to relieve deal fatigue, as brands have recently flooded shoppers with promotions — the legitimacy of which have come under scrutiny by consumers — in a bid to the budget-conscious.

JCPenney is seeking to combat deal overwhelm with a focus on real shoppers and no-fuss ways to save. A “Party Fits” spot, set in Crown Point, Indiana, stars a local barber who showcases three holiday looks she scored for a total of $168. The campaign is based on the insight that only 32% of shoppers completely or mostly trust retailers to offer a fair price.

Of shoppers this season, 38% are classified by Deloitte as value seekers, or individuals who engage in five or more cost-saving behaviors. While that could raise alarms for brands, value seeking consumers are also expected to spend 12% more than others, making them a key cohort to win over, said Brian McCarthy, a principal in Deloitte’s strategy and analytics practice.

“I think of value seeking as another way of saying engaged shoppers,” McCarthy said.

Some brands have sought to communicate their value by simplifying the process of finding the perfect gift. Target this month debuted a holiday campaign centered on an Academy of Giftology concept where various courses cater to different gift-giving needs. Sephora tapped Seth Rogen for ads that promote in-store Sephora advisors and cater to those who are searching for gifts but are clueless about beauty products.

“The idea was that if Seth can Sephora, we all can Sephora,” said Freeman. Gut New York is the agency behind the “We All Sephora” campaign.

Other marketers are taking a more traditional approach, including Walmart, which debuted ads inspired by “The Muppet Christmas Carol.” Meanwhile, Gap is iterating on its “Give Your Gift” campaign with a community-focused spot. To strike the right mix of emotional and deals-focused advertising, marketers should consider how the two strategies can complement each other to keep consumers engaged throughout the entire holiday season, Freeman explained.

“If you are only thinking about giving people the discounts, the rewards, and banging them over the head with that, they’re going to stop engaging,” Freeman said. “It’s more about the pacing of it [and] thinking about where your audience is at different points of the season.”

This year’s holiday season could be of greater importance for marketers focused on Gen Z and millennials. Together these two cohorts are expected to make up 50% of spending for the first time in the 41 years Deloitte has been running its holiday survey. Key to reaching these audiences will be focusing on preferences, like wish lists, personalized gifts and faster delivery, which matter to them at a higher rate than they do to older generations, McCarthy said.

“Younger shoppers want a bit more guidance, personalization and convenience,” he said. “Gen Xers and boomers in our study want clear value and to shop independently if the clear value is stated.”

Rising creator investments across the industry — one way marketers are attempting to reach digitally native younger consumers — are expected to persist throughout the holiday period. This season will also see an evolution of creator activations as the channel becomes more sophisticated, explained Becky Owen, CMO of Billion Dollar Boy Group.

“I think the way in which brands are integrating into the culture of creators and the culture of social is a lot more interesting,” Owen said. “It feels a lot more driven by psychology, whereas I’d say in years past, it was a lot more about just review it, get it out there, build hype, build noise, and the loudest brand wins.”

Strategic creator activations will be key as consumers show signs of influencer fatigue while brands sink billions into the channel. One way that sentiment has manifested this season is through strategies that go beyond lavish product hauls — a move many see as out of touch — to include more intentional plays like advent calendars, which can encourage earned content and product discovery if done correctly, Owen explained. Skims recently came under fire for its take on the trend, which included a $148 advent calendar filled with hair ties and bobby pins.

Brands could also build stronger consumer trust by prioritizing long-term partnerships and ambassador programs, which have recently grown in popularity, Owen said. Such is the focus for Urban Outfitters: The brand recently expanded on its “All Together Now” brand platform with “All Together Now: More the Merrier,” a campaign inspired by the insight that the majority of Gen Z plans to self-gift this season and values togetherness.

For the launch, Urban Outfitters hosted a party that included product customization stations and an immersive gifting suite from partner DoorDash. The move also brought together people who featured in other marketing pushes from the brand earlier this year, including student ambassadors and attendees of an experience in Joshua Tree, California.

“As we seek out partnerships, we’re not just looking for short-term, we’re really looking for community members that have an affinity for the brand on their own, because that’s where we get the magic,” said Julia Draper, head of digital and e-commerce at Urban Outfitters.

While the holiday season is just kicking into high gear, one takeaway is clear: Brands are willing to pull out all the stops to come out ahead during the end-of-year period. As they plan around evolving consumer behavior and map out ways to leverage flashy tactics, Gut’s Freeman emphasizes that at the center of every strategy should be a focus on the values that define the season.

“People are looking for connection,” Freeman said in an emailed statement. “They want stories they can relate to, laugh about and feel sentimental toward. They want to feel like they're part of something.”
