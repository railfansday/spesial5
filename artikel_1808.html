Concerns as Arsenal Start Handing Out New Contracts

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Arsenal’s willingness to hand new contracts to key figures such as Declan Rice, David Raya and Jurrien Timber is entirely understandable. These are not squad players being rewarded for loyalty; they are central pillars of a team that has developed into one of the strongest in Europe under Mikel Arteta. Reports indicate that Arsenal are moving towards new agreements with several of those players as they attempt to protect the core of the squad for the years ahead.

The challenge facing Arsenal is that successful teams eventually encounter a difficult balancing act. Keeping a title-winning squad together sounds attractive in theory, but football rarely allows clubs to stand still. At some stage, decisions have to be made about age profiles, wage structures and the next cycle of the team.

The next phase of Arsenal’s evolution could depend as much on who eventually leaves as who stays.

There is little debate around the wisdom of extending Rice’s contract. He has become one of the defining players of the Arteta era and is widely regarded as one of the Premier League’s outstanding midfielders. Timber’s importance has also grown significantly following his recovery from injury, while Raya has established himself as one of the league’s most reliable goalkeepers. Piero Hincapié, another player viewed as central to the club’s future, has reportedly signed a new deal already, though the club are yet to issue an official confirmation at the time of writing.

The logic behind these renewals is obvious. Arsenal have spent years building a squad capable of winning major honours. Allowing elite players to drift towards the final years of their contracts would create uncertainty and expose the club to unnecessary risk.

From a football perspective, securing the futures of those players is straightforward.

The complications arise when the wider picture is considered.

Much of Arsenal’s success has been built on careful squad construction. One of Arteta’s greatest achievements was creating a team that could improve together rather than peak at different times.

That process becomes more difficult when an increasing number of senior players move into their late twenties and early thirties simultaneously.

A cluster of important Arsenal players have contracts which expire in 2028, including Martin Odegaard, Kai Havertz, Ben White, Mikel Merino, Declan Rice, David Raya and Jurrien Timber. Many are either already in their peak years or approaching them.

There is a natural temptation to keep extending deals and retain a successful group. The danger is that a squad can age faster than expected. What appears sensible in 2026 can look problematic by 2029 if too many key players are on expensive long-term contracts at the same stage of their careers.

Elite clubs constantly refresh, they evolve while remaining competitive. Such an approach requires difficult judgment calls.

Supporters naturally focus on the stars they want to keep, but executives are forced to think differently.

Every new contract impacts the wage bill. Every long-term commitment affects future flexibility. Every decision made for one player influences negotiations with others. If Rice receives terms comparable to Arsenal’s highest earners, as has been reported, it will inevitably shape expectations across the dressing room.

That does not mean Arsenal should avoid rewarding their best performers. Quite the opposite. The club are right to secure the futures of players they believe can form the backbone of the team for years to come.

It does mean there will eventually come a point where not everybody can receive the same treatment. Some contracts will be extended, others may not. That is the reality of long-term squad planning.

For now, Arsenal’s contract activity should be viewed as a sign of strength rather than concern. Clubs in decline rarely spend their time trying to protect the futures of their most important players. Arsenal are doing so because they remain convinced that the current project has room to grow.

The more intriguing question is what happens next.

Arteta has already built a team capable of winning the Premier League and challenging for the Champions League. Sustaining that level for another five years may require a different skill altogether. It will require the ability to refresh the squad without weakening it, identify the next generation before the current one begins to decline and make unpopular decisions before they become obvious.

Those decisions are rarely celebrated when they are made – they are usually only appreciated years later; if ever.

Arsenal are entering that phase now. Keeping Rice, Raya and Timber appears straightforward. The real challenge will be determining which members of this successful squad remain part of the next cycle, and which make way for it.
