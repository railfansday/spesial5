Bradford City boss Michael Duff on risk-and-reward striker Nick Powell - and Sir Alex Ferguson admission

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

When he is fit and firing, as he is at the minute, he provides outstanding qualities at this level.

He showed that with his ninth goal of the season to put City ahead in Saturday's Yorkshire derby with Doncaster Rovers.

That he got 64 minutes in the tank was also significant.

Duff said: "It was his first league start (in 26-27) and he's been here a long time. It was always a risk.

"There are lengthy conversations from a psychological and a physical point of view, which him, the physios, the sports scientists and myself are all involved in, because he can do things that other players at this level can't."

Powell, who turns 33 in March, is in the winter of his career, but recent evidence suggests he is enjoying his football more than ever.

He commented: "I don't care if I start or not. As long as I get on a pitch and I can affect the game, that's all that matters to me.

"I think I can affect it off the bench or starting.

"It's one of those things where if you play 30 minutes a game, you get used to 30 minutes.

"You're going to have to flip that on its head and try to get used to 60, and hopefully longer, so we'll eventually get there.

"Hopefully, at the turn of the year, I can see myself starting more."

Duff added: "Nick's got an opportunity. He's missed a lot of football in his career and he's now in his later years and it looks like he's enjoying being out on the pitch.

“The only reason he's played at this level is because of his body, his injury history.

"He's influential in the group. They look up to him because none of our other players and staff have ever been at Man United. And there's a reason for that.

"So he's probably a little bit unfortunate in his career in that he joined United the year that Alex Ferguson left.

"It would be interesting to know how his career might have gone if he'd stayed. It's if's buts and maybes.

“With the greatest respect in the world, Powelly is not here for money. He's played for Man United and was on big money at Stoke."

On the 2-2 draw, Duff, whose side host former club Barnsley on Tuesday, said: "The key bit was we didn't get to the two-goal margin, I think if it went 2-0 or 3-1, the game's dead.

"But they're in the bottom four, 2-1 down and with nothing to lose and we just couldn't see that bit out."
