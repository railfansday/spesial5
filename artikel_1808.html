Nightmare at Dens Park: Dundee come to terms with history as child asks ‘what about last night?’

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

It wasn’t quite the morning after the night before. Nevertheless, Dundee players were very obviously still bearing the mental scars on Monday nearly 48 hours after a night of infamy inflicted by Hearts.

An old adage is that it’s best to go again as quickly as possible to get the performance and - most urgently of all – the result out of the system.

“To a point, yes…” agreed Steven Pressley when this theory was put to him at the sun-sprayed Dundee training facility at Gardyne College Campus ahead of Tuesday’s return to action against Hibs.

The counter argument, one the Dundee manager was clearly conscious of, is that three nights later is maybe just a bit too soon. In addition, when it comes to grounds Dundee might choose to visit for their next assignment after losing 8-0 – or should that be 0-8 – then Easter Road would not be high on the list. Dundee have not won there for a quarter of a century. The record books are being well-thumbed just now.

“We obviously don’t have a lot of time to work on elements of our game plan,” said Pressley. “It has been pretty frantic.

“Not only have we had to plan how we have to play and be prepared for Hibernian, I have also had to think a lot about the management of the group and everybody within that. I have had to find a way to stabilise the group.”

The employment of such words as “stabilise” reflect the harrowing nature of the experience. A group of 16 players including substitutes, many of them in their early 20s, endured something akin to a medical procedure.

A rampant Hearts side removed their sense of self-belief with almost surgical precision. Professional sport is often about how quickly such wounds can heal.

But this is new territory for everyone, including those as steeped in the game as Pressley. Owen Goodman, the 22-year-old keeper on loan from Crystal Palace, was perhaps left with most to feel sore about on Saturday night as goal after goal whizzed past him.

Dundee presumably have a duty of care towards him. It’s maybe one reason why Pressley decided against showing a re-run of the match the following morning. “I didn’t think putting them through the agony of watching it again was helpful,” he said.

Goodman was the last Dundee player to leave the field and one of the first in on Monday. He mentioned never having let in eight goals before, even in academy games. This was new territory for so many.

Young men such as Goodman carry a huge burden of responsibility as they attempt to bounce back from a historic defeat. An updated edition of the definitive Dundee FC history book, They Wore the Dark Blue, has just been published. It’s already out of date in one respect. Although it contains every result, competitive and otherwise, dating back to 1893, the Dundee fans' bible doesn’t include what now stands as the club’s biggest-ever home loss.

Such landmark results usually belong in the late 1800s, rendered a curiosity rather than a wounding detail due to the passage of time.

In Dundee’s case, prior to Saturday, their biggest-ever home defeat was relatively recent – 1971, against a Celtic side who had been crowned European champions in recent enough memory for one newspaper to describe them having “conjured the magic of Lisbon” at Dens Park following their 8-1 win.

Aberdeen equalled the margin of victory if not the scoreline in 2017. But Hearts outdid both those results on Saturday night. When Stuart Findlay rose like Alan Gilzean to nod in No 8 with a minute or so of the game left, the old pillars in the Dens Park main stand might have warped a fraction in recognition of the goal’s significance. Even if few, least of all Findlay, were probably aware of it at the time. A historic low dating back to 1893 – 1899 in the case of Dens Park itself – had been recorded.

Extraordinary, unthinkable results do happen. Just ask Hearts. Five goals down to Falkirk at home just a few weeks ago, they at least rallied to 5-2. Rallying was something Dundee conspicuously failed to do, which might have been the most disappointing aspect of the night for Pressley personally. He shouldered the blame himself, citing his suspicion that a poor performance had been in the post. He just didn't realise how poor. When he sought to rectify things at half time by sending on three substitutes there was precious little reaction. If anything things got worse, with Dundee reduced to ten men due to Owen Bevan’s red card.

Often teams who rack up handsome half-time leads cannot maintain such intensity in the second half due to a combination of factors, including stiff resolve from the opposition to avoid further embarrassment. This wasn’t evident on Saturday. Hearts scored another four goals to match their first-half quota and might have scored more, with another chalked off for a handball.

Although he says he did not consider tendering his resignation, it’s safe to assume Pressley endured a long dark night of the soul on Saturday night when he returned home to Edinburgh.

He said he found strength “from I don’t know where” and has been buoyed from a raft of messages of support. Perhaps Drey Wright, the Dundee wing-back, got to the heart of what it all meant on a human level.

“My six-year-old was at home watching the game, getting very upset with every goal that's going in,” said the 30-year-old.

“My eight-year-old is putting his hand on the shoulder of my wife, kind of subconsciously helping her out. So that's not easy to hear.

“I was back in training yesterday and later I was at the dinner table and my eldest says: ‘So Dad, what about that score last night?’ And it’s this kind of a back-to-reality moment. There’s no getting away from it.”
