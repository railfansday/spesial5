Piracy has been the scourge of the anime and manga industries for decades. Now, the Japanese government is clamping down on those sailing into illegal seas on the internet with "AI agents."

As per Yomiuri (H/T Automaton Media) Japan’s Agency for Cultural Affairs has trialled the AI detection system over the past year on both Twitter and across the wider internet.

The results, so far, have been promising. 84% of X posts flagged by AI were confirmed to contain illegal use of copyrighted material. Over 1,500 anime sites were also surveyed – with the number of pirated anime or manga rising to 89%.

There are now plans afoot for the AI technology to look further afield into websites and posts hosted in other countries, with findings due to be presented in early 2027. The Japanese government will also use the software to help speed up the copyright claim process, as well as directing users of said sites towards more legal means of media consumption.

In recent years, it has been confirmed that piracy to anime, manga and games in Japan costs the industries north of $35 billion dollars, a figure almost equivalent to what they bring in revenue each year.

It comes at a time where anime is on the precipice of a worldwide breakout – if it hasn't done so already. Demon Slayer Infinity Castle grossed almost $800 million worldwide, with a sequel on the way. Chainsaw Man season 2 is also likely to arrive next year alongside confirmed names such as Delicious in Dungeon season 2 and Frieren: Beyond Journey's End season 3.
