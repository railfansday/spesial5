White House removes cyber-security chief after series of tech outages

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Sean Cairncross (left) was appointed by the Trump administration

The top cyber-security official in the US government is leaving his post after a little over a year in the job, US media report.

CBS, the BBC's US news partner, and other media report that White House National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross has been fired after losing the confidence of White House chief of staff Stephen Miller.

It comes after a string of tech outages which hit banking, air traffic control and other sectors.

US officials said the outages were not caused by cyberattacks but the blackouts had sparked scrutiny from members of Congress who demanded a clearer explanation of what went wrong.

There has been no official confirmation of the removal of Cairncross but Secretary of State Marco Rubio posted on X, external to say Department of Defense chief information officer Kirsten Davies was the new national cyber director.

The AFP news agency and CBS both said sources had told them Cairncross's sacking was supported by President Donald Trump. The BBC has asked the White House for comment.

The news, first reported by Axios, external, follows security concerns, including the ability of advanced AI models - mostly from US companies - to discover vulnerabilities which humans would not be able to find on their own.

Earlier this week, Rubio said there was "zero evidence" the outages were connected, adding "we are not under systemic attack".

But the fact that so many have happened in quick succession has left some people worried about the safety of the tech which is heavily relied on in all aspects of daily life.

Hacks, AI or just overdue updates - why have there been so many outages?

Cairncross was previously a lawyer in the Republican Party, before becoming the chief operating officer of the Republican National Committee.

In the first Trump administration, he was part of the team of the White House chief of staff.

He later worked in the private sector, before returning to government in August 2025.

The White House has not publicly confirmed the departure or given a reason to why Cairncross left his role.
