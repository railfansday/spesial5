Melissa Rycroft's Legal Troubles in Texas: A Timeline of Her 2 DWI Arrests

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The Bachelor alum Melissa Rycroft’s life took a dramatic turn when she was arrested on back-to-back DWI charges just over a year apart. The former Dallas Cowboys Cheerleader — who won fans’ hearts on season 13 of The Bachelor in 2009 — has publicly addressed each incident and opened up about her mental health struggles.

Keep scrolling for a complete timeline of Rycroft’s legal troubles and the statements she’s made along the way:

Rycroft was arrested on September 23, 2025, in Tarrant County, Texas, and held for eight hours before being released on a $1,000 bond, TMZ reported at the time. A police report obtained by the outlet alleged that Rycroft told officers that another driver had hit her car and fled the scene — but an eyewitness contradicted her story, telling police that Rycroft’s SUV was the only vehicle involved and had been swerving before the incident.

According to the Tarrant County police report, Rycroft had “bloodshot eyes” and “slurred speech” at the scene, and one officer noted a “slight odor of a metabolized alcoholic beverage” on her breath. Her black Cadillac Escalade was damaged on the right side, and a nearby tree showed similar damage. Police also allegedly found “fresh droplets of colored liquid substances and spilled liquids inside the vehicle,” which they believed to be alcohol. Rycroft was taken into custody after field sobriety tests.

Ten days after the arrest, Rycroft’s attorney Bret Martin issued a statement to TMZ urging the public to reserve judgment.

“I want to remind the public of the fundamental principle of our justice system … every individual is presumed innocent until proven guilty,” Martin said on October 3, 2025. “We, as a society, have gotten completely away from this fundamental concept. My client was arrested on the allegation of Driving While Intoxicated. An arrest is no indication of guilt.”

Martin continued, “It is imperative that we closely examine the circumstances of this case — including the conduct of law enforcement, the toxicology results and the facts surrounding her arrest — before any conclusions are drawn. We respectfully request that the media and the public allow the legal process to unfold, and withhold any judgment whatsoever until all of the evidence has been properly analyzed and presented in a court of law.”

Three days after her attorney’s statement, Rycroft responded to a supportive fan comment on her Instagram.

“Life really sucks right now and I’m struggling, but I’m trying to march forward,” she wrote on October 6, 2025, replying to a fan who had encouraged her to extend herself grace. She added, “Thank you for the sweet words in what seems like a world of negativity.”

On October 24, 2025, prosecutors filed a criminal complaint against Rycroft for operating a motor vehicle while intoxicated and for having an alcohol concentration level of “0.15 or more at the time” her blood analysis was performed, Us exclusively reported. Under Texas law, a first DWI offense carries a mandatory three days in jail and up to 180 days behind bars.

Nearly a year after her arrest, Rycroft pleaded guilty — but she dodged jail time entirely. Us exclusively confirmed that Rycroft was sentenced on August 6, 2026, with a judge signing off on her plea deal on August 10. The agreement swapped 280 days in jail for 20 months of probation, along with a $2,000 fine (plus court fees totaling $2,087). Court documents revealed Rycroft’s blood alcohol concentration at the time of her 2025 arrest was .26 percent — three times the legal limit in Texas.

The terms of Rycroft’s plea deal, as detailed by Us, were extensive. She was ordered to complete 36 hours of community service within 120 days, finish a DWI education program and submit to a substance abuse screening. She wasn’t allowed to drive without a “camera equipped ignition interlock device” and agreed to abstain from “illegal use, possession, or sale of controlled substances, marijuana or cannabinoids.” She was also barred from consuming or possessing alcohol and required to check in monthly with Texas’ Community Supervision and Corrections Department.

Days before the plea was finalized, the criminal district attorney’s office filed their expert witness list and argued that Rycroft “has a reputation in the community for not being a law-abiding citizen.”

Just two months after pleading guilty, Rycroft was arrested on suspicion of another DWI. The Southlake Police Department in Texas responded to a “hit-and-run collision” on October 31, 2026, at approximately 5:50 p.m., involving a Southlake Fire Department ambulance, according to a report viewed by Us. Police said the ambulance was “stopped at a stop sign when another vehicle struck it and left the scene.” No injuries were reported.

Officers tracked down the alleged driver — Rycroft — a short time later. She was arrested and charged with driving while intoxicated (second offense), accident involving damage of more than $200 and driving with an invalid license. Bond totaled $9,000 — $7,500 for the DWI charge, $1,000 for the accident and $500 for the invalid license, per records viewed by Us. Her mugshot showed Rycroft wearing a black shirt with a skull-and-crossbones logo wearing a football helmet.

Two days after her second arrest, Rycroft broke her silence with a candid Instagram statement on November 3, 2026.

“First, please know how deeply ashamed and embarrassed I am, especially knowing how much this has affected my friends and family,” Rycroft wrote. “I want to share something personal, and to be honest, I am very ashamed to share it, but I can’t hide it anymore.”

The former Dancing With the Stars winner revealed she has been “struggling with my mental health for quite some time.”

“It has completely overwhelmed me,” she continued. “I’ve tried to hide what I’ve been going through by putting on a smile, but behind the scenes, things have been much harder than anyone realized or that I have let on, even to my family.”

Rycroft admitted she needed “help” and was finally seeking it. “I’ve fought this for a long time on my own, and it’s time to admit that I can’t do it anymore. I’m getting professional care. This is so hard to say and the shame is still very much there, but I’m ready to feel like myself again, for me and for my family.”

Rycroft shares three children with husband Tye Strickland, whom she married in 2009.
