Valve has good news and bad news for anyone interested in buying a Steam Machine. All current reservations for the 512GB model have been fulfilled and there's stock remaining, so if you want one you can just head over to the purchase page and buy it—but the 2TB model is in shorter supply so signups for the waitlist are no longer being taken.

And, sorry to say, there's also worse news: Valve says it won't be able to produce them in the quantities it had planned, and the price could increase.

Despite their high prices, demand for Steam Machines was high enough that Valve implemented a randomized lottery system to buy one: Prospective purchasers signed up for a shot, a randomized selection were put in a reservation queue with a Steam Machine reserved in their name, and everyone else went to the waiting list to be notified when more reservation spots came open.

Valve said in July that it expected to have the reservation queue caught up by the end of 2026, and that's now the case with the 512GB model: They're in an "an add-to-cart status," meaning that while supplies last, you can purchase one and it will be shipped straight away.

The situation is less happy for people waiting for the 2TB model, though. In an update posted today, Valve said all reservation holders have been sent their invitations to purchase but there are still people on the waiting list; those people won't lose their positions, but no one else will be allowed to join.

It sounds like that situation is likely to hold for at least a few months, as Valve said it expects to have more information on additional supply of the 2TB units to share in January. It also invited people on the waitlist to purchase a 512GB model instead, but warned that they'll lose their spot on the 2TB waitlist if they do.

Nobody likes to downgrade, and 512GB isn't as spacious as it used to be: Phantom Blade Zero requires an astronomical 300GB of free space for its pre-install process. While that's a bit of an outlier, games eating up more than 100GB of storage are very common these days. But Steam Machines are being hammered as much as anyone by the RAMpocalypse, and Valve said that's not changing anytime soon.

"We are continuing to encounter supply constraints for hardware components, which impacts our ability to source parts," Valve wrote in today's update. "For Steam Machine, that is especially impacting the 2TB model, where the number of customers on the waitlist exceeds our current production; even when we are able to build more it will be at smaller quantities than we planned."

It repeated that message in its conclusion: "We are continuing to build more Steam Machine units, but memory and storage costs continue to increase at unprecedented levels; as a result we don't yet know the number we are able to make, and at what price we'll be able to offer them. The 2TB model in particular will be especially constrained."
