Frida Kahlo-inspired health initiative and Ukrainian veterans exhibition among winners of prize celebrating innovative cultural projects

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Cultura en Vena, which brings art and culture programming into medical environments, is one of the five winners of the seventh edition of the awards

The charity Art Explora has revealed the five winners of the 2026 edition of its Académie des beaux-arts European Award, which recognises innovative work by non-profit arts and culture organisations in Europe. They are Polish performing arts foundation Jazdaa Polska!, Spanish art and health organisation Cultura en Vena, the French library charity Bibliothèques Sans Frontières, UK literary organisation London Writers Centre and Slovakian theatre company RAMAGU. The organisations were awarded funds ranging from €30,000-50,000, shared from a total £180,000.

The winners were announced at an award ceremony at the Victoria and Albert Museum in London on the evening of 24 November. Each of them, apart from RAMAGU—which won an audience choice award—was selected by an international jury, which included several members of the Académie des beaux-arts in Paris, such as the illustrator Emmanuel Guibert and the sculptor Eva Jospin; Vanja Kaludjercic, the director of the International Film Festival Rotterdam; and Antonija Letinić, a cultural development consultant in Croatia. Frédéric Jousset, the French entrepreneur and founder of Art Explora, was also a juror.

Cultura en Vena, who has been awarded €40,000, will puts the funds towards its Arte Ambulatorio project, which brings reproductions of works of art and other forms of creative programming into hospital spaces. The new phase of the project will involve an exhibition inspired by the work of Frida Kahlo, as well as live music sessions and creative writing workshops for children. Previous Arte Ambulatorio interventions focused on the work of the Spanish artists Francisco Goya and Joaquín Sorolla.

According to the organisation, “Frida Kahlo’s artistic universe offers a powerful entry point into themes closely connected to the hospital experience: the body, pain, vulnerability, identity, resilience, emotional expression and care.”

“Cultura en Vena takes art as seriously as care,” Jousset tells The Art Newspaper. He says the jury was convinced by “their rigour”. “They measure their impact and keep improving each exhibition based on patients’ and staff feedback,” he continues. “With over 23,000 peopkeople reached last year, they are one of the leading voices in arts and wellbeing in Spain.”

Bibliothèques Sans Frontières, meanwhile, will put its €50,000 award towards its L’autre retour project. The initiative involves workshops within which Ukrainian veterans are invited to draw, read and write with members of their community outside of a clinical environment, in an effort to help with reintegration after the military. The charity then plans to tour some of the works in Ukraine, as well as show them at the European Commission in Brussels.

“This project shows art as an act of rebuilding,” Jousset says.

The additional funded projects include developing touch-based choreography with blind and partially-sighted people in Poland (Jazdaa Polska!), making Lewisham in London the UK’s first “Borough of Literature” (London Writers Centre), and creating documentary theatre with minority groups in rural Slovakia (RAMAGU).
