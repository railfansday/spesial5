Apple's first-ever touchscreen MacBook is finally here! Find my hands-on impressions below, and scroll down for rolling coverage as reactions from critics and creators start to pour in across the web.

They said they wouldn't do it. A touchscreen MacBook has been rumoured since the dawn of time (well, the noughties), but, in a quote that's been infinitely dredged up across the internet over the last couple of weeks, Steve Jobs himself decried the concept as "ergonomically terrible". But Jobs also famously asked, "Who wants a stylus?" And the answer among millions of Apple Pencil users seems to be a resounding, "Us!"

And after a few days with the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display, I'm happy to report that this is very much a device—perhaps even the ultimate device—for creatives. But rather than the ability to touch the screen, it's all about the quality of that 'PROLED' display itself. Turns out there's a reason Apple decided to lead with that, and not touch, in the name of the device.

Simply put, it's the best laptop screen I've ever seen. With up to 2000 nits of peak brightness, 10,000,000:1 contrast, and P3 + Adobe RGB colour, this is a best-in-class display that puts every other OLED laptop screen I've used to shame. It gives creatives a portable reference-quality display, which means it recreates video, colour and contrast as faithfully as possible. Once you've used the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display, everything else looks washed-out in comparison, and for that reason alone it's impossible not to recommend for creatives – so long as the small matter of the $2,899 starting price isn't a barrier.

When it comes to touch, as I've written before, in my testing I've found it to be a nice-to-have rather than a need-to-have. Now that we're all touchscreen natives (which wasn't so much the case when Jobs made those comments in 2010), certain navigational actions just feel right, even on a laptop. Scrolling through windows, pinching to zoom in on a photo or map, scrubbing through the timeline in Final Cut Pro; there are plenty of times when touching the screen feels more intuitive than reaching for the trackpad. And Apple's subtle UI tweaks, such as having navigational icons expand when touched, make the whole thing feel more natural.

But equally, there were plenty of times when I preferred to use the trackpad – particularly when a certain level of accuracy was required. And despite Apple claiming to have produced a newly designed hinge with greater stability, it still wobbles. There's just no getting around the fact that a touchscreen on a vertical display is less ergonomic than it is on a flat one.

From a design perspective, the new MacBook Pro isn't a game-changer – unless you really missed the illuminating Apple logo (another noughties mainstay). There are improvements here and there; the Dynamic Island takes up less space than the notch, and slightly smaller bezels result in slightly larger viewing areas. But perhaps the most meaningful design change is the reduction in weight. That OLED panel has allowed for a 14% weight loss – making the 14-inch model, somewhat ironically, lighter than the 15-inch M5 MacBook Air.

But everything pales next to the screen. The PROLED XDR looks incredible. If you're looking for the most impressive visual fidelity available on a laptop, here it is. You can touch it if you like.

The MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR display is released tomorrow, with preorders available now. Prices start at $2899 for the 14-inch model, and $3399 for the 16-inch model.

Of course, it's a very pricey machine. But reportedly, Apple is trying to find ways to bring down the price in the future by using HMO thin-film transistor technology for OLED panels. It's a piece of tech also being considered by Samsung and LG to make OLED screens cheaper. Here's hoping.

What's that? You're looking for an ASMR unboxing of the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display? Whatever floats your boat.

If you're tempted by that awesome new display (or, if you like, the touchscreen), you can go ahead and order the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display at Amazon right now, starting at $2899.

We were lucky enough to attend the launch of the MacBook PROLED with XDR display in Los Angeles last week. There, among other things, we saw... Apple CEO John Ternus signing iPhones? Sure. Okay, then.

Our sister site T3 is also waxing lyrical about the new MacBook Pro, but this time it isn't the touchscreen or the XDR display that's getting the most rapturous response – it's the reduction in weight:

"I brought my personal M3 MacBook Pro to the hands-on to compare it to, and hefting them both side-by-side, the loss of around 300g of weight between generations is really quite significant. On the one hand, you might not notice it explicitly... but everything adds up, and it makes a genuine difference."

It seems that even after a short time, there's something of a critical consensus: come for the touchscreen, stay for the display.

And the YouTubers' reviews are making their way online too. Here's renowned Apple fan/creator iJustine's take. Like many, she's a fan of the glowing Apple logo.

And here's our sister site Tech Radar with, again, a very similar take: "While all the talk is about the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display being the first MacBook with a touchscreen, the touchscreen is arguably not its most interesting feature; it works fine, but macOS is still an operating system built for traditional laptop inputs. Instead, it’s the gorgeous tandem OLED screen and industry-leading battery life that should get you excited here."

The Verge's review is up, and it seems they share our opinion: the screen is incredible, the thinner and lighter design is welcome, and touch is... there. "Even if you love the touchscreen, you’ll use it a lot for a day or so and go back to using your laptop almost exactly the way you did before."

Hello and welcome to Creative Bloq's coverage of the new MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display. We'll be keeping this page updated with opinions from across the web, but for now, what do we think? In short, touch is nice, but it's that PROLED display that's the killer here. It's the brightest (and darkest) laptop screen we've ever used – and for creatives, having a portable reference-quality display could be a game-changer.
