red corrugated metal and pine plywood wrap czech village house’s extension

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

in nečín village, facha architekti renovates a converted pigsty and weekend cabin with a self-built timber extension that expands living spaces toward a stepped larch terrace.

LINKA News edited by sofia lekka angelopoulou November 16, 2026 4 min

Located on the edge of the village of Nečín in the Příbram region of the Czech Republic, House With a Red Addition is the renovation and extension of a compact family residence by Facha architekti. Originally built as a single-story pigsty and subsequently topped with a weekend cabin before becoming a permanent home, the structure retains visible markers of its incremental growth. Rather than unifying the building under a single envelope, the studio designed the new addition as a distinct architectural layer, defined by its sharp geometric form and a ventilated facade clad in red corrugated metal sheets.

The timber-framed extension expands the primary living zone toward the garden while relocating the main entrance and integrating built-in storage. On the west elevation, a large sliding window connects the living room to a larch terrace featuring wide seating steps and a retractable textile awning. Shifting the entrance into the new volume enabled a comprehensive reconfiguration of the raised ground floor, allowing for an enlarged bathroom finished in cement-based plaster and a redesigned kitchen. While the basement and attic were left largely intact, the original volume was thermally insulated, fitted with new timber windows, reroofed in corrugated metal, and upgraded with an air-to-water heat pump. Inside, pine plywood lines both the interior walls of the extension and custom-built furniture, paired with natural linoleum flooring and a rotating wood-burning stove. Consistent with Facha architekti’s hands-on methodology, the architects constructed the timber structure, metal cladding, and interior joinery directly alongside the clients.
