Decorate Your Wedding for Less – Pick a Pretty Venue and Reuse the Ceremony Pieces

The cheapest wedding decorations are the ones you never buy or rent. Book a venue that looks good on its own and ask what it includes. Then move your ceremony arch, aisle decorations, and signs to the reception, and borrow or buy secondhand before you rent anything.

Decor costs climb with every rental and every craft-store run, and a plain room takes more of both to look like a wedding.

A garden, a historic building, or a hall with good lighting and its own linens needs little decor, and anything the venue includes is one less thing to rent. Every piece you do bring can serve the ceremony and then the reception for one price.

For whatever is still missing, borrow first, buy secondhand next, and rent last. Say a rental shop quotes $1,200 for an arch, aisle lanterns, signs, and table runners. Borrow the lanterns from friends and buy a recent couple’s arch, signs, and runners for $300, and you pay $900 less than the $1,200 quote, though you’ll haul and set up the pieces yourself.

Before you sign with a venue, ask for its list of what comes with the room, such as linens, candle holders, easels, or string lights. Ask for its rules on candles and setup times, too. Many venues allow candles only inside glass that rises above the flame, and two hours of setup time rules out decor that takes all afternoon to put together.

Then plan where each ceremony piece goes at the reception: the arch behind the head table, the aisle lanterns along the bar, and the welcome sign at the door. Ask a friend to move them during cocktail hour, while you’re taking photos.

Couples sell the decor they used once they’re married, so search Facebook Marketplace and local wedding resale groups for the pieces on your list, and sell yours there afterward. Light the reception with string lights and candles in place of extra flowers. Skip any do-it-yourself project whose supplies cost about as much as renting the finished piece, since you’d put in the hours and save nothing.

On your wedding-day schedule, list each piece that moves and the friend moving it, so the arch is behind the head table before your guests sit down.
