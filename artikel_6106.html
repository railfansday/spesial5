Dusit Takes Low-Risk Bet on Yangon as Others Retreat

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Dusit, a Thai hotels and hospitality group, is entering Myanmar as others write off projects and close hotels, but on terms that limit its exposure: management contracts on existing hotels, a focus on business travel, and outside checks on the owner. If leisure demand stays weak, the owner bears most of the cost. Dusit’s main risk is reputational.

Dusit Hotels and Resorts is taking over three of Yangon's best-known hotels, even though the Thai group says it does not expect Myanmar's international leisure market to recover any time soon.

The company opened The Strand Hotel Yangon – Dusit Collection and ASAI Yangon Sule under its management last week. Dusit Hotel Inya Lake Yangon is due to follow, the company said.

"We recognise that Myanmar presents a complex and challenging operating environment, and we do not anticipate a significant recovery in international leisure tourism in the near term," Chanin Donavanik, group CEO of Dusit International, told Skift.

The Inya Lake project also marks a return. Dusit first entered Myanmar in 2002, when it took over management of the Inya Lake Hotel.

Dusit International has taken over management of three established Yangon hotels — The Strand Hotel Yangon – Dusit Collection and ASAI Yangon Sule opened last week, with Dusit Hotel Inya Lake Yangon to follow — while saying it does not expect Myanmar's international leisure market to recover soon. Group CEO Chanin Donavanik said Dusit is targeting established corporate travel demand and described the move as asset-light expansion into a neighboring country, with management contracts that leave the owner carrying the buildings and any weak trading. All three hotels share one owning company that Dusit has not named; Chanin said an independent third-party law firm ran due diligence on the owner and its business partners, a step that matters in a market where foreign hotel groups have faced scrutiny over owners' links to the military. The backdrop is a market far off its peak: Myanmar recorded 973,263 international visitors in 2025 against an official 1.8 million target for this year, versus roughly 4.7 million before the pandemic and the 2021 coup. Hongkong and Shanghai Hotels wrote off its stalled Yangon project with a $20.5 million impairment in February 2025, and half of Yangon's 12 five-star hotels had closed by mid-2023. Horwath HTL's Matt Gebbie said leisure demand will not return until government travel advisory language softens, because insurance follows the advisories.
