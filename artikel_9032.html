The Musso E80 Muse is a chair built with women in mind, and it shows. Designed to fit anyone from 4ft 9 to 5ft 11, it solved problems I didn’t realise I had. The soft, silky materials feel a world away from the usual brutalist office chair, and the lumbar support is the best I’ve tried: my lower back ache disappeared after a few days. The levers and knobs feel a bit plasticky, and it took a while to find my perfect position, but once I got there – wow. Well worth trying if you’re on the smaller side.

As most people know, most products are tested on men. From pharmaceuticals to seatbelts, women’s bodies are rarely taken into account in product creation. I’ll admit I’d never considered that my office chair feels just a bit too big for me (and it's got slightly the wrong proportions), but it’s true. Unlike the pink Biro (remember that?), an office chair is a case where a woman’s version could genuinely be considered, so I was happy to try the Musso E80 Muse. The chair has been built with women in mind in a host of ways, from its size and proportions (it’s designed to fit anyone from 4ft 9 to 5ft 11 – I'm 5 ft 5, fyi) to its materials. With all that in mind, it’s almost great. It has a couple of compromises at this price point, but I think they're worth making.

The Muse comes in soft colours: Blush, Pink, Grey and White. It’s a palette removed from the usual office chair offerings, and I won’t comment on the ‘girls like pink’ association. It’s nice to have something that looks more in keeping with my office than the brutalist chairs I’ve had before. The materials are silky smooth and soft, which is pretty delightful.

I’m not saying men wouldn’t want a chair made of something more comfortable and plush than the standard mesh of an ergonomic chair, but sitting on this one was a lightbulb moment. Other upgrades include the flex when you lean back, a handy adjustable footstool, and a much softer, more adjustable lumbar support than on any chair I’ve tried before.

As mentioned, the proportions are different – it has a shorter backrest and the chair can go down further for shorter legs. All of this is fully adjustable so, in theory, you can find the perfect fit.

So that’s the positives for design. The minus points go to some of the other material aspects. The levers and knobs feel brittle and plasticky when you adjust them, which makes me wonder if they could have been sturdier, made from slightly more premium materials. At this price point, I can see they’ve put the money into the other materials, which is understandable.

I’ll start by saying that at first I wasn’t convinced. It took me a long time to adjust the chair into the perfect position and I didn’t think I’d get there. But let me tell you, once I did – wow.

The chair has a lot more ‘give’ than others I’ve tried, allowing you to move around, and I feel much more supported, especially by the lumbar support. It took a while to get into position, but I feel its benefit more than with any other chair. I didn’t realise how supported my lower back could feel. I’m not giving a medical opinion, but my lower back ache disappeared after a few days in the Muse, which was a big surprise.

Let’s discuss seat height. Until I lowered this chair all the way down, I had NO IDEA how I could be sitting. My legs usually dangle, so I put them on a block, but being able to drop the seat all the way and then move it back and forward for the perfect support was a revelation.

This could also be the first time my head has felt supported: I can finally move the headrest all the way forward to meet my head.

The soft materials make a huge difference to how it feels to sit in the chair. It feels like the chair is rising up to support me, rather than the hard, unyielding feeling I didn’t realise my old chair was giving me.

At $369, this chair sits at the lower end of mid-range. It's an accessible price point, and it delivers a lot of benefit for certain people at this price. To keep it accessible, and to focus on the things that matter, Musso has compromised on some of the materials (levers, the finish of some of the plastic parts), but the materials that matter – the seat, arms, lumbar support pad and soft mesh, have lovely attention to detail. I think it's priced about right when you see what else is on the market.

If you have always found office chairs to be unforgiving, slightly the wrong size and shape, and unsightly then this could be a good option for you. Of course, you don't have to be a woman to enjoy these benefits, so if you fall into the height range I'd give it a go.
