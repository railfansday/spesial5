Buy your next rental where rent is high against home prices. In 15 Midwest and Southern metros, a typical house rents for 7.4% to 11.2% of its value a year. Before you offer, check the city’s taxes, insurance, and vacancy, and what houses on that street actually rent for.

That share, a year’s rent divided by the price, is the gross yield, and it decides whether rent can cover a mortgage. In Zillow’s October 2026 figures, a typical house in the San Diego area rents for about 5% of its value a year, while one in the McAllen, Texas, area rents for 11.2%.

Now figure the mortgage payment on each, with 25% down and a 30-year loan at 7%. A typical McAllen-area house, worth $189,000, rents for about $1,760 a month, roughly $820 more than its $943 payment. A typical San Diego-area house, worth about $1 million, rents for about $4,130, about $860 short of its payment of nearly $5,000.

At those terms, the typical rent is higher than the mortgage payment in all 15 metros below, by about $270 a month in Little Rock up to about $820 in McAllen. That margin still has to pay the property taxes and insurance, plus the repairs, empty months, and management every landlord budgets for, so those costs decide whether a house makes money.

Since October 2023, typical rents have risen faster than typical home prices, so the yield on a typical U.S. house has climbed from 7.0% to 7.4%.

Property taxes come out of the rent every year, and several states on this list tax a rental more heavily than an owner’s home. Texas’s homestead exemptions, which shield part of a home’s value from tax, go only to an owner’s main home, so a rental in McAllen or El Paso is taxed on its full value, the state comptroller explains. Michigan charges a rental up to 18 mills, or $18 per $1,000 of taxable value, in school operating taxes that an owner’s home skips, according to its Treasury Department.

In South Carolina, a rental is assessed at 6% of its value, against 4% for an owner’s home, and pays school operating taxes the owner’s home skips, as Richland County, home to Columbia, explains. Indiana caps a rental’s yearly property tax at 2% of its assessed value, against 1% for an owner’s home, according to the state’s Department of Local Government Finance.

Insurance is the other big cost that varies by state. In Insurance.com’s 2026 study of quoted rates for a standard policy with $300,000 of coverage on the house, Nebraska, Oklahoma, and Kansas averaged more than $5,000 a year, about twice the $2,872 national average. Texas averaged $4,582. Get a quote on a landlord policy for the exact house before you offer.

Landlord law matters as well. Oregon, Washington, and California cap yearly rent increases statewide, while 36 states bar their cities from adopting rent control at all, according to the National Apartment Association, a landlords’ trade group.

Every number above describes a whole metro, which mixes blocks where houses rent quickly with blocks where they sit empty. Before you offer, compare the rent the seller quotes with what similar houses nearby actually rent for. If you’re buying in another state, have a local agent or property manager pull those rents for you.

Start with the two or three metros whose typical prices fit your budget, and run one real listing in each through the mortgage test above, using its own tax bill and insurance quote.
