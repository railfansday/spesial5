Welcome to this week's Chutes & Ladders, our roundup of hirings, firings and retirings throughout the industry. Please submit the good news—or the bad—from your shop, and we will feature it here each week.

Carl S. Armato, president and CEO of Winston-Salem, North Carolina-based Novant Health, will retire at the end of this year after 15 years leading the system.

Armato joined the nonprofit in 1998 as vice president of finance and operations in its Charlotte and Winston-Salem physician divisions, and held increasing regional and organization-wide leadership roles over subsequent years.

He's credited with boosting access points and unifying Novant's "collection of strong regional organizations into a unified, innovative health system." He also headed substantial growth, with the organization today counting 45,000 employees, 22 hospitals and about 900 total locations.

With his retirement, current Executive Vice President and Chief Operating Officer John Gizdic will become president and interim CEO on Jan. 1, 2027. His resume includes over 30 years of healthcare leadership, including more than 20 with Novant.

> UnitedHealthcare has named Suha Assi as CEO of individual Medicare Advantage, per a LinkedIn post.

Assi previously spent nearly a decade as executive director for Medicare and Retirement in Tennessee, and she previously held roles based in the state for Cigna. She wrote in the post that the "relationships with our teams, providers and communities have shaped how I think about this business and, most importantly, the people we serve."

"As I step into this national role, I’m excited about what’s ahead for Medicare Advantage," she wrote. "I look forward to working alongside teams and provider partners across the country to modernize how we work together, strengthen relationships, and find new ways to deliver for our members."

> Daryl Tol, president and CEO of General Catalyst's Health Assurance Transformation Company (HATCo) is stepping down from the role, the venture capital firm's CEO Hemant Taneja shared Tuesday.

Tol had been with the firm for about four and a half years in the wake of his time as president and CEO of AdventHealth's Central Florida Division. During that time, he headed HATCo's partner and advisory network—helping grow it to nearly 40 providers, payers and other portfolio companies—and took the company's top spot partway through 2025. Of note, he helped HATCo close its acquisition of regional health system Summa Health and served as its interim CEO for much of the past year until the appointment of Jennifer Eslinger last month.

Tol is being succeeded by Reva Nohria, a founding member of the HATCo team who has been with General Catalyst since 2018. Taneja, in his announcement, said her work at both organizations has encompassed investment, company creation and strategic partnership, and that she will continue as an investor at the venture capital firm alongside her new appointment.

Tol will support a smooth leadership transition and remain in an advisory role, a representative of General Catalyst told Fierce Healthcare.

> Jennifer Shuford, M.D., is leaving her post as deputy director and chief medical officer of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention on Nov. 12, just four months after joining, according to a Washington Post report that was confirmed by the agency on Tuesday.

The agency told press in a statement that Shuford was departing "to spend more time with family." Her appointment was announced by President Donald Trump in April, and she joined in June.

Shuford is an infectious disease specialist who came to the federal stage from a role as commissioner of the Texas Department of State Health Services and before that, served as the state's chief epidemiologist. Notably, she was credited with heading Texas' response to a measles outbreak last year. Her departure comes as the CDC clashes with state health departments and other public health leaders over its response to a growing national measles outbreak.

Satish Pillai, M.D., is slated to fill the role in the interim as the agency seeks a replacement, the CDC said.

> Johns Hopkins Health System CEO Kevin Sowers is set to retire at the end of 2027.

Sowers has served as president and CEO of the Johns Hopkins system since 2018. He also previously spent more than 32 years as a leader within the Duke University Health System, including eight as CEO of Duke University Hospital.

The early announcement of his retirement plans gives Johns Hopkins significant time to conduct a search for a successor, with Theodore DeWeese, dean of the medical faculty and CEO of Johns Hopkins Medicine, leading the charge. Sowers will become a strategic adviser to Johns Hopkins to support the transition, the health system said.

"When I joined Johns Hopkins Medicine in 2018, I made a commitment to the organization and to myself to give 10 years of everything I had to this extraordinary place—to the talented, dedicated people who work here, and the people and communities we serve. And I intend to keep that commitment," Sowers said in a video message.

> Calley Means, a prominent senior advisor to Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. closely aligned with the Make America Healthy Again Movement, resigned from his role on Thursday.

The resignation was reported by the Wall Street Journal and confirmed by Means on social media.

Media reports from the past several months described Means' frustrations seeing MAHA policy priorities delayed or blunted by others in the administration, with the journal citing sources describing him as "disillusioned." Still, his time advising the secretary notched several wins for the movement, including overhauled dietary guidelines, steps toward greater food ingredient transparency and loosened restrictions on psychedelics as potential medical treatments.

Means wrote in his confirmation post that he still holds "tremendous respect for President Trump and Bobby Kennedy" in their battles against "the entrenched, dark forces in D.C. that profit from Americans getting sick."

"More policy and cultural change has happened around these goals in the past two years than any time in history—and I know this movement is just getting started," he wrote.
