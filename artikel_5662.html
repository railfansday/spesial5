90 New Vehicle Models Were Just Introduced in Colombia’s Largest Automotive Exhibition. 55 Were Electric.

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The last two weeks, the future was on display in Bogota’s Automobile Hall.

This Hall is Colombia’s largest and most important Automotive Exhibition, and since 2023 (17th edition, held after a hiatus of 5 years), it has heralded the unstoppable tide of EVs progressively taking over the national market. Back then, despite EVs making up less than 3% of the market, EVs were already present and relevant in the Automobile Hall, with the Changan Lumin being a particularly bright star.

EVs have been gaining ground since, with more of them arriving every year, and in the latest iteration of the Automobile Hall, we’ve reached a point of no return: EVs make up a majority of the newly announced models arriving in the country. Or to be specific: out of 90 arrivals, EVs account for 55!

32 brands brought new electric or plug-in hybrid cars to showcase in Bogota’s Automobile Hall. Even though most of them (unsurprisingly) are from China, we still find presences from 7 European and 2 Japanese brands here — all will be listed at the end of this article with their respective models.

Most of these models arrive at very competitive pricing, many times undercutting their direct gasoline and diesel competition, and with ever-improving specs — by reasonable standards, most of these cars should be able to make at least 350 km on Colombian roads, making them quite appealing options even for people who travel regularly.

Colombia’s EV market share has been growing in 2026, reaching 20% — or almost — every month since March. But with this EV avalanche (or EV-lanche), I think it’s inevitable that we will see that number go up in the months to come — though how fast exactly, I leave to you to debate.

And no, you were not the only ones to notice that there are exactly zero (0) arrivals from US brands. Tesla was of course present, but with nothing new (though they did present the Cybercab at a later date than I attended), other than Tesla, US brands are either too small or too focused on gas-guzzlers to bother even trying to compete.

Note: all prices mentioned here include taxes, but registration and insurance may not be included.

I will start with the non-Chinese EVs, because, well, they’re fewer.

The most important model here, by far, is the Renault 4 e-Tech. People outside this country probably don’t know that the Renault 4 was built here for 22 years, becoming the most affordable car for decades and a symbol of the Colombian middle class. Nicknamed the “Colombian Car” and the “Loyal Friend,” the Renault 4 has a place in the hearth of most Colombians, and, as such, the R4 e-Tech was one of the most visited cars in the entire exhibition.

Because of this, it’s certain that the car will have some buyers. But at 100 million COP (€26,000, $28,500), it’s a hard sell, particularly when the hatchback segment has stabilized somewhere around $20,000 for Chinese models. Nostalgia has a price for sure, but not that many people will be willing to pay $8,500 for it.

The BMW iX3 was also present in the hall, and I must admit it’s a very compelling and impressive car, but at 300 million COP ($85,400), there are not a lot of buyers that will be interested.

From the European side, other than Renault and BMW, we have the Cupra Terramar and the Mini Cooper SE Paul Smith Edition, both around 160 million COP (€41,700, $45,500). So, quite expensive for their segments, even though I find the Terramar a very beautiful car. The Porsche Cayenne is of course expensive beyond anything seen prior, and the Volvo EX60 has not announced pricing yet. As for Stellantis, it’s betting on its two vans, somewhat expensive but still viable, and it has the Opel Crossland EV, which has been on the market for a while but is now discounted to 90 million COP (€23,400, $25,600), meaning it’s one of the most competitive non-Chinese EVs available.

Regarding the Japanese, the Toyota BZ4X (already present) and the RAV4 PHEV (just arriving) are of course important players, but at 220 million and 270 million COP respectively ($62,600 and $76,800), they won’t sell more than a trickle of units. The Nissan Frontier Pro has the same price as the BZ4X, and, to be fair, I didn’t even notice it was available until I left the Hall.

Paradoxically, the most competitive non-Chinese EVs all come from a brand with zero arrivals: Kia. With its EV2 starting at 90 million COP and its EV3 at 120 million COP ($23,400 and $34,100), Kia has the closest prices to any Chinese competitor and can at least rely on the brand’s popularity to keep some of the market.

With that out of the way, let’s look at the Chinese arrivals, or at least some of them, as there are too many to mention.

You can see the full list of models at the end of the article. Here I will focus on those I think will make the most impact in the market.

First, one of my favorite EVs: the all-mighty Chery ICaur V23. In a previous article I mentioned how surprising its low range was … but I must admit I judged it rashly. I mean, that thing is huge. I really expected it to be much smaller, and thus I understand why it can barely edge a 360 km range (WLTP) despite a sizeable 81 kWh battery. When you’re built like a small tank, you can expect to get the range of a small tank, no surprises here.

But the great selling point for this car is its price: 85 million COP for the 4×2 version and 105 million COP for the 4×4 version ($24,100 and $29,900). We’re talking a fully off-road capable SUV for the same or less than a Renault 4 or a Corolla. Truly impressive, and an example of EV innovation in segments that many other OEMs have not dared enter, assuming they are lost to the ICEV competition.

BYD also surprised by bringing a new Sealion RWD, armed with a 70 kWh battery and aimed directly against the Model Y. At 115 million COP ($32,700), this car is $1,500 cheaper than the Model Y RWD while having a larger battery and better range. It seems the Chinese giant wants to start getting back some market share a year after the US brand took the market by storm. Better late than never I guess.

BYD is also bringing the EREV Leopard 7 under its own brand (BYD Ti7), but at 225 million COP ($64,000), it will certainly not be a best seller, even though it will probably give the likes of Toyota and Land Rover a run for their money.

Now, my dear readers, I hope you will forgive me here, but even though I actually went to the Hall to take a look to the XPeng Mona L03 specifically, it seems I got distracted by the robot and the drone-licopter, and thus this is the picture you’re getting … because I forgot to take one of the L03. Regardless, the XPeng L03 is also pointing directly at Tesla, arriving at 115 million COP ($32,700), the same price of the entry-level Model 3, except the L03 provides full self-driving capabilities for this price, to the extent that XPeng can provide that of course. This car (available in BEV and PHEV versions) and the BYD Sealion are poised to bring a significant challenge not only to Tesla, but to the entire ICEV ecosystem, which is having evermore competition in its bread-and-butter segments by the month.

There are currently 6 subcompact entry-level SUVs fighting in the 80-to-95 million COP segment: the Chery Omoda E5, the MG S5, the Dongfeng Vigo, the Faw Bestune Joy, the Changan Nevo Q05, and the Ora 5.

I chose the Ora 5 to represent the segment because it offers the largest battery (58kWh, vs 50kWh for all the others) and, as of the Automobile Hall, the lowest price for the entry-level version, at only 73 million COP (USD $20.800). This is a true bargain, making it the cheapest available SUV of any powertrain by quite some distance, and a very compelling option for budget-oriented buyers. It remains to be seen if this price will remain after the Hall.

Going by specs alone, I believe the JMEV GSX to be the best value-for-money vehicle currently available in the Colombian market. This SUV boasts an impressive 70 kWh battery with 500 km WLTP range for a mere 90 million COP ($25,600). The only thing against it is that JMEV (JMC) is quite a small brand and has not gained traction in this market, or in any other that I know of.

In Colombia, this minicar has taken the place that belonged to the Wuling Hongguang in China, and more recently belongs to the Chery QQ Ice Cream — that of a (barely) highway-capable minicar that may seat four, or two plus your groceries, but that will fit in any spot and can be parked wherever you may find a square meter available. Available with a brand new 18.1 kWh battery, the Faw Xiaoma 2.0 remains the most affordable “true” car in Colombia at 40 million COP ($11,400).

It’s worth noting that Faw has failed to gain traction in the Colombian market, and it clearly wanted to change that in this Hall, as it brought an entire armada, with its N09 Sedan available for 100 million COP ($28,300) as its flagship car.

Aside from minicars (like the Xiaoma above), the Dongfeng Box deserves a mention for being the cheapest car in the hatchback segment at 65 million COP ($18,500). With its 42 kWh battery, it can reasonably travel long distance with the current infrastructure. I’ve ridden in both this car and the Geely EX2, and, frankly, the EX2 has a higher quality feel, but this one is slightly cheaper, and no doubt this will bring some buyers.

At a price of only 75 million COP ($21,350), the Foton e-Wonder mini-truck provides full price parity with diesel versions and opens the possibility of electrification for the owners of smaller companies. At 1-ton carrying capacity, it’s a very compelling vehicle, even if its 40 kWh battery remains limited for long-distance operations.

Of course, this list would not be complete without the most affordable “real” car here (so, not minicars included, sorry Xiaoma!). The Jac E10X is a city-car available for 60 million COP ($17,000), and even though it looks good and has decent quality, it has never sold well because JAC is not a very popular brand, and because its 30 kWh battery lacks capacity for making even medium-distance trips without having at least some range anxiety.

Well, this is it. My apologies for the fans of Arcfox and Leapmotor — both are very important brands arriving in the country, but Arcfox lacks recognition and prices are a bit over the competition (they are a luxury brand, but that may not convince people who don’t already know them) and Leapmotor only brought EREVs, and as you may have seen, I focused on BEVs in the very long Chinese list.

My conclusions are probably quite predictable. China is leading and by quite an advantage, the Koreans are not too far behind, the Europeans are trying to make a comeback, the Japanese are making a symbolic (but useless) stand, and the Americans — other than Tesla — didn’t even bother to show up.

It’s hard to believe only three years have passed since Bogota’s Automobile Hall of 2023, when the Changan Lumin was the star and when we were celebrating going over 2% market share. Back then, EVs were still expensive, charging infrastructure was much more limited, and the mainstream was firmly on the pure-combustion side, with hybrids making some inroads.

Now, after the EV-lanche we’re seeing, it seems that EVs are now fully in the mainstream, with lower prices and better specs than ever before. EV sales will keep growing fast, and I expect the market to tilt even more to the side of zero-emissions transportation in the coming months. 30% BEV market share is now well within sight.
