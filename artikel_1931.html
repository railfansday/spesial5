Derek McInnes hails Rangers ‘big horse’ and reveals eye-opening stat from Motherwell win

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Derek McInnes labelled Ryan Naderi “a big horse” and a textbook example of how to lead the line as a centre-forward after helping Rangers secure a hard-fought 2-1 Premiership victory over Motherwell.

The German striker has been growing in confidence in recent weeks and once again demonstrated his importance to the Ibrox side, combining tireless pressing and intelligent movement which resulted in a scrappy late 88th-minute winner at Fir park.

McInnes believes Naderi's contribution extends far beyond his goals, insisting his work rate should not overshadow the quality of his all-round performances.

“Ryan’s game is really strong at the minute. He looks like a very confident boy and so he should be,” McInnes said. “Wrongly now, when you think of Ryan or people ask me, it’s about him knocking his pan in. His pressing is unbelievable and he’s a big horse up there. He runs and works and puts people under pressure.

“He looks tired but his performances are always strong. But he gets goals and has an influence. In that system tonight, his was the most important position for us, in terms of running channels and getting hold of the ball. His touch was one of a really confident player and he led the line brilliantly. It was a proper number nine performance.”

On his side’s performance, McInnes added: “We got our just rewards in the game, absolutely. I thought it was a very good, strong performance and I’ve been told that every one of my outfield players had a shot tonight. I’ve never known a team who practice so much shooting, and crossing and finishing during the week.

“It was the only thing missing for us, I thought it was a top class performance. We mixed the game up well. Our press was strong and we were aggressive. I’m just disappointed it took so long to get the winning goal.”

Deflated Motherwell boss Alfred Johansson admitted his side struggled to cope with Rangers' early intensity, but felt the early loss of Ibrahim Said to injury was a major blow before his side ran out of energy in the closing stages.

“They are title challengers and we have a really tough start to the game,” he said. “They put us down. When we win it back, we give it away again. They get momentum. I don't think it's a free kick that they get before their first goal, but we should defend much, much better on that defensive corner kick.

“In that moment, in the same period, we lose Ibrahim [Said], which is really tough for us, given that the players we have on the bench, many of them in the offensive positions, are not ready to play more than 30 to 45 minutes with high intensity. So it was tough to lose him, and therefore, it's strong of the players to come back and score a goal from a set play and equalise.

“But the last 15-20 minutes, Rangers take completely over and we don't have energy enough in that moment to keep the intensity up.”
