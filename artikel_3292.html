BBC Deputy Boss Questions Impact Of “Creative Choices Taken On West Coast Of United States” On UK Audiences

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

“Why are so many of the creative choices that impact audiences being taken on the West Coast of the United States?”

That was the question posed today by the Deputy Director General of the BBC, who raised concerns that UK broadcasters are losing their “grip on our creative autonomy” in a world of streamers, Silicon Valley tech giants and YouTube.

“I worry about the UK’s own voice,” Rhodri Talfan Davies told the British Screen Forum Conference in London. “I think about my own childhood and how that was dominated by UK storytelling and a sense of place and all those things. His comments came during a conference in which autonomy from the U.S. has repeatedly been raised, with both Culture Minister Ian Murray and Aardman boss Sean Clarke airing concerns about the impact of the proposed federal film and TV tax incentive on British production. Watch on Deadline

While it makes lots of its programming with American co-producers, the BBC has a “big part” to play in battling against this loss of autonomy, Talfan Davies said. This can partly be done by meeting audiences where they are “even if you believe that social media at its worst can divide people, feed lies and breed a lack of understanding of one another.”

The corporation needs to improve at shortform content aimed at young people, he said, noting how commissioners and producers are now treating this content “on a par” with longform, which represents a sea-change from six-to-12 month ago.

Soon, Talfan Davies said audiences will be able to watch more vodcasts on iPlayer, with the “traditional boundaries” between BBC iPlayer and podcast platform BBC Sounds “starting to blur because frankly it’s blurring for audiences.”

Meanwhile, with £300M ($394M) worth of savings still required as part of the brutal savings plan, Talfan Davies said genres like current affairs may head in a similar direction to factual entertainment with a focus on less shows that make a “bigger impact.”

BBC in “different place” since Brand commited his crimes

On Russell Brand and uprooting industry culture, Talfan Davies said “we’ve still got work to do,” especially in terms of dealing with complaints speedily.

“No one in the sector thinks the work is done but compared to where we were 10 to 15 years ago it feels in a very different place,” he added.

Talfan Davies was delivering a keynote at the British Screen Forum in London before Ken Loach’s producing partner Rebecca O’Brien.
