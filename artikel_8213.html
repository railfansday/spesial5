Gear Review: I Tried One of Those Thru-Hiking Bidets Everyone’s Been Talking About, and Now My Butt Really Burns. Like a Lot.

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Review by The Pointless Up and Down (PUD) News @PUDThruHikingNews. This article is satire. The events and quotations described are fictional.

Any thru-hiker who has met a backpacking bidet user knows the passion they hold for their backcountry sanitation kit. In fact, it’s been scientifically proven that ultralight bidet users are going to tell you about their bidets “whether you fucking want them to or not” (Scientific Study, 2026).

So what’s the appeal? Bidet users cite a cleaner butt and toilet-paper savings as key reasons to adopt a hiking bidet. Enthralled by passionate testimonials, I decided to put one to the test.

On the (w)hole, I was impressed by the bidet’s ability to clean my behind, and I used much less toilet paper. However, I am shocked that nobody has mentioned the almost unbearable burning sensation that follows the bidet’s noxious blast.

Photo by Arne Nordmann, via Wikimedia Commons.

I took 2 one-week backpacking trips: one with no bidet, one with the hiking bidet. I began each with the same amount of toilet paper and I ate the exact same diet so I could scientifically control my bidet-using conditions. I ingested no gastrointestinal medication on either hike — again, for science.

Ranger demonstration at an annual bear spray bidet training in Red Rock Lakes National Wildlife Refuge. Photo: USFWS / public domain.

The hiking bidet followed through on the promises of less toilet paper usage and a much cleaner butt. However, I was shocked how much this thing burned. It blasted away fecal matter, yes, but it did a number on me. Oof. A week after my test, I am still feeling the effects.

There is something to this (w)hole bidet thing, but I implore bidet makers to consider making one that blasts liquid lower on the Scoville Scale. I’m no expert, but maybe just water or something?

–Jim “Rube” Cruthers, Gear Editor, The Pointless Up & Down (PUD)
