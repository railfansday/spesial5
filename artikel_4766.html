How to Find the Optimal Romantic Partner

Choosing an optimal romantic partner is complex. We should consider the many factors and potential partners. Two distinctions are particularly important. The first is between the best person and the most compatible partner. The second is between dealbreakers (traits we seek to avoid) and dealmakers (traits we actively seek). The first distinction concerns the kind of person we are looking for; the second concerns the specific positive and negative traits we consider important. Both distinctions matter when choosing a partner wisely.

"If I had to dream up the perfect woman, she wouldn't even come close to you." — Boy Meets World

Potential partners are abundant in the romantic marketplace. We can assess their overall romantic value along two major dimensions: a nonrelational scale and a relational scale.

The nonrelational scale measures the value of traits considered independently of any particular relationship. These may include attractiveness, wisdom, intelligence, and kindness. This assessment has two advantages: It is relatively easy to assess, and people are likely to agree broadly about the value of such traits. By contrast, the relational scale measures a person's suitability within a specific relationship. It depends not only on each individual's characteristics, but also on the unique connection between them and their personal and environmental circumstances. Consequently, relational suitability is much more difficult to assess.

Consider long-term relationships. Should you marry an intelligent person? In general, intelligence is highly desirable. However, compatibility complicates the picture. A very large gap between partners' intellectual abilities may reduce their relational suitability. Similar considerations apply to many other traits and accomplishments. In enduring love, compatibility often matters more than excellence considered in isolation.

"Love thy neighbor. And if he happens to be tall, debonair, and devastating, it will be that much easier." — Mae West

"The one thing that is always a dealbreaker is a lack of physical attractiveness. Even the best personality in the world cannot seem to overcome that hurdle." — A man

Romantic search involves more than assessing overall value and compatibility. It also entails identifying specific virtues and flaws. Traits that discourage a relationship are commonly called dealbreakers. Examples include hostility, arrogance, abusiveness, excessive clinginess, poor hygiene, lack of ambition, and physical unattractiveness. Traits that encourage a relationship are known as dealmakers. Typical examples include intelligence, kindness, warmth, humor, physical attractiveness, social status, and financial stability. Everyone has strengths and weaknesses; romantic decisions depend heavily on the importance we assign to different dealmakers and dealbreakers.

Research consistently indicates that, when evaluating potential partners, people tend to weigh negative traits more heavily than positive ones. This finding reflects a broader psychological tendency: negative experiences generally affect us more strongly than positive ones. A positive characteristic may enrich our lives, but a serious negative characteristic can undermine an entire relationship (Ben-Ze’ev, A., 2019).

Dealbreakers are particularly influential when people consider long-term committed relationships rather than brief sexual encounters. Nevertheless, evolutionary pressures have likely shaped mate preferences to remain sensitive to both short-term and long-term mating strategies. Research further suggests that women are generally less tolerant of undesirable traits and more likely than men to classify various characteristics as dealbreakers in a mate-selection context (Csajbók and Berkics, 2022; Jonason and colleagues, 2015, 2022).

A practical strategy is to identify approximately five dealmakers and five dealbreakers that matter most to you. When most of your desired dealmakers are present, you have good reasons to pursue a relationship; when several significant dealbreakers are present, you may justify reconsidering it. Of course, romantic life rarely follows rigid rules. A relationship may flourish even when a partner possesses only some of the desired dealmakers or exhibits one or two dealbreakers. A lot depends on personal circumstances, individual priorities, and the severity of undesirable traits.

"I never promised you a rose garden. Along with the sunshine, there's got to be a little rain sometime." — Lynn Anderson

Finding an optimal long-term romantic partner requires focusing primarily on compatibility and avoiding serious dealbreakers. This does not mean ignoring positive attributes. Rather, the first step is to identify and avoid major obstacles to a healthy relationship. Once these obstacles fade, partners can build a harmonious and flourishing romantic bond through their strengths, shared values, and mutual commitment.
