Mark Tildesley Dies: ‘No Time to Die’, ‘F1: The Movie’ & London Olympics Production Designer Was 63

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Mark Tildesley, one of the industry’s most respected production designers, has passed away aged 63.

Tildesley’s many credits include James Bond movie No Time To Die, 28 Days Later, The Constant Gardener, The Two Popes, Jay Kelly, Joseph Kosinski’s F1: The Movie, and Sam Mendes’ upcoming Beatles movies. Mendes today described his collaborator as “entirely original and extraordinarily gifted”.

Tildesley’s career spanned more than three decades and he collaborated with leading filmmakers including Danny Boyle, Richard Curtis, Michael Winterbottom, Martin McDonagh, Mike Leigh, Paul Thomas Anderson, Cary Joji Fukunaga, Ron Howard, Ben Wheatley, Oliver Stone and Mendes. Winterbottom and Mendes were among Tildesley’s most regular collaborators. With the former, he made seven films: I Want You, Wonderland, The Claim, 24 Hour Party People and Code 46. With the latter, he made the hit zombie film 28 Days Later, as well as Millions, Sunshine, Trance and T2 Trainspotting. Watch on Deadline

Boyle and Tildesley memorably teamed up for the 2012 London Olympics opening ceremony, a feat that earned the production designer an Emmy win alongside Suttirat Anne Larlarb.

With Mendes he made Empire Of Light and was aboard for the upcoming The Beatles — A Four-Film Cinematic Event alongside Stefania Cella and Neal Callow.

He won three British Film Designers Guild awards, for High-Rise, Empire of Light and F1: The Movie, as well as a Set Decorators Society of America prize, an Art Directors Guild award, and a British Independent Film Award.

Mendes said today: “Mark was a one-off: gentle, funny, warm, mischievous, entirely original and extraordinarily gifted. My abiding memory of him is from Empire of Light, flying down the Margate seafront on a motorised scooter, his technicolored scarf flapping in the wind, twinkling like a schoolboy playing hooky. A unique spirit and a wonderful artist. I am one of hundreds of people who are desperately sad that he is gone.”

The British Film Designers Guild (BFDG) said: “We are very sad to hear the news of Mark’s death. Besides being a well-respected and extremely talented production designer he was a friend and colleague to many of our members and will be very much missed by everyone in the Industry who knew and worked with him.”
