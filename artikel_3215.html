Could SpaceX Be the Ultimate Winner of the Nvidia-Jensen Huang Split?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Terafab could be Jensen Huang's new home.

By Keithen Drury – Nov 21, 2026 at 4:00AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Space Exploration Technologies ›

Few companies that have undergone more radical changes recently than Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%), whose co-founder and chief executive officer, Jensen Huang, abruptly resigned earlier this month. Although there are calls from investors and employees to bring him back, the board of directors has stated that it has no interest in doing that.

Nobody knows for certain what Nvidia's future holds, and it may hinge on who fills the CEO role. However, there is another company that could benefit from this: Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -7.69%). SpaceX is a client of Nvidia's, so how can it benefit?

Well, SpaceX's CEO, Elon Musk, has already made it publicly known that he would love to have Huang run his Terafab division, the company’s huge factory designed to build compute hardware. That would certainly bring on an excellent technology leader who knows the industry inside and out, but there are no official reports that this is being considered at this time.

So, can SpaceX still benefit from all of this? Let's take a look.

Terafab is SpaceX's semiconductor manufacturing project in Texas. Both SpaceX and Tesla (TSLA -7.64%) are involved in it, along with Intel (INTC -7.97%). Should Terafab come into existence, its primary competitor would be Taiwan Semiconductor (TSM -9.48%).

Taiwan Semiconductor is a semiconductor manufacturer that supplies the world with the vast majority of advanced chips. Companies like SpaceX and Tesla cannot get enough chips fast enough, so they are looking to build their own fabrication facilities to replace them. Terafab also integrates memory chip production, so companies like Micron (MU -13.89%) are also in competition with Terafab. Terafab would only supply chips to SpaceX and Tesla, so it wouldn’t challenge Taiwan Semiconductor or Micron for sales to their other customers.

Chip production is no easy business, and countless advanced technologies are difficult to replicate and take years of knowledge. But if a large amount of knowledge could be imported via Jensen Huang, then Terafab could have a shot at success.

Still, it's important to remember that while Huang ran a chip company, the company was mostly centered on design. Nvidia only designs graphics processing units (GPUs), the computing engines inside data centers; it doesn’t manufacture them. Although designing high-tech products requires knowing how they are made, there is a difference between knowing a process and having hands-on experience doing it and troubleshooting it when things go wrong. Huang's knowledge would be a game changer for SpaceX and Tesla, but it may not be enough to crack into this industry.

Although Huang may be a top option for the job, this is still another bold business launch by Musk, and it will be years before this facility is operational.

So, can SpaceX really benefit from Huang’s absence at Nvidia?

There's no doubt that Jensen Huang shaped the design path of Nvidia. The whole world benefited from his prescience and leadership, whether they know it or not, and his shoes will be hard, if not impossible, to fill.

Depending on who is selected as the next CEO of Nvidia, the technical visionary mindset needs to be replicated; otherwise, Nvidia won't be the same company. This is a huge risk for any company that is currently deploying Nvidia products, such as SpaceX.

SpaceX informed investors during its Q2 conference call that it is exclusively using Nvidia products for the foreseeable future. Musk made that decision because Nvidia's new Rubin architecture is best in class. That technology won't change with Huang not in the CEO role, so SpaceX will likely continue to deploy Nvidia hardware.

So, at the end of the day, unless Huang chooses to lead Terafab for Musk, SpaceX is likely better off with Huang in the CEO role at Nvidia. The same could be said of a whole host of other AI companies that use Nvidia products. But with Nvidia’s board declaring that there is no path for return, it seems likely that Huang’s future lies elsewhere.
