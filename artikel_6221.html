Air Canada Cancels 10 Nonstop Routes: See Full List Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Renato Oliveira de Araujo | Shutterstock, Simple Flying

All airlines have route churn, and Air Canada is no exception. When the Star Alliance member's overall network is explored, ten routes that operated during the summer are not scheduled to return in 2027. Eight are international and two are domestic.

Air Canada is overwhelmingly the country's largest operator. OAG data for 2026 shows that it has 30% of domestic flights (WestJet is second with 21%) and 37% for the overall international arena (WestJet follows with 14%). For long-haul activity, Air Canada has 38% of the market (Air Transat is second with 11%).

They all had flights at some point between May 2026 and January 2027, but no service is scheduled thereafter. The entries include Vancouver to Singapore, which is currently the airline's longest nonstop operation. The final departure will leave British Columbia on January 24, 2027.

That link's axing was probably influenced by the high price of fuel, which worsens the economics of ultra-long-haul routes. The situation may also put a damper on any other ultra-long markets that Air Canada was considering announcing. This might have included Ho Chi Minh City, following Canada and Vietnam recently expanding their bilateral air service agreement and much speculation.

Other eliminated routes included the thin links from Vancouver to Nashville and Raleigh-Durham, with Air Canada's flights only lasting for two seasons. It was the first time that British Columbia had nonstop service to the North Carolina airport. Nashville was different. WestJet entered the market in May 2023, providing the city pair's first-ever flights. Air Canada arrived two years later. WestJet pulled out in June 2026, followed by Canada's flag carrier three months later.

The same period was used to examine cuts to Air Canada's domestic map. Air Canada flew between Toronto Pearson and Yellowknife, in the Northwest Territories not far from the Arctic Circle, until August 2026. This year, the A319 (Rouge) and 737 MAX 8 (mainline and Rouge) were primarily used, although the A320 (mainline and Rouge) and A321 (Rouge) appeared too.

The long route, which covered 1,655 nautical miles (3,065 km) each way, launched in 2023. Flights materialized around 19 months after the debut of Air North. Perhaps unusually, Air Canada flew on a year-round basis, although winter frequencies and capacity was, of course, significantly lower than during the summer.

Following Air Canada's exit in August 2026, Air North continues to operate, primarily on the increasingly rare 737-500. Between January and October 2027, Air North's schedule submission to OAG shows that it has 37% more seats for sale than in the same period in 2026, which partly closes the capacity gap.

Then there is Fort McMurray, in Alberta, to which Air Canada Express stopped flying from Vancouver in May 2026. The route had been part of the carrier's network since January 2009.

First, Jazz CRJ200s operated, but flights switched to the Dash-8-Q400 in October 2015. It was pulled in June 2017 but returned in December 2025, only to last six months. The exit means that Vancouver no longer has nonstop service to Fort McMurray.

At 622 nautical miles (1,152 km), it was Air Canada's longest Q400 flight from British Columbia this year. The carrier continues to serve Fort McMurray, but from its busiest hub of Toronto Pearson, with multiple jet types and variants deployed.
