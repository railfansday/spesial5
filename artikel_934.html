Arsenal's winning run ended by Newcastle in thrilling draw

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Newcastle United vs Arsenal. Premier League.

Report and free match highlights as Newcastle put on a spirited second-half showing to wobble Arsenal, who lost David Raya to a shoulder injury; Noni Madueke equalised to ensure the spoils were eventually shared

Please use Chrome browser for a more accessible video player

Arsenal's four-game winning Premier League run without conceding was ended by Newcastle in a frantic 2-2 draw.

For 45 minutes, this looked like another routine victory for Mikel Arteta's winning machine. Arsenal were dominant, controlling the tempo and suffocating Newcastle with their relentless pressure as Eberechi Eze's opener was exactly what the league leaders deserved.

But football showed yet again it's a game of two halves as Matthias Jaissle produced a tactical masterstroke to change the momentum of the game.

The Newcastle boss made a triple substitution that completely transformed the contest. Suddenly, Newcastle were bolder, braver and playing with intensity.

Harvey Barnes sparked the comeback before Malick Thiaw completed a spectacular turnaround on a night when Bruno Guimaraes returned to St James' Park as an Arsenal player but was withdrawn in the second half after a quiet performance. The first Newcastle captain to lift a trophy in 56 years following their historic 2025 Carabao Cup triumph, he was cheered at full-time when he completed a lap of the pitch.

Arsenal were rattled after falling behind. And their afternoon took another worrying turn when goalkeeper David Raya was forced off with a shoulder injury.

Arsenal boss Mikel Arteta said: "Unfortunately, he had to come off which is never good news. He will get assessed now and probably he will have an MRI tomorrow [Sunday] to see what it is exactly because at the moment I don't know. It was a knock.

"I don't know how it happened and what the injury is at the moment, so we have to wait and see tomorrow."

But champions find a way to respond and Noni Madueke delivered the equaliser to ensure they escaped Tyneside with a point that extended their lead at the top of the table to six points over second-placed Brighton, who could halve the deficit at Hull on Sunday.

"My feeling is that we lost two points today. We were exceptional in the first half. At every level. Our intensity, the clarity that we have, how to attack the jumps of Newcastle every time they wanted to go man-to-man, the composure that we have, the way we use our relationship to break their press. And it should have been 2-0, 3-0 or 4-0 up, clear.

"But it wasn't. And when it wasn't, then we expected in the second half that we have to again raise the level because the opponent is going to have a reaction. And the opponent did have a reaction and we were very below the level that is required to stretch the gap in a game like this in this stadium. And then we gave them belief.

"And I say we give them belief because we give them a goal, we give them a throw-in that we just give them the ball. We have no structure to control the opposite winger. We concede a goal and it's game on.

"From that it became a very chaotic game. Every set-piece, we know that they are exceptional at it and we concede from a set-piece.

"Then you have to react. And then we went back to the exceptional team that we were in the first half again for another 25 minutes and we scored a great goal. We create infinity of openings to create and score the third goal, but we lack some time or to be precise, to make that count."

"A big, big point. When you feel the momentum in the second half, then you're almost feeling it's two points lost. But staying humble and seeing the big picture over 90 minutes, I think it's a fair result and something we can carry on and build on.

"We tried to encourage the players. Tactically, it was not too much of a difference. Honestly, it's just about believing in being front-footed, being aggressive, doing the last steps and believing also in your teammates. We need to use the momentum and that's what the lads did in the second half playing really vertical and using the gaps and the space and that was done with success.

"You see what's possible, especially at home with our fans - an unbelievable atmosphere, the energy was giving me goosebumps.

"This needs to be the standard. This unity, players and the fans and then the playing style. It's how we want to do that. The synergy should thrive us and give us confidence that we can do much better in the future."
