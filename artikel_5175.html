Former Mt. Gox CEO Warns Pump.fun Users Their Private Keys Are at Risk

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Former Mt. Gox CEO Mark Karpelès has strongly warned users of Solana-based memecoin launchpad Pump.fun about potential major security risks associated with the new wallet migration process, which could put users' private keys at great risk.

Karpelès’ warning comes as Pump.fun has set a Nov. 30 deadline to stop allowing external wallet logins to its website. The platform is moving to email and social logins, which will enable users to access their accounts via Google, Apple and GitHub.

But to maintain their current accounts, Pump.fun is asking users to import their private keys. This is what Karpelès thinks could compromise the security of their crypto assets.

In a recent X post, Karpelès said he studied Pump.fun's JavaScript code to understand the nature of the new migration process.

The analysis has raised questions about the security of users’ private keys, despite the platform's claims.

“At best, it's a misnomer to refer to it as 'end-to-end encryption,'” said Karpelès.

According to Karpelès, users' private keys are first encrypted in their browsers before being sent through Pump.fun’s servers to the platform’s wallet infrastructure provider, Privy.

Also, Pump.fun's backend provides the public key used for encryption during migration. Users’ browsers have no way to check whether the public key is authentic, according to Karpelès.

The possibility of a security breach involving Privy’s infrastructure is another major concern.

“Your money also now relies on Privy never getting hacked,” Karpelès warned.

One of the most important aspects of Karpelès' warning is the need for migration in the first place.

He said Pump.fun could use signature-based authentication without forcing users to expose their private keys.

Pump.fun already uses a "Sign in with Solana" message to prove ownership of a wallet, according to Karpelès.

So theoretically, users can prove ownership of their wallets and execute transactions without having to import their private credentials into a new system.
