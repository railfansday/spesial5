Just 2.5% of recent Bitcoin buyers’ exchange deposits were in profit

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Coinbase received nearly twice Binance’s share, while CryptoQuant found that each exchange’s share stayed close to its usual level.

Coinbase received 49% of the roughly 706,000 BTC that short-term holders sent to five major exchanges from Oct. 17 to Nov. 15, nearly twice Binance’s 25%, according to CryptoQuant’s Nov. 19 report.

CryptoQuant found that inflows and futures selling rose across the major venues it tracked, while each venue’s share of activity stayed close to its usual level. Kraken took 11%, OKX 10% and Bybit 5%.

Coinbase’s share of short-term-holder deposits eased in the most recent week measured. The other four exchanges each gained roughly 1% to 2.5%, according to the research dashboard.

Deposit shares can stay steady even as more Bitcoin flows to exchanges, and an exchange can also receive more coins while its share falls, if deposits elsewhere grow faster. The bigger change was the share of incoming Bitcoin that was worth less than its estimated acquisition price.

CryptoQuant defines short-term holdings as coins held for less than six months. In mid-October, around 60% of the Bitcoin this group sent to exchanges was worth more than its estimated acquisition price at the time of transfer.

The seven-day share was down to 2.5% on Nov. 18, its third-lowest reading since 2021.

Bitcoin fell from about $80,700 on Nov. 4 to $62,600 on Nov. 15, and as the price fell, more of the Bitcoin reaching exchanges was worth less than its estimated cost basis.

A blockchain transfer shows that coins reached an exchange wallet, but it doesn't show whether the holder placed a sell order, whether the order went through, or at what price. As CryptoQuant explains in its guide to exchange flows, coins can also move between wallets or be posted as collateral.

Whether holders eventually locked in any losses depended on what they did after depositing.

From Nov. 12 to 15, CryptoQuant recorded $1.1 billion in long liquidations and $174 million in short liquidations. A liquidation forces the trader out when a leveraged position can no longer meet margin requirements.

Closing a bet on rising prices can add sell orders to the futures market even as prices fall.

Open interest, a measure of outstanding futures contracts, fell 19% over five days. Funding rates, which govern payments between long and short traders, stayed close to neutral.

Sell orders that traded immediately also outweighed similarly aggressive buy orders at all five futures venues CryptoQuant tracked. Together, those figures are consistent with traders shedding leverage during the drop.

A sustained rise in Coinbase’s share across comparable windows would show that more of the cohort’s Bitcoin was going there relative to the other four exchanges.

To connect such a shift to price pressure, analysts would also need to see where trades executed. In November, the familiar split of deposits, the collapse in the share sent in profit, and the wave of long liquidations point to stress that reached across venues.
