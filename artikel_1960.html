Nottingham Forest could make Zach Abbott U-turn after Southampton development - verdict

Nottingham Forest may decide to keep Zach Abbott on loan at Southampton as more minutes beckon for him in a promotion-chasing side.

Oliver Glasner saw fit to allow Abbott out on loan in the summer, as he joined Southampton for the rest of the campaign on Deadline Day.

The 20-year-old defender signed a new long-term contract extension with his boyhood club upon agreeing to the loan move, proving just how highly rated he is at the City Ground.

His time at St Mary's has certainly not gone to plan so far, with just two appearances in their bid to win promotion back to the Premier League.

Recent reports have claimed that Forest are ready to recall him in January, but they may now be handed an incentive not to as Abbott pushes to start regularly under Tonda Eckert.

Keven Schlotterbeck's long-term injury is set to open the door for Abbott to receive more game time, as he looks to nail down a spot in their main eleven in the absence of the German.

Abbott's short Forest career has shown promise, with several decent performances in their run to the Europa League semi-finals last season.

He played regularly under Glasner in pre-season, and so it has likely come as a surprise to the 20-year-old and those at Forest to see him utilised so sparingly at Southampton this season.

Abbott was forced to wait over a month for his Saints debut, then was hooked off at half-time against Norwich on 14 October and failed to appear in any of their next four games.

Schlotterbeck's early injury last time out against Preston saw Abbott introduced from the bench, and he impressed despite a shock 1-0 defeat.

New reports have since claimed that the commanding German centre-back faces around six weeks on the sidelines.

Abbott looks to be in pole position to take his place in the starting eleven, meaning Forest may not be forced to recall him after all, especially if he does well and nails down a spot in the side.

Abbott won all three of his attempted duels against Preston, completed 90 per cent of his passes and made four clearances in a stellar showing from the cold.

Eckert praised his young loanee after the clash, saying: "He was very positive. I think he's done very well. It was not an easy game to come into, especially with the amount of long balls he had to deal with, and I think he's done that well."

Abbott will surely be the man to take Schlotterbeck's place in the team after the international break, and that will be music to the ears of Forest coaches and chiefs who want him to progress and return a better player next summer.
