Ex-Scotland hero makes brutal Pocognoli admission and urges manager to follow ‘energetic’ blueprint

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Scotland have been branded “boring” under new head coach Sebastien Pocognoli and urged to rip up their current game plan to get back on track.

Pocognoli, 39, has won just two of his first six games in charge of the national team, and came in for some scathing criticism in the wake of the 4-0 Nations League defeat to Switzerland on Monday night, with many supporters believing the manager is struggling to adapt his tactics to fit the strengths of the squad.

Former Scotland international Don Hutchison believes Pocognoli’s side have looked predictable under their new manager, and believes the squad must take inspiration from fellow home nation Northern Ireland, who achieved promotion to League A of the Nations League with a battling 1-1 draw away to Hungary on Tuesday night.

“Watching Scotland last night I was bored,” he wrote on his official X account. “Watching the same football I see a hundred times over. Northern Ireland are a good example of not over playing for the sake of it. Why can’t Scotland use a fighting, energy, ratting with “intensity”? Forget this ‘keep ball style’ - it doesn’t suit Scotland.”

Hutchison, who earned 26 caps for Scotland and is best remembered for scored the winning goal in a 1-0 European Championship playoff second-leg victory over England at Wembley in November 1999, thinks that the current national team are a shining example of what can be achieved when a team plays with more intensity.

“Yep that’s how you do it,” he said after Northern Ireland’s draw with Hungary in Budapest. “Bottle. Character. Heart. Fitness and desire. Well organised but with a willingness to run hard. Well played Northern Ireland. “[Scotland should] Try something different. Why do Scotland think they can ‘out football’ the opposition? We haven’t got enough players to make that work. Just go a bit longer and get the fans engaged in the game. [Scotland were playing] tippy tappy football last night.”

Despite some stinging criticism, Pocognoli defended his side’s performance in St Gallen, saying it had some of the “best moments” he’s seen from his team so far in his half-dozen games in charge. Speaking to the BBC, he said: “The score is heavy with the 4-0, but I don't think it's a reflection of the game, if I'm really honest and objective. We had some very good moments in the game, we started the game like we wanted, aggressive away from home. We could decide to be low on the pitch, but we did what we wanted to plan.

“We played a good first half, we created chances, we were dominant in some moments in the game, and we faced Switzerland with different shapes and adapted well from the first minute. We conceded the goal from maybe one of the first chances they had. We also created chances, but we missed it. It means we were not clinical in that moment in the box.”
