The Range Rover Evoque and Velar Won’t Live Past 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

But don’t worry if you’re not in the upper 1%; JLR has plans to keep affordable Land Rover models in the mix.

Update, 11/3 1:30pm: Jaguar Land Rover has confirmed to Road & Track that the Velar and Evoque will be discontinued: "Both Range Rover Velar and Range Rover Evoque will continue to be sold in selected global markets where demand remains strong, throughout 2027. Production of both models will end in mid-2027 in line with their natural product lifecycles, as JLR's manufacturing facilities transition to build its next generation of vehicles. Solihull will continue to focus on producing Range Rover, Range Rover Sport and the forthcoming Jaguar Type 01, while Halewood will transition to produce the new Range Rover GT and a new model from the Defender family."

The Range Rover Velar and Evoque could soon be facing the axe, as a new report from Autocar contends that both of Range Rover’s smaller, entry-level models won’t see a new generation.

The report cites Martin Limpert, Range Rover’s global managing director, as saying both the Evoque and Velar will be produced through 2027, but not beyond. The current generation Evoque was revealed all the way back in 2018 and went through a mid-cycle refresh for the 2024 model year. Meanwhile, the Velar is even older, having been revealed in 2017. It too was treated to a mid-cycle refresh for 2024.

The elimination of both the Evoque and Velar would significantly increase the cost barrier to entry for a car with the Range Rover badge on it. It would also leave the “Range Rover” brand with only a few models—those being the traditional Range Rover, Range Rover Sport, and incoming Range Rover GT.

But that might not be such a terrible thing. Instead of targeting the lower end of the market with Range Rover-branded models, JLR appears ready to use both its Defender and Discovery brands for those endeavors. Reports of a smaller Defender model suggest it could easily take the place of the Evoque, and JLR has confirmed a new model of some sort will be joining the Defender line. Meanwhile, the Discovery already comes in around the same price as a Velar.

Both the Evoque and Velar launched as relatively controversial projects, but ultimately led to JLR increasing sales numbers. Applying the Range Rover badge to smaller and more affordable cars was a way to make the brand more accessible, and plenty of folks were happy to jump on the train. It even opened some strange doors, with the Range Rover Evoque Convertible being the weirdest of them all. When the Evoque and Velar finally exit, they’ll join the Discovery Sport that’s confirmed to end production this year.
