Day 9/10 – the Walking Wounded

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Route: Pennine Way thru-hike – Hawes to Keld

I kept with my plan and took a zero day.  My first priority was to get to the pharmacy as soon as it opened and buy an ankle brace.  I also decided to replace my boots, which had 450 miles on them at this point.  I had a nagging suspicion this would happen before I left home, but really wanted to drop these boots in the traditional pile at the Border Hotel in Kirk Yetholm.  My feet were really feeling battered at the end of each day, a good sign that the boots had broken down.  I still had plenty of meals, but I restocked my trail snacks.

The innkeeper moved me from the Board Inn to nicer accommodations at the Crown Inn, and I spent the day casually wandering around town and resting.  The next morning, I went down to breakfast as soon as the pub opened, and then set out for my day.

The guidebook said that today would be a long gentle climb through Hardraw and on up to Great Shunner Fell.  That is certainly what I experienced.  My ankle was fine for the first few miles.  My pain tolerance is notoriously high, so I consciously checked in with my body.  I gave myself a 2 out of 10 for pain as I continued to climb.  If the day went this well, life would be golden.

After passing through Hardraw, I made sure to take my regularly scheduled breaks: walk for an hour, rest for 10 minutes.  With the inclement weather I’d experienced on earlier days, I didn’t always stop.  I frequently snacked on a protein bar and trail mix rather than sit in the rain and cook my planned lunch.  Today would be different.  I would take it easy and stop when I was supposed to.  I would cook a meal in the middle of the day.

I was a good way up to Shunner Fell when I looked back and saw two small figures below on the trail behind me.  Could it be Graeme and Rachael?  If so, they’d catch up with me soon enough.  Before long, I heard them call out; the orange backpack cover strikes again!  We had a nice chat before they took off up the slope.

The guidebook mentioned a shelter on the top of Shunner Fell.  That seemed like a good place to stop for lunch.  Comically, the “shelter” was a cross-shaped wall with benches all around.

I picked the side with the least wind exposure, pulled out my stove and made some soup.  It wasn’t long before a few others came along the trail to rest here, including Hugo.  Hugo was a fascinating character!  Here was an elderly gent (even older than I am) who had started his walk in Lake Geneva.  Yes—Switzerland!  He had walked down through Nice, France, then on to the coast, crossed the English Channel, and just kept walking until he reached Edale.  And he was still going!  He just liked walking.  He has retired and walking incredibly long distances is what he does to pass the time.  He wasn’t sure what his end plan was, but for now, he was walking the Pennine Way.

After a pleasant visit with Hugo and a satisfying lunch, I packed up and continued down the north slope of Shunner Fell.  My ankle was bothering me a little more, maybe a 4 out of 10.  Still endurable.

The walk to Thwaite was just wonderful.  Yes, it was still windy, but the sun was out.  I was able to take off my rain suit and enjoy the weather.  The descent was gentle, with flagstone sections on the path.  Although some flagstones were submerged, my new boots are waterproof.  This allowed me to just stomp along the path ignoring the water.  Previously, I would have to hike out into the heather and grass to navigate around many of the wetter spots.  I was making great progress.

With three miles left, I stopped in the village of Thwaite for a cappuccino and a slice of cake.  My ankle was nagging me, but no worse than it had at lunch.  I thought about stopping for the night here, but the inn was completely booked.  The campground in Keld has really good reviews and they don’t accept reservations, so odds were good that I’d have a place to pitch if I arrived early.

At first, the walk to Keld wasn’t much different the rest of the day’s walk.  It was a bit steeper and crossed sheep pastures, but the sun was out and the grass was nice.  But once I got above those pastures into the hills, things took a turn for the worse.  The path hugged the hillside, was narrow, overgrown, and very rocky.  There was a young couple on the path as well, but we parted company where the sign indicated that the path to Keld and the Pennine Way diverged.  They continued directly to Keld; I followed the Way.

Conditions on the trail continued to deteriorate, becoming more rocky and treacherous with each kilometer.

On a positive note, there were scores of little rabbits in this area, scampering away as I came down the trail.  As the path worsened, my ankle really started to ache.

The trail seemed to form an endless arc around the hillside, and no matter how close my map indicated I was, I still couldn’t see Keld.  I pushed my way across the rocks and through shoulder-high ferns for what seemed like forever.  My pain was a 9 out of 10, and I pushed on only because my map said I was less than a quarter mile from the campsite.  But there was still no sign of Keld.

Suddenly, I broke through to the road.  I had arrived.  I turned to the left and made the short walk along the road to Rukin’s Campsite.  The woman in the snack shop let me know that the nicer camping was located at the bottom of the hill along the river.  I limped my way down there and was glad I did.  It was indeed lovely—probably the prettiest place I have ever camped.

I cooked some dinner and limped around my campsite for a bit while there was still light to enjoy.  As night came on, I laid in my tent and tried to face the harsh possibility that my Pennine Way adventure might be over.  I have some tough choices to make.  I don’t know what happened to my ankle, but today’s experience makes me worry that I will permanently injure myself if I carry on.
