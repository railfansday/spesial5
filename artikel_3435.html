Netflix Officially Revives One of the Worst Fantasy Remakes Ever Made

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Tom Cruise has spent most of his career making the impossible look ridiculously easy, whether that's hanging off airplanes, jumping motorcycles off cliffs, or somehow finding increasingly dangerous ways to save the world as Ethan Hunt. But even Hollywood's most committed action star has the occasional misfire. Nearly a decade later, however, it's getting another opportunity to find its audience.

The Mummy has returned to Netflix's Top 10 movie rankings, bringing Cruise's much-maligned fantasy adventure back into the streaming spotlight. The 2017 release earned approximately $410 million worldwide, a respectable-sounding figure that wasn't enough to make the movie the franchise-launching success Universal had envisioned. Intended to launch an entire cinematic universe of classic monsters, the movie instead became a rather expensive reminder that audiences aren't always interested in another big-budget IP.

Thought safely entombed in a tomb deep beneath the unforgiving desert, an ancient princess whose destiny was unjustly taken from her is awakened in our current day, bringing with her malevolence grown over millennia and terrors that defy human comprehension. The movie takes us on a globe-trotting adventure from the sweeping sands of the Middle East through hidden labyrinths under modern-day London, in a new world of gods and monsters.

The movie stars Tom Cruise (Mission: Impossible) as Nick Morton, Sofia Boutella (Kingsman: The Secret Service) as Princess Ahmanet, Annabelle Wallis (Peaky Blinders) as Jenny Halsey, Russell Crowe (Gladiator) as Dr. Henry Jekyll, Jake Johnson (New Girl) as Chris Vail, Courtney B. Vance (The People v. O. J. Simpson) as Colonel Greenway, and Marwan Kenzari (Aladdin) as Malik.

Crowe's Dr. Jekyll was intended to help connect the movie to a wider world of supernatural creatures, with Universal announcing plans for future installments involving characters such as Frankenstein's Monster and the Invisible Man. Yes, they also planned to bring in the likes of Javier Bardem and Johnny Depp for a new Dark Universe.

Despite Cruise’s star power and a big marketing push (remember that awkward cast promo image with him, Bardem, Depp, and Crowe?), the film bombed critically and commercially, earning dreadful reviews and losing an estimated $100 million for Universal. The entire Dark Universe crumbled before a second movie could even shoot.
