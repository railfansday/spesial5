UnitedHealth Sues the U.S.; Chaos at USPSTF Meeting; Antisemitism in Medical Schools

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

— Health news and commentary gathered by MedPage Today staff

Note that some links may require registration or subscription.

UnitedHealth Group sued the U.S. government after receiving lower star ratings for its Medicare Advantage plans. (Reuters via AOL)

In other legal news, an appeals court struck down a federal law that requires workplaces to provide accommodations for pregnant women, saying Congress violated the Constitution when it passed the law in 2022. (AP)

And as he prepares to leave office, Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton is dropping cases against doctors accused of violating a state ban on gender-affirming care for minors. (Texas Tribune)

New reviews from NIH scientists concluded that herbicides containing glyphosate -- the active ingredient in Roundup -- may be linked to hematologic cancers in humans and that the weedkiller may cause cancers in rodents. (Washington Post)

The first meeting of the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) in more than a year was marked by edits from political leadership and multiple members appointed by Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. needing to recuse themselves from discussions because of conflicts of interest. (New York Times)

If finalized, one of the long-delayed USPSTF recommendations would steer women away from Pap smears for cervical cancer screening in favor of HPV testing. (NBC News)

The Trump Administration has restarted the process for financing the research networks that test vaccines and treatments for HIV and other infectious diseases. (New York Times)

Federal immigration officers detained a Chinese-born neuroscientist working at Princeton University who was traveling from an academic conference. (New York Times)

The FDA said it declined to approve the cell therapy deramiocel for Duchenne muscular dystrophy in part because of "data integrity deficiencies" in the support phase III trial.

But the agency expanded the approval of somapacitan (Sogroya) to include treating growth failure associated with Turner syndrome in kids 30 months and older, Novo Nordisk announced.

The Ebola outbreak in Congo now has 11,613 confirmed cases, including 5,678 deaths, according to the World Health Organization.

In the U.S., measles cases nationwide have reached 5,838 confirmed cases, according to the CDC.

As respiratory virus season heats up, should you mask for holiday travel? (Washington Post)

Acorns, mice, and ticks are a recipe for Lyme disease. (New York Times)

A New Hampshire woman has been living with a transplanted, genetically modified pig kidney for a year, and without serious complications. (New York Times)

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani tore his Achilles tendon during a friendly soccer match. (The Guardian)

A 38-year old nurse practitioner ran in the New York Marathon during her third trimester of pregnancy -- the seventh marathon she has finished while pregnant. (AP)

The British doctor whose evidence helped convict former nurse Lucy Letby in the murder of seven babies had been barred from treating patients during the trial over concerns that he posed a safety risk -- and jurors were never told. (The Guardian)

Kate Middleton, the Princess of Wales, opens up about finding hope and joy amid uncertainty in a new documentary exploring life since her cancer diagnosis in 2024. (AP)

Do mushroom supplements improve your health? (NPR)

In a randomized trial, adding milk fat globule membrane to infant formula did not improve cognition in infants exclusively fed formula. (JAMA Network Open)

Public trust in U.S. healthcare organizations is low, but changeable, researchers reported in JAMA Network Open.

A report from the Anti-Defamation League found increasing antisemitism in the U.S. healthcare system and medical schools. (Jerusalem Post)
