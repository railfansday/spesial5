15 Best Places to Buy Bedding, Editor Picks (2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Sheets get slept in every night and washed every week (in theory) which means it doesn’t take long to learn which ones hold up under use. Every retailer here comes recommended by an AD editor or contributor who has lived with the bedding at home for a long stretch: flannel kept on the bed through May, linen hauled to a communal laundromat and back, or a sheet set that survives a dog digging in every night.

Each brand earned its spot for a reason, whether that’s price, a cooling fabric, an unusually wide color range, or embroidery done by hand. Rest assured: they’re all comfy.

For bedding basics that won’t damage the budget, Quince is our first stop. Its European Linen Sheet Set earned our pick for the best affordable linen sheets, and senior shopping editor Nashia Baker has recently added the brand to her list of favorites. “The price point is unbeatable, and I love that they offer bedding sets in beautiful colorways and a variety of fabric options,” she says. “The quality is also impressive, especially considering the affordability.”

Made from 100% European flax, the sheets cost a fraction of what competitors charge, thanks in part to Quince’s direct-to-consumer model which skips traditional retail markups. “I love its airy nature along with the gorgeous Bordeaux color that complements the rest of my decor,” says Baker. The bedding range extends to quilts and coverlets, , and the brand’s down comforter also made our ranking of the best comforters.

Anyone who has paused a Nancy Meyers movie to study the bedroom should start with Serena & Lily. The brand is based in Sausalito, California, but its textured, breezy bedding and decor would look right at home on any coast, Nantucket included. The range covers lightweight linen and crisp cotton percale in pale colors and easygoing prints.

Of all the bedding Baker has tested, the Nantucket Stripe Sheet Set ranks near the top for its light weight and “worn-in texture,” which keeps the linen soft instead of scratchy. The ruffle trim keeps her bed from looking too plain. “The striped mink print does give a seaside vibe, but it’s been neutral enough to layer with any color bed cover without clashing with the rest of my minimal, romantic decor over the few months I’ve tested the set,” she adds. For a top layer, there’s the scalloped Baywood Quilt, which Alpert likes as a light blanket for “when you want to throw something over while you read your favorite Emily Henry book in bed.”

Boll & Branch sheets arrive feeling as if they’ve already been through several wash cycles, without the fraying or pilling that usually comes with that. Contributor Rachel Fletcher tested the brand’s percale, Supima cotton, flannel, and linen, but its signature sateen stood out most for its long-staple cotton and a soft, smooth finish. “That extra-soft feel, paired with the lovely, cooling properties, make them feel like the luxury sheets they are,” she says. Converts can outfit the rest of the room too, with Boll & Branch’s mattresses and bedroom furniture.

Brooklinen started out selling two things, cotton percale and sateen sheets, and has since added cashmere, brushed flannel, and a long list of prints. Pieces come in bundled sets or one at a time, and prices land between the budget brands and the luxury labels. Our editors keep coming back to the linen sheets, which they use in every season.

“Since I don’t live my life on a Greek island, I thought white linen sheets year-round might be a slightly bold move, but the nature of the fiber is versatile and temperature regulating,” says AD contributor Madeleine Luckel, who tested the Core Sheet Set. Fletcher sleeps on the Luxe Sheet Set and credits it with keeping her cool. “I’m a hot sleeper with sensitive skin, and they’re really cool on my skin at night, which is a huge relief,” she says. “I love the breathability of these sheets.”

Few bedding brands have tied their name to sustainability as closely as Coyuchi. Its linen sheets are made from certified organic French flax grown without synthetic fertilizers or pesticides, and the brand also offers percale, sateen, and flannel sheets. The earthy color palette is a selling point on its own.

“I love the color, which is what drew me to these sheets in the first place,” says Sam Cochran, global features editor. “They’re the perfect brown—a tad auburn, ever so slightly timeworn in palette.”

Shopping writer Julia Harrison and contributor Kate McGregor agree: Garnet Hill is their “secret spot” for bedding. Harrison is devoted to the flannel sheets and admits she leaves them on the bed through May because “they’re so soft, I’d rather suffer in my own sweat than switch them out.” The ticking stripe is “perfectly classic—like a true vintage piece, but not actually vintage…that might be tough sell in the bedding department.” Alpert also loves her flannel pick, a duvet cover that “signals a cottagecore moment without going over the top.” Paired with her Ostermoor duvet insert, it’s “like climbing under a layer of meringue.” Garnet Hill specializes in vintage-inspired designs, patchwork quilts especially, made in 100% linen and 100% cotton options at reasonable prices.

Cozy Earth makes breathable bedding for people who run hot at night, and its signature bamboo bedding, made of 100% viscose, are the brand’s best-known example. “I honestly thought these sheets were silk,” says contributor Megan Wahn. “That’s how smooth and soft they feel.” Wahn tested them mostly in winter, and despite feeling cool to the touch, they kept her warm. “I’m especially eager to see how they perform in the summer,” she adds. “Something about their smoothness makes me think the summer-night sweats won’t stand a chance.”

Parachute is where we send anyone after classic, well-made cotton bedding. Its sets and bundles are designed with sustainability in mind, and the percale and brushed cotton are made by artisans at the brand’s factory in Portugal.

“We love [Parachute] because the bedding is designed to provide a hotel-like experience, offering a perfect balance of softness and crispness,” says Stephanie Kraus of Stephanie Kraus Designs, whose clients favor the brand’s linen bedding sets. “They become softer with each wash while maintaining their durability and are super soft to sleep in every night.”

Fletcher also praises the brand’s commitment to organic, ethically sourced materials; the Organic Cloud Cotton Duvet Set, for one, is made of Oeko-Tex-certified 100% cotton. “I try a lot of bedding in this line of work and the more I do, the more I appreciate ethical and organic production, even if the price tag is a little higher,” she says.

GreenRow makes charming, long-lasting bedding from recycled and sustainably sourced materials, and it has won over several people on the Shop AD team. Contributor Yelena Moroz Alpert calls the Helena Floral Sheet Set “without a doubt, the softest sheets I own.” She compares the cotton satin to velvet, and the butter-yellow florals and green micro-gingham ruffle make the set prettier still. McGregor, meanwhile, is partial to the reversible patchwork quilt. “It’s the kind of blanket that feels substantial without trapping heat or feeling stuffy,” she notes. “I feel cocooned without a down comforter, but I’m not smothered when I sleep sans AC in the warmer months. It’s a year-round bedding piece for me, especially since the color palette can lean moodier or more cheerful depending on my sheet selection.”

Rebecca Udall is a newer name and one worth knowing. Her namesake bedding line is built on romantic, careful details, such as soft-colored decorative embroidery along the sheets. McGregor’s current favorite is this scalloped percale sheet set. “The feel is like a washed percale, crisp but forgiving, and I find that the subtle color on the detailed edge gives the perfect amount of visual interest to my all-white bedscape,” she says. If you want less ornament, try the Georgiana duvet cover, with faint ladderstitch detailing hand-embroidered by artisans in Northern Italy.

Hommey, an Australian brand founded in 2020, first caught on with its boldly striped robes and nightwear. It has since carried those designs into cotton and linen sheets and duvet covers. Across both fabrics, there are more than two dozen colors and prints to choose from. For maximalists who want to try color-blocking, the brand’s bolder colorways are a great place to start.

Baker tested the brown sugar linen duvet cover and found the stonewashed, Oeko-Tex certified flax smoother and more broken-in straight out of the package than the Quince version she already owned (above). “I won’t get ahead of myself and say it feels like pure silk, but I can confirm that it’s soft and breathable almost like an organic cotton duvet after a few washes,” Baker says. She pairs the chocolatey shade with burgundy linen sheets for a moodier bed, though it also works as a neutral with lighter sheets in oatmeal, which pick up the cream trim. The one drawback is that the cover has no interior ties, so the insert can shift around inside.

Australian brand Cultiver makes linen sheets and duvets with an easy, beachy look. Its Oeko-Tex certified pieces are an investment, and a worthwhile one. Coverlets are generously sized so they drape loosely, and the envelope-back pillow cases hold their shape without looking stiff. Fletcher has put her set through heavy use and many wash cycles. “Linen is my bedding fabric of choice pretty much 365 days a year, and Cultiver’s high-quality sheet sets do it for me in a way no other brand has. Out of all the sets I’ve tried, this one has stood the test of many washes, my dog digging in them to get cozy at night, and surviving across seasons without feeling worn or threadbare,” she says.

Erica Tanov’s bedding is handmade by artisans in Jaipur, in 100% linen with embroidery drawn from traditional Indian textiles. Harrison calls hers “a lifetime set” that “draws more compliments than anything else in my apartment.” The linen is soft and tough: She hauls this sheet set to her neighborhood laundromat, and it survives the communal machines without damage. It’s the first linen set she’s found to be “city-friendly,” as she calls it. She hasn’t tried the quilts or throw blankets yet, though one already tops her birthday wishlist (this one).

It depends mostly on how you like your bed to feel and how reliable your thermostat is. If you like the crisp coolness of hotel sheets, try percale. If you like a soft, organic feel, try linen for a breezy texture, or bamboo sheets for something similar to silk. Cotton sheets (like Supima sets) are often the most durable over time, especially after many, many washes. Your warmest options are generally flannel sets, and your coolest are generally bamboo and percale. For a basic setup, we recommend a full sheet set (flat sheet included, people) plus a duvet insert with a cover. The cover protects the insert and keeps it clean, so you’ll generally only need to wash the insert every six months rather than weekly like your sheets. Harrison likes to go one step further with a bed blanket underneath her duvet and above her sheets for a bit of weight and additional warmth. If a fluffy duvet isn’t your thing, a quilt or coverlet can take its place and add a little more weight.

Start with the fiber content on the label. Search for 100% cotton, linen (the more organic, the better), and steer clear of polyesters or more plastic fibers, as they’re likely to pill more quickly and break apart in the washer and dryer.
