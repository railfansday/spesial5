OLED iMac plans may be cancelled by Apple for two reasons

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Apple is considering abandoning plans to launch an OLED iMac, according to a new supply chain report. The news comes just weeks after the first OLED Mac was launched in the form of the new touchscreen MacBook Pro.

The company is said to be holding discussions with display partner Samsung about either delaying the planned launch or abandoning it altogether, with two reasons cited …

Bring back a pro level iMac. The 27” 5k display was a very good size, make it a more accurate OLED display combined with the latest Apple Silicon, so former iMac owners have a choice where there is none now.

The all In one form factor remains convenient for labs, classrooms, creative departments, audio studios (home & pro), etc.

Keep the non OLED 24” display format positioned as a consumer model, plus lower its cost and you will see a sales uptick.

Apple’s journey into OLED screens began with the Apple Watch while other products were still using IPS LCD. The company subsequently switched to OLED for some iPhones and iPads before a long-awaited launch of the first OLED MacBook Pro back in October.

It was expected that the company would gradually transition to OLED across the full range of Macs over time. However, this process was expected to take a number of years, with the iMac not likely to join the party until 2029 or 2030.

The Elec today reports that the move may be delayed even further or even abandoned altogether, for two reasons: cost and demand.

The company is said to be discussing a potential delay or cancellation of the project with Samsung Display. High OLED panel costs and weak sales of existing OLED-equipped products are believed to be factors behind the reconsideration […]

The biggest factor behind the review is cost. The iMac sells in smaller volumes than smartphones or laptops, making it difficult to maintain competitive pricing if expensive OLED panels are adopted. Weak sales of the OLED iPad Pro are also believed to have influenced the decision.

The report suggests that incoming CEO John Ternus has been reviewing the commercial viability of upgrading more products to OLED. As further evidence, the report says Apple has sent quotation requests to LCD suppliers for an iMac screen to be launched in around 2029.
