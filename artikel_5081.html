The Simple Guide to Finding a Weighted Vest You’ll Actually Use

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Expert-tested picks that feel secure, balanced, and comfortable enough for all kinds of workouts.

Slip on a weighted vest, and suddenly your regular walk, chores, or bodyweight exercises feel a little more rewarding—the extra weight offering a simple boost in effort and making us more aware of the way our bodies maintain balance and stability.

I’m a certified personal trainer, and I sometimes recommend weighted vests to older adult clients as a way to add extra challenge to walking, cardio, and bodyweight exercises. While recent research suggests that weighted vests may not have a dramatic impact on bone density, they’re still a safe and convenient way to increase the load on your muscles and joints, helping you push yourself a little harder than usual.

Weighted vests let you add extra resistance to walking, stair climbing, jumping, push-ups, and pull-ups without needing to grip dumbbells or barbells. Some clients even wear them during everyday activities like cleaning, yard work, or mowing the lawn—making fitness a little more functional and, yes, a bit more fun.

That said, if you’ve been diagnosed with osteoporosis, consult your doctor before adding a weighted vest to your exercise routine, while folks who are pregnant or experience back, neck, or joint pain should also avoid them.

Before you take the plunge on purchasing a weighted vest, you should consider the resistance level, workouts such as walking or HIIT, and whether materials are sweat-resistant to determine which one is best for you. Ready to get vested?

After testing a handful of weighted vests for weeks, the trophy goes to the Omorpho G-Vest for its remarkable comfort, ideal weight, and sleek design. I spent a week wearing it for my regular lifting sessions and hour-long walks. It’s easy to wear for my normal gym routine, which includes moves such as walking lunges, Copenhagen planks, pull-ups, and push-ups.

However, I found it to be challenging when worn for heavier compound lifts like front squats, deadlifts, and barbell rows. Other than walking lunges, I wouldn’t normally add load to those exercises because of the logistics of holding or hanging weight, but the vest makes it possible to add resistance without gripping anything.

The fit is another reason the G-Vest earns the top spot. This vest is surprisingly comfortable considering it weighs just over five pounds from stainless steel balls. While the back panels have more weight—226 stainless steel balls on the back and 122 on the front—the weight distribution feels uniform when wearing.

It’s also compact—from the shoulder straps to the bottom of the vest is about 17 inches, which, for me, is great since I have a very short torso. It also doesn't dig into the top of my hip bone when I move around.

Even if you don’t have a short torso, you’ll still appreciate the soft-to-the-touch polyester fabric, despite it feeling like neoprene (synthetic rubber). I use adjustable cords at the sides for a customized fit. There are also straps on the shoulders to accommodate a wider frame, or if you want the vest to sit a little lower on your torso. For reference, I have mine on the shortest setting.

One downside of the G-Vest is its limited size options. There are only two: S/M or L/XL. I highly recommend using the size chart to determine which fit is best for you. I’m typically a medium when shopping for athletic tops, but I chose the L/XL because my chest circumference is on the cusp of both options. It’s also worth mentioning that the weight of the S/M vest is four pounds and 12 ounces, while the L/XL is five pounds and six ounces.

You can shell out up to $300 for a weighted vest, but not everyone needs all the extra features that come with that higher price tag. The ApexUp weighted vest is a popular option thanks to its low price point, but it offers more than just savings.

This vest is built from neoprene (which is the durable synthetic rubber you’ll find in wetsuits) and filled with iron sand, a material that’s denser than natural sand and absorbs less moisture. As a result, it won’t stick to you or clump up when you sweat.

It has a smooth, comfortable feel, with seams that are stitched over with fabric to prevent rubbing and chafing. Wide armholes and an adjustable strap across the chest mean you can get a comfortable fit, no matter your chest size.

The solo strap in ApexUp’s 10-pound version means it’s more likely to shift around during dynamic movements like burpees, making this vest a better choice for things like walking. A lot of the weight in the vest is in the front straps, so just be aware of your posture while you use this. But the mesh storage pocket in back is secure and roomy enough for your phone, which is a nice feature if you want to free up your hands.

A common complaint with this vest is the smell, which may be due to that neoprene cover. If you notice it and it bothers you, just let the vest air out a little before using it. Also, be aware that the ApexUp vest doesn’t allow you to tack on extra weight. But at this low price, you can simply buy a new one when you’re ready for more resistance and still come out on top, price-wise.

The best weighted vest is one you’ll actually wear, and the Stakt Adjustable Weighted Vest makes that part easy. Instead of looking like tactical gear, its streamlined neoprene construction fits close to the body and feels more like a weighted garment.

One way WH fitness and reviews editor Natascha Grief, NASM-CPT likes to use weighted vests in her own training and with her training clients is to increase proprioception, or awareness of the body’s position in space. Because the Stakt vest distributes weight around the torso instead of loading only the shoulders, it can provide helpful feedback for connecting with the core and postural muscles during movement.

The Stakt weighted vest starts at six pounds and a two-pound zip-on extension brings it up to eight pounds. Grief tested both configurations while walking, doing chores around her apartment, doing pushups, and adding it to a functional training workout. It delivered comfortable, evenly distributed weight that fits and feels more like an additional clothing layer than a traditional weighted vest.

The aluminum front buckles feel sturdy. The shoulder straps use a hook-and-loop adjustment system, so once Grief found the right adjustment, it was a set-it-and-forget-it situation, and the straps stayed put. “I didn’t have to interrupt any workout to readjust them,” she said.

A porous mesh liner creates a comfortable buffer between the body and the neoprene, which adds some breathability. It also zips out for washing, which is a helpful feature, especially considering that this vest is worn close to the body. The vest also has a mesh pocket for small essentials. Its reflective detailing is minimal though, so you’ll want additional visibility gear when walking in low-light conditions.

If you’re looking for an adjustable-weight option ideal for specific functional training exercises and rucking, the Hyper Vest Elite from Hyperwear is highly customizable. I tested the 10-pound model, which is weighted with 66 individual 2.25-ounce stainless steel bars, each with its own little pocket.

If you prefer heavier weights, 15- and 20-pound options are available. Plus, you can purchase a box of individual weights to load up your vest (each pocket can fit two 2.25-ounce weights).

I wore it during multiple 60-minute walks, squats, pull-ups, push-ups, and back extensions. With the full 10 pounds loaded on my body, I found it really difficult to do pull-ups and push-ups. However, when I removed some of the weights from the individual pockets, it made for a more manageable load.

That said, I’m not sure how realistic it is to load up these tiny weights into individual pouches when switching between exercises. I think it’s a great feature, but personally, I’d design my workouts to ensure I didn’t have to adjust the weights more than once.

There are adjustable cords on each side to cinch or loosen for your preferred fit, but you won’t find shoulder strap adjustments like on the Omorpho vests. I also noticed the Hyperwear vest sits nearly five inches lower on my torso than the Omorpho vest. It doesn’t dig into my hips, but I notice it grazes my lower back when walking. It’s not a deal breaker, but I prefer shorter styles. Four size options are available: small, medium, large, and extra large.

As for material, I appreciate the heavy-duty construction of this vest, which is made with Cordura, a synthetic, tear-resistant fabric popular for backpacks and military clothing. It will hold up for years, even if you plan to go hiking or wear it in the elements. I also like the reflective strips at the zipper and across the chest for increased visibility outdoors.

If you’re looking for a weighted vest with resistance that pushes your limits while not disrupting your range of motion, this one by Vivitory checks the box. Despite the non-cropped fit, it still stays in place with only two adjustable straps and distributes weight evenly (11 pounds for the medium size and 18 pounds for the large size).

WH art director Christine Giordano was pleasantly surprised by how light it felt compared to holding it. “I was nervous it was going to be too heavy for me or cause pain in my lower back, but I was wrong,” says Giordano. “It didn’t bounce up and down or move all over—it stayed snug to my body while not being too hot and bulky.”

She loves wearing it during easy walks when waiting around for her kids’ sporting events or for more intense workouts, such as CrossFit or burpees. “I’m not a huge fan of dumbbell step-ups, so I tried it with the vest for the added weight without leaning forward,” she says.

As for the material, it’s made with neoprene, a synthetic rubber resistant to sweat that Giordano says she found to be breathable. There are also reflective strips on the vest for visibility during dawn or dusk, and a small pouch at the back for holding essentials.

If you get seriously sweaty during cardio or weightlifting, this 12-pound Reebok weighted vest is an option in hot summer temps. The back has a breathable ventilation panel that allows air to circulate.

WH contributor Korin Miller has recently added it to her workout gear lineup. “I like to go hard with my workouts, and I get really sweaty as a result—I appreciate the open-air design of this vest so I can cool off,” says Miller. “That, combined with the airy fabric, means I won’t end up with the vest sticking against my skin during workouts.”

Aside from its sweat-wicking properties, its two adjustable straps allow for a customized fit during a variety of workouts. For Miller, specifically walking, cycling, rowing, and bodyweight movements, such as planks and squats.

But, it’s important to note this model isn’t ideal for jumping. “Those extra weights can flap around during bigger movements like running and HIIT workouts—basically anything that has you jumping up and down,” says Miller.

The Omorpho G-Vest Icon is a quality choice for runners and HIIT workouts, given its snug fit and moderate weight that doesn’t feel like too much. The design is really similar to Omorpho’s OG model, with soft neoprene-style fabric, stainless steel ball bearings embedded into the fabric, and waist and shoulder adjustments for a custom fit.

The main difference is the adjustable weight and reflective strips throughout for extra visibility during night runs. The adjustable weight system is genius. It involves “G-Packs” of varying weights that snap in and out, allowing you to tack on additional, evenly distributed weight fast. (Just a heads up: A set of three G-Packs that brings the weight up to 12 pounds costs an extra $129.)

We also like that the encased stainless steel weights are not featured through the chest area. If you want to wear the G-Vest Icon with just a sports bra, you won’t have any irritation from high-impact movements.

For reference, the weights go up the entirety of the front panels on the men’s version. While the vest’s fitted style is ideal for runs, its six-pound weight means it’s better suited for things like short runs and sprints.

One downside is that the neoprene-style fabric made our tester sweat a ton when worn with layers. “My first experience with the vest was under my winter jacket on a 10-degree day during an hour-long walk,” WH contributor Korin Miller says. “I sweat my butt off despite the cold temperatures, compared to having sweat evaporate from my skin at the gym.”

Besides being a WH Fitness Awards 2025 winner, the Empower Weighted Vest is the only vest we’ve seen with a cross-style fit. It’s designed specifically to fit women’s curves while offering supreme support for the shoulder blades. The fit is compact with less material at the sides, while the straps are fully adjustable.

While its main highlight is being a custom fit for women, another unique feature is six removable one-pound bags (three in the front and three in the back pockets). In the 10-pound model (weight ranges from eight to 16 pounds), the vest can go as low as four pounds when the weights are removed if you’re seeking only light resistance.

WH health and fitness editor Addison Aloian, NASM-CPT, uses it for taking her workouts to the next level. “It elevates my walks, helps my core activate more, and makes my bodyweight exercises harder,” she says. Her only complaint is that the removable weights can make it feel bulky and interfere with hitting the full range of motion with a handful of exercises. “If I wanted to do crunches, I couldn’t lie on my back without it arching, and I couldn’t do supermans from my stomach because the bags in the front made it so I couldn’t lie flat,” she says. “During other strength exercises like squats, it hit the top of my thigh and prevented me from moving down further.” Perhaps it’s best to use the added pocket weights only during walks.

As for material, it’s neoprene for optimal sweat resistance, like the Vivitory and Prodigen weighted vests.

Not everyone wants or needs the 20-pound resistance TRX’s Hexgrip Weighted Vest can offer. But this vest is adjustable, allowing you to tweak the weight in one-pound increments, giving you room to grow up to that 20 pounds. So, if you want to use it as a 10-pound vest to start, more power to you.

This sturdy vest helps you get a secure fit with the help of three wide Velcro straps, making it a solid option for dynamic movements, some cardio workouts, and general strength training. The neoprene inner is comfortable against your skin, while the snug fit and smartly positioned weights mean you don’t have to stress about the vest moving around on you while you work out.

While it’s on the heavier end, it’s not bulky. As a result, you can still feel comfortable doing your usual range of motion. Just a heads up: Some of the weights are positioned across the chest, which may feel more noticeable if you have larger breasts. But those adjustable straps mean you can lower or raise the shoulder straps to find a position that’s more comfortable for you.

The weights slip into individual pockets to prevent them from shifting around, but make sure they’re secured well before you start your workout. (Some people have reported them sliding out.) Because the adjustment process takes some time, this isn’t a vest you’ll want to adjust mid-workout. This vest also comes in a 40-pound version, although most exercisers will do best with the 20-pounder as a heavy option.

If you have broader shoulders, the RITFIT has fully adjustable straps. They're spaced further apart—one sits higher on your chest and the other closer to your waist.

It can be more comfortable if you have a larger chest, while it may run bigger on smaller frames. It also has mesh pockets to store your keys and phone.

The load on a weighted vest can range from 3 to over 30 pounds, depending on the style and brand. If you’ve never used a weighted vest or are new to resistance training, I recommend starting with a lighter option between 3 and 10 pounds. Our best overall pick is optimal for newbies since it’s five pounds.

Heavier vests are ideal for loading bodyweight exercises like push-ups, pull-ups, squats, and walking lunges. They can also be used for walking, hiking, or rucking, depending on your fitness level. Lighter vests are ideal for speed training like sprinting, interval training, and plyometric exercises like box jumps, burpees, or jump roping.

Durable materials such as Cordura, nylon, and polyester are less likely to tear from constant wear during intense movement. All of the picks on this list are made from either of these three fabrics, and I found they perform well during explosive workouts and cardio. They also wick away sweat, which is a must for ultimate comfort. As for fit, I love an adjustable closure system with a zipper or buckles at the front, waist, and shoulders for customizing the fit.
