Van Gogh Slashes Auction Record at Sotheby’s, Selling for $190.4 M.

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

After a week dominated by a Nazi-era restitution dispute, Sotheby’s finally got to sell a $180 million Vincent van Gogh painting on Wednesday night—for more than it bargained for.

Vincent van Gogh’s Châtaigniers en fleurs (1890), a picture of chestnut trees in bloom painted just weeks before the artist’s death, sold for $190.4 million (including fees), setting a new auction record for the Dutch painter. The painting, which carried an undisclosed guarantee, hammered at $165 million against an expectation of around $180 million (pre-sale estimates do not include buyer’s fees). The fifth item and cover lot for the Blaquier-Arrieta collection sale, which the house had billed as “legendary,” hung behind the rostrum presided over by auctioneer Oliver Barker.

The artist’s previous record of $117.2 million was set at Christie’s in November 2022 when Verger avec cyprès (1888), a landscape from the collection of Microsoft cofounder Paul Allen, sold, hammering for $102 million.

Wednesday’s result beats that by $73.2 million, a jump of 62.4 percent in just four years.

The record is certainly a victory for Sotheby’s, which almost wasn’t going to be able to sell Châtaigniers en fleurs. Last Thursday, descendants of Samuel Fischer, the German Jewish publisher who once owned the painting, challenged its sale, arguing that their family had been forced to part with it in Amsterdam in 1936 because of Nazi persecution. Sotheby’s disputed the claim, pointing to archival records showing the painting had sold for 68,000 Swiss francs, around $22,000 at the time, which it considered a fair market price. After several days of talks, the Blaquier-Arrieta family and the Fischer heirs reached an agreement this week, clearing the painting’s title before the sale. The terms weren’t disclosed, so it’s unclear whether the heirs will receive a share of the proceeds.

For a little perspective, van Gogh’s Portrait of Dr. Gachet (1890) fetched $82.5 million at Christie’s in 1990, then the highest price ever paid for a work of art at auction. That remained the artist’s auction record for 32 years, until the Allen sale. Now, just four years later, the ceiling has moved again.

Painted shortly after van Gogh arrived to Auvers-sur-Oise, Châtaigniers en fleurs is one of only roughly 50 landscapes the artist made during his two-month stay their before his death. Of those, the majority belong to museums, while only an estimated eight are still in private hands, according to research cited by the Art Newspaper.

This electric scene, with its thick brushwork, also hadn’t been seen in decades, last being exhibited at the Guggenheim in 1964. The consignors, Argentine collectors Carlos Pedro Blaquier and Nelly Arrieta de Blaquier, bought it in 1975 and kept it hidden for half a century. That kind of provenance and scarcity is exactly what fuels the trophy market for works of this caliber.

A record of this size for a Post-Impressionist painting might suggest that the market for this category is roaring back. While the market has certainly improved, the rise in sales of Impressionist and Post-Impressionist art by 47 percent in 2025 to $1.8 billion, according to the Art Basel and UBS Art Market Report, is still nearly $1 billion short of its $2.6 billion peak in 2022.
