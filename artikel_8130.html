Germany’s Best-Kept Secret: Winter

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Nearly everybody visits Europe in summer. That’s no exaggeration: Half of all international inbound arrivals to the continent happen between June and September, with hotels, trails, attractions, and city streets swelling with nearly three times the people you’d encounter in January.

That’s good news for winter fans. Seasonal highlights like festivals and craft markets and storybook scenery give winter travelers a whole different world to discover. Thanks to Lufthansa’s daily flights to its German city hubs from 18 U.S. cities coast to coast, enjoying the holiday traditions and empty museums requires very little effort. Just say yes.

Here’s how to get started on planning your winter getaway.

Frankfurt’s winter markets are best enjoyed on foot, as you’ll wind through more than 230 stalls. Fuel up for the walk with a Bethmännchen, the city’s traditional marzipan pastry, and warm up with a mug of hot apple wine before tracing your way along the Main River. Explore the famous Palmengarten glasshouses for a botanical escape to tropical, desert, and alpine environments, all within the urban core. Beyond the city, enjoy winter silence and peace on hundreds of marked winter trails crisscrossing the snow-dusted Taunus woods.

See why this iconic city deserves its reputation as the international capital for modern culture. Stroll from the treed paths of the Tiergarten to Berlin’s many bridges (more than Venice). Sculptors and architects have turned all of Berlin into an open-air museum, but when the weather turns, you can duck into the galleries peppering the Auguststraße and Potsdamer Straße. Stay fueled at neighborhood cafés inspired by international cuisines, and join the late-night reveling at concerts and dance clubs. (New Year’s Eve is a can’t-miss holiday.)

Elsewhere in Europe, there’s more to love about winter. In Italy, explore the eye-popping Dolomites, or visit Renaissance landmarks in Florence without the crowds. If you time it right, experience Venice’s Carnevale in late January and early February. In France, the Champs-Élysées Christmas market ends its decade-long absence this season, bringing 200 chalets to the famous Paris avenue. Prefer a warmer winter break? Head to Greece, where you can have Athens—and its ancient historic sites—practically to yourself. That’s easy to say yes to.
