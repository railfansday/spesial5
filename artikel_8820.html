My Shimano Pedals Are Nearly 10 Years Old—This 10-Minute Service Saved Me From Spending $200 on a New Set

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

A quick SPD-SL pedal service can revive old Shimano pedals—and help you avoid a costly update.

My longest-lasting pedals are a roughly 10-year-old pair of Shimano SPD XT pedals that have admittedly seen zero maintenance. Those pedals have been effectively abused on everything from getting bashed through rock gardens to long dusty or muddy days on the gravel bike. A few years ago I got my first road bike and switched to a pair of Shimano SPD-SL pedals. Since then I’ve put that bike (and those pedals) through the wringer.

I tend to stress test gear, even prior to having this job, so I enjoy taking my road bike out on everything from light gravel to singletrack that I have no business on. This means my road pedals have seen similar abuse to my mountain bike and gravel pedals. They’re still in great shape, but they could use a little love. Even pedals only used for road riding will start to get gritty over time.

Then I saw Shimano’s pedal release—not only are the old cleats not compatible with the new pedals, but the price of the new Shimano pedals starts at $200 for the Ultegra model and go up to $300 for the Dura-Ace model.

After I saw the rollout, it felt like a good time to bring my old generation SPD-SL pedals back to life and while I’m at it, give you a few tips on the process. Here we’ll run through how to service your SPD-SL pedals in under 10 minutes and save you a few hundred dollars in the process.

A quick note on reinstalling the pedals back on the bike: make sure you put the right pedal on the right side and the left pedal on the left side. I know, this sounds obvious. I’ve seen the wrong pedal get forced into the wrong side of the crank too many times, damaging the crank and the pedals to the point that sometimes both need to be replaced. Sometimes pedals are marked with an “L” for left and “R” for right, but you can always double-check to see which way the threads go. Left pedal threads will go up to the left and right pedal threads go up to the right. Then when it is time to reattach the pedals, put some grease on the threads of the crank arm and begin to thread them on by hand instead of starting with the wrench. If you can’t begin to thread the pedal onto the crank threads by hand, double-check the pedal is on the correct side, make sure it’s going into the crank arm straight, and make sure there is no debris in the hole of the crank arm preventing it from threading.
