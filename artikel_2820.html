Rapid Recap: Nets 104, Bucks 97

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Milwaukee Bucks follow a string of solid games with one of their worst in a long time, losing at home to the Brooklyn Nets, 104-97. Tyler Herro led the Bucks with 28 points and five assists, while Kel'el Ware led the Bucks bench with 13 points and 14 rebounds. Julius Randle led the Nets with 28 points, and Noah Clowney led their bench with 17 points.

The game started with the Bucks on the attack, forcing three personal fouls in the first 90 seconds and exploiting the mismatches created when four forwards and one guard are the opposing starting five. Myles Turner wasted no time pulling out his three-ball from Toronto as the Bucks quickly built a 9-2 lead. But of course, the Nets started finding their shot as the Bucks let their feet off the gas, going on a 16-2 run as Milwaukee's offence dried up like the Sahara Desert. The Bucks didn't score another field goal until the 4:08 mark, when Jaime Jaquez Jr. finally buried a three, which set off an 8-2 run that included a sweet baseline dunk by Kel'el Ware. Despite being in the bonus for more than eight minutes, the Bucks couldn't take advantage, shooting only six free throws for the quarter and ending the period down 28-24.

The second quarter started where the first quarter ended: with Ryan Rollins throwing up ill-advised shots—he was bad in this one. Meanwhile, the Nets steadily built a 41-28 lead through the first five minutes. Thankfully, the young Bucks off the bench played their typical brand of high-energy defense and held Brooklyn to six points over the next five minutes, but unfortunately, the home side couldn't buy a bucket themselves. Milwaukee did at least outscore Brooklyn 11-4 in the final few minutes to cut the deficit to four, with Ware giving fans a reason to get hyped with a monster dunk before Jaquez scored a quick lay-up to end the half on a high note. Milwaukee headed into the locker room down 47-51.

Coming out of halftime, the Bucks kept up the pace and quickly regained the lead, largely thanks to 11 quick points from Herro, and it finally felt like the home team had found a flow. Unfortunately, that flow ended as Rollins decided to dominate the ball, force shots, and make head-scratching decisions in general. Eventually, the Nets crept back ahead after another scoring drought by the Bucks late in the third quarter. If not for Ware imposing his will in the paint, the Nets may have run away with it. Instead, the big man finished the third quarter with 10 points and 10 rebounds, scoring the final four to keep the deficit at six, 77-71.

The Nets extended their lead to 10 pretty quickly to open the final frame, as the Bucks struggled to get paint touches, something that seemed like it would come easier after getting in the paint so effectively against Philadelphia and Toronto. Milwaukee made a few pushes, but each one ended in an unforced error; they just couldn't get out of their own way. The game ended ugly as the Bucks turned it over nine times in the fourth quarter alone to give this one away. One of the more disappointing efforts of the last few years, frankly, and that's saying something.

26: the number of free throw attempts the Nets had, which allowed them to hang in the game despite their horrid shooting.

Ryan Rollins needs to be glued to the bench for a game (or five).
