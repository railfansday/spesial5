What the reported Drake Maye injury means for the Patriots

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The New England Patriots did not just lose their Week 10 game to the Detroit Lions, they also saw their starting quarterback depart early in the 35-10 contest. And not just that: Drake Maye is believed to have suffered a "significant lower leg injury" that is reportedly expected to keep him out an extended period of time and might even end his season.

While further tests will be conducted to determine a return timeline, Maye's immediate future is, at the very least, in question. What does this mean for the Patriots? Let's find out.

The Patriots are 5-5 and despite dropping out of the playoff picture on Sunday remain in the race for a postseason spot in the AFC. However, if Maye is indeed forced to miss extended time or even the rest of the season, the outlook changes drastically: given all of the issues, this does not look like a playoff-caliber team without its Pro Bowl-caliber quarterback.

At least publicly, the Patriots and head coach Mike Vrabel won't care. We will hear the classic "next man up" and "everybody is preparing like a starter" lines, while there will be no outward readjustment of the goals.

Make no mistake, however. This is the worst-case scenario for the Patriots and their 2026 season. It might not mean they will go 0-7 down the stretch, especially if Maye does come back, but the sights might have to be set on 2027 sooner rather than later.

Sunday's game against the Lions might not have been an accurate reflection of their capabilities, but that doesn't mean the Patriots' offensive outlook is not bleak. Fact is, after all, that Tommy DeVito is a serious downgrade from Drake Maye.

That said, the Patriots are still expected to rely on their backup for however long Maye will ultimately be out. Rookie Behren Morton, who served as the emergency third option throughout the season thus far, will be elevated to the No. 2 spot with a third passer potentially being added to the roster or practice squad if need be.

DeVito, 28, entered the NFL as an undrafted rookie with the New York Giants in 2023. He started eight total games over two year but was waived in 2025. He subsequently joined the Patriots in a backup role, serving as the third passer behind Maye and Joshua Dobbs. When Dobbs was let go earlier in the year, DeVito officially took over the backup spot.

As such, he saw minimal action over the first nine weeks of the season. Against the Lions, however, he played most of the game and finished with 12 completions on 24 attempts for 81 yards with a touchdown and an interception. The Patriots scored once with him at the helm and punted eight times, including seven three-and-outs.

However long the Patriots' medical staff determines Maye will be out might also have an impact on other injury situations. Frankly, if the starter is sidelined and the losses keep piling up, there would be no need for any players to be rushed back from injury. There shouldn't be any way, to be honest, but the threshold of "pushing through" an injury might be reconfigured should Maye indeed be out an extended period of time.

That "pushing through" process, however, appears to be an integral part of New England's culture this season. Multiple players are currently banged up or still dealing with the effects of injuries they suffered earlier; the bye week will come handy in that regard.

If the overall goals begin to shift, however, so might the culture — for better or worse. It will be on Mike Vrabel, who was hired in 2025 in large part due to his abilities as a leader and team-builder, to make sure the locker room sees through this latest challenge in a season full of them.

As of Monday morning, three of the Patriots' final seven games of the season are scheduled as prime time games. One of those is a realistic candidate to be flexed out of its spot, and would have been so even before Maye got hurt: the Sunday night game against the Los Angeles Chargers in Week 12.

That being said, with Thanksgiving that weekend and games taking place on Thursday and Friday as well, the alternatives are actually minimal. Unless the league wants to flip it to the 1 p.m. ET spot currently occupied by Raiders-Browns, only the 4:25 p.m. ET contest between the Seahawks and 49ers makes sense. Whether or not FOX will give that one up, however, will be seen.

As for the other prime time games on the schedule, they likely will stay in their slots due to their nature as Thursday (Week 14 vs. Vikings) and Monday (Week 15 vs. Chiefs) games. Still, the two undetermined slots in Weeks 17 (vs. Broncos) and 18 (vs. Dolphins) could now very well end up as 1 p.m. games.

It could very well be tied to the "pushing through" culture at One Patriot Place, but on multiple occasions this season players have come back sooner than their initially-reported injuries made it sound. Running back Rhamondre Stevenson is the latest example: he was expected to miss multiple weeks due to the knee injury he suffered in Week 9 against Green Bay, but was back at practice four days later and ended up playing 26 snaps against the Lions.

With that in mind, Monday's reports might have to be taken with a grain of salt. With the bye week coming up, the Patriots do have some extra time at their hands for recovery.

At the end of the day, of course, they would be wise to play the long game with Maye. Yes, the playoffs are still a distinct possibility even at 5-5, but they need to be careful with their franchise quarterback.
