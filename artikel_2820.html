Purdue: 10 Wisconsin: 42 | Post Game Media

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Saturday afternoon at Ross-Ade Stadium was nothing short of a nightmare for Purdue football, as the Boilermakers were thoroughly dismantled by Wisconsin in a 42-10 blowout. From opening kickoff to the final whistle, the visitors dominated every facet of the matchup, outgaining Purdue 508 total yards to 199 and chewing up 381 yards on the ground. A failed early quarterback experiment and relentless defensive breakdowns left the home team completely overwhelmed as the Badgers cruised to a convincing victory.

Following the dismal performance, Odom saying it was the lowest point of his 27-year coaching career, the Purdue Head Coach didn't mince words in his postgame press conference, calling the effort "awful" and "embarrassing". Let's take a look at what Odom stated in the post game presser while we break down the sobering statistics from the box score and examine the errors that derailed both sides of the ball.

In 27 years of coaching, Barry Odom has seen plenty of football, yet he struggled to find words to describe Saturday's 42-10 defeat. Calling the performance "awful" and "embarrassing," Odom didn't sugarcoat the what happened on the field. "This is new territory for me. I haven't had this in 27 years of coaching," Odom admitted. "Purdue deserves better than that."

Purdue fans were quite surprised with Evans Chuba starting over Ryan Browne. Odom said that was an effort to take advantage of some things in the run game but the experiment lasted all of one possession that was a three and out. The weird thing is that Evans threw the ball twice and Purdue gained only 2 yards on a Ijeboi run. Before Browne was able to get on the field, Purdue was already down 14-0 before he then led Purdue to back to back scoring drives of his own. "They took it completely away on the looks that they were giving," Odom said of the early game plan. "All of a sudden you're sitting there and you got to get something formulated offensively, so we decided to go more 10 personnel and a little bit more 11 and it became an air game." It is hard to believe a week's worth of game planning was simply thrown out in the first three plays of the game. That seems like coaching malpractice and a reason that can be used to move on from a head coach who seems to have more questions than answers at the end of year 2.

With the running game largely non-existent and Purdue playing from behind, Browne took back over under center and finished 13-of-32 passing (with the team going 13-of-34 overall for 133 yards and zero touchdowns). Odom did state about Browne being sat the first possession: "There's not anything that he did that I was disappointed in."

Before the game, Odom knew Wisconsin's quarterback run game would be a focal point and knowing didn't help Purdue stop it. Wisconsin racked up 381 net rushing yards on 51 carries (7.5 yards per rush) and five touchdowns on the ground, led by Darrion Dupree and quarterback Colton Joseph. "We had enough guys in the box today. Wisconsin got a good push," Odom said. "We did not play on their side of the line of scrimmage. I did not think we tackled well… and then we were really bad against quarterback run game."

If you want to know how a game gets out of hand in a hurry, look at the list of big-plays. Wisconsin generated 13 explosive plays totaling 335 yards, including 11 rushing plays of 10-plus yards for 289 yards. Purdue, meanwhile, managed just two big plays all afternoon for a meager 37 yards: a 24-yard Browne-to-Cooper McCutchan pass and a 13-yard Fame Ijeboi run.Interim DC seemed befuddled about how to define what an explosive play is earlier in the week. That should have foreshadowed what was going to happen against the Badgers. The issues with the defense are larger than Kevin Kane and for a program being led by a coach with a defensive background, this performance was inexusable.

Purdue repeatedly put itself behind the chains, averaging just 3.4 yards on 23 first-down plays (78 total yards) while suffering one turnover and zero touchdowns on first down. Wisconsin operated in a different stratosphere on early downs, averaging 9.1 yards per first-down snap (283 yards on 31 plays) and punching in three first-down touchdowns, including a 49-yard burst by Dupree and a 66-yard keeper by Joseph.

Because Wisconsin dominated first down, the Badgers lived in manageable third-down situations, converting 12 of 16 attempts (75%). Even more alarming, Wisconsin rushed 12 times on third down for 96 yards—an 8.0-yard average, picking up nine first downs on the ground. Purdue went just 6-of-14 (42.9%) on third down and managed a dismal 1.8 yards per rush on third-down carries. Purdue now has allowed an atrocious 53% of third down conversions on the season, good for 137 out of 138 possible teams (Colorado State is currently at 57.8%.

When asked specifically about Wisconsin's second touchdown, where all three Purdue linebackers bit on jet sweep action, Odom pointed directly to a breakdown in eye discipline and gap integrity. "The SAM overflowed. He's supposed to be in position and through the C gap; he followed the jet action reverse," Odom explained. "Is it caused by one person out of their gap? You bet it is. But then there's 10 other guys out there that we got to continue to do our job, and we didn't on that one. Eyes were in the wrong spot." That's been a common theme across two seasons, two different DC's, and now a team that has an interim DC that should have their HC putting in more time on that side of the ball.

Perhaps the most concerning moment of Odom's press conference came when he was asked if his team lacked competitiveness, a stark contrast to earlier losses where effort wasn't in question. Odom didn't flinch or avoid the question. "Absolutely, and that's hard to say out loud, but I mean, we all saw it," Odom said, referencing the fourth quarter. "In 27 years I have not as a team that I've coached…I've been beat, but not like today."

Purdue was outgained 508 to 199 in total yards and possessed the ball for just 24:18, leaving the Boilermaker faithful understandably restless as the season winds down. Odom acknowledged the fanbase's frustration head-on while doubling down on his long-term vision for roster retention, incoming freshmen, and transfer portal additions. "I know they're frustrated, disappointed, and they should be," Odom said. "I promise them that there's nobody working harder than I'm going to to represent Purdue University, and eventually things will turn."

Yes it was the weekend before Thanksgiving Break but the announced crowd of just over 40k was the lowest announced number since the 2017 game against Nebraska. That's a sad state of affairs and one that this program simply can't afford to go through again after Brohm rebuilt the fanbase post-Darrell Hazel.
