JJ Redick is encouraging the Lakers to foul more

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Embracing contact and physicality is paramount to being a good-to-great defensive unit. The Los Angeles Lakers intend to become a good-to-great defensive unit, so they can properly complement their league-leading offense and compete for a title this season.

Currently, they're middling and sit 15th in defensive rating, including 11th over their eight-game winning streak, per Cleaning the Glass. Head coach JJ Redick wants his players to assume a hard-nosed identity, even to the point of fouling — at least in certain contexts.

"We encourage physicality. We encourage – if we're not in the bonus and somebody goes to follow and they go to set the on-ball screen to ghost or touch roll, just two-hand shove them…We encourage that," Redick told reporters prior to Sunday's win over the Phoenix Suns. "The biggest thing for us is being smart about when we're fouling and right now we're giving up 6.1 free throws per game on non-shooting fouls in the bonus and that's entirely too many. I think that's bottom third in the NBA right now so we're fouling too much in the bonus. But if we're not in the bonus, foul. That's good. That part doesn't bother me. Boxing out, whatever it may be, our physical players need to be physical and we're not going to tell them to play with handcuffs on."

The Lakers' propensity for putting opposing teams on the line with non-shooting fouls once they're in the bonus has hamstrung their defense. According to Cleaning the Glass, they're conceding a .247 free-throw rate to opponents, the 24th-worst mark in the league. During Redick's first two seasons as head coach of the Lakers, they touted one of the 10 lowest opposing free-throw rates both years.

Perhaps, that increased mark is the byproduct of Los Angeles generating an NBA-high 18.7% opponent turnover rate after finishing 15th in 2025-26 and 18th in 2024-25. Each of the top five in opponent turnover rate sports a bottom 10 opponent free-throw rate this season. Last year, three of the top five did. In 2024-25, six of the top seven were 20th or worse.

Moving forward, the Lakers will have to better balance these realities to fulfill Redick's wishes and build a physical-yet-discerning defense.
