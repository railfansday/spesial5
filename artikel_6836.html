Princess Kate Says Nature Helped Her Heal After Her Cancer Diagnosis

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

“People have long sought comfort in the natural world”

It’s not very often that Princess Catherine sits down for a traditional magazine interview, but in a new story published today in Country Life, the Princess of Wales got candid about her recovery following her 2024 cancer diagnosis and subsequent chemotherapy treatment (she went into remission early last year).

“I was told in hospital that my recovery would not be linear,” Kate says. “[That] there will be good days and bad days, that it will take time.”

She adds: “What is interesting is to see the science now back up what we instinctively know and feel. Humans have always turned to nature. Whether it is the beach for healing and recovery, the countryside for peace and perspective or the mountains to find solitude, people have long sought comfort in the natural world. It is such a restorative environment and incorporating nature into someone’s recovery pathway is vital.”

This past June, Kate completed the National Three Peaks Challenge, a feat that involves climbing the three highest peaks in the United Kingdom in a matter of only 24 hours. Part of the reason she took on the challenge was to highlight “the deeper impact of serious illness and the importance of holistic healthcare.”

The royal has also been teaming up with organizations that focus on outdoor activity and mental health, like Mind Over Mountains and the Dose of Nature charity.

In the wake of these events, interviewer Robert Macfarlane spoke with Kate about how nature tied to her recovery process. Of her “good days and bad days,” Kate noted that nature demonstrates the same ebb and flow. She said, “It shows us that periods of rest, reflection, and renewal are equally as important as moments of growth.”

Kate also spoke about her longstanding love of the outdoors. “I remember from a very early age spending a lot of time in nature, playing in streams, walking in the countryside, blackberry picking or simply time alone in my own world, enjoying the simplicity of ordinary things, such as digging under the laurel bush for what I thought was treasure,” she said. “There was always a sense of freedom I felt in nature, something about being outdoors that is hard to find elsewhere.”

She went on to note how, as adults, we often lose this sense of wonderment, and she hopes to instill the same fascination with the natural world in her three kids: Prince George, 13, Princess Charlotte, 11, and Prince Louis, 8. “We are often so busy we simply forget to pause, slow down and notice the detail in life,” Kate said. “It is something I remind myself to do with my own children; to give them freedom to explore, rather than constantly telling them what to see or what to think.”
