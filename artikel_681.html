The Time Robert Pattinson Snuck Around Rikers Island (and Why It Could Help Him With James Bond)

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Among the actor’s colorful roles is one rather surreal real-life moment in the hoosegow.

Amid all the talk of whether Robert Pattinson’s experiences will allow him to pull off a convincing James Bond, none feel as instructive as the time he went to prison.It was the middle of 2016, and Pattison didn’t commit any crimes, but he badly wanted to be seen as a someone who did. Pattinson was preparing for his role as Connie Nikas, a con on the run, in the Safdie Bros. jangly fugitive-drama Good Time.The Safdies knew some incarcerated people at Rikers, including their friend Buddy Duress, who would go on to be in the film. So, as Pattinson told a reporter writing about the movie in 2017, he begged Josh to get him into the facility. The director obliged, and while inside he and the future James Bond “just kind of went everywhere; we didn’t really ask for permission,” as Pattinson recalled at the time. (The interview was part of a story centered on walking around colorful parts of Queens with the Safdies, a tale in its own right.)The pair got deep inside the infamously locked-down institution, talking to wardens and inmates alike about life on the inside. One interaction in particular stuck with Pattinson.“I had gotten into all this makeup because I didn’t want anyone I met in there to find out who I was or what I was doing; I wanted them to think I belonged. And I thought I was convincing. And then one of the inmates said to me, ‘What are you doing in here? Is it for a scared-straight program?’ [The program to influence innocent-adrift teenagers.] I wasn’t convincing anyone.”“I thought I was looking like some kind of tough guy but he could see right through me,” he continued. (Pattinson is known for telling tall tales in interviews but this one is true — Josh Safdie confirmed the account.)On the one hand this unpersuasive cosplay can seem like one more reason the actor makes for an ill-fitting James Bond — you generally want your 007 to be a little more weathered than a pretty teenager, even ten years later (and also may validate all the skeptics who don’t like that this was kind of a corporate decision.)On the other hand, what’s more James Bond than sneaking around Rikers and getting out unscathed?A general MI6-ish resourcefulness marked Pattinson’s time working with the Safdies. Hot off his Twilight run, Pattinson had reached out to the erstwhile collaborating brothers brothers simply after seeing a promotional photo for Daddy Longlegs, their gritty semi-autobiographical coming of age movie. He then read their script for what would become Uncut Gems and begged to be in that.The Safdies didn’t see Pattison in any of the top roles, including the lead middle-aged hustler played by Adam Sandler. But the filmmakers were so intrigued by his persistence that they decided to write a whole chase-drama for him — the movie that became Good Time.Sweet-talking your way into the confidence of a pair of powerful people so they’ll do something for you they weren’t otherwise ready to do? M would be proud.
