Trezor Shipping Partner Breach Exposes 27,000 Orders as Customers Report Scam Calls

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Trezor said Monday that a data breach at Skladon, one of its shipping partners, exposed personal information tied to about 27,000 hardware-wallet orders, and that some customers have already reported scam calls. The company’s own systems were not compromised, and no wallet backups, PINs or passphrases were involved.

Skladon notified Trezor on Nov. 15 of unauthorized access to systems that held customer shipping data. The breach itself took place on Nov. 7. Trezor believes the attacker exported records for orders placed between Aug. 9 and Nov. 7. An investigation is ongoing.

The exposed records included full names, phone numbers, delivery addresses, email addresses, order dates, and the products purchased and their prices. Trezor said neither it nor Skladon ever holds wallet backups, PINs or passphrases, so the breach does not, by itself, let an attacker move a buyer’s bitcoin. It does give a stranger a home address and proof that the person bought a device designed to store crypto.

Anyone who received Trezor’s incident email and ordered in that window was affected. A small number of customers who ordered between Aug. 9 and 14 may also be included, but the company cannot email them because those details had already been anonymized under its 90-day retention policy. It is reaching those buyers through its blog and posted the same warning on X. Skladon does not ship to the United States, Trezor said, so U.S. customers were not affected by this incident. The company added that Skladon delivers to many countries, and that the leak is not limited to one region.

Trezor told customers to treat any unexpected call, text, email or letter about their order as suspicious, and to be alert to potential risks to their physical security. Scammers may quote real order details, including what was bought and what was paid, to make a message look genuine. Some customers have already reported scam calls.

“Trezor will never cold-call you or ask for your wallet backup,” the company said. Customers should contact support only through the company’s website, never through a link, QR code or number in an unsolicited message, and should never enter a wallet backup anywhere except on their device. If a message contains a threat or makes someone feel unsafe, Trezor said to contact local police first.

This is not the first time a Trezor contractor has lost customer data. In August, a breach at shipping provider ShipMonk exposed records from 13,689 customers, including 11,742 with full names, emails, phone numbers and shipping addresses. Trezor later said another approximately 67,000 U.S. customers who ordered between November 2019 and August 2021 were also affected, after ShipMonk failed to delete records it had assured the company were gone.

In September, a breach at marketing platform Brevo let an attacker send a phishing email through Trezor’s account and, the company later confirmed, export 347,149 newsletter addresses. Trezor took down the malicious domain within 20 minutes, but about 2,500 people had already clicked the link.

“We know this is not the first time customer contact details and addresses have been exposed in an incident involving one of our partners,” Trezor said Monday, adding that it is sorry, especially to anyone affected again. Hardware-wallet makers have faced this risk before. In 2020, an unauthorized party accessed Ledger’s e-commerce and marketing database, leaking over 1 million email addresses; Bitcoin Magazine later reported that more than 200,000 people also had home addresses exposed, and that some victims were later mailed fake devices.

In response, Trezor said anonymous pickup is now available in 18 European countries, so a name and home address need not reach the company, and that it is working to launch the option in the United States. It is also cutting how long it keeps order data from 90 days to 45, with plans to reduce that period further, and it has informed the data protection authority. The company said it is reviewing its relationships with third-party partners and the security requirements it places on them.
