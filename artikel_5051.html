CoW Swap Adds Native Solana Trading With Solver Auctions and a One-Year Volume-Fee Waiver

CoW Swap has launched an alpha that allows traders to start and finish asset swaps on Solana, extending an intent-based model previously associated with Ethereum. The basic difference is worth understanding: rather than manually choosing a route and submitting a conventional swap, users sign an instruction that competing solvers try to execute at acceptable terms. CoW DAO is also waiving its own volume fee on Solana for at least a year, a decision that should not be mistaken for the disappearance of all trading costs.

Before this release, CoW Swap could facilitate certain routes into Solana from Ethereum-compatible networks. The new alpha supports trades whose starting and ending assets are on Solana itself. That is a different proposition for applications already serving Solana users. The launch includes an integration with lending platform Kamino and access to a solver network that CoW DAO describes as comprising more than 30 participants. The alpha designation also matters: traders should check supported assets, order conditions and current operational limitations before assuming every feature available on other chains is identical.

With an intent-based order, a trader specifies the assets and the price or conditions they will accept. Solvers can compete to find a matching trade or route liquidity through available venues. CoW Swap says its batching and execution design can help reduce opportunities for certain sandwich attacks and avoid some failed on-chain swaps. That is a design objective, not a guarantee that every order will fill at the best imaginable price. Market depth, slippage, solver incentives and latency still shape outcomes, especially for thinly traded tokens.

CoW DAO says it will waive its own Solana volume fee for at least the first 12 months. Users should distinguish that platform charge from the economic costs of crossing bid-ask spreads, securing liquidity and potentially using network resources. Sponsored transactions can also change who pays an upfront network fee without making trade execution economically costless. The order’s displayed execution price and the eventual tokens received remain the meaningful comparison with other venues. Fee promotions are useful, but not a substitute for checking route quality.

Solana already has active trading infrastructure, so the question is not whether CoW Swap brings decentralized exchanges to a previously empty market. It is whether solver competition and order batching create a sufficiently different experience to attract liquidity and integrations. For lending applications, a reliable swap path can also affect liquidation and portfolio-management workflows. Independent measurements of fills, failed attempts, spreads and protection against adverse ordering will be more persuasive than the cumulative trade-volume figures of the protocol’s older networks.

Execution quality needs independent measurement. Users comparing an intent-based solver with a traditional aggregator should examine the final amount received after all network and protocol costs, the time needed to settle and how failed or partially filled routes are handled. The headline fee waiver should not obscure transaction costs or liquidity-provider economics that may still apply. Solana's faster settlement can improve the experience, but it does not by itself guarantee that every swap will beat a direct exchange quote under volatile market conditions.
