The tactical 'freedom' Celtic give Callum Osmand and his unlikely Camilo Duran bond

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

With Celtic a goal behind against Besiktas in the 60th minute, on came Callum Osmand. His impact was almost instantaneous, equalising with his first touch and, soon after, setting up fellow substitute Camilo Duran with a refined reverse pass.

Martin O'Neill said it was all part of the masterplan. The Celtic manager rotated his XI heavily before calling on the cavalry come the hour.

Osmand's influence on the 3-2 victory was profound. He enthused the home support with fresh attacking impetus, haring at Besiktas' defence and providing that crucial end product.

Although Osmand averages a goal or assist every 90 minutes this season, he concedes he must improve.

"I would say I'm nowhere near where I want to be personally, and there's stuff I need to tidy up on, decision-making and all this stuff," Osmand said. "But I feel like the more games I play, the more I understand and the more I learn.

"So on a personal note, that's what I want to do, just keep learning and keep understanding my role, like the team and obviously the players you're playing with.

"And I've still only played a handful of games, so it's tough learning, but I feel like when you get thrown in at the deep end, it's the best way to learn, and the worst games are what you learn off more than the good games.

"It's just as long as I can get better every game, that's all I really want to focus on."

Osmand joined Celtic from Fulham last summer as a central striker, yet since the defeat to Benfica last month, he's been moulded into a left winger.

Learning a new position isn't easy, though Osmand likes the freedom that comes with playing out wide.

"It is new for me, but I'm really enjoying it," he said. "It's a really fun position and it's very, very attacking.

"I have a lot of freedom and the staff give me that freedom and tell me to go and try and make something happen. So it's a great feeling when you know you can just go and sort of impose your game."

Osmand did exactly that on Thursday evening. O'Neill told him to 'just go' before coming on, instructing the 20-year-old to bring the energy that gets supporters on their feet.

"I think that's the best feeling in football, when you can get everyone behind you and it feels amazing, so that's what I sort of strive for, that feeling," Osmand said.

His presence helped flip the game on its head, offering Celtic fresh hope of Europa League progression after defeats to Ferencvaros, Benfica, and Celta Vigo.

O'Neill's side can look ahead to their trip to Torreense later this month knowing it means something.

"For us, it felt like a must win, that was our sort of mindset going into that. This was sort of it," Osmand said of the Besiktas game. "Like we knew we could still qualify, but we knew we had to get something out of Thursday night, and I think the boys done amazing, so hopefully we just get some confidence and build on from this.

"We have some tougher away ties, but hopefully we can use that and just use this, and that gives us confidence to play more free, and take more risks.

"Sometimes when you're not as confident, it's tough to play the football you want to play, but as you can see, when we're confident, it just goes forward every time, and it's a good feeling, so hopefully we can build on that."

For Celtic, wins like Thursday's have proven rare this season. There was a real sense of togetherness at the full-time whistle.

O'Neill raised his right fist to the heavens in celebration before gracing the turf and embracing his players.

Osmand knows Celtic haven't been at their best of late. Last weekend's defeat at Falkirk sparked some all too familiar emotions.

Thursday sent Celtic to the other end of the spectrum. Now they must stay there.

"I think we know that we're a good side," Osmand said. "We all back each other, and I think when we're all together, and the fans are with us, and you feel it, you just want to get everyone sort of on one page, and then we feel like an amazing team.

"It's sometimes tough to get to that point, because we're lacking confidence, or we're not playing as well, but I think Thursday night really showed everyone was together, and the final whistle gave us that. It should give us that confidence to go on."

Osmand dovetailed superbly with Duran after coming on against Besiktas, setting up the Colombian striker's first goal of the triumph.

That relationship extends beyond the pitch, even if a language barrier remains.

On Duran, Osmand said: "He's unbelievable, a really top player. You can just see even by the way he walks and the way he moves.

"I learn off him every day. He's an amazing guy.

"His English is really not great, but somehow we manage to talk to each other a lot. I think I find him quite a good friend of mine now.

"And although we might not speak many words, you can tell just by looking... I don't know how to describe it. Like we talk to each other a lot. Maybe it's little words, but I feel like we get on really well. He's a great guy."

You wouldn't bet against Duran and Osmand once again coming to the fore when Celtic host Kilmarnock in the William Hill Premiership on Sunday afternoon.

The champions defeated Neil McCann's side 5-1 at Rugby Park back in August. A similar result would go a long way to building on the positive feeling triggered by Thursday's victory.

"I think sometimes when you don't give a team that's obviously not doing as well respect, then you can get caught, and sometimes you don't want to get too overconfident going into these games, because then you'll get caught out," Osmand added.

"I think it's just giving every team you play respect, and knowing that most teams can hurt us, and we have to just go in with the same mentality as Thursday night, and hopefully get a good result.

"I think it's really important for the league. Obviously, we're still at the top of the league, but obviously the gap can change at any time, so it's just about winning every game possible in the league, and just building on everything."
