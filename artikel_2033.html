Daizen Maeda latest is music to the ears of Celtic - verdict

Celtic officials will be feeling validated in their decision to sanction the sale of Daizen Maeda.

The Japanese international was a fan favourite at the Glasgow club, but had expressed a wish for a new challenge.

Maeda was then ultimately sold to Ipswich Town, with the Tractor Boys forking out £10million.

However, things have not gone entirely to plan for him since he made the move to the Premier League.

The 28-year-old has struggled to make the same impact he regularly produced in Scotland.

And Celtic will be rubbing their hands over their decision to grant Maeda's transfer wish.

Maeda moved to Portman Road with a strong reputation for being one of the most dangerous and influential attackers in Scotland.

His pace, movement and finishing had long been hailed by the Scottish champions, and supporters were sad to see him exit.

Last season he picked up 20 goal contributions for Celtic, and he has struggled to produce even close to that output this campaign.

As per FotMob, Maeda has been fairly anonymous in an attacking sense for Ipswich.

On a per-90 basis, Maeda is ranked in the 9.5th percentile for shots, with just 1.05, showing he is barely getting any chances in the Premier League.

Additionally, the Asian forward is ranked in the 5.3rd percentile for chances created per 90, with only 0.17.

Failing to even set up his teammates on a regular basis as well.

Those figures highlight just how far Maeda has fallen since leaving Celtic in the recent summer window.

Ipswich are not a team that will be dominating games, so there may be suggestions that it is the Tractor Boys' style hampering him.

However, attacking players such as Jack Clarke and Julio Enciso have been far outperforming the former Celtic forward in all of the above stats.

That would suggest Maeda cannot simply use the team's overall approach as an excuse for his lack of impact.

Both Clarke and Enciso have managed to make their individual qualities count despite playing in the same side and facing similar challenges.

And for that reason, there will be joy back in Glasgow at the fee they were able to bring in.
