Geno Auriemma, Dawn Staley shake hands prior to UConn-South Carolina matchup

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

UNCASVILLE, CT — Call it the handshake watched around the world.

People all around the court before the UConn-South Carolina women's basketball game on Tuesday, Nov. 24, held up their phones to record history at Mohegan Sun Arena.

UConn coach Geno Auriemma and South Carolina coach Dawn Staley, who had a dustup in the waning moments of last season's national semifinal over a pregame handshake, did indeed shake hands before the rematch. The friendly exchange ended months of speculation about the coaches playing nice when reunited.

Staley's squad beat UConn, 62-48, in the Final Four last season, where the Gamecocks ended the Huskies' undefeated season, in Phoenix. More memorable than the score or stats associated with the game were Auriemma's actions near the end of it. He lashed out against Staley after accusing her of not shaking his hand before the contest. A heated exchange ensued and the two coaches had to be separated by assistant coaches and referees.

"For 41 years I've been coaching, 25 Final Fours and before the game, the protocol is you meet at halfcourt," Auriemma said at the time. "Anybody ever see that before? Two coaches meet at halfcourt and shake hands. Correct? Ever see it? They announce it on the loud speaker. And I waited there for like three minutes. (shrugs) So it is what it is."

In an interview with Holly Rowe, Staley denied any disrespect.

"I'm going to let you know this, I'm of integrity. I'm of integrity," Staley said. "So if I did something wrong to Geno, I have no idea what I did, I guess he thought I didn't shake his hand at the beginning of the game, I don't know. I went down there pregame, shook everybody on his staff's hand … but hey, sometimes things get heated. We move on,"

The next day, Auriemma apologized in a statement.

"There's no excuse for how I handled the end of the game vs. South Carolina. It's unlike what I do and what our standard is here at Connecticut," Auriemma said. "The story should be how well South Carolina played, and I don't want my actions to detract from that. I've had a great relationship with their staff, and I sincerely want to apologize to them."

It was announced Saturday, Nov. 21, that coaches will no longer be required to shake hands before the start of the game at the NCAA Tournament.

"The committee approved a policy change to no longer require coaches to shake hands pregame," a bullet point in the document reads. "The committee encourages coaches to shake hands informally before team introductions."
