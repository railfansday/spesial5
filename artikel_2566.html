Tee Higgins’ controversial TD proves NFL referees still don’t know what a catch is

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Tee Higgins caught a touchdown pass against the Washington Commanders in Week 11. At least we think it was a touchdown. The officials called it a catch, but despite the decisive call, it left viewers with more questions than answers.

Joe Burrow hit Higgins for a 31-yard touchdown with 1:55 remaining in the second quarter. It was a beautiful throw that Higgins caught as he fell to the ground in the end zone. Upon further review, it looked a lot like the catch that Jacksonville's Parker Washington "didn't" make on Sunday against the New York Giants. In that game officials decided that the ball was moving too much and took a crucial late score off the board. In this case it counted.

Both Joe Buck and ESPN rules analyst Russell Yurk took the opportunity to bring up the controversial call from Sunday.

Buck asked the rules expert why this was a touchdown, but Washington's catch was not. Yurk agreed with what CBS's Gene Steratore said during yesterday's game and said they both should have been catches.

This is a problem. And worse than that, it's not a new problem. The NFL still doesn't know what constitutes a catch. The forward pass has been legal since 1906. Instant replay was first used in the 1980's. We've been debating what is a catch for literal decades. This league is 100 years old and we still can't look at a slow motion replay of a football landing in someone's hands and know what the officials are going to say about it. There are different interpretations on what a catch is depending on what part of the field a guy is on. And this is proof it can literally change from day to day.

Luckily, the NFL is one of those organizations where there is no such thing as bad publicity. If everyone know what a catch was there would be less to argue about. There would be fewer topics to debate.

So don't worry about common sense or definitions. NFL referees will continue to make it up as they go and keep things interesting. Unless your team is on the wrong side of the call, does it really matter?
