Florida pastor found guilty over role in assassination of Haitian president

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Christian Emmanuel Sanon was found guilty of all eight counts against him in a Florida court on Wednesday

A Florida pastor has been found guilty of a number of charges relating to the assassination of the Haitian President Jovenel MoÃ¯se.

Christian Emmanuel Sanon, a dual US-Haitian national, was convicted of all eight charges against him, including conspiracy to kill or kidnap a person. He faces a potential sentence of life in prison.

The 67-year-old was accused of being involved in a plot to replace MoÃ¯se as Haiti's president with himself in 2021, allegedly so he could award lucrative contracts to a Florida-based security outfit once in place.

The murder of MoÃ¯se has left Haiti without a leader for five years. That political vacuum has intensified gang violence in the impoverished Caribbean nation.

A presidential election is scheduled for next year and MoÃ¯se's wife, Martine, who was injured in the attack that killed her husband, announced this week that she intended to stand.

Sanon was found guilty on Wednesday following an eight-week trial in federal court in Miami, according to CBS, the BBC's US partner. His lawyer said he maintains his innocence and that he intends to appeal against the verdict, it reports.

Prosecutors said Sanon had "political aspirations" in Haiti and, in April 2021, met several other co-conspirators to devise a plan to oust MoÃ¯se in an armed raid on the presidential palace in the capital, Port-au-Prince.

Among them were the heads of the companies Counter Terrorist Unit Federal Academy and Counter Terrorist Unit Security - collectively referred to as CTU - who would finance the coup in exchange for government contracts for infrastructure projects, and to provide security forces and equipment.

A few months later, the other conspirators realised that Sanon had "neither the constitutional qualifications nor the popular support" to become Haiti's president and so shifted to another candidate, the US Department of Justice said.

At the same time, the plot had developed from one to forcibly remove MoÃ¯se from office to assassinating him, investigators said.

Dozens of people have been implicated in the conspiracy, which prosecutors said involved co-ordinating funding for and delivering weapons and mercenaries to Haiti to carry out the attack.

On 6 July that year, a group of Colombian mercenaries entered the presidential palace and killed MoÃ¯se. His wife, who was also injured in the attack, said at the time that the assailants had "riddled" her husband with bullets.

The successful assassination plot raised questions in Haiti about the relative ease with which the mercenaries were able to gain access to the president.

The country - the poorest in the Americas - has been ravaged by gang violence for years, but MoÃ¯se's assassination ushered in a period of worsening instability that emboldened gangs and prompted the deployment of a multinational force.

However, this has struggled to penetrate areas where gangs hold sway and more than 5,700 people have been killed or injured in the year to 11 September, the UN said last month.

Sanon and several other co-conspirators were arrested by Haitian authorities, while three mercenaries were killed in a gun battle with police. Eighteen people were transferred to Florida in September to stand trial over the plot.

Four men, including the heads of the CTU, were convicted of their role earlier this year. Sanon had been scheduled to be tried with them, but his case was delayed due to health reasons, according to CBS.

Sanon was born in Haiti but moved to the US as a teenager. He told the court he had worked in churches in Haitian immigrant communities in several parts of the US including Florida, US media report.
