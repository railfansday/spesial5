Trump sealed deal for Nvidia to rehire Jensen Huang as CEO

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The stunning decision by Nvidia's board to bring back Jensen Huang as CEO was sealed by a personal promise from President Donald Trump on Sunday that Huang had not broken national security laws, three people familiar with the board's thinking told CNBC.

Several other factors also set the stage for Huang's return and accelerated discussions with him over the weekend, said the people, who spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss internal deliberations close to Nvidia's board.

A sharp stock price drop after his resignation and questions about the leadership of interim CEO Colette Kress also spurred the board's decision, the people said.

"I convinced them," Trump told CNBC's Eamon Javers in a phone interview Monday morning, shortly after Nvidia announced Huang's return.

"I made the deal," the president said. "It was a lot easier to convince them yesterday than it was a week and a half ago.

Trump said Nvidia's board asked for guarantees that Huang, who resigned less than two weeks ago, was not a threat to national security.

"And we gave them assurances, at least with everything we know," the president said. "We believe he's fine. He's absolutely fine."

Senior Trump administration officials reached out to the board overnight Friday to inform them that Huang had not violated U.S. national security laws, people familiar with the board's actions said.

After the White House outreach, the board contacted Huang, who joined meetings on Saturday and Sunday to discuss his potential return, the people said.

Huang's spokesman did not respond to a request for comment when asked about his contact with the board and other events leading up to his return.

The Nvidia founder resigned on Nov. 12 after reports that he had an affair with an Nvidia employee and with Alice Huang, a former executive at the Singapore-based company Megaspeed who is suspected of being a spy for China. A report of an affair with a second Nvidia employee broke right after he quit.

Nvidia's board named Kress, the company's chief financial officer, as the interim CEO while it searched for a permanent successor.

But within days of Kress taking over, some employees and investors who spoke to CNBC questioned her stewardship, as many of them clamored for Huang's return.

Kress' performances at an all-hands company meeting on Wednesday and on Nvidia's earnings call with analysts a day earlier were seen by some investors as uninspiring and underwhelming.

As the board faced those concerns amid a stock drop of more than 25% over the past week, Huang was aggressively working the phones with major tech CEOs who wanted to see him return to Nvidia, according to sources close to Huang.

In the days after he resigned, Huang spoke with OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, OpenAI President Greg Brockman, and Elon Musk, CEO of SpaceX and Tesla, one of the sources said.

Huang wanted his job back, but he was not sure what the return path looked like, the sources said.

On Nov. 17, less than a week after quitting, Huang issued a public statement saying he was open to returning as CEO.

"I've seen an outpouring of calls from NVIDIA's employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO," he said.

"NVIDIA is a once-in-a-generation company," he added. "If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO."

On the same day, Nvidia's newly appointed board chair, Harvey Jones, told analysts on the company's earnings call that the board did "not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO."

Kress, the following day, told company employees at the all-hands meeting, "Let's move forward."

Nvidia's public stance against Huang returning came as members of Congress moved ahead with plans to interview him at hearings and as criticism grew over the company's business in Asia.

The Department of Justice and the Commerce Department, in an unusual joint public statement on Nov. 17, said they were investigating Megaspeed for possible diversion of advanced Nvidia semiconductor chips to China in violation of U.S. export control law.

"Sensitive technologies like semiconductors are among the most valuable and strategic assets of the U.S. economy," the departments said.

But days later, senior Trump administration officials contacted Nvidia's board to tell them that Jensen Huang himself was not a risk, according to the sources.

The Department of Commerce added Megaspeed to a trade blacklist later Monday.

Trump, during his interview Monday with CNBC, was asked what Attorney General Todd Blanche had told him about potential criminal liability for Huang and the question of whether Nvidia chips had ended up in China in violation of U.S. export laws.

"They did a pretty good investigation, and they felt that he, himself, was exonerated," Trump said.

Both Trump and Vice President JD Vance became convinced that Huang was a singular figure who is preventing closed-source model AI companies, such as Anthropic, from getting too powerful, according to a person familiar with the matter, who spoke on condition of anonymity to disclose private discussions.

The person said Trump and Vance were briefed on the national security elements of the situation and saw no evidence that Huang did anything illegal or untoward in that regard.

Trump believes his administration's close working relationship with Huang gives the U.S. its best opportunity to protect national security and prevent cutting-edge AI chips from reaching banned customers, according to the person.

Trump said he spoke with Huang on Sunday, after Nvidia had voted unanimously to reinstate him.

"I called him up, and I said: 'I ask you to do one thing, Jensen. Work hard,'" Trump said.

"He knows what to do and what not to do," Trump said. "But I just said work hard because if you work hard, there's nobody that can capture what you've done. Nobody, nobody close."

— CNBC's Kevin Breuninger and Kif Leswing contributed to this article.
