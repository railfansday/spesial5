FINAL (OT): Arizona 35, Kansas State 28

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

For the first time in weeks, a K-State game was actually exciting, but after a third quarter for the ages, the Kansas State defense fell apart at the end. The Purple Cats gave up an 11-point fourth-quarter lead before falling in overtime to Arizona in a 35-28 decision at Bill Snyder Family Stadium.

Rainy conditions definitely aided both defenses, but in the phase that mattered K-State (4-7, 1-7 Big 12) came out on top, outrushing Arizona by 140 yards. But that domination had a cost; K-State hardly tried to throw the ball, while Noah Fifita had 323 yards and a pair of touchdown passes for Arizona (8-3, 5-3 Big 12) while leading the Beardown Gang to a lightning quick fourth-quarter comeback.

The game started with three straight 3-and-outs, and almost a fourth until Noah Fifita escaped a sack at his own 4-yard line to complete a pass and move the chains. Arizona then drove downfield, but Fifita threw incomplete on 4th-and-goal at the 1, and the Cats took over with the scoreboard still clean. But the Cats went 3-and-out again, and Arizona drove again from midfield. Fifita scored on a 8-yard keeper, and Arizona took the first lead.

The Cats offense finally did respond, picking up two first downs before Duff found Josh Manning on the sideline and Manning appeared to have skirted the sideline for a 35-yard gain to the Arizona 16. On review, the officials nearly overruled the call, but the evidence wasn't clear enough and the play stood as called. Two plays later, Rodney Fields hurdled a defender to pick up 13, and then barged in from the 3 on the next play to tie things up.

A 24-yard completion from Fifita to Giovanni Richardson turned into 39 when Nick McClellan was called for a face mask, but a 12-yard completion on the next play was wiped out by ineligible man downfield. Still, Fifita completed two more passes to get Arizona to the K-State 4 and 1st-and-goal, and on third down Kedrick Reescano scored on a one-yard run.

A pass interference call saved K-State from another 3-and-out, then Duff found Garrett Oakley for a first down. A 42-yard TD run by Fields was wiped out by a holding call on Manning, turning it into just a 4-yard gain. But a few plays later, Duff hit Derrick Salley deep for a 34-yard score to tie things up again.

Arizona went 3-and-out to start the second half, and after Fields covered up a Duff fumble, so did K-State. In an amazing turn of events for a K-State team, Simon McClannan's punt was blocked, giving Arizona the ball at the 21. The K-State defense prevented a first down, but couldn't prevent a 33-yard field goal by Michael Salgado-Medina.

Monterrio Elston returned the kickoff to midfield, but on 4th-and-nothing Fields was stopped at the line, turning the ball over. Arizona also went 3-and-out, and on 2nd-and-1 a few plays later, Duff had no receivers, ran for the first down… and 57 more yards, racing all the way to the end zone to give K-State their first lead of the game.

The defense locked Arizona down on the next drive, forcing Fifita to fumble on 3rd-and-11, the ball covered by Zashon Rich. Three plays later, Duff was picked off in the end zone by Cam Chapa. Two Arizona penalties and a sack by Wendell Gregory forced yet another 3-and-out, but Chase Ridley blasted a 70-yard punt to ruin the field position advantage.

The Cats went 3-and-out, choosing to punt on 4th-and-inches; the defense did their job, stopping Arizona on 3rd-and-7 with a great open field tackle by Ben Osueke. Arizona went for it on 4th-and-3, and Osueke did it again, holding the visitors to two yards and getting the ball back at the K-State 39. But another 3-and-out ensued — followed by McClannon dropping a punt at Arizona's 2-yard line. A bad snap was covered in the end zone by Gregory, and suddenly K-State was up 28-17.

Arizona drove, an interception by Rich was wiped out by an illegal substitution penalty. Arizona got down to the 4-yard-line, but the defense held, forcing Salgado-Medina to kick another field goal, this one from 26 yards out.

On 3rd-and-3 on the next drive, Fields got hit after a 5-yard gain… and carried the Arizona defense another ten yards, passing 100 on the night. On 4th-and-4 from the 42, Klein chose to punt, and McClannan dropped it at the 2 again — but didn't get the bounce, and a touchback ensued.

Fifita broke free, picking up 23 yards and another five got added due to sideline interference on K-State. On the next play, Fifita hit Isaiah Mizell from 35 yards out, and converted the two on a pitch-back to Fifita and a pass to Richardson to tie the game with 1:52 left.

The Cats went to work. Fields ran for 11. Duff ran for 9, then Fields broke free for another 10. An incompletion to Oakley was wiped out by an illegal formation penalty, then a holding penalty made it 1st-and-25 and everything stalled. McClannan put another punt inside the five, and the 29 seconds Arizona had available weren't enough once Darien Whitaker sacked Fifita. For the second time this season, K-State headed to overtime against a former Pac-12 team.

Getting the ball first, Fifita hit Richardson from 25 yards, and Salgado-Medina converted. On K-State's turn, a fake pitch to Fields led to Duff getting down to the 4, but Fields was stood up twice, setting up 4th-and-1. A fake handoff resulted in Duff being stopped short himself, and once again K-State fell in overtime.

Duff was 7-15 for 110 yards with a touchdown and an interception, while rushing for 66 yards on 11 carries. Fields ran for 144 yards on 28 tries with a score, and Joe Jackson added 29 yards on 10 carries. Manning, Oakley, and Jaron Tibbs each had 2 catches for 39, 21, and 16 yards respectively; Salley had one catch for 34 yards.

Arizona outgained K-State 422-349, and 323-110 in the air. K-State, however, won the ground game in a landslide, 239-99, one of the best performances against the run the Cats have managed all year. Take Arizona's two longest runs off the board and they only managed 54 yards on 29 attempts. The ancillary stats were basically even, except for K-State recording four sacks to Arizona's two.

Again, things are such a mess, it's really hard to quantify much. Duff performed well enough to keep K-State in the game, but not enough to lead them to victory. The defense had a hell of a third quarter, but not so much otherwise. The offensive line was again a letdown, except in the running game, which showed up again after being shaky for weeks.

No question it's Rodney Fields on offense, and on defense we'll go with Logan Bartley, who was once again all over the place. Ten tackles, two pass breakups, and his coverage was enough to help force those four sacks.

Of course this hell season's going to end with the final uninterrupted edition of Farmageddon, in the hellscape of Ames. Saturday, 1:00 PM, on ESPN+. Yeah, relegated to streaming to end the year. So it goes.
