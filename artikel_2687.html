12 winners, 7 losers from Detroit Lions’ loss to the Chicago Bears

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

It was a Thanksgiving feast for offenses on this day. Unfortunately for the Detroit Lions, they were on the wrong side of a 39-35 barnburner.

The Chicago Bears pulled ahead with a last-second touchdown pass, the culmination of a defense that could not find its footing. The Lions got some phenomenal performances from the offense, including Jahmyr Gibbs going over the 200-yard mark, but it wound up not mattering as the defense could not force Chicago off the field.

This game was Detroit's biggest test since Week 8, and while there were some positives, a lot has to go wrong to lose a game like this. Let us examine the winners and losers from this Thanksgiving Day tilt.

Note: The label of "loser" is used to evaluate and contrast in-game performance and is not an indictment of a player's ability, value, or character. The term is not intended to be disrespectful in nature.

The Lions defense had impressed over the last six games, but there was the caveat that they came against weak opponents. Detroit's first game against Chicago would be their first true test—how could they handle a high-flying offense?

It was near-universal failure from the defense, specifically in the second half. The Bears turned two third quarter possessions into touchdowns. In the fourth quarter after the Lions scored a 70-yard touchdown to go up 35-24 with 8:20 left in the game—normally the dagger—the defense utterly collapsed. They gave up an uninspired, 71-yard touchdown drive in just one minute and three plays. Detroit was fortunate that the Bears had a turnover on downs on their next drive, but promptly allowed a 68-yard touchdown drive to end the game.

The Lions were constantly getting beat in the flats, allowing D'Andre Swift to rack up a team-leading 97 receiving yards. Caleb Williams rarely had to make difficult decisions or throws because the flat was consistently open to attack. It's difficult to say initially if there was a schematic issue or a performance issue, but both problems reflect poorly on Sheppard. This persisted throughout the game—it's one thing to get beat like this early, but to get beat drive after drive on the exact same play is beyond frustrating, it's incompetent. Couple this with the Lions opting to rush just three players on the pivotal final Bears goal line possession, and it was defensive malpractice from Detroit.

Even if Detroit rebounds in the next weeks against the Atlanta Falcons or Tennessee Titans, how much faith are you actually putting in the defense? Detroit might not face another top-tier offense until their trip to Chicago in January, so my concerns about the performance of this defense will remain until then.

No Aidan Hutchinson, no DJ Wonnum. Enter Derrick Moore. He was a game-changer early on Thanksgiving. In the first half, this was his stat line: four tackles, two sacks, and two forced fumbles, one of which resulted in a safety (and the ensuing drive ended with a touchdown). Chicago scored just 10 points in the first half and Moore's performance was a key reason why.

Moore was held off the stat line in the second half, but there are some extenuating circumstances. For one, Chicago started to attack the flats more and more, thus giving the Lions pass rush little time to attack. Considering that the Bears were in obvious passing downs for most of the second half, the pass rush largely fell on him, rookie Anthony Lucas, or the blitz. Secondly, it was noted that Moore had a heating pad on his knee, showing he was battling some kind of injury. Also, on the Lions' lone second-half sack by Malcolm Rodriguez, Moore was a hair behind him and would have had the sack otherwise.

All in all, you can see the difference Moore can make when things are clicking.

Goff was locked in on Thanksgiving. Last week against the Buccaneers, Goff had one of his best games of the year, and his encore performance this week was just as good. Play after play, Goff was making brilliant throws with pinpoint accuracy. He and Amon-Ra St. Brown had a telepathic-like connection—on multiple instances, Goff put the ball in the exact spot where St. Brown would be, even with a defender a hair out of place. Goff isn't afraid to throw a block either. I don't fault him for the interception, as it was tipped and bounced off the hands of Jameson Williams.

Goff finished the day going 25-of-36 for 313 yards, one touchdown, and one interception. I don't fault him for the interception, as it was tipped and bounced off the hands of Jameson Williams. Goff is as steady as they come. He will never outrun defenses, nor is he an incredible scrambler, but he is smart and poised. Those are two traits that are invaluable for an NFL offense. I did not love the failed second-down pass to Brock Wright late in the game, both in terms of play call or execution—that one play was the extent my complaints with Goff on the day.

Joe Burrow and Brock Purdy are MVP frontrunners right now, but Goff should be on their heels. Although…

…you can make a legitimate case that Gibbs is the MVP as well. Gibbs finally got his breakthrough run this week, scampering 70 yards down the field for his third score of the day. He finished the day with an absurd 205 rushing yards on 21 carries plus three touchdowns. He quietly added five catches for 47 yards as well. A performance like this would be a game-winner under normal circumstances, but the Lions defense wasted it.

Week in, week out, Gibbs is the spark plug of the offense. He is the rare non-quarterback worthy of earning the title of Most Valuable Player. Unfortunately, having Gibbs and Goff on the same team arguably works against them with regards to MVP voting—quarterback is the more valuable position, yet Gibbs is playing at such a high level. I would personally have Gibbs ahead in that race, but we shall see come award season.

Three false start penalties should normally get Scruggs into the loser category, but I think there is a valid excuse for at least two of them: Bears defensive tackle Jeffrey Simmons was seemingly yelling the snap. This is illegal, by the way. After the second false start, Simmons received a talking to by the officials during a commercial break. It is unclear if Simmons was again jawing to force Scruggs' third false start, so I will dock him for that one at least.

Aside from the penalties, Scruggs was dynamite as a blocker. The CBS commentary crew wouldn't mention it, but Simmons was getting worked by the Lions interior and Scruggs specifically. On the pivotal 39-yard catch by Jameson Williams at the end of the first half, it was Scruggs putting Simmons on the ground:

If you rewatch the game, I implore that you watch for Simmons' orange sleeves. More often than not, it was Scruggs winning those battles, not the All-Pro defensive tackle. Simmons finished without a sack and per PFF's live stats, just one pressure recorded. Considering that Scruggs had a tough time versus Vita Vea last week, seeing him succeed against Simmons like this is a massive win. Penalties can be cleaned up, especially when the other side should have been flagged for it instead.

Normally one of the Lions' most athletic cover linebackers, Rodriguez looked anything but on Thursday. In coverage, he looked incredibly sluggish. On a D'Andre Swift reception, his slow reaction led to him getting stiff-armed en route to a 33-yard reception. On the ensuing play, Rodriguez failed to stick with Stephen Carlson on a Caleb Williams bootleg, resulting in a catch to set up Chicago deep in Lions territory. In the fourth quarter, he got juked by Williams on a scramble, giving Chicago a close fourth down attempt that they wound up converting—if Rodriguez gets the tackle immediately, the Bears likely have to punt. He saved that drive with a third-down sack a few plays later, but it was a day with more bad than good.

I try to avoid listing a player as a "loser" if their role was insignificant, but I have to put Barnes in this category for that same reason: he was seemingly benched against the Bears. When Jack Campbell went down with injury, the expectation was that Barnes would lead the linebacking group. Only a few weeks later, he has tumbled down the depth chart. Rodriguez seeing an uptick of snaps was not unexpected, but Barnes getting out-snapped by Trevor Nowaske and K.J. Britt for most of the game is a shock. Barnes would get an expanded role in the second half, but partly because Rodriguez rolled his ankle.

Barnes had a touchdown-saving pass breakup late in the game, but it was otherwise a forgettable outing for him.

St. Brown has been playing second fiddle to Jahmyr Gibbs this season, but do not let that detract from his impact. St. Brown finished as the Lions' leading receiver and he earned it with multiple tough catches. On one play, he got sandwiched by two defenders right after the catch, yet he still held on. On another, he dove out and landed hard on his shoulder and head. He stepped out for a bit but returned to action soon after.

Like Goff, he is a steady presence for the Lions offense. You know what you're getting with him, and that is sure-handedness, elite route roughing, and toughness.

Winner: Roger McCreary, CBMcCreary continues to play strong football after a rough early season. He logged three passes defended on Thursday, one of which was a tipped ball on a blitz.

Loser: Rock Ya-Sin, CBYa-Sin got beat for a pair of touchdowns, and while his coverage was generally fine, there were plenty of instances were you'd hope for a pass breakup rather than a tackle.

Winner: Sam LaPorta, TELaPorta got into the end zone for the first time since Week 5. After a slow stretch, he has now put together back-to-back solid performances.

Winner: Trevor Nowaske, LBIn general, it was a rough game for the linebackers, but Nowaske was the exception. He continues to impress when called upon, looking fast yet disciplined as a run defender and in coverage. He deserves to be an every-down player going forward.

Loser: Anthony Lucas, DEIt was a tough ask for the undrafted rookie, but he looked unrefined as a pass rusher and a liability in run defense. On multiple occasions, he failed to set the edge, resulting in a big Chicago play. Sprinkle in a taunting penalty, and it was not a great game.

Winner: Mehki Wingo, DTFilling the role of Skyler Gill-Howard, Wingo had some great pass rushing reps, including nearly getting to Williams on the Moore strip-sack-safety.

Winners: Tyleik Williams and Alim McNeill, DTsThe Bears run game had zero success up the middle due to Williams and McNeill holding down the fort. McNeill also added a sack. They have quietly developed into a top-tier tackle duo in the league.

Loser: K.J. Britt, LBBritt had some tough assignments against a good stable of Bears running backs, but he struggled keeping up at times. A bit of a return to earth for the former Patriots practice squader.

Winner and Loser: Christian Mahogany, OGMahogany was a snow plow on offense, bullying the Bears interior next to Penei Sewell and blasting lanes for Gibbs. However, the taunting penalty—even if it was a weak call—ultimately cost Detroit dearly. Being up 4 points instead of 3 changes the course of that final drive.

Loser: Jake Bates, KMahogany gets blamed for the taunting penalty, but Bates should still make a 48-yard kick. If he does, all of this discussion becomes moot.
