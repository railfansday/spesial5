US consortium wins huge Kenya mining deal, beating Chinese rivals

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The US is playing catch-up with China's dominance in critical minerals mining and processing across Africa (file photo)

A US-led consortium, Frontier Minerals, has won the tender to develop Kenya's Mrima Hill mining project, beating seven bidders, including companies from China and Australia.

The site is estimated to hold deposits of critical minerals worth more than $60bn (Â£45bn), including niobium, used in electric vehicles, aerospace and defence technology.

Frontier Minerals says its proposal aims to keep more of the economic benefits in Kenya through "processing and refining of minerals in-country".

The deal comes as Washington seeks greater access to Africa's minerals, aiming to strengthen US supply chains and compete with China.

Frontier Minerals brings together three US companies - Bunker Hill Mining Corp, American Resources Corporation and ReElement Technologies Corporation. The consortium combines expertise in mineral resource development, critical materials supply chains and advanced refining technologies.

A central part of its proposal is to develop mineral processing and refining capacity in Kenya. Brad Barnett from Frontier Minerals said the company wanted the country to capture more value from its mineral resources.

Mrima Hill is located in Kwale County, near Kenya's Indian Ocean coastline. The site covers about 390 acres of forest.

The prospect of mining in the area has raised concerns among local residents, who fear the project could damage the forest and threaten their ancestral heritage.

There are also questions over whether communities living near the site will benefit from the investment.

Barnett was keen to reassure Kenyans that they would benefit: "We intend to create lasting opportunities for Kenyan workers, businesses and the wider economy through capacity-building and targeted investment, while prioritising environmental management and performance."

The consortium must still obtain a prospecting licence and meet the necessary regulatory requirements before the project can proceed.

The tender decision has drawn objections from competing bidders.

Reuters news agency reports that two rival companies intend to challenge the selection, with Australia's RareX alleging that Frontier Minerals missed an earlier submission deadline.

Kenya's government has maintained that the process was open and transparent.

The project comes as US strives to secure alternative sources of critical minerals and reduce dependence on China, which dominates the processing of several key minerals.

Washington has been pursuing partnerships with mineral-rich African countries, including the Democratic Republic of Congo and Zambia, as it seeks to strengthen supply chains for industries ranging from electric vehicles to defence technology.

Kenya's mining ministry says the sector is an important part of its plans to diversify the economy beyond traditional sources of revenue such as tourism, agriculture and financial services.
