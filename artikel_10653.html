Grand Theft Auto 6 review: Is this the best game ever?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Grand Theft Auto 6 is the greatest open-world simulation ever created. It is a breathtaking recreation of modern America, with extraordinary attention to detail and unrivalled freedom.

Vice City and the sprawling state of Leonida feel alive in ways no game has achieved before. Every street, random character, and minor interaction contributes to a world that feels like it exists beyond the player. It’s a technical achievement that raises the bar for open-world games forever.

For all its spectacle, ambition, and criminal excess, GTA 6 is also a deeply human story about two people trying to survive together in a world determined to tear them apart. And it’s the combination of unprecedented scale and genuine emotional depth that makes Rockstar’s latest masterpiece so extraordinary.

Grand Theft Auto 6 follows the story of Jason and Lucia, both fighting to make a name for themselves in Leonida after falling on hard times. We meet the two characters years before the events of the game, as they come across each other in Vice City, both stealing the same car.

Beyond that, Lucia is searching for answers about her family, mainly her child, after she lost custody following a stint in prison. As well as trying to pay the bills while working for Brian Heder, Jason is trying to find himself after serving in the army.

To both get what they’re looking for, they must build their way up from petty thieves to big-time bank robbers, all while dealing with what Leonida has to throw at them – it can get pretty wild, too.

Red Dead Redemption 2 was, for me, the greatest game ever made. Arthur Morgan’s story still remains my favorite in gaming, but what really made RDR2 special was the immersion. The Wild West felt totally real, making even the most ordinary activities enjoyable.

GTA 6 takes everything that made RDR2 so impressive and brings it to a modern-day recreation of Florida. It feels as though Rockstar took the best elements of Red Dead Redemption 2 and GTA 5 and amplified everything. The result is easily the most detailed open world ever created and quite possibly the best game of all time.

Leonida is absolutely packed with things to do. Whether you’re exploring the wilderness, discovering new locations, encountering wildlife, or investigating mysteries, there’s always something to keep you occupied.

It feels like there’s a new random encounter waiting around every corner, and in true Rockstar fashion, the developer seems to have thought of just about everything.

Better yet, you’re rewarded for going off the beaten path. There’s so much to discover that simply taking a detour can be just as entertaining as progressing through the main story. This is a world that begs you to explore every inch of it, and it’s remarkably easy to lose track of what you originally set out to do.

The attention to detail is on another level, too. Even everyday objects like books get more care than you’d expect, weapons can be customized to your heart’s content, and there are countless interactions that reward players for their creativity.

For instance, there’s one encounter early on where an NPC is filling his truck with gas. Players can flick a lit cigarette in his direction, triggering a massive gas station explosion.

Visually, GTA 6 is spectacular and as incredible as Vice City looks, the surrounding areas are equally impressive. From the lighting and weather effects to the character models and animations, the hardware has been pushed to its limits.

There were so many times when I found myself driving down a highway or country road, simply marveling at the world. Towns feel genuinely lived in, complete with residential neighborhoods, commercial districts, strip malls, and a staggering number of interiors to explore. Even the traffic strikes a great balance between feeling authentic but never becoming so congested that it takes the fun out of driving.

The sheer amount happening on screen at any given moment is astonishing, especially considering the game’s graphical fidelity. There is a trade-off, however, with GTA 6 targeting 30 FPS on consoles. It’s disappointing not to have a smoother option, but given what has been accomplished with this world, it’s a compromise I’m willing to accept.

The sound design is superb, too. The bustling streets of Vice City are alive with traffic, conversations, and background noise, while the quieter areas of Leonida have their own distinct atmosphere. The radio stations are another highlight, with a fantastic selection of music that makes even a leisurely drive across the map an enjoyable experience in its own right.

Thankfully, GTA 6 is every bit as fun to play as it is to explore.

The missions are some of the best in a GTA game, with plenty of variety and outrageous moments that make the campaign a blast. There’s a great balance between the ridiculous situations we’ve come to expect from the series and the more serious story Rockstar wants to tell, without one ever undermining the other.

Much like RDR2, GTA 6 uses its early missions to introduce new mechanics while gradually building toward something greater. There are some minor pacing issues in the opening hours, particularly with some of the more mundane tasks, such as fixing up the house for Lucia. It  takes its time establishing the world and its characters while allowing players to get a feel for how the game plays.

But like any great symphony, there’s a gradual build before the crescendo. Once the training wheels start coming off midway through Chapter 2, and especially in Chapter 3, GTA 6 begins to unleash its full potential.

The revamped Wanted system, introduced relatively early on, adds an entirely new dimension to police encounters. Cops now take your appearance, the vehicle you’re driving, and whether you’re carrying weapons into account, forcing you to be much more conscious of your actions when trying to evade the law.

Playing as Jason and Lucia together is an absolute blast, thanks to some of the best teammate AI I’ve ever experienced in a game. If you’re using a suppressed weapon, your partner will do the same. You can even work together to line up synchronized headshots.

This isn’t some scripted interaction that requires a button press or an on-screen prompt like you’d find in an FPS campaign – the AI simply does it. Whether you’re controlling Jason or Lucia, it genuinely feels like you have a competent partner in crime and if you get caught or killed, it’s usually your own fault.

Perhaps my favorite aspect of the gameplay, though, is the focus on robberies. They’ve come a long way since GTA 4’s legendary Three Leaf Clover mission. We saw Rockstar expand on the concept with GTA 5 and again with RDR2, but GTA 6 takes it to another level entirely. Even an ordinary bank robbery can be as exciting as a meticulously scripted action sequence from a Call of Duty campaign or the Uncharted series.

Early on in Chapter 3, I robbed a jewelry store with both characters, broke into a safe, and made off with the loot, only to find myself in a heated police chase. I let my partner take the wheel while I tried to hold off the cops hot on our trail.

After putting some distance between ourselves and the police, but still not quite out of danger, I decided our best bet was to split up. I abandoned the main road, ran into the woods, and eventually stumbled across a farmhouse, where I stole another car and made my escape while my partner fenced the stolen goods.

What made the whole experience so memorable was that none of it felt like a carefully orchestrated mission. It was a situation I had got myself into and had to find my own way out of.

As you progress, you’ll acquire tools and skills that allow you to take a much stealthier approach, potentially pulling off robberies without the cops ever laying eyes on you.

This is fun in its own right, as completing a brilliant heist without getting caught genuinely makes you feel like a badass. However, the occasions when things don’t go according to plan make for some of the most riveting gameplay GTA 6 has to offer.

There’s a very real sense of jeopardy, too. With death resulting in the loss of all the cash you’re carrying, there’s a sudden rush of adrenaline when a robbery goes sideways and you’ve got a small fortune on the line.

And that’s before even getting into the campaign’s major action set pieces, which are among the finest Rockstar has ever crafted. The finale of Chapter 1 is a particular standout and comfortably ranks among the greatest missions I’ve ever played in a video game.

Story-wise, GTA 6 had enormous shoes to fill following on from Red Dead Redemption 2. Unfortunately, neither Jason or Lucia are written with as much depth and growth as Arthur Morgan or other key members of the Van der Linde gang. A lot of that comes down to Jason’s dialogue and his personality being quite dry compared to other GTA characters.

Dylan Rourke, who voices Jason, has his moments, and without getting into spoiler territory, there are some emotional moments where he nails it. However, he seems to have had a lot less to work with than Manni L. Perez, who helps make Lucia the more compelling of the two.

That said, GTA 6’s MacGuffins do a fantastic job at keeping interest incredibly high and driving the main plot in between side missions. And we are comparing it to what I think is the greatest video game story of all time.

Leonida is the most impressive open world I’ve ever explored, the missions are fantastic, and Jason and Lucia deliver a fine story that lives up to the brilliant world around them.

After more than a decade of waiting, Rockstar has delivered something truly special. GTA 6 isn’t just a worthy successor to GTA 5 and RDR2. It sets a new standard, not just for what open-world games can achieve, but for gaming itself.

Grand Theft Auto 6 is the greatest open-world simulation ever created. It is a breathtaking recreation of modern America, with extraordinary attention to detail and unrivalled freedom.
