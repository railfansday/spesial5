REIT move? Blackstone joint venture buys AWS campuses to lease back to the cloud giant

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Amazon shunts some real estate off its books, a move Omdia analyst says may be about economics, as AI buildout weighs on hyperscaler finances

Amazon Web Services and Blackstone have inked a deal that will see the private equity firm effectively take ownership of two AWS datacenter campuses and then lease them back to the Jeff Bezos outfit under an initial term of 12 years.

Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust (BXDC) says the definitive agreement will see it acquire a 75 percent majority interest in two joint ventures with AWS, in a transaction valued at approximately $5 billion.

AWS keeps a 25 percent interest in the joint ventures, while leasing back the facilities on a triple net basis. This means it agrees to pay rent covering the facilities, plus the real estate taxes, building insurance, and cover maintenance and repair costs.

A Form 8-K filed by Blackstone with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) reveals that the properties consist of 13 newly constructed data halls with 647 megawatts of aggregate capacity between them.

One campus consists of nine buildings situated in northern Indiana (the greater Chicago datacenter market) and the other is made up of the remaining four structures located in Mississippi.

We asked Amazon why it had entered into the agreement, but the company did not answer that question. Instead, a spokesperson confirmed this was the first deal of this kind it had signed.

In a supplied remark, VP of AWS Infrastructure Prasad Kalyanaraman said that "Partnering with Blackstone allows us to continue expanding responsibly while keeping communities at the center of how we build."

AWS does lease capacity from other datacenter operators, of course, and has existing agreements with partners like STACK Infrastructure to develop $12 billion worth of campus sites across northwest Louisiana, signed earlier this year.

Sale-leaseback transactions are not unheard of in the datacenter industry. QTS Realty Trust (also backed by Blackstone) developed facilities for lease by Microsoft, for example.

BXDC is a real estate investment trust (REIT), launched by Blackstone on the New York Stock Exchange earlier this year. The AWS deal is its first since then.

"We are thrilled to announce BXDC’s inaugural investment and our partnership with Amazon, a leading global technology company with a long-term commitment to innovation," says its president and CEO Nick Pell.

"The quality, basis and tenant profile of these assets make this an attractive match for the deployment of our IPO proceeds. Given the deep expertise across our platform, we believe BXDC is well positioned to capitalize on a highly compelling opportunity and deliver value for our shareholders."

Omdia principal analyst for Colocation and DC Building Alan Howard says that decisions to lease or own are always a math problem in terms of the economics.

"I'm not sure those datacenter assets bring much benefit to their financials sitting on their balance sheet and it's possible a sale/leaseback is more economical. Keep in mind that all this has nothing to do with all the IT equipment inside, rather just the building and its infrastructure," he told The Register.

"It's also clear that the massive capex spending by hyperscalers is putting pressure on their financials and they are leaning on debt markets to fund some of the buildout. Of course, using debt is not out of the ordinary, but I imagine the debt for AI buildout is significantly more now than in the past."

The transaction is expected to close in the fourth quarter of 2026 and is subject to certain closing conditions. According to the Form 8-K, the agreement can be terminated by either party if it isn't closed by December 31, 2026, with the non-breaching party entitled to a termination fee of $150 million. ®
