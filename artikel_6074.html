Buy American AAdvantage Miles For 50% Off (1.88 Cents Each): Worth It?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

American Airlines is currently offering a rare elevated promotion on purchasing AAdvantage miles. Through December 2, 2026, AAdvantage members can purchase miles with up to a 50% discount, which is potentially an opportunity to buy miles for 1.88 cents each.

While that’s not a price at which I’d proactively pick up miles, there are definitely some situations where that could represent a good value, if you have an immediate use in mind, especially for first and business class travel.

Note that offers like this are extremely rare — while American offers a 40% off promotion on buying miles consistently, the 50% off promotion seems to come around just once or twice per year.

Link: Buy American Airlines AAdvantage miles with a discount

Between 12:01AM CT on November 27 and 11:59PM CT on December 2, 2026, American AAdvantage is offering up to a 50% discount (or 100% bonus, if you prefer) on buying miles. The offer is tiered, and you get a bigger discount the more miles you buy, as follows:

As you can see, you need to buy a lot of miles to get the best price!

Ordinarily American sells AAdvantage miles for 3.5 cents each pre-tax, or 3.76 cents after factoring in the 7.5% federal excise tax. That’s before any discounts or bonuses. In this case if you max out the promotion and buy 500,000 AAdvantage miles, you’d pay a total of $9,406.25, which comes out to 1.88 cents per mile (that factors in the 7.5% tax).

Keep in mind that American Airlines mileage purchases are non-refundable, and buying AAdvantage miles doesn’t count toward your Loyalty Points total (other than any amount you may spend on an eligible credit card). Purchased miles typically post nearly instantly, but in some cases, it can take up to four hours for them to post.

The 50% discount is the best price we’ve seen from American AAdvantage in recent years, and again, it only comes around once or twice per year. With the standard 40% off promotion on buying miles, you’d ordinarily pay 2.26 cents per mile.

The American AAdvantage program ordinarily allows members to buy at most 300,000 miles per account per calendar year, before factoring in any bonuses. However, during this promotion, that cap has been increased to 500,000 miles. AAdvantage accounts less than 30 days old aren’t eligible to buy American Airlines miles. Miles can only be purchased in 1,000-point increments.

The process of buying American Airlines miles is easy — the easiest way is to simply follow this link. Alternatively, you can go to aa.com, click on “AAdvantage” at the top of the page, and then click on “Buy, gift or transfer miles.”

From there, you’ll be prompted to log into your AAdvantage account, and then that’s where you can select how many miles you’d like to buy. When you buy American Airlines miles, they’ll typically post to your account instantly.

Yes, you absolutely can buy American Airlines miles, you just have to be aware of a few restrictions. For one, your account needs to be at least 30 days old. On top of that, you have to stay within the annual cap.

It’s possible to buy miles for yourself or others, but you’ll need to be sure you use the right account to make the purchase.

Unlike Delta SkyMiles and United MileagePlus, American AAdvantage continues to publish award charts for travel on partner airlines, and redemption rates are largely quite good, especially for travel in premium cabins on partner airlines. Among the “big three” carriers in the US, I tend to think AAdvantage has the best redemption opportunities.

I find the cost of business class redemptions to be excellent in many cases. There are instances where it could make sense to pick up miles during a promotion with a short term use in mind. Now, unfortunately the catch is that partner award availability is in many instances quite limited. For airlines like Cathay Pacific, Etihad, and Qatar Airways, the airlines now largely restrict premium awards to members of their own frequent flyer programs. So definitely do your research before buying any miles.

Another thing worth mentioning is that if you redeem for travel on British Airways, get ready to pay huge carrier imposed surcharges, which could be hundreds of dollars on a long haul premium cabin trip. So that wouldn’t be my preferred use of American Airlines miles.

American Airlines belongs to the oneworld alliance, so you can redeem AAdvantage miles on all oneworld airlines.

On top of that, you can redeem miles on some of American’s other partner airlines, including Aer Lingus, Air Tahiti Nui, China Southern, Etihad Airways, and GOL Airlines, among others.

Everyone will value mileage currencies differently, and based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider AAdvantage miles to be worth 1.5 cents each. However, I tend to value points pretty conservatively, and there are many ways to get way more value from AAdvantage miles than that.

American AAdvantage miles don’t expire as long as you have at least some account activity once every 24 months. Eligible activity includes earning or redeeming AAdvantage miles in any quantity.
