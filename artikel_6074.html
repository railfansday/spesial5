World Of Hyatt Credit Card Review: Why It’s My Go-To Hotel Card

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

The World of Hyatt Credit Card is Hyatt’s co-branded Chase personal credit card. The card has a $95 annual fee, and offers strong benefits that can easily offset that, including an anniversary free night award, elite nights toward status, and more. It can even be worth spending money on the card, in order to unlock an additional free night and/or elite status.

This post is my in-depth World of Hyatt Card review. What makes Hyatt’s personal credit card so brilliant is that it’s worth having regardless of whether you want to keep it just for the ongoing perks, or whether you want to spend money on it.

When we apply for credit cards, some of the most important factors are the welcome bonus, the eligibility rules for getting the card, and the annual fee. How does the World of Hyatt Credit Card do on those fronts? Let’s take a look.

At the moment, the World of Hyatt Card is offering a limited time welcome bonus where you can earn four free night awards after spending $5,000 within the first three months. Each free night award is valid at a Category 1-4 World of Hyatt property.

World of Hyatt Category 4 properties retail for up to 25,000 points per night, so the absolute maximum value of this offer is 100,000 points. However, I of course wouldn’t value it that highly. That’s because these free night awards expire 12 months after they’re issued, and you also can’t top off these awards to redeem at more expensive properties. So actually maxing out the value of these isn’t easy.

One thing I should mention is that while I don’t love the lack of flexibility of this welcome offer, it’s significantly better than the standard bonus, which tends to be lackluster. Obviously Chase and Hyatt know that this is a card people will get for the long term value, rather than just for the bonus.

For what it’s worth, based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider World of Hyatt points to be worth 1.4 cents each.

When you pull up the World of Hyatt Credit Card application, you’ll see the following terms:

This product is available to you if you do not have this card and have not received a new cardmember bonus for this card in the past 24 months.

This is good news, as eligibility is considered independently of any other Chase credit card, and it’s also considered independently of the World of Hyatt Business Card (learn more).

It’s also worth remembering the Chase 5/24 rule, whereby you sometimes won’t be approved for a Chase card if you’ve opened five or more new card accounts in the past 24 months (certain business cards are excluded). However, that no longer seems to be consistently enforced.

Fortunately nowadays Chase uses a pop-up feature during the application process, which should let you know if you’re not eligible for the bonus on a card (this appears after submitting your application), before there’s a hard pull on your credit.

There’s not a consistent rule as to what credit score you need to be approved for the World of Hyatt Credit Card. In general, I’d recommend having a credit score in the “good” to “excellent” range.

Personally, I’d hope to have a credit score of at least 700. That being said, people with scores lower than that have been approved, and conversely, people with scores higher than that have been rejected. There are lots of factors that go into approval. Chase will consider your income, your credit history, how much credit Chase has already extended you, etc.

The World of Hyatt Credit Card has a $95 annual fee. You can add additional cardmembers to your account at no extra cost. This annual fee is well worth it even if you don’t spend money on the card, since you get an anniversary free night award that can be redeemed at hotels costing $250+ per night.

The World of Hyatt Credit Card has no foreign transaction fees, so it’s an excellent card for purchases abroad. That’s especially true when staying with Hyatt abroad, as well as for dining and commuting outside the US.

The World of Hyatt Credit Card offers pretty good points earning opportunities. While this isn’t the card that I use for all of my spending, I strategically spend money on the card, and it works great for me. How good are the points earning rates? Let me emphasize that points earning is only part of the value proposition of spending on the card. It’s also about being able to earn elite status, which I’ll cover in more detail a bit later.

The World of Hyatt Credit Card offers 4x points for spending at Hyatt hotels and resorts, including Mr & Mrs Smith properties. That’s a solid return on hotel spending, though admittedly it doesn’t beat the return you get on the Chase Sapphire Reserve® Card (learn more), which also offers 4x points on hotel purchases, and points can be moved to World of Hyatt.

Based on my valuation of Hyatt points, that’s like a 5.6% return on Hyatt spending.

Keep in mind that this is all in addition to the standard points earning at properties. World of Hyatt members ordinarily earn 5x points per dollar spent, plus elite bonuses of 10-30%. So the 4x points stacks on top of that.

The World of Hyatt Credit Card offers 2x points for restaurant spending globally. That’s obviously a widely useful return, though in fairness, it’s also hard to get too excited about 2x points in this category, given the number of cards that offer 3-5x points on dining spending.

Based on my valuation of Hyatt points, that’s like a 2.8% return on restaurant spending.

The World of Hyatt Credit Card offers 2x points on airline tickets purchased globally directly from airlines. While it’s nice to have this as a bonus category, again, it’s hard to get too excited about 2x points, given the number of cards that offer 3-5x points on airfare spending.

Based on my valuation of Hyatt points, that’s like a 2.8% return on airfare spending.

The World of Hyatt Credit Card offers 2x points on fitness clubs and gym memberships. For anyone wondering, this also includes group fitness businesses, like Barry’s Bootcamp, SoulCycle, etc. It’s nice to have this as a bonus category, since it’s pretty rare to see a bonus for these kinds of purchases. However, with other cards offering 2x points on everyday spending, it’s not all that exciting.

Based on my valuation of Hyatt points, that’s like a 2.8% return on fitness spending.

The World of Hyatt Credit Card offers 2x points on local transit and commuting, which includes ride share services such as Lyft and Uber.

Based on my valuation of Hyatt points, that’s like a 2.8% return on local transit and commuting spending.

The World of Hyatt Card offers 1x points per dollar spent on everyday spending. Generally speaking, there are better cards for everyday spending, though as I’ll explain below, some of the spending bonuses could make it worth shifting spending onto this card.

The area where the World of Hyatt Credit Card really excels is when it comes to the benefits it offers. In my opinion, these benefits more than justify the annual fee, regardless of whether you’re spending nothing on the card, or are spending a significant amount. Specifically, the benefits largely center around earning free nights award and elite status, so let’s cover those details.

Just for having the World of Hyatt Credit Card, you get a Category 1-4 free night award on your account anniversary every year. This will be issued shortly after your anniversary and is valid for 12 months.

Categories 1-4 cover a majority of Hyatt’s properties, and those hotels can retail for up to 25,000 points per night. I’ve consistently been able to redeem these at hotels costing $250+ per night, so that (way) more than justifies the annual fee.

One downside worth flagging is that you unfortunately can’t top off these awards with additional points to redeem at more expensive properties. That’s quite the contrast to the free night awards issued by IHG One Rewards and Marriott Bonvoy.

Beyond the anniversary free night award just for being a cardmember, the World of Hyatt Card offers a second free night award if you spend $15,000 on the card in a calendar year. This could be a major incentive to spend money on the card, as it makes $15,000 in spending quite a good sweet spot.

You receive World of Hyatt’s entry-level Discoverist status for as long as you have the World of Hyatt Card. The status will post for the primary cardmember shortly after you are approved. Perks of Discoverist status include the following:

Don’t get too excited here, but some status is better than no status! Discoverist status ordinarily requires 10 elite nights or 25,000 base points (which correlates to $5,000 in spending).

The World of Hyatt Card gives you five elite qualifying nights toward status annually just for being a cardmember. You will receive these within eight weeks of being approved for the card (though usually much faster), and in subsequent years they will post within eight weeks of January 1.

This will be useful if you’re hoping to earn World of Hyatt Explorist status, World of Hyatt Globalist status, and/or World of Hyatt Milestone Rewards. For context, World of Hyatt Explorist status requires 30 elite nights, while World of Hyatt Globalist status requires 60 elite nights.

In addition to the five elite nights you get just for having the World of Hyatt Card, you earn a further two elite qualifying nights for every $5,000 you spend on the card. This means that in theory, you could spend your way all the way to Explorist or Globalist status, if you wanted to.

Reaching the $5,000 threshold has nothing to do with the calendar or cardmember year — the counter simply resets every time you spend $5,000, and then you start earning toward your next two elite nights. So if you spend $4,000 in one year and $1,000 in the next year, you’d get two elite nights in the second year.

The World of Hyatt Card offers auto rental collision damage waiver coverage. Decline the rental company’s collision insurance and charge the entire rental cost to your card. Coverage is provided for theft and collision damage for most cars in the United States and abroad. Do note that in the US, the coverage is secondary to your personal insurance, though.

That’s because personally I value World of Hyatt points at ~1.4 cents each. It’s also worth being aware of Hyatt’s Dining & Spa Awards, which let you redeem points toward on-property spending.

Let me answer this very simply. If you’re eligible for the World of Hyatt Credit Card and have any interest in staying at Hyatt properties then yes, the World of Hyatt Credit Card is absolutely worth it. This is a card I personally have, and that I wholeheartedly recommend.

The World of Hyatt Credit Card is an exceptional hotel credit card. It’s a card I have, and a card I spend at least $15,000 on per year.

The card is worth having even if you don’t plan on putting spending on it — you get an anniversary free night award you can use at a hotel that costs significantly more than the annual fee, you get complimentary Discoverist status, and you get five elite nights toward a higher status tier.

Beyond that, it can be worth putting spending on the card, thanks to the unique bonus categories (4x points on Hyatt stays and 2x points on fitness and gyms), as well as the additional elite nights and second free night award.
