Why Global Hotel Alliance Is Turning to Saudi Arabia Despite Riyadh’s Slump

GHA is coming through a hard year in the UAE as one of the country's largest loyalty players. Its CEO doesn't want to grow much more there. He's betting on Saudi Arabia instead, even as Riyadh's hotels struggle.

Global Hotel Alliance, a Dubai-headquartered network of independent hotel brands, plans to grow in Saudi Arabia rather than add many more hotels in the UAE, CEO Christopher Hartley told Skift.

GHA’s network covers 1,000 hotels and over 60 brands across 100 countries.

“We don’t need to add another 30 or 40 hotels just for the sake of it,” he said of the UAE, where GHA now has about 70 hotels, including properties from home-grown operator Rotana, and from Minor International, Kempinski, Nikki Beach, Cheval Collection and JA Resorts.

The shift follows a hard year in its home market. The U.S.-Iran war cut GHA’s loyalty revenue in the UAE by 28% on a like-for-like basis to $134 million through October, Hartley said. Globally, the alliance has kept growing: total revenue rose 21% to $2.8 billion in the first nine months of this year.

Global Hotel Alliance, the Dubai-headquartered network of about 1,000 independent hotels and more than 60 brands, plans to grow in Saudi Arabia and Egypt rather than add many more hotels in the UAE, CEO Christopher Hartley told Skift. The U.S.-Iran war cut GHA's loyalty revenue in the UAE by 28% on a like-for-like basis to $134 million through October, with room nights down 22% and Dubai's average daily rate down 28.1% to $181 in October, according to CoStar's STR. International visitors to Dubai fell 38% to 9.7 million in the first 10 months of the year. GHA's global business kept growing — total revenue rose 21% to $2.8 billion in the first nine months — and UAE residents kept traveling, with their spending at GHA hotels abroad up 28%. The alliance responded to the demand collapse by chasing staycations, which rose 7%, and by discounting into its direct channels, where UAE room nights rose 27% at lower rates. Hartley said GHA has enough scale in the UAE and is taking a five-year view on Saudi Arabia, where it has signed an agreement with Public Investment Fund-backed Adeera, even as Riyadh occupancy fell to 34.3% in October from 69% a year earlier. On distribution, he said OTAs take about 20% of each booking against roughly 5% for a direct GHA booking, and he does not expect AI to shift share away from OTAs in the short term.
