Malaysia Airlines Overtakes AirAsia on India Routes by Standing Still

AirAsia’s fuel-driven retreat should be a gift to its rivals, but high fuel costs and scarce aircraft mean few can afford to take advantage. Malaysia Airlines has overtaken it on India routes with 1% more seats in a market that shrank 10%. The longer AirAsia stays small, the more of those default gains could become permanent.

AirAsia is shrinking across Southeast Asia, and the clearest sign of what that means for rivals is on routes between Malaysia and India.

For the first time since 2021, the Malaysia-based low-cost airline group is no longer the biggest operator  between Malaysia and India. Malaysia Airlines has scheduled 1.57 million seats on the route in 2026, against a combined 1.42 million for AirAsia and its long-haul affiliate AirAsia X, according to Cirium data.

Malaysia Airlines got there without growing much. Its seats are up 1% from 2025. The AirAsia group's are meanwhile down 21% — a loss of nearly 378,000 seats in a year.

The overall Malaysia–India market has shrunk 10% in seats this year. Malaysia Airlines' share has climbed from 34% to 38%, while AirAsia group’s share has fallen from 39% to 35%. AirAsia has lost its position as the largest operator between Malaysia and India for the first time since 2021, a measurable marker of the low-cost group's retrenchment across Southeast Asia. Malaysia Airlines has scheduled 1.57 million seats on the route in 2026 against 1.42 million for AirAsia and AirAsia X combined, and it got there with just 1% growth while the AirAsia group cut nearly 378,000 seats, down 21%. Fuel is the main cause: jet fuel averaged $183 a barrel in the second quarter, up 66% from the prior quarter, and the largely unhedged group posted a net loss of RM831 million ($204 million). AirAsia has suspended 81 routes, plans to return 25 aircraft to lessors early, and is seeking to raise up to $1 billion mainly to refinance debt. Rivals are collecting share by default rather than chasing it — Batik Air's Malaysia–India seats are down 9% despite telling Malaysia's government it could absorb AirAsia's routes, and only Air India is growing strongly, up 29%. With narrowbody delivery slots scarce until roughly 2033, nobody can add capacity quickly without someone else's planes, and analysts quoted by Skift said handing back jets now could slow AirAsia's eventual comeback.
