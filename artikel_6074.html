American Airlines carries more passengers and operates more flights than any other U.S. carrier, but it trails Delta and United on nearly every financial measure, and it has spent the past year trying to close the gap. American earned $111 million last year against Delta's $5.1 billion and United's $3.3 billion, and it is on track to lose money this year. Its shares are down more than 50% from their 2019 high. A Skift review of press releases and earnings calls since early 2025 found at least 50 announced changes, built around four pillars: elevate the customer experience, grow the global network, drive premium revenue, and lead in loyalty. CEO Robert Isom has begun describing American as a "global premium airline," and the carrier is bringing back seatback screens, installing Starlink on every mainline jet from 2027, ending involuntary bumping, launching the 777-300ER and A321XLR with new European routes, raising co-brand card fees, and rebuilding hubs in Chicago, Miami, Philadelphia, and Phoenix. The gap also shows up in paychecks: American set aside $55 million in profit sharing, about 0.3% of annual earnings, compared with Delta's $1.3 billion. In January, the flight attendants' union issued a vote of no confidence in Isom. Executives said there are early signs the push toward higher-spend customers is working, and chief customer officer Heather Garboden said 2027 will bring even more launches.
