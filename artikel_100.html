Australia may have ‘one foot in recession’ as unemployment surges to five-year high

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Jobless rate rises from 4.7% to 4.9% in October, Bureau of Statistics figures reveal

Australia may already have “one foot in recession” after unemployment surged to 4.9% in October, from 4.7%, in a shock increase that will add to rising worries that this year’s five interest rate hikes have pushed the economy past a tipping point.

New figures from the Australian Bureau of Statistics showed the number of employed people dropped by 43,500 in the month, confounding forecasts for jobs growth and a steady jobless rate.

Financial markets moved quickly to price out any chance of a sixth Reserve Bank rate increase, and were factoring in a rate cut by September of next year, according to National Australia Bank.

After starting the year at 4%, unemployment has now lifted to its highest level in five years to approach pre-Covid levels. In a further sign of deteriorating conditions, the number of employed Australians has dropped in three of the past four months.

David Bassanese, the chief economist at Betashares, said there were lingering questions over the seasonal movements in the labour force data that could be giving a falsely weak picture of the jobs market.

“If these employment declines prove to be real, however, they’re the type of weakness that could well mean we’ve seen the last interest rate hike,” Bassanese said.

“Indeed, consecutive large employment declines might suggest the economy already has one foot in recession.”

The shock figures validate warnings from the three dissenting RBA board members, who voted against the Melbourne Cup day rate hike and warned of a “materially sharper downturn” in the economy as households struggled to deal with this year’s substantial lift in borrowing costs.

Full-time employment dropped by 2,900 in October, the ABS data showed, and part-time employment by a steeper 40,600 people.

Amid rising concerns about the impact of AI on entry-level roles and the recent string of layoffs in the retail sector, the ABS also flagged another rise in the youth unemployment rate to 11.3% in October, from 11.1% the month before.

Sean Crick, the head of labour statistics at the ABS, said “this is the highest it’s been since October 2021”.

“In the post-Covid period (from May 2022 onwards), the youth unemployment rate has averaged 9.2%,” Crick said.

Sunny Nguyen, the head of Australia economics at Moody’s Analytics, said “the Aussie labour market has unmistakenly turned”.

Nguyen said the latest data “all but closes the door” on any chance of a pre-Christmas rate hike.

“For the RBA, this lands on a board already split over whether policy has gone far enough,” he said.

“The bank only lifted its unemployment forecast earlier this month to peak above 5% by the end of 2028; the labour market is within sight of that level two years early.”

Taylor Nugent, a senior economist at National Australia Bank, said there was a risk the jobless rate could begin to accelerate as rising unemployment hit spending and, in turn, forced employers to further tighten their belts.

“We had been expecting peak unemployment to peak at around 5.5% by the end of next year. This [latest data] does suggest that it’s playing out a bit faster than we had been expecting.”
