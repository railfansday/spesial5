eharmony app reviews: Subscribers share whether it’s worth it

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

It's incredible that eharmony has managed to stay relevant in the online dating world. The dating site launched back at the turn of the millennium and held firm by focusing on a more substantive, personality-based approach to online matchmaking. It's not perfect, of course, and different daters prefer different approaches to finding partners.

While eharmony members generally appreciate the depth of its matchmaking algorithm, they sometimes complain about the cost and the number of fake profiles, particularly since artificial intelligence has become more widely available.

Here's what real eharmony users had to say about the site, from the best features to the worst experiences:

Tinder — top pick for fast hookups

Hinge — popular choice for regular meetups

Pure — best for anonymous hookups

OkCupid — best app for inclusivity

If you're looking for some genuine substance on dating sites, you often have to filter out all the people who are only in it for short-term fun. At least, that's the case on other dating platforms. But eharmony largely bypasses that problem, first through its reputation (fun fact: it began as a Christian dating site!) and through its detailed sign-up process, which includes completing the eharmony compatibility questionnaire.

Reddit reviewers have consistently lauded this focus on substance in the screening process ("it forces you to focus on depth over pure visuals") and found the compatibility scores, assigned based on your answers to the personality quiz you take when you sign up, to be a useful way of evaluating matches before starting a conversation.

Here's a story from one older gentleman: "My mindset was that I really needed to be fully honest in my answers, because why would I pay for a service and not be honest? After a few days, I got my matches. A day later, I got a nice message from a woman 10 years younger than me. We exchanged some messages, and then I looked at our match score after she prompted me about it. We were 126 out of 140. I decided to go with it because she and I were really vibing on each other. A year later we got engaged and will marry next year."

For reference, a compatibility score of 126 on eharmony is incredibly rare. As one Redditor responded, "When I was on there, I never had greater than 105 with any match."

A common complaint over the years about eharmony has been the cost of the premium membership, which you need to sign up for to get the most out of the site (eharmony doesn't even let you see photos without upgrading to its premium service). There are three premium membership tiers to choose from (Light, Plus, and Unlimited), with pricing varying somewhat by your location, but you should expect to pay at least $15 per month for a membership, which makes it more expensive than a standard Netflix subscription but around the same price as a cheap gym membership.

Users have complained about both the price and the cancellation process: "I’ve tried eHarmony and found it way too expensive for what it offers, and to top it [off], the process of cancellation is so complicated," said one Reddit reviewer. Other users were unhappy with its automatic membership renewals or the difficulty of canceling a subscription, even just 12 hours after signing up.

That said, eharmony entices users with steep discounts — sometimes up to 50 percent off the regular membership price — so if you keep tabs on offers, you can sign up and get substantial savings.

It's a fact of online dating: different sites are built for different demographics. That means certain age groups gravitate towards certain sites. You'll already know that if you've ever used Tinder as a middle-aged adult.

Older daters, divorced daters, and anyone looking for a more mature partner should definitely try eharmony. We named it the "gold standard" for daters over 40 for a reason — the older age bracket is eharmony's wheelhouse.

Here's a typical experience from an older male dater: "45 yo divorced guy here. I signed up with eHarmony and Bumble 2 months ago based on recommendations from co-workers. I've had much better luck with eHarmony than Bumble. My highest compatibility ratings are in the 110 to 116 range, and these comprise about 33 percent of my daily feed. The other 33 percent are in the 100 to 105 rating scale, and the final 33 percent are in the 89 to 99 rating scale. [...] In contrast, I have connected with zero women on Bumble Premium after 2 months."

In the age of AI and cheaply produced deepfakes, dating sites have been swamped by fake profiles. Indeed, eharmony users have found themselves sifting through numerous fake dating profiles before finding a real human being to engage. It's frustrating.

One of the top-rated posts on the /r/eharmony subreddit in the last year decries, "The fake profile problem has got noticeably worse," complaining that of the 14 likes their account received, 11 came from obviously fake accounts.

But how big a problem is this, really? For older daters unaccustomed to filtering out fake profiles and photos, it can be a major pain in the butt, sure. But if you're even remotely internet-savvy, you shouldn't have too much trouble distinguishing between real and fake users. And you're guarantee you'll encounter this problem on pretty much every dating platform — so don't let it put you off using eharmony. And because eharmony has tight restrictions for free members, you can largely side-step the fake profile issue if you upgrade to a paid subscription. This allows you to only interact with other paying members, which effectively eliminates those annoying bots.
