You Can Play Grand Theft Auto 6 At 60fps Using This Absurdly Complicated Method

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Grand Theft Auto VI is finally out for consoles, and it's every bit the visual showcase of the generation that it was promising to be since its reveal. You'll struggle to find a better-looking game on the current generation of hardware, but one concession Rockstar had to make to achieve that was limiting its frame rate--a limitation some players are going to absurd lengths to overcome.

Irrespective of what platform you play on, Grand Theft Auto VI is limited to 30fps. Doubling that to the more desirable 60fps is something current console hardware just isn't capable of with this level of visual fidelity and simulation, which means you'll have to rely on other methods of frame generation to get there. That's the idea that underpins technology like Veyra: an open-source real-time video enhancer that uses your PC and HDMI 2.1 capture card to bring frame generation to your console.

It's a...complicated process. First you'll need a capable capture card (like one of the many from Elgato) that is able to ingest gameplay at 4K over HDMI 2.1. You'll need to connect that to your PC using certified USB-C 3.2 data cable, which needs to plug into a suitable motherboard with True USB 3.2 Gen 2 support. On the pricier end of the requirements, you will need a relatively powerful GPU, with the creators recommending a RTX-40 or newer card from Nvidia in order to tap into the card's Tensor cores (that are used for frame generation on PC). Pair that with an OLED monitor, which is also strongly recommended thanks to its near-instant pixel response times, and you've got everything you need to finally enjoy Grand Theft Auto VI (and more) at higher frame rates.

Veyra is able to take gameplay captured on your console and apply a variety of enhancements over the footage, which is ultimately displayed (and played) through the PC you're capturing the footage to. You can utilize upscaling and frame generation techniques from Nvidia, AMD, and Intel to enhance both the resolution and perceived fluidity of your console games this way, with as minimal latency as possible. Given all the parts in the chain, from your console, to the capture card, and through to your PC, escaping any additional latency is impossible. But Verya is seemingly built with mitigating latency as its highest priority, and seems to achieve that based on feedback from some users on Reddit.

Is it worth it to go through this much hassle to play GTA 6 at 60fps? If you have all the required hardware then it's at least worth a shot, even just for the sake of curiosity. But actually investing in a setup to achieve this is probably overkill. For starters, it requires a PC that will probably be capable enough to play Rockstar's latest sequel at or on-par with the console version once it does land on PC (which will reportedly be sooner than you think). Capture cards are not outrageously expensive, but they're also not free, which can also be said for all the high-quality cables you'll need between each of the three required devices.

I admit, I admire the dedication to playing Grand Theft Auto VI at launch in the best possible way, but I would wager that you're likely going to be spending a lot more time troubleshooting some incompatibility somewhere along this chain of dependencies instead of enjoying the game itself. Still, if you're curious you can find a lot of detailed information about what this setup requires and why, and determine for yourself if it's worth the price to cruise around Leonida with a bit more fluidity.
