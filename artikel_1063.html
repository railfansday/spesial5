Mauricio Pochettino endured the worst possible start to life as Manchester United manager as his side were thrashed 4-0 by fierce rivals Liverpool.

United set the tone for a disastrous afternoon early as they went a man down inside eight minutes, with Leny Yoro bundling into Alexander Isak as the last man to be shown a straight red card.

Dominik Szoboszlai fired in from the resulting free-kick to ensure Yoro’s blunder was fully punished, and Liverpool doubled their lead three minutes later when Florian Wirtz excellently improvised a volley to score.

Rio Ngumoha got a deserved goal after the break to put the game to bed, converting from Bradley Barcola’s cross, before Victor Munoz put the cherry on a ruthless performance for the hosts late on.

Welcome to Manchester United, Mauricio. For a man who had coveted the United job for years, this was an ignominious introduction. Mauricio Pochettino got a 13-minute crash course in the craziness of his new club. His first game was lost before he made his first substitution, and that came as early as the 22nd-minute. His debut brought the joint heaviest ever defeat by a United manager in his first game in charge, and the biggest in 100 years.

“So painful,” Pochettino said. “So disappointed.” As can be the way with United of late, most things that could go wrong did. When their new head coach’s name was sung, it was by the Kop. “Poch is at the wheel,” they taunted. A first meaningful day of work showed the scale of the task to steer United in the right direction.

Mauricio Pochettino is the manager Manchester United could, time and again, have appointed in the past. He marked his eventual arrival at Old Trafford by trying to shrug it off; theirs more than his. Pochettino made it to the last two in 2022, when Erik ten Hag was instead chosen, and may have seemed United’s second choice in 2016 and 2019, when Jose Mourinho and Ole Gunnar Solskjaer were preferred. But by pivoting to the future was a way of avoiding the issue of why none of them – nor David Moyes, Louis van Gaal, Ralf Rangnick, Ruben Amorim or Michael Carrick – has truly succeeded.

Pochettino’s plan to fix United may draw on a more distant past, the identity he described that seemed to stem more from Sir Alex Ferguson than his eight immediate successors, but reliant heavily on a positivity about the future. “I don't think too much in the past.
