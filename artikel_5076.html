Ukraine Legalizes Crypto After One-Day Delay

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Home » Regulations » Ukraine Legalizes Crypto After One-Day Delay

Crypto-embracing Bill No. 10225-d gets the highest-priority treatment following a slight procedural delay.

Ukraine’s parliament has approved a bill to bring cryptocurrency trading, custody and taxation under a formal legal framework.

The measure, Bill No. 10225-d, passed after lawmakers postponed consideration a day earlier, a pause that had raised questions about its immediate path despite renewed support from the parliamentary finance committee for the revised text.

The Ukraine crypto bill is designed to legalize and regulate the domestic virtual-asset market rather than leave it in a largely undefined legal space.

Its scope includes tax treatment for crypto activity as well as rules for trading platforms and custodial services.

That approach would place businesses handling customer assets under a clearer licensing and compliance structure.

The framework comes into practice on July 1, 2027. It offers more certainty over how digital-asset transactions are treated by authorities, though implementation details will matter as much as the legislation itself.

Ukraine has maintained an active crypto sector despite the strains of war, with digital assets playing a visible role in fundraising and cross-border transfers.

Formalizing the market has therefore carried significance beyond ordinary financial regulation: it could help define how crypto businesses operate in an economy still managing exceptional pressures with the fifth year of war with Russia coming along.

The Ukraine crypto bill’s journey shows how quickly crypto policy can shift even when lawmakers broadly support regulation.

The November 24, 2026 postponement followed committee backing for a revised version, while the subsequent passage points to a resolution of at least some of those immediate obstacles.

Questions remain over the final tax mechanics, the timing of licensing requirements and which agencies will oversee different parts of the sector. Market participants will also watch for secondary rules that determine the practical burden on exchanges, brokers and wallet providers.

Ukraine’s crypto move matters because it adds another sizable European market pursuing rules for digital assets rather than relying on prohibition or legal ambiguity.

The Ukraine crypto legislation may give local firms a route toward greater legitimacy, but the ultimate test will be whether the resulting framework is workable enough to retain innovation while improving crypto oversight.
