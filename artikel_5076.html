Bitcoin ETFs gained 191 institutional holders but just 25 drove all the growth

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The largest investment managers added nearly 57,000 BTC-equivalent in the third quarter as the rest collectively reduced their exposure.

More investment managers reported holding US spot Bitcoin products in the third quarter, but the growth in their positions came from a much smaller group.

Galaxy Research found that the 25 largest holders at Sept. 30 added more Bitcoin exposure than all reporting managers combined. Everyone outside that group, taken together, cut exposure.

Galaxy's Nov. 19 analysis of Form 13F filings counted 2,166 investment managers with positions as of Sept. 30, up 191 from June. Their disclosed holdings reached nearly $29.8 billion, or the equivalent of 356,491 BTC, across 13 US spot products, including an over-the-counter trust.

The Bitcoin-equivalent figure translates fund shares into their underlying Bitcoin exposure.

The filings describe a quarter that ended weeks before US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded roughly $2.4 billion in net outflows over five trading sessions through Nov. 23.

The 25 largest managers at the end of September held 60.8% of the disclosed exposure in Galaxy's dataset, and added a net 56,922 BTC-equivalent over the quarter.

The rest of the reporting managers reduced their holdings by 7,693 BTC-equivalent combined, leaving an overall net addition of 49,229.

Galaxy ranks the group by holdings on Sept. 30 and converts the change in reported shares into BTC-equivalent at September-end rates. A rising Bitcoin price alone cannot account for the increase.

Widening the group to the 50 largest holders makes the offset clearer. They added 59,578 BTC-equivalent, while everyone else cut 10,349.

Wells Fargo led the additions at 14,636 BTC-equivalent, followed by Brevan Howard at 8,606, Bank of America at 7,201, Millennium Management at 7,114, and Jane Street at 6,180.

Wells Fargo finished September with 30,553 BTC-equivalent in reported exposure, moving ahead of Jane Street, which ended the quarter at 23,167.

Galaxy counted 358 managers that disclosed positions after reporting none in June, while 167 dropped out. The newcomers had a combined 9,554 BTC-equivalent, just 2.7% of the reported holdings at quarter-end.

Separately, Bloomberg senior ETF analyst James Seyffart reported on Nov. 24 that more than 2,200 institutions held Bitcoin ETFs and said investment advisers dominated known ownership in his analysis.

A manager with a small newly disclosed position counts just as much toward participation as a large platform taking a much bigger position. And an existing holder can add a position worth far more than those newcomers put together without changing the headcount.

More managers can bring a wider set of investors into the funds while leaving the net increase sensitive to changes in a few large positions.

Across the 12 exchange-listed products Galaxy tracked, 13F filers accounted for 27.6% of the Bitcoin in the funds at Sept. 30.

The 10 largest filers held 41.9% of that disclosed exposure. A handful of financial groups can dominate the positions visible in those filings, while most of the funds’ Bitcoin backs shares held outside the 13F tally.

Galaxy’s breakdown traced 26,016 BTC-equivalent to Wells Fargo’s adviser and brokerage arms at the end of September, up from 13,219 in June.

The increase at Bank of America came mainly from its dealer and trading units, whose reported positions reached 7,626 BTC-equivalent, up from 421 in June.

Brokerage positions can include assets managed for clients, while trading positions can include inventory or one side of a hedge. SEC guidance on Form 13F requires managers to report eligible positions over which they exercise investment discretion and to leave short positions out.

A filing can show a sizable long position even if a trade elsewhere offsets some of its sensitivity to Bitcoin's price. It also gives little visibility into how many underlying investors sit behind a brokerage unit's reported position.

Across the 12 listed products, Galaxy put advisers and wealth platforms at 40.5% of reported September holdings. That category includes broker-dealer platforms, whose dealer and trading desks can also contribute to the totals.

The category added about half of the net increase in that product set.

The September quarter ended with Bitcoin at $83,580, according to Galaxy. By Nov. 16 it was $64,943, and the funds Galaxy tracked held 38,531 fewer BTC than at quarter-end.

The daily US spot Bitcoin ETF figures from Farside Investors then showed five consecutive sessions of net withdrawals from Nov. 17 through Nov. 23. Withdrawals reached $669.2 million on Nov. 20 and $569.7 million on Nov. 23.

An ETF can see withdrawals while some holders retain or add to their positions, as the largest holders increased their exposure in the third quarter while other filers cut theirs overall.

The next set of filings can show which September positions were still reported at December’s quarter-end and whether managers outside September’s top 25 increased their combined exposure.

The headcount measures participation among filers, while the distribution of changes shows whether the increase in reported fund-share exposure is spreading across more of those managers.
