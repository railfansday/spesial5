While we don’t always find the best selection of climbing gear at REI, the co-op is currently staging its big Holiday Sale, which lasts through November 23. There are some great steals for climbers in the mix, from a dry rope we love, to a solid shoe pick.

Currently, REI members can also take 20% off one full-price item, so check out our gear guides if there’s a new or full-price piece of gear you have your eye on. You can find our latest rock climbing gear guides (updated this fall) here, as well as our newest winter gear guide here. REI doesn’t have all of our top picks in stock, but you may be able to score on some of our favorites this season.

Below are a few of our favorite climbing deals on climbing shoes, ropes, purpose-built pants, and more gear that our editors and testers love.

While not a top pick in our recent shoe guide, we reviewed the Katana Lace in 2024 and called it an excellent shoe for edging and precision placements. It’s also great in narrow cracks and pockets. This is a good first performance shoe for the climber starting to think more about progression, and a great shoe for a climber who cares about performance, but doesn’t want an insanely uncomfortable pair to push their absolute limits. I’ve been climbing in the Katana since last winter and have found it a joy to wear in hand cracks, finger cracks, steep routes, and technical climbs with edging in the gym and on all types of rock. It’s not so great as a comfortable, all-day pair on moderates, or for slab, in my opinion.

Our favorite rope for trad climbing (as of fall 2026), the Black Diamond 9.4mm dry rope balances durability, sleekness, and light weight. During our testing process, we took big whippers, found ourselves hangdogging on steel permadraws, didn’t obsess over the dirt that got into our rope, and ran the 9.4 over grainy lips on sport pitches and across arêtes and edges on mixed and traditional pitches, as well. After months of testing, the rope barely showed any wear, which is notable for a 9.4 that feels skinny in hand and climbs as smooth and airy as an ultra-light—great for rope drag on monster sport climbs or zigzagging trad leads.

At the time of publication, only the 70m was in stock, with low inventory. If you’re climbing in low-moisture areas, you could get the same rope without the dry treatment (also 20% off), or save 20% on the 9.6mm version if you don’t mind the larger diameter (this version is a bit pricier because it’s bipatterned, making it easier to find the middle—worth the premium, in our opinion).

Our best all-around harness of fall 2026, the Black Diamond Solution uses what it calls Contour Edge Technology for the waist and leg loops, meaning they’re more rigid in the middle, but flexible and soft at the edges for increased comfort. Our testers loved the contoured hip support and the durability of this harness. “I took hundreds of falls and [the Black Diamond Solution Guide] gets the highest praise I can give a harness, which is that I never even thought about my harness after a fall,” one of our testers said.

Our top all-around men’s climbing pant of 2025, Black Diamond’s Alpine Light Pants feature a mobility-friendly design. We also selected them as our top women’s pick for trad climbing, multi-pitch routes, and alpine lines. While the Alpine Light pants are out of stock or low inventory right now, the Black Diamond Alpine Pants (which are, as you might expect, a bit less lightweight) are currently on sale. We found that this pant model struck an ideal balance between breathability while moving quickly, and moderately protective in light winds and rain showers with its DWR coating.

One of our top mountaineering boots of the year, the G-Summit climbing boot accomplishes a small but significant miracle: It’s a double boot that climbs like a single boot. The insulated foam liner—combined with the wrap-around inner shell and external gaiter—creates an envelope that’s warm enough for high-altitude mountaineering, and yet low-profile and precise enough for hard technical climbing up to WI6 and M7. The rubber rand was sticky enough to provide purchase in foot jams on B.C.’s Stanley Headwall, and the Vibram sole gave testers enough grip to cross boulder fields and icy scruff in Norway, Patagonia, and beyond.
