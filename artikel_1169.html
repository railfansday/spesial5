Man Utd the masters of self-destruction as pressure piles on Carrick

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

So where do Manchester United go from here? It is never a good look when opposition fans start willing a rival club to stick with their manager, but that was the ignominy that had befallen Michael Carrick by the end of an afternoon in which Lisandro Martinez’s latest plastic hardman impression embodied the very worst of United’s self-sabotage.

After seeing their team go 2-0 up when Cole Palmer converted the penalty given away by Martinez’s lunacy, the Chelsea fans taunted Carrick with chants of “You’re getting sacked in the morning” and later “You’re going down with the City”, a nod to the other Manchester club who are crisis torn for very different reasons.

As the final whistle loomed, though, and with United just grateful Estevao had not punished Martinez’s red card more severely, the Chelsea fans had changed their tune. “Michael Carrick, we want you to stay,” came the chant from the Matthew Harding Stand and that cut far deeper.

And to think that the opening 45 minutes had offered United and their fans some hope of a little respite from the storm before ultimately giving way to an ever-darkening gloom.

Carrick, who had been given a stay of execution in midweek, said he still expected to be in charge for Roma’s visit to Old Trafford on Tuesday in the Champions League. But it is hard to escape the feeling that another managerial change will be coming at United before too long even if no one can be sure what happens next.

Equally, the prospect of more upheaval in the dugout might elicit little more than a shrug from fans pointing the finger squarely at the ownership and the executives they have appointed. The players were spared this time but Sir Jim Ratcliffe and the Glazers remained the target of sustained abuse as did Jason Wilcox, the director of football.

Defeat here extended United’s winless run to nine matches, their worst sequence for 38 years. They have taken just four points from the last 21 on offer in the Premier League and seem to be sinking fast. Carrick was asked about the team becoming mired in a relegation battle. This is the territory United are in.

The strange thing is this could and probably should have been a day when they at least stopped the rot for a few days. But more non-existent defending and brainlessness from Martinez, who was sent off for planting an elbow in Wesley Fofana’s face, put paid to that in a second half in which Carrick admitted United “unravelled”.

Chelsea were cut open on at least five occasions in a see-saw first half and Matheus Cunha also had a goal disallowed for a narrow offside. Xabi Alonso’s side had opportunities of their own – you will always get them against this United team – but they were second best and lucky not to be behind. Everyone talks about United being there for the taking, but the truth is Chelsea, coming off the back of a miserable defeat to Spurs and Carabao Cup exit to Liverpool, were here.

The problem is Carrick’s team could not hit a barn door at the moment. They have scored just three goals in their last six league games and that lack of cutting edge was evident in the way Bruno Fernandes, back in the team as captain, and Amad Diallo (dangerous but with no end product) wasted excellent chances. That wastefulness would put pressure on any back line, let alone one with all the fortitude of wet cardboard. Chelsea were spared and exploited it with the help of the second and most corrosive acts of United’s self-mutilation.

We will come to Martinez’s sending off in a moment, but there had already been a cowardly act of a different kind from the Argentine defender before then in the lead-up to Chelsea’s opening goal soon after the restart.

Take nothing away from Morgan Rogers – his 40yd dash from the edge of the centre circle in United’s half to the six-yard box and the finish that followed were top notch. But where was the challenge? How could not a single player in red have stood in his path? “Come on Morgan, run through us buddy” seemed to be the message.

Michael Carrick said he expects to be in charge of Manchester United next week despite Lisandro Martinez’s moment of madness leaving the club mired in a relegation battle and on their worst run for 38 years.

United were urged to sell Martinez after the controversial Argentina defender was sent off for elbowing Wesley Fofana as Carrick accused his side of “unravelling” as they slumped to a 2-0 defeat at Chelsea.

It left United without a win in nine matches, their worst run since 1988, and at risk of being sucked into the relegation zone. They have won just two of 13 games this season.

The Old Trafford hierarchy gave Carrick a stay of execution in midweek and, when asked if he expected to still to be at the helm when United face Roma in the Champions League on Tuesday, the head coach replied: “Yes. I’m just looking forward to getting the boys ready for midweek now.”

Having been booked moments earlier for a foul on Morgan Rogers, Martinez was sent off for a second yellow. The incident came less than three weeks after the Argentinian was fined and publicly apologised for attacking United director of football Jason Wilcox and the board over transfer policy and was the latest in a series of mistakes from him on the pitch this season.

Joe Cole, the former Chelsea and England forward, believes United must get rid of Martinez.

“Whatever the next Manchester United looks like, Lisandro Martinez has run his course,’ Cole said on TNT Sports. “He’s at the scene of the crime too often for Manchester United and is at fault for moments and goals like that. So that’s probably his time done.”

Carrick refused to publicly criticise Martinez but admitted his dismissal was a “huge moment” and felt his team “unravelled” in the second period.

“Things have gone against us in a big way,” the United manager said. “It’s unravelled quickly. There’s a lot of emotion right now. It’s a huge moment in the game. Sometimes you get setbacks in games and you’ve got to deal with that better. I’ve not seen it [the Martinez incident] back so I don’t know if it was [a red card] or not. He’s gone to it and the referee gave that decision, but it’s a big moment.”

Asked if there would be a stigma attached to United potentially dropping into the relegation zone, Carrick added: “Not for me, no.”

And when asked how his players would deal with a relegation battle, Carrick added: “We’ve got to keep moving forward and winning games. There’s no words I’m going to explain to you now that makes any difference, it’s not going to change it. We’ve got to move forward and win matches.”

Good morning and welcome to live coverage of the Premier League match between Chelsea, who start the day in fifth, and 16th-placed Manchester United at Stamford Bridge. On Sunday and Monday it seemed certain that Michael Carrick would be relieved of his duties before today’s game but has survived for now, hoping to turn things around in the no man’s land that sometimes resembles a vote of confidence and at others a stay of execution.

Thirty years ago when Newcastle United were coming off the bridle in the title race with Manchester United, Alex Ferguson, as he then was, accused a Leeds team, who had been in freefall since the new year, of professional malpractice when they put in a rare display of passionate commitment and tenacity at Old Trafford in a 1-0 defeat.

“Why are they not in the top six?” asked Ferguson. “To me they’re cheating their manager. You wait and see the difference when they play Newcastle.” The hate between the Leeds and Manchester united fanbases was such, he implied, that they had decided to put a shift in because, for once, they feared the consequences if they had not. It seems to this observer that the boot is now on the other foot. These Manchester United players roused themselves to fight at Elland Road then chucked it in against Como and Bournemouth. If that’s not “cheating their manager”, what is?

Carrick will have to hope that some of the old hostility between Chelsea and Manchester United might encourage the players to run about a bit this afternoon and, at the very least, get stuck in rather than crumple spinelessly as they did last week. I doubt it would be enough to save Carrick’s job in the medium term but at least it would address whether they could stake a claim to be part of the rebuilding process.

As for Chelsea, who were awful in defeat by Brentford and Spurs either side of impressive victories over Bournemouth and at Everton, they need to find some defensive solidity, discipline and a way of terrorising defences in the absence of Joao Pedro. They are a work in progress still, in the fifth year of the hedge fund hegemony. For all that attacking talent and Moises Caicedo’s ability to steamroll midfielders, their defence does not inspire confidence and last year’s petulance continues to simmer.
