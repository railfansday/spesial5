Retailers are stocking fewer big-ticket gifts this holiday as shipping costs soar

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Trucking rates are up more than 47% from a year ago and fuel surcharges 64%, according to CNBC citing Evercore ISI

Retailers are keeping leaner stocks of big-screen TVs, grills and large appliances this holiday season, with freight, trucking and storage costs on the rise, according to CNBC.

Deborah Weinswig, CEO of Coresight Research, said the retailers she works with are cutting back on bulky or pricey products that are expensive to store. "We are going to have some of the lowest inventory levels on some of these big-ticket items we have seen in history," Weinswig said, according to CNBC.

She named three cost drivers: moving goods from origin to the U.S., carrying costs and trucking goods to stores. Each has jumped from a year earlier, she said.

General trucking rates rose more than 47% from a year earlier during the week of Nov. 2, according to CNBC, citing Evercore ISI. Industry experts attribute much of the increase to a federal crackdown on non-citizen and non-U.S.-based truck drivers. The crackdown has cut trucking capacity and is expected to keep pushing rates higher.

Fuel surcharges that carriers add to offset diesel costs are up 64% from a year earlier, according to CNBC, citing Evercore ISI. Diesel prices hit a record in September, in part because of the conflict between the U.S. and Iran, and remain elevated despite recent declines. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed past 5% in October and is still near its highest level in several years this month. Inventory holding costs track that yield.

Bill Catania, CEO of logistics technology company OneRail, said shoppers willing to wait could see bigger discounts, according to CNBC. "You're going to see more discount and price promotions as we get closer to the holiday to liquidate inventory, so they're not having to transfer it or dispose of it or position it some other way," Catania said.

Retailers are holding off on restocking until they see how demand develops following October's holiday sales, executives said, according to CNBC. Derek Leathers, CEO of Werner Enterprises, which hauls freight for big-box chains and other large retail companies, said retailers are being careful about how much they buy. "When rates are higher and the cost of capital is more expensive, you are going to have a much more intense focus on not having excess inventory," Leathers said.

Leathers said retailers are willing to spend more to lock down trucks and other transport capacity. "You can't sell it if it's not on the shelf," he said.

U.S. diesel prices had climbed to $6.51 a gallon by Sept. 20, as the conflict between the U.S. and Iran restricted tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz.

Bain & Company forecast in September that U.S. holiday retail sales would top $1 trillion for the first time. More than half of the growth is expected to come from inflation rather than additional units sold. Bain expects flat unit sales in furniture and home furnishings and in electronics and appliances, with gains in those categories coming from higher prices.
