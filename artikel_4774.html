8 Best Modular Sofas, Tested And Reviewed by AD's Editors 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

At one time, a modular sofa looked like it could have been toy furniture—each section clearly delineated. Now, seamless design allows this category of sectionals to look like traditional couches that happen to come apart in easy-to-move pieces. Better yet, this style allows you to customize furniture as your space changes. Whether you’re moving from a small apartment into a spacious four-bedroom and need to grow your seating, or you want to downsize a hulking U-shaped basement sectional into a love seat for a bedroom nook, a modular sectional is the way to go.

Style options abound for sectionals, from the artsy armless Sarah Sherman Samuel design for Lulu and Georgia to the minimalist Castlery and the iconic “Chiclet” silhouette made with injection-molded cushions from Herman Miller.

Moving around the sofa pieces is a cinch with user-friendly connector mechanisms, like slide-in glides and easy-to-configure brackets. It took contributor Yelena Moroz Alpert less than 30 minutes to maneuver the pieces of both her Burrow and Cozey modular sectionals into place. Better yet, most of the best modular sofas on this list come with white-glove delivery, so all you have to do is open the door when it arrives.

You can go as big or as small as you’d like with the Jonathan Chaise Sectional from Castlery, a modular option that starts as a two-seater and builds out to accommodate up to six. “This sofa is incredibly comfortable and feels very well-made,” contributor Rachel Fletcher says in her review. “I’d actually say it’s closer to my mattress in terms of firmness—medium firm to firm—which I very much appreciate. It’s not the kind of sofa you’re going to sink into, quicksand-style, and there’s absolutely no sagging effect.

The minimal, low-slung sofa works great in an understated space and could also appeal to a household with kids and pets. “Castlery does offer swatches and fabric samples, but I went all in without testing first and wasn’t sure what a polyester blend might feel like,” she adds. “Thankfully, it’s very soft. It’s made to resemble wool, but without the itchiness or stiffness. The heathered fabric makes it look expensive and should also help hide stains.”

The Cozey Neptune is a modular sofa that’s a jack of all trades: It can be assembled in less than half an hour, pulls out into a sleeper in two minutes flat, has hidden storage under the cushions, and is available in performance fabrics that protect against spills. Alpert chose the sofa for all those reasons, but what ultimately made the Neptune such a keeper for her was its portability. “I had no problem bringing it into the basement,” she says in her review, despite some tight angles on the landing. “Anyone who lives in a walk-up, has a tight stairway, or has narrow doorways typical of older homes will love this piece.”

Once fully assembled, it proved to be as durable as it is good-looking, treading the line between minimalism and groundedness. “I am very skeptical of couches with cushions on the back because I know that these can collapse, and that will drive me crazy,” Alpert explains. “This back is tightly upholstered, but it comes with three lumbar cushions that have a nice spring to them.” After over a year of nearly daily use from two school-age kids (and their friends), her Neptune still looks pristine.

To make the best use of the Neptune’s full-size sleeper-sofa configuration, Alpert recommends springing for the brand’s Sleep Kit ($270), which includes a topper, fitted sheet, flat sheet, two pillows and pillowcases, and a comforter. All of this fits in the three storage compartments within the seat cushions, a “saving grace” in a house with linens galore, says Alpert.

This rounded-edge, injected-foam modular sofa—affectionally nicknamed “Chiclet”—epitomizes simplicity. It’s the ideal couch if you can’t stand saggy cushions or a wrinkly back. The re-launch of the original 1976 style from Herman Miller sofa designer Ray Wilkes preserves the postmodern splendor of the first edition, but offers new fabrics and colorways, including sunny recycled wool twill and textural viscose-wool bouclé in earthy palettes. Consider this iconic sofa more of an art form than just a place to sit, even if you can build out the two-seat into a spacious six-seat perch.

No need to agonize over whether you’re a left-chaise or a right-chaise person. This reversible design lets you change your mind. Slipcovered cushions provide a relaxed, lived-in look, while its compact footprint—set on a frame made from wood certified by the Forest Stewardship Council (FSC) to be responsibly sourced—makes it an especially smart pick for apartments, condos, and cozy living rooms. The modules allow you to adjust from a three-piece sectional to a sprawling, six-piece L-shape that can host a party.

Go ahead and dramatically drop down on the Lena sofa; the armless design will keep you from awkwardly hitting the arms. This showstopper from Sarah Sherman Samuel, with artful beam legs and a foam-seat construction, allows for just enough bounce. And because the streamlined silhouette has no sharp corners, you can sit two pieces next to each other for a glorious extended bench seating suited for a large living room.

As seen in Broadway actress Barrett Wilbert Weed’s Brooklyn Heights home, Arhaus is known for statement furnishings, and the Coburn fits the bill for plush lounging in style. Its 22-inch seat depth and down-and-feather–blend cushion fill combine to create a nest that is the ultimate definition of cozy. It’s also a top pick for a family with pets or kids because it comes upholstered in Crypton, an ultra durable fabric that can withstand spills, stains, and snags.

With seven distinct seat pieces, more than 140 upholstery options, and four leg covers, this Serena & Lily sectional wins the customization game. Incorporating the new addition to the Norfolk family, a curved corner piece with a 28-inch seat, creates a sectional with a sleek silhouette worthy of a lounge, especially when you accent the setup with a well-endowed arm chair. Layer on a few throw pillows to create an illusion of a cozy pit for hibernating over the weekend.

The modular design of the Article Beta sectional can work for 13 different configurations, including a love seat chaise and an L-shaped sofa with a chaise. Your living room is truly a canvas to experiment with this sectional, upholstered in a durable blend of polyester, viscose, linen, and cotton that makes it perfect for hosting. While the Beta only comes in three colors—white, cypress green, and gray—you can easily dress it up with a woven throw or a cuddle blanket.

Which Brands Offer the Most Versatile Configurations for Modular Sofas?

Serena & Lily and and Castlery stand out in this respect for offering design-forward options in performance fabrics. Article notably offers many configurations and deep seats.

Are Luxury Modulars Pet- and Kid-Friendly?

Depending on the fabric in question, plenty of modular sectionals are pet- and kid-friendly. Performance fabrics are as kid- and pet-friendly as it gets, designed to be durable, stain-resistant, and easy to clean. Luxury leather sectionals can be less pet- or kid-friendly than those upholstered in performance fabrics, as leather is no match for pet nails, for example. Arhaus notably offers modular sectional styles upholstered in Crypton, which allows liquids to roll off, not penetrate into the seat. The material is also designed to prevent scents from seeping in, making it a go-to for a pet family.
