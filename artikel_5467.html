AAA sees fewer Thanksgiving travelers as gas prices hit records and airfares climb 17%

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Free daily briefing on global business news.

AAA projects 81.5 million Thanksgiving travelers, down 0.1% and ending a three-year streak of record holiday travel

AAA projects 81.5 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles from home for Thanksgiving, 0.1% fewer than last year, as record gas prices and higher airfares weigh on trips.

The forecast covers Tuesday, Nov. 24, through Monday, Nov. 30. The projection is about 121,000 fewer than the 81.6 million who traveled last year, AAA said.

"After three years of record highs, the number of travelers is slightly lower partly because of higher travel costs this year," Stacey Barber, vice president of AAA Travel, said in a statement. "But travelers won't notice much of a difference on the roads and at the airports, since projected volumes remain near record levels."

AAA projects 72.9 million people will travel by car, down 200,000 from 73.1 million last year. The group tied the drop in part to record gas prices. August, September and October each set a monthly record at the pump, and November could do the same. No Thanksgiving Day national average has topped $4 a gallon, and the highest was $3.58 in 2022.

About 5.96 million travelers will take domestic flights, a 0.2% decrease from last year, AAA said. Roundtrip domestic flights reserved for the holiday run 17% above year-ago prices, with the typical ticket costing $800. Buses, trains and cruises are expected to carry 2.6 million people, up 4.1%.

Gas prices were just above $4 a gallon on Nov. 9, with AAA's measure of the national average at about $4.01. AAA data show no November before 2026 had a nationwide average above $4. Prices have dropped since last week but still sit above year-ago levels.

AAA prepared its forecast the week of Oct. 12. A nationwide FAA ground stop last Friday ran past the four-hour mark and snarled air travel through the weekend, according to The Washington Times. AAA spokesperson Aixa Diaz said the stop should not meaningfully dent holiday trips unless a repeat occurs, according to The Washington Times. Diaz said people planning holiday trips rarely stay put without a major disruption, according to The Washington Times.

INRIX, a transportation data provider, expects Tuesday and Wednesday to be the busiest days on the road as people head out, AAA said. Sunday and Monday should be busiest for the return. Congestion in New York, Washington, Los Angeles and Boston could be more than double normal levels, according to INRIX.
