Norse Atlantic's Norwegian unit is filing for bankruptcy as fuel costs rise

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Norse Atlantic Airways AS canceled all flights, and the listed parent and its U.K. unit are working on a solution to keep operating

Norse Atlantic's Norwegian operating subsidiary, Norse Atlantic Airways AS, is filing a bankruptcy petition with the Agder District Court after the group failed to secure enough funding to meet its short-term liquidity needs, the company said on Friday.

The board of Norse Atlantic Airways AS concluded that there is no longer a basis for continued operations at that company and resolved on Friday to file. The district court will appoint a bankruptcy trustee to manage the process.

Parent Norse Atlantic ASA, which trades on Euronext Expand Oslo under the ticker "Norse," and other group companies are not part of the filing. All Norse flights are canceled, and ticket sales have been discontinued, the company said.

"The Board of Norse Atlantic Airways AS has made the difficult decision to file for bankruptcy despite extensive efforts from the Company and the Group to find a solution," CEO Eivind Roald said in a statement. He said the board, management and advisers of Norse Atlantic ASA and the U.K. subsidiary are working to find a solution that lets the remaining part of the business continue operations.

The company said it is weighing alternatives involving Norse Atlantic ASA and selected subsidiaries to preserve value for stakeholders.

Lessors have sent notices canceling their contracts covering the entire 12-jet Norse fleet, according to Bloomberg. Norse maintains that the notices are not legally valid.

The airline grounded its entire schedule and missed its payroll earlier this week, according to Bloomberg. Roald told staff in an email on Wednesday that Norse had failed to line up the money for that day's payroll, according to Bloomberg.

Norse did not hedge any of its jet fuel this year, so it bore the full force of price swings, according to Bloomberg. Its financial problems began before the Middle East war, but the fighting made them much worse by driving up the cost of oil.

Norse stock has lost over 99% of its value this year, leaving the company worth roughly 49 million Norwegian kroner ($5 million), according to Bloomberg.
