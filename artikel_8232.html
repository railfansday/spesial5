Hiker Trash 4-Star Hotel Crash

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Before leaving Redondela, we went back to the same bakery we visited last night. We picked up a couple of ham sandwiches—mine came loaded with egg and tuna, too—along with some pastries and coffee to fuel up for the morning.

The trail leaving Redondela climbed steeply for the first 1.5 miles. As we gained elevation, we walked straight into low-lying clouds that created a peaceful, misty backdrop around the local villas.

About an hour later, we reached the top. From here, the path travels along a ridge overlooking the water. Because of the heavy cloud cover, our views were mostly muted, though the clouds occasionally parted to illuminate the landscape below.

I could hear church bells tolling off in the distance as we entered a quiet, forested path lined with stone walls and swirling fog. Eventually, the Camino exited the woods into a hillside residential neighborhood. We wound along narrow roads past impressive homes, with small patches of forest breaking up the route.

Soon, the sun began burning off the fog, revealing the ocean far below. We heard the roar of interstate traffic beneath us, followed shortly by the sound of a second interstate above. Surrounded by the stereo sound of highways, we began our descent into Vigo.

Because we needed to drop down from the high ridge, I decided to leave the official Camino path and map out our own shortcut.

Using Google Maps, we navigated a maze of narrow pathways tucked between buildings. Multiple times, we walked toward what looked like a dead end, only to discover a hidden gateway leading to another alleyway and another set of staircases. I was amazed at both how well Google knew the local backstreets and just how far down we actually had to climb.

Eventually, we spotted a McDonald’s sign on a building below us. Knockerz was thrilled to find a rare McDonald’s on the trail—even if the food was pricey.

Vigo turned out to be a surprisingly massive city. I had never heard of it before this trip and certainly didn’t expect a metropolis.

We booked a four-star hotel for the night that only cost $100. However, because we walked into the lobby looking like “hiker trash” fresh out of the woods, I suspect the front desk clerk tucked us away from the main guests. Our initial room had an unappealing view of nearby rundown buildings.

We politely asked to switch rooms, and the staff moved us to a much nicer, higher-floor room overlooking the city and the waterfront. After dumping my pack, I spent a few hours exploring Vigo before heading back for a very comfortable night’s sleep.
