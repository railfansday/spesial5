Scotland boss Sebastien Pocognoli needs a statement win to change the narrative around his new-look side

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 13:37 EST, 15 November 2026 | Updated: 13:37 EST, 15 November 2026

While it’s fair to state that the touchpaper has yet to be lit on Sebastien Pocognoli’s tenure, he is hardly the first Scotland manager to discover that the battle for hearts and minds can be a drawn-out affair.

Although Steve Clarke marked the start of his era with an unconvincing win over Cyprus at Hampden in 2019, the Ayrshireman lost his next four games to Russia and Belgium. It would take him 15 months to chalk up what might be considered a statement victory.

That 2-1 win against the Czech Republic in Olomouc in September 2020, in his tenth match in charge, felt like a turning point for the national team and so it proved with qualification for the delayed Euros in the post. But Clarke certainly had to suffer for it.

So for all the frustration felt at Pocognoli’s new-look side failing to hit the straps in the past five matches, we have been here before. And statistically, to date, it’s also been by no means disastrous.

Notwithstanding how ordinary North Macedonia are, the Belgian is actually the first manager since Gordon Strachan in 2013 to win more than one of his first five games in charge. The history books show that Scotland don’t really do fast starts when there’s a new man at the helm.

Nonetheless, it would be hard to overstate the significance of Pocognoli seeing it all spectacularly come together as the Nations League campaign concludes in St Gallen tomorrow.

Friday’s win over the group whipping boys was certainly deserved, yet the performance, particularly in the first half, was hardly scintillating.

Even those who are prepared to be patient with Pocognoli would have been concerned at the lack of tempo and imagination in the general play in the first period.

Weren’t we told that he would bring a radically different approach to Clarke? Yet there was scant evidence on show of the ultra-offensive, high-pressing style which was billed in the brochure.

While a markedly better second half yielded a welcome first home win for the 39-year-old, no one left the arena clicking their heels at what they’d witnessed. This remains very much a work in progress.

What can be said with some certainty, however, is that the players are on board with the grand plan. When they faced the media last week, it was noticeable that Scott McTominay, Andy Robertson and John McGinn all praised the new man’s methods and ideas and predicted that they will bear fruit in the fullness of time.

You get a sense when experienced players are reading empty platitudes from a prepared script. Yet the words of the leadership group felt truly authentic on that occasion.

While Pocognoli will obviously have welcomed them, it’s not statements of support from senior figures which he needs to keep the Tartan Army onside. It’s positive results.

Come what may against Switzerland tomorrow, Scotland don’t have a match until March. That’s an awful long time to reflect if it doesn’t go well.

What Pocognoli and the travelling fans wouldn’t give for that seismic win to shift the narrative and engender a greater sense of optimism ahead of the Euro qualifiers.

Strachan’s first big scalp arrived at the fourth time of asking when a Robert Snodgrass goal sealed an unlikely win away to Croatia. Such unexpected outcomes can have a galvanising effect.

Friday’s narrow win over North Macedonia did keep the possibility alive of Scotland finishing second in Group B1, but few would be sticking their house on that yet coming to pass.

With head-to-head rather than goal difference counting if sides are locked on the same number of points, Pocognoli’s men must beat the Swiss then hope that Slovenia don’t win in Skopje. Stranger things have happened, but not too many.

In all likelihood, Scotland will finish third and be a pot three team when the draw for Euro 2028 is made in Belfast on December 6. Yet, it’s still vital that they conclude this campaign with – at the very least – a display which offers grounds for optimism.

This was always going to be the toughest assignment of the six. In the summer, the Swiss reached the last eight of the World Cup for the first time since 1954, only losing in extra-time to Argentina. That remains their only defeat this year.

They’ve hardly missed a beat in the Nations League, winning four games and drawing one, to comfortably secure promotion to the top tier.

Even though they were given a helping hand by Jack Hendry’s early red card as they won 3-0 at Hampden in September, they looked a level above Pocognoli’s men.

‘We reached all of our goals,’ said coach Murat Yakin.

‘Maybe we could have done even more at the World Cup, but we created history by reaching the quarter-finals.

‘I am happy with our playing style. There’s been good development and we have integrated new players.’

The hope for Scotland stems from the fact that Yakin is compelled to give some fringe players an opportunity to shine in St Gallen.

Inter Milan’s Manuel Akanji has become the latest big name to withdraw through injury. Johan Manzambi, Dan Ndoye and Granit Xhaka were already out.

Even then, the task for the visitors remains formidable. The only defeat for the Swiss in their past 11 games on home soil came at the hands of Germany.

‘It has happened a few times already,’ Yakin said of the injury situation.

‘But we will make sure we get a good level of players on to the pitch. It’s a good thing we have won the group already. It’s now an opportunity to make changes.

‘It doesn’t make sense to wait. We have enough opportunities and possibilities.’

It feels like a night for Pocognoli to proceed with caution. A fairly assured display against North Macedonia means Ryan Porteous, Luke Graham and Kieran Tierney will probably be asked to go again as a back-three.

Given his defensive diligence, Aaron Hickey would be favoured to displace Ben Gannon-Doak on the right flank.

As on Friday, Billy Gilmour and Lewis Ferguson should play behind John McGinn and Scott McTominay with Che Adams leading the line.

On the occasion when he’ll become Scotland’s most capped player with 103 appearances, it’s to be hoped Andy Robertson has a night to remember.

‘What can I say about him?’ added Yakin. ‘He’s a fantastic player, who has also constantly carried the team or the nation of Scotland.

‘The best players should be on the pitch and we’re happy to compete with him. It’s a nice mark for him, and in that sense, congratulations.’
