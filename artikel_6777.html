Salma Hayek Pinault, Jane Fonda, and Hollywood’s Leading Names Gathered to Toast Women In Film

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

If there was one room in Hollywood where female power was unmistakable, it was the Beverly Hilton on Thursday night, where Women In Film hosted its annual WIF Honors. Since 1977, the organization has honored some of the entertainment industry’s most accomplished names, celebrating their contributions to film and television, and this time around was no exception.

Presented in partnership with Max Mara, the night brought together Salma Hayek Pinault, Kerry Washington, Jane Fonda, Olivia Munn, Emmy Rossum, Sharon Stone, and Katherine LaNasa. Naturally, Max Mara had plenty of representation in the room, with Fonda, Hannah Waddingham, Caylee Cowan, and breakthrough star Adria Arjona among those wearing the Italian label. Washington, meanwhile, opted for Balmain, while LaNasa chose Michael Kors.

One of the night’s standout moments came when Fonda took the stage to present Hayek Pinault with the Jane Fonda Humanitarian Award. It was a particularly fitting pairing, given their longstanding commitment to using their platforms to advocate for human rights and social issues. Reflecting on their shared history, Fonda recalled traveling to Mexico with Hayek Pinault more than two decades ago to raise awareness about hundreds of women and girls who had been murdered or declared missing.

What stood out to Fonda then, and since, is Hayek Pinault’s unwavering dedication to the causes she takes on. “She’s so passionate, and what really impressed me is how she listens to people and really gets to know them,” Fonda told the audience. “You can’t just write a check, you have to be there and have kinship.”

Fonda also praised Hayek Pinault’s efforts to end systemic violence against women and girls, including her testimony before the U.S. Senate and her work through the Kering Foundation. “She understands power. We all need to understand power and learn how to wield it,” Fonda said, before delivering a final tribute. “This is a woman who knows that getting what you want often requires more than good intentions. It requires guts, being prepared to get in the ring and fight, and boy, has she fought. And the willingness to get your hands dirty.”

Dressed in Bottega Veneta, Hayek Pinault accepted the honor from her longtime friend and mentor. Rather than deliver a traditional acceptance speech, the actress settled in for an onstage conversation with Fonda that offered a glimpse into what drives her philanthropy and activism.

As for her desire to bring more authentic Hispanic characters and stories to international audiences, Hayek Pinault explained that producing became her way of creating the path she felt was missing when she first arrived in Hollywood. “I complained a lot about the lack of opportunities when I arrived here, so the only way I could do something about it was [getting] behind the camera,” she said. “I created a company that created opportunities for others.” Noting that nearly all the projects her company has produced have not featured her, she added, “It’s for other people. To tell our stories, I don’t have to be center stage.”

When the conversation turned to her work with her husband François-Henri Pinault through the Kering Foundation, she summed up the motivation behind their efforts in one word: “Love. Really, it is love. I do it with my love. I found myself a partner who respects what I do. It’s wonderful to have a man that cares about women. We do it together.”

On the red carpet, Hayek Pinault offered another glimpse into the determination that has shaped her career. “They told me to go back to my country. ‘There’s no opportunities here. You’re a big star in Mexico, go back. Don’t be silly,’” she recalled to Vogue. “They meant well, by the way.”

But even back then, Hayek Pinault trusted her instincts and saw an audience that the American entertainment industry hadn’t yet figured out how to reach. “There are 42 million Latinos in this country, and [Hollywood] literally didn’t understand it. I knew it would happen. I just didn’t know it would happen in my time.”

Hayek Pinault was among several honorees recognized throughout the evening. The WIF Max Mara Face of the Future Award went to Arjona, who’s readying to appear in next year’s hotly anticipated The Thomas Crown Affair. The Puerto Rico-born emerging talent was also fêted the night before the WIF Honors by the fashion label at a chic cocktail reception at Chateau Marmont.

Pamela Abdy, former co-chair and co-CEO of Warner Bros. Motion Picture Group, received the inaugural Vanguard Award, celebrating a career dedicated to moving the industry forward. Filmmaker Beth de Araújo and actress Gemma Chan shared the Crystal Award for Advocacy in Film for their work on Josephine, the psychological drama that made a splash at the Sundance Film Festival.

Waddingham, meanwhile, was presented with the Crystal Award for Advocacy in TV, recognizing the Emmy-winning Ted Lasso star’s commitment to creating more opportunities for women both in front of and behind the camera. And if anyone was expecting a subdued acceptance speech, Waddingham had another thing in mind. “Being in this room tonight is like the most concentrated, potent shot in the arm of every single podcast I would ever want to listen to in one hit,” she began, drawing laughs from the audience. “To even be here and share time and oxygen and a cheeky beverage or two with you all—I mean, it’s just f*cking great!”

But beneath the humor was a serious message. “I take the status, the platform, and the voice that I have been afforded incredibly seriously,” she said, reflecting on her commitment to speaking up for others, from the LGBTQ+ community to women fighting for better access in the industry. “As long as there is breath in my body, I will stand on that ladder with my incredibly high, fabulous heels. On the bottom rung, I will always give a hand to those who need it. I encourage you to do the same because the soul is so rewarding when advocating for others, and it is endless,” she concluded.
