Goodbye to metasearch having its own ad budget and scorecard. Google lets hotels place ads in its price-comparison results, but is changing how they place those ads. Will the new process make ad buys harder to scrutinize?

Google is retiring its standalone hotel ad campaigns and folding paid booking links into its main search advertising product, a shift that changes how hotels budget for, bid on, and measure metasearch (meaning, the platform's price-comparison listings).

The advertising line item hotels have budgeted for years for "Google Hotel Ads" must now be bundled into all of their work on Google Search. That leaves hotel commercial chiefs with a question: what counts as success once metasearch and paid search share an ad campaign?

A Google spokesperson told Skift the change brings bidding, reporting, and tools such as AI Max, its suite of automated campaign features, onto one platform. The update reflects only how advertisers buy ads, the spokesperson said, so free booking links are untouched.

Google is retiring standalone hotel ad campaigns and folding paid booking links into its main search advertising product, converting them automatically into "Search campaigns for Travel" in the first half of 2027, with advertisers able to migrate sooner. A Google spokesperson told Skift the change brings bidding, reporting, and tools such as AI Max onto one platform, affects only how advertisers buy ads, and leaves free booking links untouched. Consumers will still see ads in both Google Hotels and Google Search. The practical consequences land on hotel commercial teams: enhanced and percentage-based cost-per-click bidding convert to manual cost per click, travel promotion ads will count an impression for every carousel card, and past campaign performance data stays behind in the old tool. Rudolph Dekker of Adchieve said losing percentage-based bidding matters most for hotels with wide seasonal rate swings, since a fixed bid doesn't follow room rates up and down. Quentin Lederer of Cendyn said the harder change is organizational, because combining campaigns forces leadership to look at blended acquisition metrics rather than grading each channel in isolation. Cornell's Chris Anderson said the migration is also a moment to test whether paid hotel links generate incremental bookings or simply charge hotels for demand they would have captured free.
