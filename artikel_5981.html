Senate Republicans break through Washington gridlock with massive American energy, data center overhaul

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Oklahoma Sen. Alan Armstrong discusses the critical need for permitting reform to boost American energy independence. He explains that despite abundant U.S. oil and gas resources, outdated processes and litigation delay essential infrastructure projects. Armstrong, a former CEO, highlights how legal challenges can halt multi-billion dollar projects, emphasizing the need for robust political action to streamline energy development.

Senate Republicans scored a major win after advancing a massive bill that would overhaul America’s permitting system in a bid to turbocharge infrastructure and energy development in the country.

The Senate advanced the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026 (BAAJA) on a major bipartisan vote Thursday night. The legislation went through fits and starts over the last few months, until a bipartisan coalition, with buy-in from the White House, formed to tinker with the final product.

At the 30,000-foot level, the sweeping package would accelerate environmental review of projects, limit legal challenges, protect approved projects, expand the electric grid and streamline energy development in all fields — like oil, gas and renewables.

"This is a major step toward making it faster and more efficient and easier to build infrastructure projects, certainly without giving up any of the strong protections for our environment," Senate Environment and Public Works Committee Chair Shelley Moore Capito, R-W.Va., said when the bill was introduced in September.

The bill also tackles an issue that was front and center for voters across the country: data centers.

Sen. Martin Heinrich, D-N.M., the Democratic lead on the legislation, pushed for stronger language in the bill that would shield ratepayers from higher costs that stem from data center development and their usage of electricity.

That comes after Republicans tried to ram through similar safeguards before the election with a bill from Sen. Jon Husted, R-Ohio, that Democrats argued lacked real enforcement teeth.

"This process will mean that we develop responsibly. It will mean more jobs. It will mean cheaper energy," Heinrich said. "It will mean that we actually require big load centers, whether that's a factory or a data center, to not pass those costs on to the American consumer by statute, not a suggestion, an actual law."

The provision covers data centers with a demand of at least 20 megawatts of power and would require them to cover the cost of additional power for them to run, financial guarantees covering costs to build or upgrade facilities, to pay the tab if they leave an area for unpaid infrastructure costs, allow states to charge higher rates to reduce other customers’ bills and tack on power supply conditions.

Meanwhile, whether the legislation makes it through the House is an open question.

Sen. Alan Armstrong, R-Okla., who was appointed to the position earlier this year to replace Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin and has made permitting reform his primary policy issue, told Fox News Digital that there were "a lot of good supporters on both sides of the aisle in the House." But he worried about attendance issues in the lower chamber.

"A lot of the language that's in here came right out of a lot of the House’s work," Armstrong said. "So, I think there'll be some support. I just hope we have people, you know, show up in the numbers that we need to vote for it."

Alex Miller is a writer for Fox News Digital covering the U.S. Senate.
