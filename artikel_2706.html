3 takeaways from Kansas State basketball's early-season statement win vs Creighton

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

MANHATTAN — Kansas State basketball has its first statement win under Casey Alexander.

The Wildcats pulled off an 89-82 win over Creighton on Tuesday, Nov. 10, at Bramlage Coliseum.

Brandon Rechsteiner put on a show for the Wildcats, scoring 26 points. The win comes against new Creighton coach Alan Huss, who was in the mix for K-State's opening this offseason.

The Wildcats (2-1) will get a break before they return to the court. Their next game is against Montana on Monday, Nov. 16, at 7 p.m. CT at Bramlage Coliseum.

Here are our takeaways from Tuesday night's game against the Bluejays.

The Wildcats are starting to find out that they have a go-to player when the lights are the brightest. Brandon Rechsteiner put on a show.

The senior Colorado State transfer went off for 26 points, while making six shots from beyond the arc. Every time it seemed like the Wildcats needed a big shot, he was there to make it. His biggest came when he hit a trey with 5:52 left after Creighton cut the Wildcats' 15-point lead to three.

Rechsteiner kept the Wildcats in control and calm from the primary ball-handling position. He led an offense that was a lot of fun to watch.

The biggest reason why Creighton hung around with Kansas State for so long was due to the Wildcats' high number of turnovers.

K-State finished with 22 turnovers, with Rechsteiner leading the team with five. The Wildcats were outscored 30-21 in points off of turnovers.

Alexander was visibly frustrated with a few, even sending Rechsteiner to the bench after he turned it over three times before the second half's first media timeout.

Turnovers have been high through three games. The Wildcats had 12 in their opening-night loss to South Dakota State, and 17 in their win over Cal State Northridge.

Alexander teased that Malovec's debut was near on Monday before the game. The Miami (Florida) transfer's debut came immediately the next day.

Malovec checked in for the first time with 15:55 left in the first half after missing the first two games due to injury. He finished the game with 14 minutes, four points and one rebound.

Alexander said Malovec's presence will eventually settle the Wildcats down after not having played much with the team since July. He complimented his intelligence at both ends of the floor and his offensive skills.
