Lions stock report: 21 risers, 5 fallers against the Patriots

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Detroit Lions' offense got back to its overpowering ways with its first 30-plus-point scoring output since Week 5, despite not being entirely sharp. Meanwhile, the defensive resurgence continued, allowing only 10 points, although that comes with a grain of salt given the early-game injury to New England Patriots quarterback Drake Maye. Unfortunately, for the second victory in the last three weeks, the overall winning feeling was bittersweet, overshadowed by the potential season-ending loss of Aidan Hutchinson. The locker room again has to endure the perils of losing another defensive captain and one of its biggest difference-makers. His absence will place an even greater onus on the offense to perform at an elite level and help carry the team.

Dan Campbell continued to echo confidence in the team's plan to break this portion of the schedule into a four-game series, or quarter of the season, from traveling to Miami, to Munich, back home without a bye against Tampa Bay, and the final (turkey) leg coming on a short week as they host the Bears as their Thanksgiving guests. Halfway through, the team has executed well, handling its business on the business trip, with the old adage that defense travels living up to its billing. Just as importantly, Lions fans have flocked to take over both Hard Rock Stadium and now seemingly all of Munich. The resounding takeaway from the trip is the power of not just the Detroit Lions, but the fandom that's part of the journey with them.

With the Lions winning 35-10, their 25-point margin of victory being their largest since Week 2 against the Bears last season, and extending their winning streak to five games, the Week 10 stock report entails far more positive risers than fallers.

It's getting to the point where Gibbs is almost a permanent fixture in the stock-up section, as he continues to barrel through a career season playing like a legitimate Offensive Player of the Year candidate. It's impossible to leave him off after leading the Lions in both rushing and receiving yards. He notched the third-most rushing yards in a game in his career (153), the second-most receiving yards of his career (99), and the second-most yards from scrimmage (252) in a game in his career. Gibbs also recorded the second-most rushing first downs (9) in a game in his career. He now has six career games with eight or more rushing first downs, and half of them have come since Week 5. Not bad for a team that once went 70 consecutive games without a 100-yard rusher from 2013 to 2018.

Gibbs was playing at a different speed than the Patriots' defense, able to dodge, duck, dip, dive, and dodge defenders with ease while displaying a fantastic feel for pacing and patience to set up his blockers, but also knowing when to floor it and explode through the hole. Both of his touchdown runs exemplified that perfectly. The team's third touchdown drive consisted entirely of six Gibbs touches for 84 total yards, right after he and Sione Vaki nearly single-handedly marched the Lions from their own 3-yard line to set up the Isaac TeSlaa touchdown.

Having days when the play design and offensive line allow him to get into a zone and take over games for different stretches will be remarkably valuable come the postseason.

The big boys up front unexpectedly got muscled around by the Miami Dolphins' defensive front last week, not long after the team let the Packers' defensive front invade their side of the line of scrimmage. But the unit responded triumphantly with the return of Christian Mahogany to the lineup.

Although they still had some squeaky wheels in pass protection early on, something they need to continue calibrating, they were dominant for the majority of the game. They consistently reset the line of scrimmage, displaced defenders, washed out defenders at the second level like a tidal wave, hit their targets on pulls, and gave Gibbs and Vaki rushing lanes to run through. They also gave Jared Goff stability in the pocket after the early leakage and were essential in the screen passing game.

Most importantly, they started to reduce negative plays. Their stuff rate allowed was only 11.8%, the second-lowest of the season behind only the Jets game, a stark contrast to the 30.3% allowed against the Green Bay Packers. They also allowed a respectable 2.74-second time to pressure, helping the offense produce its second-highest EPA/play of the season (+0.25) and highest yards per play (7.3).

It felt like a stepping-stone game in which both sides of the line, Penei Sewell-Christian Mahogany and Tate Ratledge-Blake Miller, continued to develop chemistry and complement each other on two-man blocking assignments, while Juice Scruggs maintained his exceedingly excellent level of play at the pivot. Sewell killing two birds with one stone, without even touching one of the defenders on Gibbs' sweep touchdown, was an impressive feat that only he could pull off.

It was far too fitting for Bavarian St. Brown to put on a show while in the spotlight in his mother's native Germany. What was surprising was seeing him score multiple touchdowns from deep range, becoming the first Lions wide receiver since Marvin Jones in 2020 to score multiple receiving touchdowns of at least 25 yards in a game. In doing so, he also joined the illustrious company of Calvin Johnson and Herman Moore as the only Lions players in franchise history with at least 50 receiving touchdowns. St. Brown's surprising explosiveness also led to him tying a career-high with 24.5 yards per reception in a game.

Big-play St. Brown kicked off his fireworks show and set the tone with a wild-and-wacky, slip-and-slide 56-yard touchdown catch-and-run that was reminiscent of some of Golden Tate's most thrilling plays as a Detroit Lion. He continued his smooth navigation on the greasy, yet firm, thin-grass surface by expertly swerving around blockers for a 28-yard zoom on a tunnel screen, finding an open lane on the Autobahn before getting a little help from his friends to push him over the edge. To fully maximize the Lions' offense, St. Brown also added his trademark tough-nosed blocking effort and execution, once again proving just how valuable a team player he is.

McCreary is on his fourth defensive coordinator in his last four seasons, and the Lions were also breaking in new coverage principles to start the season. While McCreary was inconsistent early on, he may finally be settling in. The interception was a nice gift right into his breadbasket, but his pass deflection against DeMario Douglas, covering the full width of the field, was particularly impressive. On the day, he allowed only two receptions for 10 yards on four targets and a 16.7 passer rating when targeted. McCreary erased Douglas in coverage, a slot player who has been playing really good football recently.

He did so while splitting time between outside cornerback in base personnel and the slot in nickel personnel. As he continues to get more comfortable in that Amik Robertson role, perhaps his best football is still ahead of him in the second half of the season.

Sione Vaki, RB: One way to stay ahead of the sticks and mitigate negative plays is simply making sure Vaki gets some carries, as he plows through the hole and consistently falls forward.

Jameson Williams & Isaac TeSlaa, WR: While the duo "only" combined for six catches on 12 targets for 69 receiving yards and a touchdown, they made some big-time catches, including TeSlaa's spectacular, acrobatic end-zone grab and Williams' toe-tap along the sideline. It was also impressive how bought-in both are when it comes to run blocking and extra-effort downfield blocking, including on St. Brown's touchdowns and multiple explosive runs by Gibbs.

Tyleik Williams, NT: Had a lower-than-typical snap count (19) but was impactful in disrupting the run game and tossing Patriots center Jared Wilson around.

Alim McNeill, DT: Another game, another sack, a handful of pressures, and a run stop. He got the best of veteran guard Greg Van Roten multiple times throughout the game.

Derrick Moore, DE: Moore is really bursting onto the scene, with back-to-back games featuring five pressures and two sacks. He also played a career-high 40 defensive snaps and will now be thrust into a much larger role as he steps up in Hutchinson's absence.

K.J. Britt, LB: Started rotating in with Malcolm Rodriguez in the second half and immediately looked like a Stephen Tulloch-sized battering ram, while also looking surprisingly fluid and comfortable dropping into coverage.

Rock Ya-Sin, CB: Ya-Sin had ideal matchups with the physical, lumbering Mack Hollins and A.J. Brown. On 22 combined coverage snaps against the two, he was targeted five times and allowed only one catch for 5 yards.

Brian Branch, S: The human torpedo. Branch was lockdown in coverage, allowing only one catch for 6 yards on four targets, and was continually flying downhill to stuff Patriots running backs in the hole or cutback lanes at the line of scrimmage.

Chuck Clark, S: I'd check your surroundings while reading this article because Clark might fly in and tabletop you, tipping you over like a wheelbarrow out of nowhere. Clark has easily stacked his two most effective games back-to-back against AFC East foes in the Dolphins and Patriots, becoming more active making plays around the football as opposed to being stuck in the mud.

Dan Campbell, Drew Petzing, and Kelvin Sheppard: All three deserve credit for handling the international logistics and having their team prepared, ready to fight, and executing excellent game plans.

Jared Goff, QB: Goff is the most polarizing player in the stock report, no matter where he lands. The lovely Pride of Detroit commenter community will inevitably find something to disagree about. But despite the offense scoring five touchdowns against the Patriots, Goff's play was inconsistent, with some uncharacteristic decision-making and throws left on the table. He'll look to bounce back against the Buccaneers' defense and a cast of old Lions friends.

Sam LaPorta, TE: He again exceeded expectations with some of his blocking assignments, including more work in the backfield as an H-back or fullback. But you'd like to see LaPorta bring down the ball Goff delivered to him in the back of the end zone or flash his hands earlier on the seam ball down the middle. Over the last four weeks coming out of the bye, LaPorta has recorded 16 targets, nine catches, 99 receiving yards (11 yards per catch), and no touchdowns.

Ennis Rakestraw, CB: Just five snaps despite D.J. Reed being out with an injury, after playing only one snap two weeks earlier against the Vikings, and it appears Rakestraw has fallen out of favor a bit. He also had a poor missed tackle attempt on Hunter Henry during his only snap of the first half, which came on the opening drive.

Nick Whiteside, CB: In his first action of the season, Whiteside played only seven defensive snaps and was flagged for defensive pass interference on third-and-18 during the Patriots' only touchdown drive.

Khalil Dorsey, CB: Not as sharp of a day for Dorsey, who missed a tackle and allowed a catch-and-run to A.J. Brown on third down. There was another rep where Mack Hollins got open over the middle against Dorsey, but DeVito couldn't pull the trigger due to pressure. However, on an earlier third-and-20, Dorsey stopped Eli Raridon for a short gain to force a punt.
