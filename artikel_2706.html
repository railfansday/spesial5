The Colts, as expected, won't activate WR Alec Pierce from injured reserve

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

As the Colts fully embrace the making of business decisions over the pursuit of victories, this one is no surprise.

Via Ian Rapoport of ESPNFL, the Colts won't activate receiver Alec Pierce from injured reserve. He'll miss the rest of the season, with the goal of being fully and completely healthy for 2027.

The situation with Pierce becomes just another one of the tangible examples of the manner in which everything has gone sideways for the Colts over the past calendar year, starting with the November 2025 trade for cornerback Sauce Gardner. Despite knowing that Pierce had an ankle issue that would eventually require surgery, the Colts re-signed Pierce at the outset of free agency to a four-year deal with an annual average of $28.5 million.

While well below the top of the market at the position, it was a major investment in a player with a significant injury. Pierce had surgery in late March and played two games in September (catching five passes for 72 yards) before landing on injured reserve due to an aggravation of the condition.

Given the timing of the deal with Pierce (it was announced just as the March 9 negotiating window opened), it appears that the Colts put a number on the table aimed at keeping him from taking a comparable deal elsewhere. With the Colts promptly trading receiver Michael Pittman Jr. to the Steelers, and in light of the limited production they've gotten from Pierce this year, it's very easy to conclude they should have let Pierce walk, keeping Pittman and moving on.

At this point, with the team at 3-8 and pivoting to quarterback Anthony Richardson Sr., there's no reason to rush Pierce back. From the moment the Colts traded guard Quenton Nelson to the Seahawks and defensive tackle DeForest Buckner back to the 49ers, the Colts threw in the shoe on 2026. Everything that has happened since then serves only to highlight the fact that they no longer care about 2026 (to the delight of the Jets, who hold Indy's first-round pick next year), and that they're trying to best position themselves for 2027.

Keeping Pierce on IR is just another obvious aspect of that broader plan. And it bolsters the possibility that owner Carlie Irsay-Gordon will decide to give coach Shane Steichen another year.

After a hard-fought Week 7 game at Minnesota saw a potential 3-4 record slip to 2-5, the "we'll get 'em next year" plan was fully activated. While Steichen can be held accountable for the first seven games, it's hard to pin much blame on him for the final ten.
