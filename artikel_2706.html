"What If" Wolves Volume 7: Draft bust? What's that?

Very few franchises have employed more draft busts than our beloved Minnesota Timberwolves. The poor drafting started right away, and unfortunately has continued all the way up to the drafting of Rob Dillingham in 2024. But our Wolves don't just draft busts, we also trade for them and give them huge contracts in free agency.

The list of unfulfilled potential is long. Here's a quick list to refresh your mind of the hot garbage that has graced the hardwood at Target Center:

And those are just some of the first round draft picks. We couldn't stop with just draft picks though. We just had to trade for and try to fix everyone else's garbage.

Darko "Manna from heaven" Miličić (2003 – 2nd pick)

Cody Williams (2024 – 10th pick) (too soon?)

Now I know some of you may be thinking Luc Longley wasn't that bad…for the Chicago Bulls. McCants and Foye were decent players (they weren't.) Ndubi Ebi? Didn't he have six fingers? Point is: we were terrible at drafting players, trading for players, and signing players. But there is one draft pick worse than all of these.

Derrick Williams was the number two overall pick for the Wolves in the 2011 NBA Draft. Now this is rare for me, but I am going to be completely honest here: I was all about drafting Derrick Williams. Williams was the consensus second pick, right behind Duke's Kyrie Irving. Hell, some people even had Williams as the number one pick.

Williams was an All-American power forward from the University of Arizona. He led the Wildcats to the Elite Eight his sophomore year, even upsetting Kyrie and the Blue Devils with a stat line of 32 points and 13 rebounds. He was a "can't miss" prospect with a solid inside and outside game, explosive to the basket, and was a great rebounder for his size at 6'8". Over a two year college career, Derrick made 42 of 74 three-point attempts. For those of you that struggle with math, that's 56.8%.

The 2011-12 Timberwolves roster was kind of a mess. Kevin Love was the best player on the team with averages of 26 points and 13 rebounds per game which led to his first All-Star appearance. Ricky Rubio finally came over from Spain for his rookie year. Michael Beasley was at the small forward position, trying to revive his career. Nikola "Serbian Godfather" Peković ate all the meat. Luke Ridnour was there, nobody really noticed. And then there was Derrick Williams, a power forward in a small forward's body (or Caged Lion's body.) Too slow to take anyone to the rack (shoutout "mud runs"). Couldn't create his own shot. And his 56.8% three-point percentage from Arizona? It was still in Tucson.

Williams did have his flashes. He had a few double-doubles. Scored 27 against the Los Angeles Clippers one night in February. Derrick was on the finishing end of a few alley-oops from Ricky Rubio. But once Rubio went down with his knee injury (I would never forgive Kobe Bryant for this…or for some other issues), Williams struggled mightily.

I refuse to believe that drafting the biggest bust in franchise history can't be fixed. Afterall, this is the "What If" Wolves. We only draft winners. So let's go back in time and get rid of Derrick Williams and make this right. And I mean really right.

The 2011 NBA draft was considered at the time, a weak ass draft. It was Irving and Williams, then a bunch of other stuff. Granted, there are some Hall of Fame players that came out of this draft. Klay Thompson (11th pick), Kawhi Leonard (15th pick), previously mentioned Kyrie Irving (1st pick), and it make me sick to say this, Jimmy Butler (30th pick and hopefully doesn't get in the HOF). Because of the limited upside at the top of the draft board, I am going to take advantage of the ridiculous fandom that was surrounding BYU guard Jimmer Fredette.

Jimmer Fredette played just like Steph Curry, but the Utah beer version, aka weak. But the people in Utah couldn't get enough of Jimmer, so I make a call to the Utah Jazz and take advantage. I trade back in the draft by giving the Jazz the second overall pick for the following: 2011 tenth overall pick, 2013 unprotected first round pick, 2014 unprotected second round pick, and a 2015 unprotected second round pick. Utah moves up eight spots for what is basically an additional first and two seconds. Not a bad trade for either side. Maybe I could have gotten the Jazz to sweeten the deal, but their roster was just as bad as the Wolves at that time. With the second pick, the Jazz take Jimmer Fredette. This pick was made by the Jazz so fast that Kyrie was still shaking the David Stern's hand.

Now that I avoided the dreaded draft bust of the 2011 NBA draft, I patiently wait for the draft board to play out. With the newly acquired 10th pick, the "What If" Wolves take Klay Thompson from Washington State. I don't think that I need to breakdown Klay's game for everyone, but he was an outstanding player. Probably possessed the most perfect jumpshot in NBA history. The Wolves get Klay Thompson and we don't even have to give up Kevin Love for him. Check out this blast from the Canis past about the KLove for Klay trade here.

The 2011-12 "What If" Wolves continue to struggle, but they are actually building something. KLove and K-Lay (I know that is gross) shoot lights out from the perimeter. Rubio develops chemistry with the roster while running around on two healthy knees (Thompson would be the one guarding Kobe instead of Rubio). Let's say the Wolves finish the lockout shortened season with a 30-36 record. A solid start to the rebuild.

The next few years don't go any better for the Jazz. Jimmer would Jimmer, and the "What If" Wolves end up with their 2013 first round pick at 14th overall. The Wolves also have their own pick at number nine. With the ninth overall pick the Wolves start taking some risks. CJ McCollum will be our pick here. Despite coming out of tiny Lehigh University, CJ has made an incredible career for himself. Always a solid shooter and sometimes decent set up man.

One more risk to take with the Jazz pick from the Jimmer trade. With that pick the team looks back to team history for a bit of inspiration. The Wolves once took a chance on a skinny high school kid in 1995. After reviewing dozens of grainy VHS tapes, the Wolves take a skinny kid from Greece: Giannis Antetokounmpo. I can rewrite history however I want, so yes, I take Giannis. The real Timberwolves had the chance to draft Giannis twice. Once with the ninth overall pick (Trey Burke) which they traded to the Jazz for the 14th (Shabazz) and 21st picks (Gorgui Dieng). I right the wrong and take the Greek Freak.

With CJ and Giannis added to the "What If" Wolves, the team makes the playoffs for the first time since Kevin Garnett brought the real Timberwolves to the 2004 Western Conference Finals. The roster is starting to really look like a threat. Kevin Love, Klay Thompson, Ricky Rubio, CJ McCollum, Giannis (not spelling that last name again), Peković. Starting to look crazy scary.

Because the "What If" Wolves made the playoffs, they missed out on the 2014 lottery and the chance to redraft Zach LaVine. So let's say the Wolves end up with the 24th overall pick and take center Clint Capela. Capella has had a solid NBA career providing solid defense and averages of about 11 points and 10 rebounds per game. Not a terrible late first round pick. We also have a slew of second round picks, Utah's at 35 plus MN picks at 40, 44, and 53 overall.

Usually I would have waited for the spotlight to make this pick, but the NBA loves their sponsors. So with the 35th overall pick the "What If" Wolves take "a delicious cheesy quesadilla wrapped around a beefy burrito, the new Quesarito from Taco Bell."

That's right, during the Taco Bell commercial to push their new diarrhea inducing Quesarito, the "What If" Wolves draft Nikola Jokić!

Jokić, Giannis, Klay, CJ, and Capela to run with Ricky and Love. 82-0. All of the championships. All of them!

I think I still had some second round picks or something, but who cares? I sell all of those stupid picks for horses to give to Jokić and the rights to all of the Antetokounmpo brothers. Quesaritos on me!
