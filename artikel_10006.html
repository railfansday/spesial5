Microsoft Outlook outage blamed after 15 injured in hotel pool chlorine incident

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

A safety warning went missing, chemical levels soared, and five guests ended up in hospital

Most victims of last week's Microsoft outage lost access to their email, but 15 unlucky guests at a German hotel got a dose of chlorine when a warning about dangerous levels never reached staff.

The incident, first reported by German broadcaster WDR, happened on Friday at Hotel Ling Bao, part of the Phantasialand theme park in Brühl, Germany, where a malfunctioning automatic dosing system pumped too much chlorine into the swimming pool.

The hotel's monitoring system spotted the problem and emailed staff a warning about the excessive chlorine levels. Unfortunately, Microsoft's Outlook service was caught up in a global outage, meaning the warning never arrived.

Brühl Fire Department said the problem started when a technical fault caused the pool's dosing equipment to pump in more chlorine than it should have. Chlorine gas then escaped into the swimming area, leaving guests needing medical attention.

Paramedics checked over 15 people at the hotel, and five were taken to hospital. Four suffered minor injuries, while one was moderately injured and needed further treatment. The fire department sent about 40 personnel to the scene, and crews spent roughly two hours dealing with the incident.

The incident came as Microsoft was battling a major outage that began on Thursday, November 12, and dragged well into Friday. Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, and other Microsoft 365 services were knocked offline, leaving businesses unable to access email and documents. The disruption wasn't confined to office workers unable to check their inboxes, with hospitals, police forces, and government departments reporting problems accessing the Microsoft services they use for day-to-day operations.

Redmond has since blamed the outage on a bug introduced during an earlier network capacity upgrade. It sat there until a later network change set it off, and the failures then spread across the company's infrastructure. Microsoft ruled out both cybercriminals and AI as culprits, and has issued the customary apology.

At Phantasialand, though, a broken inbox was the least of anyone's worries.

The chlorine dosing equipment went haywire independently of Microsoft's troubles, but the email warning staff about the problem apparently never arrived. Whether the hotel had a backup alarm, or anything capable of shutting off the chlorine supply automatically, remains unanswered.

Why a hotel would rely on Outlook to deliver warnings about dangerous chlorine levels is anyone's guess. Email can be delayed, filtered into spam, or simply disappear when Microsoft's infrastructure throws a tantrum, none of which is particularly helpful when you've got a pool with guests breathing in noxious gas. ®
