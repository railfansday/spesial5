AI boom threatens circuit board crunch to rival pandemic-era chip shortage

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Trade body warns automotive, medical, and defense orders are being crowded out, with relief unlikely before mid-2028

The electronics industry faces a printed circuit board (PCB) shortage that could rival the pandemic-era chip crunch – with AI once again accused of hogging supplies.

The Global Electronics Association says demand is arriving "at a scale the supply chain was not built for," straining everything from glass fabric and resin to finished boards.

Its report, "The PCB Squeeze: A Wake-Up Call for Global Electronics," says AI servers need increasingly complex boards. While a typical PC board has about 12 layers of tracks, AI server boards can have 40 to 60 layers or more, and each additional layer requires more resin, copper foil, and laminate.

The supply of materials is set to be constrained through at least the first half of 2027, the report says, with meaningful relief unlikely before mid-2028.

Programs to deliver PCBs for automotive, industrial, medical and even the defense industries are being crowded out as AI soaks up scarce capacity, it claims.

The association's North American figures show orders growing much faster than deliveries. PCB bookings rose 56.1 percent in the first nine months of 2026 compared with the same period last year, while PCB shipments increased 13.3 percent.

Fabrication capacity is expanding, aimed at meeting the increased demand from the AI infrastructure sector, especially in Asia, where more than 90 percent of the world's boards are made. The report says that $10 billion has gone toward new PCB plants in Southeast Asia, and at least as much has been invested in China over the past three years, but the raw materials are themselves constrained.

It warns of lengthening lead times for those raw materials. Standard FR-4 laminate now takes six to eight weeks to ship, up from two to three weeks a year ago, while advanced low-loss laminates now take 14 to 18 weeks for delivery, for example.

Tightening supplies usually lead to price increases, and the report says that suppliers raised laminate costs by 30 percent in September, and that the price tag of some types of E-glass cloth for circuit boards has risen by 300 percent.

The report also puts copper prices up 37 percent over the past year. Concerns have been raised about copper supplies before, as The Register has reported multiple times.

The association compares the risk with the 2021 chip shortage, when disrupted supplies left manufacturers unable to secure even relatively mundane components.

New capacity is coming, but it is late and comes in dribs and drabs, according to the report, while AI demand just keeps on absorbing it as soon as it arrives.

The association's advice is for buyers to plan early, and to share realistic forecasts with suppliers rather than double-booking. Board makers should qualify alternative suppliers of materials such as laminate and copper foil before shortages bite, but it warns against stockpiling beyond real need, which adds to forward-buying pressure.

But it would like governments to step in, of course, by tracking board and material lead times with industry and offering tax incentives for investment in advanced PCB and substrate manufacturing equipment.

The wish list also includes state investment in research and development plus qualification of new alternative materials. It would like to see loan guarantees and grants to protect critical programs and small fabricators, structured so as to sustain new capacity if forward buying unravels and demand corrects in 2027.

"Yes, we are seeing constraint in upstream materials and have been reporting it for the past couple of quarters," said Cori Masters, senior research analyst director for Supply Chain Operations at Gartner.

"In addition, AI datacenter infrastructure build out is moving capacity support to high-layer count PCBs which increase throughput time and decreases availability of line utilization for more traditional PCB products," she told The Register. ®
