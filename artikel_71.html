They’re Young, Frustrated and Want France to Change. They Disagree on How.

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

One is a left-wing student leader, the other a young far-right activist. Together, Soumaya Sarré and Louise Montangon reflect the polarization of France, where more protests and strikes took place on Tuesday.

Ségolène Le Stradic, a reporter in The Times’s Paris bureau, has been reporting on the French protests since they began in September.

Soumaya Sarré, 18, is a history and Arabic studies undergraduate who grew up in a working-class district on the edge of Paris. She is a voice of the youth-led protests that have spurred unrest across France since September. “I can carry our voices, the voices of young people from working-class neighborhoods,” she told me.

Louise Montangon, 22, is a philosophy undergraduate from rural France. As the student protests gained momentum, she became one of their leading critics, regularly condemning the young demonstrators on television panels and social media. “The majority of university and high school students actually want to attend their classes,” she said in an interview.

Unknown to the public until this fall, these two women from opposite ends of the political spectrum have surfaced as emblems of the combustible French moment, which intensified again on Tuesday with more protests and strikes that shut down the Eiffel Tower and reduced regional transport services.

Ms. Sarré and Ms. Montangon aren’t formal leaders of their political camps, and their sudden prominence partly reflects society’s tendency to search for avatars for leaderless movements. Their simultaneous rise, and their divergent politics, nevertheless reflect the polarization of French society, and particularly its youth — five months from a presidential election dominated by candidates from the far-left and the far-right.

Polls of people under 25 suggest that more than 40 percent support the main far-left candidate, Jean-Luc Mélenchon, while more than 20 percent back his far-right opponent, Marine Le Pen. In legislative elections in 2024, nearly half of voters from that age group voted for Mr. Mélenchon’s party, and a third voted for Ms. Le Pen’s.

Support for moderate solutions has plummeted after years of centrist governance under Emmanuel Macron, France’s term-limited president.
