Protesters call on Israel to free Palestinian student held without charge

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Mahdi Tunsi appears by videolink in front of his attorney Nasser Odeh during his hearing at the Jerusalem District Court on Tuesday

Israeli students, activists and academics are calling for the release of a Palestinian undergraduate who was taken from his university dormitory by Israeli police earlier this month and has since been held without charge.

Mahdi Tunsi, a 26-year-old sociology and criminology student at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, appeared in an Israeli court in the city on Tuesday - a hearing that was held behind closed doors.

Israeli police say he was arrested "concerning ties to an act of terrorism".

Authorities have requested what is known as "administrative detention", which allows the Israeli state to detain people indefinitely without charge on the basis of secret evidence that they cannot see.

"We definitely think this is a political arrest," said Dr Yael Berda, one of Tunsi's university professors.

Tunsi, a Palestinian resident of occupied East Jerusalem who works with well-known Jewish-Palestinian campaign groups, was arrested a month after Israel held an election that saw Arab-led parties win a record 19 seats in parliament, the Knesset, and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's right-wing coalition lose its majority.

Since then, Berda said she had seen an intensifying campaign against Palestinian rights.

Knesset Member Dr Ofer Cassif was one of the those gathered outside the court

Outside the Jerusalem District Court on Tuesday, demonstrators waved placards with a black-and-white image of Tunsi, while crying out "shame" in unison.

"There's no due process - [administrative detention is] a sugar-coated term for being kidnapped and held as a hostage," said Dr Ofer Cassif, a Member of the Knesset representing the Arab-led Joint List alliance.

"We must all stand firm against such crimes. It's unacceptable."

Since the Hamas-led attack on Israel on 7 October 2023, which triggered the Gaza war, the number of people in administrative detention has more than doubled.

In October, there were more than 3,100 in administrative detention, according to Israel Prison Service figures published by Israeli rights group HaMoked. Most of them are Palestinians from the occupied West Bank.

Israel says that its use of the policy is compliant with international law and a necessary preventative measure to combat terrorism.

Protesters held signs calling for Tunsi to be freed in Arabic and Hebrew

In a statement, Israeli police said they would "not elaborate further regarding the investigation" into Tunsi.

He appeared via video link from prison - his hair and beard shaved off. His friends also said that he was without medication as well as his glasses, despite a judge requesting them to be brought to him in detention.

The Israel Prison Service said it acted in accordance with the court's rulings.

"When the process was complete and all the necessary approvals had been received, the detainee's lawyer was told he could pass the eyeglasses to the detainee immediately, subject to security procedures," it added.

Berda asked to enter the courtroom on Tuesday but was denied entry. According to Berda, not even Tunsi has been told of the evidence against him.

The judge has said more time is needed to consider the state's request for administrative detention. A decision is expected in the coming days.

The judge has said more time is needed to consider the state's request for administrative detention

"If they have evidence, have them bring it or release him," said Berda.

"That's why we're demanding his immediate release or moving it to a criminal track where he could defend himself."

Berda said that while Tunsi could not vote in Israel's general election because he is not an Israeli citizen, he was "engaged" and very supportive of Arab parties' involvement.

"Mahdi is one of the best human beings I know," said Noga Kaplan, who studies with him at the Hebrew University.

"He has a really pure heart and is very passionate about his studies."

Protesters demand the release of Mahdi Tunsi outside the Jerusalem Central Court during his hearing.

After Tunsi's arrest, the Hebrew University issued a statement saying it was notified a few minutes before it took place.

"The university is not a party to the legal proceedings involving the student, and we do not have information that would allow us to comment on the circumstances leading to the arrest or on the ongoing legal proceedings," it said.

It added that it was concerned for Tunsi's well-being and hoped "that the matter will be resolved promptly and that his rights will be fully respected throughout the process".

Tunsi is a resident of Silwan, a neighbourhood in East Jerusalem where, in recent years, Palestinians have been evicted by Israeli authorities, who say their properties were built illegally. Israeli settlers have since moved into some of those properties.

"Political organisation stopped after October 7 because people were getting super scared about the consequences," explained Kaplan.

"Mahdi saw studying as one of the ways to speak about it in class."

"He didn't do a lot of public stuff because what's happening now is his biggest fear."
