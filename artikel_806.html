Yoro red card overturned after successful Man Utd appeal

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Man Utd defender Leny Yoro's red at Liverpool has been overturned after a successful appeal; Yoro was sent off inside the first eight minutes for denying a clear goalscoring opportunity; United informed The FA of their plans to appeal decision on Monday; Yoro available to face Brentford

Leny Yoro's red card against Liverpool has been overturned following a successful appeal from Manchester United.

Mauricio Pochettino's side lost 4-0 at Anfield in the Argentine's first game in charge, and his plans went up in smoke when Yoro was dismissed after eight minutes for bundling over Alexander Isak as the striker ran through on goal.

Yoro was set to serve a one-match suspension but it has now been removed after United successfully lodged an appeal for wrongful dismissal.

The 21-year-old will now be available to face Brentford on Saturday at Old Trafford.

Debate on whether the foul was a clear denial of a goalscoring opportunity centred on whether Isak would have kept possession of ball as he approached the penalty area.

Under the laws of the game, one of the considerations regarding a red card for denying an obvious goalscoring opportunity (DOGSO) is the likelihood of the attacking player "keeping or gaining control of the ball".

United boss Pochettino felt that not only did Isak lose control of the ball with a heavy touch, but that United should have been given a penalty for Jeremy Jacquet's challenge on Bryan Mbeumo at the opposite end just moments before.

However, former Premier League referee Dermot Gallagher backed the on-field decision in both instances.

"If you look at it from the referee's angle, is it a foul? That's the first step. Yes. Is he in possession of the ball? From that angle, yes," Gallagher said on Sky Sports News.

"I think most people would have expected a red card. It would have been a surprise during the game if a red card hadn't been given.

"I don't know if he would have got to the ball before Lammens."

United boss Pochettino said it was not a denial of a goalscoring opportunity as goalkeeper Senne Lammens was the favourite to claim it and Yoro did not intentionally make contact with Isak.

"The ball was for the goalkeeper. In that situation, if you review the VAR you can see for sure, [Yoro] never had the intention to connect with Isak," Pochettino said after the game.

"When the control was long with the striker, the ball was going to the goalkeeper."

United midfielder Kobbie Mainoo also disagreed with the on-field decision.

"The game's been taken out of our hands by a red card that I don't think can be a red card," he said.

"No chance. The ball's already with the goalkeeper."

This is not the first time this season that a DOGSO red card has created division within the football authorities.

In October, two independent panels disagreed over whether Brighton's Pascal Struijk deserved to be sent off at Sunderland, adding to the confusion of former Premier League referees and Sky Sports pundits.

Struijk was dismissed for denying an obvious goal-scoring opportunity in the second half when he was adjudged to have fouled Sunderland striker Wilson Isidor as he tried to latch on to a pass that ran through to goalkeeper Bart Verbruggen.

Neither referee Thomas Bramall nor his assistant gave a foul, but VAR Neil Davies intervened and recommended an on-field review. Bramall watched several replays before changing his decision and dismissing Struijk.

However, Brighton appealed and an independent regulatory commission, which is convened through the FA to consider disciplinary matters, overturned the red card and subsequent one-match ban.

But disagreements over the decision continued after the Key Match Incidents (KMI) panel voted 3-2 that the VAR intervention was correct.

The KMI panel ruled that Struijk committed a "cynical foul clearly impacting the attacker's ability to gain possession of the ball which should have been penalised by the referee".

So as we have seen once again in Yoro's overturned red card, two out of the three panels - Pro Ref and the Football Association - have disagreed with each other.

We wait with bated breath to see what the KMI does when its findings are revealed in the coming weeks. Which side will it pick? And will it add to the confusion once again?
