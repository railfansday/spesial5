Why Black Friday Is the Perfect Time to Start a Gym Membership

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Most people are probably waiting until Jan. 1 to start getting in shape, whether they want to start fresh or absolve themselves of their perceived holiday indulgences. Every year, people start the new year by joining a gym crowded with others newly resolved to get in shape, paying full price for the privilege of waiting in a long line to use the squat rack. This year, try something different: Start your gym membership during Black Friday.

The week of Black Friday can deliver you a good discount on a gym membership, an empty gym, and a built-in two-month runway to turn a generic resolution into a real habit. While you're comparing deals on new tech, headphones, or smart home devices, here’s why you should also take a few minutes to scout out the gyms near you.

This week is as good a time to save of a gym membership as a new air fryer. Here are just a few examples of the offers out there right now:

Anytime Fitness is running a Black Friday offer for new members who sign up between Nov. 20 and Nov. 30, 2026, with monthly fees waived for the rest of the year in the promotion's terms (the standard access fee still applies, and terms vary by country and club, so confirm with your local location).

Orangetheory and F45 have various free-month and prize-entry promotions for new Black Friday sign-ups, and similar studios for Pilates, yoga, and strength training have discounted multi-month and annual packages.

Independent gyms, university rec centers, and local studios often run their own versions, from a free month tacked onto a longer commitment to waived joining fees.

Deals vary a lot by location, so call or stop by the clubs near you and ask what's available. Then remember to always read the fine print before you sign:

Look at the price after the promotion ends, not just the first month's rate.

Ask about fees, including joining, annual, and access fees.

Try before you buy. Many gyms offer a free trial pass. It might make sense to first visit at the time of day you'd actually go.

Be honest about distance. The best gym is the one you'll actually walk into, so pick one that's close to home or work.

Gyms also run sales around New Year's, so a good Black Friday offer isn't the only deal you'll see. Even still, the savings aren't the only reason to go now.

December is reliably the quietest stretch of the gym year: People are busy with holiday travel and work deadlines, and many tell themselves they'll "start fresh in January." That means open benches, free treadmills, and staff who have time to show you how a machine works.

That's a gift for any gym-goer, but especially if you're new. Learning your way around a gym is much less intimidating when it's empty. You can figure out the equipment, make your mistakes in peace, and build a routine before the January crowds arrive. Plus, this time of year is a perfect window to set and achieve a winnable goal.

From Black Friday to New Year's Day is about five weeks. By the time the January rush hits its peak and settles into its usual patterns, you could be eight weeks into a new routine. And as my colleague Beth Skwarecki explains, you really can get a lot stronger in 24 workouts. Beginners tend to see the fastest gains of their lifting lives in the first couple of months.

Prefer running? I know I do, and luckily, a couch-to-5K plan works the same way. Most programs run about nine weeks and build you up gradually from short run-walk intervals. Start the week after Black Friday and you'll be running for a solid stretch by early January, and you'll be well on your way to 30 minutes at a stretch by the end of the month. By the time your New Year's resolution friends are just starting, you'll already be a few runs in.

This is also the season when many workplaces and health plans wrap up their open enrollment periods, which makes it a smart time to check what wellness perks you already have. Before you pay full price for anything, look into:

Workplace wellness programs, which sometimes offer gym reimbursements, discounted memberships or fitness stipends.

Health insurance perks, since some plans include gym memberships or discounts.

HSA and FSA rules, where general gym memberships usually don't qualify, but some medically recommended programs may. Also check any year-end deadlines for money you'd otherwise lose.

Corporate and employer discounts, which many large gyms offer on top of regular promotions.

Stack a workplace benefit on top of a holiday sale, and your cost per workout can drop considerably—well before New Year's resolution time arrives.
