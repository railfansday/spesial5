Falcon 9 flies national security doubleheader

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

On Wednesday, Nov. 18, China is expected to launch a Long March 2D from the Taiyuan Satellite Launch Center in Shanxi province at 5:50 p.m. EST, according to NextSpaceflight. The payload has not been announced.

Later that night on Wednesday, Nov. 18, SpaceX will launch the classified USSF-317 mission for the U.S. Space Force from Space Launch Complex 4E (SLC-4E) at Vandenberg Space Force Base in California at 10:00 p.m. EST. Neither SpaceX nor the Space Force has said what the rocket carries. The Falcon 9 booster will land on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You in the Pacific Ocean.

On Friday, Nov. 20, a Long March 8A is slated to lift off from the Wenchang Space Launch Site on Hainan Island at 6:00 a.m. EST, per NextSpaceflight. The payload has not been disclosed, but is suspected to be a batch of SatNet low-Earth-orbit (LEO) satellites for Guowang — a state-backed broadband megaconstellation meant to rival Starlink.

Closing out the week on Saturday, Nov. 21, the National Reconnaissance Office (NRO) will launch NROL-136 on a Falcon 9 from SLC-4E at 7:30 a.m. EST. Rather than heading for a droneship, the booster will fly back to Landing Zone 4.

Last week opened on Wednesday, Nov. 11, at 6:41 a.m. EST, when a Long March 2D lifted off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center carrying CAS Pathfinder-1. The Chinese Academy of Sciences built the satellite to test key technologies for its planned Origins space science program, CGTN reported. Later that night, at 11:36 p.m. EST, SpaceX launched the classified USSF-627 mission from Vandenberg after two postponements. The booster landed on Of Course I Still Love You.

On Friday, Nov. 13, at 5:47 p.m. EST, SpaceX launched Starship Flight 15 from Starbase, Texas, aiming to catch both stages with its launch tower for the first time. SpaceX was unable to catch the Super Heavy booster after its onboard guidance system made the call to abort the attempt. Instead it exploded over the Gulf of Mexico just before reaching the water. The ship, on the other hand, reached orbit, the second Starship ever to do so, and released 26 Starlink V3 satellites.

Then on Saturday afternoon, after 14 trips around Earth, the rocket’s upper stage slowed over Starbase, dropped between the tower’s mechanical “chopsticks,” and was plucked out of the air. The catch was a first for SpaceX and spaceflight in general — no other spacecraft has ever entered orbit and then been caught at its launch site.

Russia launched a mission from the Vostochny Cosmodrome at 8:16 p.m. EST on Friday, Nov. 13. The Soyuz-2.1b carried a pair of high-resolution imaging satellites and a dozen smaller payloads.

Rocket Lab marked a milestone just after midnight on Saturday, Nov. 14. At 12:30 a.m. EST, its Electron rocket flew for the 100th time on The Mountain God Rises, lifting off from Launch Complex 1 on New Zealand’s Māhia Peninsula. The rocket placed a synthetic aperture radar satellite into orbit for the Japanese company iQPS. Synthetic aperture radar bounces microwaves off the ground to build images, letting it see through clouds and darkness.

The week ended in a loss. On Sunday, Nov. 15, at 12:40 p.m. EST, United Launch Alliance’s Vulcan rocket lifted off from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station carrying 40 satellites for Amazon Leo, the company’s broadband constellation. It was Vulcan’s return to flight after the USSF-87 mission in February — which experienced a minor booster anomaly despite successfully delivering its payload — and the first of several dozen Vulcan launches Amazon has booked. About 10 minutes into the flight, ULA reported an anomaly, and the satellites never reached orbit.

Based on data received by our Launch Director, we confirm that the Vulcan vehicle launched from Space Launch Complex 41 suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight and failed to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit. Every flight generates data, and every flight teaches us…

On Monday, Nov. 23, Arianespace is targeting 5:12 a.m. EST to launch an Ariane 64 from the Guiana Space Centre in French Guiana with 36 more Amazon Leo satellites.

Later on Monday, Nov. 23, SpaceX is slated to launch the classified USSF-266 mission for the Space Force from Vandenberg at 11:04 a.m. EST.

On Tuesday, Nov. 24, China is slated to launch a Long March 12 from the commercial launch site at Wenchang on Hainan Island at 2:00 a.m. EST, according to Next Spaceflight. The payload has not been confirmed, but is likely another batch of SatNet LEO satellites for Guowang.

On Thursday, Nov. 26, a launch window opens for the third flight of Isar Aerospace’s Spectrum rocket from Andøya Spaceport in Norway, according to European Spaceflight. Its payloads include AT-Astra, an Austrian technology-demonstration satellite built by Vienna-based R-Space. Spectrum reached orbit for the first time on its second flight in September.

On Sunday, Nov. 29, NASA and Northrop Grumman are targeting 10:47 a.m. EST to launch the NG-25 Cygnus XL cargo spacecraft on a Falcon 9 from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station. Named the S.S. James A. Lovell after the beloved Apollo 13 commander, the spacecraft will haul several tons of supplies to the space station.
