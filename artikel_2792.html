Jaguars stunned by Giants amid officiating controversy, questionable late-game decision from head coach Liam Coen

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Jacksonville Jaguars entered Sunday at 7-2 having emerged as a Super Bowl favorite out of the AFC.

The New York Giants doused them with a splash of cold water.

Plagued by red-zone woes, officiating controversy and a questionable late-game decision by Jacksonville head coach Liam Coen, the Jaguars fell 23-18 to the Giants.

Jacksonville had a shot to tie the game with fourth-and-goal at the New York 8-yard line and 3:40 remaining on the clock. New York led 23-15, and the Jaguars were down to one timeout.

But instead of going for a potential game-tying touchdown and 2-point conversion, Coen opted to kick a field goal that cut the deficit to five points, apparently betting on the Jaguars' defense to get them the ball back.

Trevor Lawrence and the Jaguars suffered a stunning loss to the Giants on Sunday.

They did not. Cam Skattebo ran for a first down before the two-minute warning with the Jaguars having exhausted their final timeout. The Giants were able to kneel out the remaining two minutes of game clock in victory formation.

It added up to a jarring loss for the now 7-3 Jaguars on the heels of an aggressive trade deadline in which they made several big swings to bolster their hopes of a Super Bowl run. The Giants, meanwhile, improved to 6-3-1 to maintain their lead in the NFC East over the 5-6 Dallas Cowboys.

Coen was asked about his decision to kick the late field goal in his postgame news conference. He cited analytics charts and described going for a touchdown as a "hope play."

"We talked about it a few times," Coen said. "Our defense, they hadn't done anything up until that point. Everything about the charts and everything talked about it said kick the field goal, get within five points.

"Go get a stop, and you feel pretty good about where you're at. Just executing that down-and-distance on a fourth down is kinda challenging. Especially, they're zero blitzing you every play, the ball's gonna come out. It's like a hope play."

Coen's comments Sunday stood in contrast to a previous discussion with Barstool Sports about a strikingly similar, but not identical hypothetical scenario.

In that discussion, Coen was asked about whether to kick a field goal or go for it when down by eight points at the 8-yard line with one key caveat: "When you're up against the greatest quarterback of all time."

"You've gotta go for it," Coen said. … "It's weird down there — and you're in an opportunity to score a touchdown, you're that close. We all know how hard it is to score in the red zone in this league. … I think you've gotta be aggressive there."

Giants quarterback J.J. McCarthy is certainly not "the greatest quarterback of all time." It's not clear if Coen would have made a different decision if facing a different caliber of quarterback.

The Giants were in control virtually from the start. They took a 10-3 advantage into the second quarter and a 20-6 lead into halftime. While McCarthy kept New York's offense moving and scoring, the Jaguars repeatedly broke down in the red zone.

Jacksonville's first three trips to the red zone produced 6 points courtesy of two field goals and a turnover on downs in the third quarter when a fourth-and-1 pass from Trevor Lawrence fell incomplete.

Jacksonville's fourth red-zone trip produced controversy on a third-down effort to cut their deficit to one possession.

They appeared to do just that when Parker Washington made a leaping catch in the back of the end zone and got both feet in bounds. But officials ruled that he didn't maintain control of the ball as he hit the ground.

Coen used Jacksonville's last remaining challenge to challenge the call on the field. The ball moved in Washington's hands after Washington hit the ground, but he never appeared to lose control of it.

But officials didn't see enough to overturn the call on the field, and the ruling of an incomplete pass stood. The result was a fourth down in which the Jaguars settled for a field goal to cut their deficit to 20-9.

Jacksonville finally cracked the end zone with a touchdown pass from Lawrence to Brian Thomas Jr. with 6:39 remaining. A failed 2-point conversion left the Jaguars facing a 23-15 hole.

Then came the decision that will prompt plenty of questions for Coen.

In the end, the Jaguars woke up on offense and limited the Giants on defense as they outgained New York, 354 yards to 238. Lawrence completed 25 of 47 passes for 301 yards with 1 touchdown and 2 interceptions.

McCarthy completed 18 of 38 passes for 173 yards with 1 touchdown. But he didn't turn the ball over as the Giants won the takeaway battle, 2 to 1.

The result will leave the Jaguars with plenty to ponder ahead of a Week 12 home game against the Titans.

The Giants do indeed have the ability to run the clock out.

The discussion for this game will be Jacksonville focused. First it will be whether or not Parker Washington deserved a touchdown on his reception in the end zone that was ruled incomplete.

The second will be Liam Coen's decision to be on the 8-yard line facing fourth down and opting to kick a meaningless field goal rather than trying to tie the game.
