Columbia Falls title hopes end against East Helena

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

COLUMBIA FALLS — The last step proved to be the toughest yet for the Wildcats.

East Helena quarterback Bearek Shuman threw three touchdown passes and Leo Longcake rushed for 194 yards and two scores Friday night, sending the Vigilantes to their first Class A football championship with a 35-9 win over Columbia Falls.

The Wildcats finished 9-2, one win short of its first title since 2017. Coach Kelly Houle wanted to talk about the seniors who got them this far.

"They bring joy to my heart every day," Houle said. "I'm just really proud of them."

Columbia Falls erased a 16-point halftime deficit to defeat East Helena 38-35 in September. This time, the Vigilantes took a 21-3 lead into halftime and gave the Wildcats few chances to get back in it.

Longcake carried the ball 36 times, while Shuman completed 12 of 18 passes for 189 yards and added 83 on the ground himself. East Helena outgained Columbia falls 458-292 and intercepted three passes.

"I think we won the line of scrimmage," said East Helena coach J.D. Ferris, who credited his linemen for the big rushing numbers.

"They carried us all year," he said. "We leaned on them all year."

Trip Ross led the Wildcats with 70 yards on 13 carries. Banyan Johnston ran for 63 yards and completed 8 of 26 passes for 155 yards. Jory Hill caught three passes for 51 yards, and Hayden Strandberg hauled in two receptions for 33.

The Wildcats forced a punt on East Helena's opening possession, then moved out from their own 3-yard line before turning the ball over on downs at midfield. Longcake punched in a 1-yard touchdown on the ensuing possession, giving the Vigilantes a 7-0 lead with 5:53 left in the first quarter.

A long Johnston run helped Columbia Falls reach the red zone early in the second quarter, but the Wildcats settled for Ross' 20-yard field goal to trail 7-3 with 8:27 left in the half.

That was as close as the Wildcats would get.

Shuman found Parker Taylor for a 25-yard touchdown with 3:42 remaining. Conor Carpenter intercepted Johnston on the Wildcats' next offensive play, and the Vigilantes overcame holding penalties to score again.

Aiden Dalton caught a high lofted pass for a 30-yard touchdown with just 11 seconds before the break, making it 21-3.

This was familiar for both teams, but Shuman remembered the first meeting and how it slipped away.

"We shot ourselves in the foot and lost that game," he said. "Most importantly, you have to be able to play all four quarters in a football game. You have to play the whole game."

To start the second half Columbia Falls picked up a fourth-and-short on a Ross run up the middle, but the drive ended with a punt after back-to-back incompletions.

Longcake and Shuman kept the Vigilantes moving. After a high snap backed up East Helena, a 22-yard catch by Carpenter helped them recover and the clock started to tick from there. Shuman capped the drive off with a 15-yard touchdown pass to Avery Hager with 11:21 remaining in the game, stretching the lead 28-3.

"We just couldn't get out stuff going tonight." Houle said. "That happens."

Johnston scored the Wildcats' lone touchdown on a 2-yard run in the fourth quarter. The extra point was blocked and Longcake finished the scoring with a 22-yard dagger with just over three minutes left.

East Helena, which opened its doors in 2019, finished the season 10-1. Ferris noted the Vigilantes' 23 seniors had been freshmen on a team that won three games. He claimed the group had been talking about winning a championship together since arriving.

"I can't even put it into words," Shuman said. "It doesn't feel real."

Houle congratulated Ferris and the Vigilantes, then reflected on what his own seniors would leave behind. He credited them with building a player-led program and setting an example for the younger Wildcats to step up next.

"I'm just going to remember the brotherhood that they brought every day," Houle said. "It was a heck of a season."
