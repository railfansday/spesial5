Read Jim Cramer’s full interview with Nvidia’s Colette Kress: Demand, buybacks and China

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Nvidia delivered another beat-and-raise quarter on Tuesday in the chipmaker's first report since Jensen Huang abruptly resigned as CEO last week amid a widening affair scandal. Revenue more than doubled from a year earlier, exceeding estimates by nearly $7 billion, while earnings topped expectations by 20 cents per share. An excellent quarter by almost any measure.

But investors remain skeptical about Nvidia's future without Huang, who famously founded the company at a San Jose Denny's in 1993. Shares fell 2.5% Wednesday, despite opening higher on the session. Will the company lose its smartest executives and engineers? Would Nvidia remain banker to the increasingly shaky neoclouds? Can it stay ahead of the competition?

On Tuesday night, Colette Kress sat down with CNBC's Jim Cramer shortly after delivering her first earnings call as interim CEO to answer these pressing questions and more.

This transcript has been edited for clarity.

Jim Cramer: After the bell, Nvidia reported earnings, and what a quarter it was. The stock jumped in after hours in response. This is the first time we are hearing from the company since its founder, Jensen Huang, stepped down as CEO last week. So let's check in with Colette Kress, Nvidia's longtime chief financial officer and now its interim CEO. Ms. Kress, welcome to "Mad Money."

Colette Kress: Thank you for having me, Jim. It's really great to see you.

Cramer: Thank you, Colette. The most important thing I heard tonight was that you increased your revenue growth forecast for next year. Previously, you said you would grow 70%. Now you're saying 75%, off of a higher base. How are you able to do that?

Kress: Thanks. Very, very important to understand how much our customers are interested in our compute infrastructure and how much they need the agentic solutions out there. So the most important thing that we have been seeing is just, again, a continuous growth in terms of the demand that's out there. But we also have been working in terms of our supply. So an increase in supply, working with all of our different suppliers, whether that be focusing in terms of what we need in terms of chips, what we need in terms of advanced packaging or memory, all of that is also coming together. We felt very confident, therefore, that we could establish that we will grow 75% year over year for fiscal year '28.

Cramer: Now, a lot of people have been saying that there was one man at this company. I've talked with you. There are another 49,000. How is the team holding up? Are there people who are saying, "You know what, this is not the Nvidia I know, I don't want to be there?" Is there a brain drain, as some people have told me?

Kress: Yeah, really great question. And remember, the company has been built on the perspective that employees see Nvidia as their life's work. And many of them have been here for decades, working with us from both a technology and a business-and-operations perspective. And now where we're standing is focusing on the business, focusing on getting all of that work done. And I had been working already with leadership. Leadership is supportive of everything we're doing and is standing up to help as we pull all employees and leaders together to move forward. But we have high confidence that our employees are definitely still working, still working with us, and enjoy really the work here at Nvidia.

Cramer: I have talked to you many times about the idea that the cheapest stock in the world is yours, and that's why you don't necessarily have to help other people buy your chips when you can just go buy your own stock. Where are you on buybacks versus lending aggressive amounts of money to, say, neoclouds? Because I think that the buybacks are a lot better for shareholders.

Kress: We have now been looking at the capability of making significant stock repurchases for our company. That is enabled by our significant growth and the cash we will have. During the quarter, we discussed increasing the amount of additional stock repurchases to more than $150 billion. That adds to a total amount at this point of $277 billion that we're going to be able to provide. We are completely committed to that. We do believe that that's something we're going to be able to complete all the way through, just to the end of 2027. So we are in line and continuing our stock repurchases for sure.

Cramer: OK. Thank you. That's important. And it's more aggressive than I thought. And I'm glad to hear that. Now, why are there many different reports about your company, and even about your accounting? I know you as the person who is in charge of the numbers. For instance, I read two different analysts who say you may be on the hook for about $530 billion in guarantees, which is worrisome because it could have a huge impact on your cash flow.  I heard a new disclosure tonight that made me feel like the on-the-hook number is not true.

Kress: Right now, our guarantees have been thoughtfully completed to date. And our guarantees are approximately about $118 billion, which includes one of the most important ones: a land, power, and shell backstop that we have with Portsmouth here in the United States, the best site for what we see, the future of multiple, multiple buildings working together to do that, about 4.5 gigawatts to do so. That is solely for Nvidia's infrastructure. It's working very well and is getting ready for all the builds it needs to do. And that is the most significant part of our guarantees. We will continue to do the best due diligence and thought regarding any additional guarantees that need to be done. That means we're going to be working with companies to make sure we understand where they are in terms of their land, power, shell builds, how they are doing in terms of getting ready for the compute, getting the capital that they need, and making sure that they have the end customer, the off-take that we'll need to complete that. So it's going to be a top priority. But we are all working together to make sure that we can support the most important ones that we think are running very well, and that's going to be a top priority for us.

Cramer: Speaking of priorities, from a lot of reporting, again, because I want to try to get all this stuff out. You were kind enough to come on and speak about anything unrehearsed. Based on much of the reporting over the last two weeks, it seems that the aggressive investments in the ecosystem, including some companies that are less than investment-grade, were a priority for your predecessor and his family members. I want to know if you were pushing back on those? And should we see less and expect less support for the ecosystem and more for the shareholders?

Kress: Our investments are an important part of the work we can do, focusing on a set of organizations that may be AI-native, maybe new types of companies starting, as well as helping those that want to build new types of models in the market. These are some of the areas that we focused on. But, yes, we're very disciplined in terms of how we think that through. There's a lot of discussion internally with a lot of the teams on what we think are the best ones that we want to do. And we're going to continue down that path to make sure we choose the right investments for us to work through going forward. But most importantly, these are companies in areas where we can see additional growth in AI. And I think those are important for us. But, again, stepping back to our business and how important our capital is going to be for procuring supply and getting ready for all of our employees as well, and all the work that we need to do from the engineering, that's going to be our top priority in terms of our use of capital to do so. So that's what I think is the most important.

Cramer: You are in a very competitive situation. For instance, AMD has a product that's a GPU that is much touted, and they think it can be moved in quicker. I know that some of the hyperscalers are very good at developing their own chips, which makes some people say, "Wait a second. Is what's really happening that Nvidia is supporting some companies that may not be, let's say, investment-grade or the kind of customer you would want — neoclouds — because it's losing business from the traditional hyperscalers and their shares are going down. Is that true?

Kress: Interesting statement, but no, it is not true. If we want to think about where we are with our hyperscalers, they are right now feverishly looking for more focus on the compute we have available. I have seen already with many of these contacting me and saying, " Great to see everything intact. Great to see that there's more supply. I need more demand." So they are all with us. And remember, there's a reason why. That is an amazing type of architecture that has been built through so much work with the different types of engineers. It is the most durable, fungible, and performant type of infrastructure out there. The idea that anything is changing among our customer types is just not true. They are all still with us. And we have a very deep breadth of different customers. And I think that's important to understand. These will be needed for all the different organizations overseas as well. They're going to want to say, "How do I have someone building a different type of data center overseas that they would want to see as well?" So I do believe it is appropriate to have different types of organizations that will be using this type of compute. But keep in mind, the stock, the amount of different types of customers, is broad. And we are going to continue to focus on that.

Cramer: This level of demand makes me ask you a particular question, which is that, if you were no longer allowed or wanted to ship into Southeast Asia for fear that it might end up in China, the flow back would not overwhelm pricing and drive it down, but instead would just help sate some of demand, but not all the demand? You do not need Southeast Asia to make or advance your numbers?

Kress: Well, what we are always working on is: Where do we feel we can provide the demand to our customers? We are always very, very cautious in terms of where we will be shipping to and making sure, yes, the data center is ready, and whether or not we also know that off-take and we know that customer piece of it. That is part of our process, and we will continue there. Let's just remember what we are right now in terms of our shipments to China. Our shipments to China are approximately about 10%. But that is our gaming business. That is our automotive business. That is our workstation business that is going there. As you also saw us announce, we would only have the licensed H200s [Nvidia's data center GPUs], and those went to China within this last quarter. And we are no longer shipping H200 at all, let alone not shipping it to China. So we are very careful and will continue to focus on that.

Cramer: I do want to ask you something that I think was a question that was handled by the board, but I didn't necessarily get an answer that I felt satisfactory. I understand, and have heard, that the chairman of the board says the board does not intend to ask Jensen Huang to return as CEO. But I have to say that perhaps the board would consider, after a thorough investigation — which I do not believe they did, frankly — that Jensen's value to our nation's technology, as expressed by our president today, could lead him to do something else for you. Perhaps a chief technologist or an ambassador for Nvidia's dominance who would not run afoul of either the SEC or anything done by Commerce or the Secretary of the Treasury.

Kress: Jensen resigned from his position with Nvidia, and the board accepted his resignation. The board has determined that they will not reinstate Jensen. And that is our statement and what the board has decided to do. We want to think about growing our business right now. We want to grow in everything we do with our employees, our customers, and our shareholders, and that is where we will focus moving forward.

Cramer: Do you think that it's possible that the two-way compute market project that Jensen started, that was going to be done with Goldman, with BlackRock, with Apollo, will continue? That's $500 billion in financing to make it happen the way we think it should.

Kress: Those financing funds are very important to us. We announced that they will continue to work with us, and their continued assistance with many of these companies that may be just shy of investment-grade and want to explore financing options; we're excited that they are part of this work. They are part of helping us create an asset class of our systems as well. No other company has that durability and fungibility outside of Nvidia. So they are important partners. We're going to continue working to help many of the different companies get the financing that they may want to do and the efficiency of that financing.

Cramer: You cleared up many questions for us tonight, and I really appreciate it. I know there are a lot of people who, I think, say a lot of things about your company that aren't true. I want to thank you, Colette Kress, who's been true north for me for many, many years, for as long as we've known each other. I want to say — I would say congratulations on the interim CEO. I'm glad you were on our show tonight, and I hope those who want to understand what kind of company this really is go back over what she said. It's quite different from what I hear every day on Wall Street. Colette Kress is the former CFO, and interim CEO, and it was great to have you on the show. Thank you, Colette.
