The Optimist: What the Turducken was that, refs?

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Despite the closeness of yesterday's 20-17 victory over the New Orleans Saints, the Panthers established themselves as the team to beat in the NFC South. A dubious honor, to be sure, but it's where we are now. The Tampa Bay Buccaneers are the only thing that resembles a threat in this division and I'm not confident that what we saw against them in Week 7 will happen again in Week 12. I guess we'll see.

The Panthers overcame aggressively one-sided officiated, middling quarterback play from Bryce Young, baffling editorial and marketing decisions by Fox Sports, and inconsistent kicking from Ryan Fitzgerald to do something they couldn't manage last season: they won a game to take control of the division. They also snapped a three game losing streak, extended their streak of not sending anybody to injured reserve to two weeks, and are set to keep bringing key players back from injuries over the next several weeks.

That said, it's not all sunshine and beignets for the Panthers after conquering the Saints in New Orleans. We can't pretend like that wasn't a rough game even outside of the liberal interpretations of several rules that the officials were using. There are four things that need to be addressed coming out of Week 10 that need to be addressed more than anything else if the Panthers want to win any more games this season.

Play-calling and execution in the red zone and in short yardage situations have been a challenge all season. The Saints opening drive ended with an interception that, after one offensive play, became first and ten at the New Orleans 11 yard line. The Panthers walked away with zero points. They traded three punts with the Saints on subsequent drives before missing a field goal.

The Panthers were managed to convert a first down or touchdown on four of eleven attempts with four or fewer yards to gain. On those attempts, they called seven running and four passing plays. That's a better balance than I thought it was going to be after the game, but the truth remains simple: the Panthers need to find a way to consistently convert in these situations or they are going to have a disappointing end to an already middling season.

You're going to hear the word "discipline" this week as a critique of Dave Canales and his coaching staff about as often as you saw a flag thrown on the field yesterday. There were a couple of false starts and neutral zone infractions that were, indeed, characterized by a lack of discipline. However I would wager that the bulk of the 17 flags thrown for 152 penalty yards (only seventy fewer yards than the Saints total offensive output) were flags that most fans would describe as "bullshit."

When you lose and you point at one sided officiating, it's called being a sore loser. I guess the Carolina Panthers are in danger of making me a sore winner. Let's take a moment to consider the penalties that keyed the Saints three scoring drives.

1st & 10 at New Orleans 48, 8:46 3rd quarter, Panthers 7 Saints 0

Quarterback Tyler Shough found a wide open Jordan Tyson for an 18 yard pass. So far so clean. Tyson fell to the ground untouched while making the catch. Panthers linebacker Devin Lloyd fell onto and over Tyson in an effort to make sure he was downed by contact. This was flagged as unnecessary roughness.

The NFL rule book, under Rule 12. Section 2. Article 8 states that unnecessary roughness can be defined as the following:

running, diving into, or throwing the body against or on any player on the ground either before or after the ball is dead;

It's possible that Lloyd's momentum qualified under the letter of this law. It is also possible, to revive a quote from last week, that this could have been described as a "bang-bang play." Unfortunately, that term is not defined in the rule book.

The fifteen yards from that penalty, added to the reception, put the Saints into the red zone for the first time in the game. Unlike during the rest of the game, the Saints did proceed to score from there under their own power.

1st & 5 at Carolina 42, 6:18 4th quarter, Panthers 10 Saints 7

Jaelan Phillips, unheld, pursued Shough and brought him down for a 17-yard sack. This should have brought up second & 22 from the Saints' 39 yard line. Well out of field goal and certainly an uphill climb to get another first down.

Instead, a selective interpretation of Rule 12. Section 2. Article 16 was enforced, calling Phillips for a horse-collar tackle and giving the Saints 15 yards instead of them losing 17. The text of the rule is below with one part highlighted for emphasis.

No player shall grab the inside collar of the back or the side of the shoulder pads or jersey, or grab the jersey at the name plate or above, and pull the runner toward the ground. This does not apply to a runner who is in the pocket area or in the area defined by close-line play. If his knees are buckled by the action, it is a foul, even if the runner is not pulled completely to the ground.

Your next reasonable question is "how does the NFL define the pocket area?". Fortunately, that is easily answered by Rule 3. Section 25. "Pocket Area":

The pocket area is the area between the outside edges of the normal tackle positions on each side of the center extending backward to the offensive team's end line. A player who is in possession of the ball is out of the pocket area, and the pocket area no longer exists, if any part of his body or the ball is outside of the pocket area.

Between the inside hip of the right tackle and the center, 17 yards behind where the ball was snapped.

Is 17 yards behind the line of scrimmage outside of the pocket area?

No, the pocket area extends back to the offensive team's end line (the back of the end zone, in NFL parlance).

Were Shough's knees buckled by the action?

No, he fell to his side. He did not fall directly backwards in the way this rule was meant to penalize and prevent.

After this penalty, the Saints ran a total of four plays for an average of 1.2 yards per play. Unfortunately, a poorly timed neutral zone infraction by Trevon Moehrig (five-yard penalty) and a ticky-tack pass interference penalty on Corey Thornton (17-yard penalty, but I couldn't find a replay last night) added insult and further injury to the pile. Penalties, dubious penalties, accounted for 42 of the Saints 70 yards on this touchdown drive.

2nd & 7 at the New Orleans 37, 1:10 4th quarter, Panthers 17 Saints 14

Tyler Dodson tackled Jordyn Tyson high. His hand was clearly around the upper chest to neck area of Tyson and replay showed that not even a single finger contacted or snared Tyson's face mask. Nonetheless, Dodson was flagged for grabbing Tyson's facemask. We return to Rule 12. Section 2, this time Article 15 holds our answers:

If a player grasps an opponent's facemask or helmet opening, he must immediately release it. He shall not grasp and control, twist, turn, push, or pull the facemask or helmet opening of an opponent in any direction.

The only part of the rule that Dodson failed to comply with is that he did not immediately release Tyson's facemask. That is because he never grabbed it in the first place. He did not grasp and control, twist, turn, push, or pull the facemask or helmet opening of Tyson. So it must be Dodson's failure to release that which he did not ever grasp that gifted the Saints 15 yards on their tying field goal drive. Keep in mind that the Saints only advanced the ball six yards after this penalty before kicking their 55-yard field goal.

Yes, there are legitimate things to clean up, but they are not the bulk of yesterday's problem. If we want to talk about discipline, let's start with all 17 of the Saints points coming on drives that were extended or prosecuted in bulk by penalties that were at best questionable and often outright in contradiction to the rules. And that's just on defense. It doesn't account for the offensive pass interference call on Darren Waller or the holding call on Luke Fortner that couldn't have stood up to review. You can't cry discipline when the Panthers are being held to a separate and higher standard than their opponents.

You can, however, get excited for what Todd Wash is doing with his defense. These guys are playing short handed, being dealt short hands, and still came up with stops all day yesterday. Somehow, the defense is not the problem with the Carolina Panthers right now.

Questionable officiating and play calling doesn't excuse Bryce Young's performance yesterday. It was about as close to Year 1-3 Bryce as we have seen this season. Indecisive and inaccurate passing kept a couple of touchdowns off the board and led to his receivers hauling in only four of 12 targets on the day. I excused that kind of performance against the Tampa Bay Buccaneers in Week 7 and the Green Bay Packers in Week 8 because Young barely had time to catch the snap before he was under pressure.

The offensive line was far from perfect (looking at you, Walker), but they held up far better than expected against a mildly fearsome New Orleans front seven. They gave Young the kind of time that he feasted with in the opening quarter of the season and we all came home hungry for more yards through the air.

Good quarterbacks have bad games. Unfortunately for Young, it is starting to be too long since he has had a good game.

Hopefully a week off for Chuba Hubbard, to start. That man single-handedly made everything for the Panthers out of the many opportunities their defense afforded them. Give the man a break, a new contract, and just about anything else he asks for tomorrow.

Everybody should also be watching the Baltimore Raven take on the Los Angeles Chargers tomorrow night on Monday Night Football. The Ravens traded for Jacoby Brissett last week because both of their top two quarterbacks are currently on injured reserve and may be done for the season. Brissett is a 10-year veteran who is parachuting into a brand new situation in the middle of the season. Who knows how he'll gel with his new receivers, but we can get an inkling of what kind of challenge he'll pose when the Ravens visit the Panthers in just six days.

A questionable offense and an already struggling defense are just the kind of challenge that the Panthers feel up to right now. We'll see if those descriptions of the Ravens hold true tomorrow night.
