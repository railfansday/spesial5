The Volvo EX60 Draws on Forest Walks and Nordic Seas

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

“Scandinavian design never shouts, but when you come closer, you are rewarded,” says Sara Erichsen Susnjar, senior color and material design manager at Volvo Cars. Detail by detail, the automaker’s new all-electric EX60 bears this out. Its seats carry a pattern drawn from two seas converging off the coast of Skagen, Denmark; its base carpet borrows its hue from foraged finds from the woods outside the company’s studio in Sweden; and an interior lighting option fills the cabin with the silhouettes of leaves.

Erichsen Susnjar, who has spent 20 years at Volvo Cars, oversaw color and materials for both the EX60 and the EX60 Cross Country. Under her direction, the team looked to the natural world for the car’s colors, patterns, and textures, often starting with what turned up on a walk.

Gathering began close to home, on outings that were already part of the team’s routine. “We are very fortunate to have the forest just outside our design studio, so it naturally becomes part of our everyday life, whether we go there for lunch, a coffee break, or simply to clear our minds,” Erichsen Susnjar says.

Over time, the team began bringing small pieces of the landscape back inside: mushrooms, twigs, stones, moss, and herbs. Each was studied for its pattern, color, and texture, then cast or digitized, and the findings became prompts for new ideas. Traces of the work appear in subtle patterns on the seatbacks and door panels, as well as in the shade of the base carpet. As Erichsen Susnjar puts it, “In that sense, a little piece of the forest has found its way into the car.”

Inside the cabin, material options include tailored wool-blend upholstery and ash wood decor, which sit alongside a 15-inch curved OLED display with Google built-in. “I think bringing natural materials into such a technical and precise environment creates a beautiful contrast,” Erichsen Susnjar explains. “They bring warmth, character and, in a way, a soul to the car. Because natural materials have their own variations and imperfections, they can never be exactly replicated, which means every interior becomes slightly unique.”

Erichsen Susnjar places the cabin’s finer details, including its piping, within a longer regional tradition. “Scandinavian design is very much about those subtle touches,” she says. “They don’t demand your attention, but you discover them over time.”

The EX60 offers six lighting colors, along with Forest Leaves Projection, which brings a canopy of foliage into the interior. The idea came from one of the specialist designers at Volvo Cars, and the team expanded on it to explore how the cabin might feel immersed in woodland.

Outside the cabin, the EX60’s exterior paint colors, among them Forest Lake and Heather Bronze, were developed as a palette that balances light and dark, warm and cool, and complements the colors chosen for the interior. Heather Bronze takes its cue from metals weathered over time and the warm cast of heather fields in autumn.

The EX60’s signature motif began when members of the team visited Skagen, where the Skagerrak and Kattegat seas meet, and observed the patterns created by their intersecting waves. Early sketches were refined into a pattern that now appears across the car. It is perforated into the ventilated Nordico upholstery and applied to the subwoofer mesh, the decor panels of the Core version, the rubber floor mats, and the knitted, ventilated seats of the EX60 Cross Country.

Beyond its connection to the Danish coastline, the crossing-wave form also proved useful in production. “There is another layer in this story, and that’s how a pattern like crossing waves was perfect for optimizing the cutting of the materials and minimizing waste,” Erichsen Susnjar says. “And because of that, none of the seats look the same.”
