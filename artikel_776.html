‘Fatherland,’ ‘La Bola Negra,’ ‘Fjord’ Lead European Film Award Nominations

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Sandra Hüller scored a dual best actress nom for 'Fatherland' and 'Rose,' with Renate Reinsve, Sebastian Stan, Penelope Cruz, Javier Bardem and Andrew Scott among the acting nominees.

Pawel Pawlikowski’s German black-and-white period drama Fatherland, and La Bola Negra, a gay Spanish epic from director Javier Ambrossi and Javier Calvo, are the frontrunners for the 2027 European Film Awards, with Fatherland racking up 11 EFA nominations to 10 for La Bola Negra.

Both Fatherland and La Bola Negra picked up EFA noms in the best European film, European director, European screenwriter and European cinematographer categories, with Fatherland also receiving European actress and European actor noms for leads Hanns Zischler and Sandra Hüller for their performances, respectively, as Nobel Prize-winning writer Thomas Mann and daughter Erika. Hüller received a second best European actress nom, for her starring role as a 17th-century woman masquerading as a man in Markus Schleinzer’s Rose. She’s going up against Penélope Cruz, nominated for Florian Zeller’s Bunker; Léa Seydoux for her leading role in Marie Kreutzer’s Gentle Monster; Mathilde Arcel for her performance in May el-Toukhy’s Venice Festival winner Woman Unknown; and Renate Reinsve for her starring role in Cristian Mungiu’s Palme d’Or-winning Fjord.

Sebastian Stan, who plays Reinsve’s fundamentalist Christian husband in Fjord, received a best European actor nomination, alongside Zischler; Javier Bardem, nominated for Rodrigo Sorogoyen’s The Beloved; Swann Arlaud for his turn in Emmanuel Marre’s A Man of His Time; and Andrew Scott, nominated for his performance in Simon Stone’s Elsinore as Ian Charleson, the British actor dying of AIDS who throws himself into a final performance of Hamlet.

Nominated for best European film, alongside Fatherland and La Bola Negra, are Fjord, Woman Unknown and La Gradiva, a French coming-of-age drama from first-time director Marine Atlan.

Best European director nominees, alongside Pawlikowski and Ambrossi/Calvo, are Mungiu for Fjord, Sandra Wollner for the German drama Everytime and Andrey Zvyagintsev for the Russian crime thriller Minotaur.

The European Animation Film nominees are: In Waves, directed by Phuong Mai Nguyen; Iron Boy from Louis Clichy; Jim Queen, directed by Marco Nguyen and Nicolas Athané; Lucy Lost, directed by Olivier Clert; and Viva Carmen from Sébastien Laudenbach.

Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor’s NAZA, an exposé of Israel’s war in Gaza, produced by The Guardian and Britain’s JW Films, was nominated for best European documentary, alongside Letters From the Silenced Country from director Andrei Kutsila; Pegah Ahangarani’s Rehearsals for a Revolution; To Hold a Mountain from directors Biljana Tutorov and Petar Glomazić; and Whispers in the Woods, directed by Vincent Munier.

All of the best animation and best documentary nominees are also nominated in the best European film category.

The 39th European Film Awards will be held in Athens on Jan. 16, 2027.
