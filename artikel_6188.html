Travelers Want Less Screen Time and More Time on the Water

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Travelers are moving from talking about getting outside to booking experiences that take them there. New research from Boatsetter points to growing participation in outdoor and water-based activities, creating opportunities for hospitality brands to make shared experiences more accessible.

This sponsored content was created in collaboration with a Skift partner.

People have spent years saying they want to spend less time on their screens. Now more of them are acting on it. Boatsetter, a booking platform for boat sharing and on-water experiences, tracks that shift in its new report, Great Outdoor Shift 2.0. The report draws on two online surveys of U.S. adults: 834 in December 2025 and 1,034 in September 2026.

Boatsetter’s 2026 Great Outdoor Shift survey shows people responding to digital overload: 77% of respondents said they had already reduced screen time and social media use, and 63% said they would trade screen time for time on or near the water. The pull is about well-being, too: 84% say the outdoors is important for their mental health.

“People aren’t just tired of their screens. They want something that makes them feel small and present. That’s what awe does, and it’s why time on the water with friends keeps topping the list of what Americans want most,” said Michael Farb, CEO of Boatsetter.

Comparing the two surveys, Boatsetter reports a 64% increase in the number of people who tried fishing or paddlesports, a sign that participation is growing. Research from Tripadvisor supports this: In the first half of 2026, U.S. bookings on Tripadvisor grew 57% for fishing charters and 37% for boat rentals compared with the same period in 2025, according to the company’s 2026 Mid-Year Experiences Trends Report.

For hotels, destinations, and other travel providers, the figures point to demand for experiences that bring people into a destination, not just around it.

Boatsetter’s research also points to a broader shift toward active, social travel. Time on the water with friends remains the experience respondents most want more of. Spending intent is rising, too: 82% plan to spend more on outdoor or water activities in 2027, and 37.5% of this year’s respondents want to spend significantly more, compared with 23.4% in Boatsetter’s 2025 survey. For hospitality brands, the practical opportunity lies in helping guests discover and book experiences, from guided excursions to activities with local operators.

The pull toward the outdoors extends beyond water activities. Hilton’s 2026 Trends Report, based on a survey of 14,009 adults in 13 countries who plan to travel in the next 12 months, found that rest and recharging is the top motivation for leisure travel, cited by 56% of respondents. Spending time in nature was also among their top desires, with 37% citing it as such. Both findings point to demand for experiences that help travelers step away from routine.

Taken together, the findings point to a role for travel companies that goes beyond providing a view of nature. In 2027, hotels, tourism boards, and travel partners can make shared outdoor activities easier to access through local partnerships, clearer booking options, and experiences suited to different interests. The opportunity is to help guests follow through on the time they already say they want outdoors.

“We’ve known the desire to get outside is there. What’s changing is that people are acting on it, and they expect technology to help, not get in the way. Our job is to make the outdoor experiences they want easy to reach,” Farb said.
