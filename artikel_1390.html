Kane’s record-equalling cap was no different to his first

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

The 125th cap was no different to the first for Harry Kane, who has now been in the business of goal accumulation for so long that it is only at the landmark moments, that all of us are obliged to step back and take in the full scale of the work he has created.

Peter Shilton took 20 years to rack up his 125 caps, while for Kane it has been just 11 – and then there are the goals. Two more on Tuesday took him to 91 which means that only 12 other England players in history have more England caps than Kane has England goals. This unassuming man from Chingford, who looks like a mildly rebellious academic from the late 1950s, was at it again: the first-time strike, the back-post header. He has some misses across these 11 years, most infamously from the penalty spot against France at the Qatar World Cup almost four years ago, so, yes, there are some regrets too. But what sticks is the way he has taken his chances – again and again.

Following his debut goal in 2015 against Lithuania, Kane has missed just 27 of the 152 England games since. He has added to the total like a great Test batsman patiently moving the scoreboard along. There was a bad run without goals in 2020 and before that in 2018. He scored only five in his first 17 caps and then scored 14 in his next 10. Against a very mediocre Czech Republic team this was classic late-era Kane, picking his moments and taking those chances with the usual absence of nerves. He was even substituted before the end.

Afterwards at a Wembley press conference to mark the occasion, he invoked the cricket analogy himself on the question of scoring 100 England goals. “Being in the 90s, I try not to think about it too much, and it’s like I guess being a cricketer in the 90s. The more you think about the hundred… you don’t always get there.” There has still not been the elusive major trophy in the Kane England years but there has been success relative to the three decades that came before it. Semi-finals, finals – a good team if not a winning team.

At the centre of it has been a man around whom there has never been any scandal or, by the usual measure, much that might be described as spectacular. Rather it has been the scope of Kane’s career that is the most striking. The volume of the goals, of appearances, the capacity to be available – to play, to score – and the absolute quality of the player himself. He is determined to wring the very last drop out of his talent and usually refuses to acknowledge the prospect of the end, although there was a nod to it this time. “At some stage you know this opportunity isn’t going to be here,” he said. “I’m not going to be around forever, and I still hope that’s a long way away. But ultimately, of course, there’s times you’re tired or you’re injured, but I know I’ll miss it when it’s done. I try to always remind myself that and always try to make myself available.”

One expects that when the end comes it will not be as rancorous as the final exit of Cristiano Ronaldo from Portugal, a drama playing out elsewhere. At some point, Kane will take his leave and it is likely to be as unassuming as the rest of it. He will be back at Wembley, injury permitting, for cap No 126 next month against Croatia. He talks like a different person to the one he was even five years ago – with a confidence and certainty about his place in the world. As opposed to the younger Kane, who seemed to regard everything outside his goalscoring tunnel vision with a general sense of bemusement.

“Availability,” he said, “is one of the biggest strengths you can have at this level for any coach.” He was keen to emphasise his “all-round leadership” too, “a lot of assets off the pitch”. He still measures himself in goals, of course, and perhaps there will be a Ballon d’Or too later this month – a prize that has eluded generations of England players before him who have won much more for their clubs than Kane has done. The good news for Kane is that his last England years will be spent in a very good England team – one which was on another level to its opposition on this occasion too.

Thomas Tuchel believes England have taken a step forward from their World Cup heartbreak over this international break and said he was “honoured” to hear fans sing his name.

England finished their four Nations League games with a 3-0 win over Czech Republic, in which Harry Kane scored twice on his record-equalling 125th appearance.

It meant that Tuchel’s team have won three and lost one, against Spain, of their four games since the World Cup, after which the England head coach was heavily criticised for the semi-final defeat to Argentina.

Tuchel seems to have won the forgiveness of some England supporters, who sang his name at the start and the end of Tuesday’s game.

“It means a lot,” Tuchel said. “It’s amazing to have a song. It’s the biggest honour. And I’m very happy and very honoured. They can be sure that I will give my absolute best to make our team win and play well.”

Asked if he sensed a change in mood around his England team since the fall-out from the World Cup, Tuchel said: “I do and I think it’s necessary to move on. I think I can only repeat that we didn’t go out in the group stage. Sometimes it feels like that and we didn’t. As I said, our aim was always to progress, move forward and improve.”

Tuchel snapped back at a reporter who questioned why he felt it necessary to repeat that England had not gone out in the group stage of the World Cup, claiming the criticism had felt a “little bit” too harsh.

But on whether England have shown they are a step ahead of where the team were at the World Cup during this international camp, Tuchel was far more positive and replied: “Of course and that’s what we have to do. That is our goal to constantly improve and develop.

Good evening and welcome to live coverage of England vs Czech Republic from Wembley in the fourth and mercifully final round of fixtures in this sprawling Nations League international break. Tonight Harry Kane will win his 125th cap and equal Peter Shilton for England appearances.

Shilton’s took him 20 years as Gordon Banks was simply too good in the couple of years following his debut and then a couple of managers, Don Revie and Ron Greenwood, preferred Ray Clemence until the latter finally plumped for Shilton as No 1 in 1979. Kane’s, by contrast, have taken him 11. In that time he has been rested for a handful of friendlies but has not been unused in a competitive match when fit since Gareth Southgate left him out of the 5-0 victory over Sam Marino in 2021’s World Cup qualifier when Dominic Calvert-Lewin scored two and Ollie Watkins one goal on his debut.

England played the Czech Republic a week ago, winning 2-0 in Prague by virtue of Anthony Gordon’s cute header and Kane’s clever shot through a defender’s legs but they huffed and puffed for ages against a team harshly reduced to 10 men until they blew the house down. They went on to marmalise Croatia 7-0 in their next game in front of a crowd that sounded like a cross between the choirs on Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2 and There’s No One Quite Like Grandma.

In the past England have had problems backing up their best performances – the 4-1 evisceration of the Dutch at Euro 96 was followed by the cagey display in the quarter-final against Spain before prevailing on penalties, the 5-1 victory in Munich in 2001 led to a drab 2-0 win against Albania at St James’ Park the following Tuesday, in 2016 they went to Berlin and beat world champions Germany but were thumped by Netherlands, who had not even qualified for Euro 2016, a couple of days later. Even their thrilling, second-half showing against Croatia in their World Cup opener in June preceded the dreary 0-0 draw with Ghana.

Given the attacking fluency in Rijeka, Thomas Tuchel must be tempted not to to ring the changes and replace seven or eight players. He will have to find replacements for the injured Alex Scott and Ezri Konsa, however, and Calvert Lewin, who has had seven minutes of action, Rio Ngumoha 19, Morgan Gibbs-White 27 and Jarrad Branthwaite one, may feel they deserve a fairer opportunity to show what they can do in a match.
