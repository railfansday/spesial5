‘The Hunger Games’ are back: Haymitch Abernathy can teach the C-suite about reluctant leadership

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Around the office today, you might hear: “May the odds be ever in your favor.” After the tumultuous technical failures of the past week, the phrase might come off as alarming, but it’s actually a Hunger Games reference.

The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping hits theaters today and tells Haymitch Abernathy’s origin story. Abernathy is an unlikely hero forced into leadership in the rebellion by circumstances out of his control.

This doesn’t mean the C-suite can’t learn a thing or two from him. Here are a few quick stats on the franchise and some of Haymitch’s best management lessons.

The Hunger Games franchise by the numbers

The Hunger Games by Suzanne Collins, the first book in the original trilogy, was published by Scholastic on September 14, 2008. It was followed by Catching Fire in 2009 and Mockingjay in 2010.

Before the second book even came out, Lionsgate acquired the film rights for the first book. It was a smart move because the four Hunger Games films have collectively made more than $1.45 billion at the domestic box office.

Collins took a break after the first trilogy. A stand-alone prequel novel, The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes, came out in 2020, telling the backstory of Coriolanus Snow. The 2023 film version made more than $166 million at the domestic box office.

Sunrise on the Reaping was published in 2025. According to Deadline, the new film is expected to make at least $80 million at the domestic box office on opening weekend alone.

Haymitch Abernathy: Reluctant leader archetype

Haymitch Abernathy, played by Woody Harrelson in the original films and Joseph Zada in Sunrise, is an unconventional leader. He would rather be drinking away his sorrows than changing the fate of Panem—but sometimes management is thrust upon us.

Abernathy excels at strategic planning. In the original books and movies, he is able to guide Peeta and Katniss through the games two times while organizing a rebellion right under the Capitol’s nose. This comes through very careful planning and deception.

In the real world, however, business leaders need to be able to communicate a clear vision and how to execute it.

Abernathy is rough around the edges—and his tough exterior allows him to work with a diverse range of difficult colleagues to get results. You could call him a people-first strategist. For instance, he was able to get through to Katniss and help her play the public relations game by pretending to have feelings for Peeta. She did that so well that her love for him ended up becoming real.

In addition, Abernathy got the vain people of the Capitol to empathize with Katniss and Peeta and send them lifesaving supplies like medicine and water.

Plan, collaborate, and adapt

Similarly, managers will have to work with many different personalities, departments, or vendors. True leaders can approach each individual or organization in a way that uplifts their strengths.

Back in Panem, all this planning doesn’t mean the reluctant Abernathy can’t adapt. He thrives under pressure. He was the victor of the 50th Hunger Games, the Second Quarter Quell. He had to compete against 47 other tributes, double the usual amount. He won by using the force field around the arena against his opponent.

In business, as in the Hunger Games, not everything is going to go according to the five-year-plan. Sometimes you have to improvise solutions as problems arise.

As it hits theaters, Sunrise will reveal more of Abernathy’s story and administrative prowess. The business world should take note.
