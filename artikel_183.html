‘Plume’ of decomposing garbage, gas prompts emergency health order for LA county residents

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Landfill operators have been ordered to provide hotel accommodations and other care to area residents

Officials in a Los Angeles county have ordered the operators of a landfill to provide hotel accommodations and other care to nearby residents after it exploded with decomposing garbage and methane gas.

A noxious, dark geyser of material and smoke began to spew from the troubled Chiquita Canyon landfill on Monday afternoon, prompting officials to issue advisories for nearby communities.

Texas-based Waste Connections, the operator of the landfill in Santa Clarita Valley, said in a statement that its crews were drilling a new well in the landfill when they hit a “pocket of gas about 150 feet below the ground”, said Aaron Katon, a captain with the Los Angeles county fire department.

Katon noted that the erupting cloud was not an explosion, but the production of naturally occurring gages produced by rotting material. The gas pocket carried with it “small pieces of decomposing garbage” as it expended into the air, Katon said, adding: “There was no fire, it was an escape of gas, and after that initial debris got blown off, the plume diminished significantly.”

The plume was “no hazard to anyone in the community”, Katon also said. A spokesman for the fire department told the LA Times that six landfill workers were taken to a hospital for decontamination.

The city of Santa Clarita said in a statement that residents in these areas “should be prepared to evacuate if instructed by authorities. This is a warning, not an evacuation order.”

The 639-acre garbage dump has for years wrestled to control a chain reaction within the landfill. The owners and operators are facing a state lawsuit alleging that they had mismanaged toxic waste.

The Los Angeles county department of public health later ordered the companies to immediately provide temporary hotel stays, filtered-air refuge centers, transportation assistance and air purifiers to affected residents.

LA county supervisor Kathryn Barger said in a statement that the “families of Castaic and the surrounding communities have lived with the burden of this landfill for far too long, and they should not have to wonder whether they are safe. I want answers, and I will not accept vague explanations. What happened at the site today, why it happened, and what safeguards failed are questions the operator and every responsible agency must answer fully and promptly.”

She continued: “Those responsible will be held accountable. My office will stay in close contact with our first responders and regulators, and we will share verified information with the community as soon as we have it. Residents should rely on official County sources and follow guidance from public safety officials.”
