Germany's Wadephul 'shocked' by Trump's Nvidia intervention

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The German foreign minister has criticized Donald Trump's interference in the chip-maker's economic activities. Jensen Huang was brought back after stepping down as Nvidia CEO amid multiple scandals.

German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul on Tuesday expressed shock over US President Donald Trump's admission that he persuaded chip-producing giant Nvidia to reinstall ousted CEO Jensen Huang.

Nvidia brought Huang back on Monday in a surprise decision, less than two weeks after the CEO stepped down amid a series of affairs.

"It shocked me, to be honest, because as far as I know, if I'm personally looking back, I have never seen or recognized such an intervention from the political side into a particular company," Wadephul told reporters at a foreign policy conference in Berlin.

"And I do have some doubts ⁠whether ​this is a very ​good model for the future," he added.

"But OK, that took place in the United States of America," the foreign minister said.

Wadephul went on to say there are enough reasons to defend the rules-based international order, and that includes political power not getting involved in the economic activities of companies.

Huang's shock reinstatement on Monday came on the back of a unanimous vote by Nvidia's board of directors.

The news was welcomed by Trump, who called it the "correct and proper decision" in a post on Truth Social. The president later said he had played a key role, having spoken "directly and indirectly" to the board of directors, urging them to reinstate the scandal-hit ex-CEO.

"I convinced them," Trump said. "I made the deal."

Huang came under massive fire after it was reported he had had an affair with the CEO of the Singapore-based company Megaspeed. She was also accused of being a spy working on behalf of Chinese intelligence.

Nvidia's shares took a hit on Huang's stepping down, but rebounded by 30% on Monday after it was announced he was being reinstalled, adding over $900 billion (€820 billion) to the company's market value.

This share price surge also appears to have benefited Trump himself, with the Financial Times calculating that the president saw his Nvidia stock go up $15 million.

Huang's return has also brought some calm to the market that appeared to be teetering, with growing concerns about the viability of the AI sector, which now makes up a substantial part of the US stock market.

It also marks a turn around from the poor showing by Anthropic's IPO launch that saw its early gains quickly melt away amid fears that the AI sector has been overvalued.
