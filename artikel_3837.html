Exoprimal was intriguing, but it never caught fire with players.

"Exoprimal is a throwback to weird, experimental early 2000s Capcom," we wrote of the team-based hero shooter ahead of its launch back in 2023. While ostensibly about battling dinosaurs, "you first go through an interdimensional time portal controlled by a rogue AI, and then you compete in a Hunger Games-style contest against alternate universe versions of yourself for the AI's amusement. That's where the dinosaur killing comes in."

Despite that intriguing premise, the end result was not particularly memorable: Perfectly fine, and with some fantastic ideas, but "more like an early access phase than a finished game," we wrote in our 69% review. And it didn't catch on: A year later, Capcom announced the end of new updates for the game, although the game remained fully playable.

But now that's changing too. Capcom announced today that Exoprimal services will be terminated on September 29, 2027, at which point the game will become unplayable. "We cannot thank you enough for the love and support you have shown over the years," Capcom wrote, "and we sincerely apologize for the gravity of this announcement."

The sale of seasonal "survival passes," which have been rotating through Exoprimal's four seasons since the end of updates in 2024, has been halted, while the game itself along with all DLC and "other products"—there's quite a lot of it—will be removed from sale on March 30, 2027. After that, the game will tick on—and Capcom hopes you will continue to enjoy it—until the end of the road in September.

It sucks to see games fail, especially when they have the potential to grow into something good, and it's even worse when they don't just tank but become completely unplayable because of their reliance on external servers that no longer exist. This is one of the primary drivers behind efforts like Stop Killing Games: Wherever possible, the end of a game's service should not signal the end of the game.

It's a principle I broadly support, but in the case of Exoprimal it's fair to say that not too many people will be impacted by the shutdown: SteamDB indicates that the peak concurrent player count over the past 24 hours was just 23. Exoprimal is also available on consoles so there are more players in total than just that, but how many more? I don't know, but I'd guess not too many.
