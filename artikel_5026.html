16 Polymarket wallets turn $20,000 into $350,000 before Jensen Huang’s shock NVIDIA return

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Bubblemaps identified unusually timed bets across two groups of accounts, raising questions about whether traders anticipated the comeback or had advance knowledge.

President Donald Trump publicly urged Nvidia to rehire Jensen Huang at 02:54 UTC on Nov. 23, according to TrumpAlert's archive.

Bubblemaps reported that 11 Polymarket accounts received their funding between 03:49 and 10:44 UTC that morning and placed their first bets within about six minutes of each deposit.

The earliest deposit trailed Trump's post by 55 minutes, and the last cleared about three hours before Nvidia announced Huang's reinstatement at 13:55 UTC.

A presidential endorsement, Huang's stated willingness to return, and the campaign for his reinstatement gave any trader a public reason to buy cheap Yes shares. Polymarket priced a return low, and Bubblemaps cited purchases as cheap as 1.5 cents a share.

Public records traced wallet 0x307 receiving 99.49 pUSD at 10:44:40 UTC, and Polymarket's activity feed shows a $99.29 Yes purchase on a Dec. 31 return one second later, at roughly 10 cents a share. Nvidia announced about three hours and 10 minutes later.

On Nov. 17, Nvidia Chairman Harvey Jones publicly ruled out reinstating Huang, even as Huang said he was willing to return.

Bubblemaps counted 16 accounts with five funded wallets on Nov. 16 and 11 funded on Nov. 23. Each account started trading within about six minutes of funding and traded exclusively in Nvidia-related markets.

The group wagered roughly $20,000 and earned roughly $350,000, a return near 1,750%, and its four largest named winners hold $275,777 of that total. Bubblemaps attributed the $350,000 to all 16 accounts combined, which leaves the Nov. 23 group's share undetermined, and both totals carry its rounding.

The five Nov. 16 accounts resist the public-information explanation that fits the later group. Their funding ran from 15:39 to 21:12 UTC, and the first deposit arrived before Trump’s support was reported at 16:52 UTC.

One account shows about $94,900 in realized profit on the November contract, according to Polymarket's closed-position data.

Bubblemaps reported the operators as unidentified, and the on-chain links among the accounts as unestablished, so shared ownership and access to confidential information would need separate proof.

Nvidia's SEC filing dates the reinstatement to Nov. 22 and omits the hour. If directors voted before Trump posted, insiders could have known the outcome while the public waited. If they voted afterward, Trump's endorsement came first, and the filing fits both sequences.

The CFTC's Feb. 25 advisory defines prohibited conduct as misappropriating material nonpublic information in breach of a duty of trust and confidence.

Its Kalshi examples, a candidate trading on his own election and a video editor trading on unpublished YouTube content, each involved an identifiable source. Applied to Nvidia, the standard would require evidence that an account operator received the board's decision through an improper disclosure or held a duty to protect it.

Matching timing, outsized returns, and Nvidia-exclusive wallets are surveillance flags that direct investigators to look for that evidence.

Polymarket says its international platform uses machine-learning systems, blockchain analysis and trade-surveillance tools, and that it prohibits insider trading.

If wallet-level reconstruction ties the 11 Nov. 23 accounts to nothing beyond Trump's endorsement and the campaign behind it, the profits become an example of cheap contracts rewarding an aggressive reading of public information.

A hypothetical trader who set the odds at 10% and bought at 3 cents would expect about a 233% return before costs, while still losing most of the time.

If on-chain researchers link even one account to improperly disclosed knowledge of the board's decision, that account's trades would fall under the CFTC's standard. The other accounts could sit on the public-information side of the line.

A confirmed case would also test whether Polymarket's own monitoring independently found the pattern.

Trump's post gives the 11 Nov. 23 accounts a plausible public explanation. The five accounts funded a week earlier need another one, and the hour of the board vote would show whether the decision existed before Trump posted.

The unresolved issue is whether these traders read public information better than the market or held information the broader market lacked.
