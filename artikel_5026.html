What is Zcash (ZEC)? How shielded transactions work

Zcash, on the other hand, gives users the option to process their transactions privately. It offers transparent transactions, which function like a normal BTC transaction, as well as shielded transactions, which allow users to keep transaction details private through advanced cryptography.

The network launched in October 2016, and the nonprofit Zcash Foundation was established the following year to support the network and its development. Zcash has since evolved into a decentralized ecosystem spanning multiple independent organizations and contributors. The Zcash Foundation continues to maintain core infrastructure and support protocol development.

Zcash’s shielded transactions make use of cryptographic proofs to verify transactions are legitimate while keeping the underlying information concealed. This means that the transaction amount, sender’s address and recipient’s address are all kept private.

To understand how this works, it helps to look at how Zcash handles private funds. Transactions on the network are processed in two parallel but separate accounting environments: the transparent ledger and shielded pools. The latter is where private transactions take place; shielded pools are parts of the chain where funds can be held and transferred without their ownership, balance and history being publicly visible.

According to figures displayed on the official Zcash network dashboard, approximately 29% of the issued ZEC supply was held in shielded pools as of October 2026. It is also worth noting that transactions moving between transparent and shielded pools can reveal the amounts entering or leaving those pools, which in turn could be used to infer relationships between addresses.

While the regular blockchain uses conventional public addresses, shielded pools instead represent funds as cryptographic records called notes. When a user processes a shielded payment, their wallet uses existing notes to create new ones for the recipient. This transaction is recorded on the Zcash blockchain, but observers cannot see who received the notes, which notes were spent or how much ZEC changed hands.

A zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) is a cryptographic method that allows someone to prove a statement is true without revealing the underlying information used to establish it. Imagine if someone wanted to prove they were over 18 without revealing their exact date of birth — ZKPs are able to achieve a similar effect by using cryptographic mathematics.

Instead of broadcasting all of the information needed to verify a payment, the sender generates a cryptographic proof of its validity. Other users can view and verify that proof — the product of the cryptographic algorithm — while the information used to generate it is kept concealed.

Zcash uses a specific type of proof called a zero-knowledge succinct non-interactive argument of knowledge, or zk-SNARK. It also makes use of cryptographic identifiers called nullifiers attached to notes to prevent double-spending.

Viewing keys introduce another level of control for Zcash users. These allow them to selectively reveal transaction data with third parties while keeping it hidden from the general public. These viewing keys give limited read-only access to the wallet without giving the holder the capability to spend the balance inside.

The network allows for different levels of visibility. For example, an incoming viewing key reveals only received payments, while a full viewing key reveals broader details of an account’s shielded activity.

This is known as selective disclosure, allowing users to choose when and with whom they share financial information. Custom controls like this distinguish Zcash from networks like Monero (XMR), where all activity is anonymous by default.

Zcash supports transparent addresses that operate similarly to Bitcoin addresses and shielded addresses designed to protect transaction details. Unified addresses simplify the process by allowing users to manage both types of transaction from inside one wallet.

New ZEC coins enter circulation through proof-of-work mining. This is the same system used by Bitcoin, wherein miners dedicate computing power to solving complex mathematical problems, with the successful miners gaining the right to produce new blocks that are in turn validated by other nodes. The specific algorithm used by Zcash is called Equihash.

ZEC also shares a similar emission schedule with BTC, with a fixed supply cap of 21 million coins and a halving in new emissions roughly every four years. The most recent Zcash halving occurred in November 2024, cutting the block subsidy (the network reward paid to miners) to 1.5625 ZEC.

Unlike Bitcoin, however, Zcash also directs a portion of newly minted coins toward network development.  A new funding model was introduced in November 2025 which now sees 80% of block subsidies paid to miners, 8% to community grants and 12% to a development fund controlled by ZEC holders.

2. What is the difference between shielded and transparent Zcash transactions?

Transparent transactions publicly reveal wallet addresses and transaction amounts, much like Bitcoin. Shielded transactions use zero-knowledge proofs to conceal this information while allowing the network to verify the payment. Transfers between transparent and shielded pools can still reveal the amounts entering or leaving those pools.

Zcash ownership and use are generally permitted in many jurisdictions, although restrictions vary by country. Some jurisdictions, such as the EU, have introduced legislation restricting cryptocurrency platforms from maintaining anonymous accounts. Although not a blanket ban, this could limit the accessibility of ZEC when it takes effect in July 2027.
