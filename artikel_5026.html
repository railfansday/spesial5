Prediction market bets placed just before big news could soon be easier to trace

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

New CFTC reporting rules could help investigators trace prediction market trades to people with early access to information.

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) approved a rule on Nov. 23 requiring exchanges that list certain prediction market contracts to collect traders' names and employers.

When trading surges ahead of an announcement, those records could help investigators identify who traded and look for links to people or organizations with early access to the news.

The final rule also requires exchanges to disclose when covered trades occurred, at what price and size, and how the contracts settled. The CFTC says the reports will help it monitor trading across markets.

Under Section 16.03, exchanges must publish each covered trade’s execution time, ticker, size, and US dollar price as soon as technologically practicable.

The data must stay free for at least a year after it first appears, with instructions on how to download, save, and search it. Public trade disclosure is familiar to exchanges such as Kalshi.

A May 13 CFTC staff letter already made prompt publication of those four fields and reporting to the CFTC under Section 16.02 conditions of relief from certain swap-reporting rules. Its updated list includes KalshiEX and Kalshi Klear.

On Nov. 23, Kalshi’s public trade feed recorded a match for 981.95 contracts in KXHIGHNY-26NOV23-T64 at one cent for Yes and 99 cents for No. The $1 binary contract asked whether New York City’s high that day would fall below 64°F.

Kalshi’s product filing identifies the exchange as a registered designated contract market. Its clearing affiliate, Kalshi Klear, told the CFTC that it clears KalshiEX’s binary event-contract swaps on a fully cash-collateralized basis.

The reporting rule covers certain event contracts classified as swaps and listed on a designated contract market.

A derivatives clearing organization must clear the contract and hold enough collateral at all times to cover the maximum possible loss. A covered contract can pay a fixed amount to one side, or pay both sides based on the settlement price in a structure where only one side profits.

The contract named The Weather Company as the source for its outcome. Kalshi’s settlement record shows a high of 69°F and a final No, with settlement recorded at 12:07 p.m. UTC on Nov. 24. The rule requires exchanges to publish whether the event occurred and, if it did, what happened, when it happened, and the source used to determine the outcome.

A reader could line up the execution timestamps with an independently verified announcement time and see which trades came first. Kalshi’s trade ID identifies the execution, and the public feed shows the trades in sequence, while the accounts behind them remain private.

Exchanges will have to collect each trader’s name, physical address, email, phone number, occupation, and employer and associate that information with every covered order or trade.

The rule also calls for the names of account guarantors and people with a financial interest of 10% or more in the trader or account, where applicable. Exchanges must keep the data for the life of the contract and for at least five years after it ends.

The CFTC’s reporting guidebook shows how the agency can use a customer account number to match an exchange’s trade report with a separate file that lists the trader’s occupation and employer.

A link to a company involved in an announcement could help investigators decide where to look. They would still need to establish when the information became available, whether the trader had access to it, and whether their conduct violated the relevant rule.
