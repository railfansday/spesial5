A Trust Fund Is About Control, Not Wealth – Who Needs One and Which Kind

A trust fund is a legal arrangement that holds money or property for someone under rules you set, and a trustee you pick carries them out. Set one up when you need to control how and when a young child or a relative on disability benefits gets your money, not because your estate is large.

Saving on estate tax is rarely a reason to set one up. The IRS puts the federal estate tax threshold at $15,000,000 per person, so most families owe none.

What a trust gives you is control over who gets your money, when, and on what terms. You, the grantor, move assets into it. The trustee you name, a relative or a bank’s trust department, manages them and pays them out by your rules to the beneficiaries, the people the trust is for.

You can change or cancel a revocable trust while you’re alive, and serve as its trustee. An irrevocable trust is hard to change, because the assets are no longer yours.

Most other families can skip the trust. Write a will, and keep the beneficiary forms on your 401(k), IRA, and life insurance current, since those accounts go to whoever the form names, whatever the will says.
