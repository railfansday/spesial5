Prime Minister Narendra Modi chaired a meeting of the Union Council of Ministers at Bharat Mandapam in New Delhi on Wednesday, hours after returning from the Philippines.

"Council of ministers meeting since morning, extensive discussions on good governance initiatives," he wrote on social media.

Eleven secretaries, including those from the Home and Finance ministries, made presentations, according to local media. Topics included a recent World Bank report on India's progress in reducing extreme poverty, governance reforms, and welfare and youth schemes.

Modi sought feedback on targets set at the ministers' September "chintan shivir," or "brainstorming camp," where ministries were told to ensure 100% saturation of welfare schemes, Business Standard reported.

The meeting lasted about three and a half hours.

Henry Nicholls made an unbeaten 113 to help New Zealand recover from 70-5 to finish the first day of the first Test at 243-5 in Wellington on Thursday. India captain Shubman Gill won the toss and chose to bowl.

Jasprit Bumrah, playing his first Test in about a year, bowled New Zealand captain Tom Latham with the third ball of the match. He finished with 3-52 from 17 overs, adding Daryl Mitchell and Tom Blundell.

Nicholls and Glenn Phillips then added an unbroken 173 after lunch as the pitch eased and India's other pacers lost their lines. Nicholls' 12th Test hundred came from 188 balls, 141 of them dot balls.

Devdutt Padikkal dropped him on 102. Phillips was 94 not out.

"You play at a venue like this and against a team like India, it's a special occasion," AFP cited Nicholls as saying.

India's batting coach, Sitanshu Kotak, said the team's less experienced bowlers could learn from how Bumrah adjusted to New Zealand conditions, AFP reported.

Parts of Bangladesh shut down Thursday as police reported two members of the ruling Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP) were killed and the ancestral home of former interim leader Muhammad Yunus was set on fire.

The unrest comes ahead of former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina's planned return from exile in India next month, despite facing the death sentence there.

Her party, the Awami League, which was banned after Hasina was ousted, called for the nationwide "lockdown" after a week of protests over the government's rejection of her request for a new passport and identity card.

Police said two local BNP leaders were killed in Narail when Awami League activists attacked a BNP march, local media reported. It added that two other people, a local BNP leader and a transport worker, died in Faridpur and Pirojpur earlier this week.

Newspaper reports said suspected BNP activists attacked or set fire to the homes of two Awami League leaders in Dhaka.

Crude bombs went off in Dhaka before dawn and at least four people were wounded, the Press Trust of India reported. Reuters said 97 platoons of the Border Guard had been deployed across seven districts, including Gopalganj and Faridpur.

Authorities said flammable material was probably thrown into an unused ground-floor room of Yunus's Chattogram family home, and that relatives living upstairs were safe. At least 37 Grameen Bank branches and offices, which Yunus founded, have been targeted since November 10, the bank said.

Home Minister Salahuddin Ahmed has said those behind the violence will be identified and prosecuted, and he accused Awami League leaders abroad of inciting it. Awami League leaders could not be reached for comment, Reuters said.

Odisha has evacuated tens of thousands of people ahead of Cyclone Akvan, which is expected to cross the coast by noon Friday, the third severe cyclonic storm to threaten India's east coast in less than a month.

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) expects landfall between Narsapur in Andhra Pradesh and Puri in Odisha with winds up to 110 kmph. The IMD now expects a severe cyclonic storm, not the very severe storm it projected earlier. Local media reported red alerts for heavy to extremely heavy rain in both states.

Reuters cited the chief minister's office, which said more than 47,000 people had been evacuated by Thursday. Local media put the figure at about 50,000 and said 3,000 cyclone shelters had been opened.

Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi said most evacuees were from Ganjam district. Schools in Ganjam, Gajapati, Puri, Jagatsinghpur, Kendrapara and Kandhamal are shut for three days from Thursday. Later, authorities announced that schools in Malkangiri, Rayagada and Koraput districts would also remain closed on Thursday and Friday.

"No evacuations in Andhra Pradesh yet," state disaster official Prakhar Jain told Reuters. "The storm has reduced in intensity."

Cyclone Arnab struck the region in late October and Cyclone Murasu earlier this month.
