The 13 Best International Fantasy TV Shows, Ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Fantasy is a very old genre of fiction, one that is well over two centuries old at this point. It has lasted so long because there is just something so captivating about worlds full of magic and monsters, or that feature aspects that are so far beyond our current existence. It's kind of hard not to get wrapped up in these worlds, especially with the rise of fantasy television, which has become more popular than ever since the 2010s started.

While there are a lot of English-language fantasy shows out there that deserve widespread success, they aren't all that can be found in the world of fantasy. Indeed, many countries around the world have tried their hand at fantasy, often to incredible results. Whether it's romantasy, supernatural drama, or fantasy horror, there's something out there for everybody, regardless of language. These are the best international fantasy TV shows of all time.

This Chinese series is honestly one of the most entertaining watches in the animated genre. Scissor Seven centers on an amnesiac hairdresser named Seven (Xiaofeng He), who moonlights as a spectacularly incompetent assassin while wielding telekinetically controlled scissors.

Scissor Seven is quite the silly indulgence, yet it’s pretty surprising how nonsense tends to become addictive drama within the show. Initially, the series uses its earlier episodes to deliver tons of parody martial-arts clichés, but as the show goes on, it keeps adding emotional consequence without losing its strange comedy. With its unique trajectory, Scissor Seven makes for some pretty memorable TV. Its unusual balance creates an animated experience that feels genuinely heartfelt, exciting, and consistently surprising, making it an easy pick for this list due to the show’s fine demonstration of contemporary Chinese animation. —Jiminna Shillingford

Mortel, which translates to "Mortal," is about two high schoolers named Sofiane (Carl Malapa) and Victor (Némo Schiffman), who are thrust into a rough situation when Sofiane's brother is murdered. Desperate for answers, Sofiane and Victor make a pact with an ancient voodoo god, giving them supernatural powers to aid them in their quest for justice. Accompanying them is voodoo practitioner Luisa (Manon Bresch), who is there to ensure the voodoo god doesn't trick the two into falling for his trap.

Mortel wasn't super popular, and it's pretty far from amazing, but it's enjoyable nonetheless. It feels like a blend of superhero dramas and fantasy shows, with a really interesting dynamic between Sofiane and Victor. The two have different sets of powers, but they don't function unless they're near each other, which opens up a lot of doors for plot twists and obstacles. Mortel is a great show for those who like supernatural dramas and murder mysteries.

Ragnarok takes its title from Norse mythology — the term "Ragnarok" refers to armageddon, when the gods will die, the sun will go out, and the world will come to a violent, destructive end. The series, naturally, is set in a remote town in Norway, which has fallen under the shadow of the tyrannical Jutul family. The Jutuls are business owners and oversee a series of factories that are polluting the picturesque Norwegian village. Little do the townsfolk know, however, that the Jutuls are actually supernatural beings called jötnar.

With the Jutuls oppressing the townsfolk, the fate of the village, and possibly of the world, falls into the hands of Magne (David Stakston), who learns that he is the reincarnation of the Norse god of thunder, Thor. Ragnarok isn't amazing, but it's very entertaining, and it was lucky enough to get three seasons, which is more than most shows get. It wasn't a huge hit domestically, but it has received a higher level of praise internationally, particularly in English-speaking countries.

If I Hadn't Met You hails from Spain, but is actually in Catalan rather than Spanish. The harrowing drama series follows Eduard (Pablo Derqui), a heartbroken man who has lost his entire family in a gruesome car accident. Devastated and adrift in life, Eduard seeks the help of Liz Everest (Mercedes Sampietro), who teaches Eduard to unlock the supernatural power contained within him, which allows him to travel to alternate universes.

This leads Eduard on a quest to find an alternate universe in which his family has survived, so that he can seek some respite from his suffering. The ten-part series only received one season, but honestly, that's all it really needed. Reviews are generally pretty positive, with viewers commending the series for its emotion and heartfelt storyline. The acting performances are also really incredible, with Pablo Derqui perfectly capturing Eduard's sense of profound sorrow and loss.

Trese is a fascinating international animated series that’s the perfect mesh of horror, mystery, and cultural storytelling. Based on the Filipino comic series by Budjette Tan and Kajo Baldisimo, the series centers around an investigator known as Alexandra Trese (Shay Mitchell) who operates at the boundary between the supernatural communities hidden within the Philippine capital and ordinary criminal cases.

Trese is an easy pick for this list because it brings supernatural creatures and the ancient legends of Philippine folklore into a rather dark and intriguing modern setting. The show's standout appeal is its sense that the setting, Manila itself, is part of the mythology. With just a limited, six-episode structure, Trese delivers the perfect supernatural show, one that is worthwhile for viewers who want adult fantasy animation that goes beyond the generic European tropes featuring demons, vampires, and fairy tales. —Jiminna Shillingford

LastMan is a truly underrated international joy that offers a distinctive combination of intense action, French animation, and supernatural mystery. Set in the corrupt city of Paxtown, the adventure fantasy follows the boxer Richard Aldana (Martial Le Minoux), who becomes responsible for protecting young Siri (Maëlys Ricordeau) from violent supernatural forces.

With its mature storytelling and energetic fight sequences, LastMan makes for a great example of international animation intended for older viewers. It’s a brilliant series that definitely deserves its flowers and far more attention, especially because French animation rarely gets associated internationally with hard-edged adult action TV, yet LastMan delivers exactly that while consistently maintaining its charming European art style. LastMan is a genuine gem that may not enjoy the popularity it truly deserves but is an excellent example of one of the best animated series ever made. —Jiminna Shillingford

Love Between Fairy and Devil is a show that cuts right to the chase with its title. It's pretty easy to infer what the plot is about, but just in case you need clarification, the unforgettable romance-fantasy series follows Dongfang Qingcang (Dylan Wang), the Demon King known as the Moon Supreme. During a war in which the Demon King assaulted the heavens, he was sealed away, only to be released by the goddess of war. Millennia later, the war goddess is reborn in the form of Xiao Lunhua (Yu Shuxin), also known as Orchid.

This is a show for all those romantasy fans out there. Featuring a touching love story of forbidden romance between immortal beings on opposite sides of the good and evil dynamic, Love Between Fairy and Devil garnered very positive reviews from international audiences, particularly in East and Southeast Asia. The first and only season consists of a whopping 36 episodes, none of which are boring or bad. Romantasay isn't a genre for everyone, and it's understandable if that's the case, but for those who do like the subgenre, this show is a must-see.

Four worlds and four completely different ideas of what a good life looks like. This is where you belong, what it would ask of you, and what you would get back in return.

You belong somewhere old enough that the mountains have names and somebody can still tell you who gave them. Middle-earth is for people who think the right thing is worth doing even when it will not be finished in their lifetime.

You want the sea, a crew, and absolutely nobody telling you where you are allowed to go. The Grand Line is the world for people whose main ambition in life is finding out what is over there.

You would rather be feared and useful than liked and useless. The Continent is a world where the monsters are the straightforward part of the job and the people are where it all gets complicated.

You want the myths to be real and loose in the actual world, and you want to find out at about thirteen that you were never anywhere near as ordinary as everybody kept telling you.

One Piece is one of the longest-running anime series ever, which has been going strong for 26 years. The series is primarily pirate-themed, with the plot hinging on a coveted pirate map that supposedly leads to untold treasures. Unfortunately, one piece of the map is missing, prompting a search across the Seven Seas for it, hence the title. The main character is an aspiring young pirate named Monkey D. Luffy, who has eaten a magical Devil Fruit, which allows him to stretch and rubberize his body at will.

The series is definitely mature, but also humorous and light-hearted. There are loads of action and thrills at sea, all accentuated by magic and mystery. One Piece has become such a staple of television that it even inspired a live-action remake on Netflix in 2023. However, the original series still reigns supreme — aside from being the best pirate show of all time, it's easily one of the greatest fantasy shows of all time, too.

Shahmaran is a Turkish series named after the mythical creature of the same name. The fantastical Şahmaran is purported to be half-woman, half-snake, and has roots in Turkish and Indo-Iranian folklore. The main character is Şahsu (Serenay Sarıkaya), a psychology professor from the Crossroads of the World — Istanbul. Şahsu moves to the smaller city of Adana for work, and while there, encounters a cult that worships Şahmaran, who are patiently awaiting the fulfillment of some sort of prophecy. Things get stranger when Şahsu meets a strange man named Maran (Burak Deniz).

Spanning two seasons, Shahmaran was commended for the performances of its main cast, as well as the family dynamics that occur both inside and outside of the cult. It was also praised for its mysterious premise, especially with the sense of intrigue that surrounds the cult. Domestically popular, yet internationally unheard of, Shahmaran is one of the most underrated Netflix original series that deserves to be seen by any fantasy fan.

Alchemy of Souls consists of two seasons and 30 episodes, with each season following its own distinct storylines, which are mostly separate but still connected to each other. The series follows mages in the fictional country of Daeho, who begin a dangerous quest to learn a forbidden spell that will allow them to switch bodies. In the process, a renowned warrior becomes trapped in a weakened body, yet still falls in love with a young nobleman.

This is another show that is largely a romantasy, which isn't everyone's personal cup of tea, so it's understandable if you're averse to checking this one out. However, for people who love a romantic subplot to go with their magic, Alchemy of Souls is about as good as it gets, bearing all the hallmarks of a classic K-drama with a twist of fantasy, all set in a vaguely Medieval world.
