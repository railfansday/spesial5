10 Years Ago Today: Hailee Steinfeld Starred In One Of The Best Coming-Of-Age Movies Of All Time

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Actor Hailee Steinfeld broke out in the Coen Brothers' 2010 Western film "True Grit" as the precocious Mattie Ross, who speaks like she's wise beyond her years but lacks the experience that brings true wisdom. We at /Film consider Steinfeld as Mattie the greatest performance in any Coens movie, which is no small feat.

Nominated for an Academy Award at only 14 years old, it would've been easy for Steinfeld's career to have peaked there. But she has kept challenging herself with music, voice acting, and plenty of new movies, and one of her best is absolutely writer/director Kelly Fremon Craig's coming-of-age film "The Edge of Seventeen."

After a premiere at the Toronto International Film Festival, "The Edge of Seventeen" hit theaters on November 18, 2016. It takes its title from the Stevie Nicks song yet applies it more literally to this story about a 17-year-old girl. The acerbic, awkward, and unpopular Nadine Franklin (Steinfeld) has lost the two people in the world who understand her. One, her father, Tom (Eric Keenleyside), who died a few years prior of a heart attack. Now, her best friend, Krista (Haley Lu Richardson), has started dating Nadine's older brother Darian (Blake Jenner), whom Nadine loathes for having their mother Mona's (Kyra Sedgewick) favor and being so effortlessly beloved and successful.

"I've gotta spend the rest of my life with myself," Nadine says while drunk. We all realize that and then come to terms with it at some point. Nadine's story fits in the gap between the realization and the coming to terms. Speaking as someone who was the same age as Nadine was in 2016, and saw "The Edge of Seventeen" in his senior year of high school, few coming-of-age movies depict awkward adolescence in the 2010s more honestly.

Critics compared "The Edge of Seventeen" to a modern John Hughes movie, as did Hailee Steinfeld herself. In 2016, she explained to Entertainment Weekly that she hoped that the movie could reflect her generation's teen years back at them while still being timeless, in the same way "Sixteen Candles" and "The Breakfast Club" — films made for 1980s kids — still resonated with her (someone born in 1996).

But "The Edge of Seventeen" doesn't soften itself by looking to echoes of older movies instead of real life. The film begins with Nadine marching up to her history teacher, Mr. Bruner (Woody Harrelson) in his classroom, announcing she's planning to die by suicide and wants someone to know first. That opening makes you think you're in for a movie about an inspirational teacher who talks a depressed student back from the edge and shows them why life is worth living. Well, not quite.

We see, as Mr. Bruner immediately recognizes, this is Nadine being dramatic. "I don't want to take up a ton of your time" she says right off, which is her first lie. The movie then rewinds (with the help of some voiceover from Nadine, though that fades pretty quickly) before catching back up with this cold open. What led to her current situation? Nadine accidentally sent a text to her crush, Nick (Alexander Calvert), describing very explicitly how she wants to hook up with him.

That complements one of the best bits of writing in the film, one that definitely helped it speak to my younger self. Nadine has a lot of movie-typical teenage boy qualities: she doesn't fit in, dresses in baggy sweatshirts, has a hopeless crush she never talks to, makes sex jokes while mistaking vulgarity for wit, etc.

During the "Edge of Seventeen" press tour, Kelly Fremon Craig and producer James L. Brooks spoke with /Film. Craig cited some "life-altering" writing advice from Brooks, that she needed to ask herself, "What is this movie saying about life?" I would answer that "The Edge of Seventeen" is about how, when you're a teenager, your problems seem like the whole world and that part of growing up is realizing others — even people who irritate you everyday (as Nadine's mom and brother do to her) — are dealing with their own pain.

Upon its release, "The Edge of Seventeen" saw only modest financial success, grossing $19 million in theaters against its $9 million budget. A spin-off TV series ordered by YouTube Red ultimately didn't move forward, either. I would also argue the film's anticipated reputation as "definitive teen movie of the 2010s" was overshadowed by Greta Gerwig's "Lady Bird," which came out the following year and hits similar beats while finding both great financial success and critical acclaim. Not that "The Edge of Seventeen" has been lost to time or anything, though.

In that aforementioned /Film interview, Craig claimed, "If [Hailee Steinfeld] didn't exist, the movie wouldn't exist," because she brought a sui generis quality to Nadine as she once did to Mattie Ross. The two would work together again on the "Transformers" spin-off film "Bumblebee," where Steinfeld starred and Craig performed rewrites. "Bumblebee" often feels like "The Edge of Seventeen" but with Transformers included.

A couple of times in "The Edge of Seventeen," Nadine looks up to the sky and asks if God is even paying attention to her. Perhaps it's not surprising that when Craig directed another coming-of-age film, she adapted Judy Blume's classic novel "Are You There God? It's Me, Margaret."
