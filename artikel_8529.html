Personal Consumer Issues • Re: Mike Piper (Boglehead) new book out [Household Cybersecurity]

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

PS: I am not a psychologist, but I have this theory. The information in Piper's book is a two-edged sword. It's good to motivate people with the fact that breaches are increasing and will accelerate with AI. But it also makes them more prone to believing a phone call about a breach that seems to be from their bank. I am perhaps more concerned with AI breach acceleration than most. AI accelerates the breaches and accelerates the scam breach claims

Anyway, I learned a lesson. I think I am more prone to hang up without giving any information, and then call my bank and also the bank credit card if that is needed.
Chapter 1 of the book is largely about a story that follows a very similar fact pattern (thief pretending to be the victim's financial institution, saying they're reaching out about a breach/fraud). It even has a heading: "'We’re Contacting You About Fraud' Is Itself a Red Flag for Fraud." One of the major themes of the book is to be suspicious of any inbound communications.

I really don't know how to be much more explicit than this section of the book:
What To Do When You’re Contacted

When a financial institution with which you have a relationship reaches out to you (whether about a suspected fraud or about anything else):

•If it’s an email or a text (or message via any other messaging app such as WhatsApp), do not reply.
•Do not click on any links.
•If it’s a phone call, take down whatever information they give you. (Or frankly just don’t answer the phone if it’s from a number you don’t know. Just listen to the voicemail, if they leave one.)
•Regardless of method of contact, do not give them any information. Not your date of birth. Not your Social Security number. And absolutely not a multi-factor authentication code. Give them nothing. Truly, nothing.
•Then reach out, via a trusted method, to the financial institution. If it’s your bank, you can call the number on the back of your credit/debit card. Or visit the company’s website via a previously saved bookmark or by directly typing the company’s known URL into your browser (e.g., Van-guard.com). Or use the official mobile app for that company to contact them. And once you know you’re actually in contact with the right organization, ask them for details about the situation.
