17 Part-Time Jobs for High School Students – Starting Age & Pay for Each

Choose a high school job by your age and school schedule first, then by pay. Federal child labor rules don’t cover casual babysitting or chores like yard work for neighbors, and they allow most store and restaurant jobs from 14. At 14 and 15, you can work at most 3 hours a school day and 18 a school week.

The Department of Labor’s rules also keep those hours between 7 a.m. and 7 p.m., or 9 p.m. from June 1 through Labor Day, which leaves out most evening shifts during the school year.

Age also decides the tasks. Until 16, you can’t bake, do warehouse work, or run power equipment such as a lawn mower. Until 18, you can’t do work the department rates hazardous, such as operating a forklift or a meat slicer.

At 16 and 17, federal law sets no limit on your hours. Your state can set its own limits, and the stricter rule applies. The Labor Department’s state-by-state table shows the hour limits states set for workers under 16 and for 16- and 17-year-olds. Many states also require minors to get a work permit, sometimes called working papers.

Each pay figure below is the median hourly wage the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported for employees in that job in May 2025, adults included. Half earned more than the median and half earned less. Federal law also lets employers pay workers under 20 a youth minimum wage of $4.25 an hour for their first 90 days. If your state or city sets a higher minimum wage, you get that rate instead, unless its law makes an exception for workers under 20.

The list covers jobs with an employer or a neighbor. Summer jobs, online work, and a business of your own are separate searches. Once the paychecks start, find out whether you need to file a tax return, and whether to put some of your pay in a custodial Roth IRA, a retirement account a parent manages for you until you’re an adult.

Childcare workers earned a median $16.82 an hour. Casual babysitting has no federal minimum age, so parents decide when you’re ready. Start with families you already know, set your rate with each one, and take a babysitting course that covers first aid and CPR before your first job.

Animal caretakers earned a median $17.00 an hour. Caring for a neighbor’s pets is one of the minor chores around private homes that federal child labor rules leave out, but Rover requires its sitters and dog walkers to be at least 18. So find clients on your own street, and meet each pet before you take the job.

Landscaping and groundskeeping workers earned a median $18.82 an hour. Mowing and raking for neighbors has no federal minimum age, but a landscaping company can’t put you on a power mower, trimmer, or edger until 16. Knock on doors in early spring with a flat price per lawn, and offer leaf raking in the fall.

Cashiers earned a median $15.81 an hour. Bagging groceries, stocking shelves, and running a register are all open at 14 under federal rules, and Publix hires front service clerks, its name for baggers, from that age. Apply on the chain’s careers site, then ask the store manager about after-school shifts.

Retail salespersons earned a median $17.03 an hour. Cashiering, selling, and stocking are open from 14 under federal rules, though a chain can set a higher minimum of its own. List the exact hours you can work after school and on weekends, since a store has to schedule you within the federal limits.

Food preparation workers earned a median $16.98 an hour. From 14, you can wash dishes, prep food, and cook on electric or gas grills without an open flame or on fryers that lower and raise baskets automatically. Baking and other cooking wait until 16, and power-driven meat slicers and dough mixers until 18. Ask for the kitchen manager when you apply.

Fast food and counter workers earned a median $15.00 an hour. Taking orders, running the register, and preparing food are all open from 14, within the same cooking limits as any kitchen job. Apply online, then stop in on a slow afternoon and ask for the manager.

The Labor Department’s O*NET database files baristas under fast food and counter workers, so the same $15.00 median applies. Federal child labor regulations name coffee grinders and automatic coffee machines among the machines 14- and 15-year-olds may run, though each café sets its own minimum age. Ask at coffee shops near your school about after-school shifts.

Waiters and waitresses earned a median $16.94 an hour, tips included, since the BLS wage survey counts them as pay. Federal law lets restaurants pay tipped workers a cash wage of just $2.13 an hour as long as tips bring them to at least $7.25, though some states require more. Serving is open from 14 under federal rules. Ask how tips are shared before you take the job.

Ushers, lobby attendants, and ticket takers earned a median $15.82 an hour. Selling tickets and working the concession stand are open from 14, and the regulations even name popcorn poppers among the machines you may run. Before 16, though, you can’t work past 7 p.m. outside summer, so ask about weekend matinee shifts.

Amusement and recreation attendants earned a median $15.46 an hour. At a bowling alley, skating rink, or recreation center, running the front counter and cleaning up are open from 14. Lead with the weekend daytime hours you can work.

Clerical library assistants earned a median $17.74 an hour. Pages put returned books back on the shelves and keep them in order, clerical work open from 14 under federal rules. Ask at the circulation desk, and check your city’s or county’s job listings.

General office clerks earned a median $21.64 an hour. Filing, answering phones, and data entry are office work open from 14. Ask family businesses, dental and medical offices, and your school’s front office whether they need after-school help.

Tutors earned a median $20.84 an hour. The federal regulations list tutoring among the work open at 14. Start with younger students you already know, in the subjects where your grades are strongest.

Web developers earned a median $44.54 an hour. The regulations also list computer programming and writing software as work open at 14. Build a portfolio first, with sites for a club, a team, or a family business, then offer to update a local shop’s site.

Lifeguards and ski patrol workers earned a median $16.14 an hour. At 15, you can guard at a pool or water park with a lifeguard certification from the American Red Cross or a group like it, and at 16 at lakes and beaches. The Red Cross requires students to be at least 15 by the last day of class, and its certification lasts two years. Ask indoor pools about year-round shifts.

Recreation workers earned a median $17.58 an hour. Camps accredited by the American Camp Association hire counselors from 16, as long as each is two or more years older than the campers in their group. Younger teens can start as counselors in training, a role that pays at some camps and costs tuition at others, so ask which kind it is before you sign up.

Before you accept an offer, ask how many hours a week the job can give you. In a school week, 18 hours at $15 an hour pays $270, while 10 hours at $18 pays $180.
