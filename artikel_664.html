Chart Rewind: In 1956, ‘Blues’ Singer Marty Robbins Swept 3 Country Charts

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The Grand Ole Opry stalwart reached a new plateau with a crossover special.

Arizona native Marty Robbins moved to Nashville in January 1953 on the same day that he joined the Grand Ole Opry, and it became a good fit as Robbins attained a new level of success, beginning with his single “Singing the Blues.” It rose to No. 1 on Billboard’s Most Played C&W in Juke Boxes chart dated Nov. 24, 1956, making him the leader on all three of the magazine’s country lists. He occupied all three simultaneously for a total of 10 consecutive weeks.

“Singing the Blues” had spent a week at the summit on each of the other two country charts, Best Sellers in Stores and Most Played by Jockeys; it would top the Juke Box and Best Sellers hit parades 13 total weeks, while reigning 11 weeks on Jockeys.

Following his 1952 debut single “I’ll Go On Alone,” which became his first leader, Robbins would accumulate 16 total country No. 1s, concluding with 1976 release “Among My Souvenirs.” The seventh of those singles, “Don’t Worry,” spent 10 weeks in the pole position, his only other single to reach double digits at No. 1.

Robbins picked up “Blues” from songwriter Melvin Endsley, who had contracted polio at age three and would be confined to a wheelchair for the remainder of his life. Robbins recorded the song in November 1955, and Columbia released it the following August. “Blues” also went to No. 17 on Billboard’s pop Juke Boxes chart, where Guy Mitchell’s version reached No. 1, among reigns on other pop lists.

Robbins ultimately joined the Country Music Hall of Fame in October 1982, two weeks before he died from a heart attack.
