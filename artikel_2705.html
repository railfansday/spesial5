Lakers must be aggressive, plug biggest hole that could sink title chances

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The Lakers are championship contenders. Luka Doncic is leading the MVP race. If the GM plays his cards right, his team could be in a position to hang its 18th banner.

But first, the Lakers need to address a glaring hole.

Brook Lopez #11 of the LA Clippers and Isaiah Stewart #28 of the Memphis Grizzlies battle for the ball on Wednesday, Nov. 25, 2026, in Memphis, Tenn. Getty Images

They need a backup center. They're dangerously thin at that position, an issue that could be a fatal flaw in a playoff series.

You know who could be an intriguing option? Brook Lopez.

The Clippers are sellers. They're open for business. Their players could be dealt at bargain rates.

With Kawhi Leonard, James Harden and Ivica Zubac gone, and the Clippers ravaged by the fallout from the Aspiration scandal, including losing five future first-round draft picks, the team is in full rebuild mode with the goal of trying to get draft assets.

That means Lopez could be prime for the picking.

The 7-foot-1 Lopez won a championship with the Bucks in 2021. He's averaging 14.4 points (55% shooting from the field, 43.3% from beyond the arc), 5.1 rebounds and 1.8 assists per game for the Clippers this season.

He'd give the Lakers a great option behind Walker Kessler. He's a strong rim protector. His efficient outside shooting could especially be an asset against someone like Victor Wembanyama, helping draw him out of the paint.

Lopez is 38. The Lakers may want to acquire a younger center who could be a mainstay on the roster alongside Doncic, Kessler and Austin Reaves in the future. And Lopez's defensive mobility leaves a bit to be desired when compared with younger, more athletic bigs.

But the Lakers are in win-now mode, and Lopez could undoubtedly help tip them over the edge this season.

Brook Lopez of the LA Clippers drives to the basket against the Memphis Grizzlies in Memphis, Tenn., Wednesday, Nov. 25, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

This much is for sure: The Lakers need to be aggressive ahead of February's trade deadline.

They have the NBA's best record at 15-3 They're the it team. These opportunities don't come around often.

It has been six years since the Lakers won their last championship in 2020. Before that? Ten years had elapsed following their title in 2010.

Sacramento Kings defender Dylan Cardwell plays defense against Los Angeles Lakers player Walker Kessler on Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, in Los Angeles. NBAE via Getty Images

"We'll be active at the trade deadline to shore up things that we see," Pelinka said on Spectrum SportsNet. "We think this team has a real chance to make a run, so we want to give the group as much talent as we can.

"Don't want to get too far over our skis with a hot start, because you don't want to get big-headed. But clearly the way it's gone so far, it's been great. And we hope it continues."

Pelinka spent the offseason building a roster around Doncic, including emptying his treasure chest to acquire Kessler in a sign-and-trade with the Jazz, giving them two first-round picks and two first-round pick swaps on top of a four-year, $130 million deal.

The Kessler gamble has panned out. He's a double-double machine. They found their franchise center. But the Lakers are paper thin behind him, with Kevon Looney falling out of the rotation and Jarred Vanderbilt giving bigger teams an advantage.

And with their neighbors down the street having a yard sale, Lopez could be a good option for a Lakers team with limited draft capital following the Kessler deal.

The Lakers have taken the league by storm this season. They're the real deal. They have a legitimate shot at winning the Larry O'Brien Championship Trophy.

They just have to make sure they plug any holes that could sink them.
