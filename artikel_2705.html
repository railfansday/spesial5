Game Preview: Knicks vs Pacers, November 22, 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Over the last few years, the Pacers have given the Knicks fits.

We all know about the recent postseason history between these two teams, although we won't go into that. Even with Tyrese Haliburton out last season, they battled and made the Knicks work. Outside of the March 17th game in which the Knicks won handily 136-110, the other three games were all close games. Despite being in the middle of a retooling and tanking season, they tried their hardest to beat the Knicks, and even succeeded in doing so once, handing them a 137-134 overtime loss in early February. It also isn't that long ago that the Knicks suffered what is probably the most frustrating loss of their season. Less than 24 hours after the Knicks' most exhilarating win of the season though, they get a chance to get some sweet revenge on an opponent is starting to become way too familiar.

The Pacers enter Sunday's contest 5-11 and are losers of three straight. Haliburton remains sidelined with a hamstring injury, T.J. McConnell is out also with a hamstring injury, and Pascal Siakam is currently listed as a questionable with a hip injury. But given their recent tendency to bring out their big guns for the Knicks, I'd expect Siakam to play. And given their recent ability to test the Knicks even without their starting point guard, I'd expect the Pacers to push the Knicks.

That is unfortunately even more the case considering the situation the Knicks find themselves in. They just played in a game that came down to the last shot and saw Jalen Brunson, Josh Hart, and Landry Shamet all play 35 or more minutes. They are also at risk of being without both Karl-Anthony Towns and OG Anunoby for a second straight game due to illness and calf tightness, respectively. We already saw Brunson stretched thin last night against a tough Trail Blazers defense. Having to go without the team's second and third-best offensive players against a Pacers team that tends to defend him tough on the second night of a back-to-back sounds like a pretty tough and unenviable task.

That being said, the Knicks should win this one. I know that was the case against the Pacers the last time they met up, but the Knicks are the better team. Being without Towns and Anunoby hurts them a tremendous amount on both sides of the ball. And that was evident in the final three quarters of last night's game, where we saw the Knicks' offense slowly revert to a heliocentric Brunson iso-fest. The defense held up, partly due to the Trail Blazers offense shooting themselves in the foot, but having Anunoby there obviously helps. And if Siakam is a go tonight, the rosters on paper match up more closely because I don't see who on this roster has the size to really slow him down if Anunoby is out again.

If Bridges continues playing the way he has as of late and Shamet continues to look like prime Stephen Curry reincarnated, it might not matter, though. Brunson will get a lot of shots, and he may not be the most efficient against what should be a very physical Pacers team that has gotten to know Brunson and his endless counter moves pretty well. But he's also been shooting the ball well, especially from three. And he's made everybody quickly forget that slow shooting start he's gotten off to.

When these two teams last met up on November 13th, Jordan Clarkson, Miles McBride, and the aforementioned Shamet combined to go just 5-16 for 14 points while Aaron Nesmith went for 18 points on 7-10 shooting by himself and the supporting cast of Obi Toppin, Jay Huff, Larry Nance, and Quenton Jackson went 9-17 for 22 points. Nesmith has struggled mightily from three over the last few games, in large part due to a hand sprain, hamstring injury, and knee soreness that have reportedly been bothering him. He's currently shooting a career-worst 25% on catch-and-shoot threes, is shooting just 27.4% from three overall this season, and just 22.7% from three in his last 10 games. Nesmith is a pivotal part of the Pacers team, but he's been hurting their offense recently by not even looking to shoot-something the Knicks' very own Hart has done at times as well.

With Brunson and Shamet likely getting a lot of attention and the Pacers needing as much offense as they can get with Haliburton out and Siakam's availability being in question, the game could come down to which group of role players can do more. Can Kelly Oubre, who went for 16 points when these two teams last met, repeat his performance? How about Bridges, who not only hit the game-winning shot last night but scored 18 points against the Pacers not too long ago? And how about the matchup between two glue guys in Hart and Nesmith, both of whom have struggled to score the ball effectively recently?

I expect the Knicks to win this game. But I said that for their last meeting as well. With both sides missing key players, it'll likely be a slugfest. Don't expect an aesthetically pleasing, or even an entertaining game. The Pacers aren't afraid to play that way, and as we've seen, neither are the Knicks. Not that it's necessarily mattered in the past, but the Knicks are trending in the right direction and the Pacers aren't. New York takes this 103-95.
