Norse Atlantic Collapses: Airline Cancels Flights, Strands Passengers

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Back in August 2026, all-Boeing 787 airline Norse Atlantic Airways announced it was in a rough financial spot, and was pursuing a sale, merger, or other arrangement. Not a whole lot of progress was made in subsequent months, and just this morning, the airline announced that it was in a “critical” financial situation.

Well, it seems that it didn’t take long for things to get much worse, as the airline has seemingly canceled all flights just hours later, leaving travelers stranded.

Earlier today, Norse Atlantic provided an update on its financial situation and ongoing strategic review. There’s no point in paraphrasing this, so here’s the direct statement from the airline:

The Company’s financial situation is critical. It is dependent on securing financing to meet its short-term liquidity needs and to continue its operations. Norse, supported by its advisors, has explored various strategic alternatives and entertains discussions for a potential takeover. In parallel, the Board and management have diligently considered other available options, including ACMI contracts. Norse is also engaged in discussions with selected parties on short term financing, however, there can be no assurances that such discussions will result in a viable solution.

To state the obvious, this sounded really, really bad. When the company was publicly stating that its “financial situation is critical” and that it needed to secure financing to meet “short-term liquidity needs,” you knew things weren’t good. I expected we’d see something bad happen in the coming weeks, but it didn’t take that long.

Norse Atlantic has canceled its own scheduled flights effective immediately. On top of that, the airline was leasing two jets to Pakistan International Airlines, and those flights were also canceled with very little notice, “due to an unexpected operational suspension” by Norse Atlantic. Travelers were literally checking in at the airport when they realized the airline was grounded.

Due to an unexpected operational suspension by our wet-lease partner Norse Air without prior notice, tonight’s PK786 (London–Islamabad) & PK710 (Manchester–Lahore) are cancelled.We deeply regret the disruption. Efforts have been made to mitigate the impact, and from tomorrow,…

So while the company hasn’t formally announced that it’s liquidating, it’s clear that operations are at least suspended, and we’ll see how this all plays out.

The airline industry can be a funny business, and there’s no clearer example of that than Norse Atlantic Airways. The airline launched in 2022, with the goal of operating long haul, low cost flights, particularly across the Atlantic.

That wasn’t exactly a unique business model, because this was exactly what Norwegian did, before it discontinued long haul flights in 2021. Not only was the idea sort of copied, but Norse Atlantic had some executives from Norwegian, and even picked up the planes that Norwegian previously flew.

But as is all too common in the airline industry, clearly they thought it would be different this time around… it wasn’t. So in 2024 we saw the airline update its business model, making the focus less about operating regularly scheduled commercial flights, and more about leasing out aircraft, all while reducing its fleet.

The company got a lifeline when IndiGo decided to lease some Norse Atlantic 787s to dabble in long haul flying, ahead of the airline taking delivery of its own Airbus A350s. However, between all the airspace closures, high oil costs, and generally declining performance at IndiGo as the airline increasingly moved away from its core strengths, the airline recently decided to scrap that experiment, and stop leasing planes from Norse Atlantic.

Since then, opportunities for Norse Atlantic have been limited. The airline did manage to lease a couple of Dreamliners to the “new” Pakistan International Airlines, but for an airline with 12 planes, that hardly pays the bills.

Norse Atlantic did still operate some of its own flights, but in a very limited capacity. The airline had some flights from Europe to Thailand, South Africa, and the United States, but most were seasonal, and they were a fraction of what they previously were. Clearly they just didn’t have enough money to “keep the lights on” anymore.

I am curious to see what comes next for Norse Atlantic’s 787s. The airline doesn’t own them, but instead, they’ll presumably return to their leasing companies. I can’t help but point out that Southwest Airlines wants to start long haul flights. The airline had been considering a Boeing 787 order, and was possibly even looking at ways to get planes faster than from a direct order (which requires waiting for years).

Could these dozen Norse Atlantic 787s somehow end up at Southwest? The interiors would just need some updates (assuming the airline wants to offer business class), but talk about a quick way to grow a decent sized fleet.

This morning, Norse Atlantic released a statement admitting that its “financial situation is critical,” and that the company’s future is “dependent on securing financing to meet short-term liquidity needs.” That wasn’t exactly a great sign, but little did we know that the company would collapse just hours later, as the airline has now ceased all flying, including on a wet lease basis.

Financial issues at the company started when… well, it launched. Norse Atlantic was just a direct replica of Norwegian, which had failed. When the airline realized that the long haul, low cost business model doesn’t really work, they tried to pivot to wet leasing. And while there’s some demand for that, it’s also not a 100% consistent business in terms of demand.

I feel horribly for all the frontline employees at Norse Atlantic who put their best foot forward. May they find good opportunities at other airlines.
