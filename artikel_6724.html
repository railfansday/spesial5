Kate Middleton Gave a Rare Look at Her Natural Hair During a Charity Visit

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Kate Middleton is known for her polished hairstyles during royal engagements, be it elegant curls, updos, or voluminous ponytails. But the Princess of Wales embraced her natural wavy hair texture during a secret visit to Dose of Nature on October 21, according to Hello! and InStyle. Kate shared photos from the visit on Instagram, along with a reflection on her time at the nature-focused mental health charity.

“A wonderful visit to pioneering mental health charity @dose_of_nature_, which supports people experiencing mental health challenges through a clinically led program that combines psychology, connection with nature, and one-to-one guidance,” she wrote. “Founder Dr Alison Greenwood and Clinical Lead Dr Georgina Gould share how spending time in nature can help us feel both calmer and more alert, exploring the science behind this, from the sound of birdsong to air in a woodland.”

Kate has rarely let her natural hair texture take center stage over the years, but her curls did make an appearance during a 2012 visit to Tuvanipupu Island.

The Princess of Wales has long championed nature through her royal work. Last month, she announced a new project from The Royal Foundation Centre for Early Childhood focused on the natural world.

She said in a video, “New scientific research increasingly shows just how important nature is for our physical and psychological wellbeing. Nature is such an extraordinary environment for us all, and in this tech-driven, digital age, spending quality time outdoors has never been more important. This project is about exploring how nature, creativity, and relationships can best support the healthy development of babies and young children. We believe that taking a whole-child approach is important, and nature becomes a wonderful place to explore how we can successfully nurture their social and emotional skills—the skills that underpin lifelong health and wellbeing. My hope is that through this practical, nature-based research, we can build supportive environments that help make nature more accessible, benefiting babies, young children, and their families across the U.K.”
