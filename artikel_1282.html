Why the USA Withdrew From the U17 World Cup in Qatar & Who Replaced Them

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

US Soccer's decision to withdraw its team from the U17 World Cup in Qatar has brought unwanted scrutiny to the tournament. The announcement was made last Wednesday, with just over a week remaining before the opening fixtures kick off.

The American squad secured their place in the tournament earlier in 2026, ensuring they would be making a record 20th appearance at the tournament. The team qualified top of their group with a 1-1 draw in their final game against the Dominican Republic. Philadelphia Union striker Malik Jakupovic starred in qualifying, scoring eight goals — including the final, decisive goal against the Dominican Republic.

But on the verge of another major campaign, and following on from the United States' co-hosting of the 2026 World Cup, the U17 squad's efforts have now seemingly been for nothing, with US Soccer making the difficult decision to pull the plug on their participation. Now leaving a glaring hole in Group J, questions have not only been asked of US Soccer but the tournament's hosts as to why the US team have withdrawn. GIVEMESPORT have provided all the details behind the late exit and who will be replacing the United States at the tournament.

It was a decision that was made with much consideration, especially with the disappointment that would be felt by the impressive U17 squad. But US Soccer's formal notification to FIFA of its intent to withdraw went beyond reasons related to the sport and the competition itself.

US Soccer has cited escalating security concerns in the Middle East as the reason behind the decision to withdraw its team. The US Department of State issued a Level Three travel advisory for Qatar last month, advising Americans traveling to the country to reconsider due to the risk of armed conflict. Further instability in the region, including missile strikes in neighboring Saudi Arabia, also influenced the travel advisory.

In a letter sent to players and coaching staff and seen by news agency Reuters, US Soccer stated that hostile behavior in the region has "the potential to escalate rapidly beyond that immediate area."

"The wellbeing of our players and staff will always be our top priority," US Soccer emphasized in the letter.

Whilst still a shock to the international football community, tournament withdrawals are not unheard of. Coincidentally, North Korea's U17 team withdrew from the U17 Asian Cup earlier this year. In 1950, India, Scotland, and Turkey — who had all qualified for that year's World Cup — all withdrew for various reasons.

Scotland infamously withdrew after stating that they would only travel to the tournament in Brazil if they qualified as British Home Champions. After finishing second in the British Home Championship behind England, the SFA held their stance and refused to send the team despite qualifying for the World Cup. India and Turkey both cited financial constraints as their reasons behind their withdrawal from the same tournament.

The World Cup is set to be bigger than ever next year but a handful of nations still missed out on the chance to qualify.

They were frustrated by the United States in their final qualifying fixture, but the US team's loss is the Dominican Republic's gain.

The Caribbean island finished second in Group E of the 2026 CONCACAF U17 World Cup qualification stage, missing out on goal difference alone. Their inability to find a way past the stubborn US defence, playing out a 1-1 draw in the decisive final group game, meant that the Dominican Republic went agonisingly close to qualifying.

But with the US team's withdrawal, the Caribbean nation will now make its history-making debut at the U17 World Cup. Under FIFA competition regulations, the governing body holds the right to appoint a replacement team when a qualified nation withdraws. The Dominican Republic earned the spot due to its strong showing during the qualifiers.

As well as holding the US to a 1-1 draw, the Dominican Republic prolifically defeated the other two teams in Group E. A 10-0 win over the Grenadines and a 5-0 win over Saint Kitts and Nevis marked the country out as one of the best performing teams that failed to qualify.

The U17 squad will now head to Qatar to join Group J, where they will battle it out against Algeria, Chile, and Montenegro for a place in the knockout phase. And the team have a number of standout players who will be looking to make a name for themselves on the international stage.

Striker Adrian Garcia Jaquez scored a number of crucial goals in qualifying, including the equaliser against the US. He is a part of La Liga 2 side Leganes' youth setup. There's also centre back Alejandro Lopez, who spent seven years in the Atletico Madrid academy before moving to Rayo Vallecano. He earned his first senior cap for the Dominican Republic earlier this year. Goalkeeper Aidan Betances has also been impressive, conceding just one goal in qualifying. He is currently a part of the New York Red Bulls academy.

US Soccer have withdrawn its Under-17 men's national team from the upcoming FIFA World Cup in Qatar.
