7 Costco Foods You Should Buy Early For Thanksgiving—And 5 You Should Skip

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Visit the warehouse before it becomes a madhouse.

It’s November, so it’s officially time to plan and shop for your Thanksgiving menu. And if you’re planning to feed a crowd and would prefer to stay within budget, it’s no surprise that Costco is one of the best places to stock up on ingredients. That is, if they’re even in stock.

The warehouse is more like a madhouse in the days leading up to major holidays. Shopping early allows you to check everything off your list and spares you a lot of stress later this month. However, not all ingredients are worth buying in advance.

I write about food and Costco for a living, and as much as I love the bulk warehouse, I can still be honest about what you shouldn’t buy. Here are the warehouse essentials I’d recommend buying early for your Thanksgiving dinner—and the things I’d skip.

Williams Sonoma suggests serving anywhere from half to an entire bottle of wine per guest at Thanksgiving. And if your local Costco sells booze, the warehouse often carries solid-quality bottles at a fraction of the retail price.

From gravy to stuffing, chicken stock serves as the foundation of some essential holiday foods. Kirkland Signature’s shelf-stable cartons are a great thing to buy ahead of time so you’re ready to go on Thanksgiving.

If you want to avoid washing your fine china and pressing linen napkins, disposable plates, silverware, and napkins are key. Costco is currently offering a wide range of options. Whether you want festive, fall-themed paper plates or elegant heavyweight plastic ones, you can save a lot of money on your tablescape by using Costco serving supplies.

If you’re baking desserts from scratch this year, Costco is the ideal spot to stock up on ingredients. From vanilla extract to pecans to cans of pumpkin puree, the warehouse carries everything you need in sizes large enough to feed your whole extended family.

Whether you’re serving them mashed, scalloped, or marshmallow-topped, you’ll most likely serve some sort of potato at your Thanksgiving feast. A 10-pound bag is one of those no-brainer Costco purchases that you can buy well before the holiday. Just keep the spuds in a cool, dark area until Thanksgiving.

If you’re starting Thanksgiving shopping early, some dairy products won’t last until the big day. Hard cheese is an exception. Costco sells huge blocks of cheddar and Parmesan that can stay unopened and fresh in the fridge for weeks. By the time Turkey Day rolls around, it’ll all be ready to go for homemade mac and cheese, scalloped potatoes, and appetizer spreads.

Butter belongs in virtually every dish on a traditional Thanksgiving menu. And these days, it’s not cheap at standard supermarkets. Thankfully, you can buy it in bulk at Costco. Kirkland Signature’s butter is just as good as brand-name, and it lasts for weeks unopened in the fridge.

Potatoes are sturdy and durable enough to sit in your pantry for weeks, but that doesn't apply to most other vegetables. Leafy greens, herbs, and pre-trimmed green beans will get nasty fast. I’d recommend skipping them on your initial trip and going back closer to the holiday to grab your vegetables.

When you’re shopping ahead of time, I’d recommend steering clear of most baked goods—especially bread—unless you plan to freeze them. Both dinner rolls and full-sized loaves will go stale before Thanksgiving. And, contrary to popular belief, you actually shouldn’t use stale bread in stuffing.

It should go without saying. If you buy milk for Thanksgiving recipes weeks ahead of time, it’ll end up more sour than unsweetened cranberry sauce.

I have mixed feelings about Costco’s pumpkin pie in general, specifically with the no-butter crust. That said, it also doesn’t hold up well if you buy it at least a week in advance. The custardy filling tends to leach moisture and give you the dreaded soggy bottom. If you insist on serving a Costco dessert this year, go for the pumpkin cheesecake instead and freeze it until Turkey Day.

This point comes with some caveats. Costco turkeys are undoubtedly a good deal, but buying them early is not always practical for some households. If you’re buying it ahead of time, the turkey should definitely be frozen to prevent spoilage. But unless you have plenty of freezer space (or a separate chest freezer), I wouldn’t recommend buying a turkey that far in advance.
