SolSmart celebrates 10 years of helping local governments go solar

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

SolSmart, a national program that helps local governments remove barriers to solar energy adoption, today marks its tenth anniversary. Since 2016, SolSmart has helped over 600 local governments and regional organizations cut red tape, increase energy choice for residents and strengthen community energy resilience. SolSmart is a free technical assistance program supported by the U.S. Department of Energy and co-led by the Interstate Renewable Energy Council (IREC) and the International City/County Management Association (ICMA).

SolSmart offers free expert technical assistance to help local governments adopt nationally recognized best practices to advance solar energy adoption. With electricity costs rising rapidly and a sixth of U.S. households behind on their utility bills, SolSmart helps local governments reduce energy costs for residents. Solar projects can help meet rising electricity demands at lower costs than other energy sources, but only if local processes like permitting and inspection allow them to come online efficiently and affordably. SolSmart enables this by helping communities streamline policies while maintaining safety and reliability.

Participating communities can earn graded designations by taking specific steps to reduce barriers to solar adoption, such as improving permitting and inspection practices and expanding information for residents. Designation provides national recognition of communities’ work to expand energy options for residents and businesses, while providing measurable benefits. Independent research, published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, found that SolSmart designation is associated with an 18–19% increase in solar capacity per month compared to non-designated communities.

“The city of Athens is proud to be one of the first communities in the country to pursue SolSmart designation,” said Steve Patterson mayor of Athens, Ohio. “When we first achieved Bronze designation in October 2017, we knew this was only the beginning of our residential sustainable energy story. Since then, we received SolSmart Gold designation in 2023 and, in 2025, became the first SolSmart Platinum community in Ohio, the 4th City in the country, and have added over 2 megawatts of solar capacity to our municipal facilities, with much more planned for the future. We continue to see a steady increase in solar installations in the City and know this trend will continue thanks to our SolSmart efforts.

More than 600 local governments across 44 states have received designations through the SolSmart program, in places such as Fort Worth, Texas, Iowa City, Iowa, and Olympia, Washington.

“Local governments are at the heart of clean energy adoption around the country. Policies on topics like permitting and inspection of solar installations can make or break residents’ access to solar,” said Brandy O’Quinn, program director of local initiatives at IREC. “When local governments take steps to remove unintended barriers to solar adoption, the results are significant.”

The SolSmart team is hosting a digital event on October 29 at 2 p.m. ET to share information about its 2026 SolSmart Accelerator.

“The SolSmart program provides free hands-on support to help hardworking local government staff align their local rules with national best practices that cut red tape while maintaining strong safety standards,” said Debra Perry, Clean Energy Program Director at ICMA. “As communities grapple with rising energy costs, increasing solar access can be a valuable way to support residents’ energy choices and advance affordability. We look forward to supporting many more communities, and invite interested local governments to access our free support.”
