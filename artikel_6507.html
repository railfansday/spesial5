This Harvest Caesar Salad Is The Fall Side Everyone Asks Me To Bring

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

My love for Caesar salad knows no bounds, but in the fall, this harvest version is a STAPLE in my rotation. I first made it for a Friendsgiving last year, and people loved it so much that I ended up making it for my own Thanksgiving and Christmas celebrations, too. Every time, friends and family begged me for the recipe—it’s that good.

The best thing about it? It only requires a couple of easy ingredient upgrades to turn the classic salad into a fall favorite—here’s what makes it truly special.

As you’ve probably realized, this isn’t your plain Jane Caesar salad. There are two easy swaps that make this salad a side dish star. First, instead of romaine as the base, this salad lets kale shine. If you’re not a kale fan, stay with me. Once it’s massaged down with lemon juice and salt, it becomes perfectly tender and downright delicious—it’s the ideal hearty base for this salad.

The second and most important swap? Instead of sourdough croutons, this salad roasts pieces of butternut squash on a bed of freshly shredded Parm to create crispy frico squash “croutons.” As soon as I removed the tray from the oven and started breaking up the cheesy squash pieces, I had to remind myself that I couldn’t eat them all on their own—at least a few had to actually go in the salad.

This salad requires a few extra steps, but they’re quick and well worth it. First, make the Caesar dressing and refrigerate it until you’re ready to assemble. If you don’t have the time, a store-bought dressing will do just fine.

While that’s chilling, you’ll prepare the butternut squash croutons by cutting up your squash, shredding some Parm on an oiled baking sheet (don't use the pre-shredded kind—it just won’t melt the same), placing your squash on top, and baking until the cheese is crisp and browned on the bottom.

After you massage the kale with lemon and salt (don’t be stingy here—put those muscles to good use), all that’s left is to assemble! I highly recommend assembling in stages—dressing with the kale, then half the squash and Parmesan—before serving with the rest of the squash, Parmesan, and remaining dressing on the side for anyone who prefers more dressing than salad (again, guilty!).
