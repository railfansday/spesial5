10 Games to Play While Everyone Else is Playing GTA6

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Elena Chapella is a Writer for DualShockers. Previously an award-winning journalist, Elena is passionate about writing what she loves.

To say that Grand Theft Auto 6 is the most anticipated game of the year is a colossal understatement, with the game already pulling in billions of dollars in pre-order revenue. However, that doesn’t mean everyone is going to be playing it.

Players could be waiting for a few patches before getting it for themselves (rather than dealing with any potential game-breaking bugs upon release), waiting for the game to go on sale (rather than paying the $80-100 full price), waiting for it to come to PC, or they may just not be interested. Either way, there are all kinds of reasons that will keep someone from playing GTA6 despite it finally coming out.

Want a world of rich lore and exploration while not having too much time on your hands? We've got you covered.

With that in mind, these players should have something to keep them occupied while others are diving into the huge title. They could play other games that recently came out, especially since this year has seen many incredible releases.

So while everyone else continues to carry the hype of GTA6, you can show other 2026 entries the same amount of love.

If you were to break open any open world game to see what lives inside, you’d find a lot of the typical elements: quest-giving NPCs, tons of exploration, a healthy amount of combat and challenge. You get that with Crimson Desert if you were to crack open its world, but you also get some of the most unique expressions an open world can take.

The game is anything but a typical open world game, with zero slog to dredge through and an unparalleled approach to such a massive world. Seriously, as massive as GTA6 is, you’re going to be spending an equal amount of time (if not a little more) in Crimson Desert’s world.

This is one of those games where you need a good amount of time to properly sit with it, wandering the world and taking its challenge like a champ. It’s not like other open world games where you can get an idea of the world based on 15 minutes of gameplay, you’re going to need closer to 15 months before you can cement your opinion.

But I’ll just spoil the opinion for you: it’s going to be so, so cool, to where you’re going to actually be upset about it being snubbed at The Game Awards.

If you grew up playing the Halo games, you’d be pleased to see that Halo: Campaign Evolved is everything you loved about them and then some. Not only is it an incredibly faithful remake for newcomers, but it also adds new content for veteran fans, meaning everybody has something they can properly enjoy.

Its singleplayer focus may be irksome for many FPS fans, but if you adored the original campaign, you’re in for the time of your life.

So sure, you may have waited 13 years for GTA6, but your inner child waited forever to feel that same magic from Halo again – and once you give the remake a chance, you absolutely will feel it throughout your entire soul.

Just don’t think about the current state of Halo, it’s better to live in ignorant bliss.

Silent Hill has been on an incredible winning streak lately, and it’s not looking to be stopping anytime soon, especially with the series’ newest entry. Silent Hill: Townfall is the most recent entry in the franchise, and it’s already carrying the torch (and tone) wonderfully.

Taking place in St. Amelia (a fictional island town in Scotland) rather than the titular town, you play as Simon Ordell as he goes through the tragedy of what happened there. Plus, you have to sneak around and hide to avoid monsters, a slight shift away from the typical survival horror tactics that the series has featured so far.

The entire game is an unforgettable experience from start to finish, with intricate and creative puzzles that will genuinely stump you and music that only amplifies your tense mood. Combine this with the incredible narrative (as standard for the franchise), deep characters (that too) and a new point of view, it’s a game that will stick with you.

If anything, if you’re a hardcore Silent Hill fan like I am, this game will just get you all the more excited for the upcoming Silent Hill Remake and future original titles.

It’s natural to feel protective over children, even in video games – but here comes Pragmata, putting you in charge of the child-robot Diana. You're not ready for the emotional bond.

As you and Diana explore the abandoned lunar base taken over by hostile AI, there are all kinds of things that you both can do: hacking, running and gunning, upgrading your abilities, teaching Diana the ways of humanity, and even dancing.

I just wish we could dance along with Diana; dancing is a joy that’s meant to be shared, and it’s far more fun to jump around with others, especially if we’re dancing with our robot daughter in space.

But as you’re both trying to get out of space and back to Earth together, you’re going to feel warm and fuzzy at every interaction along the way. It's also just an engrossing, action-packed, and creative adventure, from a new IP.

In the past few years, we’ve seen an increase in “dark wuxia” style of games, and Phantom Blade Zero seems to be our newest release in the subgenre. It’s an action game set in a literal phantom world, and you are armed with countless types of blades to help you along your journey … oh, and you’re also killer at kung-fu.

Devs describe the game as “kungfupunk,” and it’s honestly the perfect description of it. It looks good, feels good, plays good – it’s a fun time all around, with the added bonus of impeccable aesthetics from an alternate, dark fantasy version of China’s Ming Dynasty. It creates an action game that sets the bar, a new standard for games to look up to.

It walks a delicate balance between a genuine challenge and action-packed fun, and players would be happy with the entire gameplay experience.

Combined with incredible cinematography, and we have a contender for Game of the Year that’s more than well-deserved. Regardless of what players believe the Game of the Year should be, I firmly believe that this game should win, at the very least, Best Action Game and Best Art Direction.

From bizarre indies to daring blockbusters, these are the ten most extravagant video games of 2026.

Fans of the first Control game have been eagerly waiting for a sequel that’s as good as the original, and that’s exactly what we get with Control Resonant. Instead of playing as Jesse Faden, you actually play as her brother, Dylan, as he tries to save New York from anomalies – but don’t worry if you were a fan, you’ll still see her.

Cool, action-packed and undeniably wild, fans of the first Control will be happy to see that the sequel kept everything they know and love, as well as adding so much more extra flavouring (and breathtaking graphics).

It’s a direct continuation, but amplifies everything to be just better than before, including the clear Stephen King inspiration. In fact, most players are going to find themselves liking this game more than the first.

Just saying, there’s a reason that it’s a contender for Game of the Year and several other categories – and I would honestly say that it’s more than well-deserved. It’s hard to live up to such a unique hype like with Control, yet this game seems to manage the impossible.

While everyone is fighting against the police in GTA6, Gears of War fans have been fighting the Locust Horde for years; in Gears of War: E-Day, we see how this all begins. And if you’ve been a veteran Gears fan, you’re going to be pleased to know that it’s as fun as always – but then again, even if you’re a newer Gears fan, you’re still going to have a great time for entirely different reasons.

By coming to Unreal Engine 5, the game ends up going through much-needed revisions in the gameplay, especially when it comes to verticality. Yet, in spite of the adjustments, it’s still a great return to form.

Some players have complained about bugs and crashes, but they’re actually fairly few and far between, and most have been patched since its release. If one were to dive into the game now, the most they’d run into is Brumak deciding that a building is incredibly fascinating and needs a solid bit of observation.

With that in mind, this is an intense, yet super fun game that players will find a ton of time for, especially if they have been staring at it in their Play Later List.

This year has been a great year for the Resident Evil IP, with a killer movie that continues to be gushed over by fans and this year’s fantastic leading title: Resident Evil Requiem. And, to be completely honest, this is my personal pick for Game of the Year.

The game has you playing as two different characters: Grace Ashcroft, whose sections are the traditional horror experience that will have you genuinely terrified, and Leon Kennedy, whose sections are an action-packed power trip. Together, the two of them have to figure out the truth behind the newest virus, Elpis, and what to do about Raccoon City Syndrome.

It’s action horror in the purest sense: a perfect balance of horror and action, yet maintaining a focus on the horror element first and foremost. It’s executed fantastically, and I don’t think I’ve ever had as much fun playing Resident Evil as I had with Requiem.

If players were to compare all the recent Resident Evil titles in the modern era (including remakes), they’d notice something special about Requiem: it takes the best of all of them, and takes the crown as the best modern entry the series has seen so far. If anything, this makes me incredibly excited for what future RE games have in store.

If you love Star Wars and XCOM, you’re going to be over the moon with Star Wars Zero Company. It’s a genuinely perfect marriage, a blend between the two that will have fans of both IPs weeping with joy.

If you’ve ever wanted to feel like the commander of a clone army, that’s exactly the experience that you’ll have with this game: but you’re not limited to having clones in your party. Your squad can be whoever you want, and you can implement whichever strategies you need to in order to be victorious.

Many players might hear about these kind of real-time strategy games and imagine a top-down, board-like UI rather than anything more dynamic, but that’s not how it is in Zero Company. In fact, the game is incredibly cinematic, immersing you in ways that you normally wouldn’t be if it was any other RTS title.

Basically, if you’re looking for the game to have the ultimate nerd-out with, look no further.

Nintendo knows how to keep a loyal fanbase like with the Zelda franchise, and that grip has only gotten stronger with the recent release of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake.

This remake isn’t like the 3DS ports that just improve what’s already there, no, this is a remake that’s rebuilt from the ground-up, and it’s just as incredible as we remember from our childhood. In fact, many fans (including veteran fans of the OG) would argue that it’s better than the original. Of course, there are purists, but purists will always have purist takes.

It’s hard to recapture the magic of the original, yet somehow, the remake manages to make the game feel even more magical, but with more updated gameplay (more reminiscent of Breath of the Wild) for modern players and better graphics. Seriously, what is not to love about a classic like this one?

Personally, I do think that Zelda can (and very likely will) outperform GTA6 at The Game Awards, because while GTA6 was easily the most anticipated game of all time, Ocarina of Time was, is, and always will be the most iconic game of all time.

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time is one of the most beloved games of all time, but these remakes might be just as meaningful to fans.
