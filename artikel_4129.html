Xenoblade Chronicles 3 Version 3.0.0 Is Now Live, Here Are The Patch Notes

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Ahead of the launch of Xenoblade Chronicles 3's Switch 2 Edition on 3rd December 2026, Nintendo and Monolith Soft have this week rolled out a new game update.

This patch officially bumps the title up to Version 3.0.0. Nothing else is mentioned and it's further noted how "no changes" have been made when playing the game on the Switch. Here's the rundown from Nintendo's official support page:

The following updates have been made when playing the game on Nintendo Switch 2:

The following updates have been made when playing the game on Nintendo Switch:

As mentioned by Nintendo, the Switch 2 Edition adds the mythical new hero, Shimmer. There's also the new mode 'Heroes' Vault', exclusive to the Switch 2 Edition.

Apart from this, the upgrade will come with support for 4K resolution in TV mode, 60fps performance, and event scenes have been updated to be "fully voiced in English and Japanese".

If you're planning on upgrading from the Switch version, the upgrade will cost $9.99 / £7.99 or your regional equivalent.

@squiddu-real Fair enough, at least you're self-aware about the bias—most people online won't even admit that, and honestly, it can happen to anyone. What helps me is putting myself in the developers' shoes. You don't need to be a full-on programmer, but even a rough grasp of how this stuff works puts a lot of it into perspective. And even without that background, it helps to step back and ask why people are reacting the way they are: was waiting a month for a patch really that long? Does the game actually look bad, or did people just set unrealistic expectations?

With Xenoblade Chronicles X, even I'll agree they pushed that Switch 2 Edition out too fast and it needed more time in the oven. But with XC1 and XC2, while they aren't utterly flawless, I couldn't see a real reason to complain—they objectively look and run better, which is the whole point of an upgrade.

On top of that, an 8.8 user score (basically 88%) isn't "mixed" by any definition. If you go digging on forums or social media, you're always going to find people looking for things to complain about—especially these days, where it feels like half the internet refuses to just enjoy a game without picking it apart. Loud complaints online don't erase the majority of players who had a great time with it.

Also, bumping an older game to 60 FPS and adding new modes isn't as simple as people think. Games from that era often tie audio timing, physics, and system flags directly to framerate, so changing how the game runs can break things in weird, unpredictable ways.

As for the XC1 dialogue quirk: no, it's really not a bad look. It's so inconsistent that plenty of people never trigger it—or don't even notice when it happens. I didn't even know it existed until someone brought it up, and I played through the game on both Switch 1 and Switch 2. Low-priority edge cases like that rarely get touched when a team is working on top of older base code, mostly because digging into legacy scripts to fix a tiny quirk can easily risk breaking something much bigger.
