Grand Theft Auto 6 Review: a Once in a Generation Open-World Achievement

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Grand Theft Auto 6 delivers an enormous open world, a gripping story, a vibrant Vice City and an astonishing range of activities.

If there’s one thing Rockstar can never be accused of, it’s rushing out Grand Theft Auto VI. It’s been so long since the (still incredible) GTA V launched that there are people who weren’t born at the time who are now teenagers (no, you’re not old enough to play yet, son, and go to bed!).

It’s not like Rockstar wasn’t busy during that time — they’ve been constantly updating the ever-growing Grand Theft Auto Online and also happened to release one of the greatest westerns of all time in Red Dead Redemption II. Yet all of the many years of development somehow becomes immediately apparent the second you pass the title screen. Put simply, this is one of the largest and most ambitious games ever created.

GTA 6 centers around the complicated personal and professional relationship of two criminals, Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, and their journey through the realistically hard-to-succeed-in world of Vice City. That’s about the only story element I’m comfortable sharing, however, as this is a game that begs not to be spoiled and to be experienced as fresh as possible.

GTA VI’s main story is full of crazy twists and turns, surprising emotional beats, and yes, absolutely hilarious moments that push boundaries and envelopes. Along the way, you’ll meet a cast of colorful characters that will quickly endear themselves to you and ones you’d commit many virtual felonies to defeat out of seething hatred.

While the campaign is gripping and exciting all the way through, there are so many stories just waiting to be discovered in GTA VI. It feels like a living, breathing world which is all the more impressive considering there are basically no loading times or slow down upon entering a loaded save.

There’s a ton of traffic through the roads (so much so that you can’t just easily blaze through like a bowling ball as easily as in previous games), the drivers get actively mad if you crash into them, and there are NPCs going about their days on every corner. You could make a whole game into simply approaching NPCs and seeing what they’re up to — there are some surprisingly interesting happenings going on and it’s rare that even the most minor of characters repeats themselves.

From DMA Design to GTA III, GTA V, and RDR2, let's take a look back at how Rockstar Games changed open-world gaming and why GTA 6 has so much to live up to.

While you can follow the main story all the way through, complete tons of side quests or simply take part in what the most depraved of us do and make the game into a rampage simulator, there are so many activities here that almost any one of them could be focused on as a full game. There’s diving for treasure, bird watching, fishing, customizing cars and oh so many other things that are fully fleshed out and not just throwaway afterthoughts.

In fact, just existing in the game is so complex now that GTA has basically become a life sim. Upon awakening, you can eat, make coffee, watch TV, play video games, workout, watch a show, do home repairs, feed pets and enough other tasks to make a list of them as long as a full review.

It’s incredibly cool for the world to feel so realistic that you literally sometimes get stressed out from knowing you have to do a chore and can’t just go run people down (I guess I am one of the depraved ones after all). Sometimes an hour is spent in GTA VI literally lifting weights, drinking a protein drink, grocery shopping and laying down in bed doomscrolling. While that might not exactly be the sentence to sell you on the game, the fact that GTA VI is just that deep and manages to make it all enjoyable to carry out truly speaks to the attention to detail at play.

The realism also carries over into the gameplay mechanics themselves. Driving feels more realistic than it ever has. It’s easy to get wanted stars and hard to shake the cops, and people will get actively pissed off at you for doing the wrong thing. In fact, it’s realistic to the point that some might not have as much fun as they did in previous titles as you will think twice before hijacking a random car or even parking it in the wrong place (yes, the fuzz can be called on you for leaving your car at an NPC’s house).

Just existing in the game is so complex now that GTA has basically become a life sim.

It goes beyond simply just blazing through the world doing horrible things and hoping a cop doesn’t see it — there are immediate and severe consequences to your actions now.

That being said, it’s hard to criticize Rockstar for the game being too realistic, and once you adapt to its ebb and flow, this new realistic GTA experience feels all the more enveloping as it stimulates the anxiety, stress and fear synapses as much as the happiness ones, making successful accomplishments in the game all the more rewarding.

It’s impossible to review GTA VI without touching on the music, and if I had my way, this entire review would just be speaking about that. But as I don’t want to risk you clicking out since we could really use those GTA page views, I’ll keep it short.

The score itself is composed by famed French electronic duo, Justice, and is like an aural hug to the ears. The instrumentals during key action scenes are some of the best heard since HEALTH’s stunning Max Payne 3 score and get the adrenaline pumping.

The included original songs Justice recorded are so expertly crafted to sound like ‘80s hits that I was literally googling the lyrics thinking I had found long lost ‘80s songs before realizing it was actually Justice. Considering I’m big Justice fan who’ve seen their live show multiple times, that says a lot that they even fooled me.

Rockstar has released GTA 6’s launch trailer, spotlighting Jason and Lucia while confirming Justice as the composers of its original score.

It’d be fair to judge a GTA by its radio stations as they play such an integral part in the experience and are heard for literally hundreds of hours by most players, so thankfully there’s another incredible lineup here. There’s a channel for everybody — ranging from buttrock to country to reggae to classical music to many, many more.

Fans of past channels like Radio Broker and Radio Mirror Park will be happy to hear that there’s another hipster/indie style channel in GTA VI dubbed “Stockyard FM” which plays music from the likes of Hot Chip, Nation of Language, !!! and even debuts a new song from the aforementioned HEALTH. Flash FM quickly becomes a favorite as well, focusing on high energy dance pop and electro pop from artists like Billie Eilish, The Ting Tings and more.

Finally, Circoloco Records is a commendable addition featuring smooth throwback-style electronic music from Rockstar’s own record label. But expect to hear a fantastically curated soundtrack no matter which station you jam out to, with artists ranging anywhere from The Weeknd to Steve Winwood.

With how many flawlessly-executed mechanics and elements to GTA 6 there is, it’s hard to find faults. There’s not even many glitches that occur, which is shocking for an open world game of the magnitude.

Possibly the only criticism would be that it's so complex and ambitious that the learning curve is steep, and even after dozens of hours in the game, it’s easy to forget how a certain mechanic works or which button does what at times. Of course, criticizing something for being so deep proves difficult and at the very least perhaps a good problem to have in the end.

Rockstar has minimized the complexity as much as they could by providing an easy-to-access control list at all times and basically devoting the first twenty or so hours of the story as a backdoor tutorial.

Considering how enormous the scope of GTA 6 is, it’d be easy to forgive it for having visuals not up to snuff, yet it boasts some of the most realistic character models seen in gaming and uses them throughout all of the beautifully-choreographed in-game cutscenes.

The way the characters are rendered give them a certain unique realistic element that has never quite been captured before — from unique body types to random skin marks to life behind the eyes. As good as the characters look, the environments might look even better with everything from trees to the pavement itself rich in detail. There’s also incredible lighting work at play that makes everything pop like you’d expect Florida, er, Vice City to.

While it may seem impossible for anything in our universe to live up to the hype and anticipation Grand Theft Auto 6 has garnered, Rockstar has done the impossible. GTA VI offers more in a single package than any video game before it, blowing past its status as one of the best action games of all time into one of the best life-sims of all time. And one of the best driving games of all time. And one of the best…birding…games of all time? It may sound like hyperbole, but GTA VI is so chock full of content that it feels like several fully-fleshed out games in one. And that’s not to mention it actually does have several fully-fleshed out games within a game inside of it. GTA VI may not completely reinvent the wheel, but it fully commands it, delivering an enormous adventure that’s chock-full of enough things to keep you busy until Grand Theft Auto VII (please, Rockstar?). The last thirteen years were more than worth the wait and make Grand Theft Auto VI go down as an unparalleled achievement in video games.
