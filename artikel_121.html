British man recalls ‘despair’ over son’s death after fall from Cyprus window

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Joshua Foster tells of moment he ‘lost control’ of two-year-old when he fell from fourth storey of hotel

A British father who was fined by the courts after his two-year-old son fell from a fourth-floor window during a family holiday in Cyprus has recalled his “despair” when the boy slipped from his grasp.

Joshua Foster was handed a €3,000 (£2,500) penalty after he pleaded guilty to a charge of causing death through a reckless, rash or dangerous act. Prosecutors in Cyprus had refused to drop the case against him.

During recent court proceedings, it was heard that Foster thought the window at the King Evelthon Beach Hotel and Resort in Chlorakas was closed when he approached it with his son, Jaxon, to show him the view of a waterpark.

The 37-year-old “lost control” of him, the Paphos district court was told, before the boy plunged 10 metres (33ft). The fall happened on 12 July, the first day of the Fosters’ family holiday.

In an interview with the Daily Mail, Foster said: “I have a burden. I’ll have that my whole life. It’s very traumatic. My mind doesn’t want to go there. I just remember the despair of him falling, and watching him fall.

“It was just such a shock … I put him up thinking he was going against a glass pane … and then he wasn’t there.”

The toddler was pronounced dead at Paphos general hospital shortly after he suffered severe cranial injuries when he hit a veranda below.

Foster recalled his outpouring of emotion when he located his son after the fall. “I picked him up. I held him and screamed,” he said. The Essex-born auditor said he and his wife had an hour with their son’s body after he was pronounced dead.

The decision to charge Foster, reported to be a senior accountant at KPMG, was criticised by his legal representatives, as well as human rights defenders, media pundits and diplomats, arguing that it lacked compassion.

The charge Foster accepted is usually levelled in cases involving fatal traffic accidents. It carries a maximum penalty of four years in prison, but the fine he was handed was the lightest sentence possible for the offence.

At his sentencing in October, Judge Froso Christodoulidou ruled that Foster would not be jailed, before describing the incident as a “tragic event”.

The court had heard that, while the presence of alcohol had been detected in Foster’s blood, it was not enough to suggest he was impaired to the point where he had “acted recklessly”.

Foster was detained after the fall. He reportedly had to tell his eldest child, a daughter, about her brother’s death via a video call while he was being held.

He posted €30,000 (£26,000) in bail to return to the UK to attend the funeral of his son in August, but was forced, under threat of extradition, to return to Cyprus and surrender his passport by 14 September.

In a push for clemency, Foster’s legal team had appealed in September to the island’s attorney general, Cyprus’s top legal official, to discontinue the criminal procedure on humanitarian grounds. The request was denied.

Since returning home, Foster said the focus of his grieving family had turned to their daughter.

“It’s very difficult,” he said. “She waves at the sky and says: ‘I love you’ to him and blows a kiss. She’s lost her best friend. She’s gone from having a sibling to being an only child.”
