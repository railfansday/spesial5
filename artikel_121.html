How Ukraine struck the Kerch bridge, a symbol of Putin’s rule

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

With the SpaceX satellite system faltering, Ukraine’s military launched a surprise, multi-pronged attack to take down vital bridge to Crimea

At 10pm on Monday evening a Ukrainian soldier scrawled a message on a kamikaze drone. It said simply: “The bridge is fucked.” He then added: “We will stand and Moscow will fall.” It was a freezing night, with a bright starry sky and no clouds. Lines of ice had formed on the drone’s bulky wings. Soon, it would be hurtling towards its target – about 500km to the south.

Typically the soldiers who gathered in this muddy field in central Ukraine were attacking oil refineries deep inside Russia. In recent months the work had become almost mundane: they had hit so many of them. The objective that evening, however, was unique. It was a structure personally associated with Vladimir Putin and his grandiose imperial ambitions.

“We knew what the target was. Everyone was enthusiastic and excited,” one person who took part in the special operation, using the pseudonym “Leon”, told the Guardian. His 7th Deep Strike Centre is part of “Magyar’s Birds”, a brigade named after the call sign of its founder, Robert Brovdi, the head of Ukraine’s unmanned systems forces. Leon said: “Nobody gets excited any more about oil refineries. This was different.”

The soldiers got ready to launch, then watched the biplane-like drone, laden with explosives, disappear across the southern Ukrainian steppe.

Over the next four hours, hundreds of attack drones followed the same linear path, flying first over land and then over the Black Sea. In the early hours of the morning they arrived at their destination: the Kerch bridge connecting occupied Crimea with Russia. Putin opened the monumental road and rail crossing in 2018, driving across it in a Kamaz truck. This was four years after his undercover troops – known as little green men – seized the peninsula.

Russian propagandists had described the bridge as impregnable. It was, they said, an edifice as strong and unyielding as their country’s iron leader. For Ukraine, by contrast, it was a hated symbol of Russian arrogance and colonial thinking. Also, officials pointed out, it was illegal. Twice Kyiv had tried to blow it up – with a truck bomb in 2022, and a sea drone the following year.

On this occasion, the Ukrainian military’s careful, multi-stage plan worked perfectly. “It was a well-coordinated operation. Waves of drones started from different locations. The goal was to overwhelm Russia’s well-organised air defence system, rather than the bridge itself,” Leon explained.

As expected, the initial salvo tied up Russia’s local defences. According to insiders, Ukraine then launched a second drone wave. Its function was crucial and different: to act as a relay system for radio signals. Previously, Ukrainian units had used Starshield, the military equivalent of Starlink, for mid-range strikes on occupied territory. Elon Musk’s SpaceX owns the satellite internet network, which is overseen by the Pentagon and the Trump administration.

Ten days ago, however, limits were mysteriously imposed on Ukrainian drones, so they could no longer fly at above 161 km/h (100 mph). SpaceX insists there is no slowdown – a statement Ukraine’s president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, says is untrue. On Monday night, it appears Kyiv bypassed US restrictions by setting up its own extraordinary and innovative real-time video and radio connection.

Once the relay was in place, a third wave of attacks could begin. This time they featured Flamingos, a potent Ukrainian-made cruise missile capable of carrying 1,000kg of explosives – enough to seriously damage the bridge. An unknown number of missiles – some reports say at least four – were launched from southern Ukraine and sent towards Crimea.

A video from one of the Flamingos shows it drawing inexorably towards the target. The onboard camera captures a ghostly night-time scene. The crossing looms into view against a grisaille background. Suddenly a gigantic steel arch appears. The feed cuts out. Other footage shows the moment of impact: an orange fireball, pricking the surrounding blackness.

Photos taken on Tuesday morning in the aftermath of the attack show a scene of watery devastation. A massive arch had tumbled overnight into the Kerch straight, with a large concrete chunk of the road crossing. Nobody will be driving across the bridge anytime soon. The adjacent railway arch span was also damaged. “We were very happy, swapping messages all night,” Leon said.

One source in Ukraine’s armed forces said the timing of the operation – hailed by Zelenskyy as “unprecedented” – was deliberate. It was conducted ahead of a meeting on Thursday between Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Putin in the Chinese city of Shenzhen, on the sidelines of an Apec summit. “The bridge is a symbol of Putin’s rule. Destroying it sends a message to the other two great leaders that Putin is not as strong as he pretends,” the person said.

They added: “We understand that what we did shows the world that the king is nude. Putin is weak and vulnerable and we just proved it. The operation was purposefully designed for this specific historic moment. Putin’s plan was to bluff at the summit about how powerful he is. In reality, he can’t even protect the most important and symbolic pillars of his regime.”

In addition to Putin’s political embarrassment before his peers, the disabling of the Kerch bridge has practical consequences. Since annexing Crimea, Russia has militarised the peninsula, effectively turning it into a giant and fortified army base. Since 2022 it has been used as a launch platform for drone and missile attacks on the port of Odesa, and other Ukrainian cities in the region.

Russia’s occupying southern army has relied on the bridge for regular deliveries of ammunition, equipment and fuel. With the crossing partially closed – rail travel reportedly reopened on Tuesday – Russia will have to use an under fire land corridor instead, connecting its Rostov oblast with the Moscow-controlled parts of Ukraine’s Zaporizhzhia and Kherson provinces.

“We have disrupted their logistics to Crimea. There will be a lot of problems for Russia’s army in the south,” Leon predicted. Debris from the bridge strike is obstructing the narrow shipping corridor through the Kerch strait, linking the Sea of Azov and the Black Sea. The Azov sea now resembles a lake. Passenger ferries are unable to move, with the occupied ports of Mariupol and Berdyansk cut off.

The soldiers returned from their mission jubilant and elated. Back at their headquarters, they gathered as usual for a minute’s silence, in memory of those killed in Russia’s bloody war. “We had our daily ritual as usual. Then there were congratulations on a very successful and important operation,” Leon said. Then he added: “We are paying them back.”
