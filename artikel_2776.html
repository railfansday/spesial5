Broncos, ESPN, Fox Sports and others honor longtime linebacker, analyst Tom Jackson after his death

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The NFL world honored longtime Denver Broncos linebacker and ESPN analyst Tom Jackson on Sunday, days after his death.

Jackson died Friday, friend and colleague Chris Berman announced Sunday during ESPN's "Sunday NFL Countdown." He was 75.

Jackson spent 14 seasons in the league with the Broncos from 1973-1986. He earned three Pro Bowl nods and appeared in two Super Bowls with the franchise. He retired with 191 games played in his career, which was the fourth-most in Broncos history. Jackson, who played at Louisville before landing in the NFL, was inducted into Denver's Ring of Fame in 1992.

The Broncos honored Jackson ahead of their game with the Las Vegas Raiders on Sunday, and played a video tribute to him on the scoreboard at Empower Field before kickoff.

Lee Corso, who coached Jackson at Louisville, called him the "best football player I ever had."

"Not only that, he was the best leader I ever had," Corso said in a statement. "My condolences to Tom's family in this difficult time."

After his playing career ended, Jackson joined ESPN in 1987 and quickly became a fixture in the network's NFL coverage. He worked there for nearly three decades until he retired in 2016, and spent nearly the entire time working alongside Berman.

"All of us are stunned. We've lost one of the best human beings ever to walk the planet," Berman said. "I was fortunate, as you know, to work side-by-side with him for 30 years on 'NFL Primetime' and 'NFL Gameday,' now 'Sunday NFL Countdown.'"

"Tom was so much more than an accomplished NFL analyst," he added. "There was nobody kinder, there was nobody more thoughtful, more loyal, more engaging than Tom Jackson."

Jackson was behind the iconic "Jacked Up!" segment each week during the NFL season, which showcased the hardest hits throughout the league. The segment was eventually phased out as concern for head trauma and serious injury grew throughout football.

ESPN brought that segment back Sunday, and ended it with a huge hit of Jackson's.

Fox Sports also honored Jackson during its coverage, and played the "NFL Primetime" music while showing halftime highlights Sunday.

NFL commissioner Roger Goodell issued a statement remembering Jackson:

"The NFL lost a wonderful man with the passing of Tom Jackson. Along with millions of fans, I will always remember him as a player and broadcaster, but also as a friend. He was an outstanding player whose toughness and leadership helped define an era of Broncos football. He brought credibility, passion and humor to everything he did, and generations of fans came to know him through his work at ESPN. Tom helped fans enjoy and better understand football, always bringing the perspective of someone who loved the game and the people who play it. More importantly, he earned the respect and friendship of players, coaches and everyone who had the privilege of knowing and working with him."

Rich Eisen, Keyshawn Johnson, Tedy Bruschi, Randy Moss, Adam Schefter, Bill Cower and plenty of others in the NFL media world all honored Jackson and spoke about him on their respective platforms.
