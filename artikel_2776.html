'He could light up a room’: Former Washington Post, ESPN columnist Mike Wise dies at 62

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Mike Wise, whose columns appeared in the sports pages of The New York Times and The Washington Post before he helped build ESPN's The Undefeated, has died at 62.

According to a Facebook post from his close friend Pete LeBlanc, Wise died unexpectedly on Saturday, Nov. 14, in Panama, where he had made a home with his wife and three children. LeBlanc remembered Wise as one of the country's most gifted sportswriters, an accomplished author, and an Emmy Award-winning television reporter.

"Mike excelled in so many facets of his life. But above all his professional accomplishments, his greatest pride and joy was being a father to his three children and a husband to his wife," LeBlanc wrote. "If you knew Mike, you knew the extraordinary presence he had and the way he could light up a room. His energy, personality and spirit were simply unforgettable. He will be missed by many."

A Fresno State graduate, Wise joined The New York Times in 1994 and chronicled the Knicks for the paper. That beat became the backbone of his first book, "Just Ballin': The Chaotic Rise of the New York Knicks," published in 1999. He went back to the bookshelf two years later with Shaquille O'Neal on "Shaq Talks Back," which became a New York Times bestseller. In 2004, The Washington Post brought Wise to the nation's capital and gave him a column in a sports section anchored by Michael Wilbon, Tony Kornheiser, Sally Jenkins, and Thomas Boswell. He wrote that column for more than a decade, and from 2009 to 2012 he took the same voice to the airwaves as host of The Mike Wise Show on WJFK-FM.

ESPN lured Wise away from the Post in November 2014, making him one of the earliest hires for its fledgling site on the intersection of sports, race and culture. The project eventually debuted as The Undefeated before being rebranded as Andscape. Jason Whitlock, the site's original architect, later counted Wise among the small circle of collaborators who helped him map out its mission, recalling that the two had been brainstorming for the better part of a year while Wise was still on the Post's payroll. Once the site launched, Wise wrote for it as a senior writer and became a familiar face on ESPN's debate programming. In 2018, he returned to the local sports scene in Washington, joining WUSA9 as a digital columnist and on-air contributor.

News of Wise's death prompted an outpouring from colleagues across the industry, many of whom shared memories of their time alongside him.

Wise referred to himself as a "Recovering Sports Writer" in his X bio, though those who knew him best will remember him first as a husband and father of three.
