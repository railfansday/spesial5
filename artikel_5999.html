Scott Colom elected Mississippi’s first Black senator since Reconstruction

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Democrat Scott Colom has unseated Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith to become Mississippi’s first Black senator since Reconstruction.

The result is a stunning flip for Democrats in a deep-red state where they last won a Senate election in 1982, and it brings the Senate Democratic Caucus to at least 55 seats in the next Congress.

Colom was leading Hyde-Smith 49.2 percent to 48.7 percent — or by just over 4,600 votes out of more than 960,000 cast — when The Associated Press called the race Wednesday, a little over a week after Election Day. Ty Pinkins, the 2024 Democratic Senate nominee who ran as an independent this year, had 2 percent.

In recent days, as absentee and provisional ballots were counted, Colom had all but declared victory, repeatedly saying that Hyde-Smith had no path to victory.

“I’ll work every day in the U.S. Senate to build a future that generations of Mississippians can be just as proud to call home,” he posted Monday on social media.

With his victory, Colom joins several other Democrats who flipped seats in deep-red states, including Texas’ James Talarico, Kansas’ Adam Hamilton and South Carolina’s Annie Andrews. Democrats have so far flipped eight Senate seats to win control of the chamber, overcoming a difficult Senate map and giving them their largest majority in the chamber since 2011.

That majority could expand further if Democrats flip the Senate seat in Alaska, where former Rep. Mary Peltola narrowly leads Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan.

Republicans have dominated Senate elections in Mississippi in recent decades. The last Democrat to be elected senator in the Magnolia State was John C. Stennis, who won his sixth full and final term in 1982. Colom will be the third Black man to serve as a Mississippi senator after Republicans Hiram Revels and Blanche Bruce during Reconstruction.

While Revels and Bruce, who left office in 1881, were elected by the state Legislature, Colom will be the first Black senator elected directly by voters.

Colom has served as a district attorney for four counties in northeast Mississippi since 2016. President Joe Biden nominated him for a federal district judgeship in 2022, but Hyde-Smith effectively blocked his nomination by not returning a so-called blue slip, a Senate Judiciary Committee tradition that lets senators derail district judge nominees in their home states.

Senate Minority Leader Charles E. Schumer said last week that he’d had Colom in his sights for several years as a potential Senate candidate, including for a race against the state’s senior senator, Republican Roger Wicker.

“I had Scott Colom and his father come to my house, and I served them a nice little brunch or something. I said, ‘You got to run,’” the New York Democrat recalled to MS NOW. “They were smarter than me. This was four years ago. They said, ‘We don’t want to run this time, but we know that Hyde-[Smith], the incumbent, is so weak, we could beat her.’”

Still, the Mississippi race didn’t capture as much national attention as other Senate contests until the closing weeks of the campaign. A trio of Black senators, Angela Alsobrooks of Maryland, Lisa Blunt Rochester of Delaware and Cory Booker of New Jersey, all campaigned with Colom throughout the summer and fall. Key national figures, including former President Bill Clinton, former Vice President Kamala Harris and former Transportation Secretary Pete Buttigieg, also stumped for him in October.

Colom’s campaign engaged voters with quirky ads and unconventional forms of outreach, including meeting with people lining up to attend a Trump rally. One ad that went viral played on Hyde Smith saying earlier this year that people had “so many proteins to choose from” when asked about rising beef prices.

“Pick another protein?” says a man in the ad. “I’d rather pick another senator instead.”

Major Democratic outside groups made a late play in the state, with WinSenate, which is affiliated with Senate Democrats’ top super PAC, investing $2.2 million. The Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee spent $500,000 on a coordinated ad buy late last month.

The pro-Republican Senate Leadership Fund dropped $1.7 million on the race, and Trump headlined a rally for Hyde-Smith on Oct. 26.
