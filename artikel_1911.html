20 Richest Soccer Players of All Time Ranked by Net Worth

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Correspondent AUTHORITY Football journalist with experience at Reach PLC and PA Media; University of Central Lancashire graduate. FOCUS Fan-engagement content and match analysis across the Premier League and Championship. THE INSIGHT Oliver utilises a network of club and industry contacts to deliver verified reporting and fan-first content. He provides the human stories behind the results to ensure fans get the full picture as it happens.

Massive salaries, lifetime shoe deals, and global business empires have turned soccer’s biggest icons into some of the wealthiest athletes on earth.

Today, iconic figures like David Beckham earn hundreds of millions off the pitch through club ownership, fashion ventures, and brand partnerships that even dwarf their mega on-pitch earnings.

From record-breaking contracts in Saudi Arabia and Major League Soccer to royal family fortunes like Faiq Bolkiah’s, the financial scale of modern soccer is staggering.

Football Insider has ranked the 20 richest soccer players of all time by estimated net worth, combining career wages, endorsement deals, and post-playing investments.

Scroll down to see who tops the rich list, and let us know in the comments which fortune surprises you most. There are some huge shocks that nobody would have expected.

Pep Guardiola has reshaped modern soccer more profoundly than any other figure of his generation, turning his tactical blueprint into global celebrity and a £100m ($132.3m) fortune. While the Spaniard enjoyed a decorated playing career with Barcelona and Spain, the vast majority of his wealth has been built from the touchline through record-breaking managerial contracts at Barcelona, Bayern Munich, and Manchester City.

The Spaniard was believed to be earning around £35m ($46.3m) annually, with bonuses included, during his final years in charge of Man City. He owns high-value property in England and his home country, and has partnered with the likes of Puma, Nissan and Visa to bolster his whopping fortune over the years.

Ultra-passionate Atletico Madrid manager Simeone is widely regarded as the highest-paid in his profession right now, owing to how desperate the La Liga club are to hold onto his services for as long as humanly possible. Some reports estimate that he takes home around £26m ($34.4m) each year as they aim to break the dominance of Barcelona and Real Madrid.

He also earned a decent wage in a successful 19-year playing career, but has accumulated such a high net worth through management and an extensive property portfolio of over 180 apartments in Madrid that he oversees with his wife. Simeone became an ambassador for both Betinia and CampoBet in 2026 too.

Salah has utilised his amazing soccer career to amass huge wealth through lucrative contracts and a whole host of sponsorship deals. The Egyptian superstar became Liverpool’s highest-paid player ever in 2022, with a reported annual salary of £20m ($26.5m) in basic wages.

He has worked closely with major brands like Adidas, Pepsi, Vodafone and Uber, with estimates putting his endorsement income alone at around £15m per year ($19.8m) at its peak. Trabzonspor are paying the Premier League great around £15m ($19.8m) annually after he joined on a free transfer in the summer of 2026. His wider business interests are focused on real estate investment and commercial holding companies.

The first unusual name on this list comes in the form of ex-Chelsea and AC Milan striker Pato. He had a good career but failed to reach the lofty heights many expected of him, yet has turned that pain into power to become a hugely successful investor after his retirement. He did earn huge contracts at Milan and in a two-year stint in China, but his wealth mainly comes from off-field endeavours.

He has ventured into the world of gastronomy, with a chain of burger restaurants in Brasilia, while also putting his money into CoverGirl cosmetics, his own vodka brand and numerous properties in his home country. Pato is clearly looking to further his wealth in the future, after joining a consortium who completed a takeover of English club Northampton Town in 2026.

2026 Ballon d’Or winner Kane is not only loving life on the pitch with Bayern Munich and England, but he is also a supremely wealthy man off it, with a bank balance that stands head and shoulders above the rest of his international teammates. The Three Lions captain reportedly earns around £25m ($33.1m) each year before tax at Bayern Munich.

He boasts a high-profile commercial partnership with footwear brand Skechers, after signing a lifetime global deal in 2023 that earns him up to an estimated £7.5m ($9.9m) annually. Kane also regularly works with major brands like Allianz, Oura and Reflo.

The exceptional soccer career of Welsh superstar Bale translated into immense wealth, as he raked in over £500,000 per week ($661,625-a-week) in basic wages during the peak of his powers at Real Madrid. Commercial deals followed in abundance as he shone at the top of the game, with huge partnerships signed with Adidas, BMW, Lucozade and EA Sports.

Bale has dipped his toes into several different investment pathways since retiring, with a stake in a TGL golf franchise, co-ownership of a Cardiff-based bar and ties to Penderyn Whisky. His biggest step has undoubtedly been helping launch a sports investment fund worth over £500m ($661.6m), and Bale has also publicly admitted that he would like to co-own a soccer club in the near future.

Even soccer players on the level of Ronaldo were not paid close to the astronomical figures that relatively average players earn nowadays back in his time with some of the world’s biggest sides. He was one of the sport’s first truly global marketing superstars, though, signing a lifetime deal with Nike in 1994 and being one of the faces of their brand to this day.

Ronaldo boasts an endless list of partnerships with top brands that have all contributed to his huge fortune, with notable links to Santander, Betfair and Snickers. He actually had a bigger net worth in previous years, but failed investments at Spanish club Real Vallodolid and Brazilian side Cruzeiro have seen him fall down this list.

Mercurial genius Ibrahimovic has been able to transform his enigmatic persona as a player into a successful post-retirement career that has swelled his bank balance to new heights. He is one of the most recognisable soccer players of all time, and that is reflected in how unashamedly centred his commercial campaigns are on him.

After earning big money across his career with the likes of AC Milan, PSG, Barcelona and Man United, as well as being exceptionally well-paid by MLS standards at LA Galaxy, he has only continued to further his celebrity status through partnerships with Nike, Oakley Meta and XTB.

If you were waiting for the first mention of Saudi Arabian money impacting this list, then here it is. The oil-rich kingdom has enticed many of the world’s top names to the Middle East in the last five years with the promise of massive riches, and Benzema was no different. He joined Al Ittihad in 2023 and Al Hilal in 2026, with his latter contract reportedly worth over £1.6m each week ($2.1m) in basic wages.

Such incredible mega-deals came after a 14-year stay at Real Madrid that had already made him wealthy beyond comprehension. The 2022 Ballon d’Or winner maintained a long-standing relationship with Adidas until the contract expired when he was without a club in September 2026, while his effortless aura has also seen him link up with luxury brands like Fendi, Jean Paul Gaultier and Assaf.

Now a name that may only be familiar to British soccer fans, with Whelan being best known for his ownership of EFL side Wigan Athletic between 1995 and 2015. The Bradford native had a modest playing career but built his fortune after retirement by developing Whelan’s Discount Stores from the ground up and selling it on, then moving into sports retail with JJB Sports.

JJB grew exponentially in the UK as one of the market leaders for sportswear and equipment, and was valued at over £1bn ($1.32bn) at its peak in 2009. He then sold shares for a combined £240m ($317m) and effectively repeated the trick with DW Sports Fitness, all while pumping his money into Wigan to help them become 2013 FA Cup champions.

Rooney was consistently one of the highest-paid players in the world during his playing career. The Man United and England legend earned £300,000 per week ($396,975 per week) with his biggest contract extension in 2014, while he was one of the faces of Nike and synonymous with EA Sports’ FIFA franchise, appearing on seven consecutive covers between 2006 and 2012.

His status as one of the Premier League’s greatest ever players has seen the endless list of commercial opportunities continue post-retirement, with managerial deals, punditry contracts with the BBC and Sky, and his lucrative image-rights company keeping him afloat near the top of this list.

Mbappe has reached incredible heights over the last 10 years and is a global phenomenon who consistently ranks among the best scorers and players every season. Real Madrid pay him a reported £12.5m ($16.5m) in basic salary each year, while a £125m ($165.4m) signing-on bonus has been spread across his five-year deal. The French forward is not even in the prime of his career and has amassed a huge financial portfolio that rivals legends of the sport.

His commercial powerhouse has been accentuated by a massive Nike contract that ran for 20 years until its 2026 expiration, while major brands like Dior, Hublot and Oakley have all looked for a slice of the Mbappe pie with commercial endorsements in recent years.

You can be forgiven for potentially forgetting about the intricacies of Braithwaite’s soccer career, even though he has featured for the likes of Barcelona and Denmark. The Gremio forward’s huge fortune comes from a 2017 investment into American property developers NYCE Companies.

NYCE’s huge portfolio has expanded across the States in the last decade, with reports stating that they own and let out over 1,500 properties in several major cities. It was believed to have more than £270m ($357.3m) in assets in a 2026 report. Braithwaite has expanded his empire by investing into restaurants in Barcelona and Brazil, as well as establishing his own French fashion label.

Neymar still holds the world-record transfer fee from his eye-watering £200m ($263m) switch from Barcelona to Paris Saint-Germain in 2017. Paris is where the Brazilian superstar transformed from a generational prodigy into a commercial juggernaut, pocketing roughly £30m ($39m) a year in base wages before injury-hit spells and an eventual homecoming to boyhood club Santos.

His 2023 switch to Al Hilal saw utterly insane reported annual figures of £129m paid to the Selecao star as Saudi clubs looked to dislodge the European heavyweight leagues. He also reportedly signed a £23m yearly deal with Puma in 2020 after leaving Nike, while remaining in a lucrative partnership with Red Bull to this day.

David Beckham turned a lethal right foot and matinee-idol looks into the original athlete-as-a-global-brand blueprint. After winning everything on the pitch with Manchester United and Real Madrid, the Londoner became the ultimate pitchman—selling everything from underwear and fragrances to watches and whiskey for decades after hanging up his boots.

His shrewdest financial move came in 2007 when he signed for LA Galaxy, negotiating an expansion franchise option that allowed him to co-found Inter Miami for a fraction of its modern valuation. Combined with his Authentic Brands Group partnership and his Studio 99 production house, Brand Beckham has proven far more lucrative than his playing days ever were.

Being widely regarded as the greatest soccer player of all time still isn’t enough to crack the top four on this list—though Lionel Messi is hardly checking behind the sofa for spare change. Across legendary spells at Barcelona, Paris Saint-Germain, and Inter Miami, the Argentine icon has cleared over £900m ($1.19bn) in career pre-tax playing earnings while securing a lucrative MLS equity pathway modeled on David Beckham’s playbook.

Off the pitch, Messi brings in a reported £56m ($74.1m) a year in 2026 through blockbuster partnerships with Adidas, Apple, Pepsi, and Konami. Eight Ballon d’Or trophies may not outrank royal oil money or biochemical startups here, but his commercial pull across the Americas remains unmatched.

In a fair world, spending 23 years at the summit of world soccer, becoming the sport’s first active billionaire, and collecting roughly $300m a year at Al Nassr would guarantee first place. Instead, Cristiano Ronaldo has to settle for fourth—making him easily the wealthiest player on this list who actually earned his fortune purely by kicking a ball.

The five-time Ballon d’Or winner became the first athlete to clear $2bn in career pre-tax earnings, boosted by a reported 15 percent equity stake in Al Nassr, a $1bn lifetime Nike contract signed in 2016, and his sprawling CR7 hotel and lifestyle empire. With more than 660 million Instagram followers, even a single sponsored post earns him more than most European pros make in a season.

During his playing days at Manchester United and Everton, Louis Saha was best known for clinical finishing and a frustratingly fragile hamstring. Since hanging up his boots, however, the former France striker has pulled off one of the greatest post-retirement glow-ups in sports history, building a tech fortune reportedly stretching into the multi-billions.

In 2014, Saha co-founded AxisStars, a private digital network that connects professional athletes and entertainers directly with vetted commercial partners and business ventures. Alongside savvy investments in AI and blockchain, Saha still pops up on television punditry panels—though at this point, he is clearly doing it for the love of the game rather than the appearance fee.

Arsenal fans remember Mathieu Flamini as a combative midfield enforcer famous for flying into two-footed tackles and cutting the sleeves off his matchday shirt. Few at the Emirates realized he was quietly building a green-energy empire between training sessions.

While studying law and still playing for the Gunners in 2013, Flamini co-founded GF Biochemicals, the first company to mass-produce levulinic acid as a sustainable alternative to petroleum. With estimates valuing his stake at up to £10bn ($13bn), the Frenchman is a regular fixture at global climate summits—and has refused to rule out buying a major soccer club of his own.

Faiq Bolkiah tops this list by £5bn ($6.6bn), proving once and for all that having the right uncle beats winning the Ballon d’Or. Born in Los Angeles, the winger spent time in the youth academies of Southampton, Chelsea, and Leicester City before playing senior club football in Portugal and Thailand—a modest resume that contrasts wildly with his status as soccer’s richest man.

Bolkiah is a member of the Brunei royal family and the nephew of Sultan Hassanal Bolkiah, whose oil-and-gas fortune underpins an estimated £15bn ($19.85bn) family net worth. To his credit, Faiq has repeatedly played down his royal background so managers judge him on his football rather than his family vault.
