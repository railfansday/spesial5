Rockstar Devs Concerned About Layoffs Despite GTA 6 Success

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Before joining the team at DualShockers, Lauren was a Features Writer at Adventure Gamers, a Guides Writer at Gameranx, as well as contributing as a News Writer at PCInvasion and Daily

GTA 6 is here, and the open world of Leonida is ready for fans to explore. It’s still early days, but GTA 6 is on track to be one of the biggest games ever. It has tied for the best-reviewed game of all time, already generated $1.8 billion in gross revenue and counting, and broken Twitch’s viewership record in just 30 minutes.

The reviews for Grand Theft Auto 6 are in and it's now tied for the best-reviewed video game of all time, with Nintendo's greatest game matching it.

Amid all that success, you would think that GTA 6 developers are now able to kick back and relax, soaking in the excitement of sharing their work with the world. However, even though Rockstar Games hasn't revealed any layoffs, it remains a worry that looms over those who worked on the game.

In an opinion piece published in The Guardian, Animation Programmer at Rockstar Games, Nestor Uriarte, expressed concerns about job security and the pressures developers face.

He says that “while the highs of a game release like this can be incredible,” workers in the video game industry have also become accustomed to “less welcome side effects.” This covers both sides of development, as Uriarte mentions “intense overtime in the buildup and mass redundancies in the aftermath.”

Rockstar Games is one of many studios in previous years to face allegations of “crunch culture,” essentially requiring developers to work an unhealthy amount of overtime in the lead-up to a game’s launch date.

Uriarte believes that “with a strong union we could make sure there is no return to this system. We have already seen the introduction of some overtime pay, and now we want to keep on pushing to ensure that every hour worked in our industry is an hour paid.”

In the middle of the record-breaking release of GTA 6, Rockstar Games employees in the UK are voting on whether or not they want to unionize. The ballot, which will be voted on by approximately 1,600 staff, will close on November 26, 2026. The results of the vote should be announced shortly after voting closes.

Uriarte says that after the launch of GTA 6, Rockstar Games employees "have had no indication that layoffs are coming, but it’s hard not to worry about what the future holds.” He argues that the state of the industry means nobody is safe, “even my colleagues and I, working on the biggest game of all time, are worried about getting laid off. But the truth is that workers across the sector are helpless to resist the waves of redundancies crashing around us, until we get organised.”

Another threat to game development is the rise of AI. Uriarte claims, “Rockstar has said it is committed to preserving jobs and it has been clear that our games will continue to be made by people.” However, the push to secure union recognition would grant employees more of a say in that future.

Layoffs in the video game industry are commonplace, with Civilization studio, Firaxis Games, being the latest to slash its headcount. Other notable redundancies this year include EA shutting down BioWare, and the mass XBOX layoffs as part of the company’s restructuring process, among many others.

Although no layoffs seem to be on the cards at Rockstar Games, if they were to occur, it would call into question the sustainability of game development if a studio with such a huge release needs to let staff go.
