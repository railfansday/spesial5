10 Best Food Centric Video Games

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Eric Warner is a Journalist and Multimedia Producer based in New England with over 7 years of experience producing stories for multiple publications

For many countries across the globe, 2026’s holiday season is just around the corner. With those holidays come celebratory family gatherings full of all kinds of delicious food to eat.

While some people may be stressing out about the prospect of cooking so many dishes for the holiday season, there’s likely just as many people out there who not only enjoy tasting all kinds of meals but enjoy preparing them for friends and family alike as well.

For those who love cooking and all things food-related, here’s a look at the 10 best food centric video games people can enjoy playing through, whether solo or with friends, in between tantalizing holiday meals.

For a few decades now, food companies have occasionally released tie-in video games often referred to as advergames, in wacky attempts to attract gamers to purchase their products. While Burger King has released a few advergames over the years, its best was arguably Sneak King.

One of three BK games for the original Xbox and Xbox 360, Sneak King is a third-person stealth game following the fast-food chain’s now-retired Burger King mascot, as he sneaks around various locations to surprise deliver BK meals such as omelet sandwiches, hash browns, and more.

While Sneak King didn’t have a lot of depth to it, the game did represent the often wacky yet creative game design the sixth and seventh console generations are remembered for.

Anybody who grew up owning an iPod Touch, iPad, or iPhone in the early 2010s likely played some of the most iconic mobile apps of all time, such as Angry Birds, Doodle Jump, and the subject for this entry, Halfbrick Studios’ Fruit Ninja.

The premise for Fruit Ninja is simple: players must use a touch screen, VR controllers, or their own hands (in the case of the Kinect versions) to equip ninja blades and slice up as much flying fruit as possible without hitting flying bombs.

Fruit Ninja is an easy game for sure, but it’s one that endlessly fun no matter what form or mode it’s played in, whether it be the Xbox 360 Kinect version, the Fruit Ninja FX version found at Dave & Buster's and other arcades, or the latest version on Nex Playground.

J. R. R. Tolkien’s The Lord of the Rings franchise is beloved for many reasons, such as its mythology, character arcs, and fantastical creatures. Another aspect of the franchise many people adore is the calm, joyful culture of Hobbit life.

Numerous LOTR fans strive to live life as merrily as the Shire’s inhabitants, and one way people can do so is by playing the life simulation game Tales of the Shire: A The Lord of the Rings Game, set after The Hobbit but before The Fellowship of the Ring.

Here, players can create their own Hobbit as they complete tasks such as farming, fishing, and decorating Hobbit-holes in the emerging Hobbiton village of Bywater. Most importantly for this article, they can also cook a variety of dishes for their Hobbit neighbors.

In this preview, let's discuss KuloNiku: Bowl Up! A game that feels like a soft spiritual successor to the Papa Louie cooking titles at its heart.

In 2006, Nintendo helped many young gamers get interested in cooking for the first time by releasing the minigame compilation Cooking Mama on the Nintendo DS. While that game is fantastic, many Nintendo players would consider Cooking Mama 5: Bon Appétit! to be the best entry in the CM series.

The final mainline entry in the CM series, Cooking Mama 5: Bon Appétit! Features similar modes and overall gameplay to its predecessors, but introduces many new minigames and recipes such as Pumpkin Gratin, Hawaiian Pancakes, and Herb Hotdogs.

Players could even manage a burger shop, take care of pet fish, customize kitchen designs, and gather ingredients from plants, with each action feeling more precise thanks to the 3DS’ improved stylus controls.

Video games have let people play as medieval heroes, monsters, space marines, and more, but one unique game did the impossible by making playing as actual food fun. That game was Bossa Studios’ I Am Bread.

Set in the same universe as Surgeon Simulator, I Am Bread has players control a singular piece of bread as it strives to move about various locations such as a kitchen and a bathroom in an effort to become toast, whilst struggling to maintain its edibility by avoiding the ground, bugs, and other contaminants.

While the premise of I Am Bread might sound ridiculous at first, it actually winds up being a fairly hilarious, challenging, and rewarding game, with all of the crazy antics and chaos the slices of bread get into.

People love parties, candy, and piñatas, but only one game developer has dared tackling the question of how piñatas are made. As seen in Rare’s classic life simulation game Viva Piñata.

Set on the initially ruinous Pinata island, Viva Piñata has players restore a garden on the island with the goal of attracting, domesticating, breeding, and defending local piñata wildlife, so that they can eventually be sent out to parties across the globe full of delicious candy.

Viva Piñata may be 20 years old at this point, but it’s still just as fun and colorful as ever, whether people play it on Xbox 360, PC, or via the Rare Replay compilation.

Hunger meters aren’t a law of nature. These ten survival games turned food into a reward, made it optional or ditched eating altogether.

The Illinois-based game developer Young Horses is famous for its Octodad series, which itself could be considered a food centric game since a chef chases Octodad around. However, the team wound up making one of the most creative food centric games yet in 2020, with Bugsnax.

Here, people play as a muppet-like Grumpus newspaper journalist, as they set out to Snaktooth Island to document the food-like Bugsnax creatures living there only to get caught up in a mysterious conspiracy surrounding the island and its inhabitants.

For anyone who wanted to explore the living food island of Swallow Falls in Cloudy with a Chance of Meatballs 2, Bugsnax is the game for you. You can encounter all kinds of food-inspired wildlife on Snaktooth Island, such as the elephant cheeseburger Bunger and the crab apple Crapple.

Many of the best food centric games were released years or even decades ago, but one new 2026 title has quickly become a favorite among food fanatics. That game being the anime-inspired cooking and restaurant management simulator KuloNiku: Bowl Up!

The 13th game released by Gambir Studio, KuloNiku: Bowl Up! has players inherit their grandmother’s once-famous meatball restaurant in the cozy town of KuloNiku. They set out to restore it to its former glory by experimenting with new recipes, decor, and competing with other chefs in Meatball Brawls.

People aren’t just making simple spaghetti and meatball dishes, though, as customers will often request comedically complex recipes requiring players to chop, boil, fry, and torch all kinds of ingredients, with some customers even going on to become potential friends or rivals.

Conquer the depths of a dark dungeon as you craft delicious new recipies.

Many food centric games focus on people opening up a new restaurant and rising to Michelin star status, but no game quite replicates that experience in such a unique and fun way like Mintrocket’s Dave the Diver.

A primarily 2D adventure and management game, Dave the Diver follows the title character as he and his friends open their Bancho Sushi Bar near a massive, mysterious Blue Hole, with Dave having to dive for fish during the day and manage the sushi restaurant at night.

Diving for fish and managing the sushi restaurant are certainly fun activities, but there's far more to Dave the Diver than initially meets the eye. Players can fight huge sea creature bosses, sing karaoke, and even interact with mermaids from the Sea People Village.

Without a doubt, the best food centric game out there is Ghost Town Games’ Overcooked! All You Can Eat, a remastered collection of Overcooked and Overcooked 2 with all DLC expansions and completely brand-new content.

A third-person, top-down perspective cooking game, Overcooked 1 and 2 feature teams of up to four chefs working together to serve food in an orderly, oftentimes hectic, manner from restaurants, food trucks, and unconventional locations such as a space station and icebergs.

While Overcooked may not be as realistic with its food as other games, it perfectly recreates the fun, often chaotic nature of restaurant cooking into a cartoony, co-op experience and forces people to learn to work together as a team, making it a perfect food centric game.
