Clair Obscur Studio Has Lost One of Its Most Important Developers

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Jennifer Svedberg-Yen was the narrative lead on Clair Obscur: Expedition 33. She basically shaped the game's twisting and turning story, which many would argue is its greatest asset.

And so it's a bit of a gut punch to hear that she actually left developer Sandfall Interactive earlier this year — meaning that, unless she's already helped establish some of the project's narrative groundwork, she won't be involved with the studio's next game.

Svedberg-Yen dropped this bombshell in an interview with Gamesbeat, having just won a 'Breakthrough US' award at the BAFTAs for her work on Clair Obscur.

"I left Sandfall in July, after wrapping up some final stuff for Expedition. We’ve parted ways," she explains.

It should be stressed that Svedberg-Yen really did spearhead the Game of the Year-winning title's story and tone right from the start; it wouldn't have been the experience that it was without her.

"I was the third person on the team. The team didn’t exist, actually. When I joined it was two guys who were still working at Ubisoft," she explains.

"The studio didn’t exist. It was the two co-founders and then me. Then we brought in the composer, and then the art director. Several months later we brought in the third co-founder, and then several months after that started the studio. So I was one of the founding members of the team."

Svedberg-Yen continues: "We came up with the idea together from the very start. It was always, from the very beginning, a narrative-focused game. It’s a narrative-first game. That’s what the creative director always said as well. Narrative is king."

And that approach is clear to see in the finished product, which has been lauded for its plot and characters ever since its release back in April of last year.

Svedberg-Yen goes on to talk about how important it is to establish narrative cohesion right off the bat, so that the whole development cycle is built around one vision. She thinks that too many games see story as something that can be slotted in over time, or simply added later on — and the whole thing suffers for it.

As for her own creative process, Svedberg-Yen provides some interesting insight: "I don’t think I could have written something like Expedition in my 20s, or even my 30s."

"I very much believe that one of the most powerful things we can do to train our ability to tell a good story is actually to live a rich life, to observe others, to learn how people work. If you’re trying to write about people, you must understand people."

Svedberg-Yen intends to work on her own projects going forward, which includes writing for film and TV — but she's also passionate about promoting the importance of narrative in video games.

As such, she wants to set up some kind of initiative, helping narrative-focused creatives make the next step in getting their games funded, and in turn, reshape the wider industry's perspective when it comes to storytelling. Sounds like a pretty noble goal to me.

Still, with everything Clair Obscur achieved in terms of narrative, you'd think that Svedberg-Yen will be difficult to replace.

We know that Sandfall Interactive is in the very early stages of developing its next title, but beyond vague, teasing statements, nothing has been made official just yet.
