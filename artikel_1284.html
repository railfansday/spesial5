Same Level as Trafford: Leeds Can Make Will Lankshear Statement in January

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Leeds United are a club on the up, aspiring for European qualification rather than just Premier League survival this season, and their reported transfer plans reflect this ambition.

The Whites currently sit ninth in the Premier League table, just two points behind Chelsea in fifth — a prospective Champions League place. Daniel Farke's outfit have established a functional style of play in the top flight, and merely need some additional stardust on top of their solid base to take them to the next level.

Having forked out around £40 million for James Trafford in the summer, the Yorkshire club showed that they're not afraid to get the chequebook out. Signing England's most sought-after young goalkeeper was a statement of intent from Leeds, and they'll certainly be looking to build upon this with another move for a high-profile player in January.

Various reports suggest that the Whites are in the race to land the most-wanted talent in the Championship right now.

Will Lankshear has taken the English second division by storm so far this season, and is subsequently sparking interest from half of the Premier League.

The Middlesbrough striker, who signed for the Teesiders from Tottenham Hotspur for £20 million in the summer, has scored 15 goals in 16 matches this term. This prolific form has guided Boro into the Championship's top two after 15 matches, and this promotion charge likely means the north-east club won't be in a rush to sell the player in January.

Lankshear's Statistics 2026/27 (Championship Only)

That said, the sheer volume of clubs supposedly tracking the 21-year-old's progress could prompt them to change their mind.

According to TEAMtalk, Leeds, Arsenal, Manchester United, Tottenham, Brighton, Everton, Newcastle, Sunderland and Borussia Dortmund are all keeping tabs on Lankshear.

If even a handful of these clubs act on their interest, Middlesbrough could ignite a bidding war, potentially provoking a substantial offer which they deem worthy of accepting.

Some may argue that Leeds don't need to add Lankshear to their ranks. Farke already has Dominic Calvert-Lewin, who scored 15 goals across all competitions last season, at his disposal.

The England international is indeed the focal point of Leeds' attack, using his size and strength to provide the Whites with someone to play off. However, with Manchester United reportedly showing interest in Calvert-Lewin, Leeds need to be prepared to swoop for a replacement.

Even if the ex-Everton man remains at Elland Road, Farke's three-at-the-back formation can easily accommodate two strikers. Lankshear and Calvert-Lewin could form a potent front two, with one of Harry Wilson, Brendan Aaronson or Noah Okafor playing just behind them.

On top of this, if Leeds have serious aspirations of qualifying for Europe, they will need two top strikers to rotate next season. Calvert-Lewin will also turn 30 next March, so Leeds would be smart to start succession planning for life without their talismanic number nine.

Ultimately, not only would Lankshear's arrival signal a change in Leeds' general ambition and intent, but it'd take them up a level in both the short and long term. It'd tell the rest of the league where the Yorkshire side plan on going and how they're now prepared to fight tooth and nail to land the best up-and-coming talent the country has to offer.

The signing of Trafford set the wheels in motion for this new aggressive market approach from Leeds, and the potential signing of Lankshear would show that the addition of the goalkeeper wasn't just a one-off.
