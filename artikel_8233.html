15 Camping Gifts for People Who Would Rather Be in a Tent

Some people book a luxury hotel for the weekend. Others would rather throw a tent in the car and figure out where they’re sleeping later. If you have one of the latter on your holiday shopping list, we’ve got you covered. Below are the best camping gifts that will make their time outside a bit more comfortable—even if they swear they don’t care about that. From the practical gear they’ll use on every trip to the fun extras that let them have their creature comforts, these presents will have them plotting their getaway ASAP.

A good pair of hiking socks is always appreciated, but these come with a little extra feel-good factor. Made with 70 percent organic cotton and a cushioned footbed, Conscious Step socks are comfortable enough for long days on the trail (or lazy ones around camp). Even better, purchases support the National Parks Conservation Association, which works to protect America’s public lands.

This interactive water bottle doubles as an excuse to plan their next big camping trip. It comes with 63 illustrated, water-resistant stickers representing every national park in the U.S., so they can track where they’ve visited as they go. Since it’s a Nalgene, it’s also durable and featherlight, perfect for hanging from backpacks and gear bags.

This is the ideal gift for the person who considers coffee a nonnegotiable, regardless of how far they’ve wandered from civilization. This compact brewer is roughly the size of a thermos and makes hot coffee and cold brew concentrate in minutes. Simply add finely ground coffee and hot or cold water, let it brew, then use the hand press to dispense a concentrated shot into the included carafe. With no plugs and no charging required, they can throw it in their bag and go.

Camp chairs are great, but they’re not always the easiest things to lug around. This compact stool weighs just 1.2 pounds, folds down to 7.5 inches, and has a swiveling seat that lets you turn without having to pick up and reposition the whole thing. It supports up to 260 pounds, too, which is impressive for its size.

This is a particularly useful piece of gear for any camper who wants to cut down on what they’re carrying. Hydrapak’s collapsible flask has a built-in filter that removes common waterborne bacteria and parasites, allowing hikers to refill from water sources along the trail. It weighs just 4.1 ounces and packs completely flat when empty. It’s a great option for biking, hiking, paddling, and even traveling through sketchy airport bathrooms.

A headlamp is one of those camping essentials you don’t fully appreciate until you’re stumbling around camp in the dark. The Storm delivers up to 550 lumens with adjustable brightness, three different colored modes (red, blue, and green) to preserve night vision, and a waterproof-rated IPX7 construction that can handle rain and snow. It also has a lock mode to prevent it from accidentally turning on in your pack and draining the batteries before you even reach camp.

If you want your family and friends to experience comfort in camp, look no further than this hammock. The kit has everything someone needs to set up between two trees, including polyester straps and iron carabiners. The Portable Hammock has a weight capacity of 551 pounds and it exhibits both the toughness and user-friendliness that Jeep is known for: It’s made from durable nylon and conveniently packs away into a built-in pouch that doubles as a pocket when the hammock is set up.

There are hundreds of camp slippers on the market, but few are as truly warm or versatile as Glerups. Made from 100 percent natural wool, they’re naturally temperature-regulating and odor-resistant, so feet stay comfortable without getting swampy. The slip-on design makes them easy to kick off tent-side, while the rubber sole handles dirt, mud, and slippery moss with ease.

Finicky sleepers will love this sleeping bag. The Kelty Eclipse skips the traditional zipper in favor of a clever wraparound design that campers spread out when they’re hot or cocoon themself when they’re cold. What it lacks in zippers it makes up for in thoughtful details, including a self-sealing foot vent, oversized hood to keep pillows in place, and internal pockets. It’s also rated to 15 degrees Fahrenheit, so it’s built for more than just balmy summer nights.

A good outdoor blanket earns its keep in just about every situation, and Yeti’s Lowlands is designed to handle more than one night under the stars. Its waterproof, puncture-resistant bottom keeps damp ground from seeping through, while the cushioned top provides a comfortable place to sit, stretch out, or let the dog settle in. Roll it up for transport, then bring it out for everything from picnics to beach days to tailgates.

If their cooking kit is just a recycled takeout container and one random spork, please get them this Gerber set. The 16-piece set includes a saute pan, stock pot, hot pad, and tableware for four people, giving them everything they need to make and serve a proper meal without cobbling together a kitchen from mismatched gear. When they’re finished, everything nests together for easy packing until the next time they need it.

These are the pants they’ll put on once at camp and have absolutely no interest in taking off, especially in colder temps. The Fernos pair a cozy insulated interior with a water-resistant exterior, making them ideal for chilly mornings, lounging around the campsite, or layering over a base layer when snow starts to fall. In other words, they’re very durable sweatpants.

This is about as close as campers can get to bringing their mattress into the woods. The 3.9-inch memory foam pad is thick, supportive, and genuinely comfortable—more proper bed than half-deflated sleeping pad situation. It’s not designed for backpacking (don’t try to carry this on a thru-hike), but it’s portable enough to toss in the car for weekend camping trips. It rolls up tightly for storage and transport and self-inflates when unrolled, making set-up a breeze.

One of the best things about Luno’s car camping mattresses is that they’re not reserved for people with tricked-out 4x4s. The Air Pro is designed to fit a wide range of vehicles, with custom-fit options for dozens of makes and models. This particular kit includes a luxe inflatable mattress, air pump, fitted sheet, pillow, and window screen set that turns their backseat into a dreamy place to sleep. Simply put in their car’s year, make, and model and choose their window size for the gift of spontaneous overnights and long road trips.

This is for the camp chef who thinks camping dinner should involve way more than hot dogs and s’mores. The Gozney Tread is a portable pizza oven that heats up to 950°F and cooks a delicious pie in about a minute. Its compact design makes it shockingly easy to transport and it runs on propane, so they won’t need to fuss with building a fire. Sure, it’s a bit of a splurge for the campsite, but the lucky recipient will have no trouble convincing their camping buddies it’s worth the extra trunk space.

All campers know that a tent you can properly stand up in is the gift that keeps on giving. The Aurora Highrise has near-vertical walls and a roomy interior that comfortably accommodates up to six people, making it a good pick for families or groups who don’t love sleeping elbow-to-elbow. Large windows and doors let in plenty of light and airflow, while Nightlight Pockets let headlamps and flashlights cast an ambient glow. There’s also a generous vestibule for stashing muddy boots, camping chairs, and anything else that needs protection overnight.
