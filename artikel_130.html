Scammed migrant workers in UK to be considered possible victims of modern slavery

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Home Office changes policy after legal challenge over case of care worker scammed out of £22,000

Migrant workers who are financially exploited can now be considered victims of modern slavery, after a legal challenge by a care worker who was scammed out of £22,000 to pay an agent for a job in the UK that did not exist.

The care worker, Ali*, from Pakistan, was put in contact with an agent who told him he could organise a certificate of sponsorship for him to work in a UK care home. The agent was recommended by a friend who said he had been helped to secure a UK care job.

After handing over £22,000 and obtaining what he thought was a genuine certificate of sponsorship and a health and care skilled worker visa from the Home Office, he, his wife and their two young children moved to the UK in October 2023, excited about their new start.

He was keen to begin work as soon as possible as he had incurred heavy debts owed to family members from whom he had borrowed money to cover the cost of obtaining the documents.

After he arrived in the UK, he kept calling the number he had been given to find out when he could start work. Eventually, he was told to go to a car showroom where he met a man who told him he had been scammed, as he had encountered others in a similar situation who had been sent to him too.

Ali was devastated to learn he had lost all the money to a scam. The friend who had recommended the agent told him no job had been forthcoming for him either and so he had returned to Pakistan.

“At that moment I felt like my whole life had just started to cave in,” Ali said in a witness statement about his case. “We had invested everything we had and got into very heavy debts on the basis of this care worker job. I feel trapped by my debts and my family’s needs.”

He and his family were left destitute as a result of the scam. He said that as a result of the extreme stress, his hair turned white and he lost two stone in weight.

Luke Piper, the head of immigration at Work Rights Centre, said the charity had come across many other cases of migrant workers being subjected to financial exploitation. In a recent survey of more than 100 migrant care workers conducted by the charity, nearly three-quarters (74%) had experienced some form of financial exploitation.

“There is a worrying disconnect between those experiencing modern slavery and those getting help,” Piper said.

Ali was referred to the national referral mechanism for potential victims of trafficking and modern slavery and was initially found not to qualify as a potential victim of modern slavery. Supported by Work Rights Centre and Central England Law Centre, he challenged the home secretary for not including financial exploitation in the list of forms of modern slavery.

A judicial review was issued earlier this year and the decision was then reversed so that the Home Office will consider his case as one of potential human trafficking.

The Home Office has admitted in a court order that it previously “adopted an unduly narrow approach to the concept of exploitation” and said it would recognise financial exploitation as a form of modern slavery and trafficking.

Tessa Burwood, the solicitor at Central England Law Centre who represented Ali, said: “Home Office compliance checks for the health and care visa scheme are meant to ensure that vulnerable migrant care workers are only employed by genuine agencies and to prevent the forms of exploitation that are so commonplace in the sector. Something has gone seriously wrong here.

“The fact that financial exploitation will hopefully now be recognised as a form of modern slavery is a small step in the right direction but it is not the end of the nightmare for migrant care workers, who may still face years of uncertainty and instability due to the impact of this poorly conceived visa scheme.”

A Home Office spokesperson said: “While we do not comment on operational matters, the financial exploitation of visa applicants and visa holders is completely unacceptable. Where evidence of abuse is identified, we work closely with enforcement and safeguarding partners to protect vulnerable people and take action against those responsible.”
