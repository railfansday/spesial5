Klopp breaks ranks to savage Infantino: Liverpool legend becomes first boss to turn on FIFA president - who is WORSE than Sepp Blatter - as pressure mounts after World Cup farce

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Published: 08:02 EST, 16 November 2026 | Updated: 10:36 EST, 16 November 2026

Jurgen Klopp has called on FIFA's embattled president Gianni Infantino to resign, becoming the first national team manager to do so.

Infantino's reputation took a nosedive after his sycophantic behaviour towards Donald Trump before and during the World Cup - most memorably by awarding him a 'FIFA Peace Prize'.

And it only got worse when his clandestine plot to sell off stakes in the World Cup, a devious scheme which would have banked him millions, was revealed by The Times.

Now Germany manager Klopp has hit out: 'There is now a unanimous consensus that a change would make sense.

'I thought that, after Sepp Blatter, the situation couldn't get any worse - but here we are.

'Regarding Gianni Infantino, surely the next person to come along can do a better job.

Jurgen Klopp has publicly savaged FIFA president Gianni Infantino, becoming the first national team manager to do so

The Germany manager said Infantino - who tried to sell off stakes in the World Cup to his own benefit - is 'worse' than his predecessor Sepp Blatter

'Without a doubt, there are people who truly care about football and have no financial interests,' the former Liverpool manager said.

Infantino has clung on to his job thus far, surviving a crisis summit in Morocco in August, despite intense and widespread opposition.

A total of 90 nations including all of UEFA's teams rebelled, with threats to boycott FIFA competitions, and Infantino's £3.1billion plan fell apart.

But he still maintained a large swell of support among Africa, Asia, and South America - especially after offering each nation £30million if they supported his plan. His opponents pointed out that he could simply have given money from FIFA's cash reserves of roughly £4.5b.

In the pages of this historic newspaper, football legend Luis Figo wrote that 'Infantino must go' and accused him of 'debasing the office of FIFA President'.

FIFA does not have a history of picking good leaders - Blatter resigned in 2015 amid allegations of corruption, though he was cleared alongside Michel Platini last year.

Infantino now looks well-placed to be re-elected for a fourth term in next March's presidential elections.

The deadline for candidacies to be submitted is November 18.

Philippe Diallo, the French Football Federation president, has agreed to run against Infantino and has the backing of UEFA's executive committee.

'(Diallo has) extensive experience across all levels of the game, having led the organisation representing French professional football clubs, served on the board of the French professional league and within FIFA’s dispute resolution structures, before becoming President of the FFF and a member of the UEFA Executive Committee in 2023,' UEFA said.

Former Tottenham defender Ramon Vega has already announced his intention to be a rival to Infantino.

Kozo Tashima, the honorary president of the Japanese Football Association, is also reportedly set to mount a challenge.

Ex-Arsenal manager Arsene Wenger was touted as a potential challenger but it is understood he told European football’s governing body UEFA that he would not stand.
