Duncan Ferguson has apologised for his behaviour after accepting a seven-match suspension following his sending off in the Irish Premiership on Friday.

The former Everton striker was dismissed in just his fourth game in charge of Glentoran after protesting to the referees during his side’s 3-2 defeat to Cliftonville last week.

Ferguson, who has won just one of his four games in charge of Glentoran, had complained about Cliftonville’s opening goal and believed there had been a foul in the build-up.

“I want to apologise to the match officials for my behaviour on Friday night,” Ferguson said in a a statement issued by Glentoran.

“I was frustrated by what happened on the pitch and let my emotions get the better of me.

“I went too far in the way I expressed that frustration towards the officials, and I should have handled the situation better.

“I accept the suspension and I’ll learn from what happened.

“My focus now is on the players, the staff and the work we’ve got to do together, both on and off the pitch.”

Ferguson returned to management with Glentoran last month after previous spells with Forest Green and Inverness Caledonian Thistle.

He had been a pundit for ITV during the summer and worked with the broadcaster during the World Cup in North America.

Glentoran, who last won the league in 2009, sit fourth in the Irish Premiership but are 17 points behind leaders Linfield.
