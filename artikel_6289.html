United Airlines CEO Talks Starlink In Veterans Day Message

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

United Airlines CEO Scott Kirby used his Veterans Day message to thank the 8,500 veterans who work at the airline. But before turning to their service, he spent a substantial part of the message discussing Starlink, winning customers from competitors, and his belief that United is the best airline in the world. The opening offers another glimpse into what Kirby sees as the opportunity ahead in 2027.

I typically share Kirby’s internal Veterans Day message each year (he also posted publicly on LinkedIN). His appreciation for military service and his affection for the United States are familiar themes. This year, though, he began with a thank-you for the summer operation and a look ahead at how better Wi-Fi might persuade passengers to switch airlines.

Happy Veterans Day everyone. Before I start on my Veterans Day message, I want to thank everyone for a really incredible summer. All the hard work you do. There was a lot of weather this summer, but the United team just did a great job so thank you. I also want to tell you how excited I am for 2027. In 2027 we’re going have a unique advantage in Starlink. And what that means is we’re gonna have a lot of customers who are loyal to other airlines today try us because of Starlink and I can already can hear this from customers. What’s going to happen is I hope they’re going to realize that United really is the best airline in the world when they come try United. We’re gonna be able to get those customers to come to United because of Starlink but to stay with United because everything else United is better. So thank you in advance for what you’re gonna do to win new brand loyal customers in 2027 for us at United.

But what I really wanted to do today is thank the 8,500 veterans that work here at United Airlines. I think all of you know that I am deeply patriotic. I love this country. I feel fortunate to have been born in this country and think it’s the greatest country in the history of the planet, but that wouldn’t be the case if it wasn’t for all the veterans who work at United but all the other veterans and the people that serve today. So to all the veterans not just at United, but around the world who have served to put their lives on the line to keep this country free and great, thank you for your service. Thank you for everything that you’ve done. And today when you’re out working and you see a veteran, and I’m sure you will because there’s so many United, give them a special thank you for their service. So to everyone at United, thank you for everything that you’ve done. And the veterans, a special thank you on Veteran’s Day and I look forward to seeing you in the friendly skies.

The commercial opening is an unusual preface to a Veterans Day tribute, though Kirby explicitly separates it from the message that follows. That makes sense considering he is addressing all employees and using the opportunity to discuss more than one subject. Still, his emphasis is revealing.

Kirby sees Starlink as a reason for passengers loyal to other airlines to give United a try, which is likely true. The more ambitious part is his expectation that the rest of the experience will persuade them to stay.

Reliable, fast internet can absolutely influence my booking decision. If I need to work during a flight, connectivity is more important than modest differences in the seat or meal and I’m hardly alone.

The second proposition is true, though certainly easier said than done. A passenger attracted by Starlink still has to navigate the airport, interact with the crew, and arrive at the destination. A great internet connection may get someone to book once. How United handles that journey, especially when something goes wrong, will help determine whether the passenger returns.

I remain unconvinced that there is one universally “best” airline. But Kirby is giving his employees a clear assignment: use the opportunity created by Starlink to earn repeat business. That is indeed a useful objective and to Untied’s credit, it improving operational performance and overall onboard product are giving front-line staff the means to deliver.

Turning to Veterans Day, Kirby’s message is direct. He credits those who have served with protecting the country he loves and asks employees to thank the veterans they encounter at work. I appreciate that practical request and will do the same today in my own interactions (I’m not flying, though).

Gratitude does not require agreement with every military deployment or government decision. We can (and should) scrutinize those decisions while appreciating the people asked to carry them out and the sacrifices their service entails.

Kirby’s Veterans Day message combines a familiar expression of patriotism with an enthusiastic commercial outlook for 2027. His expectation that Starlink will bring new passengers through the door does indeed create a golden opportunity for United. Whether United earns their loyalty will depend on what happens beyond the Wi-Fi connection.

But today’s central message deserves its own acknowledgment. To the veterans at United Airlines and elsewhere, thank you for your service.
