Cathie Wood's ARK Sells $85 Million in Nvidia, Buys $40 Million in Crypto Stocks

Cathie Wood's ARK Invest sold roughly $85 million worth of Nvidia shares Monday while buying close to $40 million in crypto-exposed stocks, rebalancing exposure to technology and fintech stocks during a broad selloff.

The firm's daily trade file for November 16 shows sales of 543,092 Nvidia shares across two actively managed exchange-traded funds. Using Monday's $156.14 closing price, those shares were worth approximately $84.8 million; the disclosures list share counts rather than the exact prices ARK received.

ARK bought 277,523 shares of payments company Block, worth about $23 million at the close. It also added 180,534 shares of stablecoin issuer Circle, valued at $10.4 million, and 53,760 shares of crypto exchange Coinbase, worth around $6.4 million.

The purchases were spread across funds rather than concentrated in one vehicle. ARK's flagship Innovation ETF (ARKK) bought Circle and most of the Block shares, while its Next Generation Internet ETF (ARKW) bought Coinbase and the remaining Block stock.

All three crypto-linked stocks fell Monday. Circle dropped 7.3%, Coinbase declined 6.1% and Block lost 1.9%, while Nvidia slid 4.1% as investors continued selling technology shares.

That makes the whole movement seem like a shift in holdings, not evidence that ARK expects cryptocurrencies to recover before AI stocks. The manager says its funds can rebalance every trading day, and its trade notifications don't disclose why each position was changed or establish that particular sales financed particular purchases.

ARK had bought 90,443 Nvidia shares just one week earlier, on November 9, during another selloff. The latest sale therefore reverses some of that buying without necessarily suggesting the firm has abandoned the chipmaker or its long-standing investment case for artificial intelligence.

ARK also purchased 378,515 shares of SpaceX, worth roughly $52 million, while selling 30,659 shares of crypto exchange Bullish and units of Ethereum and Solana staking funds. So in this case, Wood would be selling crypto to buy one single AI stock.

Those sales included 300,000 shares of the 3iQ Ethereum Staking ETF and 350,000 shares of the 3iQ Solana Staking ETF. The firm's buying of crypto companies coincided with cuts to other crypto-related investments, a distinction that matters when trying to read a trading strategy from one day's activity.

Wood has repeatedly bought companies that handle crypto trading and payments during market selloffs. In September, ARK bought $37 million in Block stock in one session, and it previously increased its Circle and Coinbase stakes while reducing exposure to other crypto names.

The companies offer different ways to invest in digital assets without holding tokens directly. Block runs Cash App and Bitcoin-related products; Circle issues the USDC dollar-backed stablecoin; and Coinbase, the only American crypto exchange listed on the stock market, earns revenue from cryptocurrency trading and other services.

ARK's strategy has never been a simple choice between crypto and AI. Its flagship fund already counted Circle, Coinbase and SpaceX among major positions in August, alongside businesses tied to artificial intelligence and financial technology, as earlier holdings showed.

Nvidia's leadership changed five days before Monday's trades. Co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on November 11, with finance chief Colette Kress appointed interim chief executive, according to the company's SEC filing.

The chipmaker is due to report fiscal third-quarter results after the market closes Tuesday, with a conference call at 5 p.m. Eastern. Those results will give investors their first earnings update since the leadership change—one day after ARK sold more than half a million shares.
