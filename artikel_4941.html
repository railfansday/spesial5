Robert Pattinson Is Officially the Next James Bond. Director James Gray Believes He Can Pull It Off.

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

“What Rob does have is something enigmatic about him,” Pattinson’s Lost City of Z director says. “And that enigmatic quality, I think, might serve very well for that.”

THE LONG SEARCH for a new James Bond has finally come to an end: Robert Pattinson will play the world’s most famous spy in the new installment from director Denis Villeneuve. His casting marks the conclusion of a race that not too long ago looked to be down to a trio of contenders—Callum Turner, Jack Lowden, and Josh O’Connor—to go along with a “mystery candidate.” Now, the man of mystery can claim victory (Pattinson and Nicholas Hoult were both revealed as late-in-the-game candidates to play 007).

Pattinson, who first achieved fame as Cedric Diggory in Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire in 2005 before skyrocketing to another level with the Twilight franchise starting in 2008, has spent the last 15 years establishing himself as perhaps our most unique and eccentric actor, a uniquely handsome talent who seems to relish in playing a series of freaks, weirdos, and deviants. Roles in movies like Josh and Benny Safdie’s Good Time and Robert Eggers’s The Lighthouse made it abundantly clear that Pattinson’s talent and ambition reach far beyond the franchises for which he initially gained recognition.

One of Pattinson’s best roles in that period came in 2016’s The Lost City of Z, an adventure film (based on the nonfiction book of the same name) from director James Gray. In Lost City, Pattinson plays Henry Costin, the eccentric assistant to Charlie Hunnam’s lead explorer Percy Fawcett.

In making Lost City of Z, Gray got an up-close opportunity to work with Pattinson and see just what he’s capable of as an actor. “The character he was playing with me—this bearded, oddball, left field explorer type—is so different from Bond,” Gray tells Men’s Health. “But that doesn’t mean he couldn’t do it. What Rob does have is something enigmatic about him. And that enigmatic quality, I think, might serve very well for that.”

Gray, who spoke with MH just a few days ahead of Pattinson’s official casting when speculation was at its peak, was fully confident in the 40-year-old’s ability to pull just about any role off, calling him a “fabulous actor,” and reflecting fondly on their time working together in the jungle.

“Look, they will know,” Gray continues. “I mean, Denis [Villeneuve] will know, he’s probably screen-tested already. I haven’t talked to him about it, but I’m sure he’s looked at all these screen tests, and it’s a thing that you know immediately when you see it on the big screen.”

Gray was onto something, as Pattinson has now officially been cast as the seventh actor to play James Bond, following in the footsteps of only Sean Connery, George Lazenby, Roger Moore, Timothy Dalton, Pierce Brosnan, and Daniel Craig.

Pattinson is at the tail end of what’s been a truly remarkable year, starring in The Drama, The Odyssey, and Primetime, with a villainous supporting role in Villeneuve’s Dune: Part Three still to come.

It’s also worth noting that Pattinson’s output hasn’t exclusively been weirdos and oddballs (although it is also worth noting that at one point he had a streak where his characters kept masturbating on screen in every movie). The Batman he proved he can expertly lead one of pop culture’s biggest and most valuable franchises in a way that he made totally his own (and is currently in the process of filming that film’s long-in-the-making sequel), and his role as Neil in Christopher Nolan’s Tenet may as well have been his unofficial Bond audition. Pattinson’s sleek, funny, and cool as hell as a time-traveling secret agent in that film, looking handsome and dapper along the way.

Gray himself is no stranger to talented actors; his latest film, Paper Tiger, pairs Adam Driver and Miles Teller. But he emphasizes the mere fact that Villeneuve and the film’s producers had Pattinson screen test for the role. “It means that they saw something special in him,” he says. “I’m sure he could pull it off—he’s a brilliant actor.”
