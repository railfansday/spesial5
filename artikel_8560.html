The spoiled produce and forgotten leftovers you throw out are groceries you paid for and never ate. Shop in smaller amounts for meals you’ve planned, store produce where it keeps longest, eat the oldest food first, and freeze whatever you won’t get to in time.

The EPA puts the cost of uneaten food at $2,913 a year for the average family of four, about $56 a week, counting restaurant meals as well as groceries. Cutting that waste means changing how you shop and store food, not what your family eats.

You buy groceries for meals that never get made, store produce in the wrong spot, and push leftovers to the back of the fridge until they turn. Meat is the costliest food to waste, by the EPA’s count, so it’s the first thing to freeze when dinner plans change.

These habits keep more of your groceries out of the trash:

Say a family logs everything it throws out for two weeks and finds $25 a week of spoiled produce and leftovers. An eat-first bin and a Friday leftovers night that cut that in half keep about $650 a year in its budget.

Start your own log tonight with a sheet on the fridge door, one line for each item you toss and what it cost.
