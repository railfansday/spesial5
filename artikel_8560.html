Several family chains let kids eat free one or two days a week, usually with one free kids’ meal for each paying adult entrée, and a couple let young kids eat free any day. Plan your family dinners out around those days, and confirm your location runs the deal before you go.

At Luby’s, where a kids’ meal costs $6.49, two adults with two kids 10 or younger who eat there on a kids-eat-free day pay only for their own meals and save $12.98. Four such dinners a month save $51.92.

Most of these deals work the same way. Each paying adult entrée earns one free kids’ meal for a child at or under an age cutoff, usually 10 to 12. Several chains also count only meals eaten in the restaurant.

That one-for-one ratio is the catch for bigger families. Two adults with three kids still pay for one kids’ meal, and some chains cap the free meals per table or per check instead of per adult.

These eight chains run their deals on set days of the week, listed from Tuesday on.

These two chains let young kids eat free every day of the week.

Some buffets also price kids’ meals by age. At the Pizza Ranch in Ames, Iowa, kids 1 to 12 pay $1.09 for each year of age, so a 6-year-old’s buffet costs $6.54.

On days without a deal, ordering water and sharing plates keeps the kids’ share of the bill low, and restaurant apps and birthday clubs give out free meals of their own.

Not every restaurant in a chain runs its deal. Red Robin, Smashburger, and Dickey’s limit theirs to participating locations, and Pizza Ranch’s age cutoffs and hours vary by location.

Smashburger and Dickey’s won’t combine their deals with any other offer, and Red Robin won’t combine its deal with its rewards, so save a coupon or reward for another visit. Ask whether the free kids’ meal includes a drink.

Pick the day that fits your week, then call the restaurant that day to confirm it’s running the deal and how many free kids’ meals your table gets.
