Mowbray support no surprise, says interim boss Johnson

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Tony Mowbray started his managerial career with Hibernian

The overwhelming support from the world of football for Blackburn Rovers boss Tony Mowbray has come as no surprise to interim head coach Damien Johnson.

Mowbray announced on Wednesday he was stepping back from his role at the Championship club because of health issues with Johnson and Mike Dodds taking charge "for the time being".

The news sparked an outpouring of messages of support from EFL clubs and further afield.

"To someone like me, it's not a surprise," Johnson said.

"I've known him for over 10 years, but I think people who have only known him in this club for the four or five months he's been in, they can see that. That's just the impact Tony has on people, it's just a testament to Tony and his nature and how he is and how he treats people."

Mowbray, 62, returned to Ewood Park in June for a second spell as head coach and has taken them to 13th place in the Championship after they finished just five points clear of the relegation zone last season.

The former Middlesbrough and West Brom boss had previously taken time out of the game during his spell as Birmingham City boss in early 2024 following a health scare, and later revealed he had been undergoing treatment for bowel cancer.

Boro head coach Kim Hellberg was one of the first to send his best wishes to one of his predecessors.

"He's a brilliant guy. It was really good to meet him and he was absolutely brilliant every time I've talked to him," Hellberg said.

"He's a brilliant football coach. He's done a lot of good things. He's a brilliant human being and you can see everyone knows that."

Rovers have won five of their past nine league games with Johnson crediting Mowbray's impact at the club for creating a more positive atmosphere.

"When the statement came out there was concern - we care a lot about Tony and it was a little bit of a shock - we want what's best for him at this time," Johnson told BBC Radio Lancashire.

"He sent a private message to the players which was read out today [Friday] and they appreciated it. He's sent some private messages to staff but Tony's allowed us just to get on, he'll be allowed the time he needs and we'll just get on with the game."

Blackburn visit Burnley on Sunday (12:00 GMT) hoping to end a 10-game winless run against their Lancashire rivals, who have not won a home game in the league since 18 October last year.

Johnson says the match is their main focus rather than what may happen with the managerial situation down the line.

"We've just been focused fully on the game, we know it's a huge game and we've been working with the team throughout the week. The discussions have been about the game and nothing else," he added.

Meanwhile, Blackburn are set to be without goalkeeper Balazs Toth until the new year after he told Hungarian media, external he suffered a hand injury while playing for his national side against Northern Ireland on Tuesday.

"The situation is not that good, but for now it looks like I will be able to avoid surgery," he said before travelling back to England to have the injury assessed by Blackburn.
