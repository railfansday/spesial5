New York Gov. Kathy Hochul Signs New Private Listings Bill

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

New York has signed a new private listings bill, which means that properties will be required to be visible to the public as they hit the market unless the seller signs an informed opt-out consent.

Late last week, Gov. Kathy Hochul signed the Fair and Transparent Real Estate Listing Act. The language of the bill requires that real estate listings be marketed in "at least one other publication, platform or website that does not require payment" or for the consumer to work with the brokerage for access.

This follows a quick path through the state's Legislature, which approved the act in a noncontroversial vote in June. The New York State Association of Realtors® strongly supports the bill.

This becomes the latest crackdown on private listings, where a real estate agent markets a property to a limited list of buyers before making it public.

Fair housing advocates say private listings raise discrimination concerns. One study from Bright MLS found private listings take longer to sell and don't guarantee more money.

The listing must be accessible to the general public, "without limitation to a particular brokerage, franchise or select group of licenses." Also, there can't be restrictions on some or all of the details in the listing, according to the language of the bill.

The bill doesn't require a seller or lister to hold open houses or public showings if it violates any state or federal laws related to health and safety. And sellers can opt out, by signing a form that explicitly warns them that the listing will have limited visibility and possible impact on the price they get.

New York can punish violators with a fine of up to $5,000 and suspension or revocation of  their real estate license.

Earlier this year, the state saw its inventory of homes for sale fall to a 30-year low.

Washington and Connecticut have passed similar laws this year. Each has generally similar language in their bills, which also allow for opt-outs. A study from Zillow found the sales price of private listings was 1.3% less than comparable sales on the multiple listing service, costing the seller an average of $4,230 per sale. (Realtor.com® recently struck an agreement with Zillow to show preview listings from brokers who participate in Zillow’s preview program, in an effort to boost market transparency.)

Compass Inc., the real estate brokerage giant which has defended its use of private listings, said its recently unveiled See It First listings strategy complies with the law. It launched that program in September, which gives early access to homes that will soon be listed on the public market. Users don't need to work with a Compass agent to use it.

"New York’s new law preserves homeowners’ right to choose how they market their homes, including the ability to use a See It First strategy to test pricing and gauge demand," a Compass spokesperson said. "See It First early access homes comply with the new law and continue to give sellers flexibility in how and when they bring their homes to market."

New York has long struggled with housing affordability. The Realtor.com State-by-State Housing Report Card gave the Empire State an F, the lowest rating possible. Hochul has emphasized affordability concerns and made a deregulation push to make home construction easier.

“My administration is focused on making New York more affordable and liveable for every New Yorker regardless of age or income, and housing is central to that goal,” Hochul said last week.
