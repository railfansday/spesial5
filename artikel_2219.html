QPR have big January decision to make over £500k ex-Coventry City man

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Queens Park Rangers are well-positioned for a push towards a play-off place following a decent start to the season, but one of their players hasn't been getting a look in.

As teams return from the last international break of 2026, Queens Park Rangers are in a fairly strong position in the EFL Championship. Rangers return in 8th place in the table, and with the division's play-off places having been expanded from four to six from this season, staying there would be enough to earn them a shot at a place in the Premier League should they be able to maintain their position.

But success for a club doesn't necessarily mean success for all of their players, and for their Trinidad & Tobagan international forward Justin Obikwu, opportunities have been thin on the ground. Having made only a handful of appearances for the club since his £500,000 move from Coventry City in January 2026, Obikwu will be crying out for some game-time, and the January transfer window could give him the opportunity to get some of that in by going elsewhere for the remainder of this season.

While Queens Park Rangers have been going reasonably well so far throughout 2026-27, things have been more difficult for forward Justin Obikwu. The forward had started last season on loan at Lincoln City, but was recalled by Coventry in January to be sold to Rangers.

The move to QPR has not brought him many more opportunities than he was getting in the West Midlands, though.

He didn't make a single appearance for Rangers throughout the second half of last season, and this season has brought only three appearances from the bench and a total of 34 minutes on the pitch, while the attacking positions have been occupied by a combination of Richard Kone, Paul Smith, Koki Saito, Alfie Lloyd, Kwame Poku and Rumarn Burrell.

Obikwu finally made his debut for QPR from the bench against Cardiff City at the start of September, and has followed that up with brief appearances in their last two matches before the last international break, against Burnley and Lincoln. These appearances may give the forward hope that he'll get further opportunities before the end of this year, but there's no guarantee of this and if it doesn't happen for him before January, it might turn out more prudent for him to go elsewhere and get that all-important game-time.

Justin Obikwu was spotted in the youth system at non-league Harefield United and signed by Coventry City in 2021, signing his first senior professional contract two years later, but he never got to make a single appearance for the Midlands club. Instead, over the two and a half years that he was on the books with Coventry, he was sent out on loan on three occasions, twice to Grimsby Town and then to Lincoln City.

His second spell at Grimsby offered a glimpse of his potential. Although not a regular in the first team at Blundell Park, he still managed seven goals in 19 League appearances for Grimsby, and his loan move to Lincoln City for 2025-26 was a clear step up for him.

But since arriving at Loftus Road in January, he hasn't been picked in a QPR starting eleven yet. He made one appearance for their youth team in a 4-1 Premier League Cup win against Bolton Wanderers in the middle of August, in which he scored, but his appearances from the bench for the first team since then have been both brief and sporadic.

Appearing from the bench in the last two consecutive matches prior to the international break offers him a glimmer of light, but there are no guarantees that he'll get more game-time and, if anything, Queens Park Rangers are slightly overstocked with young attacking players at the moment.

A move to a League One or League Two club in the January transfer window, then, would make sense. Such a move doesn't have to close the door on him having a future at Loftus Road. He may even impress enough to enhance his chances of pushing for a first-team place for the 2027-28 season. And the club would get the opportunity to see what he can do without regular game-time without having to take any risks themselves.

Now 22 years of age, Justin Obikwu's career could be approaching a crossroads. He still has the potential to shine in the higher divisions of the EFL, but his progress has stalled, and after almost a year with Queens Park Rangers, his opportunities have remained limited. Some time away from Loftus Road could reignite his career and give his club the opportunity to see whether he can develop further as a player.
