Following leaks where it made its way into stores early, Lego Godzilla has officially been revealed... and it's monstrously big.

Towering over other models at 27.5 inches (70cm), this set packs over 5,300 pieces and shows the king of the monsters firing a nuclear breath attack. Lego Godzilla is due to launch on November 27, and you can get a better look at it via the Lego store. If you're quick, you should also receive a free gift-with-purchase of Godzilla's logo amidst a ruined cityscape and colossal footprint.

However, be aware that Lego Godzilla is also packing an equally sizeable cost at $450/£350. While that price tag may be just as large as the model it's attached to, it's worth pointing out this isn't an anomaly; it's in line with with other, similarly-sized Lego models like Avengers Tower or the Star Wars Venator ship. I appreciate that this doesn't help much if you're put off by the cost, but it's useful context at least.

UK: £349.99 at LEGOThis 18+ behemoth of a display piece is packing a mighty 5,363 pieces. Its head, arms, tail, and fingers can all be posed, and it comes with a bolt of blue flame that can be placed within its jaws if you want an action-shot.

Much like the Lego Wallace & Gromit set that fans fell in love with, this kit is part of the Ideas' range – in other words, it's based on a fan-submission that earned 10,000 community votes to become a real model. However, unlike the original design that showed Godzilla hunched over in a classic battle pose, this final draft has him standing up straight to prepare a nuclear blast that's lighting his spines up with an eerie blue glow. If that image seems familiar, it's because the idea comes straight from an iconic moment in 2019's Godzilla: King of the Monsters.

Yep, this version of the lizard is based on the Monsterverse franchise rather than the original Toho design or the more recent Minus Zero movie – as evidenced by a screenshot from King of the Monsters being featured on the back of the box.

While the aforementioned leaks got a mixed reception (with some fans being disappointed by the end result), I'm quite fond of the finished look. Those spines are such a bright blue that they sell the illusion they're glowing, for example, and it helps what could otherwise have been an indistinct lump of gray plastic pop. Did I prefer the more bestial look of the original community design? Yes. But the more I see of this, the more I like it.

I do hear those who wish this model had seen Godzilla smashing through a city, though. In fact, I've had the same conversation twice in one day with colleagues, and I think they're right; that kind of diorama would be amazing, though it'd obviously have to be a fraction of the size. Actually, I'd accept a smaller scale if it meant having little skyscrapers for the kaiju to stomp over; it'd really evoke that feeling of oversized. With any luck, it'd then be a good bit cheaper as well.
