McBurnie’s added-time double snatches win for Hull to shock 10-man Brighton

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Amid the talk of Brighton being surprise title contenders this season, these are perhaps the afternoons which show the margin that still exists, and how the Premier League can provide the most punishing of wake-up calls at any moment.

Make no mistake about it, Fabian Hürzeler’s side will play far worse than this over the next few months and win. They are still second in the table. How they left Hull without even a point almost defies belief given the dominance they enjoyed for large parts of the contest. But for all the statement victories and the headline-grabbing moments, these games are the ones which can trip you up the most.

A blisteringly cold November afternoon in Hull, against a promoted side where the expectation for a team challenging for the title is to win, and perhaps win with minimum fuss, too. On another afternoon, Brighton may have done just that, were it not for the heroics of Hull’s goalkeeper, Konstantinos Tzolakis.

But the best teams, the ones that end the seasons lifting the silverware, almost always seem to find a way. Tzolakis was the Seagulls’ scourge with a string of sensational saves that kept Hull in a contest where, for large periods, they were struggling to keep their heads above water.

However, the goal Brighton deserved never arrived – and as this game wore on you wondered if a team like Hull, whose commitment can never be questioned and who are tough to beat on their own turf, could dig in for long enough to provide a sucker punch on the counter. That moment arrived, not perhaps with Oli McBurnie’s goal that broke the deadlock in stoppage time, but two minutes before.

When João Costinha was shown a second yellow card in the 89th minute for a foul on Sorba Thomas, the MKM Stadium responded. They sensed that Tzolakis’s heroics could perhaps produce more than just a point, with Brighton forced to sit in and try to hold out for the closing moments. Hull surged forward, with McBurnie bundling home from close to range to spark delirium among the home support.

Two minutes later, with Brighton now left pressing just to try to find an equaliser, Hull caught them cold and McBurnie would tap into an empty net to seal the most unlikely of victories for the Tigers, who remain unbeaten at home since promotion. This was their first win since August, however, and a welcome tonic to put breathing space between themselves and the bottom three.

“We played against an excellent team who play intensive football,” Hull’s manager, Sergej Jakirovic, said. “I wish this feeling would never stop because we’ve had to wait for this.” But this was an afternoon that was as much about Brighton as it was Hull.

A win here would have closed the gap to Arsenal at the top to three points. In the end, this was a huge missed opportunity, with Hürzeler understandably crestfallen post-match.

“In the Premier League you can never expect to win,” he said. “For us, it’s very important with our identity and every game you have to lift your standards. If you drop, the Premier League is too good and it will punish you.”

McBurnie may have been the hero but there was no doubting the star man was Tzolakis. He denied Brighton on so many occasions and otherwise the game could have been over before the interval. First, he denied Diego Gómez from close range before stopping Yasin Ayari’s effort.

Two minutes later, Georginio Rutter was the next to be denied by the Greece international with another smart save before the pick of the bunch: Tzolakis made a reaction save with his foot to turn Gómez’s effort wide after Brighton cut Hull open at will again. At that stage it felt like when, not if, the visitors went ahead. Nobody told Tzolakis that.

Half-time did little to change the flow of momentum. Pascal Gross was next to be denied by Tzolakis before Gómez then shot wide from six yards out. At the other end, Hull were showing signs of coming to life, with two Mohamed Belloumi breaks a warning sign of what was to follow for the visitors.

Tzolakis was finally beaten by Charalampos Kostoulas when his chipped effort beat his fellow countryman, but Regan Slater was on hand to clear off the line. Then, as time ebbed away, Costinha’s second yellow decisively swung the momentum and within two minutes, Hull scored the goal Brighton had craved.

First, McBurnie bundled home before Belloumi’s break led to him squaring for the Scotland international to tap into an empty net. Was it what Brighton deserved? Probably not. But try persuading anyone of a Hull persuasion to care about that.
