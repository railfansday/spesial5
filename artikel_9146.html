Piaget’s Latest Release Is a Limited-Edition Andy Warhol Camouflage Watch

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Where do the worlds of art and watchmaking intersect? One place is the Andy Warhol Watch line, which the luxury Swiss watch brand Piaget has released since 2024 in collaboration with the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts.

To learn more about Piaget’s history with Warhol and its ongoing partnership with the Warhol Foundation, Jean-Bernard Forot, Piaget’s Head of Patrimony, joined ARTnews for a conversation as part of the inaugural Art Week NYC, a citywide festival celebrating contemporary art. Forot spoke about the history of the Maison Piaget, which was founded in 1874 in the idyllic village of La Côte-aux-Fées (a translation of which is “fairy hills”), as well as the company’s two manufactures, in La Côte-aux-Fées and Geneva, where the watches are conceived and produced. Having those two locations, he said, “creates a tension and creates a creativity that is really interesting.”

In the postwar era, Piaget became known for its bold designs featuring precious gold and ornamental stones. When it opened a US flagship on Fifth Avenue, it attracted high-profile clients like Elizabeth Taylor, Elvis Presley, and Jackie Kennedy—all of whom were subjects of Warhol’s art.

Warhol soon became a client of Piaget’s and befriended Yves Piaget, the company’s longtime chairman. The Pop artist came to own at least seven Piagets, including the cushion-shaped Black Tie Watch, which Forot speculated captured Warhol’s attention for its resemblance to a TV screen. After the company reissued it decades later, collectors lovingly called it the Warhol Watch, which ultimately led Piaget to seek out a partnership with the Warhol Foundation to officially call it the “Andy Warhol Watch.”

“We did not invent a story,” Forot said. “In fact, we are just taking a story that existed and are bringing it into the present today. That’s why there is authenticity.”

In 2025, Piaget released its “Collage” Warhol Watch, based on a Warhol self-portrait from 1986, in an edition of 50. This year’s “Camouflage” Warhol Watch, referencing the artist’s Camouflage self-portrait also from 1986, has an edition of 86 to honor the year of the work’s making. In both instances, Forot said that Piaget has developed, over many months and several tests, a “reinterpretation” of each Warhol work, leaning into the Maison’s history of pairing gold and ornamental stones into compelling juxtapositions.

“Whether you are an artist in front of a white canvas or whether you want to create a watch or a piece of jewelry,” Forot said, “everything starts with the same desire to create something new, to create something appealing, to try to create about beauty.”
