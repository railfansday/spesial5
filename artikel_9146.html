Nan Goldin to debut new film exploring nature and extinction in first UK solo museum show in over two decades

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Nan Goldin, Still from You never did anything wrong, Part II, 2026

© Nan Goldin, courtesy the artist and Gagosian.

For audiences who associate the US photographer Nan Goldin with the friends and lovers of her slideshow The Ballad of Sexual Dependency (1973-86), her forthcoming Hayward Gallery exhibition may come as a surprise. Animals, a solar eclipse and footage salvaged from nature documentaries will feature in Goldin’s first institutional solo show in the UK since her 2002 retrospective at the Whitechapel Gallery.

What links these subjects to the photographs that established her reputation? According to the Hayward’s chief curator, Rachel Thomas, the connection lies in the relationships Goldin creates through the camera, rather than the identities of those who appear before it. In Thomas’s reading, Goldin’s focus on queer communities and chosen family has widened to embrace the natural world.

Nan Goldin, Still from You never did anything wrong, Part II, 2026

© Nan Goldin, courtesy the artist and Gagosian.

Rather than being a retrospective, the exhibition You Never Did Anything Wrong presents new and recent work by Goldin. Thomas, who worked with the photographer at the Irish Museum of Modern Art in 2017, tells The Art Newspaper: “I wanted to collaborate again to reveal where her practice is moving now.” Four years in the making, the exhibition is the Hayward’s contribution to the Southbank Centre’s 75th-anniversary programme devoted to “visionaries who have fundamentally redefined culture”.

The show’s centrepiece will be the premiere of You never did anything wrong, Part II (2026). Created with the film-maker David Sherman, it draws on 16mm nature documentaries from the 1960s and 70s, and damaged soundtracks from old films, with a new score by the British musician Mica Levi. Goldin turns material associated with explaining the natural world into an abstract meditation on animals, land and impending extinction.

Nan Goldin, Still from You never did anything wrong, Part I, 2024

© Nan Goldin, courtesy the artist and Gagosian.

It accompanies You never did anything wrong, Part I (2024), which centres on a solar eclipse and draws on a myth of animals stealing the sun. Together, the films bring Goldin’s work into a world without human protagonists.

Elsewhere, Stendhal Syndrome (2024), receiving its UK premiere, places photographs of her friends and lovers in dialogue with classical art, accompanied by a score by Soundwalk Collective. Memory Lost (2019-21), meanwhile, returns to addiction, connecting personal experience to Goldin’s campaigning with Pain (Prescription Addiction Intervention Now), the group she founded to confront the opioid crisis in the US.

Art of intimacy: Young Love (2024) is one of Goldin’s works in Stendhal Syndrome © Nan Goldin; courtesy the artist and Gagosian

For Thomas, compassion connects these changing subjects. Goldin’s importance lies partly in her refusal to maintain a distance from the people she photographs, with the exhibition proposing that this approach can extend beyond her own community. “What endures across every medium and chapter is an exploration of compassion,” Thomas says. “Goldin simply expands the horizon of who and what is embraced by her gaze.”

• You Never Did Anything Wrong, Hayward Gallery, London, 24 November-14 March 2027
