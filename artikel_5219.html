Vitalik Buterin Breaks Silence on New Ethereum (ETH) Update

Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin has published a lengthy post on EIP-8037, a change planned for the Glamsterdam upgrade. In his latest post, Buterin wrote that something like EIP-8037 is, unfortunately, the only way for Ethereum to scale safely.

With the update, Ethereum will collect an additional gas fee for the creation of new accounts. Creating a new account would cost more than five times as much gas as it does now, with new storage slots and contracts getting more expensive too.

According to Buterin, execution is easy to scale, but Ethereum's state already grows by around 110 GiB per year, and every node has to keep it.

Unfortunately, it will bring more pressure on existing contracts, for example, the OP Stack bridge. The bridge has a hardcoded gas limit of 40,000, and even a small increase in state gas costs would push its operation above it. Mainnet has at least 13 contracts like that, according to the security firm ChainSecurity.

It's a double-edged sword: from one standpoint, it limits the ability of spammers and scammers to fill the network with junk accounts used by illicit actors; from the other, it creates additional financial pressure.

Glamsterdam being postponed is another pain factor here: considering the poor state of the market and Ethereum's struggle, it's clear that any delays in major updates negatively affect the network outlook. The new date is yet to be confirmed.

Vitalik Buterin has been hinting at the change in gas consumption for ages now. Back in 2024, he called multidimensional gas the hardest problem in his own post on the topic.

Setting strict limits was never a farsighted option, and developers have been told that for years. However, it's yet another question: who's actually to blame? Buterin himself admitted that this rule hasn't been written down or repeated since 2021, so newer developers simply didn't know about it.

He now suggests writing down a list of features that are likely to change, from gas mechanics to slot time.
