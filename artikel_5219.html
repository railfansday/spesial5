Circle’s $182 million crypto token sale masks revenue decline as USDC business weakens

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Circle reported 17% revenue growth and an earnings beat, but stripping out ARC presales reveals weaker underlying performance despite soaring USDC adoption.

Circle's $182 million ARC token presale drove its third-quarter earnings surprise even as its core USDC reserve income declined.

The stablecoin issuer reported adjusted EBITDA of $317 million, substantially exceeding Wall Street's $218 million consensus estimate and Clear Street's $220 million forecast.

However, the investment bank said the earnings beat was primarily driven by ARC token presale revenue, which exceeded its $90 million projection by $92 million. Excluding that contribution, Circle's adjusted EBITDA would have been approximately $135 million, down from $143 million in the preceding quarter.

The same divergence appeared in revenue. Circle reported a 17% increase in total revenue and reserve income to $865 million, but excluding the token presale would have left approximately $683 million, below the $740 million recorded a year earlier.

Its traditional reserve business remained under pressure despite growing stablecoin adoption. Reserve income fell 8% to $658 million as the average yield on USDC reserves declined by 61 basis points to 3.5%, offsetting an 8% increase in average circulation.

The weaker reserve performance reflects Circle's continued exposure to interest rates, which determine much of the income it earns from the assets backing USDC.

Clear Street also identified the presale's outsized contribution to Circle's profitability. Revenue less distribution costs reached $453 million, producing a 52.4% margin. Without ARC, that margin would have been approximately 39.7%, below the 41.2% recorded in the second quarter.

Circle's financial results came as USDC adoption accelerated across global payment networks, giving the company a growing base of activity to monetize beyond its reserve business.

CEO Jeremy Allaire said USDC accounted for 79% of stablecoin transaction volume during the quarter, up from 66% a year earlier. Onchain transfers increased 49% to $13.9 trillion, while minting and redemptions reached a record $208 billion.

The growth was particularly pronounced in Circle Payments Network, which connects financial institutions for cross-border settlement. Annualized transaction volume reached $41.3 billion at the end of September, more than 15 times its year-earlier level, before exceeding $58 billion after the quarter ended.

Circle is expanding that infrastructure through its proposed acquisition of Tazapay, a cross-border payments provider that would extend its local payout capabilities. The transaction is expected to close in 2027.

The company is also positioning its Arc blockchain as infrastructure for institutional settlement and programmable payments. More than 100 institutions and applications participated in its public mainnet launch in September, while Circle reported that USDC settled 99.6% of payment volume on the x402 protocol.

These initiatives come ahead of the GENIUS Act's implementation in January, which Allaire expects to accelerate the adoption of regulated digital dollars.

Circle expects its payments network to generate material revenue in 2027, although management has yet to provide a formal forecast. Clear Street estimates that other revenue will decline to between $28 million and $48 million in the fourth quarter, reflecting the unusually large ARC contribution recognized in September.
