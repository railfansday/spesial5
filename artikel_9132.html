Russian strikes damage historic buildings in Kyiv and Odesa, including World Heritage sites

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

St Volodymyr’s Cathedral in Kyiv Photo by Wavemeart, via Wikimedia Commons

Multiple heritage sites in Ukraine were damaged by Russian strikes on 19 and 20 November, including the Unesco-listed St Michael’s Golden-Domed Cathedral and Monastery complex and St Volodymyr’s Cathedral in Kyiv, known for its frescoes, as well as 12 cultural heritage sites in Odesa, the strategic port city on the Black Sea, which was hit a day earlier. The entire historic centre of Odesa was added to Unesco’s list of endangered World Heritage sites in 2023.

At least nine people were killed in Kyiv and other regions of Ukraine during the hours-long attack on 20 November.

“Today, Russian drones damaged one of the most famous monuments of our country, the Volodymyr Cathedral,” Tetyana Berezhna, Ukraine’s deputy prime minister for humanitarian policy and minister of culture, wrote in a Facebook post on 19 November after the cathedral in Kyiv was hit, breaking windows and frames and causing “some damage to the paintings and structural elements”.

The neo-Byzantine St Volodymyr Cathedral, built in the 19th century, is under the jurisdiction of the Orthodox Church of Ukraine (OCU), which broke with the Moscow Patriarchate and was granted autocephaly by Constantinople in 2018. Three of Russia’s most famous artists of the 19th century and early 20th century—Viktor Vasnetsov, Mikhail Vrubel and Mikhail Nesterov—created religious art for St Volodymyr’s, as did the Ukrainian artist Mykola Pymomenko, a teacher of Kazimir Malevich.

The Austrian embassy, located next-door to St Volodymyr's Cathedral, was also damaged. Kyiv's municipal cultural heritage department published an inventory of damage from the attack, which also includes the embassy of the Netherlands.

A Russian drone was shot down over St Michael’s, which is the headquarters of the OCU, on 19 November.

Berezhna’s post cites the cumulative damage from Russia’s accelerating attacks: “Since the beginning of this year, the number of monuments damaged by Russia has increased by more than a third, and by the end of October it was almost 2,200. Almost 2,700 cultural institutions were also affected.”

The minister was in Lutsk, a historic city in northwestern Ukraine, this week to plan for a conference marking the 600th anniversary in 2029 of the Congress of Lutsk, which brought together the regional monarchies of the time. A Russian strike on Lutsk in October damaged Ukraine’s largest painting, Cosmogony, a 2,000 sq. m work by the contemporary artist Petro Antyp.

Unesco has condemned the latest attacks, especially on Odesa, “which hit the centre of the city, within the boundaries of the World Heritage property ‘The Historic Centre of Odesa’” and “reportedly caused damage to several heritage buildings, including the Kalio Palace, a monument of national importance,” according to a statement released by the organisation. The Kalio Palace, built in 1834, is attributed to the Swiss Italian architect Giorgio Torricelli.

The strikes came amid escalating tensions and a diplomatic chasm that appears to be widening. The Russian President Vladimir Putin said this week that the Kremlin would not negotiate any partial ceasefire or deal with Ukraine after it struck and incapacitated the Kerch Bridge linking mainland Russia to Crimea, a symbol of Russia’s annexation of the Black Sea peninsula in 2014, on 16 and 17 November. At the same time, US President Donald Trump claimed that peace, largely on Russia’s terms, is within reach and reportedly urged European leaders to stop funding weapons for Ukraine. Trump, Putin and the Chinese President Xi Jinping signed a “peace declaration” on 20 November at a trilateral summit hosted by Xi in Shenzhen, China.

A petition to “safeguard Ukraine’s cultural heritage and artistic integrity” was launched last month by the curator Konstantin Akinsha and Bjorn Geldof, the artistic director at Kyiv’s PinchukArtCentre. It calls “for immediate sanctions and the suspension of Russian state participation in international cultural events”. Rapporteur, a Brussels-based policy publication, reported this week that ten European countries are seeking to keep Russia out of the 2027 Architecture Biennale. Russia’s participation in the 2026 Venice Biennale set off a series of protests.
