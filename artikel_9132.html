Grand Theft Auto VI takes over Miami, including its art destinations

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The digital duplicate of the Pérez Art Museum Miami (dubbed the "Future Leonida Art Museum") inside Grand Theft Auto VI Screenshot by The Art Newspaper. Courtesy Rockstar Games

Ahead of Art Basel Miami Beach, a massive marketing campaign for the blockbuster video game Grand Theft Auto (GTA) VI has transformed Miami—and the city’s art scene is coming along for the joyride.

On 13 October, the street art-focused Wynwood Walls museum unveiled a collaboration with the game’s developer Rockstar Games, inviting ten artists to create GTA-themed murals in and around the museum property. The museum also announced the partnership would include merchandising and free admission for all Miami-Dade County residents until the end of 2028, reversing a pandemic-era policy that saw tickets sold at the attraction for the first time.

The art-centric campaign is part of a massive marketing push around the game, which was released globally on 19 November to universal critical acclaim. Set in a satirical version of Miami called “Vice City”, the game world is an incredibly detailed virtual replica of the real city and the surrounding state of Florida (“Leonida”), complete with areas such as “Stockyard” replicating Wynwood with its street art and graffiti culture.

Art galleries in the digital replica of Wynwood (the "Stockyard" neighbourhood) inside Grand Theft Auto VI Screenshot by The Art Newspaper. Courtesy Rockstar Games

Demonstrating an extreme dedication to realism, Rockstar has even painstakingly replicated and renamed local landmarks such as the nightclub Club Space (“The Vault”), the beloved regional casual dining chain Flanigan’s (“Uncle Jack’s) and local art institutions such as the Pérez Art Museum Miami ("Future Leonida Art Museum", or "Flam") and the Wolfsonian–Florida International University design museum ("Hicksonian Museum of Art and Design"). Miami Beach’s Bass Museum of Art (“Vice City Museum of Art”) had its iconic Ugo Rondinone sculpture Miami Mountain (2016) turned into a monumental stack of emojis inside the game. (Unfortunately the current exhibition at the "Vice City Museum of Art", Karl Bergstrom: Diaspora, cannot be visited as the building is not currently accessible within gameplay.)

According to Jessica Goldman Srebnick, the Wynwood Walls museum’s chief executive and curator, Rockstar commissioned in-game murals from around 50 artists, many of whom have exhibited at the museum before. When the developer approached the museum to suggest a collaboration, it was a no-brainer. “Street art and gaming have a lot of shared cultural DNA, and they’ve influenced each other,” she says. The game’s creators “share our commitment to creativity, to supporting artists. They share our commitment and deep appreciation for the city of Miami. And I’m just really grateful that they are supporting something that provides more access to the arts.”

A Grand Theft Auto VI-themed mural by Queen Andrea in the real Wynwood neighbourhood of Miami Courtesy Queen Andrea and Wynwood Walls Museum

The murals in Wynwood, which will be on display until 1 January 2027, are similar to those featured in the game itself. A mural by the artist Queen Andrea, for instance, reads “Welcome to Vice City” in a curvy, neon-like font, while another by Ron English features a design with grenades in the shape of lightbulbs and the slogan: “There’s nothing more dangerous than a good idea.”

The project, as well as GTA’s larger presence in the city, has not been without controversy. The local newspaper Miami New Times reported on 10 November that one of the GTA murals had covered up a piece by the late artist Alex “Rect” Reyes, who finished the work shortly before his death in 2022. The wall had been considered a memorial by Reyes’s family and friends, who say they were not informed about its replacement. Goldman Global Arts, which owns the wall and museum, said in a statement to the New Times that it had offered Reyes’s crew a different wall to make amends.

The digital replica of Miami Beach's Bass Museum of Art within Grand Theft Auto VI, the "Vice City Museum of Art", with an emoji-themed parody of its Ugo Rondinone public sculpture Image courtesy of IGN / Official Grand Theft Auto VI Strategy Guide.

Meanwhile, a joint partnership with Miami-Dade County and the Miami Heat professional basketball team has resulted in everything from player uniforms to the Kaseya Center arena in Downtown Miami being rebranded with “Vice City”. Massive crowds turned out for a Heat game on 18 November, the eve of the GTA VI’s release, to purchase custom merchandise that was only available at the arena. The total package, which includes lighting and signage inspired by the game at Miami International Airport and PortMiami, is valued at $3.9m according to a press release from the county government. Some officials have expressed alarm at the collaboration, with one county commissioner worried that the game is “depicting Miami like a murder capital”, according to the Miami Herald.

As for the benefits for the local art scene, perhaps gamers who visit GTA VI’s virtual “Vice City Museum of Art” will be inspired to seek out the Bass on their next visit to Miami Beach.
