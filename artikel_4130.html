10 Changes You Might Not Have Noticed in The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Aden Carter is a Contributor at DualShockers who has been covering games professionally since 2020. He specializes in lists and guides, with a focus on RPGs, horror games, survival horror, Soulslikes, cozy/sim games, and action-adventure titles. Before joining DualShockers, Aden contributed to GameRant, XDA, Gamepur, I

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake is pretty amazing. The team didn’t mess around, and instead changed many aspects of this beloved Nintendo 64 title. It has updated graphics, slightly altered story beats, and more. Of course, since the team rebuilt Hyrule, that means they could make a few changes. Castle Town is now much more detailed, and even Death Mountain’s layout changed slightly.

These are just some of the big changes, however, as Nintendo made a lot more changes, and some of them you might not even have noticed since they are so small. Despite being small, these changes are just as important as the large ones, as they make the game feel more complete.

This is a very small change that you might not have realized was made between the original game and the remake, and that is that the merchants actually move. While some of the shop owners had animation before, they typically just looked at Link.

While playing the remake, you will see that each merchant is now far more animated, but the big difference is that they will actually turn their head to look at the item you are buying from them. While this change wasn’t needed, it definitely makes the shop owners feel more alive rather than watching them stand there.

Have you ever been to Castle Town at night in the original Ocarina of Time? No? Well, that is because you can’t go there at night. The drawbridge closes, preventing you from accessing the town after sunset. Luckily, you have the Sun Song to quickly change night into day, allowing you to enter the town.

This isn’t an issue, however, in the remake, as the town is constantly open to you. You can come and go as you please at any time of the day or night without anyone getting upset with you. You may have had it in your mind to keep going during the day, or maybe you just assumed that the gate was open and forgot that it closed in the original.

Fairy Thoughts didn’t exist in the original game. Instead, they are something new that is specific to the Ocarina of Time Remake. These small glowing symbols can be found on various surfaces around Hyrule, even in dungeons. Sometimes Fairy Thoughts act as hints to help you progress through the game, and other times they are small puzzles that fully restore Link’s health once they are solved.

They are a neat little change that you might not have noticed while exploring, as they can be easy to overlook. I didn’t notice them for a long time, and since I found one, I have been going around looking for more.

Link has always been known as the silent hero of Hyrule, but the remake appears to have given him a very brief spoken line.

You may have seen various little whirlwinds around Hyrule in the remake. These are used to summon the scarecrow, and they didn’t appear in the original. In the original Ocarina of Time, if you wanted to summon Pierre, you needed to find a spot where Navi would glow green instead of the normal blue. This indicated that it was a spot where the scarecrow song could be played.

Now, if you want to summon Pierre the Wandering Scarecrow, you simply need to find a place where “the straw swirls in the wind.” Which is any location with a bit of swirling wind. At least these locations are easier to find.

There are some who received the Giant’s Knife from Goron City back in the day, swung it around to test it, and then ultimately got disappointed when it broke before they even left the area and got to test it on some enemies. For those who don’t know, this weapon is very fragile, making it so it breaks after just eight hits. It is a cautionary tale that teaches everyone that nothing is permanent, except the Master Sword.

Anyway, the weapon still breaks in the remake, but there has been a change. Nintendo removed the hitboxes from walls, so you can swing the weapon around and not have it break off the environment. Instead, it will break from striking enemies, as was intended.

The Biggoron Sword is quite powerful, and in the original game, many replaced the Master Sword with it because it could be swung at the same pace and it hit harder. The only difference was you couldn’t use your shield while wielding it. Despite that drawback, the Biggoron Sword was worth holding onto, as an experienced swordfighter didn’t need to worry about using their shield for the most part.

This sword has been changed in the remake. It is still massive, and requires both hands to swing. However, the swing speed of it is noticeably slower, making it more difficult to hit some enemies with the blade. This put more emphasis on the use of the Master Sword in certain situations.

Since The Ocarina of Time is a third-person title, you might not have realized that there was a first-person option in the original. The game did go into first-person when shooting your slingtshot, but otherwise, you needed to press a button for it, and even then it was temporary.

The remake of the beloved title doesn’t even give you the option to go into first-person mode. Instead, it keeps you tied to the third-person camera. This is a bit of a strange change from the original and one that some people discovered right away, as they used to use first-person mode to get a closer look at the environment. To replace it, many have said that Nintendo should have added a photo mode.

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake has finally been released, and fans have already discovered several game-breaking glitches.

One of the side quests you can complete in Ocarina of Time is the one for the Happy Mask Salesman. This quest requires you to purchase various masks and sell them to citizens of Hyrule. However, in the original, you weren’t allowed to wear the masks as an adult. People always thought this was a bit strange, but it is a detail that didn’t matter much.

The remake, on the other hand, allows you to wear the various masks as an adult. There isn’t anything special about wearing the masks as an adult versus wearing them as a kid, but it is an interesting little detail that Nintendo added that wasn’t in the original game.

After progressing through the game, you finally make your way to the Spirit Temple. This is one of my personal favorite temples in the game, and like the other temples in the game, it has undergone some changes in the remake.

One of the more peculiar changes is the one made to the large Gerudo statue in the temple. In the original, certain parts of the statue were able to be grappled using your hookshot, but there were never any obvious grapple points. This has been changed in the remake, with the statue still being there, but the grapple points are now gone, so you can no longer grab the statue if it’s something that interests you.

This is a bit of an odd change, but one that people are thankful has been made. In the original Ocarina of Time, when you reach the Zora’s Domain, you talk to King Zora, and you eventually get him to scoot out of the way so you can visit Lord Jabu-Jabu. This scoot is obnoxiously long, as King Zora likes to take his time getting out of your way.

Well, in the remake, King Zora still scoots out of the way, but the change is that Nintendo made is significantly quicker. When timed, King Zora moves out of the way roughly eight seconds faster than he does in the original Ocarina of Time. Thankfully, we only need to wait around 20 seconds now instead of almost 30. Nintendo kept this hilarious if frustrating part of the title in, but streamlined it considerably.
