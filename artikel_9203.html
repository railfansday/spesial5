The Best Gifts for Cooks, Chefs, and the Culinary Curious

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Design Milk’s Taste columnist TJ Girard brings her perspective on food and design to this year’s gift guide, selecting thoughtful finds for home cooks, professional chefs, and anyone who loves gathering around the table.

The holiday season brings plenty of opportunities to gather around the table, and for those who love to cook, eat, and experiment in the kitchen, there’s no shortage of gift possibilities. A good gift should feel personal, whether it’s an object they’ll use for years or an ingredient worth sharing around the table.

This year’s selections come with stories of their own. There’s a Oaxacan potter who grows copal and tobacco to finish his clay, a Norwegian fisherman’s daughter who became convinced seaweed is the food of the future, and a former line cook who declared she had an apron company before she actually did.

You’ll find serving pieces that make entertaining easier, ingredients that start conversations, and small but indispensable kitchen tools that prove thoughtful doesn’t have to mean extravagant. Some are made for the table, others for the kitchen, and a few might inspire an entirely new culinary adventure.

After all, the best gifts give everyone something to enjoy, whether it’s a new flavor to try or a good story to share.

Mezcal deserves a proper vessel, and Francisco’s hand-built clay set brings generations of Oaxacan pottery traditions to the table. After learning the craft from his family and spending years away, the artisan returned home to work with the same earth his ancestors used. He uses his community’s traditional kilns and adds something of his own: copal and tobacco he grows by hand. Their sap and leaves create the smoky black finish, while shades of cream, orange, and aqua come from natural elements sourced at a nearby mine. That’s the magic of Oaxaca. Every potter has a particular source of mud and sand, so no two studios look alike.

The set for Obakki includes a carafe, platter, four cups, and a small bowl for gusano salt. To complete the gift, add a bottle of El Mero Mero Espadín, a 48% ABV Oaxacan mezcal with cooked-maguey smoke, herbal notes, and a very long finish that the producer pairs with moles, adobos, and spicy seafood. Then add Agave Worm Salt from SOS Chefs for rimming, dipping, or sprinkling over orange slices. Sip, nibble, repeat.

The Oaxacan Clay Mezcal Set is available at obakki.com for $193; the El Mero Mero Espadín is available at drinkfellows.com for $65; and the Agave Worm Salt is available from sos-chefs.com for $20.

Have a friend obsessed with guacamole? Someone who makes pesto the long way, with a lot of garlic and a little attitude? Carved from solid Mexican lava rock and paired with a Tzalam wood pestle, the Tecali Mortar and Pestle is exactly what they need. Weighing in at nine pounds, with the rough, textured surface of a traditional molcajete, it’s a kitchen tool built to last.

The piece comes from a Mexico City design collective that works with woodworkers from Veracruz and Mérida and stone carvers from Puebla. Designer Karim Molina begins every piece by exploring the raw material itself. Guacamole made in this bowl tastes better—or at least feels like it does, which amounts to the same thing at a party.

The Tecali Mortar and Pestle is available at obakki.com for $210.

A tiered stand screams special occasion. Ferm Living’s Orbelle Etagere pairs mirror-polished stainless steel with a tiered design, so everything you stack on it catches the candlelight. Give grandma’s cream puffs the moment they’ve always deserved, or create a tower of glassware beside your self-serve bar setup. At 14.2 inches tall and approximately 11.8 inches wide, it’s substantial enough to serve as a centerpiece but arrives flat-packed for easy storage.

The Orbelle Etagere is available at fermliving.us for $209.

Every great brand has an origin story, and Hedley & Bennett’s begins with a bluff. When her head chef announced he was ordering new aprons, Ellen Marie Bennett, then a 24-year-old line cook, told him she had an apron company. She didn’t, at least not yet. What she did have was the willingness to leap and then figure it out on the way down. More than a decade later, the brand has expanded beyond its signature kitchen uniforms with its first cookware collection.

The lineup combines five-ply stainless steel cookware with enameled cast iron, balancing responsive heating with serious heat retention. Available in Shiso Green, Enoki White, and French Blue, the pieces bring the brand’s signature use of color to the stovetop. They’re oven-safe to 550°F and designed for everything from weeknight meals to ambitious dinner parties. The collection is built for people who are figuring it out as they go, which, if you ask Ellen, is how everything great gets started.

The Full Cookware 6-Piece Set is available at hedleyandbennett.com for $530, with individual pieces starting at $150.

Stainless steel serveware is as functional as it is stunning. Composed of two complementary forms that give it both stability and sculptural presence, the ice cream cup from Service Projects is equally suited to a scoop of French vanilla or a small mound of buttery Castelvetrano olives.

Pair four cups with the brand’s Canteen Polished Table Spoons for a touch of Art Deco elegance at the table. It’s the perfect gift for the friend who loves making homemade ice cream or is taking their chocolate mousse recipe to the grave.

The Ice Cream Cup 02 ($35 each; $140 for a set of four) and the Canteen Polished Table Spoon ($75 for a set of four) are available at na.service-projects.com.

Photography courtesy of Holcomb and Fly by Jing

Holcomb Studio’s Float Condiment Container finally gives sauces a vessel worthy of leaving on the counter. Made from borosilicate glass, the design creates the illusion that the contents are suspended, so whatever’s inside becomes the decoration. Founded in 2021 by father-daughter duo David and Ariel Holcomb, the brand is all about making a well-lived home look good while it’s being lived in.

The five-ounce Float Condiment Container comes with a fitted lid and spoon for chili crisp, mustard, or jam. Remove the spoon, and it doubles as a pouring vessel for soy sauce or cream. For the relative who goes through a jar of chili crisp a week, pair it with Fly by Jing’s Triple Threat Gift Box, which includes three six-ounce jars: Original, Sweet + Spicy, and Xtra Crunchy Sichuan Chili Crisp.

The Float Condiment Container is available at holcombstudio.com for $45; the Triple Threat Gift Box is available at flybyjing.com for $50.

Is there a more theatrical dinner-party move than offering freshly ground pepper? The Piper Oudo grinder pairs a solid wood exterior with a precision stainless steel conical burr for an even grind. Choose Black Walnut or Hard Maple (or one of each, if you’re feeling generous). The swanky-looking handle is also ergonomic, designed to be held and twisted in several ways, and a MagPlate catches stray grounds so the counter stays tidy.

The Piper Gift Set includes four 20-gram refills of Debut Blend, a 50/50 mix of black and red Kampot peppercorns naturally farmed in Cambodia, plus a Pepper Duster. Expect notes of eucalyptus, lemon zest, and red berry. Go coarse on carbonara, burrata, and scrambled eggs, or try a pinch on strawberries and dark chocolate. The refills provide enough pepper for three to six months of finishing dishes.

The Piper Gift Set is available at piper.now for $155.

The 5 O’Clock Tray offers a practical solution for serving guests with different dietary preferences. A round marble base holds a lacquered aluminum inlay that marks the happiest hour of the day. Its compartments let you serve two things at once without a single crumb crossing the border.

Picture the quintessential holiday spread: warm blinis, crème fraîche, and chilled caviar on one side, and a vegan, gluten-free version featuring purple potato chips, cashew-lemon crème fraîche, and algae pearls on the other.

The plant-based caviar alternative comes from Lofoten Seaweed, a Norwegian brand with two interesting origin stories. Co-founder Angelita Eriksen grew up helping her fisherman father in the village of Napp and became convinced that seaweed was the food of the future while researching nutrition. Her business partner, Tamara Singer, grew up in New Zealand with a Japanese mother who cooked with seaweed at nearly every meal. Together, they developed a range of products using wild-harvested Norwegian seaweed.

Made with smoked wakame, the brand’s Algae Pearls retain their color and texture when heated. Beyond the holiday spread, try them on Christmas cod, New Year’s scallops, deviled eggs, or roasted vegetables.

The 5 O’Clock Tray by The Form Follows is available at stillfried.com for $175; Algae Pearls are available at lofotenseaweed.com for $14.

I’ve never understood why cleaning up can’t be chic. The Orb Sponge by Orb Clean is a sculptural sphere with its own drying base, designed to be displayed rather than hidden away. It’s non-scratch, odor-resistant, and dishwasher-safe. Choose Onyx or Stone, depending on whether your host’s kitchen leans moody or serene.

Pair it with the Golda Sphere Soap to turn their sink into a still life. From Supple Apothecary, the 4.4-ounce orb is formulated as a face, hand, or body wash. Coconut, olive, jojoba, and avocado oils leave hands feeling soft rather than stripped, while lemongrass and Aomori hiba oil give it a fresh, citrusy, cedar-forward fragrance. Together, they make a thoughtful gift for the host who pays attention to every detail, right down to the kitchen sink.

The Orb sponge is available at orbclean.com for $25; the Golda Sphere Soap is available at suppleapothecary.com for $30.

If you’ve ever made traditional chai at home, you know it’s worth the effort—but you also know about the boil-overs, the loose spices, and the pot you can’t walk away from. Loka designed its Chai Maker to address all three. A patented Chai Fountain keeps the brew from foaming over while gently circulating the liquid, and a built-in strainer catches the tea leaves and spices. The 2025 Red Dot Award winner is compatible with gas, electric, and induction cooktops and makes enough chai for three servings. It’s a great gift for anyone who enjoys the ritual but doesn’t want to watch the pot.

The Loka Chai Maker is available at weareloka.com for $149.

Photography courtesy of Building Block Etc.

Every serious cook has a tasting spoon they hover over the pot with, and every serious cook could use a better one. Made in Niigata, Japan, this slender stainless steel utensil has a tasting spoon at one end and a fork at the other. At 23 centimeters long, it keeps hands well away from rising steam, while the fork makes it easy to check whether vegetables are cooked through. (It’s also useful for stirring drinks when the cocktails come out.) It’s the rare stocking stuffer that actually earns its place in the kitchen drawer.

The Tasting Spoon / Fork is available at bbetc.shop for $15.

For the adventurous home cook, SOS Chefs packages a destination’s flavors into a curated pantry collection. The New York-based specialty food shop—where serious chefs stock up—brings together oils, spices, and dried or canned goods inspired by different culinary traditions. These starter kits are tributes to favorite cities, and you can taste that they are packed by people who obviously love to eat.

A Trip to Beirut offers a taste of Lebanese cooking, while A Trip to Tokyo explores Japanese ingredients. Either makes a thoughtful gift for the friend with worldly tastes who doesn’t have their next trip on the calendar.

The Destination sets are available at sos-chefs.com, starting at $165.

Cheeky, bold, and impossible to ignore, Gustaf Westman’s Chunky Beer Tray is designed to sit snugly around a bottle while holding snacks.

Its exaggerated proportions, rounded edges, and Transparent Green finish are characteristic of the playful objects the architect-turned-designer has been creating since founding his Stockholm-based studio in 2020.

But the tray has other tricks, too: Rest it on top of another bowl to create a serving platter with a hidden compartment for pistachio shells and olive pits, or fill it with herbs and flowers for a festive centerpiece.

Imagine arriving at Friendsgiving with homemade roasted nuts, a chilled pét-nat, and the Chunky Beer Tray as a gift.

The Chunky Beer Tray in Transparent Green is available at gustafwestman.com for $75.

Photography courtesy of The Tomte Cake and Hedley & Bennett

For families who love baking together during the holidays, consider The Tomte Cake. It brings all the fun of decorating a gingerbread house—without the frustration of keeping the thing assembled. The Large Family Bundle includes two gingerbread house baking pans, two Nordic tomte gnomes, two cake mixes, and a storybook that connects the activity to Scandinavian folklore.

Pair it with matching Rifle Paper Co. Holly Jolly Aprons for kids and adults, and you’ll have everything you need to get the whole family in uniform, ready to bake, decorate, and start a sweet new tradition together.

The Large Family Bundle is available at thetomtecake.com for $135; Rifle Paper Co. Holly Jolly Aprons are available at hedleyandbennett.com, starting at $66 for kids and $129 for adults.
