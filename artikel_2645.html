Packers Snap Counts, Week 10: Green Bay’s offense played heavy

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

For the second week in a row, the Green Bay Packers' reserves got on the field. Last week, it was because they were beating down the New England Patriots so much. This week, it was because the Minnesota Vikings sacked Jordan Love eight times while building a three-score lead.

It wasn't a pretty game, but that's not going to stop us from breaking down the Packers' depth chart coming out of the loss to the Vikings. Below are all of the offensive and defensive snaps their players took today.

Tyrod Taylor got one drive of garbage time, a three-and-out, at the end of the game, but he's going to see more playing time soon if the team can't protect Love.

The Packers chose to make Pierre Strong Jr. a healthy scratch in this game in favor of the three backs they kept up. Together, those players ran 16 times for 56 yards on the day. Chris Brooks, who nearly played as many snaps as both Kaleb Johnson and MarShawn Lloyd combined, only carried the ball twice for one yard.

At some point, you have to wonder if Brooks' blocking back role is a clear tell to opposing defenses that they're free to fire off the ball and get penetration, because the team is obviously going to pass the ball.

This might come as a surprise to many, but the Packers, who never had a lead in this game and were only tied for less than five minutes, played most of this game out of two-receiver sets. On a per-play basis, Green Bay averaged 2.2 receivers per play.

For whatever reason, the Packers must have thought that the key to beating this Vikings defense was to add more bodies into the formation to help block blitzes, rather than spreading out pass-catchers to put more stress on blitz paths.

Receivers Bo Melton and Skyy Moore both got hurt before they could even play offense. Melton went down with a thigh injury. Moore went down with an ankle injury after just being activated from the injured reserve this week. Matt LaFleur wasn't asked about either injury in his post-game press conference on Sunday.

The Packers averaged 1.8 tight end snaps per play once you include their extra offensive line looks. For perspective, tight end Tucker Kraft played every single snap on offense that Jordan Love did in this game. Every snap that Jonnu Smith and Mark Redman played was as second or third tight ends (Drake Dabney played garbage time).

Bringing an extra tight end in the formation only allowed Minnesota to bring another defender into the box, though. Eventually, you have to win one-on-ones. The Packers just weren't able to do that, as their heavy formations weren't able to punish the blitz-heavy Vikings on the ground.

Green Bay didn't sit any of their offensive linemen when Taylor went in at quarterback in this game. Mekhi Becton's snaps came as an extra tight end. The full-time offensive line was Jordan Morgan, Laken Tomlinson, John Williams, Travis Glover and Anthony Belton (left to right). Neither Jager Burton nor Aaron Banks got any snaps.

In the post-game presser, LaFleur said that the team wanted to move Tomlinson back to his "natural position" at left guard. That's why Banks (who usually played left guard) was displaced in favor of Glover in this game. LaFleur stated that Glover had more "time on task" at right guard.

It looks like we're going into the bye without any answer at right guard, as we're getting ready to ramp up another competition between Glover, Banks and Kevin Zeitler, who was inactive today after being listed as questionable with two injuries on Friday.

With both starters already out, Isaiah McDuffie and Ty'Ron Hopper had to play the Mike and Will positions this week. McDuffie missed one snap with an injury, forcing Nick Niemann, a core special teamer, onto the field. That one play was a touchdown, where Niemann was given a lot of eye candy and let a tight end cross his face.

Brandon Cisse left the game with a rib injury but was never officially ruled out by the team, only listed as questionable. Carrington Valentine took Cisse's snaps at right cornerback for the remainder of the game.

Usually, Valentine is the team's second slot defender in their dime personnel looks, which they rarely use. Because he was forced to play on the outside, Kamal Hadden played two snaps in the slot today. One of those was a third-and-15 conversion, where he simply got beaten in man-to-man coverage.

With enough injuries, special teamers being forced into defensive roles become a problem. Hadden had played 101 special teams snaps to just 13 defensive snaps (12 of which came in garbage time blowouts against the Chicago Bears and New England Patriots) in 2026.

LaFleur was not asked about Cisse's injury in his press conference.

Slot defender Javon Bullard was only on the field for about half of the game today, as the Vikings tried to play a heavy offense that kept Bullard sidelined in favor of an extra interior defensive lineman. The slot defender rate varies a lot game-to-game because of offensive personnel and game situation, but usually the Packers play Bullard around two-thirds of the time on defense.
