U.S. pressed China on farm purchase pledges, lifting soybean futures

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Soybean futures gave up earlier gains on Monday as traders looked ahead to a USDA crop report due this week

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer pressed China to carry out its past commitments, including agricultural purchases, during a videoconference with Vice Premier He Lifeng. Soybean futures jumped by the most in more than two weeks following the U.S. appeal, according to Bloomberg, before giving up those gains.

Bessent raised concerns about recent Chinese actions and said Beijing must follow through on the pledges it made at the Busan meeting, the Treasury Department said. He said further advances hinge on tangible outcomes and that Washington will evaluate how China carries out the deal.

Greer said China must deliver tangible steps on buying farm goods, on rare earths and on other critical minerals, according to the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative. The office said the administration will monitor compliance with past commitments while pursuing balanced trade with China.

Traders balanced fresh optimism about Chinese buying against forecasts that the U.S. Department of Agriculture will show soybean output edging nearer to an all-time high, according to Bloomberg. Futures have fallen for four straight weeks.

China voiced "serious concerns" over the latest U.S. restrictions on trade and the economy during the call, according to Bloomberg. The two sides engaged in "frank, in-depth, and constructive exchanges" and agreed to deepen cooperation while keeping disagreements in check, Bloomberg said, citing a Chinese state news agency account of the call.

Chinese state-owned companies had reserved at least 20 shiploads of U.S. soybeans in mid-October, roughly 1.3 million tons, their first purchases since the late-September summit between President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping. Beijing has committed to import at least 25 million tons of U.S. soybeans a year through 2028, along with $17 billion a year in other U.S. farm goods.

In September, Bessent said some Chinese deliverables had fallen short and that China was running late on some of its agricultural commitments, according to Bloomberg. The two governments agreed to push their trade truce out to Jan. 10.
