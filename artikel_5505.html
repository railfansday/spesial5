Does University of Phoenix understand employer needs? Harris Poll finds 94% of managers supervising its graduates say yes

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

92% say University programs align with workforce demands, and 92% agree an alliance with the University could add value to their organization

PHOENIX, Nov. 20, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- New research conducted by The Harris Poll on behalf of University of Phoenix finds that managers who directly supervise University of Phoenix graduates report strong confidence in the University's understanding of employer needs and alignment with workforce demands. Among managers supervising at least one University of Phoenix graduate, 94% say the University understands employer needs, while 92% agree its programs align with workforce demands.

The study also found that 94% of these managers say their experience supervising University graduates has positively influenced their perception of the University, and 92% agree that an alliance with University of Phoenix could add value to their organization.

Do managers believe University of Phoenix understands employer needs?

As employers increasingly emphasize demonstrable skills, workforce alignment remains an important consideration for higher education institutions. According to the National Association of Colleges and Employers (NACE) Job Outlook 2026 survey, 70% of participating employers report using skills-based hiring practices, up from 65% the previous year.

The Harris Poll findings offer another perspective on workforce alignment, reflecting how managers assess the institutions associated with graduates they directly supervise. Among managers supervising University of Phoenix graduates, 94% say the University understands employer needs, compared with 82% of managers supervising graduates from benchmark colleges and universities who say the same about those institutions. Similarly, 92% agree University of Phoenix aligns its programs with workforce demands, compared with 78% of managers evaluating benchmark institutions.

The survey also examined how managers' experiences with graduates inform their perceptions of the institutions those graduates attended. The findings across three measures of institutional confidence are summarized below.

How does supervising graduates inform managers' perceptions of University of Phoenix?

Beyond assessments of workplace readiness and job performance, the survey offers insight into how managers view the institutions that educated their employees. For University of Phoenix, these reported experiences provide an additional perspective on institutional credibility and the importance of maintaining strong connections between education and employer needs.

"Understanding employer needs is an ongoing responsibility of higher education and workforce development institutions," said Raghu Krishnaiah, chief operating officer of University of Phoenix. "Among managers who directly supervise our graduates, 98% report a positive perception of those graduates and 94% rate them excellent or above-average performers. Their feedback reinforces the importance of keeping career-relevant education connected to the workforce skills organizations need, while continuing to listen and adapt as those needs evolve."

Could an alliance with University of Phoenix add value to employers?

The survey also examined how managers view the potential value of working with University of Phoenix. Among managers supervising University graduates, 92% agree that an alliance with the University could add value to their organization.

The finding reflects managers' perceptions of potential organizational value, rather than existing alliance participation or measured alliance outcomes.

Through University of Phoenix Workforce Solutions, the University works with employers to support workforce development needs, including tuition benefits, professional development and approaches to identifying and addressing workforce skills gaps.

How does University of Phoenix approach workforce alignment?

University of Phoenix focuses on career-relevant education designed around the realities of working adults. Associate, bachelor's and master's degree programs incorporate career-relevant skills through skills-mapped curricula, helping learners identify and develop skills throughout their coursework. University faculty also bring professional experience and practical perspectives to the classroom.

These institutional practices provide context for the University's approach to connecting education with workplace needs, while the Harris Poll research separately measures managers' perceptions of University of Phoenix based on their experience supervising graduates.

What employers say about University of Phoenix graduates

The institutional confidence findings conclude a series of announcements examining University of Phoenix graduates through the experiences of managers who directly supervise them.

Earlier releases explored employer satisfaction and future hiring intent, workplace readiness and applied skills, and managers' assessments of graduates' workplace contributions and potential for greater responsibility.

Together, the findings provide a broader view of how surveyed managers assess University graduates and the institutions associated with them.

Additional findings and the complete survey methodology are available at https://www.phoenix.edu/about/employer-survey.html.
