Deadlock’s second season of ranked play just opened, and with it a rework to the way players unlock characters. Now, players don’t need to win matches, just complete enough on a hero to amass points for ranked selection.

With this change in place, multiple bot farms have opened up, slowly gathering points and ruining games to sell those same accounts to potential buyers once they’re ranked and ready.

Deadlock’s initial ranked system was a bit restrictive, requiring players to win a sizable 15 games on any hero they planned to play with, alongside 60 overall wins. Three separate heroes needed to surpass this 15-game milestone, making losses feel awful and progress feel slow.

On the bright side, the steep requirements made the process very difficult for any account resellers, requiring them to actually play out their games to win if they wanted accounts to flourish. With the change to a points-based system following ranked beta season 2, accounts have begun to set up dummy bots in matches, doing just enough to avoid getting AFK kicked without providing any benefit to the team.

Valve itself has dealt with this issue before, with a couple of different methods throughout its myriad titles. For Team Fortress 2, they let the game sit for a long while, suffering as bots, both subtle and egregious, dominated the game. Bots are still abundant in the class shooter, though stricter bot detection software has helped curb their numbers.

Over the years, titles have had to find unique ways to keep competitive modes accessible without letting in any ner-do-wells, with varying success.

CSGO and CSGO 2 both implemented a system incentivizing players to make a one-time purchase for “premium matchmaking”, alongside the occasional reward of a crate. Through this, actual players and those attempting to bot accounts are separated, as accounts aren't typically very expensive, and the cost of matchmaking can quickly add up for multiple profiles.

With more faces joining Deadlock all the time, ensuring access to the ranked playlist without letting in too many bots will be paramount, but I honestly think the old win system worked perfectly. It took a while to unlock heroes, but it ensured you knew how to pilot and win on your pick when you eventually made it to ranked. With this new bot epidemic surging, Valve has a short window to close the gap.

This has been a serious case of the absolute bummer cheat makers and bot runners continuing to be in online games, leaping at any chance for player convenience to spoil it for everyone else. We’ve seen this plague online games for decades, even as developers continue to find new ways to fight back.

Whether its spinbotters on Hightower or macros for hard inputs in MOBAs, the best policy seems to be making the process of getting to ranked just annoying enough to dissuade cheaters, either through small payments or sheer time. Either way, those who are really here to appreciate the game are more than willing to put in the time, as long as they feel Valve is attempting to solve the issue.

Valve is riding a massive high off of the City Never Sleeps update for Deadlock, and the only way they could realistically lose that momentum is if they fumble their answer to these bots. Making that mistake cursed TF2 for months of nigh unplayability, and if Valve aims to turn Deadlock into their next big thing, they can’t afford to fumble.
