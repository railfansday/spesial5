Personal Consumer Issues • Re: How much is too much? (Car question)

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

It currently has an issue with the front radar that won’t allow me to use cruise control. It’s not an issue that is covered under warranty or my auto-insurance. . . . how much is too much to pay to fix a car with that trade in value. At what point should I just get a newer car? I would probably trade in my Mazda for a gently used Lincoln Aviator about 55-60K?
It sounds like you're using this minor glitch with a trivial accessory to justify a major new car purchase. The Aviator is a want, not a need, and can in no way be cost/benefit justified on the cruise control defect. On the other hand, you've got $2.2M saved, earn $300K a year, so if you really want the Aviator, you can afford it; just recognize it for the splurge purchase it is. I drive a nearly 20-year old BMW 3 series that I love. Its KBB is around $5K. I've covered several repair bills in the $3-4K range, and if nothing major, annual maintenance on an aging Germany performance machine runs about $1,000. While repairs approaching 100% of the value of the car might seem like a lot, its easily worth it to me since I still enjoy the car and the cost of a comparable new replacement would be about $100K.

The CX 9 is a great car. You've easily got 10+ more years of service in the vehicle if you want it. If you can avoid the allure of the Aviator and the cruise control bothers you that much, find a good indy shop and be resourceful in sourcing parts. The new Mazda radar unit runs something around $2,200 and would probably run around $3,500 all-in at the dealership. By comparison, you can buy a good serviceable unit from a salvage yard for $500 and be out the door at an indy shop for roughly $1,000. Even at the dealer price, you'd be further ahead economically paying for the repair than buying a $60K replacement vehicle. That said, if an Aviator is what your heart desires, you're still going to have a CX9.
