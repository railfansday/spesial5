Crypto bridge hacks can spread losses far beyond the assets they compromise

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Recent exploits expose how broken redemption mechanisms, fragmented liquidity and compromised collateral can spread losses across DeFi lending markets.

Multichain’s version of USDC on Fantom was at nearly $0.12 on Nov. 25, more than three years after the bridge that backed it failed, while the Circle-issued USDC and the version bridged to Arbitrum were at $0.9996 each.

Recent bridge exploits at Fusionist and Cyber show how quickly a token can lose the backing or conversion route that held its price close to the original. Buyers can still find a price on a screen even as their options for converting or selling the token shrink.

A bridge lets a token circulate on a blockchain where it was never issued. A US bankruptcy court account describes how Multichain let users lock Ethereum USDC on one side and receive a representation they could later convert back.

In July 2023, Chainalysis traced unauthorized withdrawals that included USDC from Multichain’s Fantom bridge. Multichain announced that bridge service was stopping indefinitely and later said it had ceased operations. On Fantom, the damage reached lender Geist Finance, which stopped operating after taking losses.

On Nov. 25, the USDC version on Fantom was 88.18% below $1, with about $2,472 in reported daily trading volume. Circle-issued USDC and Arbitrum’s USDC.e were both 0.04% below $1, with daily volume of about $15 billion across the native listing and $2.1 million for the Arbitrum wrapper.

CoinGecko quotes (in US dollars) per token at about 18:38 UTC on Nov. 25. Circle-issued USDC and Arbitrum USDC.e were each $0.9996, while Multichain USDC on Fantom was $0.1182.

DEX Screener quoted the same Fantom token at $0.04 in SpookySwap’s USDC/WFTM pool, which held about $94,000 in assets, roughly half on the WFTM side.

Eligible Circle Mint customers can redeem native USDC for dollars under Circle’s terms, while Arbitrum’s canonical bridge locks Ethereum USDC and issues USDC.e on Arbitrum.

A holder who converts a bridged token must get the underlying asset back before asking the issuer to convert it to dollars. For specified third-party versions, Circle’s bridged USDC terms make conversion dependent on the available bridge and network.

Georges Chouchani, founder and CEO of Euclid Protocol, told CryptoSlate:

“As long as people believe they can redeem one-for-one, someone will buy the copy at 99 cents and redeem it at a dollar.”

The buyer pockets the difference after fees and other conversion costs, and those purchases can pull the cheaper token toward par. They also depend on the bridge honoring the conversion and the trader being able to complete it.

New York Fed researchers looked at hourly prices for native and bridged USDC and USDT across a selection of networks in the first three quarters of 2024, finding an average correlation of 0.381.

The researchers interpreted the imperfect alignment as evidence that moving value between chains can introduce enough friction to obstruct the law of one price, the idea that equivalent claims should trade at the same price.

Circle reported $3.3 billion of reserves at Silicon Valley Bank in March 2023, and Federal Reserve Board staff found that USDC traded below $0.90 before recovering over about three days.

On Nov. 22, Fusionist’s wrapped ACE fell from about $0.12 to less than one millionth of a dollar in about an hour on BNB Chain, according to Bitquery’s analysis of decentralized exchange trades.

Traders bought the discounted tokens and attempted to convert them back to ACE on Fusionist’s Endurance chain. Bitquery counted approvals for roughly 53.7 million ACE to return and estimated that backing fell to 148 ACE based on the bridge’s BNB Chain records.

Buying a cheap token to convert it would normally pull the prices closer together. In this case, the bridge kept approving conversions after unbacked tokens had entered circulation.

Every completed conversion would take more assets from the vault, leaving less backing for the next holder and making the price gap harder to close.

Gate announced a rollback for ACE/USDT spot trades on its exchange between 17:55 and 19:15 UTC on Nov. 22. Bithumb announced a temporary trading suspension after it had already paused ACE transfers.

On Nov. 24, Fusionist said it was coordinating recovery with exchanges and would share a timeline when it had one. An ACE holder could see a price in a decentralized pool and still face a suspension or potential trade reversal on a centralized venue.

The day before the Fusionist breach, Bitquery traced the issuance of 5,213,000 unbacked CYBER on Solana and the release of the same amount, worth about $1.1 million, from the bridge’s Ethereum vault.

In its Nov. 24 update, Cyber said it had burned an equivalent amount from team and treasury allocations and that the bridge was still temporarily suspended.

The options had already split at Bithumb, where customers could trade existing CYBER balances even after the exchange paused deposits and withdrawals.

“If you have $1 million of liquidity on each of 10 chains, $10 million exists, but a trader on any one chain can only reach $1 million of it.”

What is key for an order is the pool or venue the trader can reach. CoinGecko’s sample showed $33.8 million in reported sell-side depth within 2% for native USDC against USDT on Binance. The same measure for USDC.e against USDT0 on Curve Arbitrum was about $12,231.

Hyperbridge reported an unauthorized mint of about 1 billion bridged DOT. Its initial $237,000 loss estimate covered the immediately visible Ethereum selloff, but after reviewing four affected chains and related incentive pools, Hyperbridge revised the total to about $2.5 million in realized losses.

Chouchani pointed to lending collateral as a route for losses to reach people who never held the compromised token. In April’s Kelp bridge breach, 116,500 rsETH tokens valued at about $292 million were released.

The Aave incident report documented using some of those tokens as collateral to borrow other assets before the protocol froze affected reserves.

If the collateral can no longer be sold for enough to cover the loan, the lending market faces a shortfall. Aave’s report modeled different possible losses depending on how the damage would be allocated.

In this case, the lending market was exposed as soon as it let users borrow other assets against the compromised token, and people who supplied those other assets could bear some of the cost.

Network and contract address help identify who issued the token and which bridge stands between the holder and the underlying asset. Circle’s bridged USDC standard provides a route for eligible deployments to upgrade to native issuance by mutual agreement.

Chouchani said that a design with fewer wrapped tokens can concentrate risk in whatever coordinates settlement across chains. He pointed to smart contracts, validator assumptions, and what happens when one chain takes longer to finalize a transfer.

“No cross-chain design is free of risk, ours included.”

For a specific token, check where the backing is held, whether conversion is operating, and who can use it.

Then check the price available for the amount you want to sell, and what happened the last time trading or withdrawals came under stress.

A token can share the original’s ticker and trade at the same price on a normal day. The contract, conversion terms, and available market tell the investors what that price buys.
