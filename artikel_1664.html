Luke Graham seizes his chance to shine in Scotland back-line... now Stoke defender aims to make himself a fixture for years to come

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 17:30 EST, 14 November 2026 | Updated: 18:01 EST, 14 November 2026

Whatever concerns there may legitimately be about the limited progress seen in Sebastien Pocognoli’s brief time in charge of Scotland, the Belgian has hardly been blessed with good fortune.

In five matches to date in the Nations League, the manager has not yet been able to select the same back-three in two consecutive outings. Injuries and suspensions have seen to that.

For all that, you sense that even if he’d had a full quota of players to choose from, Pocognoli would still be figuring out his preferred defensive grouping.

This does feel like a time of opportunity for those who are fresh on the scene. Catch the eye and there’s a real chance to become established as a mainstay of the team moving forward.

Luke Graham did his chances of doing just that no harm at all in his first start and second cap for his country on Friday.

Playing in between Ryan Porteous and Kieran Tierney, the Stoke City man made a compelling case to stay there with a strong and diligent display.

Luke Graham enjoyed a composed debut for Scotland as they recorded a much-needed win

On the back of a summer move from Dundee to the English Championship, Graham appears to have taken the step-up to international level in his stride.

‘It’s something I’ve always wanted to do since I was growing up, so I have to be ready for the moment and take it when it comes,’ he said.

‘I think there’s good competition. We have a lot of good centre-halves. Obviously, it was unfortunate for Welshy (Stephen Welsh) and Binksy (Luis Binks) to go out with injury, but that opens up a space for me to come in and try to cement a place.

‘When you’re out on that pitch, you have to do everything you can to try and stay in the team. I gave my all and I’ll just need to wait and see what happens going forward.’

Timing can be vital in football. And it’s conceivable that Graham’s might just propel him to become a Scotland regular.

While playing at Dens Park saw him mentioned in dispatches with regards to a call-up, his move to England, coming just before Pocognoli was announced as Steve Clarke’s successor, has seen his dream quickly become reality.

‘I’ve always said that one thing I wanted to do in my career was play for Scotland, so to get it over the line was pretty special,’ he reflected.

Former Dundee defender Graham proudly belts out the national anthem ahead of kick-off

‘Not much is going wrong for me at the moment, so it’s just about maintaining that and keeping it going every week.

‘I wouldn’t say I merited a start as we have a lot of good players in the camp. It’s up to the manager to pick the team and, luckily, he gave me the chance.

‘He just said to go out and enjoy it and do what you’re doing every week at Stoke. I think I did all right.’

Notwithstanding how limited the North Macedonians were on the night, Pocognoli was entitled to take encouragement from the way the back three acquitted themselves.

Playing closer to each other than we’d seen in the previous matches, they were rarely unpicked by balls through the middle. Angus Gunn was not overworked in goal. Tellingly, the only concession of the night came when Scott McTominay lost his man at a corner.

‘I think a lot of it is just being brave,’ Graham reflected. ‘It’s about bravery in going man-to-man with the striker, being brave on the ball, trying to break the lines and keeping possession.

‘So, I think bravery is the main thing and concentration as well.’

Graham keeps a close eye on North Macedonian danger man Elmin Rastoder at Hampden

With the defence solid enough, Pocognoli had a platform for a much-needed win. While the narrow scoreline made the final moments more anxious than they should have been, on balance, Scotland were deserved winners.

‘It’s just about winning games at this stage of the competition, so that was good,’ said Graham. ‘I think, if I’m being harsh, we should have killed the game off before it got to 1-1. We had a few good chances to put them to bed.

‘Then we kinda shot ourselves in the foot by conceding from a set piece. But Ben Gannon-Doak does well for the penalty and for us to go 2-1 up and see it out was good.’

No one would dispute that the display was a long way from perfect. There’s significant room for improvement ahead of the concluding group match with Switzerland on Monday.

‘We’re going through a bit of a transitional spell at the minute, so it’s not going to be all smooth sailing from the get-go,’ Graham said. ‘It’s about adjusting and trying to build on performances in every game. I think it’s going to take time.

‘It’s a different style, a different manager and we just have to keep being patient. You look at the squad now and there’s a lot of new, young players coming through. It’s just about encouraging us young boys and showing we can get a place in the squad.

‘It is going to take time but, once we get there, we’re going to do really well and compete right at the top of the game.’

Graham has been a huge success in Stoke's flying start to the Championship season

Graham does look like he’s moving in the right direction. From watching Scotland compete at the World Cup on licensed premises five months ago, he hopes he too can be the toast of the nation one day.

‘I didn’t go. It was too expensive for me,’ he explained. ‘I think I was just like everyone else, watching in the pub with their mates. It was pretty amazing and hopefully I can be part of something like that one day.

‘I wasn’t too far away (previously), I think. Steve Clarke, the former manager, told me that. It’s just about trusting myself now and hopefully getting there one day.

‘There’s been a lot of hard work and dedication which has led me to this point. I’m very grateful to a lot of people who have helped me in my career so far and just to get this chance is amazing.’
