Review: Stellar Blade: Complete Edition (Switch 2) - Perfectly Playable, Despite Drawbacks

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Though the genre has evolved and deepened over time, gacha games have historically not been praised for their depth and complexity, often being criticised for being thinly veiled gambling machines. People were understandably incredulous, then, when Shift Up — the studio behind Goddess of Victory: Nikke — announced in 2019 that it would pursue a AAA passion project called Stellar Blade. Even more were then shocked a few years later when this wildly different, ambitious project turned out to be a pretty solidly made action game.

Stellar Blade has now been ported to Switch 2 (along with some cheeky new cosmetic Bayonetta DLC), bringing this titillating and gripping action experience to the Nintendo audience. It hasn’t made the transition to more modest hardware completely unscathed, but this is nonetheless a solidly enjoyable and thrilling adventure that deserves a spot in your library.

The narrative is set in the distant, post-apocalypse future, when our planet has been overrun by monstrous creatures called Naytibas. Some humans escaped after losing the war and established a space-based orbital colony called…the Colony…from which occasional squadrons of elite super-soldiers are deployed to attempt to regain a foothold on Earth.

Protagonist Eve is a member of one of these squads and the prologue opens with her landing mission, a violent meatgrinder reminiscent of the Omaha Beach scene in Saving Private Ryan in which she’s seemingly the sole warrior who survives. Aided by a mysterious man who saved her, Eve sets out to track down the Alpha Naytiba and hopefully notch a win for the humans.

It’s a decently entertaining story, but one which feels a little surface-level in many ways. Characters — Eve included — lack depth and converse in a stilted way that sounds as if they’re quoting lines at each other, and some of the later plot twists are the sort of thing that you can see coming a mile away. Yet there’s just enough lore here to keep you interested in the setting and Eve’s ongoing journey and occasional moments of levity help to keep things from feeling too dour.

Any disappointment with the storytelling is quickly forgotten by the thrill of combat, which feels like a cross between a Soulslike and a character action game as Eve deftly combos enemies with her athletic acrobatics and expert sword strikes.

Enemies hit hard and fast and can easily stunlock you, meaning that even a single average foe can take quite a large chunk out of your health bar if you're not paying careful attention and multiple foes at once can rapidly overwhelm Eve due to limited mobility and crowd control options.

Along with the usual combos you can string together with light and heavy attacks, Eve also has a variety of limited-use Beta Skills she can use to turn the tide in her favour. You build up Beta Energy from attacks and perfectly timed blocks or parries, and this energy can then be burned mid-combo to either bolster an attack or use a flashy guard-breaking attack that can put the enemy on the back foot. This system adds a nice bit of room for player skill expression, as you can find some creative ways to weave together combos and Beta Skills to positively melt foes.

Though even common enemies can put you to the test in certain configurations, this combat system really sings when you get to one of the many cinematic boss fights against powerful, grotesque monsters. These act as occasional hard skill checks that will forcibly teach you deeper knowledge of combat mechanics as you carefully study their movesets for brief gaps and learn (often across multiple failed attempts) how to best exploit those gaps with your available tools.

Eve thus feels both all-powerful and vulnerable at the same time, which lends combat encounters a thrilling and electric feel as you oscillate between slicing enemies to ribbons with practised cinematic flair and getting absolutely folded when you mis-time a dodge. Practice makes perfect, and there's lots of satisfaction that comes from progressing across the hours and going up against increasingly more complicated and dangerous foes with sharpened reflexes and skill.

Yet progress happens in more ways than just raw skill development - there are also some light RPG elements that allow you to slowly shape Eve's build over time. Not only can you use skill points to unlock and power up new combat abilities and Beta Skills, but you can also improve stats like max health and attack through finding collectibles in the environment.

Gear can also be found and crafted to give Eve certain passive buffs, allowing you to get more value out of the playstyle you generally choose. These RPG mechanics help to soften some of the harder edges of the often unforgiving combat system, giving you meaningful ways to progress over time even if you struggle greatly with losing battles to enemies.

World and level design initially follows traditional Soulslike tropes, consisting of lots of claustrophobic halls and labyrinthine paths that often loop back on each other with unlocked doors and ladders that lead back to the nearest checkpoint. While the critical path is usually obvious and easy to follow, there are often a handful of side areas to explore along the way rife with more platforming gauntlets, challenging enemy placements and extra upgrades and goodies. This design does a great job of rewarding exploration while keeping you moving forward.

Then, roughly six or so hours in, things change up with the sudden introduction of a medium-sized open-world area, filled with quests, collectibles, and points of interest to scour. Travelling between this area and a nearby hub has both gameplay and narrative benefits, as you complete quests and bounties to progress various side storylines for NPCs living there and find increasingly better gear and unlockables as quest and exploration rewards.

It admittedly seems at first like a weird left turn after the opening hours set very different expectations, but ultimately feels like a worthy addition to the gameplay loop. And even after its introduction, there are plenty of returns to the more focused and level-based Soulslike design, which balances out the free but occasionally meandering levels and quests that can come with the open world.

Graphics look decent, but not exceptional, due in part to both the overall art direction and the Switch 2’s relatively modest hardware. Some of the vistas of the ruined Earth can be quite nice to look at from afar, but there’s a lot of muted colours utilised as you trawl through tunnels, sewers, deserts, and destroyed office buildings. Environments thus tend to blend together and often lack visually interesting landmarks or set pieces, which can make the sections more focused on exploration a little more dull.

Meanwhile, the game outputs at 720p handheld and 1080p docked, which can lead to visuals that look even more muddy due to the upscaling artifacting and relatively low internal resolutions.

Regarding performance, everything technically runs at 60fps, but there's a pretty massive caveat with that claim: this frame rate is only accomplished through frame generation. Practically speaking, this means that there's a significant amount of input lag despite the visuals themselves appearing to animate smoothly. For a game this focused on quick reaction times and granular movement, that input lag can be a death knell and does noticeably reduce the playability of this port.

However, there is an option in the settings to turn this off and reduce the game to what appears to be closer to 40fps, which feels like a much more acceptable compromise. Controls are quite a bit more responsive here, while the frame rate is noticeably smoother than 30fps. There are some drops that can still happen when panning the camera around in larger areas, but it doesn’t overly affect the playability of combat encounters and this becomes less of an issue in the many smaller environments.

All this is to say, Stellar Blade is a game that appears to be stretching the Switch 2 to its limits, but it’s a perfectly playable and often surprisingly good-looking port that accurately brings this impressive game to a lightweight handheld.

It can sometimes feel easier to pick apart a port for its flaws and what it lacks, and while there are valid critiques here to be made about the sometimes disappointing visuals and performance, it’s hard to get away from the fact that this is a ‘good enough’ version of Stellar Blade that you can play wherever you want to take your console.

Despite some drawbacks, Stellar Blade stands tall as an impressive and enjoyable action game that occupies a nice niche in the Switch 2 library. Rewarding exploration, thrilling combat, and tough but fair difficulty carry this game far and make it an easy recommendation, even if its story can be a bit bland and the visuals aren’t always up to the task.

I’d recommend this to anyone looking for a higher-budget game that favours exploration and combat; Stellar Blade excels in its implementation of both and makes a great first impression as Shift Up’s entrance into AAA game development.

@AussieMcBucket I see where you're coming from and this is something I often keep in mind as I write about bigger third party projects like this. In my experience, there's often a difference between AAA games that were obviously designed for much beefier target hardware and games that either are lightweight or were explicitly designed for Nintendo hardware.

For games in the former category, they CAN be made to run on something like the Switch 2, but various visual cuts have to be made that compromise the original art direction in subtle, but often meaningful ways. For something like Resident Evil 3, these compromises are so small that they're largely irrelevant and lead to a port that's indistinguishable unless you want to really split hairs. But for Stellar Blade, there are many areas where the blurry textures or inconsistent frame rates often remind me in an unfavorable way that this hardware can't completely deliver on the game's original vision.
