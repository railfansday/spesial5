Clover Health boosts outlook amid continued membership growth, financial momentum in Q3" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Clover Health is continuing to see strong traction in its Medicare Advantage plan, with membership growing 49% year-over-year in the third quarter to approximately 157,000 members.

Alongside continued membership growth, executives touted the company’s star ratings from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services for payment year 2028.

“We are incredibly proud that our flagship PPO plan achieved five stars for payment year 2028, making it the largest five-star PPO contract in the country,” Andrew Toy, Clover Health CEO, said during Wednesday’s earnings call. “Additionally, our HMO plan achieved four and a half stars, meaning 100% of Clover members are enrolled in plans rated at least four and a half stars for payment year 2028.”

Toy said the ratings give Clover “continued flexibility to invest” in benefits and affordable care access for members, adding that the company’s HEDIS quality performance remains “industry leading.”

“And that is why we're proud that for the third year in a row, we rank number one nationally among comparable PPO plans on core HEDIS measures,” Toy said. “At the same time, we have not built Clover around optimizing to stars ratings. We are built to manage true clinical quality and total cost of care. That distinction matters.”

Toy also announced that Clover’s board appointed interim CFO Clay Thornton as its permanent CFO.

The company posted $130 million in profit, up 77% year-over-year, and total revenue of $759 million, up 53% year-over-year, according to financial results published Wednesday afternoon.

Given its performance thus far, Thorton told investors Clover now projects full-year revenue of between $2.97 billion and $3 billion and adjusted EBITDA of $75 million to $85 million. It also expects membership in Medicare Advantage to average between 157,000 and 158,000, per Thornton.

“With the performance we have delivered to date and our updated guidance, we expect 2026 to mark Clover's first full year of GAAP net income profitability,” Thornton said.

Toy said the company’s next chapter is “proving that our model can scale further” as it expands deeper into core markets and new ones, adding that “AI is a significant part” of how it scales.

“Critically, we believe that AI has reset the playing field in health plan operations, even relatively modern systems and workflows built for a pre-AI world can now be rebuilt faster with AI, accelerated with better data, and utilize far more intelligence,” he said. “We are taking advantage of this by applying AI across Clover to make the plan far more efficient to operate as we scale. AI also creates an opportunity to deliver a much better service experience for seniors.”

Among its capabilities, Clover Assistant helps providers identify diseases earlier, surfaces treatment gaps and “turns years of fragmented medical history into actionable information at the point of care,” Toy said.

“That's the opportunity we see across Clover,” he said. “Not simply using AI to do the same work at lower cost, but using it to deliver better care, better service and better operations at scale.”
