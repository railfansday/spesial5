Bayern Munich slammed as 'despicable' and accused of 'co-ordinated' plot to undermine Jamal Musiala after club chief questioned star's contract due to his health issues - with Jude Bellingham, Michael Olise and Rio Ferdinand backing the youngster

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Published: 10:38 EST, 23 November 2026 | Updated: 11:29 EST, 23 November 2026

Bayern Munich have come under criticism from FIFA's players' union and leading figures in the game amid accusations that the club is attempting to slash Jamal Musiala's wages following his diagnosis with a 'neurological dysfunction'.

The Germany international was diagnosed over the summer with absence seizures, a form of epilepsy which causes 'brief but easily treatable seizures'.

Musiala shocked the sporting world when he collapsed in two matches earlier this season, with Bayern manager Vincent Kompany explaining that the player had suffered from a reaction to a change in his medication.

Comments from members of Bayern's hierarchy have sparked controversy in Germany and abroad, with senior figures appearing to question the length of the player's contract – which runs until 2030 – should Musiala not recover his form.

Honorary president Uli Hoeness was the first to speak out on the potential drop-off in form Musiala might suffer with long-term absences, claiming earlier this month that he 'may never regain the performance level he once had'.

'We are facing a dilemma in this case, as he is suffering from an illness that cannot simply be cured, but it can be controlled to a certain extent with the use of medication,' Hoeness told German outlet BR24.

Fifpro and international footballers have come out in support of Musiala over concerns that Bayern Munich might reduce his salary

Both Uli Hoeness and Karl-Heinz Rummenigge have made comments suggesting consequences should Musiala not return to his level

Jan-Christian Dreesen also suggested that the club would need to 'assess' the situation if his performances do not return to their high heights

Chief executive Jan-Christian Dreesen then backed up Hoeness's comments and implied that Musiala's contract may need to be 'assessed'.

'We're monitoring his level of play. He'll certainly need time to improve further – like any injured player,' Dreesen said in discussion with AZ. 'And then we need to calmly assess the situation and, if necessary, sit down with his family and management.

'Either we'll all be happy together and he'll be the same Jamal he always was – or, if he's a different Jamal along the way, we'll have to discuss it.'

'If we're not consistently at a level that also aligns with the economic realities, then we'll have to talk to each other. Nevertheless, we have a valid contract, and that's the end of it.'

The 23-year-old is one of Bayern's highest-earners, having signed a deal with the club to extend his time in Bavaria in February 2025. The deal is thought to be worth £18.4million per year.

Adding fuel to the fire days later was former CEO Karl-Heinz Rummenigge, who told Sky Sports/RTL that he thought it was almost reasonable to discuss a renegotiation should Musiala not return to the level at which he once played.

'That is certainly possible and cannot be ruled out,' he said. 'Unfortunately, what we know suggests that it will be difficult to reach the level that he has so often shown here in this stadium.'

'We need to talk to the family, the player, and above all, the agency. Because then these sums are – sorry, with all due respect – no longer feasible.

'I can't imagine Jamal leaving the club or another club buying him under these circumstances. That's a problem. I think we handled this very sensitively with the player, and we're all interested in him coming back - ideally in top form. But what happens if that's not the case? Then we'll still have to think about the finances. And that's a task that the board and the supervisory board unfortunately have to make.'

Rummenigge added that he did not think it was 'problematic' for these discussions to be happening publicly, due to the club's interest in 'him coming back and playing well'.

But the player union Fifpro were forthright in their disagreement with his statements,  releasing a statement claiming that all parties currently sharing their views on Musiala's contract 'should know better'.

'Bayern's honorary president, chief executive and former chief executive have all publicly questioned Musiala's contract in quick succession,' the statement read.

'In Fifpro's view, this is a co-ordinated strategy to put pressure on Musiala and his representatives ahead of any negotiations. A club of Bayern's standing should know better.'

When this statement was shared to social media via journalist Fabrizio Romano, global talents including Jude Bellingham and Musiala's team-mate Michael Olise were among those to like the post.

Musiala collapsed on the pitch at two consecutive games in August (pictured being helped by team-mates against RB Leipzig)

The 23-year-old has been diagnosed with absence seizures and is managing his medication

Vincent Kompany has been supportive of his player's struggles, and took him off the bench with some success last weekend

Rio Ferdinand elaborated on those comments in a more detailed post on social media, adding that he believed the club are 'better than this'.

'If they want to negotiate more reasonable terms for the club going forward due to the health issues Jamal has, then surely speak to him and his team rather than to the media,' Ferdinand wrote on X. 'This situation must be hard enough to deal with for Jamal without the media and club officials talking about it publicly.

'Bayern Munich are one of the clubs who from afar have always carried themselves with a class and standard that most have admired. Sadly, this just doesn't feel like Bayern Munich in this situation.'

Criticisms were even more fiery in Germany, with the vice president of Germany's player union Michael Franz accusing the club of harassment.

'What the Bayern Munich officials are doing, and I don't mean Kompany and (Max) Eberl (director of sport), who have behaved impeccably, seems despicable to me,' Franz shared in an interview with Sport1. 'There is even something about it that resembles harassment.'

'A footballer's level of performance can be discussed publicly, but it is not right to talk about his illness and the salary he receives in such circumstances. We must not forget that Jamal Musiala has done everything in his power to come back.'

'Money has become more important than the person, and that is unacceptable.'

Former Bayern captain Stefan Effenberg stressed how important 'support and protection' from the club towards their players is, particularly for those in greater need like Musiala.

'We are talking about someone who is suffering from an illness. What I would have expected is for the club's senior officials to apologise,' Effenberg said, adding that he thought that the communications from the club had been 'disastrous'.

Musiala collapsed twice within four days in mid-August, first during Bayern's match against Leipzig – shortly after scoring – and then against Heidenheim.

He then returned to the pitch in September, netting against Bodo/Glimt in Champions League action and Union Berlin shortly before the first international break.

After suffering a hamstring injury, Musiala then spent time away from the side before his return at the end of last week – a match which also saw Harry Kane suffer an ankle injury which will keep him out for over three months.

Fifpro's statement went on to add that Bayern must be careful with their player's mental wellbeing as well as his physical issues.

'Bayern has a duty of care to Musiala,' the statement continued. 'Across professional football, uncertainty about a player's future can take a toll on their mental health, particularly when they are recovering from injury.

'Making Musiala's health and future a matter of public debate adds to that pressure. Any concerns about his contract should be raised privately with him and his representatives.

'Contracts are there to be respected. An injury or a dip in form gives a club no grounds to reopen terms it agreed to, just as a run of outstanding performances gives a player no automatic right to a pay rise.'
