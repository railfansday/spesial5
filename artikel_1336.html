Designing dislocation-driven polar vortex networks in twisted perovskites

Nature (2026) Cite this article

Twisting two atomic layers produces a geometric moiré pattern but bonding-induced interfacial reconstruction fundamentally transforms this into an ordered dislocation network—a distinction obscured in weakly bonded van der Waals systems. Although in-plane topological vortex nanostructures arising from twisting-induced lateral strain modulation have been linked to periodic moiré patterns in freestanding perovskite layers1,2,3 and 2D bilayers4, their coupling to the interfacial dislocation network in twisted layers remains unresolved. Here we demonstrate that twisted freestanding SrTiO3 (STO) layers undergo interfacial reconstruction into a network of screw dislocations, accompanied by the emergence of in-plane topological vortices. Unlike in previous reports, these vortices are associated with the dislocation network rather than with geometric moiré patterns. Four-dimensional scanning transmission electron microscopy (4DSTEM) reveals a beam deflection texture in registry with the interfacial dislocation network. A machine learning interatomic potential, trained on first-principles calculations, together with phase-field modelling, confirms that competing strains within the dislocation network stabilize polar vortex–antivortex pairs; first-principles calculations further reveal the emergence of an electronic superlattice with a well-defined periodicity. Our results establish twist-controlled dislocation networks as a new and versatile route to designing local polar and electronic structures in oxide materials.

This induces a dislocation (shaded grey region) between the two undercoordinated stackings. The insets on the right depict atomic positions across the interface for a central unit cell, illustrating the coordination environment.

Unit-cell averaged εxx, εyy and εxy (from left to right) using the described nearest-neighbour scheme for the TiO2-terminated and SrO-terminated layers (from top to bottom, respectively).

a, MLIP-DFT relaxed atomic positions at a 5° twist showing Ti atoms in the top (light) and bottom (dark) interfacial layers, demonstrating the orthogonal screw dislocation network, with a magnified view on the right. b, Twist-angle dependence of the interface energy (blue) together with the computed and analytical dislocation lengths (orange). The background colour denotes the transition from well-defined dislocations to their absence. c–f, Dislocation lengths are measured using the interlayer distance as a signature. The analytical prediction given by d = (a/2)/sin(θ/2) (orange line) matches the measured dislocation lengths.

a–d, HAADF-STEM image of the (110) cross-section of 7.5° (a,b) and 10° (c,d) twisted samples. Top and bottom images are independently sampled fields of view. The measured dislocation spacing d2 is 2.1 ± 0.2 nm in a,b and 1.5 ± 0.2 nm in c,d, agreeing well with the predictions for d2 = d/√2, as expected from this cross-section viewing plane (see text).
