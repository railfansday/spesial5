Disney and AWS Team Up to Create AI-Powered ‘Star Wars’ Robots

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The new creations, the Anzellans, will react to guests and each other in real-time, showcasing how AI could transform the theme park experience.

The Walt Disney Co. has hosted audio-animatronics at its theme parks going back to the opening of Walt Disney’s Enchanted Tiki Room in 1963. The decades since have seen presidents, beloved film characters and even Walt Disney himself turned into the audio-animatronics, relying on robots paired with scripted moments to complete the illusion for visitors.

But Disney and Amazon on Wednesday are pulling back the curtain on what Disney’s Imagineers think may be the next big thing in audio-animatronic technology: Robots with distinct personalities that can react to each other, and to visitors, powered by AI. The audio-animatronic robots in question are The Anzellans: Vilo Vele, Kado Bre’a and Jibu Bre’a, an alien species from the Star Wars galaxy that featured in the 2026 film The Mandalorian and Grogu that will make their public debut at Amazon re:Invent in Las vegas later this month. They were designed by Disney’s Imagineers, though they are powered by AWS.

“The work that we’re doing with Amazon dates back over a decade,” says Kyle Laughlin, senior VP, Walt Disney Imagineering R&D and Technology and Engineering, in an interview with The Hollywood Reporter. “We’ve continued to work together in many different forums, everything from Disney+ to supporting Imagineering and the work that we do every day, and these three Anzellans from the Star Wars universe, originally in Solo, but most recently in Mandalorian and Grogu, are these incredible droidsmiths that we wanted to bring to life.”

The three robots each have cameras an microphones, with the AWS tech helping them determine what actions they take and alien phrases they should speak.

Samira Panah Bakhtiar, GM of Telco, Media & Entertainment, Games, Advertising and Sports for AWS, notes that the script and movements were all created by Imagineers, and the aliens don’t deviate from the movements and words written by the team, even if they use AI to help guide their actions.

“One of the things I’m most proud of at Amazon is how we obsess over making our customers successful and working backwards from their needs, and in this case, in this specific project, a group of AWS team members leveraged our coding tool called Kiro to be able to help take a lot of the ideas that the Imagineering team had and be able to help rapidly get it into a place where we could execute,” Bakhtiar says.

“I think that one of the unique things here is that each character is aware of three things happening at once,” she adds. “They’re aware of the guests in front of them, they’re aware of what’s happening in the workshop, and then they’re also perfectly aware of what the other characters are doing within the scene. And keeping track of all three of those at once really gives that experience that that they’re alive, and their reflexes and their judgments like all become part of that really unique guest experience.”

“I think what our guests you know are overwhelmingly saying all the time is that they want those character interactions that feel authentic and genuine, and as you mentioned, our audio animatronics today are very scripted in terms of how that interaction happens,” Laughlin adds. “When we launch with the Anzellans at [Amazon] re:Invent, it’ll be really the first time in which we will personalize that experience based on who that guest is.”

For now, the Anzellans will only be available to view at re:Invent, though it doesn’t take much to imagine that they will end up in the Star Wars: Galaxy’s Edge worlds in California and Orlando eventually.

Still, the new robots demonstrate how Disney may ultimately use AI to improve its real-world experiences, while leaning into the creativity of humans in the process. After all, a visit to Galaxy’s Edge today will include actors dressed as characters, audio-animatronic robots, and even BDX droids, which are controlled by human operators.

“Ultimately the goal for Imagineering is to create a living world populated by all the characters you know and love,” Laughlin says. “Some of those are going to be audio-animatronics, some of those are going to be robots, some of those are going to be live performers, and some might be puppets. And I think our ability to blend all of those performances together is what makes us uniquely Disney and a one-of-one company in terms of delivering that kind of experience.”
