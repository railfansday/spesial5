Lesley Manville, Pierce Brosnan, and More Celebrate Violinist Joshua Bell at the Langham London

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

At the age of four, Joshua Bell was given his first violin, and he hasn’t put it down since. “I don’t remember falling in love with music,” says Bell. “Music has just been my life. I used to string rubber bands on my dresser drawers and make pitches by pulling the drawers out. I was fascinated with strings. It’s the closest instrument to the human voice. And I find it the most expressive instrument.”

Today, the Grammy Award–winning musician is one of the most celebrated classical violinists of his era. By 14, Bell was performing with the Philadelphia Orchestra, and would soon be touring the world. “I live in hotels 250 days a year,” says Bell of the nonstop schedule he has maintained since his teens. Specifically, Bell has been “staying at the Langham for decades now,” he says, referring to the regal central London hotel that first opened in 1865. “I chose it on my own because it was near one of my favorite halls in London, which is the Wigmore Hall. And now I stay with them whenever I’m traveling.”

That kinship was solidified over coupes of Champagne and hand-dived Orkney scallops, with the hotel celebrating Bell, a “Langham Luminary,” in its London ballroom packed with writers, artists, designers, and musicians.

Attendees included actor Simon Callow, House of Dragon actor Emily Carey, former British culture minister Ed Vaizey, Saturday Night Live UK cast producer Susie Hall, stage and television actor Indira Varma, and Verity actor Ismael Cruz Córdova.

Held in partnership with Vanity Fair, the evening marked the latest iteration of the Langham’s Luminaries in Residence program. The event series is continuation of the hotel’s legacy that brings together a diverse, influential community—an evocative reminder of prominent guests from its storied past, including Charles Dickens, Winston Churchill, George Orwell, Oscar Wilde, and Mark Twain.

“I’m not going to put my name in the same sentence as Toscanini or Mark Twain—who has stayed here, or Churchill, who has stayed here. But it’s cool to be part of the history,” says Bell. “So maybe someday, in a hundred years, there will be a violinist who might say that—that I stayed here. I don’t know, maybe that would mean something.”

It certainly does mean something. Pierce Brosnan and his wife, Keely Shaye Brosnan, “have been huge fans of Joshua for many, many years,” Brosnan tells me. “Keely went to go see him…I don’t know how many years back. Unfortunately, due to the LA traffic, she got there late, five minutes late, and they wouldn’t let her into the concert. So tonight is one of those nights.”

Bell, who performed mid-dinner with members of the Academy of St. Martin in the Fields—with a surprise appearance from his wife, soprano Larisa Martínez—brought about a collective catharsis.

Dazzling, fiery, and flamboyant, the experience of watching Bell up close is impossible to replicate. “I just think he’s incredibly soulful, and there’s something really elegant,” actor Russell Tovey tells me, before explaining how he actually discovered Bell via his Instagram algorithm.

For actor Lesley Manville, the event marked her introduction to Bell’s music. “I’m a bit embarrassed that he was not on my radar. But isn’t that nice to have a surprise like that?” says Manville. “Schubert’s String Quintet is one of my favorite pieces of music. It’s unbelievably beautiful. You think of a violin, you think of those strings. And it’s instant emotion. They just make you cry.”

There is a great power in being able to access the emotions of others, as Bell does.

“I think it is the ultimate gift to be able to make people happy,” says Brosnan. “My job is being an entertainer—to take the words of great writing and try to transform it into something that is meaningful and memorable.”

Speaking of entertaining, the week’s announcement that Robert Pattinson will be the next James Bond was front of mind. “I wish him every joy and every success. He and I made a movie a number of years ago called Remember Me. I played his father. So I was very, very pleased and joyful for his nomination and now his success, and I believe he will be magnificent.”

And that creative full circle is exactly what the night was advocating. “Our own Luminaries in Residence program reflects our belief that meaningful hospitality creates genuine connection with extraordinary talents with one another, and with arts,” Bob van den Oord, CEO of Langham Hospitality Group, explained as he took the stage. “The Langham isn’t simply a place to stay, but a place to gather, exchange ideas, and indeed celebrate.”
