Rockstar Is Promoting GTA 6 in a Country Where It Can't Officially Sell It

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Rockstar is promoting Grand Theft Auto 6 to players in mainland China, even though the game hasn't been approved for official sale there. It's an unusual situation for one of the biggest launches of the year, but the company's social media activity makes it clear that Chinese players are still part of the audience it's trying to reach.

Rockstar’s latest release has already attracted millions of viewers.

According to a Reuters report published on November 18, Rockstar posted a GTA 6 preorder-related video on Douyin, the Chinese counterpart to TikTok, on November 12. The video encouraged users to preload the game ahead of its worldwide November 19 release.

The Douyin account is verified under Rockstar International Limited and showed a Shanghai IP location when Reuters reviewed it. By November 18, it had attracted around 480,000 followers and more than 300,000 likes. That's a sizeable audience for a game that can't be sold through officially approved channels in mainland China.

The promotion itself isn't complicated. Rockstar was telling fans that GTA 6 was available to preload on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, just as it had been doing for players elsewhere. What's unusual is the market receiving that message.

Reuters was able to access both the account and its promotional video in China. Neither Rockstar nor the National Press and Publication Administration, the country's regulator, had responded to the publication's requests for comment when the report was filed.

This isn't the same as Rockstar announcing an official Chinese release. A social media promotion doesn't mean GTA 6 has been approved for official sale in mainland China. The studio also hasn't said that an approved release is on the way.

Imported video games have to pass a government approval process before they can be officially sold in mainland China. The authorities scrutinize content such as violence, sexual material, and politically sensitive themes. Given what Grand Theft Auto is about, it's not difficult to see why the series might face obstacles.

Reuters reports that the Grand Theft Auto franchise has never received official approval for sale in mainland China. GTA 6 is also missing from the regulator's published lists of approved imported games. That's the key point here. It's not a new announcement that the game has been banned outright, but rather that it lacks the approval required for an official domestic release.

The contrast is particularly striking because Rockstar is treating GTA 6 as a worldwide event. The game launched on November 19 on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, and its marketing has reached far beyond the countries where ordinary retail sales are available.

Of course, an official sales restriction doesn't necessarily stop people from playing the game. Reuters says Chinese players have been using foreign console accounts or buying imported boxed editions that include download codes. One Douyin user who said he was in Beijing posted about obtaining a Hong Kong edition for roughly 500 yuan, or about $75.

That doesn't make those purchases part of an officially approved Chinese distribution channel. It simply shows that a market can exist for a game even when the usual local release process isn't available.

It's also worth distinguishing China's situation from the ratings concerns seen in other territories. GTA 6 almost ran into trouble in South Korea, but the issue in mainland China concerns the separate approval system for imported games. They aren't interchangeable cases.

For now, Rockstar can speak to a substantial Chinese audience while GTA 6 remains unavailable for officially approved domestic sales. Whether that situation changes is something only a later regulatory decision could settle. Until then, its Douyin account is a reminder that the game's reach doesn't end at the borders of its official markets.
