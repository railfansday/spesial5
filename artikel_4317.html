It’s no secret that electric cars are more affordable to maintain than their gasoline-powered counterparts. While they may have a higher purchase price than a comparable gas-powered vehicle, EVs can save owners money over time by eliminating the need for regular engine oil changes, emissions tests, and other traditional maintenance tasks. Affordable electric vehicles are also becoming increasingly common, with even more options expected to arrive in the coming years.

However, that doesn't mean electric cars are completely maintenance-free. Like any other vehicle, EVs require regular care and attention to remain reliable and perform at their best for many years.

Although they don't have conventional combustion engines or traditional automatic transmissions, electric cars still feature components that require periodic maintenance. These include reduction gearboxes, brake fluid, brake pads, coolant systems, and tires among other things. Keeping these components in good condition is essential to ensuring an EV's long-term reliability.

For the second installment of our EV maintenance series, I'll be taking a closer look at the Hyundai Ioniq 5, one of the most recognizable electric crossovers on the market. Made in the United States and South Korea, the Ioniq 5 has long been one of the biggest rivals to the ubiquitous Tesla Model Y.

I'll break down Hyundai's recommended maintenance schedule, explaining which items are required to keep the vehicle running smoothly over the long term.

Compared to Tesla, which is known for its rather skimpy maintenance plan, Hyundai includes a generous list of maintenance items in the owner’s manual. The automaker also includes additional service actions that should be performed if the car is used in harsher-than-normal conditions, so owners know what to expect in the long run.

There’s a normal maintenance schedule and a separate section for severe usage and low mileage conditions. Hyundai also has a list of maintenance items that can be performed by the owner. I’ll go through all of them, so drivers can easily mark down what needs to be done during the car’s lifetime.

Hyundai says that normal driving conditions include distances longer than 10 miles, clean roads without salt or dust, ambient temperatures under 90ºF (32ºC), no towing, no roof rack, and normal speeds. These include repeated driving short distances of less than five miles in normal temperatures or less than 10 miles in freezing temperatures, and driving on rough, dusty, muddy, unpaved, graveled, or salt-spread roads.

But that’s not all. Driving in areas using salt or other corrosive materials, or in very cold weather, driving in heavy dust conditions, driving in heavy traffic areas with the ambient temperature higher than 90ºF (32 ºC) while consuming more than 50% of electric energy, and driving on uphill, downhill, or mountain roads repeatedly can also result in extra maintenance items.

Towing a trailer, using a roof rack, driving as a patrol car or taxi, frequently driving at high speeds, and frequently driving in stop-and-go conditions are also part of the conditions that lead to more intense servicing requirements.
