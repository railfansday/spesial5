NowThis just bought one of Gen Z’s favorite Instagram news accounts. Here’s why

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

If Instagram is your preferred avenue for media consumption, you’ve probably come across posts by Impact. The popular Gen-Z-targeted account is characterized by its quick-turn analysis on progressive, left-wing topics ranging from identity politics to mental health and niche internet trends. That winning formula caught the attention of social video publisher NowThis, which recently acquired the brand.

Launched in 2020 by founders Tim Chau and Michelle Andrews, the Impact Instagram handle was born in the apex of Gen Z’s formative years, an era marked by a hyper-online generation who reckoned with Donald Trump, COVID-19, social isolation, canceled graduations, the murder of George Floyd, and climate disaster all in the same year. Impact created timely stories around narratives that Gen Z desperately craved and traditional media often failed to cover: Its content is often among the first to share pointed commentary on trending headlines. 

When comedian Shane Gillis was being criticized online for a misogynistic SNL bit, Impact published “Male comedians don’t actually have to hate women to be popular.” After the internet was in an uproar over the U.K. banning books authored by Sally Rooney due to her support for Palestine, Impact wrote about it immediately. Impact even creates content around shared woes that span multiple generations, helping it reach larger audiences—as seen in posts like “Is it rude to ask friends to stay with them when you visit their city?” The comments section of each post is the heart of the community the Impact team has built and a glimpse into real cultural capital, making it easy to see why NowThis chose to purchase the company. 

Founded in 2012 by former Huffington Post execs Kenneth Lerer and Eric Hippeau, NowThis began as a social-first go-to for millennial content before being acquired by Vox Media. As millennials aged into the next phase of adulthood, companies and news organizations from all corners of the internet competed for the coveted attention of young people, nudging the video publisher into a new chapter. In 2023, NowThis expanded into a large-scale media network targeting Gen Z, taking on a new CEO, Sharon Mussalli, and a new editor-in-chief, Michael Vito Valentino, while also reckoning with the ramifications of significant layoffs. This latest move in acquiring Impact—for an alleged seven-figure, all-cash deal—demonstrates a commitment to reaching its target audience and the community of young people behind Impact. 

“[They] care about what’s happening in the world and want to understand how it connects to their relationships, their work, their culture, and their futures. That’s closely aligned with the Gen Z female audience we’re building for at NowThis. And we saw an opportunity to invest in that relationship and give it more room to grow,” Mussalli said in a statement via the NowThis PR team. 

She adds: “Impact brings a distinct editorial voice and a strong sense of what resonates with its community. We bring the production capabilities and experience developing original video franchises to help translate that into new series and recurring formats. Our ambition is to build something this audience can grow with, across all NowThis channels. That means giving people more than an individual story they encounter in their feed—it means creating recognizable programming they seek out, share, and return to.” 

In a statement to Fast Company, Chau, who is stepping down from the Impact staff but will remain involved in some capacity, calls the move “bittersweet.” “We put everything into Impact to get it where it is today, so stepping back isn’t easy. But we couldn’t be happier about where it’s headed, because we know NowThis is the right home for it. They’re leading in a space where social now dominates—really the first of their kind—and that’s exactly the future we want Impact to be part of.”

“My hope is that Impact stays the place young people go to for stories that actually land with them, the ones they talk about with friends, share, and sit with. Legacy media has struggled to resonate with this generation, and I hope Impact can keep being the place where they feel truly understood and connected,” Chau said.
