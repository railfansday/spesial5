A footballer who loves getting cards! Tottenham star Tosin Adarabioyo opens up on his passion for collecting as he launches trading card shop in London with Chelsea's Reece James and Levi Colwill

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Published: 18:00 EST, 20 November 2026 | Updated: 18:06 EST, 20 November 2026

Tottenham star Tosin Adarabioyo has joined forces with former Chelsea team-mates Reece James and Levi Colwill to launch a trading card shop in London.

Card Cop, opening in Borough Market on Thursday, will sell cards from a variety of sports as well as other collections such as Pokemon and Marvel.

Adarabioyo began collecting as a child and returned to the hobby in 2020. His collection, which he shares on social media, spans a variety of sports and he also has several of Brazilian icon Neymar.

'I’ve been collecting for years, and it’s great to see the hobby continue to grow,' said the 29-year-old, who left Chelsea for Spurs last summer.

'I love collecting. It’s a great way for supporters to connect with their favourite players. I’ve always felt that collectors are some of the most passionate fans.

'I’ve got to know so many collectors through the hobby. Talking about our collections is an instant icebreaker, and I know those conversations can mean a lot. I want to bring more players into the hobby and recreate those moments.

Tosin Adarabioyo, like many footballers, has a passion for collecting trading cards

The new shop is set to open in London's Borough Market next week

Adarabioyo enjoyes collecting cards of former PSG star Neymar, who won 130 caps for Brazil

'Neymar is criminally underrated to me, and I think his cards are too. Some of the images take me straight back to performances I’ve watched over the years. It’s hard to describe, but it’s like bottling up that feeling forever. Football purists will know what I mean.'

West Ham defender Kyle Walker-Peters, who played with Tosin at England youth level, is among backers along with James and Colwill.

Adarabioyo already has an online retail business, The Huddle, with guests such as Dominic Solanke, Robert Sanchez and Adam Wharton all featuring on live streams, opening boxes as fans watch.

Recent figures valued the global sports trading card market at $13.75billion (£10.5bn) in 2025, with that projected to grow to $27.52bn (£21bn) by 2034.

Formula One star Lewis Hamilton got in on the act earlier this year, launching his own venture and a shop in Frankfurt.

The seven-time world champion was already an ambassador for Topps - who also have the licence for Premier League cards - and said: 'I’ve loved collecting cards since I was a kid — Pokemon, football, anything I could get my hands on.

'To now play a role in shaping the future of the category is incredible. We have a significant opportunity to tap into the culture in a way no one has before.'
