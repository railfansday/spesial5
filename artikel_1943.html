Newcastle United fans think on-loan striker Nick Woltemade can solve their issues as the club lines up a new attacking signing in January.

Matthias Jaissle has struggled to make his mark since being appointed as Newcastle manager in August.

He oversaw a busy end to the summer window that saw the club allow German striker Woltemade to join Juventus on loan.

The 24-year-old had an immediate impact on Tyneside last season but struggled for form in the second half of the campaign.

He has impressed with the Italian giants, registering four goals and two assists in 13 appearances so far.

Newcastle's own slow start to the campaign is set to see them enter the market for a new forward, despite Woltemade still being on their books.

Matias Fernandez-Pardo effectively replaced Woltemade in the Newcastle squad in a £52million move from Lille, but he is yet to find the net in his first seven appearances for his new club.

Will Osula has been plagued by injury issues and has one strike to his name so far this term, while fellow striker Yoane Wissa has the same record in 13 appearances up to now.

According to a new report from Sky Sports' Keith Downie, Jaissle is eyeing new striker and number 10 signings in the next window, although the club are aware that landing a player in both positions is unlikely.

With Woltemade doing well away from St James' Park, Newcastle fans are adamant that he can succeed under Jaissle.

One took to X to reply to the report, saying: "Recall Woltemade = striker problem sorted, then concentrate on a number 10. This saves time and a lot of money."

Jaissle is a man under pressure and so will likely not want to be seen going back to Woltemade after he contributed to the decision to let him leave at the end of the summer window.

The Newcastle boss spent the international break in their home country and gave an interview to German press about how they have kept in contact and plans for the future.

He said: "We are continuing to follow his development very closely. I'm confident that he still has a huge career ahead of him. He is an incredibly great person and a unique footballer. We were extremely open and transparent with each other right from the start.

"At the start of the season, there was a period when he wasn't in the starting line-up and other players were ahead of him because of the way we played. He then decided to get more playing time elsewhere. That's how the move to Juve finally came about."
