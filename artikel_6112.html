End Of An Era: 50-Year-Old Boeing 747 Hotel In Stockholm Is Being Scrapped After 16 Years

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

There are various things that can happen to an aircraft when it ceases flying for good. While many are simply scrapped straight away, others are kept by airlines as training resources or sources of spare parts. Some, however, go on to be repurposed, with the Jumbo Stay hotel at Stockholm Arlanda Airport (ARN) in Sweden being a notable example. In 2009, the retired 747 was reopened as accommodation.

The 747 was an avgeek's dream, as well as a convenient place to stay for those needing to be at the airport for an early morning flight or after a late arrival. For 16 years, the jet was a bucket list location for enthusiasts from all around the world, but it closed in March 2025 due to bankruptcy. Now, after sitting dormant for almost two years, it is finally being scrapped, truly bringing the end to Arlanda's jumbo era.

Citing reports in the Swedish media, aeroTELEGRAPH confirmed on Thursday that the work has begun to dismantle and scrap the 50-year-old Boeing 747 that used to be one of aviation's quirkiest hotels. Fast progress is expected, with the double-decker quadjet set to be fully dismantled by the end of the month. It had initially been hoped that, following the closure, a buyer or new operator would be found.

However, this did not prove to be the case, and airport operator Swedavia was left to deal with the stranded Boeing 747. According to TV4, there are currently no plans for what Swedavia will do with the land once the plane has gone. Arlanda Airport Director Karl Eklund said that "I hope we can come up with something good going forward, [as] many people who come into the airport pass through here." He added:

"It's time now. Jumbo Stay has been a good feature at Arlanda for many years, but in recent years it hasn't been what you want to see when passing by."

As we shall explore in greater detail later on, the aircraft was withdrawn from service in 2002, but it wasn't until 2009 that the Jumbo Stay hotel opened. Following its grounding, the jet was acquired by entrepreneur and hotelier Oscar Diös, who decided to turn it into a hotel to provide a low-cost option for those looking to stay near Arlanda. Permission was granted in 2007, with work on the jet starting in 2008.

Following the multi-million-dollar restoration, which included a total overhaul of the 747's interior, the accommodation opened for the first time in January 2009. At the time, it was known as Jumbo Hostel, but its name eventually changed to Jumbo Stay, which it retained until its closure. There were 33 rooms in the old aircraft, including some that were located in the jet's cockpit and even within its engine frames.

However, the hotel had to close after just over 16 years of operations in March 2025 due to bankruptcy. This, aeroTELEGRAPH notes, forced the cancelation of around 800 reservations. Jumbo Stay had been suffering economically for several years amid increased competition and an advertising ban, as well as the impacts of the pandemic. Now, just under two years later, the jet is being dismantled.

According to Planespotters.net, the Boeing 747-200 that would later go on to become the Jumbo Stay hotel began its life at Singapore Airlines. As seen in the photo above, it bore the registration 9V-SQE, and flew for the carrier from 1976 to 1983. In 1984, it joined Pan Am as N747BH, before being re-registered as N727PA and named 'Clipper Belle of the Sky' shortly thereafter. It stayed there for seven years.

Having left Pan American World Airways in 1991, it joined Nationair Canada as C-FNXP in 1992. During its time at the Canadian airline, it was leased out to major Asian operators such as Garuda Indonesia and Cathay Pacific. Its later years saw it serve various US operators, before ending up at Sweden's Transjet Airways as SE-RBN in January 2002. It was withdrawn and stored at Arlanda in July of that year.
