Industry Insights: Making Pet-Friendly Vacation Rentals More Profitable

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

PetScreening founder and CEO John Bradford discusses how vacation rental operators can capitalize on growing demand for pet-friendly stays while managing property risks and improving operational consistency.

This sponsored content was created in collaboration with a Skift partner.

This edition of “Industry Insights” features an interview with John Bradford, founder and CEO of PetScreening, a platform that helps property managers screen pets and assistance animals and manage related policies.

Bradford sees a growing business opportunity for vacation rental operators willing to accommodate pets. According to AirDNA findings cited by PetScreening, pet-friendly listings generated 14.4% more annual revenue potential and commanded 6% higher average daily rates than non-pet-friendly properties. Bradford shares how operators can capitalize on this demand while addressing concerns about property damage, liability, and policy compliance.

Skift Studio: What are the biggest opportunities for short-term rental operators when it comes to accommodating pets, particularly in terms of revenue growth and guest demand?

John Bradford: The biggest opportunity is the gap between the number of travelers who want to bring their pets and the number of rentals that welcome them.

Approximately 71% of U.S. households own a pet, yet only about 30% of U.S. short-term rental listings are pet-friendly. That creates an opportunity for operators to differentiate their properties and reach a large, underserved audience.

There is also a real revenue opportunity. In a recent 2026 Pet Influence Study by Veridata Insights, 67% of pet owners said they would pay at least $25 more per night for a pet-welcoming vacation rental. AirDNA also found that pet-friendly rentals are being introduced into the market at twice the rate of new non-pet-friendly rentals.

That doesn’t mean every property needs to accept every pet. But with the right policies and processes, operators can pursue additional bookings and revenue without giving up operational control.

What are the biggest challenges or risks facing vacation rental operators when it comes to pets, and how can they better manage them?

One of the biggest challenges is that pets and other animals are not always disclosed at the time of booking. In our analysis of reservations from integrated short-term rental clients, PetScreening submissions identified approximately 60% more pets and animals than the booking data originally indicated. We found that nearly 70% of assistance-animal submissions involved an animal not noted on the original booking.

This doesn’t necessarily mean guests intentionally withheld information. The value is that property managers learned about these animals before check-in, giving them time to clarify details, apply appropriate policies, and prepare the property. That visibility helps prevent undisclosed arrivals and reduces the risk of property damage, compliance issues, fee disputes, and safety incidents. Clear policies and connected documentation further reduce risk.

How is PetScreening helping move the vacation rental industry forward by making it easier for operators to adopt and manage pet-friendly policies?

We’re helping operators move from an informal, property-by-property approach to a more consistent and repeatable process. PetScreening gives teams a way to understand who is traveling with a pet or assistance animal before arrival, communicate expectations, and apply policies more consistently.

Guest attestations and clear documentation add a layer of protection by creating a record of what was submitted and acknowledged. That means fewer surprises for operators, owners, and guests.

We often hear from third-party managers that our platform is a business development tool that helps them attract more owners and convince them to become pet-friendly. When there is a way to manage pet policies, owners feel more comfortable accepting pets.

Our larger goal is to help create a hospitality industry that is both more pet-inclusive and more pet-responsible, across vacation rentals, hotels, campgrounds, and timeshares.

As someone who travels frequently, what’s your favorite travel tip or hack?

Whenever I can, I bring my dog Berkeley along when I travel. Before we leave, I make sure her FidoAlert profile is up to date, and her QR code ID tag is attached to her collar. If we ever get separated, I can send an alert to nearby pet finders, and someone who finds her can scan her tag and send a text alert to let me know she’s been found. It gives me a little extra peace of mind while we’re away.
