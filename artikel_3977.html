Hardware Review: Astro A50 X Lightspeed - The Ultimate Gaming Headset, But Not Suited to All

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The Astro A50 X Lightspeed is a pair of headphones I’ve been looking forward to trying out for a while. This is a high-end headset that actually utilises HDMI passthrough rather than the traditional dongle connection.

This is by far the most expensive headset I’ve ever tested, so let’s see if that moolah translates to a genuinely great audio experience.

The Astro A50 X Lightspeed is a pair of wireless gaming headphones from Logitech. They operate out of a base station, which both charges the headset and enables you to switch sources between PS5, PC, and Xbox.

It’s rocking Pro-G Graphene drivers, a 48kHz flip-to-mute mic, Lightspeed wireless technology for quick connections, a playsync button that allows you to switch sources, a game and voice chat audio balancer on the right earcup, and Bluetooth, which can be used simultaneously with the wireless HDMI connection.

In total, this headset will set you back £309.99/$399.99, and is available in White, Black, and Mars Red variants.

Within the Astro A50 X Lightspeed box, you have the headset itself, the charging station, the power cable, and connecting USB-C to A connectors.

Truth be told, setting this thing up is a bit of a headache, and it requires what seems like a lot of wires. So in my setup, I’m connecting the headset to both my PS5 and my PC. With that, I need the power cable running to the station, but it also splits and runs to the PC.

I also have a USB-C to A cable connecting to my PS5. Then you have the HDMI cables, which aren’t provided. You need one running from your PS5 to the station, and another running from the station to the TV or monitor.

In total, that’s four cables running out of the back of that station (one splitting off to power and PC), and it could be more if you decided to connect an Xbox too. It’s far from the tidiest setup, and it took me a little bit to ensure my desk wasn’t a complete mess of cables.

Before we get to the sound, we have the headset itself, which is a lot lighter than I expected. It features a plush fabric headband and ear cups, which is a little different from the usual faux leather designs of other gaming headphones.

The first thing I thought when I noticed this was, “I wonder if it gets hot?” and sadly, it actually does. Don’t get me wrong, these things are very comfortable; however, if you’re in a hotter room, or maybe you’ve been wearing them for extended sessions, your ears will get warm. The Sony INZONE H9 IIs also utilised fabric around the earcups, but were quite breathable by comparison.

However, heat aside, I loved the feel of these headphones. They are quite flexible and are easy to find the right fit for your head. If you’re in a cool room, you’ll be able to wear these things for hours on end without feeling any discomfort.

The right earcup, which houses most of the controls, thankfully isn’t overcomplicated with tons of buttons – something that’s annoyed me in some other headsets. Instead, you have a volume rocker, which actually controls the sound with accuracy – not something every headset gets right – a Bluetooth button, the playsync button to change sources, and the power button.

Switching sources is one of the big features of this headset, and I loved being able to easily do so at the touch of a button. This might not be something that the average person is utilising all of the time, but as someone who regularly switches between recording gameplay on my PS5 and then writing some video scripts, I actually found it super useful.

An interesting quirk of this HDMI setup, however, is that you must select your PS5 as your source through the headset for it to play through the television or monitor; otherwise, it’s as if there is no video source. However, the charging station does support HDMI 2.1, meaning you can utilise passthrough of games running up to 4K 120Hz, allowing for 24-bit high-resolution audio.

On the side of the right earcup, you also have a game and chat audio balancer, which is really easy to use and doesn’t get muddled up with any other controls.

On the left side, you have the flip-to-mute mic, which is also quite flexible. It’s claimed that this is producing “broadcast quality” comms. I doubt I’d go that far, but the mic on the Astro does sound fantastic and is much better at cutting out those additional room sounds like breathing.

I was actually playing games online with someone else who was using the same headset and found it actually runs a little loud, but this is something you can easily fix in the party settings, or by adjusting your own mic gain on the PS5.

Logitech claims you can get around 24 hours of playback on this headset. It’s not something I really got to properly test out, however, and I doubt you will either. Because the base station also acts as a charging station, I was always just quickly dumping them there at the end of a session. It meant that battery life became a complete afterthought for me, which is really what you’re looking for.

The station itself will showcase how much charge the headset has, as well as the source that’s currently selected, and whether Bluetooth is active. As well as being handy, I think this station looks really nice, and it quickly blended into my desk setup.

The only main complaint I really have with this setup, and it’s maybe personal to me, is that the headset isn’t very mobile. Where most dongle headsets can be switched to another PS5 in another room, or taken out of the house if you’re taking your PS5 somewhere else, the Astro A50 X Lightspeed is very much a stationary headset.

I suspect for most people this is a non-issue, but as someone who has a PS5 in my office and then one in my living room, I wasn’t easily able to bring this headset downstairs.

The audio experience is really what you’re here for, and on that front, the Astro A50 X Lightspeed does not disappoint. These headphones have spectacular range.

I’ve used them to play quieter games like Zero Parades and it picks up all the little minutiae of dialogue and environmental sounds. I've also played bombastic games like Modern Warfare 4, where explosions are brought to life with powerful bass. Then, switching that source, I’ve spent countless hours listening to music and even taking part in calls, and the quality persists.

The Astro A50 X Lightspeed is a jack of all trades headset, but it may also be a master of all. I was thoroughly impressed with the audio quality on display, which, to be fair, is expected with the price tag attached to this headset.

The one curious omission from this headset is active noise cancelling. It’s not featured at all, so you’re still going to hear your own voice or the surrounding sounds during your gaming sessions.

Maybe as a byproduct of that, I did find the headset a little quiet. I tend to like my headsets to run loud, so I often felt like I wanted a bit more in terms of pure volume – although I’m sure my ears will be glad that isn’t the case.

If the out-of-the-box experience isn’t quite right for you, then there is a Logitech app, on both mobile and desktop, which allows you to further customise your audio profile to get the right fit for you.

There’s no getting around the fact that the Astro A50 X Lightspeed is an incredibly expensive headset. With that, I’d really only be happy recommending it to people who know they’ll get the full use of its features, namely that of source swapping.

As someone who regularly splits my time between PC and PS5, being able to switch sources with the touch of a button is a Godsend. It’s really the thing that differentiates this headset from the rest.

However, strip that away, and you still have a solid headset that masterfully balances the audioscape of both quiet and explosive games, thanks to HDMI-passthrough 24-bit audio. On top of that, you have a mic that’s a cut above the industry standard, and a flexible, lightweight design that does run a little hot for me, but is still incredibly plush.

It might not be the right headset for everyone, but if this sounds like it would work for you, you can’t get much better than this.
