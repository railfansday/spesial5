Five athlete-team disputes in Bay Area that predate Brandon Aiyuk's stare-down with 49ers

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The San Francisco 49ers' long, tumultuous contract dispute with Brandon Aiyuk came to an end on Monday when the team waived the wide receiver. It already rates as one of the most contentious confrontations between player and team in Bay Area sports history.

This all started in 2024 with Aiyuk requesting a trade amid contract extension talks. Then ACL and MCL tears in his right knee during Week 7 of the 2024 NFL season - seven games into his freshly-signed $120 million extension - further complicated the relationship.

Aiyuk failed to fulfill his rehabilitation and team participation responsibilities, prompting the 49ers to void nearly $27 million of guaranteed money. That led to a series of public outbursts from Aiyuk; he posted on social media pictures of himself in Washington Commanders gear asking for a trade, posted video of himself appearing to speed down the street near Levi's Stadium in his car, and disparaged team leadership.

Aiyuk stopped reporting to the 49ers entirely and the team made it clear the 28-year-old would not be invited back. Contract terms are still in flux after the divorce with the 49ers seeking $13 million of his bonus money reimbursed.

It has been an odd saga, but it's also not the first time a disgruntled athlete made a stink about his relationship with a Bay Area team and the money he's owed. Here are five other times something similar happened.

Wide receiver Terrell Owens played his first eight NFL seasons with the 49ers, but left for Philadelphia after a dispute with the team. (Lance Iversen/S.F. Chronicle)

Terrell Owens versus 49ers, 2004: Tension had been growing between star wide receiver Terrell Owens and the 49ers.

Owens, openly ready to part ways with his longtime team after years of blow ups and acrimony, planned to void the final three years of his contract and become a free agent by the March deadline in 2004. His agent, however, didn't realize the deadline to void had moved from March to Feb. 21 and reportedly missed the deadline, which meant the 49ers maintained they technically still had the rights to Owens. And they planned on taking advantage.The 49ers pulled together a trade that would send Owens to the Baltimore Ravens for a second-round pick in the 2004 draft. Owens, believing he was a free agent, had negotiated terms to join the Philadelphia Eagles.

The NFL Players Association intervened, and the dispute went to arbitration. The Ravens got out of the entire transaction. Owens wound up in Philadelphia, but before the Eagles sent a fifth-round pick to Baltimore and defensive end Brandon Whiting to the 49ers as compensation. Owens signed a seven-year, $49 million deal with the Eagles.

The 49ers got something out of the debacle, and Owens got out of San Francisco.

Chris Webber performed well with the Warrors, but he and coach Don Nelson butted heads over what position he should play and Webber was dealt to Washington after his rookie season. (Fred Larson/S.F. Chronicle)

Chris Webber versus Don Nelson, 1994-95: A sign-and-trade deal ultimately helped resolve one of the most high-profile feuds in Golden State Warriors history.

The Warriors looked to have found a franchise savior in 1993 when they acquired No. 1 pick Chris Webber from the Orlando Magic.

After signing a 15-year, $74.4 million contract, Webber won Rookie of the Year honors while leading Golden State to the playoffs. But problems brewed under the surface.

Webber and head coach Don Nelson had engaged in a dispute over how Webber would be used - Nelson wanted him to play center while Webber thought his skillset lent itself to roles outside the post. The feud boiled over and became public.

Webber didn't want to play for Nelson and opted out of his contract to become a restricted free agent; reports then indicate Nelson offered to quit if it meant Webber would return to Golden State.

Webber sat out of the 1994 training camp and the first six games of the 1994-95 season with the Warriors as contract talks stalled - Webber wanted an opt-out clause to create another escape route - but the two sides came to an agreement on a deal with the intention to trade him.

A day later, the Warriors traded Webber to the Washington Bullets for forward Tom Gugliotta and three first-round draft picks. Nelson wound up resigning anyway.

Catfish Hunter became a free agent when he A's owner Charlie Finley mistimed payments to an annuity and Hunter's case went to arbitration. (Bettmann Archive via Getty Images)

Catfish Hunter versus Charlie O. Finley, 1974: Before Curt Flood's lawsuit and the advent of regulated free agency in 1976, Oakland A's ace Catfish Hunter, a future Hall of Famer, used a contract dispute to become one of baseball's first free agents.

In 1974, Hunter claimed that A's owner Charlie O. Finley had failed to pay $50,000, half of his yearly salary, into a long-term annuity fund. Peter Seitz, an arbitrator, determined it was a breach of contract and Hunter was freed from the contract with the A's, becoming a free agent.

The pitcher would eventually sign a five-year, $3.25 million contract with the New York Yankees. It was the first multi-million dollar contract signed in baseball, according to the Baseball Hall of Fame. Though this contract dispute didn't grow into an acrimonious spectacle, it had historical significance.

Wide receiver Antonio Brown got a big contract from the Oakland Raiders, but a dispute over what helmet he could wear led to friction with the team and he never played for the team. (Kevin Abele/Icon Sportswire via Getty Images)

Antonio Brown versus Oakland Raiders, 2019: Troubled wide receiver Antonio Brown was traded to the Oakland Raiders before the 2019 season, but never played a snap for the silver-and-black as a result of contract disputes before the regular season even kicked off.

A helmet debacle proved the most consuming tiff between player and team. Brown insisted on wearing an older helmet model that was no longer NFL-approved. Brown threatened retirement and filed grievances in protest.

The Raiders fined Brown for missing practices and Brown fought back, reportedly getting into an altercation with then-general manager Mike Mayock at the Raiders' facility.

The Raiders levied another, heftier fine on Brown and voided $29 million in guarantees in his contract. Brown responded by posting, in part, "Release me, Raiders" on Instagram.

The Raiders obliged and Brown agreed to terms with the New England Patriots the same day.

Evander Kane became embroiled in controversy with the Sharks over COVID protocol in 2021, an issue that led to his departure from the team. (Josie Lepe/For the S.F. Chronicle)

Evander Kane versus Sharks: Star wing Evander Kane had been under investigation by the NHL both for betting on his own games and for allegations of abuse against his estranged wife while he played for the San Jose Sharks. The investigation found no evidence supporting either allegation.

Failure to follow COVID-19 protocol in 2021, though, became the primary catalyst for the Sharks in 2022 to terminate Kane's seven-year, $49 million contract he had signed in 2018. Kane had served a 21-game suspension for turning in a fake COVID-19 vaccination card and further violated restrictions while playing for the American Hockey League's San Jose Barracuda by traveling home to Vancouver, British Columbia, while in protocol.

The relationship between Kane and the Sharks grew strained and the team reportedly looked to trade him, to no avail. The Sharks determined the COVID infractions were a breach of contract and, initially, Kane had to forfeit the nearly $23 million that remained on his deal. The NHL Players' Association filed a grievance against the Sharks; Kane's agent said the team didn't have sufficient grounds to terminate his contract.

Kane was released in January 2022 and signed a one-year deal with the Edmonton Oilers shortly after. He then inked a four-year extension with Edmonton in July 2022.

Kane settled the grievance with the Sharks in September 2022.
