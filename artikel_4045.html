Review: Call Of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 - The First COD I've Cared About In Ages

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

For many years now, I've felt like I knew exactly what to expect from a new Call of Duty game without ever needing to play it. When I do eventually get stuck in, because I'm a COD enjoyer deep down, they're usually par for the course: action-packed, testosterone-fuelled kiddie crack designed to overstimulate, lock you in, and keep you playing.

Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 surprised me, though. Activision's latest entry is so much more than my rather crass judgement would’ve suggested, delivering the best campaign experience I've had with the FPS series since World At War. What a corker this one is, let me tell you!

So, let's talk campaign. It's easily the most cinematic, well-acted and tense narrative to come out of Call of Duty in years. Whilst it's fun to swing Captain Price's big, meaty fists around, for me, the real stars are the Korean and American marines who make up Sierra-1.

Throughout the 6-8 hours it takes to complete, MW4's globetrotting campaign pops us into the boots of Private First Class Park Hyun-Jun and wastes no time unleashing a full-scale invasion upon South Korea. As far as opening missions go, 'Mayhem Walking' is jaw-dropping in its depiction of the devastation of Hajin City. It’s an absolute banger.

Defending against North Korean paratroopers sets the tone perfectly for a campaign that just doesn't falter. Mission after mission impressed me and kept me wanting more. I suppose my assumptions were still at least half-correct.

Yes, it's a thrill to experience Price face off against his former teammate Ghost in exhilarating knife-to-knife combat. It's nerve-wracking to ransack North Korean homes in search of defectors. It's just that I've never doubted Call of Duty's knack for pulling off an action-packed spectacle: rather, its ability to make me care about the characters caught up in it that I’ve questioned.

Sierra-1, along with the few stragglers who link up with the squad, provides that human element and gives me a reason to care about the people I’m fighting alongside. Military conscription is mandatory across Korea, and as a result, MW4's characters are far more relatable and genuine, adding a sense of personal investment in their survival that I hadn't experienced before.

Then, once I'd shot all the bad dudes who needed shooting and overthrown the biggest bad dude, Modern Warfare 4's latest iteration of DMZ was there to keep me locked in. It's clever, really. With the campaign being my favourite part of the package, DMZ taps into that by carrying the narrative forward through its PvPvE extraction mode.

I've never considered myself a big fan of the looting and extraction gameplay loop, with Marathon being my main exception. Sure, it's thrilling when you get it right, but it's even more frustrating when you get it wrong. I get what I'm talking about is predominantly a skill issue, but for a casual 'coddy' like me, I think Activision may have got it right.

Deploying into the DMZ with a friend and working through its various story missions has been a lot of fun. One of the earlier missions particularly tickled me, as I was tasked with extracting a bedbound patient from a hospital.

I don't know about you, but I found it quite silly - in the best possible way - to duck enemy fire behind a hospital gurney occupied by a sedated chap. Wheeling him through the facility, up elevators, and away in a chopper was a lot of fun. Even if realism should probably have demanded that he extract in the form of Swiss cheese.

There's a lot to DMZ that I'd wager I haven't fully experienced yet, but I reckon I've sampled enough of its ingredients to appreciate what makes the extraction mode such a tasty side dish to the campaign. The skill trees feel a little forced, but upgrading my chosen operators is moreish, and taking out baddies to warrant a death squad coming after you is thrilling.

So far, I haven't encountered any immediate PvP deaths that have made me want to curl up and never deploy into the DMZ again. Overall, it's decent, but there’s one thing I honestly wish wasn't in the game (campaign included): the developer's decision to repeatedly put dogs in the firing line.

John Wick was years ago. If the intention is to motivate players by shooting dogs, consider this my objection. I don’t think it’s a necessary storytelling device, and I’d happily see it left out in the future. Just let sleeping dogs lie.

Another minor issue I have with Modern Warfare 4 falls under its multiplayer offering. Specifically, skill-based matchmaking and some inconsistencies with hit registration.

I actually really like the gunplay and movement in MW4 (my favourite being MW2), but thank f*** omnimovement is gone. Everyone seems to move more like actual humans and less like over-caffeinated gibbons. The gameplay itself, across all modes, is a lot of fun and feels great. It's just that my competition always seems to be that little bit better than me.

Listen, I'm not claiming to be anything special when it comes to Call of Duty PvP, but I'm not crap either. I just don't have time to master movement, equipment and loadouts. Going in raw, I'd still say I'm pretty handy at pointing and shooting, which usually leads to me being placed against decent opposition. My problem with COD multiplayer, and I've felt this way for years, is that you either lean forward, join the sweats and have a less enjoyable time, or they destroy you, which isn't much fun either. Modern Warfare 4 feels like it hasn't fully figured out that balance either.

Don't get me wrong, competition is great, and I do love getting spicy from time to time. But when you're 34 and hopping on for one or two games after suffering through a long day of AI fearmongering, can't a guy just sit down and have fun?

It's made worse by the fact that, even when I do slap up, I still lose fights because of inconsistent hit registration. In MW4, I've landed five registered shots from my 25th Anniversary Xbox M4 rifle square into an enemy's face, only to get folded by a single shot to the gut. It hurts.

The bones are there with multiplayer, but the system needs some work.

As an overall package, Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 is generally solid. It looks and plays great on Xbox Series X, delivering its cinematic campaign and a thoughtful iteration of DMZ, all complemented by the fun-junky action of multiplayer.

This is the first COD game I've really cared about since World at War, and I can actually see myself playing it for quite a while.

Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 is a certified banger and thoroughly deserving of all the praise heaped upon it. This is the first COD game I've genuinely cared about in over a decade, and it’s one of 2026's best new titles.

Its cinematic action, star-studded cast, and tense depiction of Korea at war deliver an unforgettable batch of campaign missions. Once it's all over, DMZ impresses with a surprising level of depth and plenty of opportunity for co-op shenanigans. Multiplayer is exactly what you'd expect: moreish fun for hours on end, but suffers the familiar curse of SBMM.

If only the devs could ditch the frequent and forced killing of in-game dogs, then I'd easily give MW4 a higher score.
