Funny ha-ha or funny peculiar? Yesterday had plenty of both

Here’s a question: did last night’s Newcastle United 2 Arsenal 2 match at St James’ Park show we can be the equal of the reigning Premier League champions?

At 16 minutes past six, after a miserably one-sided first half, you would have been embarking on mission impossible to find anyone saying “yes” among the 52,000+plus in the stadium or the millions watching on TV.

By the final whistle, blown nearly 10 minutes after the 90 by Chris Kavanagh, few would have replied “no”. Such is football.

Let’s start at the beginning, or at least on the 12.30 Azuma train from King’s Cross to Newcastle. The LNER service was predictably packed with Gooners. Passengers in Carriage J were overwhelming confident of a one-nil or two-nil to the Arsenal, as they like to crow in north London.

Based on their team’s results this season, that was to be expected. They don’t score many and, apart from a blip near Brighton, they concede even fewer.

Arsenal are, in a word, efficient. I find them boring. The descriptions are not mutually exclusive. No way as the train rumbled into the magnificent Central Station about 32 minutes late was I looking forward to a feast of entertaining football.

In dire need of a drink, I walked up Pink Lane with Big Nige, who had boarded the delayed train at York after it made an unscheduled stop south of the old Viking stronghold of Jorvik. Known as Eboracum in Roman times, btw.

Pink Lane was awash with happy Londoners. There are many, many reasons to be proud of Newcastle; one is the warm welcome the natives offer strangers (excluding Mackems, obviously. There have to be limits).

Which was why the mild hostility that greeted me in the Mosaic Tap was a big surprise. A young chap called Rob walked up to our table, where Paul and Gowa had already got in the drinks, and said: “You can’t wear that scarf in here.”

His mate had apparently clocked me as a Gooner, having mistaken my half-and-half for an Arsenal favour.

A little bit of background. Said scarf was a souvenir from the Bayer Leverkusen v NUFC match. Not that I bought it on my visit to the BayArena shop a mere 11 months ago, when it would have cost £20. This one was acquired in October for a tenner in the NUFC pre-Christmas online sale, while present-buying for my daughter Jo and her family. The PIF need all the help they can get . . .

For all those who detest a half-and-half, that’s your prerogative. I view it as a reminder of a great trip, more useful than a match programme and less likely to be lost.

Rob quickly realised his error, probably because I had slipped into my best am dram persona by screaming: “Look at the scarf! Look at it! You need to go to Specsavers, mate!”

The faux outrage reduced poor Rob to a gibbering, hilariously apologetic wreck. “Sorry, sorry, I’m really sorry. It was my mate . . . What are you drinking, can I get you one, please?”

The free glass of malbec was much appreciated, thanks. Considering the prices at Mosaic, the scarf has probably paid for itself. Funny ha-ha, Rob.

We played the “who would you select” game and all four of us agreed Gonzalez was not justifying his place. Starting Steur was a no-brainer, the back five picked themselves, based on the Fulham game, with Livramento available if needed to give us a second-half lift. The midfield and attack, apart from Steur and Barnes, were as clear as a peasouper.

The big news at 4.15 was Bruno G starting for his new team. Who had expected that?

There was just time for a quick one in Bar Loco, where I bumped into the legendary Tony Mallabar, before safely negotiating Turnstile 31 at the Leazes End. My seat in row MM was in line with the edge of the six-yard box, which gave a perfect view of the fourth goal, unfortunately.

The Booing of Bruno had been a hot topic. My opinion is that every opposition player should be relentlessly jeered, booed, abused and barracked (isn’t SJP on Barrack Road for a reason?) That’s what I recall, perhaps falsely, from big games in the Seventies . . .

Anyway, the Brazilian midfielder was booed from the warm-up until his replacement by Lewis-Skelly when we were leading 2-1. The effort of singling out our former captain probably backfired, because it distracted the more vocal supporters from doing the job of cheering their favourites.

Not that the players or the play gave reasons to be buoyant in the first half. Arsenal did what Arsenal do, using their 11 players against our outfield 10. Whenever Osula or Willock or Murphy or Barnes or Steur attempted to put pressure on Raya, he found the spare man whom our would-be presser had just left unmarked. Boring and efficient by Arsenal. Half-cocked and aimless by the Mags.

In my opinion, Arsenal are no great shakes, whatever the title says. A great team have a top, top striker. They don’t. As they proved by managing only one effort on target before the interval. Hornicek didn’t have a prayer once Hall chested down a long ball and instantly lost possession near the edge of his box.

Our left-back is a wonderful footballer but, having rarely played as a defender in his youth at Chelsea, he is still learning some of the skills essential to be a watertight obstacle.

Murphy tried to catch Raya off his line with a speculative long-range effort and one other shot flew narrowly wide of Raya’s left post via a deflection off Barnes. There was almost nothing else of note in the first half as the champions biffed nearly every shot high, wide and ugly.

Burn and Thiaw stood reasonably firm, Dedic and Murphy seemed on different wavelengths, Steur harried Bruno G and Barnes showed some unlikely aggression. Miley plugged away and Osula ran gamely but United lacked any sort of perceptible gameplan. We rarely managed to find a player in space. Worrying times.

Where was our next goal coming from, wondered my mate Dave at the interval. It had been a while at SJP.

The answer came within four minutes of the restart, soon after the first of three stoppages caused by apparent injuries to Raya. More importantly, after Matthias Jaissle made a triple substitution.

Harvey Barnes smashed a shot low to the keeper’s right, ending a quick attack. Willock had pressured Odegaard into presenting the ball to Osula. An assist on the headless chicken’s credits, fitting reward for another tireless display.

This was when the match became funny peculiar. Miley man-marked Bruno, Arsenal misplaced passes, United won 50:50s, we found space to run with purpose. The gulf in monetary and player resources vanished as the Mags reverted to our best version of Howe-ball.

Only Hornicek of the summer 2026 signings was still on the pitch, Steur having been subbed.

Arsenal looked distinctly second-best. Raya went down again. A corner on our right from Hall was aimed beyond the far post, where the unstoppable Man from Blyth powered a header goalwards. In a frantic kerfuffle, a couple of blocks merely delayed the inevitable. Thiaw smashed it home and we were ahead.

Around me in row MM, the earlier, almost incessant moans and grounds became voices of encouragement. Supporters who had failed to back their team in a dire first half were suddenly full of the joys of November . . .

Ramsey, Livramento and Elanga all caused Arsenal problems. For a few minutes, we battered them.

Then Raya collapsed for a third time. The long delay gave the champions a break they desperately needed.

Whatever anyone else says, Jaissle and his men were right to keep pressing for a third. Arsenal were always going to fashion at least one good chance in the time remaining after we went ahead. To sit back with 28 minutes left, plus a lot of stoppage time, would have been professional suicide.

My suspicion is that those who now bemoan our aggressive play would have whinged even more if the team had sat back. The match became attack and counter-attack. Arsenal, through Madueke doing a decent impression of the absent Saka, kept cutting onto his left foot. Hall, sometimes having to mark two opponents, let the right winger swerve inside and slam a low shot through two defenders and past Hornicek.

The keeper got two hands to the ball but could only push it into the Leazes End net.

Havertz nearly produced what would have been a truly wonderful moment when his head diverted a late Hall corner beyond Kepa and against the crossbar. So near, yet so far.

Then Barnes capped what I reckon was his best game since Wembley 2025 by outstripping Rice on the left touchline and playing the ball perfectly to Ramsey on the edge of the 18-yard box.

In the words of John Ando Anderson, just hit the bloody thing, man. Ramsey did. Sadly, though, instead of putting his laces through it, he tried to bend the ball into the postage stamp and it flew high and wide.

What a feather in Jaissle’s cap a win would have been. One point was good, three would have been great.

The late introduction of Wissa, who replaced Willock, was a failure. He contributed almost nothing, which has been the story all too often since his expensive arrival.

I would have thrown on Fernandez-Pardo as the game opened up. No team enjoy speedy opponents running hard at them and the Belgium international could have been the joker in Jaissle’s pack.

On the balance of play a draw was reasonable but this, like the Liverpool result, felt like two points dropped.

What is inarguable is the team’s willingness to battle for their mates, their manager and the Toon. Long may that continue.
