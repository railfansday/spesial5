Sources: Man United's real reason for delaying Michael Carrick sack revealed

Man United's hierarchy are reluctant to sack Michael Carrick because of their recent track record of hiring and firing managers, sources have told Football Insider.

Carrick is under huge pressure to keep his job despite his team twice overturning two-goal deficits against Aston Villa on Sunday in a 4-4 Old Trafford thriller.

His side also showed resilience and fight to come back from behind to beat Roma in midweek, but he remains close to losing his job.

The Red Devils' board are reportedly weighing up relieving him of his duties if they are well beaten by Villa.

As I revealed on the latest edition of Football Insider’s Transfer Insider podcast, United chiefs are keen to give Carrick more chances as they want to end their recent cycle of dismissing bosses following a poor run of form.

Fans are yet to turn on the manager, given his decorated history as a United player, but another big defeat may leave them with no choice but to wield the axe.

Erik ten Hag and Ruben Amorim have each been relieved of their duties and given huge payouts since INEOS took control of United's footballing decisions.

Carrick would not need to be paid as much, but the Red Devils chiefs are keen to offer him more time to turn things around due to how quickly United have turned managers over in recent years.

With that said, the nature of their defeats this season have left a sour taste in the mouth and Carrick could be sacked if Villa run riot at Old Trafford.

Speaking on the Transfer Insider podcast, I explained: "The thing with the United hierarchy is that they’ve chopped and changed so many managers in the past and it’s not really worked out either, so they may be a bit reticent to get rid of Carrick just yet, and obviously there were some signs of recovery in that win against Roma.

"I'm sure they will be waiting to see what type of performance they get from United against Villa, who are obviously a very well-run club under Unai Emery. I think it’s game by game at Man United right now, and we’ll see what happens at the weekend."

Carrick has shown that he has what it takes to manage United, as he was given the permanent job following some outstanding form in the second half of last season.

Former boss and player, Solskjaer, was linked with another return before Carrick was appointed in January, and he has backed the 45-year-old to turn things around in the coming weeks and months.

Speaking to Norwegian outlet TV2, he said: "When I spoke to United, I also boasted about Michael. 'If you choose him, you will get a good man,' I said. I think he did the same for me.

"Michael has what it takes to take that club forward. I think he's the right man. He understands the club, understands the fans and knows what's needed."
