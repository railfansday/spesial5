Borrow audiobooks free through your library’s apps before you pay for a plan, and place holds early on the books you want next. For a book with a wait you won’t sit through, pay for the cheapest plan that fits how you listen, and pause or cancel that plan in months you don’t need it.

Audible’s Premium plan costs $14.95 a month, or $179.40 a year, for one credit a month, each good for an audiobook you keep. If your library offers them, Libby and Hoopla lend audiobooks free to anyone with its card.

Hoopla’s Instant titles, marked with a lightning bolt, are always available with no wait list. A Libby title can have one, so place a hold as soon as you hear about a book you want. Suspend any hold you aren’t ready for, and you keep moving up the list without the book arriving until you unsuspend it.

Check what else your library card covers before you renew any other subscription.

Libro.fm charges $14.99 a month for one credit, also good for an audiobook you keep, and splits its profits with an independent bookstore you choose. LibriVox has free recordings of classics old enough to be out of copyright, read by volunteers.

On a credit plan, buy extra titles in member-only sales, and pause it in months you won’t listen. Audible lets you pause a monthly plan once every 12 months for up to 90 days, while Libro.fm lets you put your membership on hold anytime and keep your unused credits. A commuter who borrows most books but wants three new releases a year with long hold lists can keep a Libro.fm membership on hold except in the three months those books come out. At $14.99 a month, that’s $44.97 a year for three credits instead of $179.88 for 12.

Before you spend a credit, search Hoopla for the book, since an Instant copy there takes one of your free monthly borrows instead.
