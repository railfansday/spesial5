Dundee manager Steven Pressley wanted to 'crawl under a rock and hide' after humiliation by Hearts

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Published: 16:26 EST, 23 November 2026 | Updated: 16:50 EST, 23 November 2026

Dundee manager Steven Pressley is nothing if not honest. Having taken full responsibility for his team’s 8-0 thrashing by Hearts on Saturday, he now admits that the experience left him wanting to ‘crawl under a rock and hide’.

You won’t hear him writing it off as a ‘bad day at the office’ or branding it a freak result from which he and his players would quickly move on. Dundee’s heaviest-ever home defeat in the league was an indignity that really hit their popular manager. And hit him hard.

‘It’s been a difficult 24-48 hours,’ said Pressley. ‘There’s been a lot of hurt, a lot of embarrassment. As you can imagine, at times I’ve wanted to crawl under a rock and hide.

‘It’s difficult on the back of such a disappointing result. But I will say this: I suffer a lot from vulnerability at times, but for some reason during difficult moments I seem to find strength from somewhere.

‘I don’t know if it’s from my family, from messages that I’ve received, from my reading. But I’ve had to show strength. I have to support the players. I have to make sure we navigate our way out of what was a really embarrassing moment.

‘We had a good meeting yesterday. We chatted a lot about the various aspects, and hopefully we can start responding to the disappointment.’

That process starts tomorrow night against Hibs, who suffered a humiliation of their own when they lost 4-0 at home to Dundee United on Saturday. Pressley says the immediate objective for his team is to go there with a better attitude than they have shown lately.

He had feared a setback coming for Dundee – ‘not 8-0 but a disappointing performance’ – because he had ‘sensed a little bit of subconscious complacency’. He felt his team had been over-hyped by outsiders on the back of a start to the season in which they won five of their 12 matches before the international break.

‘We’re nowhere near the levels that people keep telling us we are,’ he said. ‘We’re not a good enough team not to do the hard yards. Our whole strength has been built on the cohesion of the team, the unity of the team, the aggressiveness of the team.

‘And I think what Saturday did highlight is exactly what I’ve been saying. If we get lazy on the hard yards, we’re a very vulnerable team. We have to be at our absolute best to get results.’

He hopes the Hearts game will be ‘a reset moment’ that eventually helps his players to improve, but there are no guarantees from Pressley that they will shake it off at Easter Road tomorrow.

Asked if he expected a positive response from them, he said: ‘I don’t know. I know that they’re hurting. I know that they want to respond in the best possible way. That part I don’t question. I know how much it’s affected them.

‘But we’ve not had a lot of time. I just hope we can bring some pride back to the football club with the right type of performance.’

The good news for Dundee is that they are up against a team who have been hopeless in their own stadium recently. Second-bottom Hibs have lost nine of their last ten Premiership games at Easter Road and haven’t taken three points there since early April.

Supporters turned on the Edinburgh side after the defeat by Dundee United, but Hibs midfielder Jamie McGrath has dismissed the idea that he and his team-mates don’t enjoy playing in their own ground.

Jamie McGrath was in the Hibs side that suffered another defeat at Easter Road on Saturday

‘Personally, I always like playing at home,’ said McGrath. ‘I love the pitch, I love the stadium. It’s a big open pitch, which I think should suit us. But for whatever reason, so far this season we haven’t been able to capitalise on playing at home.

‘It’s hard to put your finger on one certain thing. Maybe just being more decisive in both boxes or probably conceding goals at crucial times in the game, like after five minutes on Saturday, we just give ourselves a bit of a mountain to climb.’

It looked like Marink Reedijk’s side had turned the corner when they followed up a rousing victory over city rivals Hearts in the Premier Sports Cup semi-final with a 4-0 win at Aberdeen in their last game before the international break.

So McGrath is hoping Saturday’s aberration was a blip. ‘We’ve been on a decent run the last month and probably the international break came at a tricky time for us. We were just kind of building momentum and had two good wins back-to-back against two good teams. It’s frustrating not to build on that.’

McGrath is unfazed by Hibs’ lowly league position and senses a chance to change that with three matches in the space of nine days against other sides in the bottom six.

‘It’s not something that we’re concerned about at the minute. It’s obviously not where we want to be. We want to try to get closer to that top six and this two-week period is quite crucial. If you put a little run together, you can boost yourself right up the league.’
