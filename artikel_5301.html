The ETF industry is entering a new phase of competition. Once dominated by low-cost index funds, the market is expanding into active management, cryptocurrency exposure, derivatives-based strategies and specialized investment themes. The shift is changing how asset managers develop products and compete for investor capital. Global ETF assets reached $22.83 trillion in September 2026, while U.S.-listed funds attracted another $204.2 billion in October. Yet the more important change is happening in capital allocation: investors are increasingly using ETFs to access strategies that extend well beyond traditional market indexes.

This report examines the key trends reshaping the ETF market, the rise of new investment products, and how these developments could redefine asset management.

The global ETF market added more than $4 trillion in assets during the first nine months of 2026, supported by investor inflows and gains across financial markets. According to data, global ETF assets increased from $18.69 trillion at the end of 2025 to $22.83 trillion by September 30, 2026. Net inflows contributed $2.25 trillion, while market appreciation added another $1.89 trillion.

More significantly, nine-month inflows had already exceeded the previous full-year record of $2.09 trillion set in 2025. Equity ETFs remained the industry’s largest segment, with $17.74 trillion in assets, followed by bond ETFs at $3.57 trillion. Alternative ETFs held $772.1 billion, while commodity ETFs accounted for $471.7 billion.

Active management is emerging as one of the ETF industry’s fastest-growing segments. The appeal is straightforward: investors can access portfolio managers’ security selection and allocation decisions through an exchange-traded structure. The latest inflow figures suggest that demand is expanding faster than the segment’s existing asset share.

Active ETFs attracted $675.2 billion in net inflows during the first nine months of 2026, compared with $1.58 trillion for passive products. That gave active funds nearly 30% of total ETF inflows, despite representing just 12.81% of industry assets.

The gap between asset share and inflow share is significant. Active ETFs are attracting a larger proportion of new money than their current market presence would suggest. Product launches reinforce that shift. Of the 89 U.S. ETFs launched in October, 65% were actively managed, according to FactSet. Equity strategies accounted for approximately half of new launches.

Cryptocurrency ETFs have created a new distribution channel between digital assets and traditional financial markets. Spot Bitcoin and Ethereum products allow investors to gain exposure through conventional brokerage accounts rather than directly managing wallets, private keys or exchange accounts. This has made digital assets more accessible to investors who prefer established investment platforms and fund structures.

The development also brings traditional asset managers into direct competition for crypto allocations. Firms such as BlackRock and Fidelity can distribute digital asset products through established investment networks, while specialist providers compete through product design, fees and market positioning.

Crypto ETFs remain exposed to price volatility, custody arrangements, liquidity conditions and regulatory uncertainty. The wider significance is that digital assets are becoming part of the ETF product landscape rather than remaining confined to dedicated cryptocurrency exchanges.

Options-based ETFs are expanding the range of outcomes investors can pursue through exchange-traded products. Covered-call funds, buffer ETFs and defined-outcome strategies are increasingly being used to target income, manage downside exposure or modify participation in market gains.

The growth of ETF-linked derivatives trading provides a measure of the activity surrounding these products. Cboe reported that ETF options averaged 28.3 million contracts per day in the third quarter of 2026, up 1% from the previous quarter. Single-stock options averaged 32.7 million contracts, while index options recorded 6.1 million.

The activity highlights ETFs’ growing role in the derivatives market. It also provides an important foundation for products built around option premiums and defined investment outcomes.

Asset managers are increasingly using thematic ETFs to package exposure to industries expected to benefit from long-term economic and technological changes.

Artificial intelligence, semiconductor manufacturing, cybersecurity, defense, nuclear energy and critical minerals are among the themes attracting investment interest. These products allow investors to access groups of companies linked to a particular industry without selecting individual stocks.

The scale of the broader U.S. ETF market illustrates the opportunity. U.S. ETF assets increased from $12.63 trillion in September 2025 to $16.15 trillion in September 2026, while the number of funds expanded from 4,181 to 5,368, according to the Investment Company Institute.

The ETF expansion is not being driven exclusively by new investment products. Traditional asset managers are also reconsidering how existing strategies are packaged and distributed.

Mutual fund-to-ETF conversions offer a route to bring established portfolios into an exchange-traded structure. For eligible U.S. funds, the structure can provide potential tax advantages, intraday trading and access to brokerage distribution networks. The shift is particularly relevant for active managers. Instead of building a new strategy from the ground up, firms can consider offering existing investment approaches through ETFs.

October’s launch activity illustrates the direction of product development: 65% of the 89 new U.S. ETFs launched that month were actively managed. FactSet also reported that equity strategies represented approximately half of those launches.

The growing complexity of ETF strategies is bringing regulatory oversight into sharper focus. On June 30, 2026, the Securities and Exchange Commission issued a request for public comment on ETFs seeking exposure to innovative asset classes or using novel investment strategies. The consultation examined how regulators could accommodate product innovation while maintaining investor protection and orderly markets.

The SEC raised questions around the regulatory status of certain novel ETFs, their oversight and the registration process. The public comment deadline was August 31, 2026.

The SEC has not finalized a new rule through this request. However, its review could influence how future products are structured and evaluated. For asset managers, regulatory clarity will be an important factor in determining how quickly new investment strategies can reach the market.

The ETF industry is moving beyond its traditional role as a low-cost vehicle for index investing. Active products are attracting a larger share of new capital, crypto funds are extending access to digital assets, and options-based strategies are giving investors more targeted ways to manage portfolios.

Yet traditional equity and bond funds continue to dominate industry assets. The next phase of growth will depend on whether specialized products can maintain investor demand while delivering clear investment value.
