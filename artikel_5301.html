Tomorrow’s US Consumer Price Index report for October 2026, due at 8:30 a.m. ET on November 10, arrives at a delicate moment for risk assets. Mark Zuckerberg’s Meta AI predicts what the upcoming CPI data drop could look like and how the market may react.

After September’s hotter-than-expected print (headline +0.6% month-over-month and 3.7% year-over-year), markets are braced for another reading that will shape December Federal Reserve rate expectations.

Consensus currently clusters around a headline rise of +0.2% to +0.3% MoM and a core increase of roughly +0.2% MoM, with the annual headline rate expected near 3.7%.

My base-case prediction is a mild downside surprise relative to the more hawkish end of expectations: headline CPI at +0.2% month-over-month and core CPI also at +0.2%. Energy prices appear to have stabilized after September’s sharp gasoline spike, while shelter and services inflation show early signs of moderation.

If those assumptions hold, the report would mark a clear deceleration from the prior month without signaling a sudden collapse in price pressures. Year-over-year figures, if published, would likely remain sticky near 3.6–3.7% for the headline and around 2.5% for core.

Such a print would not eliminate the possibility of further Fed tightening, but it would reduce the urgency for an aggressive December hike and ease upward pressure on Treasury yields and the U.S. dollar.

Right now, analysts and prediction markets price the odds of a December rate hike at 75% to 80%, but if the Meta AI CPI data prediction plays out, that number could drop sharply.

In the current environment, where Bitcoin has been trading near multi-week lows around the mid-$70,000s, and Ethereum has hovered near $2,100–$2,150 amid ETF outflows, a softer inflation reading would be interpreted as modestly supportive for liquidity-sensitive assets.

A cooler-than-feared CPI has historically triggered short-covering and a relief rally in crypto. Bitcoin could stage an immediate 3–6% bounce, potentially reclaiming the $78,000–$80,000 zone as traders dial back the most aggressive rate-hike pricing.

Ethereum, as a higher-beta asset, might outperform on the upside, pushing back toward $2,300–$2,400 if risk appetite returns. Altcoins that have been particularly beaten down could see sharper percentage gains as capital rotates back into the sector.

The reaction would likely be sharpest in the first 30–60 minutes after the release, followed by a digestion period. Sustained upside would depend on whether yields continue to ease and whether equity markets also respond positively.

A “Goldilocks” outcome, soft enough to calm inflation fears but not so weak that it raises recession concerns, would be the most constructive scenario for digital assets. Of course, risks remain.

If core inflation prints at 0.3% or higher, or if energy and shelter components stay sticky, the opposite reaction is probable: a quick drop in Bitcoin toward the low $70,000s and further pressure on Ethereum.

Positioning is already fragile after recent outflows, so any hawkish surprise could amplify selling. Overall, the balance of probabilities points to a modest cooling that gives the crypto market temporary breathing room.

While one data point will not reverse the broader macro narrative, a softer CPI could provide the spark needed for a short-term relief rally and remind participants that the inflation path is not a one-way street higher.
