Could You Handle Losing $741 Million? Crypto Community Revisits 8,999 BTC Mystery as Market Stays Muted

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

With the crypto market remaining undecided and major assets led by Bitcoin (BTC) and Ethereum (ETH) recording only small price movements, the crypto community is revisiting stories of Bitcoin fortunes lost through irreversible mistakes.

Several X users raised these cases this week, including one of an early Bitcoin holder who reportedly lost access to thousands of coins now worth about $741 million.

Notably, while the coins remain visible on the blockchain, their owner, known as Stone Man, appears to have lost access to the wallet needed to recover them.

The incident dates back to August 10, 2010, when Stone Man described his predicament in a BitcoinTalk forum post titled “Lost large number of bitcoins.”

He explained that he had accumulated 9,000 BTC through an exchange and transferred the holdings to a Bitcoin client running on a Debian Linux live CD.

After backing up his wallet file to a flash drive, he attempted to send one Bitcoin to himself. However, he closed the Bitcoin program (client) before the transaction received confirmations, shut down the computer, and subsequently reloaded an older wallet backup.

His balance then showed only 1 BTC, alongside a transaction indicating that 8,900 BTC had been sent to an unfamiliar address.

“I do have the address that the 8,900 were supposedly sent to, but the old wallet file is gone for good,” he wrote.

The original BitcoinTalk post referred to 8,900 BTC, but the figure appears to have been a mistake. ZyCrypto confirmed that the associated Bitcoin address holds 8,999.00689948 BTC, according to blockchain records.

Another incident in May 2024 showed that not every major crypto loss ends the same way. An address-poisoning attack led to the theft of approximately $72 million in wrapped Bitcoin, prompting the victim to negotiate directly with the recipient.

“You’ve won, brother. You can keep 10% and return the 90%. We can act like nothing happened,” the victim wrote in an on-chain message.

The parties agreed that the recipient could retain 10% as a bounty in exchange for returning the remainder, and funds began moving back to the victim.

However, such recoveries depend on circumstances and, in some cases, the recipient’s cooperation. Blockchain transactions are generally difficult to reverse, while lost private keys can leave assets inaccessible indefinitely.

Moreover, the story echoes the case of James Howells, a British IT worker who said he accidentally discarded a hard drive containing the private keys to approximately 8,000 BTC in 2013. The drive reportedly ended up at a landfill in Newport, Wales.

As Bitcoin’s price climbed over the years, the lost coins’ potential value grew into the hundreds of millions of dollars. Howells pursued efforts to search the landfill, including proposals involving excavation and environmental safeguards, but Newport City Council opposed the plans. He abandoned the effort in 2025 after years of unsuccessful attempts to recover the hard drive.

Estimates from digital asset custody firm BitGo suggest that between 2.3 million and 4 million BTC may have been permanently lost, representing a significant share of Bitcoin’s 21 million maximum supply.

However, the exact figure remains uncertain because dormant wallets cannot automatically be classified as inaccessible, and some holders may still control their coins despite years of inactivity.
