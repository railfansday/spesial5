29 Years Later, This Banned Masterpiece Is Still the Greatest Mythology Movie Of All Time

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Big-budget mythology movies are always a huge box-office draw, but bringing ancient legends to the big screen is never as simple as it looks. Everyone has their own idea of how these gods, heroes, and sacred stories should be portrayed, and even the biggest filmmakers can struggle to get it right. It's a challenge that 2026's biggest projects, from Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey to Nitesh Tiwari's Ramayana: Rise of a Legend, are facing today. But one film learned just how costly that clash can be nearly three decades ago.

Ramayana: The Legend of Prince Rama first screened at the International Film Festival of India in 1993, but its journey to theaters was anything but smooth. Co-directed by Yugo Sako, Koichi Sasaki, and the Father of Indian Animation, Ram Mohan, the ¥800 million Indo-Japanese production brought together 450 artists to adapt Valmiki's Sanskrit epic.

Yet, objections from Hindu nationalist groups and Indian officials over portraying Hindu deities as animated characters kept the film out of Indian cinemas, with the controversy unfolding amid the tensions following the December 1992 religious dispute in the country.

Despite everything, the film survived through television broadcasts and eventually earned a 4K theatrical release in India in January 2025. Its hand-drawn animation, Vanraj Bhatia's unforgettable score, and voice cast featuring Amrish Puri, Arun Govil, Bryan Cranston, and James Earl Jones make it more than just a cult classic. It's the kind of film that makes the wait feel worth it.

Just like Nitesh Tiwari's newly released Ramayana: Rise of a Legend, which hit theaters on November 5, 2026, Ramayana: The Legend of Prince Rama brings the same ancient Hindu epic to life, only through beautifully hand-drawn anime.

Both films follow Prince Rama of Ayodhya, the seventh avatar of Lord Vishnu, born to King Dasharatha alongside his three brothers, Bharata, Lakshmana, and Shatrughna. After killing the demoness Tataka for Sage Vishwamitra, Rama travels to Mithila, where he breaks Lord Shiva's sacred bow, Pinaka, and wins the hand of Princess Sita, an incarnation of Goddess Lakshmi. His brothers also marry Sita's sisters.

But the celebrations don't last long. On the eve of Rama's coronation, his stepmother, Queen Kaikeyi, demands that King Dasharatha honor two old promises: crown her son Bharata and send Rama into exile for 14 years. Rather than let his father break his word, Rama accepts the punishment and leaves Ayodhya with Sita and Lakshmana.

Their forest exile takes a dark turn when an encounter with demoness Shurpanakha brings them into the crosshairs of her brother, Ravana, the ten-headed king of Lanka. Using the demon Maricha in the form of a golden deer to lure Rama and Lakshmana away, Ravana abducts Sita and kills the noble vulture Jatayu during his escape. While Tiwari's Ramayana ends with Sita's kidnapping ahead of its 2027 sequel, the 135-minute anime doesn't stop there. It follows Rama and Lakshmana as they join forces with Hanuman and Sugriva's Vanara army and march towards Lanka for the battle to bring Sita home.

Even nearly three decades later, the hand-drawn animation in Ramayana: The Legend of Prince Rama stands alongside the greatest anime films of the 1990s. For years, its brilliance remained buried under political red tape and complaints about portraying sacred deities as "cartoons." Thankfully, the film finally got its long-overdue redemption on January 24, 2025, when Geek Pictures India brought a 4K remaster to Indian theaters for the first time. The release paired the original English audio with new Hindi, Tamil, and Telugu dubs overseen by Baahubali screenwriter V. Vijayendra Prasad.

While anime is growing in popularity around the world, there are still a handful of series that are infamous for their bans in certain countries.

The film also made a mark beyond India. In 2001, it received a localized US release titled The Prince of Light, with Breaking Bad star Bryan Cranston voicing Rama and James Earl Jones as the narrator. It even made the initial nine-film shortlist for the first Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.

Frustratingly, the 1993 classic still isn't available on any official streaming platform. After everything it took to reach theaters, it's a shame that fans are still left relying on YouTube uploads and the Internet Archive to watch Ramayana: The Legend of Prince Rama.
