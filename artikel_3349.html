Cliff Booth's Sequel Needs To Explain What Really Happened To His Wife

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth should resolve one of the darkest unanswered questions from Once Upon a Time in Hollywood. The arrival of any Quentin Tarantino movie is an event, but when said movie is a semi-sequel to one of his best works and it's also directed by David Fincher, that excitement needle goes all the way to 11.

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth picks up eight years on from Once Upon a Time in Hollywood's ending, with Brad Pitt's titular stuntman having become a fixer for movie studios. Outside of Pitt, Timothy Olyphant is the only other cast member returning for this Netflix-produced spin-off, though Leonardo DiCaprio was offered - and passed - on a cameo as Rick Dalton.

It appears this David Fincher movie will have few ties to the previous outing, but by definition, it should reveal more about Pitt's enigmatic Cliff. One dangling Once Upon a Time in Hollywood thread it should untangle involves what actually happened to Booth's late wife Billie (Rebecca Gayheart), who died under mysterious circumstances on Cliff's boat.

Tarantino shows a brief flashback to the moments leading up to Billie's death in Once Upon a Time in Hollywood. She and Cliff are on his boat, and he sits dejectedly drinking a beer and balancing a harpoon gun on his knee while a drunken Billie berates. The film cuts away at this point, revealing that the harpoon gun went off and killed her - but no one knows if Cliff intentionally shot her or if it was an accident.

This event understandably blackens Cliff's name in the stunt community, and he struggles to find work outside of being Rick Dalton's (Leonardo DiCaprio) stunt double. Tarantino intentionally left Cliff's role in his wife's death a mystery, and there is plenty of evidence for either reading. He is generally shown to be laid-back and easygoing - but there are multiple points where he can be especially brutal when angered.

Tarantino clearly wants viewers to like Cliff too, so showing him blasting his wife - even if she is depicted as a cartoonish nag - would have worked against that goal. Still, as Pitt's fixer becomes the lead in The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth, the spinoff should actually settle this mystery once and for all. Considering the 1970s-set film is about exploring Hollywood's seamy underbelly, it should pull back the curtain on Booth too.

Now, it should be noted that Tarantino has technically already settled whether Cliff killed his wife. His 2021 novelization of Once Upon a Time in Hollywood reveals that, yes, he ABSOLUTELY shot Billie, with the harpoon having essentially cut his wife in half. The novel at least reveals Cliff was instantly remorseful and literally held his dying wife together for hours, waiting for the Coast Guard to arrive.

The two even made peace with each other over their toxic marriage before she died. The novelization also made Booth more of a sociopathic figure, revealing that he not only killed many Japanese soldiers during the war, but that he had killed several others in anger in the years since. The Once Upon a Time in Hollywood book paints Cliff as a much darker character, but it should be underlined that the movie and the book are two different things - even if they come from the same writer.

Novelizations often expand upon or offer alternate readings of a given film, so there is nothing to say that the Cliff from the book is the same one Pitt is portraying. In short, The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth could have a totally different read on Pitt's former stuntman, and maybe even reveal that Billie's death was indeed an accident. Either way, it would be nice if Fincher's sequel gave this question a definitive answer.

Eight years after the events of Once Upon a Time... in Hollywood, former stuntman Cliff Booth works as a Hollywood fixer, navigating the complexities and challenges of the studio system.
