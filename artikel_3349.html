10 Unwatchable Movies That Were Pure Cash Grabs

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Essentially, a cash-grab film is a movie made primarily—or even solely—to make a quick profit by riding on an existing brand or trend, rather than telling a high-quality story that feels creative or necessary in the slightest. From nostalgia bait sequels to Oscar bait fare, there are many ways for a studio to make a cash grab and get money to (hopefully) make slightly better films in the future.

Alas, we do not live in a world with a perfect film industry, and that means that throughout history, the big screen has been subjected to many a cash-grab film so atrocious that it's absolutely unforgivable. From big-studio disasters like 2015's Fantastic Four to low-budget trash like Winnie-the-Pooh: Blood and Honey, these really are among the most shameless films ever made.

They weren't the first superhero lineup that Marvel Comics ever wrote, but because they were the first superhero team of the modern Marvel era, the Fantastic Four are often given the moniker of Marvel's First Family. Their 2005 and 2007 movie adaptations were poorly received, but they look like The Godfather and The Godfather Part II next to the next adaptation in the timeline: Josh Trank's Fantastic Four.

It's one of the worst superhero movies ever, a pre-merger Fox disaster that was made almost exclusively as a low-effort contractual obligation in order to retain the movie licensing rights to the characters. You can tell that absolutely no love or passion went into any aspect of the movie, which is probably why it's estimated to have lost the studio around $100 million dollars.

Directed by David Dinetz and Dylan Trussell, and written by Dinetz, Trussell, Logan Paul, and Jake Paul, Airplane Mode began shooting in 2016, but was shelved due to the controversy surrounding Logan Paul and his Aokigahara Forest video. It was eventually given a pathetic mini theatrical run and released in the summer of 2019 on iTunes.

At that point, can you really call a film whose distribution strategy was so clearly meant to bury it from the public eye a true cash grab? Arguments can be made in either direction, but one thing is certain: Airplane Mode is the absolute bottom of the barrel of influencer-era opportunistic content, full of painfully unfunny gags that make it feel like an overextended social media sketch.

Following the success of 1994's The Mask, plans for a sequel began immediately, but after Jim Carrey declined the role, plans fell through. It was in 2001 that the project was revived, which resulted in Son of the Mask. Calling it one of the worst sequels in film history would be an understatement, seeing as this is truly one of the most disastrous cash grabs of the 21st century so far.

It was almost entirely Carrey's manic physical comedy that made The Mask what it was, and with him gone, Son of the Mask has absolutely nothing of value to offer. Gone is the over-the-top, cartoonish style and tone of the original, giving way to a loud, juvenile, and disjointed CGI-fest with some of the most nightmarish visuals and writing imaginable.

Slender Man was created by Something Awful forum user Eric Knudsen in 2009, soon becoming a viral creepypasta Internet meme. In 2014, a series of violent acts connected to fans of the character sparked a moral panic. A whopping four years after that, well after the character had already slipped out of the mainstream, Sony decided to make a horror movie adaptation directed by Sylvain White, Slender Man.

It's one of the worst psychological thrillers of the last 25 years, a late-to-the-party viral trend cash-in whose success was made impossible not only by the fact that its subject matter was no longer popular, nor just by the sensitivity of the real-world tragedies connected to the character, but by the fact that the film sucks. It looks cheap, it's not scary, and it was so poorly edited in an effort to secure a PG-13 rating that its story doesn't even make sense.

Sometimes, if you want things done right, you have to do them yourself. Clive Barker understood that, so he adapted his own 1986 novella The Hellbound Heart into 1987's Hellraiser, one of the most perfect horror movies ever made. One of horror's most iconic film franchises was thus born, and unfortunately, that resulted in one of the worst horror movies of the 21st century, Hellraiser: Revelations.

It doesn't really get much more cash-grabby than a microbudget flick rushed into existence over the course of mere weeks solely so Dimension Films wouldn't lose the rights to the franchise. Since the idea of making a good Hellraiser movie wasn't even in the studio heads' minds when Revelations went into production, the result is nothing more than a low-effort, shoestring-budget disaster that even Clive Barker disowned.

Throughout the 2010s, Tom Hooper was on a bit of a roll, achieving mainstream award success and prestige with films like The King's Speech and Les Misérables. A film adaptation of Andrew Lloyd Webber's cult classic stage musical Cats had been in the works since the '90s, but it wasn't until Hooper came around that the project finally materialized. Boy, does the entire world wish it hadn't.

Calling Cats one of the worst movie musicals of all time is letting off the hook far too easily. It's Oscar bait at its most shameless (and least successful), one of the biggest wastes of a big budget and A-list stars that Hollywood has produced in decades. It's visually ugly, tonally uneven, and a testament to studio hubris gone atrociously wrong. Hooper's career was derailed and hasn't really gotten back on track since.

DreamWorks Animation has been producing some of the greatest animated films in Hollywood since the '90s, and even though Megamind was originally only a modest success, it started growing a passionate cult following over the years. Knowing that, Peacock wanted to capitalize on the film's newfound popularity and make a TV show sequel, with the television film Megamind vs. the Doom Syndicate serving as a backdoor pilot.

Though it didn't release theatrically, thankfully meaning that no big screen was ever tainted with this affront to humanity, Megamind vs. the Doom Syndicate is nevertheless one of the worst animated movies of all time. With none of the charm and none of the humor that allowed the original to become a modern cult classic, it's a poorly-written disaster that looks ugly, sounds cheap, and offers nothing of value for fans.

After A. A. Milne's 1926 classic children's book Winnie-the-Pooh entered the public domain in 2022, a slasher adaptation reimagining the book's characters as horror villains became fair game. It's not necessarily a broken premise on paper, but Winnie-the-Pooh: Blood and Honey is such a lazy execution of the concept that it's genuinely one of the worst slasher movies of all time.

Focused on absolutely nothing more than being edgy by putting a dark twist on a beloved childhood icon, Blood and Honey seems like nothing but a low-effort shock-value stunt. It's a concept that seems ripe for building a cult following, but the film is so dull, boring, and poorly made that it's not even entertainingly exploitative enough to become a so-bad-it's-good cult classic.

In 2007, Paramount and Nickelodeon announced that they had signed M. Night Shyamalan to write, direct, and produce a trilogy of live-action films based on Avatar: The Last Airbender. If any one thing is true about Shyamalan, however, it's that he can be quite hit or miss; but never before had he missed in a fashion so spectacular as the one seen with The Last Airbender.

Four worlds and four completely different ideas of what a good life looks like. This is where you belong, what it would ask of you, and what you would get back in return.

You belong somewhere old enough that the mountains have names and somebody can still tell you who gave them. Middle-earth is for people who think the right thing is worth doing even when it will not be finished in their lifetime.

You want the sea, a crew, and absolutely nobody telling you where you are allowed to go. The Grand Line is the world for people whose main ambition in life is finding out what is over there.

You would rather be feared and useful than liked and useless. The Continent is a world where the monsters are the straightforward part of the job and the people are where it all gets complicated.

You want the myths to be real and loose in the actual world, and you want to find out at about thirteen that you were never anywhere near as ordinary as everybody kept telling you.

This is yet another instance of the destructive power of studio hubris. Paramount and Nickelodeon likely thought that, with a built-in fanbase already in the bag, there was no way Last Airbender would fail. Alas, they hired a team who clearly didn't understand the project in the slightest, resulting in a wooden, poorly-paced, creatively bankrupt cash grab that's among the worst action movies in film history.

When it comes to built-in fanbases, how much more of an assured public can you get than by adapting Akira Toriyama's Dragon Ball manga series and its anime adaptations, some of the most universally beloved Japanese art of modern times? Unfortunately, on top of being an atrocious adaptation of the source material, James Wong's Dragonball Evolution is also genuinely one of the worst sci-fi action movies of all time.

Absolutely everything about Dragonball Evolution makes it look like a lazy, misguided, generic Hollywood action flick. With next to zero creative respect for Akira Toriyama and pretty much nothing of the world-building or character work that made the source material work so well in the first place, Dragonball Evolution is left with nothing to do other than falling flat on its face in every single department imaginable.
