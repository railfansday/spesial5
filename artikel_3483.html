5 Most-Watched Movies To Stream This Weekend (Nov 13-15)

An animated nightmare with button-eyed parents, a Roman gladiator fighting for survival, and a Polynesian princess answering the ocean's call have something unexpected in common: Americans are watching it all these days. An animated nightmare with button-eyed parents and a Roman gladiator — they’re watching them both in enormous numbers. Throw in a devastating hurricane and a kidnapping thriller, and the latest streaming rankings cover just about every mood imaginable.

Choosing what to watch this weekend has become a surprisingly interesting problem. According to Nielsen's latest U.S. streaming movie rankings, covering October 19–25, 2026, these five movies collectively generated more than 1.5 billion minutes of viewing. The chart measures total viewing time on television screens in the United States, with familiar favorites competing against recent releases. Here are the five most-watched movies, their streaming homes, and the reasons they are attracting so much attention.

Eleven-year-old Coraline Jones (Dakota Fanning) discovers a mysterious doorway inside her family's new home, leading to an alternate world where everything initially seems better. Her parents are more attentive, the food is delicious, and her neighbors are considerably more entertaining. Unfortunately, the Other Mother (Teri Hatcher) has black buttons for eyes and increasingly disturbing expectations. Coraline turns a child's frustration with ordinary family life into an extraordinarily imaginative nightmare.

Henry Selick's stop-motion animation remains astonishing, particularly the contrast between Coraline's gloomy home and the elaborate wonders awaiting beyond the little door. But the Other Mother's affection becomes genuinely frightening once its conditions emerge. The film is adapted from Neil Gaiman's novel and possesses the rare ability to delight children while making adults profoundly uncomfortable. Its 198 million viewing minutes also demonstrate the remarkable staying power of a classic fantasy movie released more than seventeen years ago.

A dream promotion turns into a nightmare when Wirpul (Benny Emmanuel) takes command of a yacht off the coast of Acapulco, Mexico. His estranged half-brother Candi (Kristyan Ferrer) joins him for the job, despite their strained relationship. Then Hurricane Otis intensifies with terrifying speed, trapping the brothers aboard a vessel facing a Category 5 storm. Surviving Hurricane Otis dramatizes events surrounding the devastating 2023 hurricane.

The movie is directed by Jorge Michel Grau and builds the tension around the brothers' difficult relationship and the impossible decisions forced upon them. Their wealthy employer's yacht represents an opportunity for financial security, yet protecting it becomes increasingly dangerous as the storm approaches. The contrast between economic necessity and personal survival gives the disaster considerable weight. It was just released on Netflix on October 23, but the Mexican drama attracted 203 million viewing minutes during its opening reporting window, securing fourth place.

A politician's carefully cultivated image becomes dangerously fragile when his family is targeted by kidnappers. Animals stars Ben Affleck as Mark Kimball, a Los Angeles mayoral candidate whose young son is abducted, leaving him and his wife Abigail (Kerry Washington) scrambling to gather the ransom. Their desperate efforts expose troubling questions about wealth, ambition, and the lengths people will go to protect their families. The crisis also threatens to destroy everything Mark has worked to achieve politically.

Affleck directs and co-writes this dark thriller movie, surrounding its central couple with a cast that includes Gillian Anderson and Steven Yeun. The kidnapping creates an uncomfortable collision between public responsibility and private desperation, with Mark's political ambitions complicating decisions that could determine his son's fate. Animals was released on Netflix on October 9, and it has attracted attention despite a divided critical response. Its 292 million viewing minutes were enough to place it third on Nielsen's latest movie chart.

More than two decades after Ridley Scott brought ancient Rome to spectacular life, Gladiator II returns audiences to the Colosseum for another story of revenge and political betrayal. Lucius (Paul Mescal) has built a life far from the imperial capital, but a Roman invasion destroys his peaceful existence and forces him into slavery. As he becomes a gladiator, he attracts the attention of the calculating Macrinus (Denzel Washington), whose ambitions threaten to reshape Rome's future.

Washington's theatrical performance gives the film a particularly entertaining antagonist, while Scott delivers extravagant arena battles, including a confrontation involving a flooded Colosseum and sharks. General Acacius (Pedro Pascal) and Lucilla (Connie Nielsen) add competing loyalties to a story already overflowing with political intrigue. The sequel's return to streaming prominence demonstrates the continued appetite for historical spectacle, with 299 million minutes watched across its listed streaming platforms. It narrowly finished ahead of Animals to claim second place.

Disney's live-action Moana is currently enjoying the top of Nielsen's streaming movie rankings. Moana (Catherine Lagaʻaia), in this live-action remake, is played by Catherine Lagaʻaia. She is the adventurous daughter of Chief Tui, a young woman who feels an irresistible connection to the ocean despite her father's insistence that she remain on the island of Motunui. When darkness threatens her community, Moana ventures beyond the reef and seeks the help of Maui (Dwayne Johnson), a powerful demigod whose legendary accomplishments are matched by his enormous ego.

The movie is directed by Thomas Kail; the 2026 adaptation revisits the beloved 2016 animated adventure with live-action performances, sweeping island landscapes, and familiar songs. Johnson reprises his role as Maui, while Lagaʻaia brings a new interpretation of the determined heroine to the screen. The story's celebration of courage, family, and cultural identity continues to offer considerable appeal to audiences seeking an accessible family adventure. With 579 million minutes watched, Moana comfortably outperforms every other movie on the chart, generating nearly twice the viewing time of second-place Gladiator II.
