Netflix's 4-Part Sci-Fi Series Is So Good, Even Its Weakest Episode Is A Masterpiece

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Netflix's four-season science fiction TV series is great from start to finish, and even its weakest episode is miles better than the sci-fi shows you'll find on other streamers. Netflix's sci-fi offerings are not quite at the level of a streaming app like Apple TV, with its huge library of great shows in the genre, but it still has some winners.

Netflix has taken shots at a variety of genres and storytelling styles, sometimes hitting it out of the park but missing badly on other occasions. It has managed to carve out one niche, though, and that's with anthologies. Black Mirror, Guillermo del Toro's Cabinet of Curiosities, and Beef are all celebrated anthologies.

One of its best is also one of the streamer's best science fiction shows, Love, Death & Robots. The four-season, 45-episode anthology series consists of 6–21 minute shorts, all animated and written by different creative teams, with a handful of groups double-dipping over the seasons.

The only thing uniting the stories, save for the occasional multi-parter, is that they each deal with one of the themes of the title, and often deal with more than one. Love, Death & Robots premiered in 2019, and has had such illustrious directors as David Fincher craft their own episodes.

With 45 episodes, you would think that more than a handful wouldn't make the grade, but Love, Death & Robots is one of the rare shows where every episode is worth watching. There are certainly some that are worse than others, but even the least of them stands up to the best other sci-fi shows can offer.

The worst episode of Love, Death & Robots is probably not objective, considering how unique each episode is, and how varying animation styles, tones, and subject matter will appeal to everyone differently. However, a good case can be made for "Life Hutch" being the worst. Then again, another case can be made for it still being very watchable.

Season 2, episode 7, is "Life Hutch", an episode that uses photorealistic animation and stars Michael B. Jordan as Terence, a space pilot who gets stranded on a desolate planet and fights a murderous robot. Jordan could certainly have been used better, and the flashbacks take up too much time that a short episode simply doesn't have to spare.

However, there's still a lot going for it, including the animation that will have you wondering if everything is actually live-action with a few CGI touch-ups here and there. Jordan may be underused, but even a little Jordan is better than no Jordan. "Hutch" probably ranks low on most people's "best of" Love, Death & Robots lists, but that doesn't mean they're skipping it.

Love, Death & Robots makes every episode of the series watchable by giving each creative team total freedom to tell their story. If someone wants to make a classic sci-fi tale of mech-suits fighting alien invaders, they are free to. If they want to put on a Red Hot Chili Peppers concert with characters designed like marionettes, they can do that too.

There's a lot of freedom on display in Love, Death & Robots, and even the episodes that don't work as well still showcase creators putting exactly what they want on screen. You don't sense an invisible hand guiding the Love, Death & Robots episodes; it's a show for animators and writers as much as it is for the audience.
