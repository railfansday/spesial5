Ahsoka Season 2 Confirms Sabine’s New Look That Honors Her Most Important Rebels Arc

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Sabine Wren's new look in Ahsoka season 2 honors her most important Star Wars Rebels arc. After a long, nearly four-year wait, Ahsoka season 2 premieres on Disney+ in two months. This will mark a turning point for the New Republic era, as the recently returned Imperial mastermind, Grand Admiral Thrawn, declares outright war against what used to be known as the Rebel Alliance. Star Wars Rebels favorites Ezra Bridger, Hera Syndulla, and Garazeb Orrelios will have their work cut out for them.

Of course, in a show called Ahsoka, it can't all be about the Ghost Crew's continued fight against Thrawn and the Imperial Remnant. Ahsoka Tano and her new (again) apprentice, Sabine Wren, are stuck in Star Wars' game-changing secondary galaxy, having essentially traded places with Ezra and Thrawn. They'll uncover dangerous secrets on Peridea, linked not only to the Dathomirian Nightsisters but also to the Mortis gods, first introduced in Star Wars: The Clone Wars. What dark force is Baylan Skoll drawn to? What overwhelming power might be unleashed?

It's a good thing Sabine unlocked her full Force potential at the end of Ahsoka season 1, when she pushed Ezra onto the Chimera, helping him finally escape from exile. Now, Ahsoka can begin training Sabine in earnest. As Anakin Skywalker's Force ghost questions in the Ahsoka season 2 trailer, Ahsoka and Sabine will need to decide whether they consider themselves Jedi. Ahsoka left the Jedi Order during the Clone Wars. Sabine, meanwhile, is a proud Mandalorian warrior. Can she be both? Does she want to be? Sabine's confirmed new hairstyle suggests this will be the crux of her arc in Ahsoka season 2.

Since her Star Wars Rebels debut, Sabine's haircut and color have highlighted her character, each style reflecting a different era of her journey. This tradition was carried on in Ahsoka season 1; the scene where she cuts her hair not only signified her commitment to finding Ezra and rejoining the wider world, but it also paralleled Kanan Jarrus' symbolic haircut in Star Wars Rebels season 4, when he decided to join the mission to save Hera from the Empire's clutches. That mission resulted in Kanan's heroic yet tragic death. Sabine's haircut in Ahsoka honored his legacy and showed how much he meant to her.

Following brief glimpses in the Ahsoka season 2 teaser trailer, ScreenRant's exclusive winter preview image (shown above) offers a closer look at Sabine's new hairstyle. Clearly, this dynamic duo has been stuck on Peridea for a while. Sabine's season 1-era dark purple pixie cut is long gone, replaced by a grown-out bob. More important, however, is the coloring. The new growth is silvery white, while the ends are light purple, a remnant of her former style. This is the same color scheme she wore in Star Wars Rebels season 3.

That was a pivotal season for Sabine. After finding the abandoned Darksaber, the unusual lightsaber forged by the first (and only) Mandalorian Jedi, Tarre Vizsla, Kanan convinced Sabine not only to train with it but to use its symbolic power to reconcile with her family and free her people from the Empire's influence. Training forced Sabine to confront her guilt over her role in the Empire's oppression of Mandalore and reclaim her heritage, even when she felt she didn't deserve it. This can't be a coincidence.

Sabine's new hairstyle suggests that her Jedi journey will continue to parallel her experiences as a Mandalorian, specifically what she learned and overcame in Star Wars Rebels season 3. Mandalorian Jedi are exceptionally rare in Star Wars history, and Sabine is already an outlier. Her connection to the Force wasn't discovered until she was an adult — even Kanan and Ezra didn't sense it, at least not to any noticeable degree, during their time together in Rebels.

How will embracing the Force change her perspective? How does she feel about the Jedi ideology as a Mandalorian? Jedi are peacekeepers, while Mandalorians are warriors — they always have been. She fell out with her family and the rest of her people because she defied their self-destructive loyalty to the Empire. Sabine might struggle with that kind of ideological divide, especially after losing her family and her homeworld in the Great Purge of Mandalore. Ahsoka stopped training her the first time because she feared Sabine's grief and anger would make her dangerous.

Ahsoka season 2's first trailer was released at D23, so here are the biggest things Disney has already confirmed for the upcoming Star Wars show.

As Kanan pointed out in Star Wars Rebels season 3, episode 15, "Trials of the Darksaber," Sabine has a history of retreating into herself. "You're not fighting me. You're fighting yourself. And losing." To make this work, she has to learn to accept every part of herself, the same way she did when she connected with the Darksaber and reunited with her family. That takes time, effort, and emotional vulnerability.

Of course, during the New Republic era, the Jedi are all but extinct, and Sabine's Master isn't a typical Jedi, either. Perhaps it was always meant to happen this way. Perhaps Sabine was always meant to discover her connection to the Force later in life, so that she could chart her own course as a hero. Between Ahsoka, Kanan, and Ezra's influence, Sabine has all the support she needs to become something different when Ahsoka returns for season 2.

Ahsoka season 2 premieres Wednesday, January 20, exclusively on Disney+.
