DOJ Moves to Forfeit $1.1 Billion in USDC Stolen in Binance Hack

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Justice Department filed to forfeit more than $1.1 billion in USDC stolen from Binance by North Korean hackers, and Circle agreed to issue replacement tokens.

The Justice Department filed a civil forfeiture complaint Monday to take ownership of more than $1.1 billion in USDC that North Korean hackers stole from Binance last month, tokens that Circle froze but says it cannot move.

The complaint, filed in U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, targets all USDC held at 36 addresses across 18 blockchains. The department said the U.S. has now helped freeze, seize or return more than $2.9 billion in crypto from multiple sources since the Oct. 29 theft.

On Nov. 12, the department said it had court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion, out of a joint government and industry freeze of more than $2.7 billion. A temporary restraining order issued that day was served on Circle the same day.

Monday’s filing is the step that would let the government take title to the funds.

Circle blocked access to the USDC on or about Oct. 29, the complaint says. But the stablecoin issuer told prosecutors it does not have the technical ability to transfer the tokens out of the blocklisted addresses, which remain under the hackers’ control.

Instead, Circle has agreed to issue new USDC equal to the frozen amount and send it to the U.S. once a court enters a forfeiture order. The complaint calls those tokens “Substitute USDC.”

Several of the addresses are smart contract and hot wallet addresses on Binance’s own platform. The hackers took them over in the attack and drained some, but not all, of the funds before Circle stepped in, prosecutors wrote. “It appears Binance no longer controlled them,” the complaint says.

The filing also lays out how investigators say the breach began. A Binance employee contacted by a purported recruiter agreed to a job interview and was told to download software that contained malware, which matches what the exchange said earlier this month.

The infected device let the attackers plant an EIP-7702 injection. That feature, added to Ethereum in a 2025 upgrade, lets ordinary wallets temporarily act like smart contracts. Prosecutors said the hackers used it to take control of Binance’s hot wallets and route funds to a “sweeper” contract that moved them out as soon as they arrived.

The first large transfer, about $780 million in USDC, left Binance on Ethereum at about 4:41 p.m. ET on Oct. 29, the complaint says. Another $43 million followed 18 minutes later. Investigators also traced AVAX that was split up, bridged and swapped until it became about $1.56 million in USDC.

U.S. agencies attribute the theft to North Korean military hackers known as the Lazarus Group, or APT38. Investigators said some wallets that collected Binance funds also received money from the roughly $387 million Bitget hack in September, which the exchange’s CEO tied to North Korea.

The complaint also lists earlier heists it attributes to the group, including about $308 million from Japan’s DMM Bitcoin in May 2024 and about $1.5 billion from Bybit in February 2025.

“North Korean cyber hackers stole more than $6 billion in cryptocurrency from Binance, but swift coordination between the government and private sector partners helped prevent a substantial portion of those assets from disappearing,” said Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva of the Criminal Division.

FBI Cyber Branch Operations Director Brett Leatherman said North Korea “depends on sophisticated laundering techniques” to turn stolen crypto into money for the regime. The FBI, the Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are handling the case.

“Secret Service investigators worked with precision to identify and freeze stolen funds within minutes of the hack,” said Special Agent in Charge Todd Bratz. “The criminals behind this intrusion believed they could outrun law enforcement, and they have been proven wrong.”
