Here’s what happened in crypto today

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Need to know what happened in crypto today? Here is the latest news on daily trends and events impacting Bitcoin price, blockchain, DeFi, Web3 and crypto regulation.

Today in crypto, Bitcoin fell below $60,000 for the first time since July amid a broader selloff in US stocks, European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde warned that differing rules could fragment Europe’s tokenized securities markets, and institutional holdings of US Bitcoin ETFs reached a record high in the third quarter.

Bitcoin fell below $60,000 on Friday for the first time since July, extending its recent losses amid a broader selloff in technology stocks and other risk assets.

Bitcoin (BTC) had fallen as low as $58,811 before recovering slightly to trade around $59,503, down 5.6% over the past 24 hours as of 16:52 UTC on Friday, according to CoinGecko data. The cryptocurrency has fallen 13.8% over the past week and nearly 30% over the past month.

The decline came amid a broader selloff in US equities. The Nasdaq Composite closed down 5.28% at 22,617.73, while the S&P 500 fell 3.69% to 6,910.77 on Friday, according to Yahoo Finance.

Shares of Anthropic (ANT), which made its Nasdaq debut on Thursday, closed down 8.50% at $88.91 amid concerns over valuations and spending in the artificial intelligence sector.

European Central Bank (ECB) President Christine Lagarde warned Friday that Europe needs common rules for tokenized securities before incompatible standards take hold across trading platforms.

Speaking at the Frankfurt European Banking Congress, Lagarde said blockchain technology lets firms issue, trade and settle securities on a single platform. The global markets for tokenized assets was worth about 23 billion euros in March, five times its size a year earlier, she said.

Europe could build a single market for those assets, Lagarde said, but if platforms adopt different rules as they grow, they may be unable to settle trades with one another. She said lawmakers have a role alongside the ECB.

“Common rules will be needed too, and those lie with legislators,” she said.

The Eurosystem launched Pontes in September, giving financial institutions a way to settle tokenized securities trades in central bank money. Earlier in November, the European Stability Mechanism used Pontes to settle a 1 billion euro (about $1.1 million) digital bond.

Through a second project, Appia, the Eurosystem is working with market participants on common standards for tokenized securities. It plans to publish a blueprint in 2028.

Institutional investment managers’ holdings of US Bitcoin exchange-traded funds climbed to a record high in the third quarter, as they reversed three straight quarters of net selling, according to regulatory filings.

The 13F filings, analyzed by Galaxy Research, show that the reporting companies added an equivalent of 49,229 Bitcoin (BTC) in the third quarter, outpacing the 47,054 Bitcoin they shed over the last three quarters combined. This lifted the reported total holdings to 356,491 BTC, the highest on record.

“The quarter’s accumulation was the work of a few dozen large institutions,” wrote Galaxy’s head of firmwide research Alex Thorn.

Quarterly 13F filings provide a point-in-time snapshot of US-listed securities held by reporting institutional investment managers. Thorn said half of the net addition of spot Bitcoin ETF shares came from advisors and wealth platforms.
