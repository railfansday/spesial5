Netflix Eyes Return to Cannes After 10 Years as Festival Lobbies for Joel Coen’s ‘Jack of Spades,’ Starring Josh O’Connor

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The streamer hasn't debuted a film at the festival since 2017.

The ice appears to be thawing between Netflix and Cannes.

The streamer, which hasn’t debuted a film at the festival in almost a decade due to France’s strict rules around theatrical releases, is eyeing a return to the south of France in 2027, Variety has learned. Ted Sarandos suggested as much earlier this year, when he told Variety he hoped to “do something special” for Cannes’ 80th edition. Now it turns out that Cannes boss Thierry Frémaux has also been making a push to bring Joel Coen’s “Jack of Spades” to the festival for more than a year, sources say. Netflix declined to comment.

The American gothic mystery, starring Josh O’Connor, Patsy Ferran, Frances McDormand, Lesley Manville and Damian Lewis, was at one point expected to be ready for this year’s Cannes, then Venice, but is now on track for next May. Coen also has a strong relationship with Frémaux, who honored him alongside his brother Ethan at Lyon’s Lumière Film Festival.

But there’s a catch. Netflix recently acquired “Jack of Spades,” complicating plans for what would otherwise have been a natural Cannes contender.

That means the only way for Netflix to return to Cannes with “Jack of Spades” would be out of competition, which has been a deal-breaker for Sarandos. Sources say Frémaux has been trying to convince Netflix and Sarandos that an out-of-competition premiere can be just as prestigious as a competition slot, pointing to the recent examples of Martin Scorsese’s Apple-backed “Killers of the Flower Moon” and Tom Cruise’s “Mission: Impossible – The Final Reckoning,” both of which made a major splash on the Croisette without competing for the Palme d’Or.

But recent changes could help smooth the path towards a Netflix comeback on the Croisette. The streamer’s newly-appointed chief content officer Peter Rice is a longtime friend of Cannes who has known Frémaux since his days at Searchlight. His arrival could help bring the two sides closer after years of tensions over theatrical releases.

Netflix may also have good reason to seek a return to Cannes. After losing a few big-name filmmakers, the streamer could be looking to ramp up its prestige profile. While it has increasingly turned to Venice to launch major awards contenders, including Guillermo del Toro’s “Frankenstein” and Noah Baumbach’s “Jay Kelly,” Cannes remains the world’s biggest film festival and arguably the most powerful platform for launching auteur-driven movies.

Netflix knows that better than most. Despite not bringing its own movies to the festival in 10 years, it has snapped up several on the ground there. In 2024, it bought Jacques Audiard’s “Emilia Pérez” which went on earn 13 Oscar nominations and win two Oscars, including best actress for Zoe Saldana. This year, it acquired “La Bola Negra,” now its main hope for awards season — with many tipping it for best picture.

“Jack of Spades” would seem a perfect fit for the festival, where Joel Coen is one of its favorite auteurs. Alongside his brother Ethan, the director has brought films including “Barton Fink,” which won the Palme d’Or and best director awards, “No Country for Old Men” and “Inside Llewyn Davis.”

Netflix hasn’t taken a film to Cannes since 2017, when it premiered both Bong Joon-ho’s “Okja” and Noah Baumbach’s “The Meyerowitz Stories.” It sparked an outcry from French exhibitors, prompting the festival to change its rules the following year to require competition films to receive theatrical releases in France.

The streamer has, however, softened its stance on theatrical releases considerably. “La Bola Negra” is in the middle of a 46-day theatrical rollout, the longest Netflix has given any of its films to date. Next year, “Narnia: The Magician’s Nephew” will have an exclusive theatrical run, as will Taika Waititi’s animated take on Roald Dahl, “Charlie vs. the Chocolate Factory.”
