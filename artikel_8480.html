Calculate Your Net Worth – Use Today’s Values, Not What You Paid

Calculate your net worth by listing everything you own at what it’s worth today, including your home and car at what they’d sell for, then subtracting every debt. Write the total down with the date, even if it’s negative, as the baseline you’ll measure your progress against.

Your home and car need the most care, because what you paid for each is rarely what it would sell for today. A car loses value as it ages, and a home’s price rises or falls with the local market.

Almost everything else comes straight off a statement or an account page, so the whole count takes about 15 minutes with your account logins ready.

Then list every debt, including your mortgage, car loans, student loans, credit cards, medical bills, and money borrowed from family. Use each one’s current balance from your latest statement, not the total of your remaining payments, which includes interest you haven’t been charged yet.

Money in a traditional 401(k) or IRA hasn’t been taxed yet, so you’ll owe income tax on what you withdraw. Count the full balance or subtract tax at the rate you expect to pay in retirement, and use the same method every time, because the change between counts is what you’re tracking.

A negative total isn’t a verdict on you. It means your debts outweigh what you own for now. Write the total and today’s date in a spreadsheet or a free tracker app. Then count again on the same date next year, valuing everything the same way, or track your net worth each quarter to see how much your own saving added.
