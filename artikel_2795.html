13 Game Review: Suns by the Numbers

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Phoenix Suns just finished the second night of a back-to-back with a road win in Denver, and they sit at 6-7 through 13 games. We're officially 15.85% of the way through the 2026-27 season. Time is flying!

It's been a rollercoaster start. The night before Denver, the Lakers came into the Mortgage Matchup Center and handed Phoenix a loss. Twenty-four hours later, the Suns went into altitude on tired legs and got their first road win of the season. That's the season in a nutshell… a team that is capable of scoring with anyone but keeps giving away possessions on the other end.

Seed: 11th in West (half a game behind Dallas for 10th, 1.5 behind Denver for 9th)

Don't let the 18th-ranked scoring fool you. The Suns play at the league's 22nd-ranked pace (98.88), so raw points undersell them. Per possession, this is a top-12 offense that shoots it well from deep and takes care of the ball, with a turnover rate (14.6%) that ranks 11th.

Phoenix is 28th in free-throw attempts at 21.2 per game. They're also dead last in fouls drawn at 19.4 per game, so nobody in the league puts less pressure on a defense. Opponents make 21.2 free throws per game against this team. Split it out, and the Suns hit 16.2 per night while opponents hit 21.2. That's a five-point gap at the stripe every game, and the Suns' average scoring margin is -3.4. The free-throw line alone covers more than the entire deficit. Fixing this means attacking the paint more and settling for fewer clean pull-ups.

Now, on to the defense and rebounding numbers.

This is where the season is being lost. The Suns are dead last in rebound percentage and dead last in defensive rebound percentage (62.5%). It isn't close, either: the next-worst team, the Lakers, is at 64.1%. Opponents are grabbing 13.6 offensive boards a night, tied for the 4th-most allowed in the league.

The painful part is that the defense does its job early in possessions. Phoenix forces 17.2 turnovers per game, 5th-most in the NBA. Then they give it all back. They don't finish stops with a rebound. They send opponents to the line 27.4 times a night (22nd), even though they rank 13th in fouls committed, which points to shooting fouls and early trips to the bonus. And they let teams shoot 37.1% from three (26th).

A team that ranks 25th in blocks and 30th on the defensive glass isn't winning the size battle at the rim, and the numbers back up what we see on film. Guards and even wings are leaking out before the board is secured, and nobody is putting a body on the weak-side crasher.

The length shows up in the passing lanes (5th in forced turnovers), so the athleticism is there on the perimeter. It just isn't being turned into box-outs, rim deterrence, or trips to the line. Until someone owns the paint on both ends, every stop costs double the effort, and every bucket comes the hard way.

The net rating is -3.0 (21st), which is a fringe play-in team at best. The good news is the offense is real enough that the ceiling is higher than 11th. Clean up two areas, the free-throw gap and the defensive glass, and this team's overall profile moves toward neutral or better. And more importantly, it wins them games.

The formula for the next game is simple. Five guys hit the glass, the wings stop leaking early, and the offense gets downhill to earn more free-throw attempts. Win the possession battle, and this team is well above .500. Lose it again, and Phoenix stays in play-in territory.
