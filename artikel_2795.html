Ashton Jeanty's bad season running the ball overshadows his receiving production

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

In the time of hot takes, nuanced opinions rarely get much attention, and so in the case of Raiders running back Ashton Jeanty, little has been said about him this season other than that he’s a bust. Last year the Raiders took Jeanty with the sixth overall pick in the NFL draft, and this year he’s averaging 3.09 yards per carry, worst in the NFL among the 34 running backs with at least 70 carries this season.

First running back drafted to last running back in the yards-per-carry rankings? Bust. Case closed.

Except it’s not that simple. There’s no excusing the Raiders’ awful rushing offense and Jeanty’s role in it, but it’s also unfair to talk about Jeanty without acknowledging that he’s one of the best receivers out of the backfield in the NFL.

Jeanty has 46 catches this season, second only to Cincinnati’s Chase Brown among NFL running backs. And Jeanty has 417 receiving yards, second only to San Francisco’s Christian McCaffrey. There are a lot of running backs who get praised for what they can contribute in the passing game -- Detroit’s Jahmyr Gibbs, Atlanta’s Bijan Robinson, Pittsburgh’s Jaylen Warren -- and Jeanty has produced more in the passing game than all of them.

Jeanty’s 17 receiving first downs are tied with McCaffrey for the most of any running back in the NFL this season. Although Jeanty has only one rushing touchdown this season, he has three receiving touchdowns, tied for the second-most among NFL running backs. (Kansas City’s Kenneth Walker has four.)

On Sunday against the Seahawks, when Raiders rookie quarterback Fernando Mendoza struggled in his first NFL start, Jeanty was his leading receiver, with four catches for 46 yards. With the Raiders trying to mount a comeback in the fourth quarter, it was Jeanty turning a short pass into a 38-yard gain that got the Raiders into range for the field goal that made it a one-possession game. The Raiders lost, and Jeanty deserves his share of the blame for their inability to do anything on the ground, but the loss would have been worse if not for Jeanty being available as an outlet receiver for Mendoza.

Jeanty hasn’t been the player the Raiders thought they were getting after he was college football’s leading rusher at Boise State. But his contributions to the Raiders’ offense are real, even if they’re not spectacular.
