Miami vs Notre Dame Prediction: Odds, expert picks, team and player news, betting trends, and stats

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The game of the week is in South Bend! The #1 Miami Hurricanes (8-0) go to #11 Notre Dame (7-1) on the biggest Saturday of the year. In the initial College Football Rankings, Notre Dame is 11th; presumably, if the Irish lose to Miami, the Irish are out of the playoffs, even with Boston College, SMU, and Syracuse looming. Miami beat Notre Dame 27-24 on a field goal with 1:03 remaining to open the 2025 season, so this is also a revenge spot for Notre Dame. The Irish opened as a -3.5 point favorite, but big bettors have moved this line to Notre Dame -7!

Both Miami and Notre Dame showed struggles last week. Notre Dame trailed 13-7 to Navy before going on a 28-0 run to end the game, while Miami was spotted playing tennis with North Carolina, going back and forth in a 38-35 shootout. It was the second time Miami was pushed to the brink of their first loss (Florida State), but the Darian Mensah and Malachi Toney connection seemingly can't be stopped, unless there was an injury. Rumors are Toney hurt his ankle at practice on Wednesday and his status is questionable ahead of this game.

Mensah and Toney are the frontrunners for the Heisman, while Notre Dame's CJ Carr is fourth in terms of odds. This game will shape the Heisman market as well as Georgia versus Ole Miss and Ohio State against Oregon. The matchup in Notre Dame and Miami that everyone will be locked into is Toney versus Leonard Moore. Notre Dame and Moore haven't faced a passing attack all season quite like this, and those one-on-ones between the two will be highlighted every drive.

The biggest question for a lot of people in this game is: can the Miami defense slow down Notre Dame's offense? That's a fair point. My biggest question is, can CJ Carr win a big game? He's 0-3 against top 16 teams and 17-0 outside that range. His best win would be over #20 USC or unranked Arkansas? This is a major test for Carr, just as much as it is for the Miami defense.

Let's dive into the schools on both sides of the ball, look at a few key players, and see where the numbers take us.

Miami enters the week with the highest-scoring offense in the country, posting 47.6 per game and scoring at least 41 points six times. Their season-low is 33! Rumors have it that Malachi Toney suffered an ankle injury this week in practice and could be questionable for the game, so this offense would take a major hit if he was unable to go. However, Miami has a plethora of weapons outside of him. Javian Mallory has exploded onto the scene and is splitting carries with Mark Fletcher, and while Toney has 73 receptions and the next closest is at 28, Miami does have weapons like Cooper Barkate (from Duke), Daylyn Upshaw, and Elijah Lofton. They would be No. 1 or 2 options at most schools, but Toney is often so open that the other receivers don't see as many targets. Darian Mensah would have to spread the ball around if Toney is out, but Navy did show some holes in the Notre Dame rush defense, so there's hope for Fletcher-Mallory to find daylight.

Miami Player to Watch on Offense: QB Darian Mensah

Darian Mensah enters the Week as the Heisman favorite for the second time all season and the first time in over a month. In the last two games, Mensah has seven touchdowns, 661 passing yards, and an 80% completion percentage. He has four games with 300-plus passing yards, and he's thrown three or more touchdowns in seven out of eight games. Mensah leads the country in completion percentage (80.7%), tied for the lead in touchdown passes (25), and interceptions (1). This will be the first defense Miami has faced that is top 20 in defense (WF is now in the top 20), so, simply put, Mensah's Heisman moment comes down to this. He will have to find receivers outside of Toney if Moore is lined up specifically on him, but the offense is dynamic enough that he can be a decoy and they can still move the ball.

Miami's defense is top 25 in the country with 17.7 points per game allowed, but they've been disregarded as a top-tier unit because of 43 points to Florida State and 35 to North Carolina. The Hurricanes will have a lot to prove, especially in the secondary. Miami gave up 298 passing yards and four touchdowns to Ashton Daniels (FSU) and 341 yards and four more touchdowns to Billy Edwards Jr. (UNC) in two of the last three games. CJ Carr could certainly have a day if the Irish want to be aggressive and work downfield. However, that's not how the Irish operate now. Miami's rush defense is formidable, but it will have a massive challenge ahead trying to contain Notre Dame's three-running-back committee. Miami is the 37th-graded rush defense per PFF and 108th in coverage grade. Miami's defense, on paper, looks outmatched against Notre Dame, which is one of the reasons money is coming in on the Irish.

Miami Player to Watch on Defense: DE Marquise Lightfoot

Last week, Marquise Lightfoot had two sacks at North Carolina, tying his season-high, plus the second-most tackles in a game of his career (7). Lightfoot has seven sacks on the season now and also earned his second forced fumble last week. Miami ranks 14th in pressure created on defense, and Lightfoot on the edge is a big reason why.

When Notre Dame scores 28 points with CJ Carr, they are 15-1 over the last two seasons compared to 1-2 when they don't. The Irish have answered a lot of their questions in the running game this season with a three-back committee, which will need to find success against Miami. It was Carr's first game, but the Irish will have to be ultra-aggressive and learn from their mistakes.

Notre Dame Player to Watch on Offense: QB CJ Carr

CJ Carr went 14-of-19 for 210 yards and two touchdowns against Navy. Notre Dame only had three drives and 19 plays in the first half, but scored two touchdowns. In the second half, Notre Dame scored on three straight possessions to put the game out of reach. Carr has 18 passing touchdowns to one interception this season and has six games with two-plus touchdowns and seven with 200-plus passing yards. Miami ranks 14th in pressure and 108th in coverage grades, so the more time Carr has, the more likely he is to move the ball on Notre Dame.

Notre Dame's defense has been graded as a top-10 defense all year long, and now they have another chance to go out and prove it. Notre Dame struggled in the first half against Navy, but routed them in the second half, holding the Midshipmen to 20 total yards on 16 plays and four drives! It was Notre Dame's most dominant half of the season as they enter the biggest game of the year. The Irish have the seventh-ranked rush defense and 12th-ranked coverage grade this season. However, Notre Dame allowed BYU to average 4.0 yards per carry and score twice on the ground, plus 239 yards and two more touchdowns through the air. BYU was also without arguably their best offensive player, LJ Martin- sound familiar to this game? Notre Dame will have another chance to learn from their mistakes.

Notre Dame Player to Watch on Defense: DL Boubacar Traore

One of my favorite players to watch on Notre Dame this season has been Boubacar Traore. Even when he isn't making the tackle or getting the sack, he's one of the main factors in why a hole or gap opened up for a teammate to make a play. Traore tied his season-high last week with six tackles against Navy and is still looking for his first sack since 10/10. Miami is 84th in PFF's pass-blocking grades, so Traore will be in the hunt this weekend to improve on his team-high 3.5 sacks. Traore had a season-high six tackles and a sack last year against Miami.
