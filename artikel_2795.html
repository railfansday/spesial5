Kingdom Keys revisited: Chiefs turn two keys in win over Falcons

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Every week, "Kingdom Keys" identifies three things the Kansas City Chiefs must accomplish to win their upcoming game. Afterward, we revisit those keys to determine which ones Kansas City actually turned.

The Chiefs defeated the Atlanta Falcons 35-13 on Sunday, improving to 7-2 behind five touchdown passes from Patrick Mahomes and another strong defensive performance.

Kansas City clearly accomplished its first two keys. The third requires a little more nuance.

The offensive key was for Kansas City's wide receivers to punish Atlanta whenever the Falcons committed additional resources to stopping the run.

Chiefs wide receivers combined for 12 receptions, 222 yards and three touchdowns. They accounted for nearly 64% of Mahomes' 348 passing yards and three of his five touchdown passes.

Rashee Rice led the group with five receptions for 82 yards, including a 50-yard completion that helped set up Kansas City's fourth touchdown. Josh Downs made an immediate impact in his Chiefs debut, catching four passes for 53 yards and a touchdown after having only two full practices with Mahomes.

The production extended beyond the top two receivers.

Rookie Cyrus Allen caught two passes for 44 yards, including a 39-yard touchdown that extended Kansas City's lead to 21-3. Andrew Armstrong turned his only target into a 43-yard touchdown that put the game away in the fourth quarter.

The original goal was for the receivers to combine for at least 150 yards. They surpassed that mark by 72 yards.

The Falcons wanted someone other than the Chiefs' running backs to beat them. Kansas City's receivers provided a convincing answer.

Bijan Robinson entered the game averaging more than 125 yards from scrimmage per contest. Kansas City held him to 77.

Robinson rushed 16 times for 71 yards and caught four passes for just 6 yards. He scored Atlanta's only touchdown on a three-yard run in the fourth quarter, but the Chiefs prevented him from controlling the game.

Kansas City did allow Robinson to break runs of 17 and 14 yards. Outside those two carries, however, he gained only 40 yards on his other 14 rushing attempts.

The Chiefs were especially effective against Robinson as a receiver. His four catches produced only 6 yards, and two receptions lost yardage. Taking away those easy completions forced Michael Penix Jr. to throw elsewhere.

Kansas City also consistently put Atlanta behind schedule. The Falcons converted only five of their 16 third-down opportunities, finishing at 31.3%. Using third-and-7 or longer as the definition of third-and-long, Atlanta converted just two of 10 attempts.

One conversion came on a third-and-11 completion to Jahan Dotson in the second quarter. The other came when Drake London gained 20 yards on third-and-8 in the third quarter. Otherwise, the Chiefs repeatedly ended Atlanta's possessions in obvious passing situations.

The pressure also returned. Kansas City recorded three sacks, including one from Ashton Gillotte on third-and-7 that forced Atlanta to settle for a field goal. George Karlaftis recorded 1.5 sacks, and Chris Jones shared another.

Kansas City didn't completely eliminate Robinson, but it kept Atlanta's best offensive player from dictating the game. That was the goal.

Harrison Butker officially finished without a field-goal attempt, making this key impossible to grade as a success or failure.

However, that does not mean the afternoon was meaningless.

Late in the second quarter, Butker connected on a 41-yard field goal. Atlanta was called for defensive offside, erasing the attempt from the official statistics and giving Kansas City a first down. Mahomes found Downs for an 18-yard touchdown on the next play.

The kick did not count, but it still went through the uprights.

Butker also made all five of his extra-point attempts. Those kicks don't prove his season-long struggles are over, especially since most of his problems have come on field goals of at least 50 yards. Still, cleanly converting six kicks — including the nullified field goal — can help him start rebuilding his rhythm.

Butker still needs to prove he can consistently convert field goals under game pressure. Sunday simply did not provide enough official opportunities to make that determination. What it did provide was a small, positive step.

The Chiefs definitively accomplished two of their three Kingdom Keys against Atlanta.

The wide receivers delivered their best collective performance of the season, producing 222 yards and three touchdowns. Defensively, Kansas City held Robinson below 100 scrimmage yards and limited Atlanta to two conversions on 10 third-and-long opportunities.

Butker did not record an official field-goal attempt, leaving the third key ungraded. However, making five extra points and watching a 41-yard attempt split the uprights gave him something positive to carry forward.

Kingdom Keys final tally: Two accomplished, one ungraded
