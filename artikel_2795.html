NFL mock draft 2027: 4 QBs selected in first-round projection with Arch Manning twist

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Not just the holiday season, but the mock draft season.

Now that the NFL's trade deadline is in the rear-view mirror, and we have reached the second half of the season, the first round of the 2027 Draft is taking shape. We already know the teams likely to be picking near the top of the draft. Team needs are becoming clearer, where teams might be picking in the draft is coming into focus, and the prospects themselves have begun to take shape.

So let's kick off this joyous season with our first mock draft of the cycle. Here are the picks, along with some big-picture thoughts following the mock draft.

We start with the trade inside the top five, with the New York Jets moving up to No. 2 to draft Drew Mestemaker from Oklahoma State.

There are a few things to discuss when it comes to this hypothetical.

The Jets are loaded with three picks in the first round, but if they want to draft the quarterback at the top of their board, they will likely have to move ahead of the Miami Dolphins at No. 3. With picks No. 4 and No. 13, they can do just that, while retaining a pick at No. 6 (courtesy of the Indianapolis Colts and the Sauce Gardner trade) to address another position of need. In this scenario that is Notre Dame cornerback Leonard Moore.

But … trading up for a quarterback prospect who did not even start for his high school team and is just in his first year as a starter for a Power 4 school?

There is a lot to like about Mestemaker, from his ability in the downfield passing game, his athleticism, his willingness to challenge tough windows over the middle, and his touch when layering throws to all levels of the field. But in many ways it is that fact, that he is wrapping up his first year as a starter at a Power 4 school, that works in his favor. The trajectory is impressive and the upside is off the charts. I was a fan of his after last year, and have only grown more impressed with his profile.

As for the Chargers' side of this trade, they get a new target for Justin Herbert in Charlie Becker out of Indiana, and Ashton Hampton to address a need in the secondary.

This is likely the next big question: Where is Arch Manning?

Sitting here on the cusp of Thanksgiving, I'm not sure Manning enters the draft. His game against LSU — much like his games against Ole Miss and Ohio State earlier this season — was another microcosm of his current draft profile: Some great moments late, overshadowing some difficult moments earlier.

Now, maybe Manning does enter the draft, and if he does it will be interesting to see how the greater draft community stacks him with the rest of this class. But right now, I think it is more likely he heads back to Texas, or even entertains the transfer portal, than he heads to the NFL.

Now let's talk about the rest of the quarterbacks … both those listed here and two names that are not.

We can start with the two quarterbacks not listed here in this first-round mock: Austin Simmons and Trinidad Chambliss. Beginning with Simmons, much like Manning I think he heads back to Missouri for another season. Simmons recently signed a new deal with the school for 2027, but he does have an option to enter the NFL Draft. But with the Tigers' recent slide, I think it is likely he heads back to campus with that new deal in hand, to try and make another run.

As for Chambliss, as much as I like him, I do wonder if the NFL will view him the same way that I do. He might slide to the top of the second round, which will not be a bad thing for him.

That leaves Dante Moore as the next quarterback off the board, to the Cleveland Browns. While Deshaun Watson has stabilized that position this season, Cleveland still needs a long-term solution, and Moore was a potential top-five pick a year ago, and the talent is there.

Then we get CJ Carr to the Minnesota Vikings at the end of the first round. I think he would be a good fit for Kevin O'Connell, and it sets up a scenario where yet again Kirk Cousins could be mentoring a first-round pick. This idea sent me down the road of a "Mandalorian" world where Cousins just mentors a first-round pick to start each season before moving on to a team making a surprise playoff run despite a glaring need at the position. A hired gun, spaghetti Western style, year after year.

I've said to much. But you know where to find me Netflix.

But the more I thought about pairing Jaden Baugh with the Houston Texans, the more sense it made. And one does not need to look far for inspiration. Take the Kansas City Chiefs, who stabilized their running game with the addition of Kenneth Walker III this past offseason. Walker has given Kansas City an explosive element in the run game, and put the Chiefs back among the AFC's list of elite teams.

For a team like the Texans, bolstering the running attack makes a world of sense. While part of it is game-script dependent, Houston ranks 29th in the league in rushing yards per game, and 24th in Expected Points Added per Rush.

Going offensive line here is also an option for Houston but Baugh, who is explosive both on the ground and through the air, is a fun fit for them.
