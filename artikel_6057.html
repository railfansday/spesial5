Air India and Air India Express now both have new CEOs, and the group’s transformation looks steadier for it. The harder test is whether the new leaders can cut losses that are still running in the billions.

Air India Express on Wednesday announced the appointment of its chief financial officer Deepak Rajawat as CEO, effective Dec. 1, placing a finance and commercial executive at the helm as the airline enters a more demanding phase of its growth under Tata Group, which controls the Air India Group.

Rajawat succeeds Aloke Singh, who left in March to join rival IndiGo as chief strategy officer. Singh oversaw the fleet growing from 26 planes to more than 100, and its merger with AirAsia India also took place on his watch.

Air India Express has named its chief financial officer, Deepak Rajawat, as CEO effective December 1, putting a finance and commercial executive in charge as the Tata Group's low-cost carrier moves from rapid fleet expansion to improving efficiency and financial performance. Rajawat succeeds Aloke Singh, who left in March for IndiGo, where he is now chief strategy officer; Singh oversaw growth from 26 planes to more than 100 and the AirAsia India merger. The appointment lands against heavy group losses: Air India Group reported a combined net loss of about $2.3 billion in fiscal 2026, more than double the prior year, and Singapore Airlines, which owns 25.1% of Air India, booked $281.5 million as its share of those losses for the six months to September 30. Air India Express itself was hit by foreign exchange losses and operational disruptions in fiscal 2026, and management has said it will prioritize cost rationalization, network optimization, and revenue enhancement. New Air India CEO Tewolde Gebremariam has been exploring shared corporate functions and cost reductions across the two airlines, even as they pursue different growth strategies. Rajawat inherits about 585 daily flights, 48 domestic and 17 international destinations, a fleet of more than 100 Boeing 737s and Airbus A320s, and a group plan to allocate 190 of the 470 aircraft ordered to Air India Express.
