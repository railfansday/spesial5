Thieves steal 5,000 endangered ants from nature reserve in bizarre theft

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Thieves have stolen an entire colony of rare ants from a protected nature reserve, with as many as 5,000 insects potentially taken.

The colony of narrow-headed ants was removed from the reserve in Devon, England, the species’ “last stronghold” in the country.

The theft was discovered during routine checks in mid-October, when researchers found a hole where one of the nests had previously been.

According to Devon Wildlife Trust, the ground around the missing nest showed signs of digging with a spade, suggesting someone had deliberately removed the colony.

A single narrow-headed ant nest can contain between 1,000 and 5,000 ants, including breeding queens that can live for up to 25 years. However, the exact number taken remains unknown.

The species was once much more widespread across England, but habitat loss has reduced its population to a single remaining location. Small numbers also survive in Scotland.

Julia Balfour, a species recovery officer at Devon Wildlife Trust, explained that the loss of even one colony could have lasting consequences.

“The removal of an established nest is a significant loss for this species. Narrow-headed Ant colonies develop slowly, and each mature nest, with its long-lived queens, represents many years of growth that cannot quickly be replaced,” she said.

“Our most recent survey recorded 142 active nests on the nature reserve, so every nest matters. This is the last population of Narrow-headed Ants in England, and losing even one colony sets back years of careful work by our staff, volunteers and partners.”

The theft has been reported to police, who warned that damaging or disturbing a protected nature reserve is a criminal offense.

“Sites of Special Scientific Interest are legally protected, and it is a criminal offence for any person to intentionally or recklessly damage, destroy, or disturb the protected area.”
