Liverpool forward Cody Gakpo struggled to have any impact as the Netherlands were thrashed 5-0 by Germany in the Nations League on Monday.

Derry Scherhant, Florian Wirtz, Nico Schlotterbeck and Brajan Gruda got the goals for the home side, on what was a truly embarrassing night for Xavi Hernandez's team.

The Dutch arrived in Berlin with the top spot in group two almost wrapped up, needing just to avoid defeat by more than three goals after they had boosted their goal difference by seven against Greece on Friday.

Gakpo, 27, failed to score in that game and he had even less of an impact against Germany, faltering in the face of his former manager Jurgen Klopp.

The winger has not looked quite the same since returning from injury, with his fitness still looking to be in question.

He has scored just once since making his return, with that strike coming from the penalty spot against Chelsea in the Carabao Cup.

Though Gakpo has been starting on the right of the attack for Liverpool of late, he will soon find his position under threat.

Both Victor Munoz and Rio Ngumoha have been in good form in the position, and will hope to usurp their senior.

Right now, the 27-year-old is doing little to argue against exactly that, producing another disappointing display on Monday.

In Gakpo's defence, he was not alone in struggling against the onslaught of German attacks, but he is considered a leader and held to higher expectations.

The forward misplaced a whopping 12 passes in his 78 minutes on the pitch, was dispossessed once and dribbled past twice.

He took only two touches in the opposition box and lost five duels, in what was a dismal display.

Dutch outlet Voetbal Primeur gave Gakpo a 4.5 out of 10, and added: "The attacker showed a few nice flashes, especially shortly after half-time, and injected some life into the game, but even Gakpo couldn't turn the tide. He just stood there and watched."

In an equally harsh verdict, Soccernews gave him a four and said: "Not a great international spell for the Liverpool left winger. Against Greece, he was the only attacker without any impact, and in Berlin, he was invisible except for one attempt.

"When it came to tracking back with the score at 3-0, he refused to make an extra effort. Xavi won't like that."

Upon their return to action, Liverpool will face a difficult run of fixtures and Andoni Iraola will look for leaders.

The Reds will host Mauricio Pochettino's Manchester United at Anfield on Sunday before facing Everton in the Merseyside derby and Chelsea soon after.

Gakpo will be one face that Iraola will certainly expect contribution from, and he must ensure that he provides it.

If not, his position on the right will almost certainly be under siege from the younger forwards behind him.
