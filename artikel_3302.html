‘The Fall and Rise of Reggie Dinkins’ Bobby Moynihan Explains That Unexpected Thanksgiving ‘Obsession’ Crossover

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Carmelo and Delilah’s secret is out, and Moynihan teases who might stick around once the Thanksgiving dust settles.

Editor’s Note: The following contains spoilers for The Fall and Rise of Reggie Dinkins Season 2, Episode 4

For Reggie Dinkins (Tracy Morgan), Thanksgiving is about family, food, and making sure no one ever scores more than seven touchdowns during the big game. So when his record is on the line in Season 2 of The Fall and Rise of Reggie Dinkins, Rusty (Bobby Moynihan) uses his “One Wish Willow” to make the guy about to break it… explode. While he should’ve been more specific, things don’t go as planned.

Meanwhile, Brina (Precious Way) is trying to get through dinner without getting on the wrong side of Reggie’s mom, Gigi (Leslie Uggams). But right when Jerry Basmati (Craig Robinson) celebrates the new record, everybody finds out the big secret: Carmelo and Jerry’s daughter Delilah (Arica Himmel) are dating. It’s all very Montague and Capulet, obviously — but at least Arthur (Daniel Radcliffe) got to eat some soul food.

In our latest Collider Signature Q&A breaking down Monday night’s episode, “Turkey Can Be This,” Moynihan gets into why Rusty would spend his one wish on somebody else’s football record, what it’s like to eat Thanksgiving dinner for two weeks on the set of their critically acclaimed sitcom, and how much messier things get for the family from here following that shocking reveal. He also teases something coming up for Rusty that might be his favorite thing the show has ever done.

COLLIDER: Your comedy has meant so much to me and my sister over the years. We’ve watched you on SNL since the start when you first joined, and seeing you [on the Halloween episode] was so exciting. I had a funny feeling you’d be on because it’s been 10 years since the David S. Pumpkins sketch. What was it like returning to that skit 10 years later from the premiere of when it first came out?

BOBBY MOYNIHAN: Thank you... It was insane. It kind of all happened in a perfect SNL Mad Lib fashion in the sense of, I’m in New York shooting Reggie, where we have the last two episodes we’re still shooting, but it’s Halloween, so I didn’t have to shoot on Friday, so I decided to last-minute fly home to LA to go see my kids for Halloween.

I got on the plane, and my wife sent me a text saying, “Hey, I saw an article saying it’s the 10-year anniversary of David Pumpkins.” I went like, “Huh! Okay,” and I just kind of clocked it. Like 20 minutes later, I get a very cryptic — well, it wasn’t very cryptic at all; it was just a message from the stage manager at SNL that just said, “Can’t wait to see you this week!” And I was like, of course, this is how I find out.

Then the next thing I know, I’m in LA, and I take my kids to a parade, and then I get back on a plane, and I fly back to New York, and I’m on SNL again, and I’m doing Drunk Uncle, also, because the Brewers are in town. It was a very SNL 24 hours. I didn’t know it was happening, and then all of a sudden, I’m on the other side doing an interview the next week, going like, yeah, that place is singularly insane and wonderful.

What’s it like revisiting those two characters from such different parts of your SNL career in one night? They’re both so beloved. It was so fun to see you with Tom [Hanks] and Mikey [Day], but then Drunk Uncle is his own thing. He’s a Funko Pop, after all!

MOYNIHAN: I feel very lucky. The 50th was an insane experience all around as a fan and somebody who was on the show. The 50th kind of broke me in half as an SNL cast member. Then all of a sudden, now, this felt like the afterparty. This felt like, “Oh, now I’m just going to go have fun! And I get to do both? I get to do Pumpkins and this dumb thing?” It was a blast.

Me and Tom Hanks standing in the hallway, just talking about how stupid it was that we were back 10 years later, and how absolutely insane it is that we had no idea that night. We were like, “This is so stupid. What are we doing? This one’s not going to make it.” And now that we were standing there 10 years later, my favorite moment by far is when Trump steps in, and people are like, “Aww! No! David Pumpkins!” It’s like, all you did was hate David Pumpkins, and now he’s gone. It’s hilarious. The whole thing is insane and very silly.

Let’s talk about Reggie Dinkins. Again, another show that I love so much. This show makes me laugh out loud every second. It’s literally one of those laugh-a-minute sort of shows. My biggest criticism is you’re laughing so much you miss the other joke, and then you have to go back. It’s always a rewatch show, which I love about this.

MOYNIHAN: My third viewing is always my favorite.

Okay, I’ll have to make it to a third. [Laughs] One of the things I love so much in Episode 2’s “Spaghetti Loop” is that Brina called Rusty a “skin-covered Paddington,” which is the best description ever, but it’s also so sweet. Do you think Rusty understands how important he is to everyone, or does he just still see himself as Reggie’s best friend who happens to live there? Because I feel like he’s more than a sidekick. He’s not just a sidekick; he’s everything else.

MOYNIHAN: Oh, yeah. I think he’s very happy to be where he is, but I think this season in particular is him going, “What am I?” Or at least it’s the first time he’s been rocked into somebody going, “What do you do here?” And he’s like, “Uh, I talk to Reggie.” [Laughs] He’s got to kind of answer for himself this season a tiny bit, and what does he do to people, and what does that set him off? It was fun. This season is a blast.

I feel like she planted these seeds of self-doubt, and he’s going to be listening to self-help podcasts.

MOYNIHAN: [Laughs] It’s a very fun season with Brina. There’s a big arc with Brina this season. Me and Precious [Way] have had an absolute blast together this season. She is fantastic.

Thanksgiving at the Dinkins house is absolute chaos, especially with Reggie’s seven-touchdown record, and Rusty is so desperate to protect him that he uses his One Wish Willow, and things go horribly wrong. Of all the things that Rusty could wish for, why do you think protecting Reggie matters so much to him, especially that record? I know they’ve been through so much together.

MOYNIHAN: That’s it. That’s Rusty. He’s Reggie’s best friend. That’s his identity. He’s there to make sure that Reggie is okay. That’s why he exists, and he’s very happy doing that. It’s so funny. I haven’t seen that episode yet, and I totally forgot about that moment with the One Wish Willow.

It’s great to have that connection to Obsession, which I feel like this is what the show does so well, like how 30 Rock did, too, where they’re very meta in terms of the pop culture zeitgeist.

MOYNIHAN: Oh, in the first episode of the season, they say, “Reggie’s episode of SNL, they put it back on Peacock.” God, that kills me. [Laughs]

It’s so good! I love that Reggie is pretty quick, though, to turn on Rusty when things go wrong. It’s kinda sad. [Laughs]

MOYNIHAN: The whole family. Everybody does.

Do you think there’s a point where Rusty might finally get tired of living in Reggie’s shadow? Could we see him putting himself first? Because I love the side gigs where he was a toe model. He’s just trying to find, I think, independence in some way.

MOYNIHAN: I’m going to right now, in this moment, say thank you because I feel like if we get to Season 3, I’m going to go to the writers and be like, “I think this is the season where Rusty, like How Stella Got Her Groove Back, decides he’s more, and he’s got to spread his wings a little, and then realizes he’s made a grave, grave mistake. He should have just hung out with Reggie more.

Do you think that he would ever have that? I feel like they can’t, because I feel like they’re so tightly-knit, and they love each other so much. They’re like brothers. I would never see that.

MOYNIHAN: No. I keep saying they’re not Bert and Ernie; they’re Ernie and Ernie. They’re two Ernie’s having a blast. There is no Bert. Well, Daniel [Radcliffe]’s the Bert.

It was nice to see Leslie Uggams join the Thanksgiving festivities. I love her as Reggie’s mom, and she has a great relationship with Rusty. She has a great relationship with Monica, too, but not so much with Brina. What was it like having Leslie on set and expanding the Dinkins family in this way? Because I do love that every season we’re going to get a little bit more lore in Dinkins life, which is perfect for a documentary.

MOYNIHAN: It’s the best. Easily the funniest and most I’ve ever laughed on set, easily, ever in my entire life, is Leslie Uggams. I haven’t seen that episode, so I don’t know which take they used, but when she exits the room, and just weirdly goes, “Meow, beep boop beep.” Do you know what I’m talking about? And weirdly exits?

MOYNIHAN: She did, like, 100 takes. There was an awkward moment, and they were just making her say crazy stuff. Oh my God, it was the funniest thing in the world. I hope something makes it. It’s got to be in there because it was very major to the plot.

There are many scenes that are so funny, and that’s why my sister and I are like, “You have to pause. I have to hear that joke again. It’s so good.

It’s such a good episode, and it’s such a fun episode because there are so many different characters. You have the cousin there. You also have Daniel in love with the cooking, and he’s just eating, and he wants to get married to her. When you have an episode that big, and there’s so much chaos, what is something that you’re so excited about when you’re seeing it on paper, and you’re like, “I cannot wait to get that scene done?” Because I think that’s the most important part where you guys get to really play around with the written work and then expand on it.

MOYNIHAN: It’s funny, what I remember most about that is it was Thanksgiving. It was like being at a real Thanksgiving for two weeks, because we’re just shooting all that, and there was real food, and our props department is insane. It was all really delicious, hot Thanksgiving food constantly, so I think I gained another 100 pounds. It was amazing, and also just super fun. That’s my favorite episode, maybe, to shoot because it just felt like that! It felt like this was Thanksgiving with the Dinkins family, and now we have all these new weirdos that are probably coming back at any point. The lore is getting bigger. That show in particular might have been my favorite to shoot this season.

It’s a really funny episode, and there are so many shenanigans that happen. Obviously, the biggest thing that everybody finds out is that Carmelo and Delilah are dating, and it’s a terrible way to find out because everyone’s already upset about the game, and then all of a sudden they see that at the end, and Jerry Basmati’s really excited because he’s like, “We broke his record!” What can you tease about how much worse things are going to get, especially with everything blowing up in this family dynamic?

MOYNIHAN: I think they’ve done a fantastic job at doing a Season 2. As a fan of Robert [Carlock] and Tina [Fey] and Sam [Means]’ stuff, I can’t believe that we’re so lucky that we got a Season 2 and that this is where it’s going. I don’t want to spoil anything. It definitely gets higher stakes towards the end of the season, and who ends up with who, and who ends up gone, and who is sticking around. It’s a fun season. I’m just really happy.

It also makes me happy that we might get a Season 3 because it means that we keep building toward something, and I don’t ever want to stop this show. If they told us, “Okay, you’re going to do this for 20 years,” I’d be like, “Great. Thank you very much.”

I love how you answer with the power of a Marvel actor, and there are NBC snipers around, and you’re like, “I can’t say too much!”

MOYNIHAN: That’s funny. I have given away spoilers already, but it’s more truly, I’m just a fan of the show. But also, I respect NBC. Always have, always will. [Laughs]

I’ve been in interviews where I think showrunners have accidentally given me spoilers and I’m like, “Oh, did you really have to say that?”

MOYNIHAN: Rusty dies. You know that Rusty dies this season?

That’s horrible! RIP, king. [Laughs] I will ask, though: do you ever think that we’d be able to see Rusty the way Monica is dating now, which is so great to see her get her groove? Do you think maybe we could see Rusty in a relationship with anyone? I think he has that heart for more. You know something!

MOYNIHAN: Well, I know a lot of things. By the way, Rusty has had maybe the best track record women-wise on the show, or at least partner-wise. Rusty does fine, which is hilarious. We should definitely see more of that this season. I think it was Season 1 when you saw a couple of ladies leaving the house. You'll see some more of that this season from Rusty.

But also, without spoiling anything, something is coming that makes me so happy. It might be my favorite thing that’s happened in the whole series. Yes, I can’t wait. I also just love hearing more about Rusty’s backstory, and we find out some stuff soon.

He’s like those little Russian dolls when you just keep opening up, and you get more and more and more. There are so many different things.

MOYNIHAN: When [the showrunners] come to me, I go, “So what’s up, guys? What’s about to happen next?” When they told me this one, I went, “Just thank you. Thank you so much. I love being on your show.”

I love this show, and I’m so happy that I got to talk to you today. I have one last question. Going back to the SNL of it all, because there’s an SNL UK. I don’t know if you got to see George Fouracres.

MOYNIHAN: He was there this weekend! I missed it. I was in and out. I saw that he was there, and I’m a fan!

As someone who’s part of such a big SNL history, how do you feel when you see those two shows coming together and converging? Because having James Austin Johnson was amazing.

MOYNIHAN: The best. I love it. I mean, I think I’m different in the sense that I will wear it on my sleeve; I’ve always been a fan of SNL, and I definitely keep up with it. I love the UK version. It’s so cool. I love that they’re doing it. It makes me happy. I love that SNL’s still around.

As an ex-cast member, to go back after 10 years and do a sketch that you never thought was going to make it, and then also they’re like, “The Brewers are here. Do you want to do this Drunk Uncle?” It was a very SNL space campy weekend for me where it was like I got handed this very big SNL package present from the universe of just, like, “Hey, you want to come back and have an absolute blast again really quick?”

It was also very, very interesting and cathartic to see all the new people and what the show has become after the pandemic and how they do it differently. It’s a crazy place.

Hypothetically, the same way that James got to go overseas, if one of your SNL characters ever had to go across the pond to be on the show, who would you choose?

The new Prime Minister looks a lot like the last guy...

MOYNIHAN: If they said, “Come over here and do any of them?” It’s so funny, my first honest thought was, “Man, I’m old, and I don’t like going through customs.” But of course I would do it. Of course. I would be there because I love SNL so much. It’s also like I would kind of just, as a fan, love to go see how they do it over there. That would be nuts.

That would be really fun. I think you would be great. I was talking to my sister, and we were like, “Drunk Uncle as the American Drunk Uncle would be great because can you imagine a Drunk Uncle with a British Drunk Uncle?

MOYNIHAN: I want to go, but wear a top hat and a monocle and just make fun of Daniel Radcliffe the whole time.

The Fall and Rise of Reggie Dinkins airs Mondays at 8:30 p.m. EST on NBC and streams the next day on Peacock.
