In a bathroom remodel, put your splurge into one feature you’ll see or touch every day, like the vanity top or showerhead, and buy budget versions of the other finishes. Don’t skimp on what the walls hide, like the waterproofing behind the tile and the valve behind the shower handle, since fixing either later means opening a finished wall.

A bathroom looks expensive because of what the eye lands on first, like a stone vanity top, a rainfall shower, or one wall of standout tile. Premium material on every other surface raises the bill without changing that first impression.

Choose the splurge by how often you’ll use it. You touch the vanity top and stand under the showerhead every day.

Everywhere else, these budget choices hold up:

One swap can pay for the splurge. Say a couple wants a $1,200 quartz vanity top, and their quote prices 120 square feet of marble tile at $15 a square foot, or $1,800 before labor. Porcelain that looks like marble, at $2 a square foot, costs $240 for the same space, a $1,560 saving that pays for the top with $360 left over. Refinishing a tub that’s still in good shape and shopping salvage stores cut the bill further.

Don’t cut corners on three things. Tile and grout let water through, so the waterproofing membrane behind them keeps the wall dry, and a failed one means tearing out the new tile. Replacing the valve behind the shower handle later means opening the wall it’s in. And the EPA advises a working exhaust fan vented straight outside, not into the attic, to clear the moisture that lets mold grow.

Ask the contractor to name the membrane, valve, and fan by brand and model in the quote, so none gets downgraded to lower the total.
