Nvidia’s chief executive, Jensen Huang, has been placed under “independent investigation” by the tech company after reports emerged he had been “the target of a foreign intelligence effort”, namely a close relationship ‌with a Chinese businesswoman who is accused of passing information to ‌Beijing. Nvidia stock is down 5% in pre-market trading as a result.

The company’s board said in a statement that while it had no reason to believe that either US national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, its inquiry would examine all available information. Republican lawmakers have expressed alarm, asking Huang to give the House foreign affairs and intelligence committees a closed-door briefing next week on Nvidia’s “security protocols against foreign adversary espionage”.

More than 90 heads of state and government, along with officials from close to 190 countries, are expected in Antalya this week for the latest round of UN climate talks. Against a backdrop of rapidly rising temperatures and extreme weather, with fractious geopolitics made worse by soaring inflation driven by the US-Israel war on Iran, the fortnight-long meeting will focus on “implementation” of the world’s targets to cut greenhouse gas emissions and help those stricken by the climate crisis.

Chris Bowen, Australia’s climate and energy minister and president of negotiations for Cop31, which his country is co-hosting with Turkey, told the Guardian: “We need a tangible outcome … that people can see is real. We need progress, it needs to be a step forward on previous Cops.”

Donald Trump set the stage this weekend to push his administration further right, claiming the US took a “giant step back” after voters widely rejected his party. Despite Republicans losing both chambers of Congress in Tuesday’s midterms – with voters repudiating key policies from sweeping tariffs to the war with Iran – the administration appears set to double down.

Yet Trump struck a contradictory note on immigration. Speaking on Sunday, he admitted his deportation policy was “too tough” and politically damaging, offering to negotiate a deal with Democrats to let some undocumented immigrants remain. That olive branch, however, clashed directly with his decision to elevate Stephen Miller, the architect of the crackdown, to White House chief of staff.

Polish prosecutors opened a terrorism investigation after the man opened fire on Saturday afternoon in an attack that shocked the country.
