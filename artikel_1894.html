Man City could let Gianluigi Donnarumma go on 'structured loan' deal - Transfer Expert

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Manchester City may have to explore the option of a subsidised loan if they want to offload struggling goalkeeper Gianluigi Donnarumma.

That is according to former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that few clubs would be able to afford a move for the Italian.

The 27-year-old is firmly out of form at the Etihad Stadium, and questions are being raised about his long-term future with the club.

Wyness believes that City may only be able to find a move away for Donnarumma if they can negotiate a "structured loan" deal with another club.

The gloveman's lofty wages are expected to pose a huge stumbling block to any potential permanent exit next summer.

Donnarumma started 34 Premier League games last term, and City's lack of an obvious in-house replacement means he is likely to be heavily relied upon once again, regardless of his poor form.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that City are likely to have to help fund the cost of any deal in order to offload Donnarumma.

The Scottish businessman feels that a loan move with Enzo Maresca's side contributing a significant chunk of his wages could be the best way forward.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained how City's options are limited when it comes to the shot-stopper.

"It'd have to be a structured loan where there's going to have to be a big part of his wages paid for by City. That would be the other option," he said.

"Look, it's difficult with goalkeepers. We all know he's been one of the world's greatest goalkeepers for a long time. And when you fall out of form as a keeper, it takes a while to get it back.

"Hopefully he can regain that confidence and get it back. It could be a loan spell that could do that. But it's certainly a very costly problem to have right now.

"They're going to have to take some pretty quick action. I'm sure in Europe there'll be a number of takers for Donnarumma given his pedigree and a belief that he can get himself back on track, and I'm sure he can."

Wyness told Football Insider earlier this week that a move to Saudi Arabia would have been a "certain" option for City and Donnarumma just a few months ago.

However, the Pro League have recently introduced a new cap on spending which limits clubs to spending just 75 per cent of their revneue on their first-team squads.

Wyness believes that now creates major doubt over the teams' ability to splash the cash on big-name stars from top European leagues.

He feels that Donnarumma would have been a prime candidate for a move of that ilk, but that option may not be on the table anymore.
