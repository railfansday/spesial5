Aston Villa and Newcastle United, two clubs with plenty in common.

Newcastle United winning a first major trophy in decades in 2025.

Aston Villa winning a first major trophy in decades in 2026.

In recent years Newcastle United getting into the Champions League not once, but twice, for the first time in decades.

In recent years Aston Villa getting into the Champions League not once, but twice, for the first time in decades (since the days it was called the European Cup).

The two clubs with proud histories also have another far bigger thing in common.

They are by far and away the two disruptor Premier League clubs, the pair who have tried hardest to break the modern day ‘Big Six’ financial stranglehold on long-term power and success.

Aston Villa and Newcastle United have flown closest to the Sun and suffered because of it, with UEFA SCR restrictions battering them into submission. If you don’t follow their warnings and get back in financial check, then face a ban from the Champions League (and other UEFA competitions) in the future.

It is all part of the endless vicious circle for clubs such as Aston Villa and Newcastle United who are attempting to narrow that yawning financial chasm that exists between themselves and the ‘Premier League Big Six’ who have been allowed to build such seemingly unchallengeable financial powerhouses across many years when Mike Ashley ran United into the ground and Villa suffered in many ways of their own.

This is the Champions League story of recent years for Aston Villa and Newcastle United.

2023-24 season Newcastle United compete in that season’s Champions League but Aston Villa don’t. Aston Villa qualify for next season’s Champions League but Newcastle United don’t.

2024-25 season Aston Villa compete in that season’s Champions League but Newcastle United don’t. Newcastle United qualify for next season’s Champions League but Aston Villa don’t.

2025-26 season Newcastle United compete in that season’s Champions League but Aston Villa don’t. Aston Villa qualify for next season’s Champions League but Newcastle United don’t.

2026-27 season Aston Villa compete in that season’s Champions League but Newcastle United don’t. Qualifying for…Aston Villa??? Newcastle United???

2026-27 season Competing/Qualifying for…Aston Villa??? Newcastle United???

The pattern couldn’t be clearer for Aston Villa and Newcastle United in recent years.

An ability every other year to qualify for the Champions League BUT in years when playing in the top European competition, unable to also qualify for the following season’s Champions League.

Four seasons in a row we have had either Newcastle United or Aston Villa disrupting the route of Premier League Big Six clubs to the Champions League BUT from where I am standing, it looks like that run is going to end now. I find it very unlikely that either Aston Villa or Newcastle United will qualify via the Premier League.

I think that now things have well and truly caught up with the two clubs and their ambitions.

I think to properly stand a chance of having a season where you can compete in the Champions League and attempt to qualify via the Premier League for the following season’s UCL competition, you need to have a squad with enough quality and depth to cope.

The financial rules make this all but impossible for Aston Villa and Newcastle United as things stand.

Wages are the most crippling thing, the Premier League Big Six are able to pay far bigger wages than Newcastle United and Aston Villa. It reaches a point where they can’t pay their star plays anything close to the market rate (what they can get elsewhere). It also means that to try and somehow have a large enough squad to cope with both Premier League and Champions League, you need to sells some/all of your best players so you can afford to bring in enough signings to try and give you the depth.

The reality in recent years when Aston Villa and Newcastle United have been trying to do both, compete in the Champions League AND qualify for next season’s, they have to play their small group of very best players far too often. This all but guarantees these star players will pick up injuries and/or get fatigued and their effectiveness is vastly diminished.

I think the perfect storm has now arrived.

All of the above issues have been impacted by the Premier League PSR and now SCR restrictions BUT even more so by the far stricter UEFA SCR restrictions on allowed spending on transfer fees and wages as a proportion of your revenues (UEFA allows 70% of ‘Adjusted operating revenue’).

I know that as football fans (especially those of Newcastle United and Aston Villa) we are heartily sick of all the accountancy boring side of things and how it constantly impacts the coverage of Premier League clubs these days.

To simply spell it out though, it basically means that if your revenues are twice as big as another club’s, you can spend twice as much on wages and transfer fees.

Roughly speaking, the likes of Arsenal, Liverpool, Man City and Man U have twice as big revenues as Aston Villa and Newcastle United have per season, whilst Chelsea and Tottenham are also way ahead AND have various other elements that give them an inbuilt advantage, which has been put in place across many years.

This past summer transfer window, in order to comply with UEFA SCR restrictions and avoid a future ban on taking part in UEFA competitions, both Villa and Newcastle had to take drastic action when it came to buying and selling players AND their wage bills.

It would have been unrealistic and reckless this summer for a club like Villa or Newcastle to agree to long-term huge wages of a level that ‘Big Six’ clubs can comfortably afford.

You then have Newcastle United and Aston Villa also having to sell at least some of their best players because they generally bring in the biggest instant benefit to a club’s accounts. The transfer fee agreed (less the player’s current book value) all goes into that current season’s accounts as a profit. However, when a player is bought the transfer fee that is agreed is spread over the length of his contract, when it comes to showing as a cost in the annual accounts. So if you sign a player for £50m on a five year contract, each season it shows as a £10m cost in the accounts. So basically if you sell a player for a big transfer fee it can help you afford a number of players for a total higher than that transfer fee BUT they still eventually have to be accounted for. Plus of course all Premier League clubs are constantly having those annual transfer fee costs for previous signings going into the accounts.

So what did Aston Villa and Newcastle United do in summer 2026?

They both sold a lot of their best and highest paid players to other clubs. In general, these players went to clubs who paid them far higher wages. Then Villa and Newcastle generally recruited players on lower transfer fees, many of who they also hope will improve in terms of value as well as ability in years to come.

Newcastle United lost the likes of Gordon, Trippier, Bruno and Tonali, having previously had to sell Isak and sacrifice Minteh and Anderson as well.

As well as previous players they had been forced to trade, this summer Aston Villa moved on the likes of Rogers, Watkins, Martinez, Digne, Konsa and Tielemans.

Interesting to see where many of these players are now at:

Bruno (Arsenal), Rogers (Chelsea), Isak (Liverpool), Tielemans (Man U), Tonali (Tottenham), Martinez (Chelsea), Anderson (Man City), Konsa (Arsenal)…

So not only are Aston Villa and Newcastle United forced to move on many of their best players in order to stay inside the UEFA and EPL SCR restrictions, they then see most of them end up at the very Premier League Big Six clubs that they are trying to compete with, against the odds.

When it comes to power and success long-term, it feels very much like tossing a coin and it is heads the Premier League Big Six win, whilst tails sees Villa and Newcastle lose.

Aston Villa and Newcastle United currently sit 12th and 13th in the Premier League table, from 11 attempts each they have won three EPL matches apiece.

Where might the pair have been instead if the financial restrictions didn’t leave them hamstrung?

What if they weren’t prevented from matching the wages that Premier League Big Six clubs are able to pay, were able to keep all of their best players AND bring in similar quality ones that would help persuade them to stay AND enable them to properly compete long-term?
