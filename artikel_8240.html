Georgia’s Entire 78-Mile Appalachian Trail Section Will Require Bear Canisters in 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

A hiker carries a bear canister on the Appalachian Trail. (Photo: Courtesy Appalachian Trail Conservancy)

Hikers on Georgia’s stretch of the Appalachian Trail will need to store their food in a hard-sided canister next year according to a new order from the U.S. Forest Service, a change that follows years of rising bear encounters on the southern miles of the AT.

In a press release, the USFS said that the rule would take effect January 1, and would apply to anyone camping in a 1-mile-wide corridor along the entire 78-mile section of the AT, including hikers staying in shelters. The requirement is year-round and also applies to the AT Approach Trail.

Alternative food-storage methods like bear hangs and Ursacks will not be allowed. Hikers who violate the order could face a fine of up to $5,000 or 6 months in jail.

“With the Appalachian Trail going through the heart of North Georgia’s black bear habitat, we want the hikers who flock here to understand how incredibly valuable these public lands are—for people and for wildlife,” Chattahoochee-Oconee National Forest Supervisor Judy Toppins said. “Finding a balance that protects hikers, wildlife, and forest health is our goal.”

As visitation to the Appalachian Trail has risen in recent years, human-bear conflicts have too, with a 2025 survey by the Appalachian Trail Conservancy finding that bear reports had risen by 75 percent that year alone. A disproportionate amount of those run-ins happen in Georgia: Morgan Sommerville, the ATC’s director of visitor use management, said that while just 3.5 percent of the AT lies in Georgia, the state accounted for more than 12 percent of its bear encounters.

The new requirements represent the largest expansion of food storage rules on the trail yet. Previously, only a few short segments required a canister, such as the 5-mile segment between Jarrard and Neel Gaps in Georgia’s Blood Mountain Wilderness. Hikers who didn’t want to tote a hard-sided container could simply avoid camping in many of those.

“The ATC has recommended that overnight campers on the Trail carry a bear-resistant food storage container since 2017 because of how adept bears have become at defeating traditional food hangs and other storage methods,” Somerville said. “This new order along the AT in Georgia will require all overnight hikers and backpackers to use reliable food storage gear—a hard-sided bear canister—on their AT adventures, which will protect themselves and the bears that call the AT home.”

Getting AT thru- and section-hikers to embrace the new guidelines could be a challenge. While the ATC has recommended for almost 10 years that hikers carry bear canisters, data suggests that few do. A 2025 survey by The Trek found that 13.6 percent used a canister, compared to 43 percent who used a bear hang and 32.3 percent who chose the riskiest option and slept with their food.
