Portugal’s new No 7 Forbs channels Ronaldo for winner as Wales are denied

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

One way or another, Portugal’s No 7 shirt has caused quite the commotion in recent weeks and months, if not years. Perhaps it was inevitable, then, that the first win of the post-Cristiano Ronaldo era would come courtesy of the player wearing the updated jersey, one with a cross through the number to preserve Ronaldo’s eternal legacy, as the Portuguese Football Federation put it. A sumptuous finish by Carlos Forbs, a second-half substitute, was ultimately enough to clinch victory on a topsy-turvy night with Portugal surviving Pedro Neto’s sending-off for two yellow cards.

For Wales, a Nations League relegation playoff double-header awaits in March, with Slovenia, Hungary, Republic of Ireland or Bosnia and Herzegovina – who knocked them out of World Cup qualifying this year – all potential opponents in Friday’s draw in Switzerland. Afterwards Craig Bellamy stressed the importance of keeping elite company to accelerate improvement but reiterated that he will not be in the job beyond Euro 2028, after which his contract expires.

“If we want to develop as a ­football nation and continue to move ­forward, we have to be playing against the best teams,” Bellamy said. “It’s only going to improve us. I was the best manager going in [Group] B, finished top … how good am I? Now I’m in [Group] A, we’ve played quite well, but I’m crap. It’s about playing the best teams, that’s how we’re going to get better. I won’t be here for the next Nations League, I won’t be the manager then, so I’m doing it for the next one.”

This was a welcome victory for ­Portugal, who advanced to the quarter-finals as group winners after a bruising 4-1 home defeat by Denmark in Braga. Turn a new page and all that. There were plenty of positive takeaways for Wales too. David Brooks cancelled out Bruno Fernandes’s opener and Wales seized the lead through Sorba Thomas, who applied the ­finish after a slick team move. But Neto, who picked up two bookings inside two minutes, the second for a foul on Brennan Johnson, levelled before half-time. Bellamy’s side were fun and, at times, freewheeling but Forbs had the final say with a neat run and finish.

Bellamy stressed the importance of locking down Vitinha and João Neves, two of his favourite players, but Wales did not allow the Paris Saint-Germain pair to have things all their own way. It was a ludicrously open game and, approaching the half-time interval at 2-2, a stray Renato Veiga pass supplied the all-action Harry Wilson with a speculative opportunity from halfway. Wilson intercepted the ball and, after taking a touch to roll it into the opposition half, the Leeds midfielder launched a marvellous left-foot lobbed effort towards goal, his 45-yard shot skimming the roof of Diogo Costa’s goal net.

Portugal may have levelled a few moments earlier, Neto easily beating Jay Dasilva in the six-yard box to prod in Francisco Trincão’s brilliant cross, but Wilson’s attempt at the audacious was another gentle reminder of Portugal’s precarious state. After suffering their heaviest competitive home defeat against Denmark on Saturday, Portugal were again susceptible defensively. Before Thomas capped a scintillating team move to seize the lead on 40 minutes, Wilson twice went close, Diogo Dalot making a desperate block for the first.

This was an entertaining contest for which it was worth braving the winter conditions. When Fernandes picked his spot on 11 minutes, sending an unerring strike into the top corner, Portugal appeared in the groove. But Wales responded two minutes later when Brooks, who scored with a superb dinked finish in Norway, dug out another clever effort. Wilson saw a shot repelled by Costa but when the ball squirted free Rubin Colwill had the presence of mind to calmly square for Brooks, who squeezed a left-foot shot into the far corner. Colwill, a fan favourite here for Cardiff, thoroughly enjoyed his first Wales start under Bellamy.

Colwill impressed in a deep midfield role alongside Josh Sheehan of Bolton, whose former clubmate Aaron Collins, now of MK Dons, arrived from the bench midway through the second half for his debut. Portugal, meanwhile, introduced João Félix and the Club Brugge winger Forbs, the latter wearing the tweaked No 7 shirt that will always be ­synonymous with Ronaldo. An unmarked Ben Cabango, who replaced Joe Rodon as a precaution at half-time, went close to earning Wales the lead with a header. The weary look on Costa’s face as he pawed Cabango’s header away from goal for a corner said it all.

The moment Forbs surged on to a loose Cabango header and then at Davies before applying a low finish provided light relief on an uncomfortable night. For Wales, a challenging campaign ended as it began, with a heartening defeat by Portugal.

“Look, it’s a £1.2bn squad against a £170m squad,” Bellamy said. “I live in the real world. I’m beyond happy. I couldn’t ask for more. Against a top team, if you make a couple of mistakes as we did, you will get punished. We’ve offered a really good version of ourselves, we’ve been a massive part of this group. It’s just about those fine margins.”
