Paul Finebaum says 'there are enough votes' for SEC to kick out LSU

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

On Thursday, Dec. 3, SEC presidents and chancellors will meet to evaluate LSU's continued membership in the conference after the university has been engaged in several high-profile player eligibility standoffs with the league over the past several months.

According to one of the most prominent voices in college football, it may be more than just bluster.

During an appearance on ESPN's "Get Up" on Tuesday, Nov. 24, longtime SEC and college football pundit Paul Finebaum said "there are enough votes" to boot LSU from the league of which it is a founding member.

Under SEC rules, a vote of at least two-thirds of the league's other 15 presidents is required to end a school's membership.

"They have the votes to kick LSU out," Finebaum said. "There's a lot of murmur in that room to go ahead and do it. The consequences are massive. We've never seen anything quite like this before."

While he believes there's a possibility it could happen, Finebaum added that it's still unlikely, at least based on conversations he's had. Still, even if LSU remains in the league, he believes the Tigers could face harsh penalties.

"Most people don't think it will happen, but something serious is going to happen, whether they get kicked out or whether there's a suspension or an expulsion of the coach," he said. "Nobody really knows for sure, but the SEC is not going to let this go on any longer."

It's not the first time the conference has threatened to expel LSU.

In September, the SEC scheduled a vote to decide whether to kick the Tigers out of the league after LSU football attempted to add two players, tight end Dae'Quan Wright and defensive lineman Zxavian Harris, who had signed NFL contracts and spent time in training camps after playing for new Tigers head coach Lane Kiffin at Mississippi during the 2025 season.

The Tigers never officially added either player to their roster, though, so the expulsion vote never occurred.

The most recent question over expulsion centers around the LSU men's basketball program and coach Will Wade. Over the offseason, shortly after Wade was brought back for his second tenure leading the program, the Tigers got a commitment from RJ Luis, the 2025 Big East player of the year who had signed NBA contracts with the Utah Jazz and Boston Celtics. After Luis received a preliminary injunction from a Louisiana judge that barred the NCAA or SEC from declaring him ineligible, he was added to the team's roster and even played in a game, scoring 11 points and pulling down nine rebounds in a Nov. 18 win against Louisiana Monroe.

One day later, LSU president Wade Rousseau released a statement saying he instructed Wade not to play Luis.

Luis' participation in the game violated a rule the SEC passed in August barring member institutions from having athletes on the roster who had previously signed contracts with or declared for the drafts for the NBA, NFL or WNBA. The measure allowed SEC commissioner Greg Sankey to enforce the rule with full authority, which included penalizing offenders.

"This is not a bluff," Finebaum said Tuesday. "We are officially at DEFCON 1 and the question is why. The answer is Will Wade."
