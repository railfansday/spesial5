Has Rasul Douglas been Adam Peters’ best off season addition?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Following an extended time period on injured reserve for 2025 second-round pick Trey Amos, the Washington Commanders added Rasul Douglas late in free agency, signing the veteran just before the start of training camp in July. At the time, there were multiple questions surrounding the addition of a soon-to-be 31-year-old cornerback for a team that desperately needed playmakers in the secondary.

Douglas worked behind fellow cornerbacks Mike Sainristil and Amik Robertson to start the season, and while he only played 38% of the team's total defensive snaps in Week 1, Rasul immediately made a mark with his physical play style.

Eventually, Douglas would take over a full-time role for the Commanders' defense in Week 4, logging 85% of the total defensive snaps and recording 5 tackles, 1 assist, 2 stops, 1 interception, and 1 pass defended versus the Colts. Post-game, Rasul proved his fit for Washington's competitive culture, stating the secondary's improved performance was not good enough, as the team didn't win the matchup. The following week, Douglas backed his words with his own play, finishing second on the team with 9 tackles and 1 assist while being graded with 4 receptions for 39 receiving yards allowed on 9 targets by Pro Football Reference.

Trey Amos returned to the lineup in Week 6, and while his return, along with the addition of safety Nick Cross, has been attributed to the improvement of the Commanders' secondary, Douglas' performance as a third cornerback cannot be overlooked. Since Week 6 in San Francisco, Washington's defense has given up just 18.5 points per game and the secondary has not allowed more than one passing touchdown in each of those contests. While that stretch did include games against the struggling Eagles and last week's road trip to New York, it also included games against the 49ers and Rams. Through Week 10, per @SFdata9ers, Douglas has a league-low 39.6 allowed passer rating while also leading the team in interceptions with 3, including a game-sealing grab at Metlife Stadium.

Identified in the off-season as a potential fit for Daronte Jones' defensive scheme, in addition to being a ball-hawk, Douglas has been a sure tackler, recording 29 total tackles (22 solo) on the season and 7 passes defended; just 2 behind full-time starter Amik Robertson. With Robertson's availability in question for Monday night's matchup against the Cincinnati Bengals' elite passing offense, Douglas may be called on to prove his value yet again, but fans may take comfort in the fact the Commanders' cornerback ranks 9th on Pro Football Network's cornerback grading list headed into Week 11.

What is the best part of Rasul Douglas' performance thus far?

He was signed to a 1-year, $1.8 million agreement with only $580k of guaranteed money and up to $2 million available in incentives. Those numbers rank him 95th on Spotrac's average cornerback contract rankings, yet halfway through the season, Douglas is playing at a top ten level. That makes the free agent addition arguably the best of Adam Peters' off season acquisitions so far and a player the Washington Commanders should consider keeping around next season.
