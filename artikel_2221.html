How much West Ham paid to sack Nuno Espirito Santo after missing the chance to do it for free

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

West Ham made the decision to sack Nuno Espirito Santo on Monday, after a 3-1 home defeat to West Brom. WHUFC have acted quickly to hire Scott Parker as their new boss, but they had to pay a sizeable compensation fee to part ways with Nuno.

The Athletic states that the Hammers paid around £2m to relieve the Portuguese boss of his duties. This is after having the option to do so for nothing after their relegation from the Premier League was confirmed in May.

New manager Parker had the support of board members as early as May, before opting to stick by Nuno. Mark Noble was among the 46-year-old’s strongest backers but Dutch coach John Heitinga was also considered as a potential replacement.

As soon as the decision was made to stick by Nuno in the summer, the pressure was on for the West Ham boss. He failed to keep them in the Premier League, but there was hope he could get them back there having led Wolves to Championship promotion in impressive fashion in 2017/18.

There were encouraging signs, as the Hammers rose as high as top of the table during a run of 10 Championship games unbeaten. They scored for fun too, scoring 30 times in the first 11 matches, including big wins over Wrexham, Lincoln City (both 6-0), Derby County (3-0) and Wolves (4-2).

However, frustrations over the style of football continued with cautious play in possession persisting despite WHUFC’s star power in attack. Defensive concerns persisted too, and when the warning signs led to defeats against Southampton and west Ham, the crowd had turned on Nuno.

United are ultimately made to pay for sticking by Nuno in the summer – £2m to be exact. The appointment of Parker for nothing limits the expense of this managerial change, but when the Portuguese boss could have gone in the summer for nothing, it has proven to be a costly decision by the club.

West Ham’s appointment of Parker has led to a mixed response. Many are pleased to see a former Hammers star and three-time Championship promotion-winning manager join the club, but there are some reservations over his style of play.

Like Nuno, Parker’s tactics in recent jobs have been built on a solid defensive foundation, showing a willingness to let the opposition have the ball, and when his sides do have it, playing conservatively.

However, Parker has adapted his tactics over time. While in charge of Fulham, his side had the second-highest average possession in the Championship with 60.8% (Fotmob), and they went on to win the league. They also recorded the highest xG (90.4 – 20 more than any other team), most big chances created (136) and most touches in the opposition box (1,352).

It will be intriguing to see if Parker reverts to this, or sticks by the defence-first tactics that saw him win promotion with Burnley after conceding only 16 goals in 46 matches.

WHUFC supporters want promotion at any cost, but the criticisms of Nuno’s style of play suggest they would rather brings more forward-thinking and controlling football.
