Peppermint oil significantly lowers blood pressure in just 20 days

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Peppermint oil, best known for its refreshing flavor and soothing effects on digestion, may also offer a surprising benefit for people with mildly elevated blood pressure.

Researchers at the University of Lancashire found that consuming a small amount of peppermint oil each day for just 20 days significantly reduced systolic blood pressure (the top number in a blood pressure reading). Participants who received peppermint oil had systolic readings that were an average of 8.5 mmHg lower than those who received a placebo after accounting for their starting blood pressure.

The findings, published in the journal PLOS One, suggest that peppermint oil could provide a simple, inexpensive, and well-tolerated way to help manage mildly elevated blood pressure.

Lead author Dr. Jonnie Sinclair, Reader in Sport and Health Sciences, said: "High blood pressure is one of the biggest causes of heart disease and death worldwide, and it costs a huge amount of money to treat. Although medicines are commonly used to treat it, it's not always clear how well they work in the long-term, and they can cause unwanted side effects."

How Peppermint Oil May Lower Blood Pressure

Peppermint naturally contains several biologically active compounds, including menthol, which gives the plant its distinctive cooling sensation, and flavonoids, a group of plant compounds known for their antioxidant properties.

Peppermint also has anti-inflammatory and vasodilatory properties, meaning it may help blood vessels relax and widen. When blood vessels widen, blood can flow more easily, potentially reducing the pressure exerted against artery walls. These properties may help explain peppermint oil's effects on blood pressure, although the precise mechanisms require further investigation.

To examine these potential benefits, researchers recruited 40 adults between the ages of 18 and 65 with elevated blood pressure or stage 1 hypertension, an early stage of high blood pressure. Participants were randomly assigned to one of two groups.

One group consumed a total of 100 microliters of food-grade peppermint oil each day, equivalent to one-tenth of a milliliter, divided into two doses diluted in water. The other group received a peppermint-flavored placebo that contained none of the active peppermint oil compounds.

Significant Blood Pressure Improvements in Just 20 Days

After 20 days, average systolic blood pressure among participants taking peppermint oil fell from approximately 130 to 122 mmHg. Meanwhile, blood pressure remained virtually unchanged in the placebo group.

Systolic blood pressure measures the force of blood against artery walls when the heart contracts. Persistently elevated readings can damage blood vessels over time and increase the risk of serious health problems, including heart attacks, strokes, and heart failure.

Although systolic blood pressure was the primary focus of the study, researchers also examined body measurements, blood test results, diastolic blood pressure (the bottom number in a blood pressure reading), resting heart rate, mental well-being, and sleep quality.

A Promising, Low-Cost Approach to High Blood Pressure

Dr. Sinclair added: "Our findings were very positive and they have significant clinical implications, especially given arterial hypertension is the most common preventable risk factor for cardiometabolic disease and the greatest single risk factor for global mortality.

"Peppermint oil is low in calories and price so it's proved to be a very simple and cost-effective solution to potentially treat millions of people around the world."

While the results are encouraging, the study involved a relatively small number of participants and lasted less than three weeks. Larger and longer clinical trials will be needed to determine whether the benefits persist over time and whether peppermint oil could complement existing blood pressure treatments.

The researchers used peppermint oil specifically intended for human consumption. The findings should not be interpreted as a reason to replace prescribed blood pressure medications or ingest essential oils that are not formulated for oral use.
