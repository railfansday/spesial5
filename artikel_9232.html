Kindness in Store

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The most trusted resource for mindfulness and meditation.

New contributor John Ptacek offers a lyric reflection on how small acts of kindness in public spaces highlight our longing for connection and our capacity to extend compassion even in difficult times.

We behave rather well in grocery stores, wouldn’t you say?

There we all are, from different backgrounds, in different modes of dress, shopping for different dietary preferences, moving along at different speeds, some clutching baskets, others pushing carts, wending in and out of aisles and finally assuming our place in the checkout line without crashing into each other. Good show!

In politically divisive times, the grocery store can feel like an oasis of civility.

Sometimes our behavior is exemplary, like when some poor soul accidentally sets off an avalanche of oranges by extracting the exact wrong one from the mountainous display, and one or two of us scramble into action, scooping them up and cheerfully placing them back in formation. Or when one of us notices someone behind us in the checkout line with just an item or two in hand and offers to let them go ahead.
