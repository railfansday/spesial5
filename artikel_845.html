Max Dowman shines in England colours and sends another record tumbling

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Midfielder’s under-21s winner eclipsed Jude Bellingham’s mark and there was other talent to admire against France

There may have been an Mbappé on the pitch – Ethan, the younger brother of Kylian, with whom he shares a near-identical gait – but by the end there was only one precocious talent on the lips of those watching England overcome France in their under-21s clash. Max Dowman had done it again, lighting up another game, this time in filthy conditions at Ashton Gate. It was fitting he had the final say in a 3-2 win. The noise that accompanied his match-winning goal? Well, beyond the palpable excitement in the stands in Bristol, that would be the sound of the teenager sending yet another record tumbling.

Which one takes your fancy? Take your pick. At 14, playing for Arsenal Under-19s, he became the Uefa Youth League’s youngest goalscorer. This time last year, after taking to the field at Slavia Prague aged 15 and 308 days, Dowman became the youngest player to feature in the Champions League. In March, six months on from his professional debut, he became the Premier League’s youngest goalscorer. He is the youngest player to earn a Premier League winner’s medal. Last month he was presented with three Guinness World Records.

In scoring his first under-21s goal, in the penultimate minute of regular time, Dowman eclipsed Jude Bellingham to become the team’s youngest scorer at 16 years and 320 days, three days after surpassing the Real Madrid midfielder’s record as the under-21s’ youngest player. Bellingham was almost four months older than Dowman when he scored his first goal for the age group, in September 2020. Bellingham made only four appearances for the under-21s, a result of being fast-tracked into the senior team, and Dowman could emulate him again, though he is expected to feature in next summer’s Under-21 European Championship in Albania and Serbia.

The common view from coaches and teammates, and something stressed by Lee Carsley, the under-21s head coach, is that it is easy to forget Dowman is six weeks shy of his 17th birthday. Against France, it was also extremely easy to overlook that this was Dowman’s first start for the under-21s, after a substitute appearance against Portugal last Friday. His first action set the tone on Monday, using his body to box off the ball then jinking up the right flank, zipping it through the legs of Dayann Methalie before skating forward and sending the Sunderland left-back juddering on to his backside.

Dowman exhibited plenty of neat touches, and a couple of effortless first-time passes, but those who have watched him develop say his work rate and willingness to do the other side of the game are among his biggest strengths.

“We look at Max and see how good he is on the ball, how attacking he is, but I thought he was outstanding off the ball,” Carsley said, presumably thinking of the moment Dowman scampered deep into his own half to intercept a stray France pass. He was not finished after scoring either. At one point he had five blue shirts haring after him as he stormed into the France half and, seconds later, his quick feet lured the opposition captain, Nathan Zézé, into a foul on the edge of the France penalty area.

Dowman is among the next wave of players pushing to step up to Thomas Tuchel’s squad, perhaps at its forefront. It is a well-trodden path and the current crop need not look too far for inspiration. Elliot Anderson and Jarell Quansah, both of whom started England’s triumph at the last under-21 Euros in Bratislava, began Sunday’s memorable victory for the seniors in Madrid. Jobe Bellingham and Josh King are the latest players to make the transition into Tuchel’s squad and more will follow.

Jack Hinshelwood and Trey Nyoni, of Brighton and Liverpool respectively, impressed at the base of midfield and the 19-year-old Mikey Moore, another to score on his first start for the under-21s, shone down the left flank, opening the scoring. The Everton midfielder Harrison Armstrong entered as a substitute and supplied Dowman’s winner, and Armstrong’s clubmate Tyrique George also showed promise after replacing Moore. Archie Gray captained the under-21s against Portugal but did not feature on Monday, with Carsley revealing he has spoken to the Tottenham midfielder about Scotland wanting him to switch allegiance. “I just said to him: ‘Keep me in the picture, if it’s something that you were thinking of,’” Carsley said. “I think Archie is very connected to our pathway.”

England’s budding talent is not confined to the under-21s. Carsley took a closer look at Brian Madjo, the imposing 17-year-old striker who scored on his Aston Villa debut in the Super Cup in August, the Manchester United defender Ayden Heaven and the West Brom forward Jimmy-Jay Morgan, who signed from Chelsea in the summer, before his squad departed for Bristol last week, the trio joining the under-21s for four days of training at St George’s Park.

Dowman, after two under-21 appearances comprising a little more than 120 minutes, is putting the squeeze on. “Max is doing something special for his age and he is pushing people a lot older than him,” said Moore, who is on loan from Tottenham at Köln and hopes to face his idol Harry Kane and Bayern Munich on Friday. “He’s a top kid. You kind of forget how old he is. My little brother is 17 and he [Dowman] feels older to me than my little brother … He has been top and everywhere he goes, he just takes that next step.”
