England get one-match suspended ban on away tickets for anti-pope chants in Spain

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Uefa also fines FA £12,500 for offensive chants

England have been handed a ­suspended ­one-match ban on away ticket sales by Uefa in relation to “­racist and/or ­discriminatory” behaviour by fans at Sunday’s 3-2 Nations League victory over Spain. The punishment is suspended for two years.

Uefa has also fined the Football Association €15,000 (£12,500), with the behaviour in question believed to be the chant of “Fuck the pope and the IRA” at the ­Metropolitano ­Stadium in Madrid. A further charge in the next two years could trigger a ban on away fans for a competitive game.

In a statement given to the Mail, the FA said: “We will not ­tolerate this unacceptable and deeply ­offensive chanting, and will work with the police who attend all ­England home and away matches to identify the perpetrators and take the necessary action.”

Uefa’s control, ­ethics and ­disciplinary body announced that it had decided “to fine the English ­Football ­Association €15,000 and to order against the ­English Football Association a full ­restriction on selling tickets to its ­supporters for one representative team competition away match, for the ­racist and/or discriminatory ­behaviour of its supporters.

“Said full restriction on selling tickets to supporters for one away match is suspended for a ­probationary period of two years, starting from the date of the present decision.”

The relevant article of Uefa’s disciplinary regulations reads: “If one or more of a member association or club’s supporters engage in the behaviour described in paragraph 1 [insulting the human dignity of a person or group of persons on whatever grounds, including skin colour, race, religion, ethnic origin, gender or ­sexual orientation], the ­member association or club ­responsible incurs a minimum of a fine and either a ­partial stadium closure or a ­restriction on selling tickets to its supporters for away matches.”

The match had been preceded by Thomas Tuchel reminding his players of the anti-­racism protocols, given the last match between the sides in Madrid, in 2004, had notoriously been marred by abuse aimed at England’s black players.

Last month Spain were fined £15,000 and ordered to play their next friendly with a partial stadium closure after discriminatory chants about Muslims during March’s match against Egypt in Barcelona.

Sunday’s chant is a staple for some England fans but sung relatively rarely in stadiums. Several more frequently heard chants target only the IRA, including inserting “No surrender” into the national anthem.
