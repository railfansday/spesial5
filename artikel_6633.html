Joseph Zada of The Hunger Games Is Still Getting Used to Being a Movie Star

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Joseph Zada is doing “very, very, very good,” he tells me, with a heavy emphasis on the third very and an audible exclamation mark. And why shouldn’t he be?

His much-anticipated film, The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping, finally comes out this week. Zada and I spoke the morning of his New York City premiere, while he was being shuttled from one press obligation to the next, already go-go-go before actually hitting the red carpet Monday night in custom Louis Vuitton, punctuated with a pair of gold-toed cowboy boots from Pharrell’s first collection for the brand.

In the film, Zada plays a young Haymitch Abernathy, who we follow through his own Hunger Games, some twenty-five years ahead of the events of the original trilogy. When asked about the movie, the 21-year-old Australian demonstrates palpable care and excitement, even a bit of sadness that it’s over. “I just think that it was the most I’ve had to prepare and the most support that I’ve had and the most that I’ve ever stepped into a character’s shoes,” Zada says. “And it was just such a joy to act in this and to feel what it was like to really be this character.”

Since being cast, he’s barely had a second to stop and reflect on joining one of the biggest franchises in the world—and this comes right on the heels of East of Eden, too, in which Zada played another haunted young man. He laughs when I note the comparison: “I guess I just pride myself on my ability to show my vulnerability.”

Below, read our full conversation with the emerging star as he reflects on his Hunger Games journey, talks red carpet style, and shares his thoughts on Robert Pattinson playing James Bond.

Joseph Zada: Yeah, I wonder if I’ll be nervous.

Maybe towards the start. It doesn’t get old… but it gets familiar.

Well, I did SiriusXM this morning, and then, I mean, it’s basically just been like, "Hey, do press all day and then go to your premiere." So there’s not much time for a ritual, so I guess I go a little bit nonverbal and half-dissociate.

No, it’s so much fun. I have a lot of fun at these premieres. It’s always like a crazy, crazy crowd. The way they’ve set it up has just been otherworldly, and I’m not sure if I’ll ever have it again, but I distinctly remember each stop and the feelings that I’ve had thus far, and it doesn’t get old. It’s always crazy to see this many fans and the passion they have for this project. I mean, it’s not every day that you get to step into something with a built-in fan base where people are already coming out and supporting it before they’ve even seen it. So it’s a really special experience, and I don’t know when I’m going to be able to get that again.

Where did I have the most fun? I had a lot of fun in London because my little sister was there, my little sister and my brother’s girlfriend, so I got to hang out with them and I haven’t seen them in a while. And Mexico. Mexico was the craziest event that we’ve had. There were literally thousands, several thousands of people there. The reception was incredible.

Am I allowed to drop this? Oh, yes, that is what this [interview] is for. I’m wearing a Louis Vuitton suit—custom, custom—that Jason [Bolden] and I have worked together on, and I’m a big fan of the boots. The boots are pretty dope.

I feel a lot more comfortable in these clothes nowadays, and it’s actually become a lot more enjoyable to put them on. And it feels like more of an armor and less of a scary thing, and it feels like I build a lot of confidence being able to do this.

My red carpet style, I think... I don’t know. Just whatever Jason puts me in for the night.

Yeah, I think just doing it so often and finding different looks and just being able to wear all the different brands and find what I’m comfortable in.

I pride myself on my ability to have vulnerability. I think as a young man, that’s what I find interesting in these performances of these young men coming of age, and I think that was really what I was just looking for in my early career. I modeled my career on people like Timothée Chalamet and Lucas Hedges, and I love the way that they kept their heart on their sleeve and how they feel like an open nerve. I love being able to portray that in my performances.

I just think that it was the most I’ve had to prepare and the most support that I’ve had and the most that I’ve ever stepped into a character’s shoes and really, really felt like I was there every single day and just had the most incredible support in terms of my cast mates and my directors and my producers. And it was just such a joy to act in this and to feel what it was like to really be this character.

I guess everything just kind of changed just like that. Everything just changed in one quick second, and it feels like... I guess I haven’t really thought about what it was like before. I’ve just kind of been trying to ride the wave. I’m sure being able to go back home for Christmas, I’ll have some time to reflect, but right now it’s just—what’s the day ahead of me? And just making sure I’m ready for all of that.

I’m just excited to be at home every single day and watch movies with my mom and my siblings and see all my friends. That’s what I’m really excited for. I’m just excited to also just turn off my phone for a little bit, maybe go on a little road trip down to South Australia and get a little beach house and not bring my phone and have six days where I’m just off the grid.

That’s a great question. I’ve got Apocalypse Now on 4K Ultra HD on my projector that I bought at home, and I cannot wait. I feel like people ask me what was the best part of my year, and I just start naming all the movies that I’ve watched. And genuinely the happiest that I ever am is when I’m watching a film, which is kind of sad but also kind of cool.

I think... I don’t know. I loved Wild Horse Nine. That’s a favorite I’ve watched in cinemas. I loved Primetime too, because I love Rob Pattinson. I love Skyler Gisondo.

I did just see the Bond casting news! I am chuffed. That is so exciting. That is beyond exciting. He is just the most phenomenal actor, and he really just makes the most interesting choices. And I think him in that role, the stoicism that he’ll bring along with those really interesting choices, it’s going to make that character so interesting. He’s a really special actor. It feels like every young male actor that I speak to feels like that is a person to look to right now and a person’s career to emulate in terms of the risks and the commitment to his craft and all these roles that he’s churning out.

I don’t know. It’s so ridiculously helpful to have a book with you because it’s just so much information, and I love preparing for roles and having a book because it’s just an internal monologue, which is what you should be doing for every character. You read a scene, and you should be writing what is going through your character’s head. And when you have a book, it’s all done for you, or at least you have a good foundation.

I hope I get to do as many book adaptations as possible, but I think what I’m really looking for is just a great director and a great script. I’m not sure what’s [next]. I’ve got a fresh slate right now, but there’s a couple of things floating around.
