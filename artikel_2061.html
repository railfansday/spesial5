Newcastle United "would love" the chance to tie right-back Tino Livramento down to a new contract, sources have told Football Insider.

The 23-year-old is currently out injured, but the Magpies have no doubts about his ability once he is back fit.

The England international is heading towards the end of his deal in the summer of 2028, but Newcastle are hoping to agree an extension before the end of the season.

Sources say that he is still viewed as a key player on Tyneside, despite his consistent availability issues.

Livramento was only fit enough to start 14 games in the Premier League last term, and has played just 33 league minutes so far in 2026-27.

However, Newcastle are prepared to look past that issue long-term, and are hoping to strike an agreement for a new deal to protect their asset.

Livramento's absence for Newcastle has been eased a little by the impressive performances of Amar Dedic of late, but he is still a big miss for them.

The former Chelsea academy graduate is capable of playing in either full-back position and has excelled in both roles at times in recent years.

Livramento remains extremely highly regarded in the North East, while he has also had sustained England recognition at senior level.

The defender was named in Thomas Tuchel's squad for the World Cup, but was ultimately unable to take part due to injury.

His reputation has carried over all of the injury issues, and Newcastle are prepared to keep him around for several years to come.

The club are hoping to have an agreement in place sooner rather than later, but that will depend on the player's desire to extend.

Former Sunderland chief scout Mick Brown told Football Insider on Tuesday that the Newcastle board are still "firm" in their backing of manager Matthias Jaissle.

The German has been under intense pressure from the fans in recent weeks amid a run of just one win in their last five games in all competitions.

However, Brown feels that the Newcastle hierarchy aren't "worried" about their season just yet, and are prepared to give Jaissle the time he needs to turn things around.

The North East outfit lost several key players in the summer and overhauled their squad, and the club are aware that the issues won't be solved overnight.
