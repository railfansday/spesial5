Sheffield United's Jordan Ayew on the pressures and pleasures of being a national icon in Ghana

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Ayew had an eventful international break as the Black Stars completed home-and-away victories in their African Cup of Nations qualifiers against Somalia.

Ghana’s captain scored the winning penalty in Monday's 1-0 win, but only after having a spot kick saved in the 2-0 home success four days earlier.

It puts the country back on track to qualify for a competition they have won four times after opening with consecutive defeats.

The 35-year-old, who made his 125th appearance in Thursday's game, is his country's most-capped player and this year became the oldest person to represent them at a World Cup. It brings plenty of hero worship, but also great expectation.

Ayew is the son of legendary Ghana player Abedi Pele, and brother of Andre, whose cap record he broke this year. Pele, a European Cup winner with Marseille in 1993, is regarded as one of Africa's all-time greats.

"It comes with a lot of pressure, a lot of expectations as well, but we play football to set examples for the next generation," said Ayew of his trips to play for his country.

"I try to do best, even for my kids, for my nephews, my nieces. I try to set examples for the young ones in Ghana that if you believe in your dream, it's possible.

"I'm living my dream. I'm 35 and I'm playing in the Championship. For me, it's a big achievement, and I hope I can keep going as long as I can but I'm not clueless.

"I am very smart to understand that I'm setting the right example for the young ones back home, and for me, that is the most important thing, to let people know that if you believe in your dream, anything is possible.

"You have ups and downs, but you never have to give up because in life, things change very quickly."

On the pressures of his family background, Ayew said: "I've been in that since I was maybe 12, and expectations back home were very big – a bit too much, but it is what it is.

"I think so far I've given a good account of myself. There's more to come, in my opinion.

"Everything is in God's hands so I try to do my best on the pitch, and give myself the best opportunity to perform every time for club and country."

The World Cup could have been a perfect send-off for Ayew, especially considering he did not get picked up in the summer transfer window following his release by Leicester City. But he persevered and was signed as a free agent by Sheffield United in September, and has no plans to retire yet.

"The fire is still burning," he insists. "The day the fire stops burning, then I'll just hang up my boots.

"When you get to this stage, it's day by day and everything that comes, you take it and you try to do your best.

"I still have the energy, I still have the strength, I still have the belief that I can help both my club and in Ghana as well.

"But at the minute, I think I'm still important to the team in Ghana."
