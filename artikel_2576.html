7 things we learned from the Broncos 38-24 win over the Raiders

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The win over the Las Vegas Raiders was a bit emotional.

Winning is always a great feeling, especially one over the Raiders.

But prior to kickoff of Sunday's game, we got word that legendary Denver Broncos linebacker, Ring of Famer, and Hall of Fame broadcaster Tom Jackson had died on Friday at age 75.

There's no doubt that Jackson would have loved the Broncos beating Las Vegas as they did on Sunday, 38-24. And any win over the Raiders is a great win, especially one that bumps Denver's record to 8-2 and puts them in a great spot for the AFC Playoffs. Denver leads the AFC West and is the No. 2 seed in the AFC.

Here's what we learned from Sunday's win.

What's been clear the last few games is now crystal clear. The Broncos have their running back in rookie Jonah Coleman.

It's clear that Coleman brings an element to Denver's offense that makes it, well, run.

The rookie had not one, not two, but three touchdowns in the Broncos' win over Las Vegas. Coleman also finished with a game-high 111 yards rushing on 24 carries for an average of 4.6 yards per carry. He also added four receptions for 18 yards. Coleman is also the first Broncos running back to rush for 100 or more yards in 19 games.

As the season heads to the stretch run, Denver needs to keep feeding Coleman.

When the Broncos defense needs a big play, it's usually Ja'Quan McMillian who rises to the occasion.

The Denver cornerback did so again on Sunday with a game-sealing pick-six. And it was a helluva catch by McMillian.

For Sunday's game, McMillian finished with eight tackles (four solo) and one pass defended.

Whatever the reason, and it's likely a combination of factors, Denver is now susceptible to big plays. There was the long touchdown reception to Ashton Jeanty, yet just a few plays before that, the Broncos had the Raiders pinned inside their 5-yard line on second-and-20. Las Vegas picked up the first down.

On the drive that cut Denver's lead to a touchdown, the defense allowed a 39-yard reception.

In the last two games, the Broncos have overcome this. But it's not something they want to continue, especially defensive coordinator Vance Joseph, who has said he wants this corrected.

But more than anything, he's just damn fun to watch play the game. Allen has some J.J. Watt in him, and not just because of the arm brace.

Allen's stat line for Sunday's game shows he does everything.

He had three tackles (one solo). .5 sacks, 3 passes defended, and two QB hits.

If all you do is look at the stats from Sunday's game, you lose all context for how well Nix played.

The Broncos quarterback finished 19-for-35 passing for 225 yards with a touchdown and an interception (a hail mary at the end of the first half).

But Nix looked and played like the QB we saw last season. He was sharp on his reads. He took shots down the field. And if not for some key drops, he would have had a few more touchdowns and close to 100 more yards.

Paired with Coleman being the running back and unlocking Engram. Denver's offense is getting better and better.

There was no bigger or greater Raider Hater than Jackson. One of the key cogs of the Orange Crush Defense hated the Raiders. That hatred survived his days at ESPN.

To get the win over the team Jackson despised was a fitting tribute.

And throughout the day, you saw the impact he had on the game and the broadcast industry through the heartfelt messages and words people shared.

But don't lose sight of the impact he had on the Broncos. Without No. 57, there is no Orange Crush.

He was vital to that magical 1977 season.

And I don't know if it was the dust or some unknown allergies, but my eyes got misty when the video surfaced of Payton paying tribute to Jackson, his wife, and his family.

We believe, TJ. And you're a big reason why.

Thank you for the memories on the field and your magical pairing with Chris Berman on NFL Primetime.
