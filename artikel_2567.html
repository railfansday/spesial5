The four Bills integral to Thanksgiving vs. Chiefs

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The time has arrived. An old foe is in their way another time.

Kansas City has scored at least 34 points in three straight games, and Buffalo's defense has not allowed a touchdown since the middle of the Ravens game on Nov. 1. One of those streaks gets tested Thursday night at Highmark Stadium. The Chiefs (8-2) can pull even with the Bills (9-1) and take the head-to-head tiebreaker with a win, so the AFC's top seed is on the line along with the bragging rights.

Since the bye, opponents have scored 10, 6, 12, and 9 points against Buffalo, and the 26-9 win over Miami showed both sides of that run. The defense gave up 185 yards and three field goals, while the offense turned five trips inside the 20 into two touchdowns and Josh Allen threw two interceptions. Patrick Mahomes will not hand out drives the way Kyle McCord did, and the Chiefs rank fourth in EPA per play. Two players need to raise their level, two need to hold the one they've found, and I picked all four with Mahomes, Travis Kelce, and these Chiefs in mind.

Allen has nine interceptions in ten games, five of them in the last four, and both of Sunday's came in the final two minutes of the first half. By his own account, one was a tip and the other a bad decision, a third-and-10 throw over the middle with 25 seconds left that safety Zayne Anderson read the whole way. A third interception was wiped out by a penalty. PFF charged him with two turnover-worthy plays, and he was pressured on 14 of 49 dropbacks.

The trend is not new. PFF had his turnover-worthy play rate at 4.5% through Week 10, his highest since his rookie season in 2018. On defense, the picks have been cheap: he has lost an average of 2.78 EPA per interception this season, third-lowest among the 35 quarterbacks with at least 100 attempts. The volume is the problem, and a Chiefs defense that is eighth in EPA per play and has held its last three opponents under 14 points will take every gift.

I want three things from him Thursday. The first is simple: avoid the forced throw over the middle and take the available checkdowns when necessary. The second is his legs. He had zero rushing attempts against Miami after 11 rushing touchdowns and 290 yards through ten games, and Kansas City entered Week 11 allowing the sixth-fewest yards per carry in the league. The third is take over from the get-go. This defense can slow down the Bills' run game if they choose to establish it early and have Josh playing off it. However, If a free Josh Allen is established early, this offense can be unstoppable. The start of this season showed it might not be sustainable for an entire season since it puts Allen under too much duress, but if there are games where it could be used, next Thursday night is one of them.

Josh isn't a quarterback in decline. I see one who has played from ahead for four straight games, has been protected against questionable opponents, and has taken the shots the scoreboard allowed him to take. Thursday won't give him that cushion. It's Superman time.

Chubb has 1.5 sacks in ten games on a three-year, $43.5 million contract with $29 million guaranteed. Pro Football Reference credits him with 10 quarterback hits, so the pressure is arriving without the finish. Against Miami, his former team, he had two tackles and none for a loss.

The snap counts show why this game matters for him. He played 67 of 82 snaps against the Rams in Week 5 with T.J. Parker at 17. In Week 6 it flipped to Parker 41 and Chubb 21, and against the Jets it was Chubb 42, Parker 39. Parker (3.5 sacks) had two of them on consecutive plays in the fourth quarter and five pressures on 26 pass-rush snaps, per PFF. The rookie has made his case for snaps, and Chubb has to make his.

Kansas City's line is the place to do it. Josh Simmons is on injured reserve, and with Trey Smith and Jaylon Moore out at Atlanta and then Arizona, Mahomes was pressured 24 times and hit eight times in Atlanta, per PFF. Kahlil Benson alone allowed eight of those pressures, and he had the fifth-worst pass-blocking grade among qualifying tackles in early November. If Rousseau stays over the right tackle, as he has for most of the season, Chubb is the one who gets Benson.

I'm not asking for a three-sack night. Mahomes went down once in Atlanta despite all that pressure, so hits that don't finish won't be enough. I want Chubb to turn his 10 hits into disruption that forces bad throws and bad decisions. With Deone Walker, Greg Rousseau, and now Ed Oliver playing the way they are, he has to take advantage of it.

Rousseau's 10th sack against Miami made him the first Bills player with double-digit sacks since Leonard Floyd in 2023, and his previous career high was eight. He entered Week 11 leading the NFL with 56 quarterback pressures, per PFF, and added four more against the Dolphins in PFF's initial charting (the two sacks and two hurries). The first sack came on a bull rush that drove right tackle Austin Jackson into McCord, and at this pace he finishes with 17.

People are noticing it. Against the Jets, he had two pressures and a 44.1 PFF grade on 42 snaps, a quiet afternoon that showed how much offenses are adjusting to stop/avoid him, opening up opportunities for his teammates to take advantage of. The unit as a whole was still great, not allowing a TD for the 3rd consecutive week.

It's Mahomes now, though. He makes the sack the hardest stat in football. He threw for 348 yards and five touchdowns at Atlanta while under pressure on 24 dropbacks and sacked once, then threw for 324 and three more against Arizona. Rousseau's job is to take away the half-second Mahomes needs to reset and to hold his lane, so Mahomes can't climb the pocket or escape to his right. If Smith and Moore are still out, he should get reps against some questionable opponents.

Benford left the Jets game in the fourth quarter with a groin injury and missed Miami, and Joe Brady said the injury is not considered long-term, which leaves Thursday open. If he plays, my expectation is that he gets a large share of Kelce's snaps.

His season has had two halves. Through three games, he allowed a 124.4 passer rating when targeted, and PFF had him 97th among 99 corners entering Week 4. He rebuilt it. In Week 8, per Next Gen Stats, his coverage EPA of −5.8 was sixth in the NFL, he was eighth in catch rate allowed over expected, and his tight-window percentage was 50. Brock Bowers finished with four catches in Week 7, Zay Flowers had six yards total across his losses to Buffalo and Jacksonville (PFF), and Justin Jefferson caught three passes for 58 yards on 11 targets in Week 9, with Benford leaving in the third quarter with a wrist injury.

Kelce is a different test. He looks rejuvenated with 41 catches for 586 yards and four touchdowns, a career-best 14.6 yards per reception, and he caught two touchdowns against Arizona, both before halftime. He gives up about four inches and 45 pounds to Benford (6'1″, 205 vs. 6'5″, 250), and he's a master at finding space between zone assignments. If healthy and good to go, I expect Jim Leonhard to throw a heavy dose of Benford at Kelce, especially on money downs.

Kansas City leads the NFL in yards per carry (5.2) and in EPA per rush, and Kenneth Walker III, who has 872 yards at 5.5 per carry in eight games, is expected back Thursday. Rookie Emmett Johnson ran for 100-plus yards in back-to-back games while Walker was out. The Bills' interior has to stop that before Mahomes gets to play with the lead.

DT Deone Walker: He posted a 92.3 initial PFF grade against Miami, the best among Buffalo defenders with more than 20 snaps, with three hurries on 21 pass-rush snaps and a run stop. The Dolphins finished the first half with 38 yards of offense. His PFF run-defense grade was 61.9, 41st among interior defenders at midseason, and that is the number Walker will try to improve against Johnson and Walker III on Thursday. His pass-rush grade of 86.1 ranked second among 126 qualifiers.

LB Terrel Bernard: He had a 72.5 PFF grade against the Jets, and over the two games before Miami he had 14 tackles, two passes defended, an interception, and no missed tackles. He played only 28 snaps against the Dolphins (62%) and made two tackles, where he could rest a bit for a short week. His season grade is up to 66.6, with an 80.9 passer rating allowed when targeted. He'll be extremely important against Walker III and Johnson.

WR DJ Moore: He caught eight passes for 91 yards and the four-yard touchdown against Miami, and he came back after a second-quarter knee scare. With Kincaid out, he and Shakir carried the passing game, and Moore's touchdown was the one clean answer in a red zone that otherwise stalled. Against a defense that has held its last three opponents under 14 points, Allen will need his most reliable target to make some plays for him.

RG O'Cyrus Torrence: His 58.2 initial PFF grade against Miami was the lowest among the Bills' starting linemen, and a false start turned second-and-28 into second-and-33. Allen was pressured on 14 of 49 dropbacks. Now Chris Jones arrives, who put six pressures on 29 pass-rush snaps against the Jets, according to Next Gen Stats. Torrence hasn't cost the Bills any games. He'll have a different kind of opponent lining up against him this time, though.

TE Dawson Knox: He entered Sunday with 14 catches on 20 targets for 86 yards and a touchdown, and he played 60% of snaps before garbage time against Miami, below his 70% season average, per PFF. Kincaid has 32 catches for 487 yards, and is part of what I call the "Big Three" of this Bills' pass-catching group, alongside Moore and Shakir. If he is limited or out again, the tight end room in the passing game is basically Knox plus Keleki Latu, whose snap share rose to a season-high 36% on Sunday. Knox is mostly a blocker, and that part will matter against Kansas City's front. However, without Kincaid, the catches should matter too.

RB James Cook: He ran 22 times for 89 yards and his 10th touchdown against Miami, which extended his streak of 75-plus yards and a rushing touchdown to nine games, one more than John Riggins in 1983 and LaDainian Tomlinson in 2006. It also came at 4.0 yards per carry against a Dolphins front missing three defensive tackles on injured reserve, below his 5.1 season average. Don't get me wrong, he hasn't been bad, but Kansas City allows 3.7 yards per carry on the road, so the run game has to step up.

The Bills have won five straight regular-season games against Kansas City, and the Chiefs have usually won when the stakes were highest. A Thanksgiving win would give Buffalo the tiebreaker and the AFC's top seed, though January will decide how much of that matters. I'd trade a loss on Thursday for a win in January, but it doesn't work like that. The elusive number-one seed is very achievable this year, and winning against Kansas City puts the Bills in a great position to finally clinch it.

Despite my desire, I don't expect HC Joe Brady to change back to early-season offense from the get-go. They'll try to establish their run game and test Kansas City's run defense, daring them to stop James Cook. I just hope he doesn't take too long to adjust in case it doesn't work, because this possibility is real, and on the other side of the ball, the Chiefs offense is very capable of causing more problems for our defense than our last few opponents did. Their run game can keep Josh off the field, and Patrick Mahomes is making great decisions on a well-designed, dump-off-heavy passing offense designed by Andy Reid. I'm very curious to see what Jim Leonhard has prepared for them.
