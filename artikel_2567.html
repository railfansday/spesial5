'A forced decision' - Gallagher retires from MMA

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Northern Ireland's James Gallagher has announced his retirement from mixed martial arts at the age of 30.

He has been a key figure in the Irish fighting landscape since bursting on the scene as a teenager.

But after repeated injury setbacks and struggling for consistency in recent years, Gallagher has decided to hang up his gloves.

"This is not how I wanted my career to end. It is not a decision I made lightly, and it is certainly not a decision made because I lost the desire to fight," Gallagher posted on Instagram.

"The reality is, circumstances and stipulations outside of my control have forced me into a position where I can no longer continue doing what I have dedicated my entire life to.

"There are things happening behind the scenes that I cannot speak about at this moment.

"But there will come a time when I can tell my side of the story, and when that time comes, people will understand."

Gallagher came through John Kavanagh's SBG team ranks as a teenager, turning professional in 2015 a week before his 19th birthday.

His brash personality and grappling skills quickly drew attention to him as he stormed into a 7-0 perfect record with six submissions.

Repeated comparisons to Conor McGregor did not stop Gallagher from carving his own path and becoming one of Bellator's most recognisable stars.

Gallagher fought five times in Dublin, always drawing a big crowd, and his first defeat in 2018 came via knockout by Ricky Bandejas in the United States.

The Strabane native regrouped to win four in a row but was beaten by Patchy Mix in 2021 in front of a home crowd.

Mix went on to be Bellator champion while Gallagher's career stalled because of injuries and inactivity.

Gallagher was one of Bellator's key European stars but left soon after the MMA promotion merged with the Professional Fighters League (PFL) in 2024, and following the third defeat of his career to Leandro Higo.

He was wished a happy retirement by a host of Irish sport stars including Paul Hughes, Mick Conlan and McGregor.

Former UFC fighter Paddy Holohan branded Gallagher "a young veteran" of the fight game.

"From a young kid growing up in the Ireland, finding his place through martial arts, to signing with Bellator at 18, competing against some of the best in the world, reaching number six in the world, fighting at Madison Square Garden, selling out crowds at the 3Arena, and representing Ireland on the international stage, I gave this sport everything I had," Gallagher said.

"The sacrifices, the injuries, the weight cuts, the training camps, the missed moments with family, the highs, the lows, the setbacks, the comebacks.

"People see the walk to the cage, but they rarely see everything that goes into it."

Gallagher was marked as a future world champion from a young age, but despite not reaching that feat, he said he had "no regrets".

"I know what I have accomplished. I know what I sacrificed. And I know what I deserved.

"To my family, coaches, team-mates, sponsors, supporters, and everyone who stood by me throughout this journey - thank you. You helped make a kid's dream become a reality."

He would fight twice more in MMA, his most recent in Japanese promotion Rizin last April where he lost to Kazumasa Majima via submission.

Gallagher retires with an overall record of 13 wins and four losses.
