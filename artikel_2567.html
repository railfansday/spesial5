IND vs NZ 2nd Test predicted playing XIs: Will Mohammed Siraj make a comeback to the Indian team at Christchurch?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

India and New Zealand meet in the second and final Test at Hagley Oval from Friday.

Shubman Gill has indicated India will field a similar side to the one in Wellington.

New Zealand have lost Blair Tickner to a hamstring injury, with Matt Fisher called up.

India and New Zealand begin the second and final Test of their series at Hagley Oval in Christchurch on Friday, November 27, with play starting at 11 am local time, which is 3:30 am IST. Whoever wins takes the series, and a second draw leaves it shared.

The opening Test at the Basin Reserve produced a great deal of batting and very little else. India declared on 720 for 9, built around KL Rahul's 136 and Rishabh Pant's 128, after New Zealand had made 368 on the back of Henry Nicholls' 175, and the hosts closed on 239 for 5 to save the game.

That draw hurt India more than it hurt New Zealand, given the ground they need to make up in the World Test Championship cycle. Jasprit Bumrah took five wickets across the match, four of them in the first innings, and still could not force a result on a surface that offered nothing to either attack.

Shubman Gill said afterwards that for the seamers "there was nothing at all" at the Basin Reserve, and that the spinners got little help either. Hagley Oval has historically been the opposite sort of ground, which makes India's bowling combination the main talking point of the week.

On the evidence of what the captain has said, probably not. Gill was asked about his bowling options after the first Test and indicated the side would stay largely as it was, telling reporters at the Basin Reserve that "in the second Test, most of the team would be similar".

Mohammed Siraj was left out at Wellington in favour of four other seamers, with Bumrah, Akash Deep, Anshul Kamboj and Prasidh Krishna sharing the new ball and Washington Sundar the only spin option. Gill called it a hard decision at the toss and did not dress it up afterwards.

Speaking at the post-match press conference, the captain said, "We played the best attack for this match. Siraj's a really good friend and dropping him was a really tough call." He added that "a player can make a comeback by performing well", which places the onus on the bowler rather than on the conditions.

The numbers behind the call are not flattering. Siraj has 144 Test wickets from 48 matches, 108 of them away from home or at neutral venues, but took only five wickets at 28.60 in three Tests in 2026, and he has said a two-month layoff disrupted his run-up.

India's other option is Ravindra Jadeja, who would add a left-arm spinner and considerable batting depth at the expense of a seamer. Prasidh Krishna's 2 for 23 from 19 overs in New Zealand's second innings was the most economical return of the match, which makes him difficult to leave out.

New Zealand Cricket confirmed on Tuesday that Blair Tickner had been ruled out of the Test with a right hamstring injury picked up in training in Wellington. Matt Fisher, whose only Test appearance came against Zimbabwe last year, has been added to the squad as cover.

Tickner did not play the first Test, so the change does not affect the XI. The more significant absence is Mitchell Santner, who fractured his right hand during the one-day series against India and is out for the rest of the tour, leaving the hosts without a specialist spinner.

Tom Latham leads a side still adjusting to life without Kane Williamson, who retired from international cricket in June. Latham and Devon Conway opened at the Basin Reserve, with Nicholls at three and Rachin Ravindra and Daryl Mitchell following him.

Two of the 15 Tests played at Hagley Oval have been drawn, and nine of them have been won by New Zealand. The side bowling first has won nine times, which is the strongest argument for whoever wins the toss sending the opposition in.

India's only visit to the ground ended badly. They were bowled out for 242 and 124 in February 2020 and beaten inside three days, with Jamieson taking 5 for 45 in the first innings on a surface that did what Wellington's did not.

Leaving Siraj out again is defensible on form, but it leaves India with the same attack that bowled 116 overs in New Zealand's second innings for five wickets. If Hagley Oval behaves as it usually does, the combination will matter less than who holds their lengths in the first hour.
