Travelodge has a new chief executive, 38 safety recommendations to implement, and debt to refinance. Peter Krueger starts Tuesday.

Travelodge's new boss is a former investment banker, and he'll need to be. The UK budget hotel group named Peter Krueger CEO on Thursday alongside third-quarter results that showed earnings falling. He inherits a safety scandal, rising costs and debt that starts coming due in 2027.

Krueger joins from TUI AG, where he was most recently CEO of the Holiday Experiences segment, overseeing the German package holiday group’s hotels, cruises, and excursions businesses. Before TUI, he spent 13 years in investment banking at Deutsche Bank.

Krueger succeeds Jo Boydell, who resigned in August as part of the fallout from a sexual assault at a Travelodge property in December 2022 and the company’s handling of the victim’s subsequent complaint.

Travelodge has named Peter Krueger CEO, bringing in a former Deutsche Bank investment banker and most recently CEO of TUI AG's Holiday Experiences segment, at a moment when the UK budget hotel group is squeezed on three fronts at once. Third-quarter results showed nine-month revenue of £811.9 million ($1.1 billion), up 3.7% year over year, but group EBITDA fell to £133.6 million ($175 million) from £140.2 million ($184 million) as roughly £30 million ($39 million) of cost inflation from wages, a higher UK tax burden, business rates, and rent reviews ate most of the trading gain. Krueger also inherits the fallout from an independent review by Paul Greaney KC into a December 2022 sexual assault at the Maidenhead hotel, which found Travelodge's room-access security "not fit for the purpose of keeping guests safe" and its treatment of the victim "disgraceful." The review's 38 recommendations will require material capital expenditure that CFO Ray Reidy said cannot be quantified until March, with a formal progress update due March 2027. Behind that sits the balance sheet: a £50 million ($66 million) revolving credit facility expires in October 2027 and £415 million ($552 million) of bonds mature in April 2028. Reidy told investors that cutting costs enough to offset next year's headwinds "isn't going to be possible" and that revenue growth would have to do the work.
