What Pro-Lifers Need to Win Elections

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

In election after election, pro-lifers are being outspent and outorganized.

Demonstrators participate in the annual March for Life in Washington, D.C., January 23, 2026.

It’s become something of a truism that the pro-life movement lacked a plan for how to pursue its goals after Roe v. Wade was overturned. Indeed, three more states (Nevada, Virginia, and Missouri) saw abortion-related statewide ballot measures go against pro-lifers in 2026. The recriminations have been bitter against the leaders and key advocacy groups on the pro-life side: They are political naifs; they are incompetent at messaging and strategy; they are self-important, wannabe big shots who just want proximity to power; they are too focused on federal politics; their organizations’ CEOs make too much money (hilariously false — ask me how I know) for too little results.

There is plenty of blame to go around, but I think such critiques are missing the key, practical problems the pro-life movement faces. The reality is that the pro-abortion side simply has far more money and organization than the pro-life side, and these gaps have been decisive in election after election. Without the funding for political organization and campaigns, pro-lifers simply cannot win.
