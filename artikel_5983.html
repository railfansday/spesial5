IndiGo Adds Southeast Asia Flights as Westbound Costs Climb

IndiGo calls it "disciplined growth." It's also a retreat to safer ground: Southeast Asia suits its narrowbodies while fuel is expensive and Pakistan's airspace is closed. But it's the same region where IndiGo cut six routes this summer, so the real test comes after the holiday peak.

IndiGo is adding more flights to Southeast Asia at a time when rising fuel costs and geopolitical tensions are making some of its westbound operations more expensive.

India’s largest airline will launch daily flights between Delhi and Kuala Lumpur, as well as Bengaluru and Hanoi, from Dec. 20. It will also increase Delhi–Singapore services to twice daily from the same date and Delhi–Bangkok flights to 19 a week from Dec. 28.

The additions give IndiGo more capacity in a region that has long been popular with Indian travelers. They also come as the airline is becoming more careful about where it deploys aircraft internationally, with higher fuel bills and airspace restrictions making some routes harder to operate profitably.

For the quarter ended Sept. 30, international capacity, measured in available seat kilometers, fell 3% from a year earlier, while domestic capacity remained broadly flat.

IndiGo is adding capacity in Southeast Asia while pulling back elsewhere internationally. India's largest airline will launch daily Delhi–Kuala Lumpur and Bengaluru–Hanoi flights from Dec. 20, double Delhi–Singapore to twice daily, and raise Delhi–Bangkok to 19 weekly flights from Dec. 28. The additions come as IndiGo's international capacity, measured in available seat kilometers, fell 3% in the September quarter and is expected to shrink again in the December quarter. Fuel expenses rose 67% to INR 99.7 billion ($1.05 billion), nearly 38% of total expenses, and the airline posted a net loss of around INR 18.8 billion ($197 million) despite 26% revenue growth. CEO Willie Walsh said IndiGo has recovered only about 60% of the fuel cost increase through fares and surcharges, and that capacity decisions are now being made flight by flight. Southeast Asia suits the airline's narrowbody fleet and short flying times, while Pakistan's airspace closure since April 2025 has made westbound routes from Delhi longer and more expensive. IndiGo still targets international flying at roughly 40% of capacity by 2030, up from about one-third today.
