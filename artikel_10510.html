Why CeraVe’s NBA partnership is delivering layup after layup

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

When CeraVe anointed Houston Rockets forward Kevin Durant as the “New Face of Legs” in February 2026, it wasn’t your typical A-list athlete partnership. There was lore involved.

Ads featured him rubbing his long legs with CeraVe’s moisturizing cream, a cheeky reference to a viral 2021 post from NBA Hall of Famer Isaiah Thomas that drew attention to Durant’s dry-looking legs.

The campaign led to a 43% sales lift, the L’Oréal-owned company says, in addition to more than 4.1 billion impressions and 83 million organic video views.

It was part of a multiyear partnership with the NBA, “which gives us an amazing opportunity to link skincare with performance,” says Esther García, CeraVe U.S.’s general manager.

Three months later, the company struck marketing gold again: It appointed retired NBA icon Carmelo Anthony as the brand’s “Head Coach” and, in a teaser for the launch of its anti-dandruff hydrating shampoo, featured Anthony’s alter ego Hoodie Melo, a guise he admitted was used many times to hide his own dandruff.

Subsequent ads capitalized on Anthony’s “three to the dome” hand sign, which he famously flashed after scoring three-pointers, to call attention to the shampoo’s three key ceramide ingredients.

For another campaign, after commenters described CeraVe as “the GOAT of skincare” on social media, CeraVe created a goat mascot named “Sarah V.” She went on to attend NBA games and other events, always with a product sample on hand.

Meanwhile, for the launch of its invisible mineral sunscreen in August, CeraVe pushed back against the Gen Z trend of “tan maxxing” (forgoing sunscreen to achieve a deep bronze). The skincare brand transformed a Florida State University sorority house backyard into a sun-safe space to educate students about healthier habits.

“It was a way of being there, having the conversation,” García says. While science is key, it’s nothing if people aren’t excited to try the products.
