Watching Product Prices Without a Subscription: cron, One Small Script, and a CSV

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Posted on Nov 14 • Originally published at Medium

Every price-tracker-as-a-service wants an account, has alert limits, and eventually asks for a subscription - to tell you that a number on a webpage went down. If you can run Python on your own machine, you do not need any of it.

Here is the whole system: a script on a cron schedule that fetches the page, pulls the price out, appends a row to a CSV, and pings you on Telegram or email only when the price crosses your target.

The fallback chain covers most shops you will ever watch. For stubborn ones, right-click the price, Inspect, copy a selector, done.

Alert when the price hits a target, or when it drops by a percentage. One fetch per product per run - polite by design.

Two checks a day, 7 AM and 9 PM. Change it to hourly if you are hunting a flash sale. On Windows, the README includes the Task Scheduler equivalent.

Anyone waiting on a GPU price drop, tracking supplier listings, or holding a holiday shopping list who does not want yet another web app and account - just a script on their own machine, with the price history in their own CSV.

The whole thing is small on purpose: one readable script (about 200 lines), the JSON config, a README with selector-finding tips and ready-made cron/Task Scheduler lines, Telegram bot setup in five minutes, and an offline fixture so you can try it without touching any website. You can build it yourself in an evening from the description above.

If you would rather skip the build, I packaged exactly that as the Price Monitor Kit - a $1 instant download, priced at $1 on purpose while I collect the first round of honest reviews. If it disappoints, the product Q&A is right there; that is what the price is for.

Either way: stop checking prices by hand. Let cron do the staring at webpages - it is better at boredom than you are.
