Sunrise Scramble

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

I bring back a classic backpacking mistake in time for sunrise. I enjoy views of the Hudson Valley and camp above some noisy animals.

Sunrise Mountain, I couldn’t get it out of my head. I hiked over something called Sunrise Mountain and couldn’t get myself up to see a sunrise there. It was a good time of year for sunrises, the nights were long, allowing ample time for sleep before getting up and finding a place to catch the sun as it breaks over the horizon. I had gotten up early yesterday morning and had a glimpse of the pre-sunrise sky on my drive into town. It was exquisite. Even though time had moved back an hour and sunrise would now be at 6:30AM instead of 7:30AM, I knew that my body clock was more aligned to the cycle of light and dark than the numbers on my phone’s lock screen. I could wake up at 5:40AM, get myself together, and hike up the 1.1 miles the east-facing viewpoint south of Culver’s Gap. Part of me thought I was nuts, but nonetheless I set an alarm for 5:40AM and dozed off to sleep.

Except I didn’t set an alarm for 5:40AM. Like something out of an episode of Seinfeld I set the alarm for 5:40PM. Lucky for me I have my “punctuality anxiety” system to fall back, which rarely fails to deliver. I was up before the alarm and could wiggle enough that I felt I could get myself up and actually do this. I pulled myself together, decided it would be best to pack my breakfast and have it on trail to make time, and was heading toward the trail at 5:50AM. I had strapped on my headlamp, which I hadn’t used since May, to light up the trail, though after about 10 minutes it was easy enough to see anyway. I did notice during that 10 minutes that it was dimming itself, I made a note to look into this since I wouldn’t want that happening when I really needed it.

I reached the viewpoint by 6:20AM, which is when I noticed that I forgot my water bottle in rush to leave the van. I had flashbacks to one of my first days in Pennsylvania when I managed to forget a series of critical items during a calamitous day. I ended up hiking the better part of 2 days without the use of my water bottle. I knew I couldn’t afford the time to hike back to the van and continue my planned itinerary, which called for at least 27 miles. I began coming up with alternative ideas while the sun peered over the horizon, smirking at my classic blunder.

As I was adequately hydrated and could filter water into my cold-soaker if I really needed it, I decided to keep my momentum and continue hiking south. The trail up on the ridge in the Delaware Water Gap National Recreation Area was decidedly easy. There were frequent views into the valley and several white tail deer roaming the area. I used the mild conditions to my advantage and hiked 11 miles to a viewpoint above Fairview Lake. I turned around and used the return leg to take the alternate route around Crater Lake (not the national park). This allowed me to get some closer pictures of the lake and make use of some side trails parallel to the AT. After that it was the AT the rest of the way back to the van at Culver’s Gap.

Culver Lake, from roughly where I took pictures of the sunrise this morning.

I was finally able to have my breakfast having survived the trek without water. My trail snack stood in for a breakfast and kept my energy levels up. From Culver’s Gap I drove to the Appalachian Trail crossing on Millbrook Road. It turned out to be a small trailhead that didn’t quite look suitable to leave a vehicle overnight, but I decided to go for it anyway. My motivation was catching the sunset. When I pieced together an alternate route, I knew I’d be leaving a gap between Millbrook Road and the point where I turned around earlier in the day. I be hiking south since that is where the better view points and tent spots were. I only had to hike about 2 miles to them, which is why I decided to hike 22 miles in the first out and back stint. Tomorrow I’d continue the journey south all the way to the Dunnfield Creek Trailhead near the Delaware River, then turn around, pass the van, and continue north to the point where I turned around. It was the best way to split the miles between the two days and still work in a night on the trail with a sunset and potentially sunrise.

I set off from Millbrook Road and stopped by the Rattlesnack Spring to collect 2 liters of water for the night. From there it was a fairly mild hike up to the Catfish Fire Tower. I could see a ranger positioned high above in the tower and an old-school forest service truck parked at the base of the tower.

Past the tower the viewpoints and tent spots came fast and furious. I was holding out for one with a sunset view, but all were positioned on the east side of the ridge. Eventually I settled for one of those spots that did have an incredible view of the valley. Down below, dogs were barking and a rooster was crowing. Occasionally a passing plane drowned them out. Despite the noise I was content with my spot because you just can’t beat being perched on a cliff with a front row seat to a sunrise.
