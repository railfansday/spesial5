Regarding the Orient Express Sailing Yachts tipping policy, the company states that gratuities are included in the fare — “however, if you wish to offer a tip as an extra gesture of appreciation, it will be warmly received.”

I just have to emphasize that the level of inclusions really impressed me. Seriously, I was served caviar with breakfast, lunch, and dinner at various points (and even cocktails!), and the house champagne is Veuve Clicquot La Grande Dame 2018 (which retails for around $200 per bottle).

Orient Express Sailing Yachts currently has a single ship, which is named the Corinthian (a second ship is being introduced in 2027, and it’ll be named the Olympian).

The ship entered service in 2026, and is 722 feet (220 meters) long, 82 feet (25 meters) wide, and 26,600 gross tons. With 54 accommodations and a maximum capacity of around 110 guests, there’s a roughly 1.5:1 staff to guest ratio, given that there are around 170 staff onboard. The spaciousness of this ship, and the amount of space allocated to each passenger, really is next level.

The Corinthian also has the title of being the world’s largest sailing yacht, as it has three automated SolidSail rigid sails on tilting carbon masts, to provide wind propulsion, alongside hybrid LNG auxiliary power. It’s just such an elegant and distinctive looking ship, that it can’t help but turn heads. For those curious, it was explained that the sails reduce emissions by around 10%, and no, the ship doesn’t “heel” in the way a smaller sailboat would. Unless you’re looking up, you wouldn’t realize there are even sails.

You can find the full Corinthian deck plan here. As you’d expect for a sail boat, there aren’t quite as many floors as on a traditional cruise ship. Guests can access a total of five floors, between levels three and seven. To briefly cover that:

Near the ship’s entrance, you’ll find a very cool directory of sorts, which shows the floorplan of the ship. You can click any amenity, and then it will light up, so that’s super handy.

The ship has two sets of elevator banks, as well as two sets of staircases, so getting around the ship is really easy. We always used the stairs, since we were typically within a couple of floors of where we needed to be.

As you’d expect, the ease of boarding a ship varies greatly depending on its size, but Orient Express Sailing Yachts really took it to the next level in terms of how seamless it was. We had spent a night at the Hôtel Dieu Intercontinental Marseille. Our journey included the transfers, so an Orient Express representative met us at the hotel, and then we were driven to the port.

Getting onboard the ship literally couldn’t have been easier. There were no formalities in the port, but instead, we were escorted straight through security, and then check-in was handled once onboard.

There were no other guests as we boarded, and the staff really went above and beyond — for example, the crew member who escorted us through security and onto the ship insisted on holding an umbrella over our heads, to shade us from the sun.

Our check-in process onboard took all of five minutes, and at that point we were introduced to our butler, Reuben, who escorted us to our suite, and took care of everything from there. I should mention how Reuben was German, so they specifically appointed us a German butler, which I thought was super fun (so we communicated with him exclusively in German).

We didn’t have a stop that involved tendering, but for those situations where you do, it’s an efficient process, given how few guests there are. Our one stop had us docked in port, and there was a nice seating area outside the ship with bottled water, sunscreen, etc. Admittedly a setup like this is more useful when you’re waiting on a tender, rather than just walking directly onto or off the ship.

Orient Express Sailing Yachts has 54 suites onboard, spread across over a dozen different categories. The entry level suites here are among the most spacious at sea, while the penthouse suites really take luxury and space to the next level.

Our sailing was rather empty — only 19 of the 54 suites were occupied — and we were upgraded to a higher suite category (I believe they had blocked most of the entry level room categories, as they were doing some minor updates to them ahead of the ship heading to the Caribbean). So let me share my thoughts on that suite first, and then I’ll share some pictures of other suites that I had the opportunity to tour as well.

We stayed in an Appartement Prestige, which is one of the more premium standard suite categories, but below the duplex suites and penthouse suites. These suites are marketed as being an average of 1,119 square feet (104 square meters), so they’re incredibly spacious.

We were assigned suite 518, located on the fifth floor. The hallways on the Corinthian are just stunning, as you can see below. It really feels more like a luxury hotel than a ship, especially in terms of the width of the hallway.

The concept of forgetting that one is on a ship continued inside the suite. The interior felt more to me like a “Palace” hotel in France, than what you’d expect at sea. I can’t even imagine how much money was spent on the interiors, as the quality of the finishes, from the wall treatments to the carpet, as well as the furniture, was just unreal.

Upon entering the suite, there was first a dining area with a table and a chair, as well as a desk with a chair (that chair could also be moved over to the dining table). It’s not often you get this kind of space in a cruise ship suite.

The minibar was also in this area, and was well stocked, with everything being complimentary. This included a Nespresso coffee machine, cocktails, wine, beer, soft drinks, etc. They were also happy to replenish the minibar however you wanted, so there are really no limits.

That entry area then flowed into the living room, which was gorgeous. It had a couch and a coffee table, as well as a hidden TV (which would pop up from in front of the mirror).

Waiting on the dining table was a lovely welcome amenity, consisting of some freshly baked treats, a fruit plate, and a bottle of Veuve Clicquot 2015 Rose. There was also an Orient Express branded journal, along with two Orient Express water bottles, which guests could take home.

Double doors then led into the bedroom, which had a very plush king size bed.

Much like with the bedroom, the bathroom will make you forget that you’re on a ship, as it had double sinks, a soaking tub, a walk-in shower with amazing water pressure, and a toilet in a separate room.

There were also a couple of closet areas, so there was plenty of space to store luggage and clothes.

The suite then had a balcony that stretched the entire length of the suite, with very comfortable furniture.

Beyond the generally beautiful design, it has to be pointed out just how over the top everything was. I mean, the deck furniture uses fabrics from Loro Piana, just to give you a sense of just how high-end it is.

Housekeeping in the room was great (it was taken care of twice daily, and always well timed), butler service was very useful (more on that a bit later, when I talk about service), Starlink Wi-Fi was lightning fast, and I also love how Orient Express Sailing Yachts includes laundry. You could get unlimited washing, dry cleaning, etc., so that’s a very nice feature.

In the interest of checking out different suite products, I also had the chance to tour the Suite Panoramique, which is the entry level accommodation onboard, located on levels four and five. These are 506 square feet (47 square meters), so they’re spacious for an entry level suite on a cruise.

This had the same general design concept as the suite we were in, except there wasn’t a fully separate living area. The suite had a comfortable king size bed, a desk with a chair, a dining table, and a huge window with a double daybed of sorts.

While there was no bathtub, the bathroom still had double sinks, a walk-in shower, and a toilet in a separate room.

To point out the obvious, these entry level suites don’t have balconies. Of course people prefer to have balconies, but this is just a simple reality of the difference between sailboats and cruise ships, in terms of how many levels there are, how far above water they are, etc.

Orient Express Sailing Yachts’ top end suites are really next level, so I also had the chance to tour the Zephyr Suite, which is located on level seven, and is the second most premium suite onboard. This two bedroom suite has 2,486 square feet (231 square meters) of interior space, plus a terrace that’s 1,206 square feet (112 square meters).

What I thought was so cool was how unique the design of this suite was, as the top suite products seem to have a totally original design. This very much had a residential vibe, with a long hallway connecting the various parts of the suite.

However, perhaps the coolest feature was the replica of an Orient Express Heritage cabin, intended to pay homage to the legendary train.

The Orient Express Sailing Yachts Corinthian has two pools onboard, with one being rather traditional, and one being unlike anything else at sea (at least to my knowledge). The main “leisure” pool is located on the fifth floor, at the back of the ship. While the pool has beautiful aesthetics and lovely deck space around it, this isn’t some destination pool in and of itself. So if a huge pool is a crucial factor in picking a ship, Four Seasons Yachts might be a better option.

We’re all different, and personally, I’m not much of a pool person. Quite to the contrary, I actually loved this pool deck, since it was mostly covered and in the shade, so I could nicely sit there and enjoy the breeze (funny enough, Four Seasons Yachts has an amazing pool, but I didn’t actually spend much time there, due to the lack of shade).

The ship then has a second pool, which is among the most memorable you’ll find on any ship. The lap pool is 54 feet (16 meters) long, and it’s located under the sails. It really feels like you’re in a cave, since it’s on a deck that’s one level down from the main upper deck. What a pool!

On the pool front, really my only disappointment was the lack of a hot tub. As much as I find hot tubs to be kind of gross, I do love them, especially on a ship, so I was sad there wasn’t one of those. In general, the newer ultra high end cruise lines seem to be lacking in that department (the same was true on Four Seasons Yachts).

Beyond the pool setup, on the seventh floor you’ll find a huge deck you can hang out on. There are lots of lounge chairs out here, though it’s not necessarily that popular for just lounging around. The star of the show here is what’s above.

The time when the deck really gets popular is when the ship pulls into or out of ports, given the panoramic views from up here, plus the ability to watch the sails be lowered and raised. I must say, it was absolutely mesmerizing to watch. They made a big event of departures, with live music, cocktails, etc. It really felt like a cocktail reception, only more intimate.

Speaking of deck space, another fun aspect of the ship is how you can actually just freely walk up to the deck in front of the bridge, and even all the way to the bow. How fun!

The Orient Express Sailing Yachts Corinthian wellness complex is located on the fourth floor, and that includes a Guerlain Spa. This area is an impressive 5,380 square feet (500 square meters), with four treatment suites (including a double suite), as well as all kinds of other traditional spa facilities.

Guests can always use the facilities, even without an appointment, and that includes a sauna, steam room, relaxation area, locker rooms, etc.

The spa treatment rooms are absolutely gorgeous, whether we’re talking about the couples room, or the individual ones.

Ford and I got a couples massage during our final day (where we were at sea), and I can honestly say it was one of the best spa treatments I’ve ever had. Not only was the therapist I had incredible, but the massage bed was so comfortable, and there was something so calming about the view, the gentle sway of the ship, etc.

For those curious about the pricing, you can find part of the spa menu below.

The ship also has a full hair salon and barber shop, so there are no shortage of opportunities to get your grooming needs taken care of here. The barber shop even leads to the speakeasy — more on that later.

The Orient Express Sailing Yachts Corinthian has a gym located on the fourth floor, which can be accessed by guests 24/7. The gym isn’t huge, but is more than sufficient given the size of the ship, in my opinion. Every time we used it, we were alone.

The gym had a good selection of cardio and weight equipment, as well as a separate room for aerobics, boxing, etc.

There was an excellent personal trainer onboard, and it was possible to schedule sessions with him at no extra cost. So it’s not just for group fitness, but even for personal sessions, on a space available basis. That’s really nice, as virtually any other cruise would charge for that, in my experience.

The Orient Express Sailing Yachts Corinthian has an incredibly impressive entertainment area, located on deck four. The issue is, it’s not really used a whole lot. Now, I’m not sure I’d actually call this an “issue,” but let me explain.

When you think of mega cruise ships, nightly shows and entertainment are among the most popular attractions. The thing is, the smaller the ship is, the less interest there is in things like this, and that’s especially true for the cruise lines that are primarily targeted at people who haven’t historically been cruisers.

So the Corinthian has a huge theater with dozens of seats, but there’s not a whole lot that goes on there.

Perhaps the more interesting entertainment feature is the private cinema, where you can have them screen a movie (or whatever) for you.

There’s even a recording studio onboard — what a cool concept, and in addition to being able to play instruments here, I heard people have used this to record podcasts, etc.

On the topic of entertainment, the ship also has a card room on the fourth floor, with chess, checkers, decks of cards, etc.

Separately, there’s also the yacht club, which is basically a very elegant lounge area where guests can hang out, watch TV, etc.

Lastly, the ship has the library on the fourth floor, which has an incredible selection of books. Everything on the Corinthian is so thoughtful, including the book selection. As it was explained to us, the 1,500-book library is supposed to represent the most important books of the century, and were curated by the historic Parisian bookshop Galignani.

I know there’s sometimes confusion about whether Orient Express Sailing Yachts is kids friendly, and the answer is… sort of.

On most voyages, guests onboard must be 16 years of age or older. However, on dedicated family voyages, children ages six months to 15 years old are allowed as well. Infants under six months aren’t permitted onboard, and per the terms, “families traveling with little ones under two years old must be accompanied by a dedicated babysitter throughout their voyage.”

Quite honestly, it’s logical that Orient Express Sailing Yachts would have this policy, since the ship’s venues feel really intimate, the pools aren’t huge, etc.

For family voyages, the Corinthian does have a kids club onboard, which offers activities with a dedicated team. Babysitting services can also be arranged for excursions or gala evenings. But in terms of being kids friendly, I definitely think it’s an area where Four Seasons Yachts wins, as all voyages are kids friendly, the pool is huge, and there’s both a kids club and teens club.

Orient Express Sailing Yachts has an incredibly impressive selection of restaurants, bars, and lounges onboard, with the culinary vision overseen by well-known chef Yannick Alléno. Before I get into details, let me just say that I found the quality of food and drinks onboard to be truly exceptional.

I try not to exaggerate, because I think far too many people are a little too generous when describing food on cruises (or flights, for that matter — what’s with all these people describing airplane food as Michelin dining?). However, I found the quality of catering on Orient Express Sailing Yachts to just be in a completely different league than Four Seasons Yachts.

I was also delighted by the generosity of the included options, with an incredible alcohol selection, caviar accompanying dishes at breakfast, lunch, and dinner (and even cocktails), etc. It’s also interesting to note that no reservations are allowed onboard, except at the fine dining restaurant that costs extra.

Everything was always available whenever we wanted it, though people will otherwise probably have mixed feelings on that policy (conversely, the issue when you do allow reservations is that people hog them and then just cancel at the last minute, leaving other guests with limited options).

La Terrasse is Orient Express Sailing Yachts’ all-day dining restaurant, serving breakfast, lunch, and dinner. It’s located at the back of the sixth floor, and has both indoor and outdoor seating. The fact that this is the casual restaurant really tells you the quality of the experience, as there are white tablecloths all hours of the day.

This is the primary restaurant where breakfast is served, and you can find that menu below.

Breakfast is entirely a la carte, though at the start of the meal, a basket with a rotating selection of croissants and pastries was brought out.

There was a large selection of fresh juices and coffee available. My one disappointment was that the coffee was just okay, and clearly came from a machine. That’s ironic, because there’s plenty of delicious barista coffee on the ship, so this seems to be one of the only venues where that’s the case. Given the quality of service onboard, I’m sure they’d also be happy to get you a coffee from somewhere else, if you prefer.

We found the breakfast selection to be very good, and over the course of our stay tried the acai bowl with coconut and seasonal fruit, chia pudding with fresh mango, the mixed berry plate, the crispy and soft blinis with caviar cream and smoked salmon, the avocado toast, and more.

We split the chickpea hummus with parmesan to start, and then had the smoked salmon club sandwich and Thai beef salad as our mains. At our server’s insistence, we finished off with a floating island for dessert. YUM.

Across the board, the food and service were exceptional, so it’s amazing that this is just the most basic restaurant onboard, as it only gets better from there.

L’Ecrin is Orient Express Sailing Yachts’ elevated dinner restaurant, with a focus on seafood and pasta. It’s located on the sixth floor, and it exclusively has indoor seating. This restaurant doesn’t cost extra, but in my opinion is on par with the paid restaurant on some of the other top ships.

Then for mains, we had the warm lobster with olive oil and mayonnaise and the caponata and polenta. Again, this was very, very good.

For dessert, while I had heard amazing things about the suzette pasta (flambeed tableside), we decided on the signature bronte pistachio ice cream coupe, which was insanely indulgent.

What a meal! This was definitely my favorite restaurant onboard.

First, we have to talk about the restaurant as such. Can you believe this is on a ship? This is without a doubt the most beautiful dining venue I’ve ever seen on a ship. With just over half a dozen tables, it’s also a really intimate space.
