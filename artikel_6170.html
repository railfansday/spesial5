Review: United Airlines 737 MAX 8 Economy Class Newark To Nuuk, Greenland

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

My United Airlines flight from Newark to Nuuk, Greenland featured a friendly crew, two meal services, and a spectacular approach onboard a Boeing 737 MAX 8 in economy class. Economy Plus was perfectly comfortable for the four-hour daytime journey, though the flight became much fuller than I had expected thanks to a large contingent of United employees taking advantage of their travel benefits.

Greenland had been on my bucket list for years, and United’s nonstop service from Newark offered an opportunity to finally visit. I was heading back to Germany to pick up my children after their summer visit with family and decided to take a rather scenic route via Nuuk and Copenhagen.

I had already traveled from Palm Springs through San Francisco and Denver to reach Newark. By the time I boarded this flight, I was tired…but very excited about what lay ahead.

I booked the entire United itinerary from Palm Springs to Nuuk using 40,000 Air Canada Aeroplan points, plus $5.60 in government taxes and the CAD39 partner booking fee.

A cash ticket for the itinerary was about $1,600, so I was pleased to use miles instead. The premium cabin on the Greenland flight, marketed as Premium Plus, was sold out when I booked (and I don’t mean no saver partner award space, but sold out completely).

My overnight flight from Denver arrived at the A Gates, and the Nuuk flight would depart from the same terminal.

There was a United Club nearby, but I wanted a shower and barista-made coffee, so I took the airside shuttle bus to Terminal C and visited the United Club near gate C123. I wrote separately about that visit. There was no wait for a shower, and although I would have preferred hotter water and stronger pressure, it was refreshing to clean up after the redeye.

An illy double-shot cappuccino helped too. I had breakfast, got some work done, and then returned by bus to the A Gates about 30 minutes before boarding.

This turned out to be a much more energetic flight than I expected.

The night before departure, the flight appeared less than half full. By the time we pushed back a few minutes late, it was much closer to capacity, with a large number of United employees traveling on standby.

I initially had an Economy Plus row to myself, but two jovial United employees joined me. They were heading to Greenland for just one night. As I explained in my first impressions of the flight, several employees described arriving in Nuuk, exploring town, taking a twilight boat excursion, and visiting a bar before returning to the airport for the morning flight back to Newark.

That sounded absolutely exhausting to me, but I understood the appeal. If I had those travel benefits, I would certainly be tempted to use them on a route like this! There was certainly a festive atmosphere onboard, and I enjoyed hearing how others planned to spend their short time in Greenland.

Boarding was delayed 20 minutes, but it was finally time to step onboard.

I was warmly greeted by Craig, the purser, as I boarded. More on him below.

United’s 737 MAX 8 features 150 seats one economy class in a 3-3 configuration. Seats are Collins Meridian slimline seats and feature an adjustable headrest and seatback screens. Legroom is 30 inches (34 inches in Economy Plus), the seat is 17 inches wide, and seats recline two inches.

I selected a window seat on the left side of the aircraft in Economy Plus.

There are individual air nozzles onboard as well as seatback power (A/C under the seat and both USB-A and USB-C under the screen).

The window seat on the A side proved an excellent choice during our approach, though the best side will depend on which direction the aircraft lands, which will depend on the winds.

Despite a flight time of only about four hours, there were two services during the flight.

I had ordered United’s “balanced diet” special meal, which consisted of chicken pieces in tomato sauce accompanied by a side salad, bread roll, and “That’s It” fruit snack for dessert. It was not exactly gourmet, but it did the job and I had some club soda to drink with it.

The regular choices for passengers were pasta or beef. Is the diet meal worth ordering?

As we approached Nuuk, I received an Indian vegetarian wrap (other passengers received a pizza pocket). It was not necessary for such a short flight, but appreciated nevertheless. I enjoyed a cup of illy coffee as well.

United’s seatback screens feature a deep content of movies and TV shows with a handful of game and audio options. The same library of content was also available via streaming to your own device.

Complimentary earphones were provided, though you could pair your own device to these screens, which I did.

The flight featured Viasat Wi-Fi, available for purchase for $10 ($8 for MileagePlus members) and it worked well over the Atlantic.

United’s 737 MAX 8 has one lavatory in the front for premium cabin passengers and two in the back for economy class, which is not a great proportion.

The purser, Craig, was a highlight of the flight.

Apparently, he worked nearly every Nuuk flight that summer and has become something of an institution on the route. He was friendly, professional, and knowledgeable, making his way through the cabin and chatting with passengers.

We had a nice conversation about restaurant recommendations in Nuuk. I appreciated speaking with someone who knew the destination and could offer advice before I arrived. What a fun assignment to spend the summer flying to Greenland!

A good crew can make an ordinary flight enjoyable, and Craig helped make this unusual route feel even more special. Between the enthusiastic employee travelers and his warm welcome, the mood onboard was very pleasant.

I had chosen an “A” window seat on the port side, which gave me a northerly view during our final approach. Aircraft can arrive from the opposite direction depending on the wind, so the view will vary, but I was very happy with my choice.

As we descended below the clouds, tundra and floating ice came into view. Then the sun broke through, revealing the mountainous landscape around Nuuk.

I have flown over Greenland many times, looking down from cruising altitude and wondering what it would be like to visit. Seeing it from this perspective, knowing I would soon be on the ground, was quite different.

Some of the mountains still held snow, and the landscape looked rugged and sparsely populated. It was majestic.

I shared more photographs in my earlier post about the approach. My arrival in the Faroe Islands a couple of years earlier may have been even more scenic, but this was nevertheless a wonderful welcome to Greenland.

This is a flight where I would strongly recommend a window seat.

We landed at 7:08 pm local time under clear skies and taxied to a parking position outside the terminal.

Air Greenland-branded stairs were brought to the aircraft, and we walked down onto the apron. It was about 55ºF outside…crisp, clear, and beautiful.

After the long journey from Southern California, I was finally in Greenland.

Next came immigration, which turned out to be a story of its own.

This was such a fun flight. Economy Plus was comfortable for the relatively short daytime journey, Craig provided excellent service, and the atmosphere onboard was cheerful…it almost felt like an inaugural flight. The approach into Nuuk was the highlight and made me very glad I had selected a window seat on the A side (though again, I was fortunate because the approaches change based on prevailing wind conditions).

Getting to Newark had required several flights and an overnight journey from Denver, but this final segment was a pleasure. I was thrilled to finally reach Greenland and appreciated being able to do so on United.
