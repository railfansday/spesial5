Here Are 3 Consumer Stocks I'd Buy With $1,500 and Hold Through the Next Recession

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

These three companies offer bright long-term futures.

By Lawrence Rothman, CFA – Nov 19, 2026 at 9:10AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Royal Caribbean Cruises ›

Certainly, no one can predict when a recession might come. Recently, many economists thought we'd have an economic slowdown in 2023 following the Federal Reserve's rate hikes the previous year.

While that never came to pass, we will have a recession at some point. We've already seen the market react negatively amid fears of how a technology spending slowdown will affect the overall economy.

Investors can prepare themselves by buying and holding stocks of strong consumer goods companies through volatile periods, since these stocks can provide outsize gains over the long haul. That's because their short-term volatility gets smoothed out over time.

These three stocks, with their strong market positions, offer this potential.

Royal Caribbean Cruises (RCL -2.01%) is one of the most popular cruise line companies, commanding a high market share. Operating under the Royal Caribbean (family and premium segments), Celebrity (premium), and Silversea (ultra-luxury) brands, it generally targets a higher-income demographic.

Their customers aren't immune from outside economic forces. People will undoubtedly cut back on expensive vacations if their personal finances significantly worsen. That would hurt short-term results, and the stock price is likely to decline.

Still, Royal Caribbean remains a popular option, and demand will likely bounce back. You can see that from the current results.

Its third-quarter load factor, a measure of occupancy, came in at 112%. Load factor is an industry benchmark that divides passenger cruise days by available passenger cruise days.

This helped drive quarterly revenue 9% higher year over year. Management expects the same growth throughout 2026. Bookings remain strong heading into 2027, with next year up by a high-teen percentage.

Coca-Cola (KO -3.00%) belongs to the consumer staples segment. Theoretically, that means demand for its products shouldn't dip much during a recession. However, a significant portion of the company's revenue comes from outside the home (e.g., stadiums, movie theaters, and restaurants).

Naturally, that would affect the company's revenue. It's an extreme case, but stay-at-home orders during the early days of the pandemic significantly hurt Coca-Cola's product volume in the away-from-home market.

But it bounced back when governments lifted restrictions, and it will likely do so again. Despite higher prices presently hurting consumers, they're still buying Coca-Cola products.

You can see that in the company's sales. Most recently, Coca-Cola's third-quarter adjusted revenue grew 7% year over year. Higher volume primarily drove the increase.

Coca-Cola has also reliably increased dividends, an important source of the stock's total return, which could help offset the stock price decline during difficult days. In fact, the company has raised payments for 64 straight years. That makes the company a Dividend King, or one that has increased dividends for at least 50 consecutive years.

Home Depot (HD -0.51%) has the highest sales among home-improvement retailers. With a ubiquitous presence and attractive prices, the retailer draws both do-it-yourself homeowners and professional contractors.

Naturally, demand for its products and services fluctuates with the economy. That's because homeowners typically don't undertake major renovations when they have dim employment prospects. Homebuying, another source of projects, typically wanes during recessions.

Currently, demand for major renovations has slipped as people pay more for everyday items. Additionally, home sales have dimmed as mortgage rates have jumped.

Meanwhile, despite these factors, Home Depot has posted positive same-store sales (comps). Its fiscal third-quarter comps grew 2.2%. The period ended on Nov. 1.

Shareholders can also confidently collect dividends, even during difficult days. Home Depot has raised payments every year since 2010. Even during the Great Recession, the company kept dividends constant from 2007 through 2009.
