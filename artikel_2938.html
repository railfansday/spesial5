Apple Is Beating the Rest of the "Magnificent Seven" in 2026. Can It Do It Again in 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Amazon, Alphabet, and Meta Platforms have pushed their free cash flow into the negative due to AI spending, while Apple has continued to generate cash.

By Daniel Foelber – Nov 27, 2026 at 7:59PM EST

Apple (AAPL +0.83%) is the best-performing "Magnificent Seven" stock of 2026, up nearly 32% year to date. It got there by largely sitting out the data center spending race.

The question for 2027 is whether that edge is priced in.

In the fiscal year ended Sept. 26, Apple posted revenue of $478.8 billion, up 15%  year over year, and diluted earnings per share of $8.98, up 20%.

Apple's capital expenditures (capex) were $9 billion, down from $12.7 billion. It generated about $136.1 billion in free cash flow (FCF).

By comparison, some Magnificent Seven companies have turned FCF negative because of higher spending on artificial intelligence (AI). Amazon (AMZN +3.96%) expects about $220 billion in 2026 capex, Alphabet (GOOGL +0.68%) (GOOG +0.54%) $195 billion to $205 billion, and Meta Platforms (META +3.13%) $135 billion to $145 billion. All three reported negative FCF in their latest quarters.

Instead of spending heavily on capex, Apple's AI costs are reflected in its rising research and development spending -- which jumped 32% to $45.8 billion. Apple's margins are also under pressure from rising memory chip costs, although it has been able to offset those so far with price increases. CEO John Ternus called current memory chip pricing "unprecedented" on the Nov. 2 earnings call and said Apple expects to pay more beyond December.

Consensus analyst estimates expect earnings of $9.43 per share in fiscal 2027, representing about 5% growth. Apple stock trades at 39.9 times earnings at the time of this writing -- which is a steep premium for a moderate growth rate.

Given the AI spending timeline, I could see cash burn continuing for many hyperscalers in 2027. But as Magnificent Seven stocks with less expensive valuations begin converting record spending into earnings, they could look like better buys than Apple.
