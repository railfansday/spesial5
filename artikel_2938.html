Scouting New York Jets offensive tackle Blake Freeland

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

The Jets recently signed former Colts offensive lineman Blake Freeland to their active roster from the Bengals' practice squad. Today, we break him down in detail.

The 25-year old Freeland is listed at 6'7" and 302 pounds and was a fourth round pick out of BYU in 2023. He has started nine games in his career, all of which were for the Colts in his rookie year.

Freeland was a three-star high school recruit but headed to BYU with zero experience of playing on the offensive line having been a quarterback for two years before moving to tight end and defensive end as a senior. He also played basketball and represented his school in shot put and javelin throwing.

On his arrival at BYU, Freeland converted to offensive tackle but didn't see the field until the middle of his freshman season. However, he ended up starting the last seven games and held up reasonably well, as he only allowed one sack.

He didn't begin his sophomore year as a starter, but ended up making a total of eight starts in 2020, during which he showed promise by not allowing a sack all year.

In 2021 and 2022, Freeland started all 26 games and was twice named to Phil Steele's all-Independent Conference first team. He was also a third team AP all-American in 2022.

Heading into the 2023 pre-draft process, Freeland was being touted as a possible day two pick. However, his rawness was exposed during Senior Bowl week. Even so, he managed to stablize his stock with good workouts at the combine and his pro day.

The Colts eventually drafted Freeland in the fourth round and he started off his rookie year on the bench. He soon made his first start due to a week four injury, and by the end of the year had started nine games.

In 2024, he failed to earn a starting role and spent the first half of the season as a healthy scratch. He played in the last eight games, although he only played 23 offensive snaps, 22 of which were in the season finale.

In year three, a serious leg injury during preseason forced Freeland to spend the entire year on injured reserve and then he faced an uphill battle to make the team out of training camp this summer.

After being released in final cuts following preseason, Freeland signed to Cincinnati's practice squad before being poached by the Jets last month following Max Mitchell's concussion.

Let's move onto some more in-depth analysis of what Freeland brings to the table, based on in-depth research and film study.

Freeland is extremely tall, with excellent length, but was not strong enough when he entered the league so has no doubt been trying to add weight and is almost certainly heavier than his listed weight of 302. He managed 25 bench press reps at his pro day.

The rest of his workout numbers are impressive as he ran the 40-yard dash in under five seconds at the combine, then broke the combine record for offensive linemen with a 37-inch vertical jump. His broad jump of 120 inches was also impressive.

Having posted underwhelming agility numbers at the combine, Freeland did those drills again at his pro day and improved them dramatically.

Freeland has extensive experience of playing at both tackle positions. He started two seasons of each in college and has played both during his preseason appearances. At the NFL level, his first two starts were on the left but the other seven were on the right.

He would not be suitable for an interior role and has never played there at any level.

Freeland only allowed two sacks in his entire college career, with a low pressure rate. However, BYU's system operates with a lot of screens and RPO's so he didn't play as many true pass sets as you'd like to see from an incoming prospect.

Not surprisingly, he struggled during his nine starts as a rookie, giving up four sacks.

Talented NFL level players were always going to be able to exploit his lack of experience, and it didn't help his confidence that he faced Aaron Donald in his first NFL start.

Despite his athletic numbers, Freeland can sometimes be slow to get into his stance and susceptible to being beaten by speed rushers around the edge.

However, his biggest concern is his anchor. He can really struggle to repel bull rushers and speed-to-power moves.

Freeland's run blocking grades according to Pro Football Focus were very good when he was in college, but he was less consistent at the NFL level. The 2023 season when Freeland played extensively for the Colts was Jonathan Taylor's least productive of his career and he only had one 100-yard game which came on a day when Freeland didn't play.

Once again, play strength is an issue here, as he will get stood up on the edge and allow his man to fight off his block in the trenches.

He does give a good effort though, and his athletic ability means he can get out in space to good effect.

Freeland had some decent moments on short yardage plays, even though he's not much of a drive blocker. It may suit him that effort is arguably more important than technique in such situations.

Freeland obviously has the athleticism to get out in front of a screen and, as noted, it was something BYU did regularly when he was there.

Considering his speed and agility numbers, it is disappointing to see that Freeland is sometimes stiff and mechanical in his movements.

One other obvious area that needs work is his hand placement. He can be slow to get his hands up and defensive players will take advantage of this.

His other big issue is pad level. This was always going to be an issue for someone as tall as him and he can tend to play with a narrow base so is often overpowered.

Freeland had seven penalties in seven games as a freshman but improved his on-field discipline over the course of his college career with just nine penalties in the last three seasons.

At the NFL level, he's been flagged six times. Four of these were false starts and two were for illegal man downfield. He has zero holding penalties.

Freeland's only role on special teams in college was as a blocker on the placekicking unit and that has been the case in the NFL too. He has had no issues or penalties within this role.

Freeland displayed good patience on stunts as a college player, and has shown he can handle those at the NFL level too.

However, it's in those one-on-one battles – especially as a pass rusher – where his lack of experience can be detrimental as he can be slow to react to or anticipate counters.

Freeland is a player who is known for his toughness and a relentless work ethic. He also has good leadership traits, having been a three-time high school captain – he played two years as a quarterback, remember – and also a captain at BYU.

To miss your entire third season having entered the NFL as someone who was considered a raw developmental prospect is rough luck and Freeland's fractured leg in 2025 has obviously hurt his career.

Prior to this, injuries weren't an issue as he played in 44 games in his college career despite being a healthy scratch for the first seven weeks.

As noted, BYU's scheme used a lot of screens and RPO's and, as such, probably wasn't ideal preparation for entering the NFL. However, Freeland has since had four camps with the Colts and spent a couple of months in Cincinnati this year.

Although current Jets offensive coordinator Frank Reich left the Colts in 2022 and Freeland was drafted by them the following season, there would presumably have been some overlap between his scheme and that of the incoming offensive coordinator Jim Bob Cooter, given that most of the key personnel was the same, so the Jets should have some concepts he's familiar with.

He was a teammate of current Jets cornerback Mory Bamba at BYU and his fellow practice squad members Jimmy Morrissey and Samuel Womack with the Colts.

The Jets picked up Freeland after Mitchell's concussion forced him to go on injured reserve. This meant that they only had three healthy tackles on their active roster, causing them to have to elevate Brant Banks from the practice squad a few times.

Once Freeland is up to speed, he'll likely be active on gameday and could eventually challenge Chukwuma Okorafor to be the swing tackle, given how unimpressive Okorafor has been seen leaving the Steelers. One of them might need to be cut when Mitchell can return, though.

Jets fans will, of course, hope that Armand Membou and Olu Fashanu remain healthy, in which case Freeland wouldn't get a chance to play. However, he could still be brought back as a project player next year.
