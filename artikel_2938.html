Duolingo's 2026 Outlook: AI Personalization Sustains Growth for 65 Million Daily Users

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

This under-the-radar software stock boasts a Superscore of 89 from our Hidden Gems Primary database, part of The Motley Fool's Moneyball Database system. Here's why.

By Anders Bylund – Updated Nov 23, 2026 at 5:33PM EST

You sit down with a mobile app to learn a new language, but you soon realize you are not just learning vocabulary. You are part of an elaborate, data-hungry experiment that spans 65 million daily active users. Duolingo (DUOL +1.43%), a Pittsburgh-based company, has turned language education into a high-engagement, gamified machine. The stock currently trades at $152.31 as of Nov. 19, 2026, though it has experienced a volatile year, with a 15% decline over the last 12 months as of Nov. 23.

Our proprietary Hidden Gems scoring system assigns Duolingo an overall Superscore of 89 out of 100, placing it in the Strong category. This score ranks it in the Top ~1% of all companies we analyze, ahead of roughly 99 out of every 100 stocks in our database. Because the business is highly capital-efficient, it earns substantial profit on a relatively small base of tangible assets, which is a rare quality that allows it to turn each percentage point of revenue growth into outsize returns for shareholders. Investors are often willing to pay a higher multiple for this kind of efficiency, though it does not eliminate the valuation risk inherent in the current share price.

Duolingo has been a difficult stock to love lately. It's down 73% from the all-time peak in May 2025, haunted by slowing user growth and unstable profit margins. Skeptics worry about generative AI stealing Duolingo's lunch money, as chatbots look ready to teach anyone anything, all in a phone app or a browser window.

I think that's a mistake, based on my experience in several language-oriented businesses.

As long as these conditions hold, Duolingo will have a place in the online learning market. The lessons learned from language-learning courses have already yielded a hit in the popular chess curriculum, with 10 million daily active users and a solid setup for future in-app purchases.

And with the help of AI back-end tools, Duolingo plans to launch more non-language courses over time.

"In terms of new courses, we're not announcing new courses at the moment," CEO Luis von Ahn said on the Q3 earnings call. "When we added Chess, already AI helped quite a bit. And we were able to add Chess, idea to being in the app, in about 9 months. We can do that even faster now."

The stock might not follow suit immediately, as Wall Street can be stubborn about both market darlings and underrated winners. But this looks like a great time to start building a Duolingo position for the long haul.
