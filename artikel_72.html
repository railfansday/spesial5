South Korea catches 111 students with new measures against exam cheating

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

How South Korea tackles cheating on its college entrance exam

South Korea's education ministry says 111 candidates were found with unauthorised smart devices during a high-stakes nationwide exam, after officials introduced urgent measures to tackle rising cases of AI-assisted cheating.

Five of the candidates were caught red-handed using smart devices during the exam, authorities said on Friday.

Half a million students sat for the college entry exam, also known as Suneung, which took place this week under the shadow of several recent high-profile cheating scandals.

Prior to the Suneung, authorities announced anti-cheating measures such as emergency alerts sent to every single mobile phone in the country to detect unauthorised devices in exam halls.

On Friday, President Lee Jae Myung called for an overhaul of the exam system to tackle AI-assisted cheating.

In South Korea, public examinations "significantly affect the distribution of opportunities and resources", Lee said, adding that using AI to cheat in such tests "rocks the basic order of our society by undermining fairness and trust".

Concerns over academic dishonesty had been brewing in recent weeks, after reports of candidates cheating with the help of AI devices.

Last month, two candidates were caught cheating with AI glasses during university entrance exams.

The education ministry said earlier this month that four students across middle and high schools were caught using AI glasses to cheat this year.

These fears came to a boil over the weekend, when the top scorer of South Korea's certified tax accountancy exam admitted to cheating by using a miniature camera, mobile phone and AI tools.

His confession came amid speculation over his unusually high score, with just one out of 160 questions wrong.

"I used a microcamera to photograph the test questions and sent them to an AI, then checked my answers on my smartphone to commit the cheating," said the candidate, 34-year-old Han Won-seok, in a video published on Sunday.

His confession, days ahead of the Suneung, prompted authorities to introduce last-minute measures to prevent cheating during the national exam.

The measures included nationwide emergency alerts on the exam day.

Three alerts were sent between 08:00 and 15:00 on Thursday, causing phones across the country to blare out in unison - even if they were on silent mode, and even if they belonged to tourists using data roaming.

The phones did not ring out if they were switched off or on airplane mode.

It was the first time that South Korea tapped on its national disaster alert system to catch exam cheaters.

Candidates also had to cover up the buttons on their shirts and jackets to block any hidden cameras, and authorities conducted inspections of eyewear.

All this made even honest test takers paranoid.

Ahead of the Suneung, one high school senior told the Korea Times that he was worried his horn-rimmed spectacles would be mistaken for smart glasses.

Another told news outlet Yonhap that he usually wore glasses but made an effort to put on contact lenses for the Suneung to avoid raising suspicions.

Held every November, the Suneung is an infamous eight-hour marathon of back-to-back exams.

Students attempt about 200 questions across various subjects, which include Korean, maths, English, social and natural sciences, among others.

The country itself comes to a standstill - construction work halts, planes are grounded and military training gets suspended to facilitate an optimal testing environment.

Many South Koreans see the Suneung as a culmination of years of formal education - and a turning point that determines their university placements, careers, and social statuses.
