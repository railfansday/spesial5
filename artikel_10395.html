Holiday gift guide 2026: 20 thoughtfully curated tech gifts that will charm and delight everyone on your list

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

As the world obsesses over AI and conventional tech products balloon in price, the thought of buying gadgets for anyone this holiday season might feel a little intimidating. But that’s not to say that there aren’t options out there. I often find that the best gifts are charming, focused devices with a touch of style. 

My selection process for this gift guide was entirely subjective. Almost everything on it is a gadget or design object with an element of tactile satisfaction for how it handles an everyday task. You won’t find a boring black rectangle anywhere on the list, and you shouldn’t have to think too hard about the soaring cost of memory.

Apple: The First 50 Years by David Pogue

Published to mark Apple’s half-century this year, veteran tech journalist David Pogue has marked the occasion with a hefty tome that serves as a biography of the company itself. Pogue conducted 150 interviews for the book, including figures like Jony Ive and Steve Wozniak, and the presentation benefits from hundreds of photos throughout. This is a book that’s worth owning in print.

Twelve South Airfly SE

Some airplane seatback screens support Bluetooth, but it’s not something you can always rely on when boarding a long-haul flight, as many screens still won’t connect to noise-canceling headphones. Twelve South’s AirFly SE is an inexpensive dongle that plugs into your seat’s headphone jack and transmits the audio to your cans. You’ll never need to use airline earbuds again.

Sharge Flow Mini Go

Sharge’s whole thing is designing utilitarian gadgets with a stylish twist, and the Flow Mini Go is one I can recommend to anyone who ever finds themselves running low on a charge. It has a 5,800-milliampere-hour (mAh) battery, 22.5-watt charging and a built-in cable, as well as an option to attach it directly to your phone’s USB-C port. Just as importantly, it’s only about as big as a lipstick tube. 

Nothing Ear (a)

At $79, Nothing’s Ear (a) model is my pick for affordable wireless earbuds. They sound great and look even better, especially if you opt for the construction-equipment-yellow pair. Even the charging case is cool, with a design inspired by medicinal blister packs.

Logitech MX Master 4 Developer Edition

If you know someone who uses a mouse all day but has never really thought about the actual mouse they’re using, the MX Master 4 might just change their life. The MX Master series has long been considered the leading productivity mouse due to its unique free-spinning scroll wheel, and the latest model adds a customizable haptic thumb panel that buzzes to confirm a shortcut. The new Developer Edition adds a “Hello, World” prompt and a reference to the Linux kernel’s release date.

BOOX Palma 3

The Boox Palma 3 is an E Ink reader the size and shape of a smartphone, offering a convenient antidote to doomscrolling that you can take anywhere. Because it runs on Android, you can easily add your Kindle and Kobo libraries straight from the Google Play store. This latest version features a streamlined aluminum design and stylus support.

8BitDo Retro Mechanical Keyboard

8BitDo is best known for its excellent range of gaming controllers, and its first mechanical keyboard turned out to be just as haptically satisfying. Launched in versions that evoked the Nintendo Entertainment System and the Famicom, the lineup now includes models inspired by the Commodore 64 and the IBM Model M.. Each comes with enormous programmable buttons you can place next to the keyboard, making copying and pasting feel like a session at the arcade.

Balmuda The Lantern

Balmuda is a hip Japanese appliance maker best known for its minimalist steam toaster, but its equally obsessive take on the camping lantern may be a more practical gift. It has a single rotary dial that takes you from a warm, flickering candle-style effect at the low end to a brighter reading light when you turn it up. The light is diffused through a pipe for a soft glow, and its rechargeable battery means you can use it anywhere from the bedroom to the balcony.

Analogue Pocket

The Analogue Pocket feels like it came from an alternate universe where Apple designed the Game Boy. It has a gorgeous stripped-down design, an amazing high-resolution screen, and programmable hardware that has been tuned to provide the most authentic reproduction possible of Game Boy and Game Boy Advance games. The newer Analogue 3D does much the same thing for the Nintendo 64, if your gift recipient is more of a couch potato.

Lego Game Boy

Lego’s Game Boy is another meticulous reproduction of the Game Boy, but from a totally different angle. This kit is incredibly fun to build, making you build up the hardware from the circuit board level and work on the mechanisms of the satisfyingly squishy buttons and D-pad. As a display piece, you can swap out cartridges and lenticular screen inserts for Super Mario Land and The Legend of Zelda: Link’s Awakening.

Backbone One

The BackBone One has been around for a while, but together with its Pro version, it still remains the top name in smartphone game controllers. And the proposition is more compelling than it used to be, with modern phones now powerful enough to run solid versions of high-end console games like Assassin’s Creed Mirage and Death Stranding. Backbone offers models with both PlayStation and Xbox controller layouts, depending on your preference.

Aura Carver Mat

Digital photo frames have been a clichéd throwaway gift for a long time, which is why Aura’s products stand out. The Carver Mat is a landscape, color-calibrated matte screen with a paper-textured border that automatically dims its lighting to match the ambient surroundings, making it feel much more natural than other digital frames. It can display two portrait photos side-by-side and even supports Live Photos from an iPhone. 

Kodak Charmera

The Kodak Charmera hits on two recent trends: Labubu-style blind-box collectibles and the throwback use of lo-fi digicams. Each $35 box contains one of seven retro designs, and the tiny camera itself has several filters and frames. The image quality is not great, of course, but the Charmera is well-designed and well-named—it’s the perfect stocking stuffer.

Fujifilm Instax Pal 2

If you like the idea of a tiny pocket camera but want the results to be a little more usable, Fujifilm has you covered with the $169 Instax Pal 2. It’s shaped like a vintage range finder, like many of Fujifilm’s more advanced mirrorless cameras—but even though it fits in the palm of your hand, it makes room for an optical viewfinder, a 1-inch display, a command dial for color effects, and a lever to swap between aspect ratios. Photos can be transferred directly to your phone or to an Instax Link printer.

Peak Design Mobile Tripod

The biggest problem with tripods is finding a way to take them with you. But here’s one you won’t even notice in your jeans pocket. Peak Design’s take on the mobile tripod folds three legs and a full-on ball head into a package that’s about 5 millimeters thick. It snaps onto your MagSafe-compatible phone with minimal obtrusion, and unfolds into a stand you can angle for group shots, video calls, or hyper-lapses.

Native Union Pop cables and phone

I’ve long been a fan of Paris-based Native Union, which puts out some of the most thoughtfully designed tech accessories around. Its coiled Pop Cables are particularly great, avoiding tangles and saving desk space—and now the company has revived its original Pop Phone, giving you an old-school handset to conduct your Zoom calls like you’re in an episode of Mad Men. There’s even a hang-up button.

Xiaomi Portable Electric Air Compressor

If you know anyone who rides a bicycle and plays basketball, this gadget might just change their life. For everyone else, it’s still worth keeping around—I’ve recommended it to countless friends. This USB-C-powered air compressor has modes tailored to all sorts of devices and displays the real-time pressure rating on its LCD screen.

Nintendo Sound Clock: Alarmo

Trust Nintendo to come up with a bizarrely useful take on something as mundane as the alarm clock. Anyone who shares a bed with a partner may consider it a hard sell to start each morning with sounds from Mario Kart or The Legend of Zelda. But the motion-sensing feature alone is a game changer for the groggy moments before getting up; the screen stays switched off until you turn your head to check the time.

Teenage Engineering Pocket Operator

Swedish design company Teenage Engineering first made its name through its iconoclastic synthesizers, and it sounds like the company might be moving on from that heritage. If that’s the case, now could be a good time to stock up on its $99 Pocket Operator series. It’s a range of exposed circuit boards with clicky switches, rotary dials, and monochrome LCD panels, all amounting to remarkably capable instruments.

Yukai Engineering Nekojita FuFu

In Japan, if you’re sensitive to hot food or liquids, you’re said to have a nekojita—in other words, a cat’s tongue. Yukai Engineering is attempting to solve this condition with a tiny, rechargeable cat that attaches to the rim of your coffee cup or ramen bowl. Its internal fan drops the liquid’s temperature to a drinkable level in just a few minutes, and the cat looks cute in the process.
