HBO's 6-Episode Sci-Fi Masterpiece Predicted The Future Almost 10 Years Ago

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Nearly 10 years ago, a 6-episode science fiction TV show on HBO predicted the future, and that's probably not a good thing. Forget robots, alien invasions, and Black Mirror-style reality warps; the most frightening sci-fi TV shows are the ones that are only slightly removed from reality.

It's those science fiction series that take reality and stretch it just a bit further that can be the most discomforting, because the predictions they make are all too comprehensible. HBO has one of these types of sci-fi shows that touches on the next few years of society, and its predictions are as grim as the story is thrilling.

The aptly named Years and Years, an HBO/BBC One co-production, begins during the same year it came out, in 2019. Episode 2 jumps to 2025, and each season afterward takes place in the successive year. As time passes, the world changes. Technologically, in small ways, with the advent of burgeoning artificial intelligence and computerized systems.

Societally, things change rapidly and monumentally. International disputes, resource scarcity, and the growing climate crisis have pushed the world into an untenable state. We see the slow collapse of functioning society through the eyes of the Lyons, a Manchester-based family, who are struggling with their own internal issues as they deal with the ones afflicting England and the rest of the world.

Years and Years ties in real-world events, including, in particular, the 2024 election of Donald Trump, which the series manages to accurately predict five years earlier. From there, alt-right leaders around the world begin to shape the lives of the people, including the Lyons, in dangerous ways, and worse, in eerily recognizable ways.

Years and Years does not use technological inventions or fantastical events to kickstart the downward trend of society. The issues in Years and Years extend from a confluence of artificial intelligence stealing labor, resource scarcity, increasingly paranoid world leaders, and climate change.

This has led to mass unrest, large-scale immigration, and a deep-seated fear that leads some to seek relief from the most deplorable leaders. In England, that person is Vivienne "Viv" Rook (Emma Thompson), a businesswoman and celebrity who uses foul, xenophobic, and brutal language to disparage the "others" in England and sow fear and anger.

The result of her leadership, and similar populist anger across the globe, leads to nuclear war, mass incarceration and deaths, and a swirling vortex of crime and mayhem imposed upon immigrants as well as citizens. Nearly 10 years later, scenes of immigrants being rounded up into poorly staffed and underfunded camps lined with chain-link fences don't seem so fantastical. Nor do moments of characters losing their jobs and money due to artificial intelligence, with little in the way of recompense.

Years and Years has an 89% on Rotten Tomatoes, but if you ask most people, they probably haven't heard of it. It's a criminally underrated masterpiece and a series that deserves a bigger audience. It's unique in its scope, jumping into the future every episode and only giving the viewers snippets of the Lyons family's lives.

These snippets are more than enough to explain what's going on with the rest of the world, and they allow for some truly shocking moments in the story. The science fiction elements of Years and Years are modest, but they are all the more important for how they are expertly woven into the plot and themes.
