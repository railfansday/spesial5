Sources: Everton exploring cost-effective Paul Mendy deal

Everton will be resistant to making a big move for Cagliari striker Paul Mendy in January, sources have told Football Insider.

The 19-year-old has been linked with a surprise move to the Toffees, but it is understood that they view him as more of a long-term target at the moment.

Mendy only broke into the Cagliari team back in February, and is deemed to be a more cost-effective solution than some of Everton's other targets.

However, sources say that the club feel it would be "too big a gamble" to pin their hopes on Mendy in the second half of the season.

The Merseysiders feel that Mendy has a big future ahead of him, but would prefer to add someone who is more "tried and tested" this winter.

Mendy has struck one goal and two assists from 12 games in Serie A so far this term, and is likely to continue developing with Cagliari for now.

Mendy has enjoyed a breakthrough six months with Cagliari, and it has grown his reputation beyond the confines of Serie A.

He won his first senior caps for Senegal in November, and netted his first international goal with a late strike against Sudan in AFCON qualifying.

Everton have identified Mendy as a striker who could have a huge future ahead of him at the top level, but they have a limited sample size in terms of what they have seen from him so far.

David Moyes' side are desperate to bring in support for Thierno Barry at the top of the pitch, but Mendy is being seen as a step too far at the moment.

It would be a big step up for him to immediately perform in the Premier League, but that will not stop Everton from paying close attention to his progress across the next 12 months.

Former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness told Football Insider on Wednesday that the Toffees' Hill Dickinson Stadium is a "shoo-in" to host the Nations League finals if the FA can win the rights.

The FA have already made their interest in hosting the event clear, should England get past Norway in the quarter-finals in March.

Wyness believes that the Hill Dickinson is the obvious choice to host the event, given that Wembley is already being used and the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium is understood to not be preferrable for the FA.

The FA are currently battling against rival nations for the hosting rights, but should they win out, then the event could be on Merseyside from 9 June until 13 June next summer.
