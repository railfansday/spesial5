Michael Morrison reveals major Gianfranco Zola error at Birmingham City

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Former Birmingham City captain Michael Morrison has revealed a major error which arose during Gianfranco Zola's ill-fated reign in charge at St. Andrew's @ Knighthead Park between December 2016 and April 2017.

Birmingham are currently a club on the up, with Chris Davies' side topping the Championship for the first time in 18 years over the course of the international break.

Even though Knighthead Capital Management have pumped millions of pounds into various aspects of the club, locals in this part of the Second City haven't lost sight of what was a previously torrid era.

Constant turnover of managers, players and ownership issues were major contributing factors to said demise, with Zola's short time at St. Andrew's epitomising such chaos, as Blues went from potential play-off contenders under to requiring a final-day relegation escape in the same season.

Zola was appointed as Gary Rowett's successor in December 2015 in rather controversial circumstances shortly after a takeover at St. Andrew's from Birmingham Sports Holdings Limited.

Birmingham were seventh in the Championship at the time when the former Chelsea icon and ex-West Ham United and Watford boss signed a two-and-a-half-year contract.

His first match in charge came two days later against high-flying Brighton and Hove Albion in a televised encounter, which Blues lost 2-1 despite taking the lead through Lukas Jutkiewicz.

Featuring on the 'Made in Brum' podcast, Morrison, who made 183 appearances for the club, has revealed that Zola made a glaring error in his first meeting with the players at Wast Hills.

"Sometimes you'd go 'Gianfranco Zola, this is going to be amazing', but in my experience, when you bring them people in, they need to have good people around them," Morrison explained.

"I didn't think he brought in very good staff around him, and that was part of the problem.

"In Gianfranco's first meeting, I remember meeting him and he said 'I'm not going to change too much in a short space of time, we'll see how it goes, we won't be out on the grass too long', and we were out on the grass for two hours, which is a long time to be out before a game, and then we conceded in the last minute.

"Straight away you're like 'you've flogged us there'. If you hadn't had done that, and that's when I talk about wins going by.

"I remember in the first meeting, he's talking about Brighton's team: 'they've got this player, that player. They've got Sam B*****s up front, and we're like 'don't you mean Sam Baldock', who was the Championship's top scorer at the time," the former Blues man added.

"Straight away you're kind of laughing, but if this guy is getting the top scorer wrong on his first day, you think it could be a disaster, and it turned out to be that."

As Morrison alluded to, Zola's reign was nothing short of disastrous, even though Birmingham had the crux of an experienced Championship side and attempted to add further quality in the January window with the additions of Emilio Nsue, Craig Gardner, Cheik Keita and Kerim Frei.

However, Zola won just two of his 24 matches in charge, with it taking 10 of those for the first victory to come against Fulham in February 2017.

He then resigned two months later after an Easter Monday defeat to Burton Albion, saying: "It is not that I like quitting, but Birmingham deserves better."

Since then, the Oliena native hasn't returned to management, whilst Harry Redknapp was appointed as his successor, although he, like Zola, would find his St. Andrew's tenure extremely short-lived.

Ultimately, Birmingham continued to go through several managers after Zola's exit, with multiple years of stagnation in the Championship resulting in a relegation to League One in May 2024, less than a year after Knighthead's takeover.

An even costlier decision was made during that particular season too, as John Eustace was sacked in October 2023 despite the fact Birmingham were sixth in the table, with high-profile replacement, Wayne Rooney, lasting less than three months in charge, winning just two of his 15 games.

However, the club's American investors have since learned from their own mistakes by keeping faith in Chris Davies, who has refined his tactical approach this season, and it has certainly got Birmingham fans dreaming of promotion after an extremely encouraging start.
