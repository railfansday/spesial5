Intuit vs. Oracle: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Intuit's sticky tax and small business ecosystem faces off against Oracle's push into AI cloud capacity, and the trade-offs between them look starkly different.

By Sara Appino – Updated Nov 21, 2026 at 6:40PM EST

Investors often find themselves choosing between specialized software leaders and broad infrastructure giants. Deciding whether to buy Intuit (INTU -12.77%) or Oracle (ORCL -8.48%) depends on your preference for consumer stability versus enterprise growth.

Intuit serves as the backbone for personal and small business finances, while Oracle provides the essential databases and cloud infrastructure that large organizations require. Both companies are currently integrating artificial intelligence to enhance their platforms. This comparison examines their growth rates, balance sheets, and relative valuations to determine which stock fits best in a 2027 outlook.

Intuit provides essential financial technology solutions for millions of global consumers and small businesses through its core brands like TurboTax, QuickBooks, and Credit Karma. The company currently serves approximately 93 million users and relies on major distribution platforms like the Apple (AAPL -1.90%) App Store and Google Play Store. To support its vast software ecosystem, it maintains strategic partnerships with major cloud infrastructure providers including Amazon (AMZN -4.10%) and Alphabet (GOOGL -2.70%) (NASDAQ:GOOG).

In the 12 months ended Oct. 31, 2026, revenue reached nearly $21.9 billion, representing growth of close to 12.8% compared to the prior twelve-month period. Net income for this time frame was approximately $4.8 billion, reflecting the company's ability to maintain high net margins while expanding its user base. This steady financial expansion is largely driven by consistent, recurring demand for its digital accounting and tax preparation platforms among mid-market businesses.

As of its July 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is nearly 0.4x, a metric that compares total debt against the value of shareholder equity to assess financial leverage. The current ratio is approximately 1.5x, reflecting a healthy ability to pay short-term obligations using its most liquid assets. In the fiscal year ended July 31, 2026, free cash flow reached nearly $8.7 billion, though note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 23.3% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Oracle offers enterprise software, cloud applications, and infrastructure services through a global delivery model that serves corporations, government agencies, and educational institutions. The company distributes its complex database systems and cloud applications in more than 145 countries and operates through a network of resellers and system integrators. It recently focused on expanding its presence in tech stocks by building out massive data center capacity specifically designed for high-performance artificial intelligence workloads.

In the 12 months ended Aug. 31, 2026, revenue reached close to $71.8 billion, a significant growth rate of approximately 21.6%. Net income for this period was nearly $18.9 billion, showcasing the scale of its operations as it transitions legacy customers to its newer cloud models. This robust increase in sales is largely attributed to the rapid adoption of its cloud infrastructure services and the ongoing modernization of enterprise data systems.

As of its May 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 3.7x, indicating that the company uses a substantial amount of debt relative to its equity to fund its capital-intensive operations. The current ratio is roughly 1.1x, suggesting that its ability to meet immediate financial obligations is currently lean compared to more liquid software rivals. In the fiscal year ended May 31, 2026, free cash flow was a negative $23.7 billion because its massive investments in data center equipment and physical assets exceeded the cash generated from day-to-day operations.

Oracle contends with fierce competition in the cloud and software industry from giants like Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%) and Amazon. The company deals with a complex supply chain and potential volatility from international operations, including geopolitical instability and trade restrictions that could affect global deployments. Its large-scale transition to a cloud-first strategy requires consistent execution and significant capital spending to avoid losing market share to more specialized infrastructure providers.

With a slightly lower Forward P/E relative to future earnings estimates and a more attractive P/S ratio, Intuit currently trades at a valuation discount relative to Oracle.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

I'd go with Intuit. Small businesses build their books, payroll, and payments around its software, which makes it hard to leave and gives Intuit a dependable base to grow from. And it is now beginning to win larger companies. QuickBooks, TurboTax, and Credit Karma are brands many people already know, and its software for mid-sized businesses is growing about 40% year over year.

What I like is that Intuit keeps returning cash to shareholders, with a dividend that rose 15% over the past year, and it is building AI into its products, so customers can now use QuickBooks inside AI tools like ChatGPT and Claude. Its latest quarter also came in above the company's own targets, and it expects solid growth this year.

To give Oracle its due, it rents out the computing power AI companies need, and its cloud infrastructure sales have more than doubled year over year. But it is borrowing heavily to build data centers and burning cash while it does, and a big share of its contracts comes from one customer, OpenAI.

Intuit's growth, by contrast, comes with healthy profits and cash returned to shareholders, not a heavy debt load. For long-term investors, a profitable software company that is expanding into bigger businesses makes Intuit the better buy for 2027.
