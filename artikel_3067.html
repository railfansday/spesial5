Axon Enterprise vs. Okta: Which Security Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Two security giants, one physical and one digital, with very different risk profiles and valuation gaps.

Choosing between a public safety titan and a digital identity leader requires weighing physical security against digital infrastructure. Both Axon Enterprise (AXON +4.26%) and Okta Inc. (OKTA +2.18%) offer high-growth potential in essential markets.

Axon Enterprise focuses on equipping law enforcement with hardware and data management, while Okta secures user access across cloud environments. They are often compared because both leverage software-as-a-service models to build recurring revenue and deep customer loyalty.

Axon Enterprise has transformed from a weapon manufacturer into a comprehensive technology provider for the defense stocks and public safety landscape. The company sells Taser devices alongside body-worn cameras and a cloud-based evidence management platform. Its customers include federal, state, and local law enforcement agencies globally, with a direct sales force that recently expanded through exclusive distribution rights for drones.

In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, revenue reached about $2.8 billion, representing a significant increase of approximately 33.5% compared with the prior fiscal year. The company reported net income ofaround $124.9 million, maintaining a positive bottom line even as it invests heavily in new product lines. This growth highlights the increasing adoption of digital tools by public safety agencies worldwide.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.6x, a metric that compares total debt to the value of shareholder ownership. The current ratio, which measures the ability to pay short-term obligations using current assets, is approximately 2.5x. Free cash flow for the fiscal year was about $75.1 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 300.1% of operating cash flow, meaning reported cash generation is heavily inflated by this non-cash add-back.

Okta operates in the competitive identity and access management market, helping organizations manage secure logins for employees and customers. The company provides a cloud-native platform that integrates with thousands of applications, making it a central hub for corporate security. It serves more than 20,000 customers, ranging from small businesses to massive global enterprises, through a mix of direct sales and partner ecosystems.

In the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, revenue reached approximately $2.9 billion, which is an 11.8% increase over the previous year. Okta reported net income of roughly $235.0 million, a notable improvement in profitability as the business scales its software platform. This growth is driven by the steady demand for secure, automated identity verification in an increasingly remote work environment.

As of its January 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is close to 0.1x, indicating a very low level of debt relative to equity. The current ratio stands at approximately 1.4x, suggesting the company has enough liquid assets to cover its upcoming liabilities. Free cash flow for the period was nearly $863.0 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 61.5% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since stock-based compensation is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Axon Enterprise faces risks related to its heavy dependence on law enforcement budgets and the public acceptance of its Taser products. The company must navigate rapid technological shifts, including artificial intelligence integration and cybersecurity threats to its sensitive data. Furthermore, it deals with regulatory and litigation risks involving firearm classifications and use-of-force incidents. Competitors like Motorola Solutions (MSI +1.49%) also vie for similar government contracts.

Axon Enterprise appears more attractively valued than its peer when looking at sales multiples, though both companies command premium valuations for their growth. The P/S ratio measures market capitalization against sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

Okta should enjoy revenue growth of about 10% this fiscal year to $3.2 billion, with a roughly 50% improvement in net income to more than $350 million, according to consensus analyst estimates.

Longer-term, enterprise agentic adoption is prompting companies to think more about the importance of identity security as a critical part of their corporate defenses. Analysts also see AI-driven opportunities from Okta's agentic products, as well as strong traction with newer products such as Okta Identity Governance and Okta Privileged Access. Deal sizes for AI-specific deals are larger than the company's average, and management says such deals are increasingly the topics of conversation with new and existing clients. That bodes well for the future.

Axon Enterprise has a fine outlook for growth as law enforcement is increasingly equipped with Tasers and the public is becoming increasingly demanding that agents wear body cameras. Its cloud-based evidence control system is also an increasingly sought-after service for law enforcement organizations.

Expansion into Europe and Canada should also help, as should a focus on producing personal consumer-protection devices. In particular, all the data and video created by Axon customers have to be managed, and the company is seeing strong demand for its AI-based management systems from customers who find many of the services create efficiencies within their operations.

In fiscal 2026, sales are seen growing well, up about a third to $3.7 billion, with net income of around $290 million and free cash flow of $425 million, well over 2025's levels.

Both stocks are priced like growth stocks according to their forward P/E ratios, above. Okta's sales growth, however, isn't as growth-stock-like as we like to see. Axon is delivering more top-line growth, which makes it the choice here.
