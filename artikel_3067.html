Don't Need Your RMDs? Why Investing Them Could Be Your Smartest Move Yet

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

There's a benefit to putting your RMDs to work in a taxable brokerage account.

By Maurie Backman – Nov 27, 2026 at 6:36AM EST

If you have a pile of money sitting in a traditional retirement account, you can't just leave it untouched forever, even if you don't need it and would rather pass it along to your heirs. Contributions to accounts like traditional IRAs and 401(k)s are made on a pre-tax basis. But the IRS eventually wants its cut. That's where required minimum distributions (RMDs) come in.

RMDs apply to traditional retirement accounts starting at age 73 or 75, depending on your year of birth. And they can be a huge hassle from a tax perspective.

Converting a traditional IRA or 401(k) to a Roth IRA before RMDs begin could solve the problem. But not everyone is a candidate for a Roth conversion, and you may not be able to do one in full.

If you're stuck taking RMDs you don't need, all isn't lost, though. One savvy move on your part could help turn an otherwise annoying situation into an opportunity.

While the IRS might force you to take money out of your retirement account, it can't force you to spend it. If you don't need your RMDs, investing them in a taxable brokerage account could be a solid move, especially if your goal is to leave behind a sizable inheritance.

Under current federal tax rules, inherited taxable investment assets generally receive a stepped-up cost basis to their fair market value upon the original owner's date of death. So let's say you invest $100,000 of RMDs since you don't need the money to cover your costs. If that $100,000 grows to be worth $200,000, your heirs could inherit your taxable brokerage account at a $200,000 cost basis.

If your heirs then immediately sell those assets, they shouldn't owe capital gains taxes. And if they wait to sell, they'll only owe capital gains taxes on appreciation that happens after your passing.

People with large legacy goals often focus on Roth conversions, since inheriting a Roth IRA can be more advantageous than inheriting a traditional IRA. But if a Roth conversion doesn't make sense for you, consider investing your RMDs in a taxable account and earmarking them for your loved ones.

You'll still have to deal with taxes on your RMDs if you go this route. But you could end up sparing your heirs a major tax headache down the line in return.
