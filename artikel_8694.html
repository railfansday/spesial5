A winter storm can cut off your heat and power for several days, so prepare before it hits. Stock water, food that needs no cooking, medicine, flashlights, and a charged power bank, choose a safe way to stay warm, and learn how to keep your pipes from freezing.

Once the heat is off, the water in your pipes can freeze, expand, and split them.

Homeowners insurance claims for water damage and freezing averaged $15,400 from 2019 through 2023, the Insurance Information Institute reports. The other danger is carbon monoxide, a gas you can’t see or smell, which builds up when you heat with a gas oven or run a grill, camp stove, or generator indoors.

Picture two neighbors without power for three days. One drips the faucets and keeps the family by a wood stove, and no pipes freeze. Next door, a pipe in an outside wall splits and floods the basement.

Last, find your main water shutoff, the valve that stops water from pouring out of a burst pipe. Look where the water line enters the house, in the basement or crawl space, or next to the water heater.
