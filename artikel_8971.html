The Design Village: Educating the Next Generation of Designers for Social Transformation

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

What if a design school were designed the way we hope designers will one day design the world: starting from people, place, planet and purpose rather than from convention?In 2011, a small group of practiconers and academics set out to answer that question on the outskirts of Delhi. The result was The Design Village (TDV), an independent design school in Noida, India. It was built from first principles as a response to the gaps in design education and it now has a European campus in Lombardy, Italy. This book tells the story of that experiment. It is part manifesto, part memoir, and part working guide to design education of the 21st Century.

What if a design school were designed the way we hope designers will one day design the world: starting from people, place, planet and purpose rather than from convention?In 2011, a small group of practiconers and academics set out to answer that question on the outskirts of Delhi. It was built from first principles as a response to the gaps in design education and it now has a European campus in Lombardy, Italy. This book tells the story of that experiment. It is part manifesto, part memoir, and part working guide to design education of the 21st Century.

Co-authors Mridu Sahai Patnaik, Paul Hekkert - co-founders of TDV, trace how a school can be organised around three convictions. It can be impact-first, measuring success by what students do for society. It can be pedagogy-centric, treating how we learn as a design problem in its own right. And it can be unreasonably reciprocal, building a learning community where students, faculty, industry and the neighbourhood all give to and learn from each other.Promoting a non silo approach, the book follows real studios, projects and villaging (character building). It looks honestly at the tension between idealism and running a school, and at what it takes to keep an educational vision intact as it grows across continents.With a foreword by design theorist Don Norman, the book is meant for educators, architects, designers, students and anyone who believes that the way we train designers shapes the cities, objects and systems we will all live with. It is an invitation to see design education not as preparation for a profession but as a practice of social transformation.
