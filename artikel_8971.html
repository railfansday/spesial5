Inside Edward Said’s World

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

A new film about the Palestinian thinker, reviewed by a former students of his, and when does a documentary become an art performance?

‘Tis the season for great documentaries. Twenty years after the death of Palestinian literary critic and theorist Edward Said, a new film revisits his life and legacy. Moustafa Bayoumi, a student of Said himself, posits that it offers a new generation a glimpse of Said’s indomitable energy and inexhaustible fight.

The lovely Eileen G’Sell takes a look at Trisha Ziff’s latest documentary, Frida: The Girl That Just Doesn’t Go Away, which screened recently at DOC NYC. The film considers the commercial afterlife of Frida Kahlo, asking how the artist’s personal history has been whitewashed even as her image circulates endlessly through retail culture. What happens when an artist’s legacy becomes as much a consumer product as a creative one?

And finally, if you haven’t seen Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s You Can See Everything, what are you waiting for? It may be the best film I’ve seen all year, full of unexpected twists, turns, and glorious awkward pauses. Though billed as a documentary, the film raises what I think is a more intriguing question: Is Fielder a performance artist playing a documentarian? Not to toot my own horn, but I explore that possibility and more in my recent review, though be warned: spoilers abound.

As an art historian, it’s hard not to relate what you see in everyday life to history — not in the sense that every lily pad recalls Monet or snowballs bring David Hammons to mind, but in the sense that sometimes art really does imitate life. This happened to me recently, maybe 10 minutes into Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s You Can See Everything. I watched Fielder hold painful, prolonged eye contact with Elizabeth Holmes, the convicted fraudster whose eerily large blue eyes seem to enact their own kind of performance. Naturally, I thought of Marina Abramović’s “The Artist is Present” (2010). Perhaps it’s an obvious, pretentious comparison. But hear me out: Is Fielder a documentarian, or is he a performance artist playing one? | Rebecca Schiffman
