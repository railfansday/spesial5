Despite off day, Bills too much to handle for Dolphins in Week 11

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

In the immediate aftermath of the Buffalo Bills' Week 11 win over the Miami Dolphins, many will scratch their heads wondering what they watched unfold Sunday afternoon. To be clear her: What a strange win by the Bills. Buffalo's offense barely resembled the group most have watched in 2026.

It's important to step back a moment, however, and realize that Buffalo has built a good, often great team. The Bills haven't trailed in a game since October 18 against the Las Vegas Raiders (hat tip: Carson Hayek). Buffalo has also three consecutive games by 17 or more points. What about the defense? Well, that group hasn't allowed a touchdown in three straight games.

It may be true that the Bills didn't execute their most impressive game plan of the season, and that they strayed too far away from their true identity until crunch time. It's clear that quarterback Josh Allen didn't have his typically dominant effort against the Dolphins, even though he did throw for 304 yards, but he didn't need to play lights out.

Interestingly, Allen attempted zero rushes. In fact, during a first-half 4th & 1 situation, Allen handed the ball off to running back James Cook III, who scampered through the right side of the line to move the chains. The intent seemed clear that the move was to avoid exposing Allen to unnecessary contact, at least. That move can only work so well when you ask the quarterback to drop back 45 times. As a result, Miami's defense hit Allen six times, sacking him on three occasions.

Most believed that Buffalo would use their dominant rushing attack to try and wear down the Dolphins' trio of backup defensive tackles in the first half. It was tough sledding early, perhaps surprisingly to Brady and the offense, and Cook had just 13 carries for 36 yards and a touchdown at halftime. Allen was forced into two consecutive interceptions late in the second quarter, with a third erased due to penalty.

Then consider the decisions on special teams. First there was the surprising fake punt that proved successful thanks to a pretty 30-yard throw and completion by rookie punter Tommy Doman Jr. Doubling down on surprise (as much as is possible in today's rulebook), the Bills then decided to declare an onside kick attempt after a score. Analyst Adam Archuleta wondered if they saw something on film to warrant the try, and if they'd take the delay-of-game penalty instead of running it. We know now, they didn't attempt the onside try.

As has been the case in so many games this season, the second half was a different story on offense. The return to form just took a bit longer. Late in the fourth quarter, Archuleta mentioned the curious shift in game plan on offense. Understandably, they had entered four-minute offense territory, but the point was well-understood as a discourse on their pass-heavy approach early in action.

The Bills looked like the better team all game long, but it also seemed like they missed out on a lot of points they'd otherwise score. They don't call the NFL a week-to-week league for nothing, and divisional games are always tough to predict regardless of record and any familiarity (or lack thereof) with the opponent.

I keep being called back to the strangeness of this game. Is it possible they were using Week 11 as an opportunity to put a fair amount of new things/alternate tendencies on tape for the gauntlet facing them the next month? Certainly, there's potential for it to be the case.

It's true that Buffalo has a very short week to prepare for the 8-2 Kansas City Chiefs, which could have played into the offensive game plan when you consider Allen's shift to playing dropback, quick-release football from the pocket — that also erased him from the rushing column.

I'm not the only one to consider this move, as evidenced by a tweet from ESPN Bills beat reporter Nick Suss that wondered if they were "giving the Chiefs a crapton of looks they have to prepare for on a short week, even if the Bills don't plan on using any of them again on Thursday."

Is it possible that situation played into the decisions made in Week 11? Again, One Bills Drive will never admit to it, and rightfully so, but it's possible that they used today's game as an opportunity to stress KC's pre-game load ahead of Thursday night.

True or otherwise, it's difficult to spend too much time ripping apart a team that blows out its opponent, while covering the enormous 16.5-point spread. The Bills continue looking really good even on off days, and Buffalo once again prove to be too much to handle for the Dolphins. You could say that about most of their opponents right now.

Will that the case Thursday? We'll find out if the Bills can add to the right kind of "just deserts" for your Thanksgiving Day celebration!
