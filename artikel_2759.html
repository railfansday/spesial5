Jayson Tatum owns late technical that sealed Boston's loss to Denver

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

To be clear: Jayson Tatum's technical foul with 6.4 seconds left in regulation wasn't the reason Boston lost to Denver on Wednesday night. The Nuggets dominated the second half, and when they doubled Tatum, the Celtics' offense struggled to be efficient, especially when their 3-point shooting went cold.

But after a Jamal Murray bucket that put the Nuggets up 104-101 with 6.4 seconds left, Tatum was hit with a controversial technical for throwing the ball toward the official John Goble, who wasn't looking at him, rather than handing it to baseline official, Eric Dalen, who was near him. Tatum and the Celtics were frustrated about a no-call on a screen to get Murray open for the shot and Dalen saw Tatum's toss as throwing the ball at an official in frustration. Tatum said postgame that was not the case.

"I told [Dalen], I wasn't mad. Wasn't a foul on me, we just called a timeout," Tatum said. "John Goble called a timeout and pointed at our bench. Thought he was looking this way, but yeah, he called a tech. For me, just find the female ref that was there, or the other ref, and whoever was probably closest, I should have just gave them the ball."

Murray sank the technical free throw, making it a four point game, and that was essentially the ballgame. The Heat could not just tie it in one possession.

After the game, coach Joe Mazzulla said Tatum has to be better as the team leader.

"We can't put ourselves in that position to even give the refs an opportunity to interpret it a certain type of way," Mazzulla said from the podium. "Gotta be better."

Tatum dropped 20 on the Nuggets in the first half. However, Denver focused more on doubling him in the second half, and he scored just four points on 2-of-6 shooting with five turnovers. Mazzulla said Tatum needs to be better under pressure than he was in the second half. Boston also needs to find a way to score more efficiently when defenses focus on him, because that is what will happen in the playoffs. There is a long time to go between now and the start of the postseason, but this is when coaches and teams need to build the good habits and patterns they will need in the NBA's second season.
