Delaware Blue Coats check-in: Labaron Philon lights it up

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Philadelphia 76ers' season has largely lived up to expectations so far. With such a loaded roster, it was inevitable that several players would struggle to find consistent playing time. As a result, the Sixers have sent some of their young talent down to Wilmington, Delaware, for valuable G League reps, including rookie Labaron Philon.

So, how have these players performed when given extended opportunities? Let's dive into the Delaware Blue Coats, who kicked off their season in recent weeks, and take a look at some of the bigger storylines emerging in the G League.

The Sixers, as expected, haven't used Philon much during his first dozen or so career games. The Sixers sent their rookie down to Delaware to play in their Nov. 13 matchup against the Maine Celtics. Philon would get the starting nod in this game, playing 36 minutes and recording 20 points, nine rebounds, eight assists and a steal on 7-of-18 shooting with just two turnovers.

Philon showed off his skill set well in this game, with no shortage of creation for himself and his teammates, as evidenced by his eight assists. He had several impressive finishes through contact, with his handle and playmaking ability on full display. While the efficiency left something to be desired, the process itself was good, and Philon looked like the scorer he's advertised as.

Philon would promptly get recalled after this game, which presented a good opportunity for him to get some needed reps in an organized and officiated professional setting.

Along with Philon, Justin Edwards was assigned to Delaware, playing in the back-to-back set against the Maine Celtics. In his first game, Edwards struggled mightily, shooting 1-of-10 from the field and recording two points, four rebounds, and two steals in 30 minutes. Unlike Philon, Edwards was not recalled after the first game and also played in the second matchup against Maine, scoring 23 points while recording two assists and two steals in 37 minutes of play on 9-of-17 shooting from the field.

The first game has unfortunately been Edwards' season in a nutshell. He has struggled to get going and often has a clunky offensive process. At times, it seems like he's overprocessing or doesn't know when to assert himself. The second game was much better, and at this point in his career, that should be the stat line we're accustomed to seeing from Edwards when on assignment.

It's notable that the Sixers didn't recall Edwards after one game in the G League. When called upon in the NBA, Edwards has looked lost out there. It might be beneficial to let him continue getting G League reps, with extended touches, to rebuild some of his confidence and rhythm.

The Sixers had two of their three two-way contract players, Caleb Love and Rayan Rupert, suit up for some G League action. Rupert played only in the first game, scoring 13 points in 34 minutes while shooting 6-of-12 from the field and 1-of-6 from three, along with four rebounds, four assists, and a steal. Love would play both games, scoring 22 points on 8-of-15 shooting in the first game and 35 points on 10-of-20 shooting from the field in the second game. He shot 7-of-15 from three in the second matchup and also recorded two steals.

The G League is a tough environment for a guy with Rupert's skill set (toolsy, low-usage, gap-filler) to shine, but overall, I thought Rupert did a decent job in the first game, which was a rough one for Delaware. Love, on the other hand, continues to be my personal favorite two-way contract player currently on the roster. He's a gifted scorer and shooter, and that was evident in both games he played. While he might be limited defensively, there is some value in having a guy deep on your bench who isn't afraid to let it fly, like we've seen with Anfernee Simons.

It's still early in both the NBA and G League seasons, with mixed results as expected. You can tune in to the Delaware Blue Coats, potentially featuring an assignee or two, on Monday, Nov. 16, at 7 p.m. The game will be streamed on the NBA app and NBA.com.
