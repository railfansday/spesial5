Game Preview: San Antonio Spurs vs Los Angeles Lakers

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Luka Doncic and the Los Angeles Lakers have kicked off the post-LeBron era of the Purple and Gold with a bang. They currently boast the league's best record, an achievement built upon the offensive prowess of Doncic and an opportunistic defense that's been the best at generating turnovers that lead to buckets. The caveat to all this though is that, much like San Antonio, they haven't really had to play anyone yet, as is demonstrated by their opponents holding a cumulative .384 winning percentage, the lowest mark in the league thus far. Doncic has also been carrying the sort of offensive load throughout the first quarter of the season that is typically unsustainable across 82 games. There's a strong possibility that he gets some reinforcements tonight in the form of Austin Reaves, who is hoping to make his return tonight from a bruised heel that's kept him out of 5 consecutive contests and 6 of Los Angeles' last 7.

The Spurs have had a great start to the year overall, but similar to the Lakers there's been a bit of chatter about the quality of their opponents in their 15 wins. While taking care of business against teams you should beat is a sign of being a good team, San Antonio is hoping to be a great team. Even coming off a loss to a Portland team that they faced in the playoffs last season, this game against the Lakers and a player of Doncic's caliber will be their stiffest test since the loss last week to Cade Cunningham and the Detroit Pistons, the night Dylan Harper went down with his injury. With both Harper and De'Aaron Fox both possibly back in the lineup, can they quiet some of that noise tonight?

Spurs Injuries: Tobias Harris, calf (OUT), Dylan Harper, hip (questionable), De'Aaron Fox, shin (questionable)

Lakers Injuries: Austin Reaves, heel (questionable)

Luka Doncic is having an absurd start to the season, leading the NBA with a scoring average of 36.6 points per game. He has an insane 40% usage rate though, amongst the highest in NBA history were it to stay at that level all season and very much reminiscent of how his days with the Dallas Mavericks went. Asking Doncic, who has only played 70 or more games twice in his career, to keep this up for an entire season seems like a tall task, but Doncic hasn't really shown a willingness to play any other way. San Antonio does have more than a few options to throw at him though out on the perimeter, plus the benefit of the always-lurking Victor Wembanyama to deter his drives to the paint. Doncic is capable of beating a lot of teams by himself, but if San Antonio can minimize some of the damage that the rest of the Lakers are capable of playing off of him, they could have a recipe for success. A weak point for him: he's averaging 5.1 turnovers per game, which leads the league.

While the Lakers have been the best team at forcing turnovers this season, San Antonio has been among the best at taking care of ball. Los Angeles forces 18.8 turnovers a game and scores 24 points per game off of them, fueling their already lethal offense with easy opportunities in transition. With Dylan Harper and DeAaron Fox both questionable for tonight's game, the Silver and Black could find themselves lacking in strong ball handlers that can protect the rock and set the offense up in the halfcourt. Stephon Castle, who has kept his turnovers down this year in general and especially as of late with his fellow two guards missing some time, will need to play clean basketball tonight and limit some of those easy looks that this Lakers team has thrived on.

Dylan Harper was upgraded to questionable late last night on San Antonio's injury report. Tonight would be just 11 days since he suffered his injury against the Pistons, which would put him way ahead of the 3-week timetable that was initially announced after the injury. The Spurs very much need everything Harper has to offer when healthy if they hope to compete with the league's best, but how limited he will be in his first game back remains to be seen. It wouldn't be at all shocking though to see Mitch Johnson and his staff adhere to some sort of a minutes restriction with the 20 year old Harper, but the fact that he's healthy enough to be considered questionable for a game already is a great sign that he'll be out there sooner rather than later.
