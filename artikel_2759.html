Mile posts: Items on Grace Steinmetz, Isaiah Hammerand, William Wadsley, Keelee Leitzen Adrienne Buettner-Cable

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Second was the word for two Iowa college cross country teams and two individuals.

A year after the Wartburg College men, Iowa State men and Dordt University women claimed third-place finishes at national meets, the Division III Knights and the NAIA Defenders moved up a spot to claim runner-up trophies over the weekend.

Dordt came into the event at Fort Vancouver in Washington on Friday, Nov. 20 as the top-ranked squad by the U.S. Track & Field and Cross Country Coaches Association, but the Defenders had to settle for their best finish in program history after they were toppled by the College of Idaho, 74-107. No. 2-ranked Idaho erased a one-point deficit to Dordt over the final 3 kilometers by improving 33 positions over the final 3K. Idaho scored 3-15-17-18-21 with all five runners finishing as All-American to defeat the Sioux Center school, which scored 4-10-27-29-37.

Dordt leads off this edition of the WEEKEND UPDATE of the best performances by Iowa-based collegiate and post collegiate distance runners and triathletes.

The All-American (top 40) from Iowa for Dordt was 34th-place sophomore Addison Liston. The Unity Christian (Orange City) grad coved the 6K course in 22 minutes, 6.1 seconds as the Defenders' third-best finisher. Liston repeated as All-American. The Dordt leader was sophomore Joy Koole in fifth place in 21:37.0.

The leading Iowan was Bellevue University sophomore Grace Steinmetz, who claimed runner-up honors to improve on last year's fifth-place national finish. The Council Bluffs native who attended Heartland Christian High School ran 21:03.7 to trail Taylor junior Jaynie Halterman by 5.6 seconds. Steinmetz's previous best NAIA national finish was third place in the indoor 5,000 in 17:08.38.

Northwestern College had reason to celebrate after senior Emily DeGroot, a former MOC-FV (Orange City) prep, became the first Red Raider All-American since 2017. De Groot earned the last All-American position in 40th place after crossing the finish line in 22:15.5. DeGroot improved 79 positions from a year ago.

Will return to the NAIA meet with more highlights, but switching to the NCAA Division III meet at Carleton College in Northfield, Minnesota, on Nov. 21. The Wartburg men also entered the competition as the top-ranked squad, but were victimized in their hunt for their first NCAA team title by a near-perfect day from Johns Hopkins. The third-ranked Blue Jays put all five of their runners in the top 20 to score 63 points and cruise to the title.

The Knights matched a program best from 2022 and 2024 with their runner-up national effort with 163 points. Over the last five cross country seasons, the Wartburg men have averaged a 2.8-place finish at nationals, best in the nation. But the wait for a national title will have to wait.

Knights senior Isaiah Hammerand also came up short of his first national title after dueling with Wisconsin-Stevens Point senior Logan Murphy over the 8K race. Murphy pulled away over the final 3K to win the title in 23:37.0. Hammerand, who prepped at Western Dubuque, came in second in 23:55.0 to improve on a ninth-place finish at nationals from 2024 and a 10th place in 2025. Hammerand finishes as a three-time All-American in cross country. Hammerand had won the Interregional Invitational in Rock Island, Illinois, on Oct. 17. Murphy was sixth that day, 10.8 seconds back.

Wartburg junior Solomon Zaugg, a former competitor for Mediapolis, was an All-American for the first time by placing 36th and finishing as the No. 2 scorer for the Knights. Zaugg ran 24:24.3.

The 20th-ranked Central College men, like Johns Hopkins, had a historic day by securing the best team finish in program history. Buoyed by All-American seniors William Wadsley (ninth, 24:06.7) and Jack Brown (24th, 24:20.6), the Dutch scored 256 points. Central improved 36 places over the final 1,000 meters to top the sixth-place effort from 2013.

"There's just so much to say about performance today," Central coach Joe Dunham said in a university athletics release. "It's the greatest race in the history of our school at the NCAA championships."Wadsley, a Gilbert grad, ran the best race of his college career by knocking about 15 seconds off his personal record for 8K. Wadsley's previous best in three NCAA cross country appearances was 208th a year ago. Brown, the reigning NCAA indoor mile champion, was 87th at last year's NCAA meet. He went to Norwalk.

Helping Wartburg's cause was junior and ex-Glenwood prep Andrew Smith in 58th place in 24:38.3. He was the team's final scorer. Also running for the Knights was junior Clay Warson (203rd, 25:34.6) and sophomore Ethan Boston (231st, 25:50.2). Warson competed for Madrid, Boston for Linn-Mar (Marion).

Central ran an all-Iowa squad and helping the Dutch's historic run was junior and No. 3 runner Donovan Card (55th, 24:37.2), freshman Isaac Rankin (86th, 24:52.8) and junior Jack Every (148th, 25:13.1). Card attended Norwalk, Rankin graduated from Knoxville and Every ran for Dallas Center-Grimes. All three were running at nationals for the first time.

Luther College had two individuals competing at nationals and freshman Adam Gray, a former Cedar Rapids Kennedy athlete, claimed 67th in 24:46.9. His teammate, senior Michel Heinrich, was the Norse's first All-American since Isaac Jensen in 2016 by placing 27th in 24:21.0.

Fifth-ranked Wisconsin-La Crosse, the defending champion, settled for ninth place this time with 278. Freshman Kyle Johnson, a Cedar Rapids Prairie product, was the Eagles' No. 4 finisher in 85th place in 24:52.6. Johnson sat out the Midwest Regional meet at Rock Island a week ago.

Other Iowans competing at the Bill Huyck Championship Course was Loras College sophomore Cameron Gotto (230th, 25:49.9), Central College freshman Knute Rogne (257th, 26:08.7), Central freshman Carter Nunn (26:20.3) and Simpson College junior James Johnston (287thy, 27:17.2). Gotto ran for Pleasant Valley, Rogne competed for Lake Mills and Nunn and Johnson are Marshalltown grads."Unfortunately, James wasn't healthy today and able to perform up to his high standard," Simpson coach Heath Moenck said of the American Rivers Conference runner-up. "While today didn't go as well as he would have hoped, it doesn't diminish his season. James had one of the greatest seasons in Simpson history."

In the women's 6K competition, Loras College redshirt sophomore Keelee Leitzen improved 49 positions from her nationals finish of 2025 to take 10th place and earn All-American honors. The Dubuque Hempstead grad and Iowa State transfer ran a new personal record of 20:58.9.

The Wartburg College women claimed 24th in the team race and junior Lydia Maas was the final All-American in 40th place in a new best of 21:30.9. The former Hampton-Dumont runner was 169th at nationals a season ago and 161st two seasons ago.

Central College didn't have a complete team, but three Dutch made the field as individuals, with sophomore Maddy Stevens the top finisher in 62nd place in 21:45.3 in her nationals debut. Stevens attended Dallas Center-Grimes. Freshman teammate Audrey Dummermuth grabbed 114th in 22:10.0. She ran for Johnston. Dutch senior Teah Miller took 252nd in 23:03.3. Miller went to Iowa Falls-Alden.The host Carleton squad fielded a team with three Iowans, all Iowa City High grads, and they contributed to the Knights' 23rd-place effort. Freshman Abigail Burns was the team leader in 95th place in 22:03.6. Sophomore Nicole Peterson was Carleton's No. 3 finisher in 139th in 22:15.4. She made a big move up from 272nd place a year ago. Twin sister Nina Peterson was next to finish for the Knights in 205th place in 22:37.7. Nina was making her debut at nationals.

The sixth-ranked Johns Hopkins women lived up to their No. 6 national ranking by placing sixth with 273 points. Sophomore Ani Wedemeyer, who ran for Pleasant Valley, was the squad's No. 6 finisher in 153rd place in her nationals debut. Wedemeyer ran 22:19.7.

Three Wartburg juniors and one sophomore rounded out the team's finishers. Ava Vance placed 201st in her third appearance at nationals in 22:36.1. Vance is a former Ballard (Huxley) athlete. She was No. 4 on the squad. Sophomore Claire Hoyer, an ex-Dubuque Senior prep, claimed 224th in 22:48.7 in her nationals debut. Junior Sydney Bochmann, who stayed in town after graduating from Waverly-Shell Rock, came in 244th in 22:55.3. She was making her second nationals appearance. Junior Anna Quillin was the final Knight to finish in 256th in 23:07.9. The Solon product was making her debut at nationals.

Second-ranked Williams stormed to the team title with 63 points, well ahead of No. 3 Middlebury (122). The individual champion was Vassar senior Haley Schoenegge in 20:04.8.

Shifting to the NCAA Division I meet in Terre Haute, Indiana, on Nov. 21. The eighth-ranked Iowa State men claimed sixth in the team standings with 230 points. Leading the team and picking up All-American honors were juniors Robin Kwemoi Bera (29th, 29:09.9) and Joash Ruto (30th, 29:12.0) in the 10K race.

Tenth-ranked Arkansas finished ninth with 294 points and redshirt junior Allen Bonnesen, who graduated from Danville-New London before starting his career at Iowa State, was the final scorer in 162nd place in 30:35.2. Bonnesen was making his nationals debut.

Northern Arizona came in ranked 20th and ended the race that way. Running fourth for the Lumberjacks was senior Ford Washburn in 118th in 30:13.3 in his third nationals appearance. The Iowa City High grad was 113th at nationals in 2025 and 129th in 2024.

The rankings went as expected, with No. 1 New Mexico claiming its first team crown with 69 points, 38 better than No. 2 and runner-up Colorado. Louisville sophomore Elsingi Kipruto dominated the final 2K of the race to win the NCAA title in 28:27.7.

In the women's 6K, Des Moines Roosevelt grad Adrienne Buettner-Cable topped almost 200 runners by placing 67th in 19:46.0. She missed her PR by 4.4 seconds, when she won the Patriot League title last month. The Boston University senior was making her nationals debut.

Arkansas placed 24th out of 32 teams and redshirt junior Paityn Noe, a seventh-place finisher at nationals in 2024 before sitting out with injury last season, was 81st for the Razorbacks in 19:55.7. The former Ballard (Huxley) runner was No. 2 on the squad while making her third appearance at nationals.

Iowa State finished 18th and sophomores Betty Kipkore (12th, 19:02.3) and Rosemary Longisa (16th, 19:05.7) picked up All-American honors for the Cyclones. Running sixth in her nationals debut was redshirt sophomore Olivia Verde. The ex-Johnston prep came in 257th in 22:28.0.

Fourth-ranked Alabama won its first NCAA team title with 120 points while North Carolina State senior Hannah Gapes was the national champion in 18:44.3.

Turning back to the NAIA meet in Vancouver, where Mount Mercy senior Abby Knepper secured her best finish in her fourth appearance at nationals. The ex-Beckman (Dyersville) runner claimed 45th in 22:23.6 while missing an All-American spot by five positions. St. Ambrose senior Kaitlyn Knoche claimed the fourth-best finish in Bees history while placing 64th in 22:38.7. Knoche, who attended North Scott, was 44th a year ago. She also ended her career with four appearances at nationals.

Mount Mercy senior Kalin Rotzoll, a former Clear Creek-Amana athlete, was 97th in 23:00.1 in her third appearance at nationals. Rotzoll improved 32 spots from two years ago. Freshman Katie Young was Dordt's No. 6 finisher in 105th place in 23:05.9. She graduated from Unity Christian (Orange City). Mount Mercy junior Lourdes Mason, who went to Liberty High, was 109th in 23:08.7 in her debut at nationals. The Mustangs placed 21st in the team standings.

Morningside University sophomore Estefana Beisswinger took 124th in 23:18.3. The ex-Cherokee Washington runner was competing at nationals for the second time. Northwestern College senior Kayden Spencer was 147th in 23:32.8. Spencer ran for Ogden and DMACC before landing in Orange City. It was her second appearance at nationals and she moved up 55 spots from a year ago.

Taking 153rd for William Penn was sophomore Claire Rye in 23:36.4. Rye, who attended North Central (Manly), improved 42 spots from a year ago. The final runner for Morningside was junior and former Sergeant Bluff-Luton athlete Gabby Ryan in 154th in 23:37.1. It was her second nationals appearance.

The final runners for Mount Mercy were junior Annabelle Neal (190th, 23:53.3), junior Maria Dudzik (245th, 24:31.6) and sophomore Cora McArtor (269th, 25:00.2). Neal and McArtor are Cedar Rapids Kennedy products while Dudzik ran for Beckman.

In the men's 8K competition, Dordt took 21st and junior Trace Obbink was the Defenders' No. 3 finisher in 133rd place in 25:55.6. The former Le Mars Community runner was making his nationals debut. Mount Mercy sophomore Jack Stamp, a former Anamosa competitor, took 145th in 26:00.4 in his nationals debut. Sophomore teammate Noah Gregoire, who prepped at Liberty High, was 165th in 26:08.2. It was his second nationals appearance. Freshman Owen Miller, the final Mustang to earn a spot at nationals, was 204th in 26:22.7. He ran for Cedar Rapids Prairie.

Dordt's lineup was rounded out by sophomore Caleb Van Beek (220th, 26:32.3) and junior Brett Gifford (236th, 26:38.3). Van Beek attended Western Christian (Hull) while Gifford is a former Dowling Catholic prep.

Cumberlands won the team crown with 113 points, 79 ahead of Trinity Western, and Mid-America Christian junior Mohamed Guled was the individual champion in 23:51.2.

The NJCAA held the annual Half Marathon Championship three days after its national cross country meet, on Nov. 17 in Huntsville, Alabama. The Iowa Western teams both picked up team titles, with the men scoring 11 points to top New Mexico JC by six points for the 13.1-mile distance. It was their third consecutive team title. The women's team scored seven points to cruise to the title by 41 points over Fort Hays Tech Northwest.

Taking 28th place for Southeastern Community College was sophomore Ty Muniz, who hails from Columbus Junction. He ran 1:11:57.5. Running 39th for Iowa Western was sophomore and ex-Urbandale competitor Landon Hartley in 1:12:52.9. Iowa Central sophomore Liam Shannon, a former Fort Dodge St. Edmond athlete, was 41st in 1:12:56.3. Sophomore teammate John Ferguson, a Cedar Falls grad, was 59th in 1:14:36.1. The Tritons were eighth with 97 points. Southeastern sophomore Carter Easton, who ran for Center Point-Urbana, was 60th in 1:14:42.8. The winner was New Mexico JC's Ahmed Faris in 1:05:51.0.

The women's champion was Iowa Western sophomore star Juliana Sakat in 1:16:08.3.
