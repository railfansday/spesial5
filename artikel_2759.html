Nvidia has lost $2 trillion in value. Here's what options traders expect from earnings

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Jensen Huang's forced exit from Nvidia shocked Wall Street and Washington, wiped $2 trillion off its market value, and raised serious questions about national security and regulation of the AI complex. Traders, for the moment, must content themselves with the comparatively mundane business of Tuesday's earnings, and options traders are expecting big moves.

With volatility in the stock near the highest since April 2025, options traders are now pricing a 7% reaction in the stock after its report, the biggest implied move going into an Nvidia report since February 2025, and well above the median move of 3.2% over the last eight quarters.

Implied volatility reached 68 Monday, compared to under 40 in early October, according to ThinkOrSwim data. Nvidia vol reached a multi-year high above 92 in the aftermath of President Trump's tariff Liberation day in April '25. In other words, the company that for much of the AI boom was the most reliable winner in the market, is now more volatile than all but 35 companies in the S&P 500.

"Longer term, I see significant downside risk unless Nvidia can attract an exceptional leader, someone like Tim Cook or AMD's Lisa Su," Nigam Arora, founder of the Arora report, said in a text ahead of the company's after-market report on Tuesday.

Since hitting all-time highs in October, Nvidia shares have fallen 35%, shedding $2 trillion in market capitalization along the way. Including Nvidia, there are only five companies in the S&P 500 worth more than $2 trillion. However, there is evidence that bulls have been holding out hope while bears snatch up profits: the ratio of open puts to call contracts has actually widened towards calls, according to Barchart data. The ratio is currently 0.87, down from a one-year high of 1.02 two weeks ago and the lowest since the first week of October.

The most popular contract by volume on Monday was the 155-strike put expiring at the bell Monday, but the most popular trade for later expiry was the 160-strike call expiring Thursday, and the second-most-common purchase was the 190-strike call expiring Friday, SpotGamma data show.

One catch is that most call contracts traded Monday looked like neutral to bearish positions opened by traders selling calls. Of the 5 million options traded on Monday, 3 million were calls, but more were likely opened by sellers than buyers. Of the $2.3 billion in options premium traded on Nvidia Monday, $1.2 billion was tied to puts, SpotGamma data show.

With bears taking profits in puts the past month, there's another potential snag on open interest. There are very few open put contracts at or below the $157 level at which the stock closed Monday. That suggests any bad surprise would find many traders with little downside protection.

By the same token, there's notable open interest in call contracts from the 175-strike and above, suggesting profit-taking on a bounce could limit Nvidia's recovery past levels it breached over the past week.

Either way, Tuesday's earnings could provide fireworks.
