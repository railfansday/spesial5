Spurs 'a match for anyone' as win at Man City boosts European hopes

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Spurs secure famous win as Man City falter again

Dreams of reaching the Women's Champions League may have seemed fanciful for Tottenham Hotspur when the season began.

The Women's Super League has an established big four, and big-spending London City Lionesses were the side making the most obvious play to disrupt that dominance.

But a hugely impressive and deserved win at Manchester City on Sunday afternoon proves Spurs are a team on the up, and the table shows they remain firmly in top-three contention.

Two late goals secured a 3-1 win at Joie Stadium, but this was no smash-and-grab job. Spurs were more dynamic than sluggish City, pressing them into submission.

They restricted their hosts' stellar attack to precious few chances and took the ones they created.

"When we are on our game, we can be a match for anyone," Spurs manager Martin Ho said in his post-match media conference.

"It gives us confidence - but it is just one game."

Victory kept Spurs, whose highest finish in the WSL is fifth, firmly in the mix this time.

They are fifth again, but just three points behind London City Lionesses who occupy the final European place as the league heads into a last international break of 2026.

Chelsea lead the way from Manchester City, with Manchester United one point ahead of Tottenham, who in turn sit handily clear of their sixth-placed north London rivals Arsenal.

The big four are therefore all present in the top six. London City are right there, too, and Spurs, with six wins from 11 games so far, are gamely pressing their own case.

What made Sunday's win and performance from Tottenham even more impressive is how dreadful their recent record against Manchester City had been.

Tottenham had lost six of their previous seven away matches against City in all competitions, averaging over four goals conceded per defeat.

Last season City won this fixture 5-2 with Khadija Shaw scoring a 13-minute hat-trick, the quickest in WSL history.

Shaw's early goal on Sunday, her 16th in league matches versus Spurs, could easily have started a landslide.

That was expected. City had won each of their past 17 WSL home games, including all 10 in 2026 â until Tottenham tore up the script.

The outcome means Manchester City, beaten at Chelsea in their previous outing, have suffered consecutive league losses for the first time since January 2025.

Spurs have rediscovered form after losing all three league games in October while conceding 14 goals

Tottenham's victory came down to Ho's brave tactics and his team's superb execution of them.

After going behind, they pressed City constantly, forcing errors and creating chances. That tenacity won them countless one-on-ones, with City unable or unwilling to play over the press.

After her goal, Shaw was rendered anonymous by the hard work and close marking of centre-backs Toko Koga and Amanda Nilden.

Down the right, Kirsty Hanson dominated Carlotta Wamser, could have been awarded a penalty and provided the low cross for Cathinka Tandberg's equaliser.

In midfield, Drew Spence pulled all the strings and covered the entire pitch. At 34, she is perhaps playing the best football of her career and deserved her assist for the winner. After Matilda Vinberg put Spurs in front, several players ran to celebrate with Spence.

"When you come into this, you wonder if it will go against you, but the players were outstanding," said Ho.

"We haven't won here in so long, so we know it will be a tough game, but I have belief in my team when we are on song. I thought we were outstanding - we worked every blade of grass."

Spurs are clearly capable of taking the game to anyone in the league and have now scored 28 WSL goals this season, with only Chelsea and London City having more.

In the second half they adapted to City sending on such fluid attacking threats as Mary Fowler, Kerstin Casperij and Beth Mead.

They lifted the gas from the press, applying it more sparingly, and were solid in defence.

It shows that Ho has learned painful lessons from the past - whether from Spurs' poor record against City, including two five-goal losses in 2025-26, or conceding six to Chelsea and London City back to back in October.

"We have maybe been naive at times and I own that, so we are more reserved," Ho said of his tactical approach. "It was managing that, and the players' communication was excellent."

City were limited to fleeting chances. Jade Rose blasting over after keeper Lize Kop parried a Lauren Hemp free-kick was as close as they came to a second goal, and those were their first shots after a 50-minute drought.

When Spurs got their late chances through the hard work of Vinberg, they could strike for three points.

Vinberg had quite the rollercoaster afternoon. She was initially named on the bench but was promoted to the starting XI 15 minutes before kick-off as Olivia Holdt was suffering stomach cramps.

Perhaps unprepared, she gave away the ball playing out of defence leading to City's opener. But she grew into the game, scored Spurs' second goal and set up Jess Naz for the third.

It was a great moment for Naz, on her 100th WSL appearance of a career that has been disrupted by injury.

Spurs are back in Manchester before Christmas, visiting United on 20 December.

There will be real hope of a positive result as they press their way into contention for what would be a historic achievement of a top-three finish.
