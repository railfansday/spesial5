Brighton's perfect blend: The duo key to Seagulls' rise

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

There's a 16-year age gap between Gross, 35, and Vuskovic, 19, but the pair have been central to the stunning start Brighton have made to the Premier League; watch Brighton vs Newcastle live on Sky Sports Premier League on Sunday; kick-off 2.05pm

It was a painful Sunday afternoon at Hull, but by the time Brighton pair Luka Vuskovic and Pascal Gross arrived at the beachside restaurant on Tuesday, you got the impression they didn't need too much of the bracing near-frozen sea air to boost the mood.

Yes, they were disappointed, but they weren't going to let a few minutes and Oli McBurnie overshadow a start to the season that has taken the Seagulls second in the Premier League table after 11 games.

Not many would have predicted such a lofty position for Fabian Hurzeler's team - including some of the Brighton team themselves - but the club are playing some thrilling football, and the 26 goals they have scored has helped them beat the likes of Arsenal and Man City already this season.

Sitting in front of me are two of the reasons why. They are both too modest to entertain that, but at the heart of this side's success is an almost seamless tactical and generational blend epitomised by Gross and Vuskovic.

Gross has been a revelation since his return earlier this year, something he admits he was nervous about doing. "You always hope for the best, but you never know. Coming back is not always easy."

The German international is making it look effortless. Opta have the 35-year-old top of their running stats, with 131.5km covered so far this league season, and only Declan Rice has more assists (4 vs 5).

But Gross' influence can't only be measured in numbers; this anchor or "connector" - as Hurzeler likes to call him - goes far beyond. No wonder his contract was recently extended for another season.

"I love the place, a lot of important things in my life happened here," he says.

"I have my family here, my kids are happy, my dog... it's a beautiful place with a lot of special people, where I feel really connected, really comfortable with the whole culture.

"We have so much talent and we have a lot of good people, but from different backgrounds. So that's really challenging, but I love the challenge because they're all good people we have in the dressing room.

"I was, at one point, a guy who came from another country, but I wanted to adapt, I wanted to be part of the culture. You need to help and support the guys. I really believe in what the club is doing. I think I can be a big part on and off the pitch."

The German international returned to Brighton after his dream move to Borussia Dortmund - the club he supported as a boy - ended early. He was desperate to show he could still perform at the highest level consistently. "I had to prove a point for myself, but also I wanted to repay the trust the club showed in me."

Do you remember playing better at any point in your career, I ask, telling him The Athletic had him as their number one player of the season so far.

"I don't believe in that. I don't see it like that. Maybe our start and the little things getting over-exaggerated.

"I think I am playing at my best, but maybe I think I'm a very good player in a good group who's working well, because everybody is improving. The way we set up, the way we play, I think everybody looks good."

Those stats, I say, are remarkable and again he down plays the praise. But he does concede: "They are really good, but because the team is playing well. If the team is playing well, everybody individually maybe looks a bit better."

Gross is honest enough to admit the start has surprised him. "No, I wouldn't say I expected that. I always hope for the best, but I know every game, like we saw on the weekend, is so different and so difficult that momentum can change quick."

Now, he says, comes the big challenge: the busy period. "It's a different sport, I always say". But you get the sense Brighton will be ready for it.

Alongside Gross, Vukovic has been another of the central characters in Brighton's success this season. He arrived in the summer from Tottenham in a club record deal worth £46m.

Vuskovic is no stranger to breaking records - two days after his 16th birthday, he became the youngest player to feature in Croatia's top flight. He is the fourth generation of his family to play football. Defending is very much the family business.

At 19, he has already played football in Croatia, Germany, Belgium and Poland, which has certainly helped him prepare for life as a central defender in the Premier League.

He says: "When you join as a 30-year-old guy, compared to say 15, instantly you have to grow up, you have to realise something in your life, and you have to mature.

"I had a really good group of people at Hajduk Split that made me mature fast enough, and they really helped me. In each of the countries, it was very helpful to develop, to grow as a human, as a football player."

Vuskovic's form in these first few months of the season has prompted a few questions about why Spurs let him leave in the summer and brought in Brighton's Paul van Hecke.

He doesn't see it as "rejection" and appreciates everything they did for him but, he adds: "In the end the coach came and said he didn't see me in his system, which is completely normal in football. I think in the end everybody earned (something). I gained experience, I moved to another club and I think we are all very happy about it.

"They told me they don't see me (as part of the plans), so there was no point in fighting for my place. I wanted to go somewhere where the coach would tell me, 'If you train well you will play', and that's exactly what happened here.

"The move, for me, was perfect. I love the players, I love the staff, the club. They've been all very nice to me since the first day I came, and I would change nothing."

For all the praise - and there has been plenty - Vuskovic is fiercely critical of himself. This is a player, after all, who employed a private analyst to make sure he was ready for the Premier League.

We talk about the two goals conceded late on against Hull he reflects: "I think I could have done more. We all could. We had our chances, but in the end, we didn't take them. Maybe we didn't have the luck. There's nothing to be sad or angry about, we can only learn from it and come back stronger."

If they keep learning and progressing what are this team capable of?

His reply? "Being at the top part of the Premier League, competing with all the top teams, also in Europe, if we are focused every game. Right now it's amazing, but we need to keep a clear head and just focus on what's next."

Pascal Gross and Luka Vuskovic were speaking at an American Express Shop Small campaign event aimed at encouraging people to support local businesses.
