Where Are Rick Dalton and Sharon Tate in ‘The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth’?

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

[Editor’s note: This article contains small spoilers for “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth.”]

It should come as little surprise that David Fincher’s “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” focuses on, well, Cliff Booth. The Quentin Tarantino-penned sequel to the filmmaker’s own 2019 Oscar-winning hit “Once Upon a Time in Hollywood” has always been billed as a spinoff following the continuing adventures of his fictitious (yet still iconic) stuntman Cliff Booth (Brad Pitt). But Tarantino has always loved building out his own shared and connected universe(s), and fans sure love exploring its outer limits.

“Once Upon a Time in Hollywood” and, by extension, “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” occupy a unique place in Tarantino’s cinematic world, as they’re both part of his so-called “Realer Than Real World Universe” and his “Movie Movie Universe.” Tarantino didn’t just keep crafting his movies-within-movies world with the hit film — he also introduced real-world (realer than real world?) elements and people (the Manson Family, of course, and all that entails). That bent continues in “Cliff Booth,” which is rife with both out-there elements and very real references (time for that “Hee Haw” resurgence, we guess). But the film also has to follow its own series logic, which means, yes, it’s the Cliff Booth show, but hey, where are our “Hollywood” co-stars Rick Dalton (Leonardo DiCaprio) and Sharon Tate (Margot Robbie)?

[Once again: This article contains small spoilers for “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth.”]

When we first meet Cliff and Rick, the duo are long-time colleagues and best pals. As his stunt double, Cliff is tasked with doing some serious heavy lifting for Hollywood star Rick, which inevitably (?) leads them to off four members of the Manson Family. The raucous and wild violence the duo inflicts on the foursome isn’t just a fun bonding exercise for the pair. It also ensures that the nefarious group can’t go next door to enact the infamous (and very real) Tate murders. Win-win!

The film ends with an invitation for Rick to slide on over to the Tate house for a thank-you drink, presumably putting him in the path of Roman Polanski, Sharon, and all the star power they carry. “Cliff Booth” is set eight years after those events, and while Rick may have enjoyed a career upswing in the interim, his current professional endeavors have taken him far from Hollywood.

While we never see Rick in the film (despite ongoing rumors that DiCaprio might appear for a quick cameo), Cliff mentions him frequently and fondly, including telling potential paramour Karen Crosby (Elizabeth Debicki) that their professional entanglements ended when Rick got, ahem, “too fat” for Cliff to believably double him and then moved to Australia to star in the TV series “Dingo Dan.” Further details on the show are slim, but we do know it’s a Western TV show that’s proven quite popular and durable (Aussie Karen seems to like it), and Cliff makes it sound like Rick is enjoying himself. Not too bad.

By blending his two movie universes with “Once Upon a Time in Hollywood,” Tarantino got to both delight in late-’60s Hollywood magic and “save” Sharon Tate, her unborn son, Abigail Folger, Wojciech Frykowski, Steven Parent, and Jay Sebring. That sort of revisionist history was the film’s buzziest twist and, as I argued at the time, the most emotional choice Tarantino has ever made in his filmography.

And while we don’t get treated to much more Sharon in “Cliff Booth,” we are granted some, in the form of a Red Apple Cigarettes commercial led by a beaming Margot Robbie, returning to her original role as the doomed actress. (The fake cigarettes are another Tarantino-verse staple, and Rick even appears in his own commercial for them during the end credits of “Hollywood.”)

In the short cameo, Sharon looks healthy and happy, and she’s clearly still enough of a star to warrant her own advertising campaign, built on her smile and her zippy shimmy. As Cliff watches the commercial at the Marina del Rey condo he shares with his dad (Peter Weller), he takes the time to stop, observe, and notably smile at the star. It’s a small grace note, and a very effective one.

One other star of “Once Upon a Time in Hollywood” is missing from “Cliff Booth,” though. Just where the heck is Brandy?

Netflix will release “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” in IMAX theaters on Wednesday, November 25. It will be available to stream on Netflix starting Wednesday, December 23.
