AI agents are already booking travel, and they get things wrong. The industry hasn’t settled who absorbs the cost when they do. Citi wants the agent companies on the hook, which would spare hotels and airlines, but could slow how fast agents transact.

When an AI agent books the wrong trip, someone has to pay for the mistake. Citi thinks it should be the company behind the agent, not the hotel, airline or card issuer.

“Who takes the agent liability” is the biggest question banks, payment networks, merchants and AI companies are trying to answer, said Abhinav Anand, Citi’s head of value cards, lending and commerce for U.S. consumer cards, at the Skift Commerce Summit in New York on Wednesday.

In Citi’s view, Anand said, liability should fall “to the agent themselves, the company that is actually coming with the agent.”

Citi's head of value cards, lending and commerce for U.S. consumer cards, Abhinav Anand, said at the Skift Commerce Summit in New York that liability for AI agent errors should sit with the company that builds the agent, not with hotels, airlines, or card issuers. Anand said one way to do that is for agents seeking to transact on Visa or Mastercard to accept some liability when they register with the networks. He cited a Visa study of more than 75 agents that he said found 43% made mistakes, with about half of those completing the purchase anyway, and even the best performers — which he said included Google's Gemini and Meta's Muse — getting it right about 82% of the time. To settle disputes, Anand said the industry is working to carry the traveler's purchase instructions inside the tokenized transaction so issuers and merchants can see the original intent when a charge is disputed. He also said early industry data show travelers arriving through agent-led search and discovery are 40% to 50% more likely to convert and spend more per transaction. Citi, among the first issuers enabled for agentic payments with Mastercard and more recently Visa, is building its own AI-enabled travel concierge inside its app; third-party agents can't use Citi ThankYou points today.
