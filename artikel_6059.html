Royal Air Maroc Business Class Boeing 787 Review: The World’s Worst Flat Bed?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

To finish off the return portion of our Orient Express adventure via Africa trip, we flew Royal Air Maroc’s Boeing 787-8 business class on the 8hr25min flight from Casablanca (CMN) to Miami (MIA). While the flight operated relatively on schedule, it otherwise had quite some room for improvement.

The version of Royal Air Maroc’s 787 that we flew had what I’d consider to be the world’s worst flat bed business class seat. When you combine that with no Wi-Fi and incredibly lackluster service, it’s hard to praise the airline too much.

To Royal Air Maroc’s credit, the airline is on balance the best northern African carrier, in my opinion. And the flight attendant working our part of the cabin was at least well intentioned. The food was relatively decent as well. It also has to be pointed out that Royal Air Maroc’s 787s have four different types of configurations, and these seats are limited to most of the 787-8s (while the 787-9s have better seats).

So while I have a hard time giving Royal Air Maroc too much credit for our experience, I also view the airline in a better light than carriers like Air Algerie, for example.

Ford and I booked an itinerary on Royal Air Maroc all the way from Malaga to Miami via Casablanca. The reason was because the airline offered the best schedule that would get us home the same day after our Orient Express Sailing Yachts cruise.

Royal Air Maroc often makes only one business class seat available, so I had to get a bit creative to make this work. I ended up booking one ticket using 57,500 American AAdvantage miles plus $85.72 in taxes & fees, while the other ticket was booked with cash, at the cost of $1,620, with the following itinerary:

10/09 AT989 Malaga to Casablanca departing 2:15PM arriving 1:30PM10/09 AT204 Casablanca to Miami departing 3:00PM arriving 7:45PM

Obviously paying cash for a ticket wasn’t ideal, but that still ended up being less out of pocket than flying British Airways business class through London on awards, factoring in the carrier surcharges.

We had a fairly short connection in Casablanca, as we had just arrived in Royal Air Maroc’s 737 business class from Malaga. We first had to clear transit security, which only took a few minutes. While we would’ve had access to Royal Air Maroc’s lounge, we didn’t really have much time, so we headed straight to our departure gate.

Our flight was departing from gate A10, with boarding scheduled for 2:10PM, 50 minutes before departure. As is common for US-bound flights, the departure area was roped off from the rest of the terminal, and there were some boarding pass checks. However, we weren’t subjected to extra screening (I assume that’s just for those with “SSSS” on their boarding pass).

I must admit, Royal Air Maroc’s livery is pretty beautiful, no?

I also have an embarrassing confession to make. Boarding started a few minutes ahead of the published 2:10PM time, and I attempted to board… only to find out that I was accidentally attempting to board the flight to New York. Gate A9 and A10 are right next to one another, the monitor was tiny, etc. In fairness, I wasn’t the only person attempting to board the wrong flight, so it seems like that could maybe be organized a little better. 😉

Anyway, boarding did end up starting right on schedule, with wheelchair passengers being invited to board first, followed by business class passengers and oneworld elite members.

Royal Air Maroc’s Boeing 787-8 business class takes up the entire space between the first and second set of doors. It consists of 18 seats, spread across three rows, in a 2-2-2 configuration. We were in seats 2A and 2C, the window and aisle seat in the second row on the left side.

Royal Air Maroc has the Stelia Equinox 3D seat, which like I said, is the worst flat bed business class in the world, in my opinion. I just don’t understand what the designer of this seat was thinking.

I understand that in theory this is supposed to be a high density layout, but with just 18 seats in that space, it’s hardly efficient — American has 20 reverse herringbone seats in the same space on the same plane (though admittedly on Royal Air Maroc the lavatories are also in front of the second set of doors, so it’s not a fully fair comparison).

What makes these seats so bad? Well, basically everything. The seats completely lack storage, and they have the most bizarre top/bottom configuration when you recline your seat. Fortunately I was traveling with Ford (no one I’d rather be in these top/bottom seats with!), but if next to a stranger, these are just downright bad.

Where do we even begin? For one, these seats have the padding (and look) of an ironing board. So the comfort is definitely lacking.

Ford had never been in these kinds of seats before, and as he sat down and looked forward, he was genuinely confused by how the ottomans were at completely different levels — that’s understandable confusion!

In this configuration, the tray tables fold out from the center side of the seat, and are so poorly designed. They basically refuse to stay in place, and if they move even just a little, they sort of “plop” down, as they go off the edge of the little “holder.”

Then there’s the whole console between seats, where you’ll find a panel with the seat controls, a headphone jack, an entertainment controller, an AC power outlet, and a USB-A outlet (there’s no USB-C or wireless charging).

The seat just completely lacks when it comes to storage and comfort, as there’s not really anywhere practical to place things. For example, if you have your laptop out and then the meal service starts, where are you supposed to place it? There’s nowhere to put it, other than the floor.

Perhaps the one positive thing I can say about this Royal Air Maroc hard product is that there were individual air nozzles, which at least helped with being able to regulate the feeling of being too warm.

I’m not trying to be sarcastic, but I do consider this to be the world’s worst flat bed business class. On the plus side, I guess at least it’s not angled (is that really a thing anymore?), and the version of this seat on Philippine Airlines A330s is worse.

Royal Air Maroc’s 787 business class has 18.5″ seat back entertainment screens, with audio and video on demand. The system was far from cutting edge and the selection wasn’t massive, but it also wasn’t terrible. There was a decent selection of movies, TV shows, music, games, and of course the map feature.

I tried my best to count the number of entertainment options, and I got to around 230 movies, so that’s not a bad selection. However, the options also weren’t terribly modern, for the most part.

Unfortunately there was no Wi-Fi on this plane, which is pretty uncompetitive nowadays. The good news is that Royal Air Maroc plans to introduce Starlink Wi-Fi in 2027, so that’ll be a major upgrade.

When it comes to Royal Air Maroc business class amenities, a pillow and blanket were waiting at my seat upon boarding. The blanket was cozy without being overly luxurious or warm, while the pillow was fine, but a little on the small side.

Later on in the flight I discovered there were also mattress pads in the overhead bin, but go figure that no crew member ever informed any passenger of this (let alone offered to make beds), which is such a missed opportunity. How sad when the airline invests in something like that, but it’s totally wasted, as I didn’t see a single passenger using these.

Headphones were also waiting at each seat, but they were very much on the cheap side, and didn’t have good noise cancelation, and also weren’t very comfortable.

Amenity kits had been placed at the seats before boarding, and were fine. I kind of liked the Royal Air Maroc branding on the kit as such. In terms of contents, there were socks, eyeshades, a dental kit, and a small selection of products from Moroccan skincare brand Nectarome.

During boarding, the crew came around with pre-departure drinks, including the choice of lemon mint juice or water, followed by warm towels.

The menu and drink list for the flight were also distributed.

There were no slippers or pajamas. All-in-all, the amenities were okay. They weren’t terrible, but also weren’t memorable.

Boarding took a rather long time, as the flight was full. The main cabin door closed at 3:10PM, 10 minutes after our scheduled departure time. At that point the safety video was screened, which was interesting to watch.

At 3:15PM we began our pushback, and then at 3:25PM we began our taxi.

We had a brief taxi out to runway 35R, where we had to hold for a few minutes. We were cleared for takeoff at 3:40PM, and rocketed off into the afternoon sky. The views on the climb out were lovely, and the seatbelt sign was turned off around five minutes after takeoff.

Once we leveled off, we had around eight hours remaining to Miami.

In business class on this Royal Air Maroc flight, the main meal was served after takeoff, while a snack was served before landing. In theory the menu talked about how there was dine on demand.

I’m sure that could’ve been requested, but that’s not something the crew ever offered — they just took meal orders before takeoff, and then served everyone the main meal after takeoff. Much like with the mattress pads, it just seems like there’s a gap between what they’re trying to go for, and what they’re actually offering.

You can find the Royal Air Maroc business class menu and drink list below.

The first round of drinks were served around 35 minutes after takeoff, so credit to the airline for the speed at which the first drink was served, plus the overall presentation.

I had a glass of the Moroccan white wine. That was served with a canapé consisting of sweet chili prawn with sesame seeds, smoked salmon rose, and a foie gras mousse bite, along with two packaged snacks (cheese biscuits and roasted salted almonds).

That was a lovely start to the meal, but Ford had less luck. He ordered a Diet Coke, and when he opened the can, it exploded. I understand the concept of cans sometimes having issues, but I’ve never seen anything like this before.

It wasn’t just that an ounce or two immediately sprayed out, but instead, within two seconds, there was no liquid left in the can, and instead, it was all over him and the cabin. I don’t even understand how that could happen. So it took quite a while for us to clean up that mess.

There were three options for starters. I ordered the tuna tataki with mixed lettuce, mango, and wasabi cream, while Ford had the harira and dates (for the record, I would’ve gone for the more Moroccan option as well, but wanted to give Ford his first choice, and get pictures of different things). Both were quite decent, but nothing great.

Eventually our appetizers were cleared, and then our mains were brought out. I had the grilled sea bass with chimichurri sauce, with quinoa, lentils, and mushrooms, while Ford had the beef tajine with prunes, dried apricot, and almonds. Both dishes were fine — the fish surprisingly lacked flavor, and the beef tajine was just okay, not bad, but not as good as you’d hope for the flag carrier of a country.

After the main course, there was the choice of a couple of desserts, fruit, or cheese. Ford and I each ordered one of the desserts (cheesecake and a pastry with amlou), and then split a cheese plate.

All-in-all, the meal wasn’t bad, and wrapped up within two hours of departure. The challenge was more the service. I understand based on the above pictures, everything actually looks pretty decent and organized. But the crew had a complete inability to offer drink refills and remove plates, even when specifically requested. It wasn’t that they weren’t nice, they were just really disorganized and slow with responding to requests.

For example, below is what Ford’s tray looked like when we were having the cheese course. You’d think they could’ve cleared something, but nope, they just kind of looked at it. I’ll talk more about service later, but getting a drink refill was quite the undertaking.

Royal Air Maroc’s 787 business class lavatory situation is a bit confusing. There’s one lavatory at the very front of the cabin, but it seems to be reserved for the crew. At least that’s the impression I had, as that’s what the flight attendants suggested to me.

There were then two lavatories behind business class, but the one on the left (on my aisle) was for economy (so the curtain was hung around it accordingly), while the one on the right (the other aisle) was for business class.

One lavatory for 18 business class passengers isn’t ideal. The issue isn’t the ratio as such, but instead, if one person is using the lavatory for an extended period, you’re kind of just locked out. The bigger issue is that going from the left aisle to the back right requires either going through the economy galley or forward galley, and the crew didn’t seem thrilled by traffic there.

Anyway, the lavatory as such was a standard Dreamliner one, so wasn’t half bad. And while I wouldn’t say it was clean, it also wasn’t filthy.

Once back at my seat, we reclined both of the seats to get some rest. This is where you really realize how absurd these seats are. Imagine you’re sitting in one of these seats next to a stranger, or imagine if you’re in the “top” seat and want to sleep, while the person in the “bottom” seat doesn’t. The person in the “top” seat is basically hanging over you.

Since we were traveling together, this wasn’t a huge deal, but I just can’t believe anyone thought this would be a good seat standard on a long haul flight.

With no Wi-Fi and not a whole lot to do, we tried to get some rest. We fell asleep with around 6hr30min remaining to Miami, while approaching the Azores…

…and we woke up with around two hours remaining.

Honestly, that was some very good rest for me on a plane, since I’m usually a terrible sleeper. And while I’d love to credit the seat, the truth is that just not knowing what to do with yourself really helps with getting some rest. I knew I couldn’t be productive, there wasn’t much on the entertainment system for me to enjoy, etc.

The crew served the pre-landing snack around 90 minutes before landing, and there were two choices. One choice was a salmon sandwich and tandoori chicken sandwich, and the other choice was a beef burger and chicken burger, and we had one of each. That was served with a side of yogurt with almonds, dates, and amlou.

I didn’t find it to be a terribly large meal, but it was sufficient.

In addition to water, I accompanied the meal with a mint tea, which was relatively nicely presented.

Compared to Air Algerie, service on Royal Air Maroc was excellent… but that’s about all I can say. To the crew’s credit, they were relatively charming, and smiled every once in a while. So friendliness wasn’t the issue.

Instead, the problem was just how reactive service was. If there was an empty glass, they’d just look at it, rather than removing it, or offering a refill. And even if you asked for a refill during a meal, it would take 15 minutes for it to happen. As another example, with the pre-landing meal, they never proactively asked anyone if they wanted something to drink — they just poured water, and that was it (though they were happy to fulfill requests). These just seem like basic things that should be fixed.

This felt like assembly line service to the max, in terms of the service flow being about what was convenient for the crew, rather than what passengers wanted.

At around 7:15PM Miami time, the captain was on the PA to announce that we’d soon start descending, and would be landing in around 45 minutes. Our approach ended up stretching on quite a bit, as we flew down the coast of Florida, before flying out over the Everglades, and then turning around.

We ended up touching down in Miami at 8PM, on runway 9. While we got off the runway very close to our arrival gate, we ended up just holding position for around 15 minutes on a taxiway, with no update from the pilots. We finally pulled into our gate at 8:20PM.

We quickly cleared immigration and customs, and were excited to be heading home, wrapping up a fun and memorable trip!

I can’t complain too much about our Royal Air Maroc 787 business class flight. We were close to on time, the crew wasn’t unfriendly, and since I was traveling with Ford, I didn’t mind these seats. The food also wasn’t bad.

However, when compared to other business class experiences, there’s definitely huge room for improvement. These seats are really quite uncompetitive, the lack of Wi-Fi was disappointing, and the service was just lacking. Royal Air Maroc will soon introduce Starlink Wi-Fi and is also introducing upgraded business class seats, and I feel like if they invested a bit in service training, they could really be a very competitive airline.

I’d say that by regional standards, Royal Air Maroc is still the best carrier in northern Africa. But that’s not to say that there isn’t (big) room for improvement.
