Muslims to outnumber Christians by 2035, research shows

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Pew Research Center projections show that there will be 2.57 billion Muslims and 2.52 billion Christians by 2035. Researchers say differences in fertility rates account for the change.

Muslims are going to outnumber Christians by 2035, according to projections from the Washington-based Pew Research Center.

Pew said that while both religions are growing, Islam is growing faster than Christianity.

The main reason is Muslims tend to have more children than other major religious groups, according to the study.

"It's the babies,” Pew senior demographer Conrad Hackett told the AP news agency.

Pew data shows that Muslim women had an average of 3.1 children between 2020 and 2025, compared with 2.5 for Christian women. For all other women, the average was 1.6, lower than the 2.1 needed to replace each generation.

According to the center's estimates, there were 2.27 billion Christians and 2.02 billion Muslims worldwide in 2020.

New projections show that there will be 2.57 billion Muslims and 2.52 billion Christians by 2035. If current trend persists, the global Muslim population is projected to exceed 3.6 billion by 2070, while the number of Christians is expected to fall just short of 3 billion.

Alan Cooperman, Pew's director of religion research, told AP news agency that most of the projected growth of Muslims and Christians was occurring in countries where they already are the majority. He says conversion plays only a minor role.

"I don't think this is a situation in which the growth of Muslims is coming at the expense of Christians. I do worry about mischief being made with headlines or narratives that take this finding and portray it as sort of a clash where Christians are losing to Muslims,” he said.

Gina Zurlo, a senior researcher at Harvard Divinity School specializing in religious demographics, said inter-religious relations are "extremely important" in light of the trends.

"I hope people seek opportunities for greater inter-religious education. Personal contact is important — when people have personal relationships across religious differences, it ‘humanizes' the ‘other' in fruitful ways," she said.
