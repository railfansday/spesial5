Schumer's grip on power slips as Dems begin to revolt against him, eye new leadership

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Several newlyelected Democratic senators suggest they are not committed to supporting Sen. Chuck Schumer as Senate majority leader.

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., is riding high after flipping the Senate, but a growing rebellion among longtime and fresh-faced Senate Democrats may throw a wrench in his plan to seek another term as the top dog in the upper chamber.

The top Democrat, who has led the Senate Democratic caucus for nearly a decade, has made clear he intends to run for another term as leader and take on the role of majority leader once more.

Schumer only needs a majority of Senate Democrats to vote for him to keep the job, but some want to take stock before jumping in to back him.

"We need a conversation about leadership going forward, because we need new energy and ideas," Sen. Richard Blumenthal, D-Conn., told reporters on Tuesday when asked whether he supported Schumer.

But he quickly followed up by noting that he expected that Schumer would be "re-elected as leader, because no one has come forth willing to take the position. But we need a conversation about leadership."

Schumer, during an appearance on the New York Times’ "The Daily" podcast, was asked if he expected a challenge in running for another term as leader.

When pressed, Schumer argued that the Senate Democratic caucus is "focused on moving forward."

"Everyone’s very happy with what we all did. But this is just the beginning," Schumer said. "As I said before, and I’ll say it again and again to my caucus, to the country, to everybody: If we don’t deliver, all this will go away. This is just the first step. But I think we’re united together to do that."

Fox News Digital reached out to Schumer’s office for comment on unrest among Democrats over his plan to seek another term.

Many of the newcomers to the Senate, which spans the ideological gamut from moderate to progressive, have made clear they won’t support Schumer, including the likes of Sen.-elect Adam Hamilton and Troy Jackson.

Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., speaks during a rally at the National Mall in Washington D.C., on Sept. 19, 2025. (Celal Gunes/Anadolu via Getty Images)

And the sentiment that the immediate future of Democratic leadership needs to be litigated is percolating among some Senate Democrats, including Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., who is favored by progressives in the upper chamber to run for the job.

"I’ve said very clearly that we need to have a discussion in the Democratic caucus," Van Hollen said during an appearance at Crooked Con over the weekend.

"We need to understand what the Democrats are going to do with this new responsibility and these new powers," he continued. "And that nobody should get a blank check without a full understanding from the caucus as to where we’re going to go."

And Van Hollen, who has previously said his focus was more on "kicking the tires" on a run for higher office in 2028, is reportedly mulling a bid for Schumer’s job, according to Politico.

Fox News Digital reached out for comment from Van Hollen’s office to confirm if he was considering a leadership bid.
