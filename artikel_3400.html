20 Most Universally Loved TV Couples

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

It's astonishing that romance series are considered niche, as almost everyone has opinions on the couples in the shows they watch. In fact, some of the most spirited TV discussions come from arguing about the best romance in a given series. It's been over 20 years since Buffy the Vampire Slayer ended, yet the Buffy-and-Angel vs. Buffy-and-Spike debates still rage on. Meanwhile, Gilmore Girls fans are no closer to reaching a consensus on Team Dean, Team Jess, or Team Logan than they were when the show aired in the 2000s.

But as fun as it is to put ourselves into staunch TV romance camps, it's just as wonderful when the stars align, and everyone can universally agree on small-screen pairings that are absolutely magical. This is no easy task — these relationships need enough ups and downs to keep audiences hooked, but not so toxic that they turn viewers off.

This can also change over time. Audiences who once eagerly tuned in to watch Ross and Rachel or Jim and Pam's relationship unfold have since reevaluated the couples and their romantic storylines as dysfunctional and grating. For a couple to be truly universally loved, they must still make their fans swoon.

The longest-running will-they, won't-they couple in television history, Olivia Benson and Elliot Stabler have been making audiences swoon for almost 30 years, all without ever having kissed — though they memorably came so close in Law & Order: SVU season 24. First meeting as partners in New York's Special Victims Unit, Benson and Stabler had instant rapport and crackling on-screen chemistry. Over the years, their loyalty and love for each other have only strengthened as they prove that sometimes the slowest-burning relationships are the hottest.

Most universally loved TV couples are popular because they're between two lovable people, but Blair and Chuck showed that you can still be a terrible person and have audiences root for your romance, because what's really important is being right for each other. Blair and Chuck are a perfect match in every way, from their raw ambition to their unbridled, animalistic mutual desire. One of Gossip Girl's harshest realities is that every character is actually awful, but these two scheming lovebirds make no apologies for it.

Forbidden love is always titillating, and it doesn't get much more forbidden than falling for a priest. However, while audiences may have been first hooked on the taboo element of Fleabag's season 2 romance, what really makes this relationship special is how vulnerable she and Hot Priest are with each other, thanks to their instant trust and connection. Going their separate ways in Fleabag's ending is so beautifully bittersweet because although they aren't together, their lives have been changed forever from having experienced the kind of love that they shared.

Willow and Tara were one of the first long-term Sapphic couples in television history, but their Buffy the Vampire Slayer romance is about so much more than breaking barriers. In a show full of toxic romances, these two stood out for forming a connection based on genuine warmth, affection, and empathy. Though this wasn't always maintained, as Willow's magic addiction temporarily tore them apart, it's clear that they belonged together, making Tara's "bury your gays" death one of the grimmest moments TV has ever seen.

Grand romantic gestures and tumultuous love triangles certainly make for great TV, but Janine and Greg show that in matters of the heart, it's often the little things that make the biggest difference, whether that's in drawing a couple together or keeping them apart. The Abbott Elementary characters' slow-burn road to romance was paved with small acts of kindness, like Greg being the only one to notice and compliment Janine's new hair, while their reasons for not leaping into a relationship were also grounded, like Janine needing time to be single as an adult. Ultimately, it all served to make their eventual, inevitable coupling feel earned.

Opposites-attract couples always have a fun frisson, but they carry extra emotional weight in teen dramas, where stepping outside your social circle and status is even more high-stakes. However, Seth and Summer's romance was so much deeper than just another nerd wooing the prom queen.

Teen dramas have always dominated television, but there are only a handful of titles that remain truly iconic in the age of modern streaming.

The O.C. characters' witty banter was bar none, and while it kept audiences entertained, what really made them root for these two as a couple was that they never stopped growing. Their wedding in The O.C.'s series finale was not only romantic but earned after four seasons of consistently being better for each other.

Parks and Rec is full of great couples, with pairings like Leslie and Ben or Ann and Chris also worthy candidates as one of TV's most universally loved couples. However, there's something even more special about two very different yet equally odd people not only appreciating but loving each other for their eccentricities. Because of this, April and Andy are free to be themselves, and their relationship is a wonderfully weird playground full of goofy inside jokes and alter egos. While they never take life too seriously, April's black-cat cynicism and Andy's golden-retriever enthusiasm help balance each other out.

Whereas many of TV's most famous LGBTQ+ couples have defining moments of tragedy, David Rose and Patrick Brewer are an ultimate celebration of queer joy. Though their relationship is hardly problem-free, it's always rooted in true love and pure happiness. Together, they are grounded and authentic, while their romantic moments, from Patrick publicly serenading David to their mountaintop engagement, are simply the best.

It's one thing to be a happy, healthy couple in the world of a low-stakes sitcom; it's another to maintain such a relationship when crises and trauma are an almost daily occurrence. However, Randall and Beth Pearson's marriage not only holds steadfast throughout six seasons of This Is Us, but it's a consistent bright spot in the tearjerking drama series. Throughout fostering and adopting a child, mental health struggles, and major career upheavals, the couple may experience friction, but they ultimately never lose their sense of playfulness, fizzy banter, or love for each other.

While Ross and Rachel were kept in perpetual will-they, won't-they purgatory throughout Friends' 10-season run, Monica and Chandler had a much more organic yet entertaining path from friends to lovers. What started as a shocking London hookup twist in season 4 grew into one of sitcom history's best partnerships thanks entirely to Courteney Cox and Matthew Perry's chemistry. Monica and Chandler experience huge struggles and don't always see eye to eye, but once they get together, there is never a question of whether they'll break up because it's clear that their love is so strong.
