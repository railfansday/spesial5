Nintendo's On The Hunt For A Digital Archivist To Preserve Its Rich History

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

With closing online storefronts, the move to digital media and the rise of streaming, there are few gaming industry topics more discussed right now than preservation. Fortunately, it looks like Nintendo is making an effort to ensure that its history doesn't get lost to time (thanks, Time Extension).

Nintendo of America is currently hiring a Digital Archivist to "support the preservation, organization, and accessibility of historical hardware and software information," according to the job description. In short, this person would be responsible for ensuring that all things Nintendo are kept safely stored away and easily accessible.

We're sure that there are a few of you lovely lot reading whose brains are already like a Nintendo archive, and it certainly sounds like the job comes with its fair share of perks. The chosen applicant will surely have unrivalled access to Nintendo's historical records, get the chance to "Interview subject matter experts and long-tenured employees to capture institutional knowledge," and organise one of the richest histories in gaming. Damn, and there we were getting a kick out of putting our important paperwork into colour-coded binders.

What's extra cool is that this will be the person responsible for finding and dishing out these records to anyone at Nintendo for future use — you know, for when someone comes asking who that moustachioed fella in the denim overalls is.

Of course, the job comes with a huge amount of technicalities, too. The listing requires "Experience developing metadata structures, taxonomies, classification systems, or naming conventions," is looking for someone familiar with "metadata and archival standards such as Dublin Core, DACS, EAD, PREMIS, MODS, or similar frameworks," and has at least two years of previous work in Records, Metadata, Knowledge, or Digital asset management. In short, it isn't for any old Joe.

But hey, what an opportunity! This is a 12-month contract based out of Redmond, WA, and it will presumably result in Nintendo's 100+ year history living on for many more to come.

If you think this one might be for you, you'll find even more about the job in the official listing.
