Lifeline takes up to $9.25 a month off one phone or home internet plan if your household’s income is low or anyone in it gets SNAP, Medicaid, SSI, federal housing assistance, or a veterans pension. Apply through the National Verifier, Lifeline’s application site, then pick a company whose plan costs little or nothing after the discount.

Only about 8 million of the nearly 38 million households that qualify have signed up, according to USAC, the nonprofit that runs Lifeline for the Federal Communications Commission (FCC).

You qualify if your household’s income is no more than 135% of the federal poverty guidelines, a limit that Lifeline’s income table shows in dollars by household size. You also qualify if anyone in your household gets SNAP, Medicaid, Supplemental Security Income (SSI), federal housing assistance such as a Section 8 voucher, or the Veterans Pension and Survivors Benefit. A February 2026 FCC proposal would limit Lifeline to U.S. citizens and certain legal immigrants and could require a full Social Security number, but it isn’t final.

Each household, meaning everyone at one address who shares income and expenses, gets one discount, so a parent on SSI who lives alone can get their own. On qualifying Tribal lands, the discount is up to $34.25 a month.

Apply online through the National Verifier, by mail with a paper application, or with help from a phone or internet company that takes Lifeline. Oregon and Texas residents apply through their state’s Lifeline program instead.

Then use the Companies Near Me tool to find the companies serving your ZIP code, and compare what each charges after the discount, since some Lifeline phone plans cost nothing. Choose a plan with data, because phone service alone gets only up to $5.25. A retiree on SSI who puts the $9.25 toward a $15 prepaid plan with talk, text, and data pays $5.75 a month before taxes.

On a free plan, use the service at least once every 30 days, or the company can cut it off after a 15-day warning. Lifeline rechecks your eligibility every year, and a recertification notice left unanswered for 60 days ends the discount. Other programs offer emergency help with rent, utilities, and food.

Before you apply, gather a benefit letter issued in the past 12 months, a screenshot of your benefits portal, or last year’s tax return, since Lifeline can ask for proof.
