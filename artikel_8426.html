Before you launch a crowdfunding campaign for an emergency, compare what each site deducts from every gift and how many days the money takes to reach you. GoFundMe fits most campaigns, but a site that pays the next business day, or a nonprofit that pays your medical providers directly, can fit yours better.

GoFundMe charges nothing to start or run a personal fundraiser in the U.S., but it deducts 2.9% plus 30 cents from every donation. On $10,000 raised in 200 gifts of $50, that’s about $350 gone before payout.

That’s the low end of what these sites cost. Three of the five below charge no platform fee by default and deduct only card processing fees, while two take a larger share, so the same $10,000 leaves between about $9,250 and $9,650 for your bills. One site pays the next business day, while a GoFundMe gift can take up to two weeks to reach your bank.

GoFundMe leaves about $9,650 of the $10,000 and pays only into a bank account, by direct deposit. Each gift takes up to five business days to process and two to five more to reach your bank. Set up transfers within 120 days of the first donation, or the payment processors can refund the gifts or turn them over as unclaimed property. GoFundMe doesn’t allow raffles or rewards for donors. It’s the right starting point for most campaigns, since more than 190 million people have used it to give or raise money, by its own count.

Spotfund charges no platform fee and deducts the same 2.9% plus 30 cents per U.S. gift, so it also leaves about $9,650. It sends each gift to your U.S. bank account on the next business day. It runs medical, emergency, and memorial campaigns, and it suits an organizer whose bills can’t wait two weeks.

FundRazr charges no platform fee while donors are asked for an optional tip, so you pay only the processing fee, which it lists at 2.9% plus 30 cents, and keep about $9,650. Stop asking donors for that tip, and FundRazr charges a 5% platform fee on top. On a keep-it-all campaign, the kind that doesn’t require hitting a goal, each gift goes straight into your own Stripe or PayPal account, which moves it to your bank daily, weekly, or monthly as you choose. It doesn’t allow raffles or online auctions, and it suits an organizer who wants each gift in their own account as soon as it’s made.

GoGetFunding keeps 4% of what you raise on top of processing, for a total of 6.9% plus 30 cents a gift through its own card payments, which leaves about $9,250. Card gifts are held for seven days before you can withdraw them, while gifts made through PayPal usually reach your PayPal account at once. It takes donations in 23 currencies, so it’s worth its higher cost mainly for an organizer or beneficiary living outside the U.S.

Help Hope Live is a nonprofit that pays approved medical bills straight to providers or reimburses you for eligible costs. Its fee on online gifts totals 5.65%, about $565 on $10,000, unless donors choose to cover it, and the gifts are tax-deductible for the people who give. A medical professional has to confirm the diagnosis before you can request funds. It suits a patient on Medicaid, since money the nonprofit controls likely won’t count against eligibility, though Help Hope Live says to check with your state’s Medicaid office first.

Since three of the five deduct the same 2.9% plus 30 cents a gift, choose by when your bills come due, where you and your donors live, and whether a nonprofit should pay your providers for you.
