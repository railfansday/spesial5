Chelsea Could Claim Share of £164m Compensation

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Shumail Sajid writes about Manchester United for Read Man Utd and Crystal Palace for Read Crystal Palace, with occasional pieces on other Premier League clubs. He has also written for FanSided's Red Devils Dispatch, Yardbarker, Anfield Central and The Sports Side.

Chelsea fans have followed Manchester City’s financial rules case for years. Everyone at Stamford Bridge knows a guilty verdict could cost City serious money, and much of it could end up with rivals lower down the table.

Reports now suggest an independent panel has reached a decision. If that holds up, the scale of any Chelsea Manchester City compensation payout could run into tens of millions of pounds for the Blues alone.

The Premier League charged Manchester City with 115 alleged breaches of financial regulations in February 2023. That followed a four-year investigation into the club’s accounts. The case has hung over the competition ever since. Every top-flight club knows the outcome could change how recent Premier League seasons are remembered.

This matters directly to Chelsea. If City are found to have broken spending and disclosure rules, clubs who finished below them could argue they lost out on prize money and European qualification. Chelsea have competed directly with City for top four places and cup competitions throughout that period.

According to The Athletic, Manchester City were found guilty on all but one of the 115 charges by the independent panel. The Premier League has not confirmed the verdict or issued a statement.

Manchester City have not accepted any suggestion of guilt. The club released a statement addressing the ongoing process directly.

“The Premier League process remains ongoing, with significant elements to be completed, and subject to strict confidentiality. As such, Manchester City FC’s position remains consistent with the Club’s statement of February 2023. ‘The Club has diligently respected due process for eight years on the basis that the Premier League Board and Executive would behave as an independent, impartial and fair-minded regulator, free from partisan influence.'”

That statement makes clear the club does not see the case as settled. Until the Premier League confirms a result, the reported verdict will linger on for official sentencing.

Data specialists at OLBG have estimated the potential Chelsea Manchester City compensation windfall if City are found guilty. They believe City could owe domestic rivals well over £500m in lost Premier League prize money if the charges are proven.

Within that total, London clubs are said to be due around £164m between them. Chelsea, Arsenal and Tottenham Hotspur are among the clubs who could be entitled to a share. Other Premier League sides could benefit too, depending on where they finished compared with City in the seasons in question.

Prize money in the Premier League is tied closely to league position. If City gained points unfairly, that would have pushed a rival down the table and cost them money too. That is the basis for the OLBG figures, and it explains why the sums involved are so large.

For Chelsea, even a share of that £164m pot would be a big boost. It could help fund new signings, or simply ease the pressure around the kind of transfer valuations at Stamford Bridge the club faces every window.

City chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak has already made clear the case is far from finished. He described the process as having “a long way to run.” That points to appeals and further legal steps, whatever the panel has decided.

Chelsea, for their part, carry on with life under Xabi Alonso regardless of how the case unfolds. Attention stays on squad rotation and Xabi Alonso’s squad management strategy, not on any potential windfall. The financial case will not change anything on the pitch in the short term.

The Premier League has stayed silent on the reported verdict, and confidentiality remains strict around the process, according to reports from Football.London. Chelsea fans should expect a long wait before any compensation, if it comes at all, reaches the club’s accounts.
