Never mind the quarterbacks — Patriots must improve their defense to make the playoffs

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

When you're tethered to bad quarterback play in the NFL, it can seem like a hopeless endeavor.

While all the cool kids are out there making effortless big plays with the likes of Joe Burrow, Josh Allen, Trevor Lawrence, and Brock Purdy, those kids stuck at the end of the playground need to level up for any chance. And unfortunately, this is the position the Patriots find themselves in as long as Drake Maye is out with the fractured fibula he suffered in last Sunday's 35-10 loss to the Detroit Lions.

The other quarterbacks on the Patriots' depth chart — Tommy "Cutlets" DeVito and rookie Behren Morton — are not ready for primetime, and DeVito proved his part of that in the Lions game. He completed 12 of 24 passes for 81 yards, one touchdown, one interception, a passer rating of 54.3, and an EPA per dropback of -0.55, which was the NFL's fifth-worst in Week 10. Even when DeVito connected with his receivers, those completions were too often "cemetery balls," throws that led receivers straight into converging defenders.

It's unrealistic to expect DeVito to become something he's not for the rest of the 2026 season, even with the Week 11 bye that gives him an extra week to prepare. And because Maye's injury happened after the Nov. 10 trade deadline (something I've proposed that the league should extend for situations like this), New England's options are limited.

So, do the Patriots punt on the season at 5-5? Certainly not, but how do they get past a clear deficit at the game's most important position?

The good news is that the Pats had a similar situation as they headed into the 2025 playoffs. Maye had played at an NFL MVP level in the regular season, and then, things fell apart. Maye was sacked far more often than in the postseason, and it showed.

But that didn't matter as much as it might on the team's path to Super Bowl LX, because all of a sudden, Zak Kuhr's defense came together in an utterly ridiculous fashion.

So… that's the good news. The 2025 Patriots proved that you can overcome quarterback issues if your defense suddenly goes on an epic clip. The bad news is that the 2026 Patriots defense has not met those standards. Especially over the last two weeks, when they had it handed to them in a 45-27 Week 9 loss to the Green Bay Packers, and what happened to them in the Lions game.

How can Kuhr and his staff turn this around? Because if the Patriots keep playing defense like this with the quarterback play they're going to get… in the immortal words of the great philosopher David Lee Roth, "Might as well jump."

In 2025, Gonzalez was one of the NFL's best cornerbacks, and a big reason that defense carried the offense to the Super Bowl. He allowed 46 catches on 106 targets for 498 yards, one touchdown, one interception, 13 pass breakups, and an opponent passer rating of 57.0 — fourth-lowest in the NFL among cornerbacks who played at least 50% of their teams' snaps. This season, he's given up 31 catches on 46 targets for 469 yards, four touchdowns, one interception, five pass breakups, and an opponent passer rating of 120.7. Among the issues this season is that Gonzalez is a natural press cornerback who is playing more off coverage, and that isn't working.

The splits tell the whole story. No matter what Kuhr does with his other cornerbacks, he needs Gonzalez to play more press than off snaps. Not that it's perfect — Packers receiver Matthew Golden nuked him for completions of 16 and 43 yards out of press with no safety help up top — but the Patriots need their top pass defender to do what he does best.

By the way, the Patriots should also be playing more man coverage and and less zone coverage. The splits are rather obvious there as well.

Through the first eight weeks of the season, the Patriots' pressure rate of 42.4% was the NFL's fourth-highest, but against the Packers and Lions, that pressure rate dropped all the way down to 35.8%, far more middle of the pack. The Patriots do have above-average disruptors in Elijah Ponder, Gabe Jacas, and Christian Barmore, but down the stretch of the season, they're going to need to scheme pressure — and they'll probably need to do that without blitzing at a high rate. This season, the Patriots have allowed an opponent completion percentage of 67.2%, an opponent passer rating of 100.0, and an EPA per dropback of +0.071 when they send five or more rushers.

More simulated pressures, where there are second- and third-level blitzers, and linemen who drop into coverage, may be an (if not the) answer. Kuhr's defense has been more of a balls-out endeavor when blitzing; I believe that more post-snap trickeration to take away the quarterback's hot reads, and mess with the offense's protections, would make a difference.

"This past Sunday just wasn't our best, and nothing was really working out there," Kuhr said last week as his defense was unfortunately getting prepared to be declawed by the Lions after getting smushed by the Packers. "There has to be a balance. You know, I would say one of the things when you play really well at something in the beginning of the season, people start to adjust and adapt and attack it. So you can't just live in one world and try to put those guys in some bad situations.

"But at the end of the day, you know, every call has a beater to it, and they have to have a tough play call to that play. We've got to just challenge and fight through the down and try to make a play. It's the same as we have all year."

Adjusting, adapting, and attacking is what the Patriots must figure out how to do before their Nov. 29 game against the Los Angeles Chargers. Yes, that could amount to an extra bye week given the troubles that Justin Herbert and company have experienced this season under offensive coordinator Mike McDaniel, but these Patriots can't afford to take anyone — or anything — for granted.

Because if this defense can't answer the call, the Patriots' 2026 season is basically over. It's pretty simple.
