Lakers’ trust in starter’s defensive gambles paying off

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Matisse Thybulle's explanation for how he steals passes midair while opponents try to get the ball up the court is simple.

Even if the act of stealing the pass like a free safety intercepts a quarterback isn't.

Los Angeles Lakers guard Matisse Thybulle (4) steals the ball from the Sacramento Kings during an NBA game, Wednesday, Nov. 4, 2026, in Sacramento, Calif. AP Photo/Scott Marshall

"I just look at the person passing; I just make eye contact with them," Thybulle said after Tuesday's shootaround ahead of the Lakers' home game against the Hornets. "Because most guys aren't really throwing no-look passes. Like Luka [Dončić], [Nikola] Jokić, there's a couple guys in the league who really throw nowhere passes, especially in transition.

"So if you just look people in their eyes, they're going to look where they're throwing the ball. I can just get a sense from the eye contact."

Matisse Thybulle of the Los Angeles Lakers blocks a shot by Devin Booker of the Phoenix Suns on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026, in Phoenix, Arizona. Getty Images

But if everyone was able to pick off passes like Thybulle, who's shown a knack for playing the passing lanes throughout his eight-year NBA career, then they would.

So what makes him more equipped at picking off passes in a way that looks easy?

"Just being willing to take the risk," he said. "I've missed a bunch of gambles as well as getting some. So just being willing to take the risk of assuming that where they're looking is in fact where the guy is trying to pass the ball to."

The Lakers have benefited from the defensive gambles the 6-foot-5 wing takes.

They entered Tuesday leading the league in defensive turnover percentage (18.7), according to Cleaning The Glass, and Thybulle is a significant reason.

He entered Tuesday ranked No. 2 in the league in total steals (31) and deflections (60).

Giannis Antetokounmpo shoots against Matisse Thybulle, left, and Austin Reaves during an NBA basketball game Monday, Nov. 2, 2026, in Los Angeles. AP Photo/Jayne Kamin-Oncea

"Me and [assistant] coach Greg [St. Jean] have talked a lot about that — just trying to find ways for me to be me within the foundation of our defense," Thybulle said. "And they like me gambling because it generates turnovers, whether I'm getting the steal. It's just a matter of doing it in ways where our defense can absorb the mistake, if you will. If I do it in ways where our defense is structured to be able to absorb it, then it's something that we can just use, and I try to use to give us an advantage."

The Lakers empowering Thybulle to take advantage of his defensive freedom, similar to how Dončić takes advantage of his offensive freedom, goes back to trust that was established during the preseason and training camp.

"It can be challenging at times to play with someone like me who's taking a lot of risks, gambling, getting out of position and sometimes throwing off the team defense," he said. "And it can be really easy for them to just be like, 'Well, Matisse keeps messing up our defense. Like, what the hell is he doing?' But they understand and they let me learn from my mistakes."

He continued: "I gamble and sometimes I don't get the pass, but sometimes I get a steal on the back end. There's just the rapport that's grown through that."
