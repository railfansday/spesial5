Nets snap a five game losing streak, beat the Bucks 104-97

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The Brooklyn Nets and Milwaukee Bucks came into tonight's game like two school kids trotting onto the playground, one pointing and chirping at the other "you're the worst defense," while the other bickered back "no, you're the worst defense."

Each youngster had some merit to their argument. Brooklyn had just given up 130+ in three of their last four games, and ranked dead last in defensive rating ahead of the contest. Milwaukee ranked behind them by a few fractions of a point.

Brooklyn would eventually win the squabble, and the game, but it'd take them some time to get there, not that anyone's complaining here.

Brooklyn came out the gates without a whole lot of ball pressure. In fairness, they had Danny Wolf matched up initially against Tyler Herro, which leaves you very little spatial insurance to work with. However, the Nets also looked out of sync rotating around the Milwaukee offense, which didn't help either. As Brooklyn shot 1-6 from the field and the Bucks pranced out to a 9-2 lead, it just looked like more of the same in the early going.

As I mentioned, we'd eventually find out otherwise. And while real turnaround would soon come on the defensive end, things had to start for Brooklyn at the other.

After Milwaukee's opening jolt, the Nets went on a 13-0 run in the heart of the first to go up 22-12. Michael Porter Jr., who spoke to Brain Lewis this week about how he's struggled to "find his rhythm," was in his grove amid the stretch, putting in eight first period points.

Getting five from Day'Ron Sharpe and Julius Randle each, as well as eight points off the bench in the period, the Nets led after one by a 28-24 margin.

Despite being less of a scorer this time around, Mikel Brown Jr. looked comfortable out there early on as well, starting for a second straight game. He shot 0-5 from the field in the first half but clocked in as a +4 with two assists with zero turnovers.

Also like last game, Grant Nelson joined him on the floor for a few minutes in the second. Nelson only finished with one point, but grabbed six rebounds, contributing to a strong collective effort from the team on the glass, where the Nets won 52-44. Porter Jr. also grabbed 11 rebounds, his most in a game all season if you discount the overtime affair vs Boston.

Brooklyn would need every one of those boards especially in the first half, where neither team shot it at a particularly high clip, as the Nets posted .400/.348 splits, while the Bucks had .432/.273 marks.

At the break, Brooklyn led 51-47, largely thanks to Randle, who had 16 points on 7-10 shooting from the field and 2-4 from deep. MPJ went quiet in the second, still rocking with eight points by then. Day'Ron Sharpe put us on early triple-double watch with five points, 10 rebounds, and four assists at the break.

Herro, however, had all the stats to begin the third. He poured in nine points during the period's first four minutes to put his team back in the lead for the first time since the 6:57 mark of the first. The Nets, meanwhile, opened the period 3-12 from the field and 0-5 from deep. Two of the makes came from Brown Jr, so at least there's that.

Sarcasm aside, as one young guard for Brooklyn started to turn it on, another followed. After Milwaukee looked ready to pull away, Egor Dëmin checked in, scoring five points himself and assisting on two baskets in just a minute and a half. His effort helped ignited a 13-2 run that put the lead back in Brooklyn's hands.

At the same time, Randle continued to mine inside for points. He'd eventually go for 28 in the game on 10-16 shooting. That not only gave Brooklyn consistent offense, but slowed things down and allowed them to set up easily at the other end. Even with Herro's early offensive excellence, the Bucks went 7-23 from the field in the quarter, and the Nets led going into the fourth by a 77-71 score.

"I think everybody was able to just put their minds together and not just focus on team defense, but their own island, like how we say 'guard your yard,'" Dëmin said. "I think that's where it came from. Everything else was easier because everybody was so locked in and super focused on how to guard the man in front of you rather than relying on someone else around."

And believe it or not, in that period, the defense continued to improve. On Milwaukee's second possession of the frame, Porter Jr. poked the a ball away in the backcourt, which Nelson dove across mid court to save. Still, the Bucks got it up and forward, but then Keon Ellis came charging over, intercepting the rock and then dishing it ahead Porter Jr. for an and-one finish. That was just one of the Nets seven steals in the fourth.

"Go out there and play with physicality," Sharpe said. "Go out there and play with ball pressure. Execute with staples and have each others back. I felt like we did a great job at that tonight."

Sharpe never got the triple double, but did finish 11 points, five assists, and 14 rebounds while shooting 5-10 from the field and hitting a three.

The Nets got up by 11 early in the fourth. After a few turnovers early in the frame, the Bucks cut it back down to eight, and we looked in store for a tight finish.

But in a game where Brooklyn's supposed fatal flaw had carried them, it was only right that Noah Clowney, who's struggled with his shot all year, iced things out. In the fourth, he added nine points on 4-6 shooting, scoring from all over the court to keep the lead afloat. He finished with a season-high 17 points, also grabbing five rebounds while shooting 7-13 from the field.

"This is the Noah Clowney that we need," Fernández said. "It gives us a starter off the bench and it's very, very good for this group, because it brings a player on both ends."

Dëmin, who's also had a difficult start to the year, leveraged his size to play some inspiring defense down the stretch as well, finishing as a +5 in the quarter. For the game, he had 15 points, four assists, three rebounds, a steal, and a block while shooting 5-11 from the field.

"Yeah, he went back to doing the simple things, and it started with his physicality on defense," Fernández said. "They felt him the whole game. His length. He can rebound, he can be in the gaps…Proud of him for sure."

Sure, the Nets still turned it over on back-t0-back possession with the Bucks only down 11 and under a minute to go. Dëmin missed also missed an open dunk. They're still a young, flawed team. They didn't solve all their issues tonight, but they did win the night, and snapped their five game losing streak.

Final: Brooklyn Nets 104, Milwaukee Bucks 97

Mo Wagner subbed out at the end of the first period and did not return for the rest of the game. At half, the Nets ruled him out with left knee soreness.

"I don't know. I was about to sub him in, and I saw him limping, and I was like I got to save him from himself," Fernández said. "He was there to do whatever it took for his team and his teammates, and at the end I decided to not bring him back in. Obviously, I don't know what it is, but the good thing, the great thing is he was cheering for everybody. He was cheering fro Grant [Nelson] the whole time, and that means a lot for the group, and it speaks to the type of person he is."

We'll provide updates on him once they're made available.

Day'Ron Sharpe had a season-high 14 rebounds tonight and also recorded his 5th double-double of the season with 11 points. It matches his double-double total from last season, tied as the 2nd most in his career (6, 2023-24). This was his 7th career game with 10+ PTS, 10+ REB and 5+ AST.

Noah Clowney had a season-high 17 points off the bench tonight against the Bucks. He has tied his season high with three 3-pointers made.

Michael Porter Jr. logged his third double-double of the season with 12 points and 11 rebounds. It is his 15th time recording at least 10 rebounds as a Net.

Brooklyn gets right back in action after the holiday with a road matchup against the Toronto Raptors on Friday. The North has lost seven of their last eight, including a contest to the Bucks on Monday. They're certainly looking at this one as a "get right" game. The game tips off at 7:30 p.m. EST.
