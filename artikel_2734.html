From 1-5 to above .500, the Suns are doing something right

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The Phoenix Suns are a game above .500. When this team started the season 1-5, I wasn't entirely convinced I'd have the opportunity to type those words anytime soon. Yet here we are, thanks to some improved chemistry, particularly among the starting unit, and a favorable stretch of the schedule.

How much of this resurgence can be attributed to genuine improvement versus the quality of competition? Only time will tell. We spend so much time dissecting how games are won or lost that we occasionally lose sight of a rather important detail. A win is a win. And in the NBA, every single one of them matters.

Yes, the Suns defeated the Utah Jazz by 10 points on Friday night, despite allowing Utah to trim the deficit to four in the closing minutes. Naturally, some will point to that late surge as evidence that this wasn't a particularly impressive victory. The funny thing? Many of those same people will dismiss Wednesday's win over the Sacramento Kings because, well, that's what the Suns were supposed to do. Don't be that guy.

Phoenix put itself in a position to win on Friday night, although Utah certainly helped the cause by missing 13 free throws. The result? A third consecutive road victory and a team that suddenly finds itself on the right side of .500.

Winning in the NBA is hard. I don't care who you're playing, how many injuries the opposing team is dealing with, or how ugly the basketball might look at times. Every team in this league has talented players capable of making your life miserable on any given night. Funny how that works, too. When an undermanned opponent beats the Suns, we're quick to praise their resilience and talk about how there's still some fight in that dog. But when Phoenix beats an undermanned opponent, suddenly the victory comes with an asterisk in the court of public opinion.

The win over Utah wasn't necessarily pretty. Some stretches made you uncomfortable, moments that invited frustration, and a fourth quarter that probably didn't need to be as interesting as it became. But it was effective. It added another victory to the win column, extended the momentum of a successful road trip, and gave the Suns something positive to carry into Monday's matchup against the New Orleans Pelicans.

It certainly beats the alternative. Never forget that alternative, either. Because if the Suns had blown that lead and lost to Utah, we'd be spending the next 48 hours dissecting every possession, questioning every rotation decision, and wondering whether this team had learned a damn thing from its early struggles.

Instead? Good win. Hop on the plane. On to the next city.
