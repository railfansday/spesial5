Inez and Vinoodh Mark 40 Years With a CFDA Lifetime Achievement Award

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The celebrated photography duo and couple will be honored at the annual ceremony.

This evening, celebrated photography duo Inez van Lamsweerde and Vinoodh Matadin will be given the Geoffrey Beene Lifetime Achievement Award at the CFDA Fashion Awards. The pair, who are married, have been working together for 40 years, creating ad campaigns for brands like Gucci, Chanel, and Saint Laurent and shooting for major magazines.

“We didn’t know it was possible for [the Geoffrey Beene award to be given for] photography because it was always designers until now,” Matadin tells ELLE. “We’re just so happy that the CFDA is recognizing photography as such an integral part of the industry we all work in. The fact is that we as photographers translate a designer’s vision into an image or a film, so their designs live on in another world, in another way, in other media.”

This year also saw “Can Love Be a Photograph,” a museum retrospective of their work at the Kunstmuseum Den Haag in the Netherlands. Below, Van Lamsweerde and Matadin spoke with ELLE about the award, working together as spouses, and how digitalization is changing how we connect.

Inez van Lamsweerde: What was so unbelievable about that experience of choosing 200 works to reflect on our 40 years of working together was that now there’s a new baseline. Now this is from where we keep going, keep growing, keep having new ideas.

Vinoodh Matadin: At one point we said it’s not a retrospective, it’s a futurespective.

IVL: In most of the works in the exhibition it feels like there’s an element in this work that we could explore more, that asks for more new work to be made. It’s incredibly energizing and exciting to see that ideas from the mid-’90s can still be developed more.

VM: We realized when we did this exhibition that our work is almost timeless because it deals with the real and the unreal.

IVL: What’s so exciting every time is to step into someone’s world and hear about their influences, their inspiration, who they want to talk to, who they want to relate to, and what part of our work represents that for them. Then together, we form the ideas for a campaign. It always starts somewhere and then it ends somewhere completely different because as you go along together and work with the models or stylists, there’s new input all the time.

IVL: There is a series we made called “Thank You Thighmaster.” It’s large-scale photographs of a model shot nude in the studio that we then combined with faces of mannequin dolls from the ’70s. We took away all the orifices to show a hermetically closed person that still has all these feelings inside of her, but no way to communicate, because computers are the way we now are in contact with each other, and physicality is less and less important.

IVL: The title of the exhibition and the book that goes with it is “Can Love Be a Photograph?” To us, it’s not a question, it’s a statement. The exchange that we have with the person that’s sitting in front of our lens and then the viewer’s relationship to what they see on the photo is actually the most important thing. It’s this triangle of relationships, of emotion, of trust, of reflecting back on yourself that really drives everything for us. The bottom line of that is we love people. It’s just the most wonderful life to learn from everybody every day.

VM: The sad part of all this digitalization is that it was invented to connect us all. Now, we’re all disconnected while we’re all connected. No one can communicate with each other, and we don’t know what’s the real and what is the unreal anymore, and what is the truth. The only thing that we still have left is human contact and being in the now.

IVL: If you’re both rigid, it doesn’t work. You have to give and take one or the other way. I think a lot of people say to us like, “Oh my God, I could never work with my spouse.” We’re just constantly thinking, How could you not? For us, this is our way of living, and we’ve now also been together and worked together longer than we weren’t together.

VM: We still have the feeling we just started.

IVL: We still feel like young photographers.
