Nvidia Stock Jumps 9% Pre-Market as Trump Pushes Huang’s Return: Will the Board Bend?

Nvidia stock (NVDA) rose about 9% in pre-market trading on Monday, November 23, after President Donald Trump urged the company’s board to bring back Jensen Huang. “I hope the Board of Nvidia rehires Jensen Huang,” Trump wrote on Truth Social late on Sunday.

BREAKING: President Trump says he hopes Nvidia’s board, $NVDA, rehires Jensen Huang.“It would be the best thing for the company and for the USA,” Trump says. pic.twitter.com/sARHIMKFb9

The bounce follows a brutal run. Nvidia closed Friday at $125.53, down 14% in a single day, its worst session since the DeepSeek sell-off in January 2025. It was the stock’s seventh losing week in a row, its longest streak since 2008.

Trump wants Huang back, but the path is far from smooth. Nvidia’s board says it will not reinstate him. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent wants answers on export controls, and the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is reportedly looking at the company’s accounting.

So where does Nvidia stand now, and can Trump’s backing turn the stock around?

Huang resigned as CEO, president, and board member on November 12, the company said in its CEO transition release. Chief financial officer Colette Kress became interim CEO.

He left after admitting to an extramarital affair. US intelligence officials suspect the woman, Alice Huang (no relation), of spying for China. She is a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore company suspected of diverting Nvidia chips to China against US export rules.

Trump has backed Huang ever since. On November 16, he told reporters he “would not mind” a return and said Huang had “learned a big lesson.” On Friday, he said he would love to see Huang “be given a second chance.” Sunday’s post went further.

The board has not moved. Chair Harvey Jones said the board “unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.” Nvidia has hired the search firm Heidrick & Struggles to find a permanent chief.

Outside pressure is building anyway. An online petition backing Huang, signed by Elon Musk, has passed 200,000 names.

The administration does not speak with one voice. On November 15, Bessent called for a “dramatic shakeup” at Nvidia and said the board should hire a CEO from outside.

He also asked whether Nvidia staff were “pressing these seemingly illicit sales to goose their revenues and their earnings.” He compared the case to a bank, saying regulators “would be in there right now.”

By Sunday, his tone had changed. “I said they should go to the outside, but he’s on the outside now,” Bessent told CBS’s Face the Nation. That left the door open to a return without endorsing one.

The China question has not gone away. Bessent said Nvidia must tell the administration by November 25 whether shell companies were used to get its most advanced chips into China. “Was it at the lower levels? Was it the middle levels? Did it go all the way to the top?” he asked.

The selling has nothing to do with demand. On November 17, Nvidia posted third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier and well above the $109.1 billion analysts expected. It guided to about $130 billion for the current quarter, assuming no data center sales in China.

“Our momentum is real,” Kress said. The market did not agree. To see how the scandal and the stock lined up, we matched each day’s close since Huang’s affair came out with that day’s news.

Nvidia stock had already slipped 22% from its October 6 record close of $239.24 by November 6, as investors worried about AI spending. Then the scandal hit. Over the next 10 trading sessions, the shares lost another 33%, falling on nine of those days.

The earnings beat bought no relief. From the November 17 close of $155.46, the shares dropped 19% in three sessions, with Friday’s 14% fall driven by the SEC report. Nvidia now trades at about 8.4 times expected earnings, CNBC calculated, against about 29 times for AMD.

At $125.53, Nvidia stock closed at its lowest level since May 2025. It is down 47.5% from the October record, a loss of about $2.7 trillion in market value.

Nvidia stock trades around $136 before Monday’s open, going by Hyperliquid’s Nvidia perpetual contract, which trades around the clock. It sits well below its 50-day exponential moving average (EMA), an average that gives recent prices more weight, at $185, and its 200-day EMA at $202.

XYZ:NVDAUSD 1D chart. Source: Hyperliquid

Trump’s support gave the stock a lift, but the board, Bessent’s November 25 deadline, and the SEC will decide what comes next. Until Nvidia answers the China questions, a rebound above $150 looks hard to hold.
