Alaska Dem Peltola defeats Sullivan, flipping 9th GOP Senate seat after 'decoy Dan' uproar

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Alaska Democratic Sen.-elect Mary Peltola reacted Tuesday to defeating Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan, saying "we are going to put Alaska first." (X/Mary Peltola)

Democrats flipped a ninth GOP-held Senate seat in the 2026 midterm elections, with former Democratic Rep. Mary Peltola defeating two-term Republican Sen. Dan S. Sullivan in a state that President Donald Trump carried in 2024.

Peltola's victory gives Democrats a 56-44 majority in the U.S. Senate.

"Alaskans have voted for change, and I'm so honored to be your next U.S. senator," Peltola said in a social media video released after The Associated Press called the race Tuesday two weeks after Election Day. "Together, we're gonna put an end to the era of self-serving politicians lining their own pockets at your expense. And together, we are going to put Alaska first."

Peltola defeated Sullivan with 50.1% of the vote, avoiding a ranked-choice vote count.

The contest emerged as one of the nation's most closely watched Senate races, with Republicans defending a seat in a state President Donald Trump carried by roughly 13 percentage points in 2024, and Democrats eyeing a rare opportunity to make inroads in traditionally Republican territory.

The controversy deepened just days before Election Day when The New York Times reported that Peltola campaign aides, attorneys and national Democratic operatives had participated in a secret effort to recruit a candidate sharing the Republican incumbent's name.

Alaska Democratic Senate candidate Mary Peltola speaks at an election night event Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026, in Anchorage, Alaska.  (AP Photo/Lindsey Wasson)

According to the Times, text messages and interviews revealed a broader operation, internally dubbed the "ballot composition project," aimed at recruiting candidates who could siphon votes from Sullivan. The effort reportedly involved the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee and included discussions about establishing a new right-wing political party.

Peltola's campaign denied involvement with either Sullivan campaign, while the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee (DSCC) also rejected allegations of participation. The revelations prompted Republican Gov. Michael Dunleavy to order an investigation into whether the alleged recruitment effort violated Alaska law.

The Cook Political Report rated the contest a toss-up, reflecting the unusually competitive environment in a state that has generally favored Republicans in federal elections.

Peltola, a former state lawmaker, made history in 2022 when she became the first Alaska Native elected to Congress, winning a special election to succeed the late Republican Rep. Don Young.

She defeated former Republican Gov. Sarah Palin in that contest before winning a full term later that year. Peltola subsequently lost her 2024 reelection bid to Republican Nick Begich III, setting the stage for her Senate campaign.

Sullivan, a Marine Corps veteran and former Alaska attorney general, first won his Senate seat in 2014 by defeating Democratic incumbent Mark Begich, Nick's uncle. He secured reelection in 2020 and sought a third term emphasizing his experience, national security and Alaska's energy interests.

The 2026 race also featured an unusual ballot controversy involving Dan J. Sullivan, a retired teacher who shared nearly the same name as the Republican incumbent.

Alaska election officials initially moved to remove the second Sullivan from the ballot amid allegations that his candidacy was intended to confuse voters or siphon support from the senator. The candidate denied wrongdoing, and the Alaska Supreme Court ruled that he could remain on the ballot. The DOJ sued in October to mitigate any "misleading" impact of the newcomer Sullivan.

The controversy added another complication to Alaska's ranked-choice voting system, which allows voters to rank candidates in order of preference.

Under the system, a candidate receiving a majority of first-choice votes wins outright. Otherwise, the lowest-performing candidate is eliminated, and ballots are redistributed according to voters' next available preferences until a winner emerges.

Anchorage, Alaska's largest city, is seen against the Chugach Range. (Getty Images/Getty Images)

Peltola had previously benefited from the system during her 2022 congressional victories, when ranked-choice tabulation helped determine the outcomes of races featuring multiple Republican opponents.

The Alaska contest carried national significance as both parties battled over control of the Senate, with the result determining whether Republicans retained the seat or Democrats added it to their column.
