Bitcoin tests $62K support – Can BTC bulls hold without Spot demand?

After the $3 billion crypto liquidation wave that occurred on the 13th and 14th of November, the liquidation numbers have slowed slightly.

A relatively small liquidation number of just over $563 million was recorded on the 15th, and just $289.9 million in liquidations across the market on the 16th of November, according to CoinGlass data.

AMBCrypto reported recently that the Bitcoin [BTC] price move down to $62k acted as a leverage flush. It remains to be seen whether the bulls can enforce a recovery from here.

In a post on X, Glassnode showed that the price was trading just above a dense cluster of short-term holder supply. The darker cells denote more BTC supply in STH custody at that cost basis.

The $62k-$63k area was a particularly dense STH supply cluster, and in recent days, there has been a positive reaction. The same area had been the base of the previous impulse move higher in mid-August.

If this area is breached, it could lead to another capitulation and new lows being formed.

Crypto analyst Axel Adler Jr. noted that the bulls were trying to push BTC prices higher in the futures market. The hourly taker buy-sell imbalance reached +8,709 BTC on Monday, the 16th of November.

Yet, the price was yet to push higher. The first areas of interest were the imbalance at $66.2k-$66.9k and the local high at $70,214.

Deeply negative apparent demand (30-day sum), with a 30-day reading of -247,700 BTC and a yearly measurement of -1.21 million BTC, highlighted severe market stress.

Crypto analyst Darkfost wrote that the metric, which is an indirect on-chain proxy based on new issuance versus changes in supply inactive for over a year, does not measure net cash outflows.

Nevertheless, it has historically been a reliable indicator that helps confirm trend strength. Right now, any price rebound without apparent demand easing toward neutral or positive levels would signal weakness.
