Some big employers still give part-time workers health insurance. Most require about 20 hours a week, and a few set no hours minimum. Before you apply, find out how many hours you’ll need, how long you’ll wait, and whether the plan covers hospital care or pays only set dollar amounts.

Employers like these are the exception. In March 2026, just 23% of part-time workers in private industry had access to medical benefits through work, against 87% of those working full time, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

Under the Affordable Care Act, an employer with at least 50 full-time workers, counting part-timers’ hours toward that total, can owe the IRS a payment if it doesn’t offer coverage to employees who average 30 hours a week. A part-timer who gets a tax credit for a Marketplace plan, the health coverage people buy on their own, can’t trigger that payment, so covering part-timers is each employer’s choice.

Each employer also sets its own hours requirement. Nine of the 10 below publish theirs, from none to 20 hours a week, and at REI and U-Haul some part-timers get a more limited plan than full-timers do.

The Marketplace at HealthCare.gov sells individual health plans, and income-based tax credits can lower the premium.

A job’s offer that you turn down keeps you from those credits only if it’s affordable and meets the minimum value standard. That standard asks for a plan built to pay at least 60% of medical costs, with substantial coverage for hospital stays and doctor visits.

Most job plans meet it, so ask the employer whether its part-time plan does before you enroll.
