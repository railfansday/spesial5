Wolves 117, Rockets 107: The Blue Tsunami in Houston

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

As soon as the game tipped off, one thing was very clear: This was a completely different level of basketball compared to Monday night's game. Unique matchups (LaMelo Ball on Jabari Smith!), shot-making, and game plan discipline meant that the first half was full of back-and-forth, high-quality hoops.

Ayo Dosunmu was such a blue tsunami in transition that even Steve Kornaki had to pause what he was doing and just say, "Wow".

Dosunmu went a perfect 6/6 at the rim on his way to twelve first-half points. On the other side of the floor, Jonathan Kuminga was fantastic on Amen Thompson, playing a brand of athletic and physical basketball that the Wolves haven't been able to do before against Thompson.

The physicality was of note, as he did get a flagrant one after Sengun's face invaded his personal space. Ultimately, it may have been worth it for the repeated over-the-top crowd reaction to the replay, which was genuinely funny.

And finally, the first half story wouldn't be complete without mentioning Jaylen Clark, the certified stuff doer. He had two steals, two offensive rebounds, and a drawn offensive foul. Luckily for the Rockets, the Wolves decided they wanted to keep the game close by shooting free throws with their opposite hands, hitting on just 5/10 as the teams went into halftime tied.

The good basketball continued into the second half, though the third quarter did leave me with a pressing existential question like: "What exactly is a moving screen?" and "Why don't defensive players just run into the screener and fall down every time?"

Outside of that Marcus Smart-inspired existential crisis, both teams essentially played each other even as they headed into the final quarter with the Rockets up two.

As you can see, the start of the fourth quarter was something!

LaMelo Ball committed three fouls in about 30 seconds and put the Rockets in the bonus. But then goes out and totally redeems himself with eight straight points, part of his twelve-point fourth-quarter effort. The back-and-forth affair continued until Jonathan Kuminga drained two huge threes, which, paired with dive-on-the-floor defense and excellent ball movement on offense, would be enough to push the Wolves over the top for the 117-107 victory in Houston.

Let's just get this one out of the way. If the Wolves had lost this game, this would have been the lament in Timberwolves land. They went 10-for-19 from the line, and missing free throws has absolutely been an epidemic for this team. It didn't cost them the game tonight, but they have way too many good shooters for these 1-2 trips to the line.

+14: Points Off Turnover Advantage for the Wolves

Hello defense! From the get-go, I loved the way the Wolves matched up with the Rockets by putting Ayo on Van Fleet, McDaniels on KD, Kuminga on Amen Thompson (more on this soon), and letting LaMelo guard Jabari Smith Jr, a matchup that would force them out of a lot of their offensive sets if they wanted to take advantage of it. This all led to eighteen turnovers for the Rockets and forced them into hitting a lot of tough shots all night.

Just when we are all ready to be mad at you for three fouls in thirty seconds, you go out there and totally redeem yourself! LaMelo takes home this award for being fantastic in the fourth quarter. He had the perfect balance of finding his own shot, creating offense for others, and forcing Houston to bring double teams that he broke, and helped lead the Wolves to their first clutch-time victory of the season.

The seven turnovers are a reminder it wasn't perfect, but his +15 team high with 20 points, six assists, and four – count 'em, four – stocks show a player that filled the stat sheet and played winning basketball.

The Straight to Jail Award: Jonathan Kuminga

One of the most encouraging aspects of the first nine games of the season for the Wolves has been Jonathan Kuminga's defense. It's one of those things that, if it stays at this level, could be a difference maker come April. In past seasons, Amen Thompson's elite athleticism has been a matchup issue for the Wolves, but Kuminga was all over him tonight, forcing a 3-for-9 night from the field with three turnovers and a team-worst -11.

Straight to jail for Amen Thompson tonight!

The And I Helped! Award: Ayo Dosunmo, Bones Hyland, Jaylen Clark

Too many good games tonight to not single out more than three players in the awards segment. If not for LaMelo's big fourth quarter, the Big Ticket was Ayo's. He was relentless in attacking downhill when he got the matchup he wanted, had a 6-0 AST/TO ratio, and was our main source of offense for most of the first half.

Bones Hyland is just a bucket. Scoring 19 points in just 16 minutes is a needed injection of life into an offense that is missing its high-volume scorer.

Already mentioned it earlier, but it deserves to be said again: Jaylen Clark is a certified stuff doer. He finishes the night with 10 points on 4/6 shooting (including two of three from deep!), three rebounds, two steals, and a drawn offensive foul. But the biggest sign of encouragement? He played 25 minutes!

Rudy Gobert had one of his better defensive games against Sengun, but my goodness he needs to finish better. It is not a good sign that my confidence needle in his ability to finish a contested layup has moved into the tossup range with his ability to make a three

Another solid outing from Joan Beringer! By my count, he's now stacked four-to-give good performances in a row

Trey Lyles is the last player hasn't been mentioned, but he was also solid too! He's becoming a very valuable role player that knows what he does best and stays in that zone.
