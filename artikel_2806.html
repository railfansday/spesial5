Lakers needed a little bit from everyone in win against Kings

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

LOS ANGELES — If Friday's 106-104 win against the Sacramento Kings was any indicator, it's as if the Lakers have played bad teams so often over the past two weeks that they've started to become one.

"That was a—a win's a win," Lakers center Walker Kessler said as he labored into his postgame presser.

But just as the trillions of cells in the human body help fight a cold, Los Angeles got (and needed) a little bit of everything from everyone to get over this Sacramento flu.

Whether it was Kessler's first 3-point attempt and make of the season, seven assists from Quentin Grimes, who yet again hit a game-sealing three, five combined offensive rebounds from Jarred Vanderbilt and Sandro Mamukelashvili off the bench or 14 points from Matisse Thybulle, the Lakers needed every bit of it.

"Certain nights, whether it's the team, how we're playing, the atmosphere of the arena, you can just be a little flat," head coach JJ Redick said. "And I thought we just had to be tougher and pick up our energy. They accepted the challenge."

Friday marks the end of a stretch of four games in six nights for the Lakers, who improved to an NBA-best 14-3 record and are one of six teams to reach the 17-game mark. Los Angeles is also tied with three other teams for most back-to-back games thus far at four.

They haven't been absent of casualties during this early war of attrition. Austin Reaves missed his fourth consecutive game with a left heel bruise he suffered against Milwaukee. That the Lakers have certainly felt the impact of playing without their second-best player is no revelation, but Luka Dončić has perhaps felt his absence the most.

Dončić, the league's leading scorer, extended his shooting slump on Friday, scoring 25 points on 2-of-9 shooting from three in another game where he was the focal point of the opposing defense every second he was on the floor. Dating back to the Milwaukee game, his shooting percentages have dipped to 41.4% from the field and 30.6% from beyond the arc.

The double-teams and blitzes he saw earlier in the season were less effective when Reaves was there to make the defense pay. But without him, the weight of the offense is much heavier on Dončić.

That's where Los Angeles's role players come in. Just as inconsistent as their roles have been so far, their impact on winning has been just as consistent. Redick said postgame that their plan in the offseason was to surround Dončić and Reaves with complementary players, and that despite having played three-quarters of a game with both of them available over their last seven games, the Lakers are 6-1.

"It's a credit to the group [for] sticking with it," Redick added. "Basketball's a game of mistakes. We played through some mistakes tonight, just like we did the other night against Charlotte. Good win. We'll take it."

The locker room is beset by these shooting slumps. Dončić, Mamu, Ziaire Williams and Jake LaRavia, among others, are simply going through one of those stretches. Redick plans to use the Lakers' forthcoming two-day break — a rarity so far this season — to ensure the team continues to stay sharp and get shots up.

After Monday's rematch against Utah, the team will enjoy another three days off before clashing with San Antonio. Just as Grimes broke through a rough start to the season, Redick believes others will come around, no matter how much a slump can weigh on someone.

To that point, as Redick recently stayed up late on the iMovie app on his phone, cooking up a two-minute highlight video of six-time major champion golfer Rory McIlroy to show his players — a video he humbly thought was "well-produced and edited" — one of McIlroy's quotes stuck with him during this trying time:

"When you're not playing well, it feels like your good game is so far away. And then once you start playing well, you realize it was never that far away to begin with."
