Mormon Wives' Avery Woods Claims Explicit Pics Got Sold on 170,000 Porn Sites

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Secret Lives of Mormon Wives: Orange County star Avery Woods made startling claims about how her past explicit photos made their way to nearly 200,000 porn sites.

“When I was being told that people were sending these photos around and then when I watched the scene, it made me kind of nauseous. It made me sick to my stomach. I have children and obviously that account was deleted on my own,” Woods, 31, said in Us’ exclusive clip from the Tuesday, November 24, episode of the “Not Skinny But Not Fat” podcast.

Woods clarified that she “chose to delete” her OnlyFans account “so many years ago,” adding, “I didn’t know those photos still existed. So I hired a private investigator to find out where these photos are coming from. Apparently, those photos have been sold in over 170,000 different pornography websites — illegally — without my consent. I had no idea they existed.”

The reality star clarified to podcast host Amanda Hirsch that the NSFW sites continue to profit off her.

“People kept [my photos from OnlyFans]. Then they sold them to all these sites and my PI was like, ‘They’re making between $5,000 and $30,000 a month off your body,'” she said. “I had no idea. I don’t ever Google myself.”

Woods continued: “They’ve been working on it for months [to take the photos down]. … There is such a vast spectrum when it comes to OnlyFans. You can sell feet pics and you can make sex videos.”

After her past on OnlyFans came up during season 1 of Mormon Wives: OC, Woods accused some of her costars of “trying to make it seem so much worse” than it was.

“I understand being protective but this is not an active thing that I’m doing or participating in. I am very confident in my body. I’m proud of it,” Avery, who is married to David Woods, said in the clip. “It had two C-sections to birth my babies. I love posting a sexy bikini pic on Instagram. I think women’s bodies are beautiful.”

Avery found herself shocked by the way her decision was addressed.

“Watching that scene, I had to go to the bathroom and throw up. I puked,” she added. “It was so degrading watching women that I really [thought were] my friends. We have spent so much time together and watching that made me ill when they were passing it around,” she continued. “If I had witnessed that for somebody else — where they pass inappropriate photos of somebody that I cared about around — I would be like, ‘That is not OK. You need to stop.'”

She continued: “I understand that people might have an issue with things I’ve done in the past, and I can’t change that. But I can be better moving forward.”

Avery was asked on the podcast if she was concerned that mentioning the photos on the show would make people search for them, to which she replied, “It happens and as much as I can encourage people and beg them not to do that, they’re going to.”

Before Mormon Wives: OC premiered, Avery also spoke to Us about the challenging onscreen moments.

“I think in the moment when you’re blindsided like that, and you know that the whole world is going to see it, it just is very overwhelming,” she exclusively told Us. “I have worked a lot over the last year of really getting my mental health in check, and so I just wanted to prioritize that.”

Avery noted that the woman she was is a “completely different person” than who she is on reality TV.

“And I think that’s OK as a human to grow and learn and be different moving forward,” she said. “So when you bring a dynamic and a group like this together, a lot of things can be dredged up from the past. But I just wanted to be very honest and vulnerable, and talk about being the provider for my family and doing what I needed to do in order to feed my kids and make sure they’re taken care of.”

The Secret Lives of Mormon Wives: Orange County is streaming now on Hulu and Disney+.
