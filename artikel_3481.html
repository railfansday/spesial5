Christmas in July? Why Stop There?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Why do we let something like the calendar dictate our holidays? The fact that Thanksgiving and Christmas are just four weeks apart means by the time you’ve returned from November family visits, unpacked, shopped, decorated, done the party circuit, bought gifts, and maybe—maybe—worked for a few days, you have to turn around and celebrate again. It’s exhausting and expensive and too warp-speed to feel like that much of a good time. I’m not arguing to ban Christmas—which I am using as a catchall for nondenominational winter holidays, however that looks to you—what I am proposing is, in fact, much more exciting. I think we should switch the dates of Christmas and Valentine’s Day.

Consider it! Instead of condensing so much effort and so much merriment into such a ludicrously small window at the very end of the year with nothing but the bland hell of winter ahead of us, let’s maximize our time by stretching it out. Valentine’s Day is for romance, whether that means watching Bridget Jones’s Diary with friends, eating overpriced meals at candlelit restaurants, making cards, or mainlining chocolates. So on December 24, celebrate platonic love with friends—à la Galentine’s Day, except we should think of a better name—and maybe go on a date on the 25th. If that date goes well: bam! You now have a plus-one to a New Year’s Eve party and a midnight make out partner.

If the holiday season has wrapped by February 14, the weeks following are the shoulder season. Life is slow. But by the time everyone trickles back home from watching Die Hard in their pajamas or shopping post-Christmas sales or going to the gym, you only have to survive one month until the sweet relief of spring in late March. Nothing makes more sense for how to survive the long nights and cold of winter.

So we go all-in on spring. Mid-April is when it likely won’t snow again; around then you no longer need heat—at least in New York City—so let’s make that a national holiday celebrating warm weather with seasonal bouquets (tulips, lilacs, peonies), foraged ramps, and maybe specials on dry-cleaning winter coats or sales on shearling boots.

But why stop there? We in America could take a page from France, where May can have four(!) public holidays as a kind of summer practice run. I’d be more than happy to celebrate VE Day in solidarity: Liberté, égalité, holiday! In our version, maybe botanical gardens will be free to the public for picnics. Maybe amusement parks will be gratis? Maybe lobster rolls and strawberry shortcake will be handed out on the street? Can the first summer blockbuster of May be an annual event with free popcorn? This month of long weekends will culminate in Memorial Day barbecues, which is a perfect bookend to summer with Labor Day—no notes.

Let’s keep summer luxurious, so summer Fridays will not be at the discretion of employers but mandated by the government for those three months. The Fourth of July can stay where it is with the caveat that if it rains, we get to observe it on the next day forecast for hot dog weather. Nothing important happens in August—phone it in, federal holiday or not. Labor Day stays, of course. The week following it in New York is Fashion Week, which runs concurrently with the US Open for tennis—very busy but very social. We’re going to adopt that nationwide as a festive season soft launching the country into fall.

And finally, let’s turn our attention to that somber stretch of time between early September and Thanksgiving. We don’t get enough time off, so we will move Presidents’ Day from February to mid-October for another much-needed holiday. Then cook a big turkey dinner on Thanksgiving, see your extended family, watch football, whatever, and delight in the fact that you don’t have to do it again until February. But start figuring out your Valentine’s Day dates now—those prix fixe dinners with chocolate lava cakes really book up fast these days.
