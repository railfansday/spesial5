All Zelda: Ocarina of Time Dungeons, Ranked From Worst to Best

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

While every moment of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time is memorable, it’s the Nintendo 64 game’s dungeons that really stick in your memory. Each one is a tightly constructed puzzle box built around the tools you collect inside, culminating in an often-excellent boss fight. The game’s terrific Switch 2 remake doesn’t mess with that strength; it only refines some of Zelda’s best dungeons in tasteful ways.

It was a joy to revisit those dungeons during my playthrough, not just to experience some nostalgia, but to appreciate some timeless Nintendo design. Since I left the remake with some strong opinions about which ones are best in class, I’ve decided to definitively rank the eight primary dungeons in Ocarina of Time from worst to best. (I’ve left out side-dungeons like the Gerudo Training Ground and the Bottom of the Well, though those contain some killer bits of puzzle design, too.)

On paper, the Shadow Temple sounds fantastic. It’s a dungeon that fully realizes Ocarina of Time’s underlying horror by forcing Link to face off with Gibdos, Like Likes, and Dead Hands. In reality, it’s a fairly straightforward temple that revolves around a gimmick that sounds cooler than it is: a ghost ship ride. The remake misses an opportunity to make that sequence more exciting, leaving it feeling like an amusement park ride. The one saving grace here is Bongo Bongo, a bizarre boss that comes totally out of left field to make for one of Ocarina of Time’s most delightfully weird combat encounters.

Like the Shadow Temple, Jabu-Jabu’s Belly is an iconic temple that doesn’t quite hold up to its reputation. Don’t get me wrong: It’s perhaps the most creative dungeon in the entire game. Link gets swallowed by a giant fish and has to cut his way out of it by attacking its electrified uvulas. But the reality is that it’s a fairly small dungeon with an underexplored Princess Ruto escort gimmick and the least memorable boss in the entire game. It still deserves all of its flowers for being a funny, gross-out idea, though.

Dodongo Cavern is by no means a weak dungeon. It’s a fun, early-game area that gives you a lot of room to play with bombs. (That ends with you feeding them to King Dodongo, one of Zelda’s most classic boss mechanics.) It’s more that it’s outclassed by the Fire Temple later in Link’s adventure. The only way that Dodongo’s Cavern really stands out is in its central room, which has a massive skeleton looming over it. Dropping bombs in its eyes to unlock the route to King Dodongo remains a great final puzzle, as does setting off a bomb chain reaction to knock down a staircase.

Depending on who you ask, the Water Temple is either an abomination or the best Zelda dungeon. The truth is that it’s neither: It’s just a pretty good dungeon held back by some repeated backtracking. The idea of unlocking new areas in a flooded temple by changing the water level makes for a great gimmick that adds some clever spatial reasoning to the mix. The remake even makes it easier to navigate, thanks to better visual signposting and streamlined controls. Even with those changes, it’s still easy to get mixed up and wind up trekking back through routes to reset the water. The Longshot is a bit of a cop-out key item too, but the area’s pair of standout bosses in Dark Link and Morpha more than make up for that.

Even though it’s little more than an introductory dungeon, The Great Deku Tree is a perfect bit of Zelda design. The opening section, which has you climbing webs and avoiding Skulltulas, is a unique bit of vertical exploration that makes it special. Falling through the big web at the center of the dungeon to get to the bottom floor is a magical moment, too. And then there’s Gohma, a top-notch first foe that’s easier than ever to appreciate with the remake’s control tweaks. It’s a little small and the puzzles are simple, but it’s a delightful first challenge for Link.

As a dungeon that lands right near the midpoint of Ocarina of Time, the Fire Temple does a great job of (ahem) heating things up. It’s one of the more complex dungeons in the game, combining high-stakes platforming with a Goron-searching throughline that really shakes up the linear dungeon design at just the right time. It also features one of the best items in the game, the Megaton Hammer, which is especially satisfying to use in the remake. Volvagia might just be the most spectacular boss fight in the entire game too, aside from the final showdown. Top to bottom, it’s a meat and potatoes dungeon that nails all the essentials.

Key item: Silver Gauntlets, Mirror Shield

As the last dungeon in the game before Ganon’s Castle, the Spirit Temple makes for a great final exam. Its defining feature is the fact that it’s a dungeon in two parts, as Link needs to explore it as both a child and an adult to complete it. It’s the kind of ingenious idea that you would find in a game like Link’s Awakening, and it’s a little surprising that Ocarina of Time doesn’t play with that time-travel dynamic more elsewhere. That unique hook makes it feel like two dungeons in one, as you solve puzzles with both the Silver Gauntlets and the Mirror Shield. The final battle against Twinrova is a high point too — especially in the remake, which does an excellent job turning the witches into lovable foes. It’s a wonderfully creative way to send Link on his way to his journey’s end.

Zelda dungeons don’t get much better than the Forest Temple. Really dialing into Ocarina of Time’s spooky side, it’s a literal ghost house where Link has to track down and defeat four Poes. That quest sends him through twisted hallways and flipped rooms that require just enough spatial reasoning to feel gratifying without being a headache. Using Link’s arrows to shoot Poes out of picture frames, reorient rooms, and light torches makes for a strong puzzle dungeon that gives you multiple uses for your newfound item. The fact that it all ends in the memorable Phantom Ganon fight, a battle that brilliantly teases the ending encounter, makes it that much stronger. It’s a quintessential Zelda dungeon, and Ocarina of Time wouldn’t feel as magical as it does without it.
