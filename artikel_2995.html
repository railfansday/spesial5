President Donald Trump's "Big, Beautiful Bill" Dropped a $169 Billion Bombshell on Social Security

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Donald Trump’s flagship tax and spending law indirectly dug a steeper hole for America’s top retirement program.

By Sean Williams – Nov 26, 2026 at 6:36AM EST

During the first year of each of President Donald Trump's two nonconsecutive terms, he spearheaded the passage of a flagship tax and spending law. The first, the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (signed December 2017), permanently lowered the peak marginal corporate income tax rate from 35% to 21% and has been the catalyst behind record share buybacks on Wall Street.

Meanwhile, the "Big, Beautiful Bill" (signed July 2025) made the personal income tax brackets from the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act permanent, as well as introduced a bevy of temporary tax breaks for calendar years 2025 through 2028. While Trump's newest law is helping some taxpayers retain more of their income, it's also projected to drop a $169 billion bombshell on Social Security.

While these tax breaks are helping tens of millions of Americans retain more of their income, they're hindering Social Security's primary income generator, the 12.4% payroll tax.

Last year, the payroll tax accounted for over 91% of the $1.45 trillion in income collected. President Trump's Big, Beautiful Bill will narrow the pool of earned income subject to the payroll tax through 2028 -- and therein lies the problem.

Shortly after the Big, Beautiful Bill was signed into law by the president, Sen. Ron Wyden (D-OR) requested a financial outlook assessment on America's top retirement program by the Social Security Administration (SSA). In early August 2025, the SSA's Office of the Actuary (OACT) delivered a detailed 10-year projection.

According to the SSA's OACT, Donald Trump's flagship law is estimated to increase costs for the program's trust funds by $168.6 billion from 2025 through 2034.

US Old-Age and Survivors Insurance Trust Fund Assets at End of Year data by YCharts

Furthermore, the OACT projected that the Old-Age and Survivors Insurance trust fund's asset reserve depletion timeline would shift forward, from the first quarter of 2033 to the fourth quarter of 2032, because of the Big, Beautiful Bill. The 2026 Social Security Board of Trustees Report confirmed this estimated shift in its June release.

To be clear, Social Security's litany of issues and its $29.3 trillion long-term funding shortfall primarily stem from several ongoing demographic shifts. This includes a decline in net migration into the U.S., historically low birth rates, and rising income inequality, among other factors. Trump's Big, Beautiful Bill is a small piece of a very big problem.

Nevertheless, Donald Trump's flagship tax and spending law has dug an even steeper hole for Social Security, and in six years (or potentially less), retirees may pay the price.
