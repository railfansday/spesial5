Digital Product Defaults Tech Companies Should Rethink

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Below, members of Forbes Technology Council identify default settings and behaviors that tech companies should reconsider and explain why. They also share how more thoughtful choices on the part of product developers could build trust, reduce risk and improve the user experience.

Calendar products should stop treating every free slot as usable time. If the default availability exposes the whole day without setup, travel or recovery buffers, colleagues book meetings back-to-back, and the owner must absorb the collision. Few users know to configure hidden buffers, so the schedule rewards whoever books first. Build a small, visible transition gap into availability and let people shorten it when their work allows. Show clear buffers beside each slot. - Jagadish Gokavarapu, Wissen Infotech

Tech companies should rethink enabling AI-generated recommendations or automated actions by default. Users may not realize when a system moves from assisting to acting on their behalf. Products should make that distinction clear, keep consequential actions subject to explicit approval, and allow users to change those permissions easily. - Vibhor Kumar, EDB Postgres AI

Forbes Technology Council is an invitation-only community for world-class CIOs, CTOs and technology executives. Do I qualify?

Apps should not request access to contacts, location, microphones or photos before those capabilities are actually needed. Just-in-time permissions give users better context for their decisions and reduce unnecessary exposure of personal data. Convenience should not require unrestricted access. - Prashanthi Kolluru, KloudPortal Technology Solutions Pvt Ltd.

Business software should distinguish an unanswered field from a confirmed zero. In a sales forecast, zero should mean no revenue is expected, not that nobody entered an estimate. Users often leave prefilled values untouched, making incomplete forecasts look finished. Default to “not provided” so managers can see where information is missing before relying on the totals. - Nicholas Domnisch, EE Solutions

Two-factor authentication is crucial. There are still far too many companies that don’t require any other form of authentication besides username and password. Enabling two-factor authentication is one of the best ways to protect yourself from phishing and compromised credentials. I urge every company to consider shipping their product with two-factor authentication turned on. - Mark Beare, Malwarebytes

Tech companies should rethink enabling AI features by default without making data use clear. A user accepting convenience is not necessarily consenting to their data being retained or reused. Default to minimum data collection, explain the trade-off and let users choose. Responsible AI starts with the decisions we make on users’ behalf. - Helen Wang, Apex Group

Few users change defaults, so the default sets how much oversight most people get. AI features that act on their own are a good place to rethink it. For consequential actions like a cash withdrawal or a self-checkout override, start with a quick human confirmation. Once evaluations show the AI handles edge cases reliably, loosen that step and let users choose. - Andy Zosel, Diebold Nixdorf

I’d rethink the default sorting in business software. For example, when CRM users see records ordered by recent activity rather than what needs attention, urgent opportunities can disappear beneath less important updates. Defaults should prioritize the next useful action, not simply what changed last. That small design choice can shape decisions across an entire team. - Sergii Grushai, Peeklogic

Notification settings need to be rethought. Most products ship with everything on, and most users never touch the setting, so the default becomes the policy. People then mute the whole channel and miss the one alert that mattered. Default to tiered, role-based alerts where only critical items interrupt and the rest roll into a digest. In operations, the alert nobody reads is worse than no alerts at all. - Steven Singer, Arbill

Asking for location access only once, at setup, is something to rethink. People tap “Yes” to get started, and the app keeps collecting data in the background for years afterward. The company ends up holding a movement history that it never needed for every customer and that it now has to protect. Ask for location access at the moment a feature needs the user’s location and for that session only. - Kriti Faujdar, Yelp

Audit logging is a setting that’s often overlooked. Many SaaS platforms ship with minimal logging or keep logs for only days, with the detailed tier sold as an upgrade. Often, no one really notices until an incident happens, and then the evidence is simply gone. Default to logging that actually supports an investigation; you can’t reconstruct what you never recorded. Review your logging and how you audit the logging process. - Dan Sorensen, Nexus Security Advisors

One that I would question is the default implementation of all new features for current users. A feature that might seem great to a product team may end up disrupting the workflow that a user has learned over many years. Users do not change defaults until there is a problem. Companies should implement new features gradually, with explanations of why the change is good and how to go back. - Ajay Pandey

Training AI on customer content needs to be rethought. Several platforms now feed what people post into their models unless the user finds an opt-out, and most never will. When nearly everyone leaves a default in place, the default is the company’s choice, not the customer’s consent. I would make model training opt-in, explain the trade in plain language at the moment it matters, and accept a smaller dataset. - Brandon Fuchs, Balenor Pharmaceuticals Inc.

Single sign-on should be made the default option. Not only does it free up the end user’s mental capacity, but it also gives the IT department tighter control over the software. Yet, if it isn’t implemented during the initial rollout, it is seldom added later. - Kevin Korte, Univention

The “Remember this device” setting should grant temporary rather than indefinite trust. A laptop can be known by a product long after it is no longer owned or used exclusively. Let device trust be time-limited and easily inspectable. Device recognition does not imply the same person now owns the laptop. - Kavitha Thiyagarajan, Newrez LLC

For years, companies have defended their brands against online fraud by registering a huge defensive domain portfolio and setting them to auto-renew. But changes to the internet and the nature of business have made this unsustainable. A robust defensive portfolio now needs to cover tens of thousands of domains, or more. The cost, effort and risks have skyrocketed. Truly proactive protection, such as domain blocking, is now a crucial tool in the brand protection toolkit. - Tony Kirsch, Brand Safety Alliance (a GoDaddy Registry Initiative)
