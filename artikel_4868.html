Nvidia Shares Rise After Earnings Beat in First Report Since Jensen Huang's Exit

Nvidia shares climbed roughly 2% in after-hours trading Tuesday after the AI chipmaker delivered another record quarter, beating Wall Street expectations on revenue and earnings while forecasting stronger-than-expected sales for the current period.

The results offered some relief to investors after a brutal selloff that wiped hundreds of billions of dollars from Nvidia's market value following the resignation of co-founder Jensen Huang.

According to Nvidia's earnings release, revenue reached $115.8 billion for the fiscal third quarter ended October 25, up 103% from a year earlier and comfortably above analysts' expectations of approximately $109.1 billion.

Adjusted earnings per share came in at $2.68, beating the roughly $2.47 consensus estimate. Net income under generally accepted accounting principles reached $65.3 billion, more than double the $31.9 billion reported a year earlier.

Nvidia's data center division generated $107.6 billion in revenue, up 110% year over year and accounting for approximately 93% of total sales. The company also confirmed that its Vera Rubin NVL72 computing platform has begun shipping, extending the hardware roadmap that helped make Nvidia the dominant supplier of AI processors.

The company expects fourth-quarter revenue of $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, compared with Wall Street estimates of approximately $125.3 billion, per Reuters. That forecast includes an additional week in the reporting period and assumes no data center computing revenue from China.

Tuesday's report marks Nvidia's first financial results since Huang resigned November 12, ending his 33-year tenure following revelations of extramarital affairs involving company employees and a businesswoman suspected by U.S. intelligence officials of having ties to China. Those intelligence allegations remain unproven, and an independent investigation is ongoing.

To add more drama to the story, the Securities and Exchange Commission opened a civil investigation into whether Nvidia violated investor-disclosure laws.

Colette Kress, Nvidia's longtime chief financial officer and interim CEO, sought to reassure investors about the company's future. "Our momentum is real," she said in the earnings announcement, adding that the Vera Rubin rollout was off to a strong start and the product roadmap remained on track.

Huang said Tuesday that he would be honored to return as CEO if the board believed it was in Nvidia's best interests. His statement followed public support from Elon Musk and President Donald Trump, although the board is not considering reinstatement and continues searching for a permanent replacement.

Nvidia's fundamentals look considerably better than its stock chart. Despite Tuesday's rebound, shares remain roughly 35% below their October peak near $240, and the technical indicators suggest sellers still control the broader trend.

The daily chart shows immediate support around $150, close to the $148.80 low reached during last week's selloff. A decisive break below that area could expose further downside, while a sustained recovery would first need to reclaim resistance around $164.40 to $165.60.

That former support area has become resistance following the breakdown, as Decrypt's earlier technical analysis identified. Above it, the chart shows additional hurdles near $176 and between $184 and $189, making a return to the previous trading range far from automatic.

The Relative Strength Index, or RSI, is around 22.5 on the supplied daily chart, well below the conventional oversold threshold of 30. That makes a short-term rebound plausible, but oversold readings are not proof that a bottom has formed.

The Average Directional Index, or ADX, sits near 45.4, indicating a strong prevailing trend, with directional signals favoring sellers. The faster exponential moving average remains above the slower one, a positive longer-term configuration that has not prevented the recent collapse.

Tuesday's earnings beat gives bulls a potential catalyst, but the after-hours bounce has yet to reclaim the chart's first major resistance around $164.40. Kress is scheduled to lead the company's 5 p.m. Eastern earnings call, the first without Huang, as investors look for answers about Nvidia's leadership and its next phase of growth.
