Rangers Daily: Igor, Henrik … is Garand next?; Senators trade rumors

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

So, the New York Rangers go their first 100 years without a goalie goal, and now their netminders can't stop filling the net at the other end of the ice?

OK, admittedly a bit of a stretch, but there's been two goalie goals scored by the home team at Madison Square Garden in the first two months of the season. This after Hall of Famer Henrik Lundqvist shot one down into an empty net during the Rangers Alumni Classic at MSG on Sunday afternoon.

🚨 WE HAVE A HENRIK LUNDQVIST GOAL 🚨 pic.twitter.com/44jBWQvlze

Of course that goal doesn't count in the record books, but c'mon, how fun was it to see The King score, flop to the ice in "shock" after doing so, then pick himself up and skate to the bench and take part in the traditional post-goal celebration, fist-bumping his teammates?

What makes this even more fun — or funny — is that of all the amazing attributes Lundqvist had during his time as an elite NHL goaltender, and eventually becoming the winningest goalie in Rangers history, he was never a really good puck-handler nor a threat to score into an empty net.

Maybe the 44-year-old's been working on some skills since retiring.

That goal didn't make the NHL history books. But Igor Shesterkin's goal on Oct. 1 in New York's home opener against the Tampa Bay Lightning absolutely counted. Shesterkin scored the first goalie goal in Rangers history and the 21st by an NHL goaltender (including during the Stanley Cup Playoffs).

That's a bittersweet moment, considering how the absolute joy of Shesterkin scoring that goal disappeared eight days later when he tore up his knee and is now out for the season.

THE FIRST RANGERS GOALTENDER TO SCORE A GOAL:IGOR SHESTERKIN pic.twitter.com/k7ITHnvtza

But, OK, let's play along here. That's two goals by Rangers goalies in two months. Is there more goalie magic offensively on the near horizon?

If so, the bet here is on Dylan Garand to have a better chance at accomplishing the feat — or even attempting to score a goal — than Joonas Korpisalo. The 24-year-old rookie has the swagger to do so, not to mention some pretty darn good skills handling the puck. Take a good look at his outlet passes, especially during Rangers power plays, which help spring his teammates.

He's no Shesterkin handling the puck, few goalies are his equal in the League, but Garand's actually very good. And, again, he's got that confidence and moxie to try it. Just go back to last week against the Edmonton Oilers. Sure, he had a brutal turnover right on to the stick of Connor McDavid that set up an Oilers goal in the opening minutes, but Garand came right back in the second period with a pair of aggressive clears up ice which showed how confident he remained in handling the puck.

The way things are going these days, keep an eye on Garand, especially in his starts at MSG, to see if he can make it a Rangers triple play, of sorts, if the opponent's net is empty at the other end of the rink.
