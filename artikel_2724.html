Deion Sanders returns to Cincinnati with baseball memories: 'I wanted to stay'

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Former Cincinnati Reds outfielder Deion Sanders returns to Cincinnati this week as a football coach in the baseball town where he loved the team owner despite her racist reputation − and where he found his faith in religion after a suicide attempt in 1997.

Sanders, the coach at Colorado, talked about it Wednesday, Nov. 18 on the "Colorado Football Coaches Show" as his team prepares for its next game Saturday at the University of Cincinnati.

Sanders said he "loved" Cincinnati and called it "unbelievable."

"I enjoyed my stay there," Sanders said. "I wanted to stay there and play full-time baseball."

Sanders told the show's host, Mark Johnson, that he even had his attorney reach out to the Cincinnati Bengals at one point to see if he could play NFL football there, too. It didn't work out.

But just about everything else did. Sanders, a two-sport star in baseball and football, played for the Reds across four seasons in 1994, 1995, 1997 and 2001. During that time, he played in 226 games, batting .260 with 104 runs scored and 94 stolen bases.

Off the field, the memories burn brighter, including that of the owner, Marge Schott, her dog Schottzie, riding his scooter to work downtown and how he "came to know the Lord" during his time there.

Schott, the Reds' former owner, died in 2004 and sold controlling interest of the team in 1999 after previously being suspended for using racially offensive language. She once praised dictator Adolf Hitler in an interview for being "good" at the start of his rule in Germany. Others testified that she called two Black Reds outfielders, Eric Davis and Dave Parker, "million-dollar (racial slurs)."

But Sanders told Johnson he loved working for her.

"She was labeled as this, and I never saw it," said Sanders, who is Black. "We led the league in African-Americans. So how could that be?"

In 1997, when Sanders was with the Reds, he reached a low point, as he recounted in his 2024 book entitled "Elevate and Dominate." He said his first marriage was dissolving and his wife was threatening to take away his children.

He drove his black Mercedes off a cliff in a suicide attempt, according to the book. The drop was 30 to 40 feet, but he survived without a scratch. He made it a turning point after that.

"That's where I came to know the Lord," Sanders said Wednesday. "That's where I surrendered and I said, 'Lord, you go to me. I'm giving it up.' So that is dear to me, but I loved playing there, man."

Sanders also brought up a story from when he attended spring training with the Reds in Plant City, Florida. Schott had a St. Bernard dog named Schottzie that was often around the team, and one time it was playing around a pond near the team's facility.

Sanders freaked out when he saw this. As a Florida native, he knows what's in those ponds sometimes.

"Anybody from Florida knows you don't let a dog play around a pond in Florida," Sanders said. "And Ms. Schott was around there, you know, like just throwing food out, like she was feeding the fish. And they were just letting this go down. And I ran over there, like, 'Mrs. Schott, no … C'mon. You don't want to do this. This not going to end well.'"

Sanders' warning was apparently heeded. The dog didn't get eaten alive.

And now it's on to Cincinnati for Sanders.

"You loved your time there, didn't you?" Johnson asked Sanders.

This will be his first trip there as a college football coach. He's otherwise been back to Cincinnati in recent years to visit his friend Adam "Pacman" Jones, a former player with the Bengals. "Everything is different" there now, Sanders said of the downtown.

The stakes are high for him, too. His team needs to win its final two regular-season games to be eligible for a postseason bowl game. The Buffaloes are 4-6 after upsetting Houston last week, 42-32. His four-year record at Colorado is 20-27.
