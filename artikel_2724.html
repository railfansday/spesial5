In this edition: Jordan says "What's up, doc?" to a new generation, LSU and Michigan compete for the hot seat, college basketball overindulges on Feast Week, the NBA starts strong, and more.

Michael Jordan wore this Jordan 11 colorway in "Space Jam." 30 years later, it's still an icon, releasing to customers once again this month. (Nike)

Just when you think you've heard every story about Michael Jordan's competitiveness, a new one emerges — like the time he broke "Space Jam" director Joe Pytka's nose playing basketball on set.

What's up, doc? That anecdote comes from episode two of "Space Jam: Beyond the Jam," a six-part, behind-the-scenes documentary from Jordan TV, Jordan Brand's new home for premium storytelling. It's one piece of a strategy to revitalize Jordan's rich cultural legacy for new generations.

Happy anniversary: 30 years ago this month, "Space Jam" hit theaters worldwide, grossing $231 million globally and $91 million in the U.S. against an $80 million reported budget. The movie captured lightning in a bottle, harnessing Jordan's global notoriety, his return to hoops, and the Looney Tunes' cross-generational appeal.

Pass the torch: Jordan's star power has resonated across decades, and his legacy is eternal. However, among today's younger fans, the moments that transformed him into an immortal and his brand's products into cultural icons are growing fuzzier. The "Beyond the Jam" series is the type of vehicle that can recast them in vivid focus.

With contributions from today's stars like Azzi Fudd and Jayson Tatum, the series builds a bridge between the children who grew up loving "Space Jam" (and Jordan) and those who haven't built the same connection. The documentary's release coincides with a "Greatness is never out of reach" campaign and a "Space Jam" collection.

"When we think about how we bring these products to this generation, we are really trying to make sure that we're creating core memories — the same core memories you and I had growing up watching this film," Jordan Brand CMO Caitlin Sargent told Yahoo Sports.

Restoration project: These storytelling efforts come amid a brand reset at Jordan. At Nike's recent investor day, management said it expects the brand's revenue to fall slightly from fiscal 2027 to 2031, with modest growth beginning in fiscal 2029.

Jordan Brand is reducing supply to restore a premium feel and distinction to its most iconic franchises, while balancing the portfolio with new products. For those icons, that means both reduced availability and reimagined versions.

"The main piece is making sure that each one is brought to market with the same level of thought, obsession, craft, and storytelling as the originals," Sargent explained, emphasizing the importance of elevating to the standard that Jordan himself holds the brand to.

Game on: The task ahead may not be as foreboding as the Monstars once were, but both the Tune Squad and Jordan Brand share a common trump card in His Airness. The brand is finding new ways to play it.

After the season's first month, the NBA is averaging a record 2.62 million viewers in its national broadcasts on ABC, ESPN, NBC, and Prime, up 37% from last season.

Boots on the ground: Local broadcasts are also performing well, delivering 24% viewership growth. However, several teams switched to over-the-air broadcasting arrangements after Main Street Sports collapsed, and the cable-to-OTA transition generally boosts viewership.

Bonus big number: The NBA announced it's raising $11 billion to build and renovate arenas and surrounding districts in Europe, with participation from Apollo Sports Capital, Ares, Arctos, Blackstone, HPS, J.P. Morgan, Sixth Street, and Stonepeak. Coming fall 2027: NBA Europe.

MLB's postseason player compensation pool totaled $114.5 million this season, down 11% from 2025. For the champion Brewers, players earning a full share received $493,044 each.

Less seats, less money: The pool is funded by gate receipts from the games guaranteed to be played in each round. Dodger Stadium has the league's largest capacity at 56,000, with the team's World Series runs fueling bigger pools in 2024 and 2025, and its absence weighing on 2026.

NWSL regular season viewership fell 19% in 2026 to an average of 173,000 viewers across ABC, ESPN, ESPN2, CBS, and Ion, per SBJ.

Silver lining: Audiences tuned in when it mattered, with average playoff viewership up 13% to 494,000 through the semifinal round. However, the 1.12 million championship viewers fell just short of last year's record 1.18 million.

Too often in modern college sports, off-field controversies overshadow the joys of on-field competition. This week's events elevate the messiness to new levels of embarrassment.

Bayou battle: When Will Wade played RJ Luis, signer of multiple pro contracts, last week, he may not have expected that he was sealing LSU's expulsion from the SEC. Believe it or not, expulsion is no longer a far-fetched possibility; the league's presidents will meet December 3 to decide LSU's fate.

The court documents paint a messy picture of the brewing battle between LSU and the SEC — a more than 3-month-old, contentious sparring match that has even involved the Louisiana governor and attorneys general.

But are the threats of expulsion real? Would the SEC truly kick out of the conference one of the longest-standing members and biggest brands?

If you speak to those within the conference, that answer is yes. In fact, those in the SEC — and even some at LSU — believe that league presidents have the necessary 11 votes (of the 16) to dismiss the university.

It would take a special disaster-class to displace LSU from the college sports hot seat. Hail to Michigan, the hot seat's conquering heroes.

Costly course reversal: Michigan's AD search, thought to be complete with the expected hiring of Trev Alberts, began anew after Michigan reversed course, potentially risking a $17 million buyout for Alberts' zero days on the job (legal dispute incoming?!).

So let's make sure we have this straight.

The University of Michigan, which has known since late July that outgoing athletic director Warde Manuel's last day would be Dec. 31, rushed to hire Trev Alberts as its new athletic director last week. The decision was made by incoming school president C. Cybele Raver, who hadn't even officially started yet but sped up the AD search so that Michigan could ostensibly fire football coach Kyle Whittingham and run a coaching search as quickly as possible.

Then, upon agreeing to terms with Alberts, who resigned from his job at Texas A&M, Michigan learned through media reporting about Alberts' somewhat questionable comments from a deposition related to a human-resources issue two jobs ago at Nebraska. Because those comments were going to draw negative attention to Alberts, and Raver was going to have to answer some uncomfortable questions of her own to start her tenure, she decided to pull the plug on the whole deal and start over.

As America prepares for hearty helpings of turkey and football this week, college basketball is gorging on neutral-site festivities, cramming the table with offerings of varying desirability.

Feast or gluttony? Feast Week features over a dozen tournaments and countless standalone neutral-site matchups (ex., Duke vs. UConn), with hundreds of games packing the schedule. Wednesday and Thursday alone will offer viewers about 24 hours' worth of Top-25 programming.

Take the show on the road: An enticing concoction of money, exposure, and — to a lesser extent — resume strength has lured teams away from home over the holiday, with the 2024 introduction of the Players Era Championship supercharging the financial element.

Guaranteed fees: Top draws can earn significant participation fees from tournament promoters, providing meaningful dollars in an era of budgetary distress.

NIL opportunities: In some cases, these fees are earmarked for NIL opportunities for players. For example, Players Era agreed to provide Kansas $2.75 million in NIL money for its participation, per Sportico.

Resume boost: Strong neutral performances are treated more favorably than home games in NET rankings. Plus, opponents ranked in the top 50 count as Quad 1 games at a neutral site, versus only the top 30 at home.

Can the math keep mathing? The sustainability of heavy payouts is uncertain, particularly given sluggish attendance. Players Era has inked a media partnership with ESPN and landed a Big 12 equity investment, while also cultivating a roster of corporate sponsors, declaring that the 2025 iteration self-funded its operations. But skepticism persists, and long term, only audience interest will ensure the feast continues.

Food for thought: Will America greet Feast Week with the enthusiasm fit for a freshly plated dinner or the begrudging acceptance of a room-temp third helping?
