Kalshi takes down controversial Trump 'mention' markets at CFTC's request

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Prediction market platform Kalshi quietly paused trading in a handful of "mention" contracts tied to President Donald Trump at the end of last week at the request of the CFTC, the company confirmed on Tuesday.

Mention markets are made up of contracts that ask traders what specific words will be used in a speech, a corporate earnings call or during a television broadcast.  Earlier this year, a White House teleprompter operator used inside information to place trades in mention markets related to the president's comments.

Markets paused include those asking traders what nicknames Trump will say before 2027, what the president will say in November and what countries and companies Trump will say before the end of the month.

The move comes after the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, the federal agency overseeing prediction markets, said in a letter to exchanges in September that mentions contracts have a greater manipulation risk.

A spokesperson for the agency told CNBC that the commission has placed holds on the certification of some of Kalshi's proposed mention markets. The spokesperson cited a Kalshi contract on hold that asked what key words would Trump say before a certain date. The contract was originally self-certified by Kalshi in February 2025.

"The CFTC stayed the certification of these contracts because they may be susceptible to manipulation, which is consistent with the staff advisory the CFTC put out two months ago," said Brooke Nethercott, the agency's spokesperson.

In a statement, Kalshi spokesperson Dani Lever said Kalshi is complying with the order.

In August, reports emerged that the CFTC was conducting an internal review into mention markets. The agency also requested that Kalshi, which is the only U.S.-regulated exchange that features the contract type, take down its sports-related mention markets in response to the inquiry. Kalshi complied.

A Trump-related mention market drew scrutiny in July after it was revealed a White House teleprompter operator placed trades on markets related to the president's comments. In a settlement with the operator, the CFTC ordered a three-year trading ban on the individual and a penalty amounting to over $172,000.

Kalshi has defended mentions markets even as the CFTC has become increasingly skeptical of them, saying that the individual words of prominent people often cause billions of dollars of movement across financial markets.

In addition to scrutiny regarding mention markets, the company is also facing a probe from the CFTC about its communications about new products with the agency, though a spokesperson for the company told CNBC that it was working to resolve the issues.

As of Tuesday morning, a mention market asking what Vice President JD Vance would say during the New York Times DealBook Summit event on Wednesday is still active.

Disclosure: CNBC and Kalshi have a commercial relationship that includes customer acquisition and a minority investment.
