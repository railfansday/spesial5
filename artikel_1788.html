OLIVER HOLT: Man United ARE in a relegation battle - anybody who denies it is deluding themselves - and Mauricio Pochettino is already showing signs of cracking under pressure

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Published: 07:54 EST, 23 November 2026 | Updated: 10:31 EST, 23 November 2026

Early on in On the Road, somewhere between Chicago and Denver, Jack Kerouac described the journey his main character was taking and some of the trepidation that came with each new horizon. ‘I was halfway across America,’ he wrote, ‘at the dividing line between the East of my youth and the West of my future.’

Late on Sunday afternoon, as winter’s grey light turned to darkness on Merseyside and a bitter wind blew in off the Irish Sea, Manchester United’s new boss, Mauricio Pochettino, freshly arrived from Cincinnati, Ohio, arms folded grimly and tightly across his chest, stood on the touchline at Anfield and realised the full extent of what he had just got himself into.

Sure, the United job was too big to turn down. However far the club has fallen, it still has that lure. It still has the name, even if the name is now just one of those building facades on a film set.

There is the name and then there is the nothingness that is concealed beyond the name. There is the mismanaged, mistreated void that United has become.

And now United need to find out which version of Pochettino they have purchased. Because he is at a dividing line, too. Do they have the Pochettino who blazed a trail across the football landscape with Espanyol, Southampton and Spurs, although he did not win trophies at any of those destinations?

Or do they have the Pochettino whose reputation gradually became becalmed at Paris Saint-Germain, Chelsea and the USA national team? They better hope they can restart something in him because this is the biggest and the hardest job Pochettino has ever had and it will take a Herculean effort just to survive.

Do Man United have the Mauricio Pochettino who blazed a trail with Espanyol, Southampton and Spurs? Or do they have the Pochettino whose reputation has fluctuated since?

That is what this season is about now for these fallen, ruined giants. It is about survival. Nothing more. If Pochettino did not realise it before, he will have realised it during that calamitous 4-0 defeat at Anfield: United are in a relegation battle and anyone who denies that is deluding themselves.

That is just pride talking. The remnants of what pride remains at United, anyway.

Maybe it hasn’t quite seemed real until now. Just like the prospect of Leicester City winning the Premier League didn’t start to feel real until around Christmas of 2015-16. United going down would be up there with Leicester winning the title in terms of seismic events in the modern English game. It’s time to start taking relegation seriously.

If you take the blinkers off for just a second, if you banish the muscle memory that tells you United are too successful and too rich to get sucked down into the Championship, then you have to accept that all the ingredients are there for United to get relegated. Take that name away and what’s left?

What’s left is a team that last won a Premier League game on August 30. What’s left is a team that has won one game all season. What’s left is a team that has conceded 34 goals across all competitions, more than any other team in Europe's top five leagues. What’s left is a team that has eight points from its first 11 league games.

Just for context, the Spurs side that spent most of last season haunted by the prospect of relegation, had 18 points at this stage of that campaign. If you still think the rabble currently representing the club are too good to go down, I think you are deluding yourself.

Yes, United played more than 80 minutes of the game against Liverpool with 10 men and, yes, the decision to send off Leny Yoro for his challenge on Alexander Isak was harsh. But the dismissal was also a fig-leaf for Pochettino and United. It was convenient. It was a disguise for what lay beneath.

United also brought it on themselves. Pochettino made one big selection decision before the game when he picked Matthijs de Ligt ahead of Harry Maguire. It was what former bosses in the English game might have described as a ‘big rick’. It was a terrible misjudgment.

Maybe the sending off of Leny Yoro was harsh. But United brought it on themselves

Matthijs de Ligt (right) was bullied by Alexander Isak at Anfield. Pochettino's decision to play the Dutchman was a terrible misjudgment

De Ligt’s first action was to watch a long ball from Ronald Araujo sail over his head, gazing at it with a mix of horror and wonder, as you might if you were watching the trajectory of an alien spaceship. Yoro was not quick enough to read De Ligt’s discomfort and so the horror ensued.

It was Pochettino’s first game and it is impossible to judge him yet but it is possible to say this was a bad start.

There were even a couple of worrying signs in his post-match press conference when the bonhomie and the relaxed demeanour that are among his great strengths deserted him.

When he was asked about United being in the relegation zone, he snapped that it was a ‘cheap’ question because he had only just arrived at the club. He said he saw fight in his players, which few others had seen. He blamed everything on the sending off of Yoro, even though it was his selection of De Ligt that had led to it.

A few alarm bells started to ring. Only faintly and only in the distance but they were ringing nonetheless. Pochettino has had big jobs before but he has never had a job as big as Manchester United where the scrutiny and the expectation and the judgment is relentless and unforgiving.

Pochettino has dealt with Daniel Levy at Spurs, which may not have been an easy relationship, but he has never dealt with a clown-show like the one that Jim Ratcliffe has put in place at Old Trafford.

United is rotten. Everyone can see that. Off the pitch, the club is at war with its own fans. The club gives every impression of trying to move on its most loyal supporters so that it can replace them with tourists and day-trippers who will spend more on tickets and in the club superstore and at the food stands.

‘The way you treat us is a disgrace,’ that banner in the Stretford End said a few weeks ago, and that is how the fans feel. They are more loyal than United deserve but their patience with their owners ran out a long time ago. Pochettino has a disaffected fanbase to deal with as well.

United is rotten on the pitch and it is rotten off the pitch. It feels as if the club is being slowly poisoned in plain sight. It feels as if it is getting weaker and weaker and weaker. And when Brentford arrive at Old Trafford next week, they will smell blood. Every club that comes to Old Trafford now smells blood.

Harry Maguire looks dejected at full-time on Sunday. United is rotten on the pitch and it is rotten off the pitch. It feels as if the club is being slowly poisoned in plain sight

Still think they’re too good to go down? Who are they better than in this league? Hull City? United don’t have the spirit of Hull City. They don’t have the togetherness. They don’t have the pride.

Hull got battered by Brighton on Sunday afternoon and still won 2-0. United could never have done that. United have already lost to Hull and Brighton this season, too.

So, yes, United are in a relegation fight. They’re one off the bottom of the league in the last week of November. Of course, they’re in a relegation fight. It’s on their new boss to get them out of it.

If Pochettino is on the wrong side of the dividing line of his career, if the best part of his journey is behind him, United are in big, big trouble.
