Steelers release Aaron Rodgers following criticism of Mike McCarthy: Possible landing spots for QB

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

Aaron Rodgers' time with the Pittsburgh Steelers has ended after he heavily criticized Mike McCarthy hours after Pittsburgh made him inactive for Friday's game against the Broncos. Shortly after his criticism, the Steelers announced that they had released the quarterback, and he officially hit the waiver wire Wednesday afternoon.

Any team can sign Rodgers until 4 p.m. ET on Thursday. If no team claims him by then, he will become a free agent.

Rodgers, 42, had re-signed with the Steelers in hopes of finishing his career with a successful reunion with McCarthy, his coach with the Packers from 2006-18. Instead, Rodgers will look to finish his career elsewhere.

It appears that Rodgers' breakup with Pittsburgh was the result of a disagreement between Rodgers and McCarthy over play-calling duties. The Steelers had been running more hurry-up offense in recent weeks. In that situation, the play-calling duties are more split between Rodgers and quarterbacks coach Tom Arth. McCarthy clearly wanted a more collaborative approach.

Rodgers said that his desire to handle more of the play-calling duties was a byproduct of, in his words, McCarthy being late and sleeping through team meetings. Rodgers said that McCarthy said, "1-2-3, Packers!" at halftime during Pittsburgh's Week 11 loss to the Eagles. Rodgers said that was essentially the last straw and decided to address his concerns with McCarthy this week.

Those discussions led to the end of Rodgers' time with the Steelers, who will face the Broncos on Friday with Mason Rudolph under center.

While the Steelers move ahead, Rodgers will now try to figure out his next move. Here are five possible landing spots for Rodgers, who did not say whether he still plans to retire after this season, given what has transpired over the last 24 hours.

At this point, there are only two realistic possibilities for Rodgers if he wants to be a starter. The best option of the two is New England, who recently lost Drake Maye to a potentially season-ending injury.

The Patriots have lost three out of their last four games, but they still have a decent shot at making the playoffs, especially if the Steelers fall off without Rodgers. New England has a chance to get back over .500 this week if they can defeat the 1-9 Chargers in Los Angeles.

Would Rodgers make much of a difference? He would be an upgrade over Tommy DeVito, but the odds of the Patriots actually defending their AFC title are slim either way. The Patriots have struggled on both sides of the ball this year and have four games left against teams with winning records.

Baltimore is in a total freefall. After a 2-0 start, the Ravens have lost seven straight games and are sitting by themselves in the AFC North cellar. Lamar Jackson's season-ending injury essentially ended Baltimore's season.

The Ravens recently acquired Jacoby Brissett, who played relatively well in close losses to the Chargers and Texans. There's a chance, however, that the Ravens may decide to add Rodgers to their quarterback room to see if he can provide a further spark.

Baltimore would also gain an advantage from an intel standpoint if they signed Rodgers. The Ravens have two upcoming games against Pittsburgh, their longtime rival.

This is mostly a nostalgia move. Rodgers is a California native who grew up a diehard 49ers fan. The 49ers famously passed on him in the 2005 NFL Draft, and Rodgers has never been shy about his desire to show his childhood team they made a big mistake.

In this scenario, Rodgers would have the chance to go home and play for his childhood team, albeit in a backup role. He also could serve as a mentor for Brock Purdy, who is having an MVP-caliber season. Given the 49ers' injury history, Kyle Shanahan probably wouldn't mind having a backup with Rodgers' experience.

Another nostalgia move that would be largely ceremonial. While his exit from Green Bay wasn't perfect, Rodgers is currently on good terms with both Matt LaFleur and Jordan Love. Unlike his mentor, Brett Favre, Rodgers could close out his career with the team that shaped his NFL legacy.

It wouldn't be the perfect ending, but a return to Green Bay — even as a backup — would give Rodgers some semblance of closure to what has been a Hall of Fame career.

Rodgers can also retire, which may or may not come on his terms.

If he goes unclaimed and no one signs him this season, Rodgers would have to wait to see if another team comes calling this offseason, which might not happen. It's very possible that Rodgers wouldn't have played this season if the Steelers didn't offer him a chance to return.

Rodgers wouldn't be the first professional athlete to retire that way. In fact, there are a host of great players across numerous sports who have had their careers end in a similar fashion, albeit without the scorched-earth interview. Players seldom get the John Elway retirement where they get to ride off into the sunset with a championship. More often than not, players are told when the ride is over. That can happen to Rodgers, or he can choose to shape the narrative by calling it quits on his own terms.
