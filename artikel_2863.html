The real season begins for Army football as Air Force visits West Point

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Three days in the fall - a season unto itself.

Talk about the College Football Playoff, the increasingly irrelevant conference campaigns and the soon-to-be fractured bowl season all you want. The one apparent constant in the ever-changing world of college football has been, is now and, hopefully, shall be the Commander-in-Chief's Trophy series.

Contested annually since 1972 - with roots dating all the way back to 1890 - the nation's three service academies have waged battle on the fields of friendly strife, American patriotism and tradition wrapped in gridiron glory. To some, it is considered football in its purest form.

"Obviously, a big week here at West Point, getting ready for Air Force Academy rivalry games; they're like no other,'' said Army head coach Jeff Monken.

At 6-2, Air Force is having somewhat of a revival year following consecutive losing seasons for the first time since 2017-18. The Falcons beat Connecticut last week, making them bowl eligible. Army heads in at 5-3, still stinging from a resounding 49-22 loss at Memphis; the Black Knights need just one win vs. Air Force, East Carolina or Rice in order to become bowl eligible. With each school at two league losses, their conference title hopes are practically dashed, but the CIC has become the most important goal for each of the three academies.

Air Force got a leg up with a 14-9 win over Navy on Oct. 3 - those two schools are always paired up first, followed by the Army-Air Force matchup and culminating with the Army-Navy game in December. The school that wins two contests claims the CIC Trophy; in case of a three-way tie, the school that last won keeps the trophy for another year. Navy currently has the prize, but the Falcons can claim it for the first time since 2022 with a win on Saturday evening. Army needs to sweep to bring it back to West Point for the first time since 2023.

Army is a six-point favorite, per CBS Sportsline.

This decade, the CIC games have generally been close-fought, low-scoring affairs: Of the 19 games contested during the 2020s, ten have been decided by one score and 15 have not yielded more than three touchdowns by either club. Six of the last eight Army-Air Force games have been decided by one score.

"These games are so closely contested, just year after year,'' Monken said. "It just seems like it comes down to a final drive and it's just an absolute battle and dog fight every single season.''

Another similarity has all three academy teams focused on being run-oriented - in fact, Army (342.5 yards per game), Air Force (341) and Navy (295.8) are ranked 1-2-3 in the nation's rushing stats. What's remarkable is Air Force is doing this despite losing all-star starting quarterback Liam Szarka to season-ending leg injury in the Navy game, plus two more at the position, putting the ball in the hands of senior Kemper Hodges and freshman Howard Fisher (four games). Hodges was converted from fullback to quarterback last week and ran for 200 yards and four touchdowns.

"They're well-coached,'' Monken said of the Falcons, led by Troy Calhoun for the past 20 seasons. They're having a really good year and they've been impressive. Their defense continues to be really tough. Their run game is as strong as anybody's in the country, and it's going to be a great test for our football team.''

In a series dating back to 1959, Army has never beaten Air Force four times in a row. Prior to the current three-game winning streak, Army posted three wins in a row from 1976-78. The Black Knights beat the Falcons in 2017 and 2018 only to lose in 2019 in Colorado, and again in 2020 and 2021 only to lose in 2022 in Arlington, Texas.

Air Force has dominated the series, 38-21-1, bolstered by a seven-game win streak between 1989-95; eight wins in a row from 1997-2004; six wins from 2006-11; and, four wins from 2013-16, the latter encompassing Monken's first three seasons. Army is 7-5 vs. Air Force under Monken.
