Value of Things: In the rearview mirror

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

We come to a very special Friday edition of Value of Things as the Texans won a boring 26-3 game on Thursday night football. Boring is good in this case. It was boring aesthetically and boring statistically. It was boring in every conceivable way. but it was the kind of boring that DeMeco Ryans imagined them being before the year started.

Obviously, people will want to big picture this thing and they will try to read into it what they will. We've seen this movie before. They did this to the Titans and Chargers. Those were two crappy teams that have only found one win on the season a piece. A part of you is thankful it wasn't against you and also thankful that you didn't allow them to stick around in the end. The other part of you realizes that there is virtually nothing that you can take from those games and transport it to any other game.

As we like to do in wins, we will look at the basic numbers from the game to see if we can see any trends building in those numbers. We will also look at the great, good, and bad from the game to see who gets game balls and what we can't ignore in victory what we would be screaming about in defeat. As per usual, we will also have our own C.J. Stroud section.

This was a thorough beatdown and I suppose we can take some solace in that. From a statistical standpoint, this is one of those get right games on both sides of the ball. It is one of those games that will getting you looking at the aggregate and saying, "see, it's not so bad." The defensive numbers will be elevated to average and the offensive numbers will end up looking better than we think. Sometimes the aggregate can fool you.

The Tennessee Titans game will probably go down as the best defensive effort of the season. There are so few shutouts in the NFL, so that one feels special even if the numbers might not be as good as they were here. I'm not sure what to make of Daniel Jones. He has moments where he looks more than competent. He uses his legs and you look up and he has turned in an impressive performance. Most of the time it looks like this. Granted, he doesn't have any receivers to speak of and if you bottle up Jonathan Taylor he gets really exposed. I suppose when you add it all up, he looks like a backup quarterback.

We've seen these performances from Davis Mills too. There are days when you think, "if I squint hard enough and let my imagination run wild then Mills looks like a starting quarterback." Then, something happens and you are reminded why Mills is a backup quarterback. Jones is effectively the same guy, but because of his draft pedigree he has managed to fool a couple of teams into believing he can be the guy. Be that as it may, the Texans did not give Jones or the Colts any hope in this one and that is what should have happened. Sometimes the great is simply doing what is expected. We have reached this portion of the season.

Maybe my eyes were playing tricks on me, but I thought I saw a Texans running back gain more than 100 yards in a football game. It was a Thursday night game, so maybe I dozed off and dreamt that. In all seriousness, this is what the Texans envisioned when they traded for David Montgomery. This was the game they had in mind. If you add in a healthy Woody Marks in the next game (hopefully) then you might actually have something. Of course, some of that might be a mirage given your opponent. The Colts did trade away a number of good players and some of them were on the defensive line.

The Texans again leave a football game surrendering only one sack. We have to reiterate that there is a difference between pressures and sacks. You can almost imagine Nick Caley as "well…actually" guy bringing up sack and turnover numbers to justify his existence. I said what I said last time and that opinion has not changed. Let's see what happens when the Texans face a team that is actually trying to win football games.

There was nothing truly ugly in this game because it was 26-3. When you thoroughly beat down an opponent you could argue that there is nothing really that bad. I would agree with that sentiment as well. I suppose this is a catch all for those few plays where the Texans seem to lose focus. We will get to Stroud in a minute, but defensively they are still giving up explosive plays. There is still the occasional blown assignment and you were just lucky to be facing a team that couldn't exploit those blown assignments. You might say that we are expecting perfection and that would be a fair reply, but when you are a 4-6 football team you put yourself in a position to need perfection.

This will go down as a statistically brilliant game for Stroud. He went 21 for 27 with 194 yards, three touchdowns, and the one pick. He did throw one other ball that should have been picked and almost was. The one interception he did throw seemed random and could be written off as bad luck, but it was a case of him trying to thread a needle and failing. So, this was not a great effort from Stroud in terms of how everything looked. The official count has him with a 118.2 rating. We will just say that this is one of those games that averages everything out. There are games where his rating is lower than what it probably should be for one reason or another.

The next step in the Stroud evolution is having a sixth sense for what the game calls for. There are games that call for him to be a gun slinger because he is all the Texans have. Then there are games where the defense does what it is supposed to do and the running game shows up. He started off taking too many chances, but settled in as the game went on. The sooner that Stroud can recognize the version he is supposed to be the better he is going to be. Granted, we are talking about the difference between being a top ten and top five quarterback. We are fortunate to be at that point in the Stroud experience.
