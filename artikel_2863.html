What we learned as Warriors' 3-point struggles continue in loss to Mavericks

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

What we learned as Warriors' 3-point struggles continue in loss to Mavericks originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

Not all blowout losses are the same, but it can feel that way when they keep coming.

That's where the Warriors are Wednesday night after a 116-94 loss to the Dallas Mavericks extended their losing streak to four games by an average margin of 22 points.

Golden State took a 4-3 lead 81 seconds into the game, fell behind 19 seconds later and never led again at American Airlines Center.

Yaxel Lendeborg and Brandin Podziemski, the team's No. 2 and No. 3 scorers behind sidelined Stephen Curry, combined for 23 points on 11-of-31 shooting from the field, including 0 of 8 from beyond the arc.

Will Richard came off the bench to lead Golden State scorers with 20 points, adding four steals and three assists. The latest starting lineup – LJ Cryer, Draymond Green, Charles Bassey, Podziemski and Lendeborg – managed only four 3-point makes.

Here are three observations from a game in which the effort showed some improvement, but the result was the same:

Losing Curry was bound to damage the Warriors' offense, as it always does, but his absence is no excuse for watching the defense turn soft as hospital cotton.

The Mavericks in the first quarter shot 57.1 percent, including 50 percent from distance. They bumped it up to 59.1 and 57.1 in the quarter, finishing at 51.6 and 41.5.

This follows the New Orleans Pelicans shooting 52 percent from field, including 50 percent from distance to take charge in the first quarter. Which followed the San Antonio Spurs posting 62.5/55.6 splits In the first quarter of a rout. Which followed the Houston Rockets going 55.6/60 to crush a fourth-quarter comeback.

Golden State's offense has plummeted from a 122.2 rating (No. 1 in the NBA) during the eight-game win streak to 98.0 (worst in the league) in three games before Wednesday.

But defense – which is supposed to be the most controllable component of the game – has gone from a 105.8 rating (fourth in the league, second in the West) during the win streak to 120.1 (23rd) since going on the road.

The Warriors often look like zombies on defense and Green, the anchor on that end, looks exasperated.

After being blown off the floor in the first 12 minutes of their last three games, coach Steve Kerr pleaded for more "juice" at the start of game.

He got just enough for the Warriors to avoid instant humiliation.

After scoring first, on a Podziemski midrange jumper 17 seconds after tipoff, Dallas responded by blistering Golden State's defense with triples to build a 16-point lead (30-14) that brought back ugly memories.

The Warriors, however, didn't sag, closing the quarter with an 11-5 run that trimmed the deficit to 10 (35-25) at the buzzer. Though their offense was mediocre and their defense was deficient, the energy was constant. Some of the "juice" came from Bassey's activity in the paint in his second start this season and first since Nov. 1.

Unlike the last two games, during which the Warriors were buried before halftime, the effort was enough to keep them competitive.

Each team's budding star was featured, as their performances were bound to be a huge factor the outcome.

The Warriors didn't get nearly as much of a boost from Lendeborg as the Mavericks received from Cooper Flagg.

Lendeborg, the rookie who turned 24 last month, finished with 13 points on 6-of-18 shooting from the field, including 0 of 3 from beyond the arc. He added five rebounds and five assists to finish minus-25 over 30 minutes.

Flagg, in his second season but still a month away from turning 19, blasted out of his recent slump – he averaged 15.7 points, on 34.8-percent shooting over the previous three games – with 33 points on 11-of-15-shooting from the field, including 3 of 5 from deep. He played 34 minutes and finished plus-32.

These two will see plenty of each other in the coming years, and each is destined to have a huge impact on the fortunes of their team.
