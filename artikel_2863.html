Is Hardik Pandya the missing link for PBKS? Can he help PBKS finally win an IPL title

Cricket News explores: Does Hardik boost PBKS' chances of winning a title in IPL 2027?

Punjab Kings have made a major splash ahead of IPL 2027, with Hardik Pandya joining the franchise from Mumbai Indians in a trade deal that sees fast bowler Arshdeep Singh moving to MI.

The two franchises have agreed to the proposed player swap, and the deal has received formal ratification from the BCCI and IPL governing council. Hardik is not expected to captain the franchise, as Shreyas Iyer is already fixed for the role under head coach Ricky Ponting.

The big trade move could be translated as an attempt by PBKS to find the missing ingredient in their pursuit of a maiden IPL title. The franchise has reached the final twice, in 2014 (as Kings XI Punjab) and 2025, but lost to Kolkata Knight Riders and Royal Challengers Bengaluru, respectively.

Hardik, meanwhile, has won five IPL titles, including four with MI and one as Gujarat Titans captain in 2022. He has also been part of India's successful 2024 and 2026 T20 World Cup campaigns.

However, his second stint at MI was far from successful, with inconsistent performances, reported dressing-room concerns and recurring injuries. His arrival could strengthen Punjab, but there are questions to address before jumping to whether he can help the franchise finally lift the trophy.

Hardik's arrival would add another dimension to an already well-stocked all-rounder department:

No. 3 T20I all-rounder Azmatullah Omarzai.

The issue? Most of their established all-round options are overseas players. Hardik would offer a high-quality upgrade as an Indian middle-order batter and seam-bowling option.

His batting position is the first question. PBKS' top four were productive in 2026, with Prabhsimran Singh scoring 510 runs, Shreyas Iyer making 498, Cooper Connolly accumulating 491 and Priyansh Arya contributing 364 at a strike rate of 211.6.

The middle order was less consistent, making No. 5 the most logical position for Hardik, with the freedom to move down to No. 6 depending on the situation. His previous association with Ponting at MI could also help the coach get the best out of him.

Stoinis will present a potential selection dilemma if he remains in the squad, given the similarities in their batting roles. Hardik bowled more regularly than Stoinis in IPL 2026, although his economy rate of 11.43 was hardly reassuring.

But, even then, Hardik's value as a bowler and batsman has never been in question, if he's fit.

Arshdeep's departure will leave a major hole in their bowling attack. The Indian left-arm pacer was PBKS' leading wicket-taker in 2026 with 14 scalps and offered an established powerplay and death-over option.

Sportstar, which broke the news of the Hardik-Arshdeep trade, also mentioned that Punjab are in touch with Lucknow Super Giants pacer Mohsin Khan in a possible exchange involving Stoinis. Mohsin could help address the left-arm pace shortage, although his own injury record is a concern.

In the 2026 season, PBKS' collapse was remarkable. They won six of their first seven matches but suffered a five-match losing streak and finished fifth with 15 points.

Hardik will have a huge responsibility to fix the middle order, which came under heavy scrutiny last year.

Hardik's return to MI via an all-cash deal in 2024 was expected to be a major chapter in his IPL career.

One of his demands may have been the captaincy, as he took over the big role from Rohit Sharma that season in a difficult transition. It was met with criticism from fans (he was booed at the Wankhede) and reported issues within the dressing room.

Mumbai finished last in 2024, winning just four of 14 matches. Hardik scored 216 runs at an average of 18 and took 11 wickets at an economy rate of 10.75.

His individual performances improved in 2025 as MI reached the playoffs. He scored 224 runs at a strike rate of 163.50 and claimed 14 wickets, although Punjab eliminated them in Qualifier 2.

The 2026 campaign was disappointing again, with Mumbai finishing ninth and Hardik managing 206 runs and just four wickets.

A move to PBKS could offer a fresh start, particularly because he would not carry the responsibility of replacing an established captain. With Shreyas leading the side and Ponting handling the coaching, Hardik could concentrate on rediscovering his best all-round form.

Hardik has already demonstrated that he possesses the temperament and ability to deliver on the biggest occasions. His success with Mumbai and Gujarat, along with India's consecutive T20 World Cup wins, makes his championship credentials difficult to question.

He played only 10 of MI's 14 matches in 2026, missed three games with back spasms, and subsequently suffered further setbacks during rehabilitation in May. Since then, he has not returned to competitive cricket.

For PBKS, the challenge is to consistently get the best version of Hardik on the field rather than be satisfied with his signature making the team look good on paper.

They need his middle-order power-hitting and bowling contributions. More importantly, Hardik's skillset and experience make him an X-Factor - something the franchise needs to finally help them win their first-ever silverware.
