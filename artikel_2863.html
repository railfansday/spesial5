Which NFL trade do you wish the Lions had made?

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The NFL trade deadline has come and gone, and the Detroit Lions made just a modest move. They sent a 2029 sixth-round pick for depth safety Andre Cisco. While Cisco had been taken out of the starting lineup for the New York Jets—who are sporting a solid duo of Dane Belton and Mikah Fitzpatrick at the position—he comes to Detroit with plenty of playing experience and a knowledge of their system, having played under Aaron Glenn. It appears Cisco will have a role quickly with the team.

"We would like to get him going ASAP," Lions coach Dan Campbell said Wednesday. "We'd like to get him going. He's obviously going out to Germany with them. Our plan is to get him going in this game and let him help us a little bit. How much? That's not quite there yet, but we do have a plan for him to help us."

For many fans, that move wasn't enough to move the needle for the Lions. They were hoping for a bigger splash, and potentially some help at the linebacker and cornerback positions. So today's Question of the Day is:

My answer: First, let me explain why I asked the question this way.

Many people are mad that the Lions didn't go out and get a player like Riq Woolen, Zack Baun, or Donte Jackson. But it's hard to get mad at a move no one made, because we don't know what conditions were required to trade for those players. Were they even available to trade for? Did the Lions try aggressively to acquire one, but the team wasn't offering them at a reasonable price? Did the Lions ignore those players completely because they didn't view them as scheme fits?

It's impossible to informatively critique the Lions for failing to make a move when we have zero reliable knowledge on what it would've taken to acquire those players. Those conversations feel like a waste of time and energy.

However, we do know the conditions it took to trade for players who were moved over the last few weeks. We know for certain that they were available for trade, and we know (around) what it would've cost Detroit to acquire said player. So, among the moves that were made over the past few weeks, which one appeals to you the most?

Then two trades that were agreed upon fell through: Bengals trading for EDGE Arnold Ebiketie and Broncos trading for LB Daiyan Henley.

So, essentially, zero trades were made at positions of need for the Lions. I saw many wishing they had traded for Henley (Broncos were going to spend a fourth and 2028 fifth-round pick for him), and at this point, it's unclear why the trade did not go through.

I will say, I'm intrigued by the other safety moves that were made. I think Jessie Bates is a game-changer, but a second-round pick for a half-season rental is a bit pricey for a Lions team that is getting healthier at the safety position right now. Hooker is a younger option who is signed through 2028 and comes with reasonable price tags of $9.57 million in 2027 and $11.8 million in 2028. If the Lions don't believe Kerby Joseph will be around beyond this year, this could've been a very savvy move for what amounted to a second-round pick with a fifth-round pick in return.

However, the trade I keep going back to is the Joey Porter Jr. one. I still think that's a move I would've made (cost the Cowboys a sixth and a 2028 second-round pick). While Porter has struggled with injuries with Dallas, his play on the field has been as advertised. I thought he was a great scheme fit, and a potential long-term option for a position Detroit has struggled to find consistency at. I wrote more about why I thought the Lions should've traded for Porter Jr. back in October.
