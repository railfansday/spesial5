Swansea City face Vitor Matos exit twist - 4 managers must be lined up ASAP

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Swansea City fans were rocked by the news on Monday night that Vitor Matos was being looked at by Ligue 1 club Strasbourg, with pressure mounting on current manager Hugo Oliveira.

The Portuguese boss was recently linked with the Crystal Palace post, but after Swansea's form dipped with four defeats in five, and the Eagles appointed Thomas Frank to replace Pierre Sage, Swans supporters felt they'd be able to appreciate Matos a little longer.

Strasbourg's official approach to Matos indicates that the drop in form in the league hasn't impacted his standing amongst managerial circles, and the Ligue 1 side, who are sitting 13th with just three wins from 10, are keen on bringing him in if they pull the trigger on Oliveira.

It's not an ideal scenario for any club to be in, but after a strong start to the Championship campaign, with the South Wales side sitting sixth after 15 games, Swansea will need to act fast to bring someone new in if Matos decides to leave.

These four coaches should be in line to take the reins.

With Swansea's recruitment drive shifting to more of a project-based outcome in the summer, following their partnership with Tony Bloom-led Jamestown Analytics, there could be a more project-heavy hire in the dugout if Matos departs.

However, if the Swans want to keep the momentum from the first 15 games high and hire someone set to continue this promotion push immediately, Carlos Corberan should be right at the top of their list.

The Spaniard has been out of work since leaving Valencia in September. He's helped his boyhood club improve after slow starts and secure a mid-table finish in 2024/25 and 2025/26, and in the Championship, he forged a reputation as one of the better coaches in the division with both Huddersfield Town and West Brom.

Corberan took both of those to the play-offs, despite them being rather worse for wear beforehand, and at the Hawthorns, especially, had his sides playing some of the best football in the division, per the underlying numbers.

There's still plenty of room to improve in this Swansea squad, despite their position, and Corberan could be the missing piece to do so. Plus, the 43-year-old could finally become a Premier League boss in South Wales, something which has eluded him so far in his career.

Perhaps not the most popular choice among the four, but Steve Cooper was the last manager to oversee something close to a successful period at Swansea, finishing in the top six in consecutive seasons between 2019 and 2021.

Since then, the Swans haven't finished any higher than 10th. No parachute payments have helped, but perhaps the numerous changes in managers haven't either.

Swansea fans weren't totally enamoured with the football being played; others were totally fine with it. What's important, though, is that it brought about results at this level, and for a Swans side who want to maintain this play-off finish, bringing in someone who hasn't finished below the top six at this level wouldn't be a bad decision.

Cooper knows how to come in midway through a campaign and oversee results, too, having taken Nottingham Forest from the bottom of the division to play-off success in the space of eight months.

The task isn't as drastic back in South Wales, so he should be able to achieve some success.

In Matos, Swansea found success with a young, up-and-coming coach from a foreign league. Perhaps they could do so again, but this time with a name they know all too well from his time as a player in South Wales.

What Pablo Hernandez is doing in his first managerial role with boyhood club Castellón in the Spanish second tier is massively going under the radar.

He took over as interim boss in September last year, oversaw their best finish in nearly 20 years, and now has them second with nine wins in 13 games. They've conceded just five goals, which is quite remarkable, and they could be on their way to La Liga for the first time since the 1990/91 campaign.

The 41-year-old already has that connection with Swansea, spending two seasons there as a player and helping them win the EFL Cup in 2013, so he knows what it's like for the South Wales side to be in the top flight and competing regularly.

It may be difficult to persuade him to leave this project at his boyhood club, but the possibility of helping bring a team back to the Premier League could be tempting enough.

Another project-based manager could see Jack Wilshere make the step-up into the Championship after a year cutting his teeth in League One with Luton Town.

The 34-year-old was unable to help the Hatters earn promotion in his first half-season in charge, but his side did show signs of excellence in the latter stages once things started to properly click. Luton went 12 games unbeaten to end the year, winning the EFL Trophy along the way.

Now, this season, Wilshere has his side playing good football; the underlying numbers have them among the best teams in the division, and they currently sit fourth, five points off Stockport County in second and with a game in hand.

Swansea haven't shied away from hiring out of the third tier. They did so with Russell Martin in the summer of 2021, and that came after he'd overseen mid-table finishes at MK Dons.

Wilshere is in a much better position as a manager now, and is one of the more promising young coaches in the EFL. He has Championship potential, and Swansea could be the side to harness that.
