When did Scotland last beat Switzerland? Full head to head record and last five games

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Scotland’s Nations League campaign wraps up on Monday evening with a tough encounter against Switzerland as the side aims to snatch an unlikely promotion playoff spot in Group B1 from Slovenia.

Head coach Sebastien Pocognoli finally got his first home win with a 2-1 win over North Macedonia on Friday, and now his team will look to make it back-to-back wins for the first time under his management while hoping Slovenia slip up in the group’s other game.

However, should Scotland fail to win in St Gallen, hopes of promotion back to League A will be extinguished. A third-place finish will also have ramifications for the upcoming Euro 2028 qualifiers, which are drawn in Belfast this December.

But when did Scotland last beat Switzerland? What is their head-to-head record, and when did they last meet? Here’s everything you need to know about Scotland’s record against Switzerland.

The teams have faced each other a total of 18 times, and have met twice in major tournament finals, with both encounters taking place in the group stage of the European Championships.

They last faced each other just last month in the Nations League, where Switzerland ran out 3-0 winners after Jack Hendry was sent off early on at Hampden Park. Prior to that game, they drew 1-1 at Euro 2024 in Germany, with Scott McTominay opening the scoring early on for Scotland, before Xherdan Shaqiri equalised shortly afterwards.

From their 18 games played, Scotland have eight wins, giving them a 33.3% win percentage. Switzerland have won a total of six times - a 22.22 per cent win percentage. A further four games have ended in a draw.

Despite having the better head to head record against the Swiss, the Scotland men’s national team have not tasted victory against them for over 30 years. Their most recent win came at Euro 1996 at Villa Park, Birmingham on 18 June, where they earned their first win a 1-0 win in their final group game at the tournament.

The victory came via a fine strike from Ally McCoist in the 36th minute, but was not enough for Scotland to qualify for the knockout stages of the tournament, with the Netherlands finishing above them on goal difference. The Switzerland side that day included future Celtic defender Ramon Vega.

Scotland line up that night: Andy Goram, Tom Boyd, Colin Calderwood, Colin Hendry, Stuart McCall, Gary McAllister, John Collins, Tosh McKinlay (Scott Booth, 60), Craig Burley, Gordon Durie and Ally McCoist (John Spencer, 84).
