Eagles’ offensive changes produced less than encouraging results

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The more things change, the more they stay the same.

The Philadelphia Eagles made notable changes to their offense in Sunday's win over the Pittsburgh Steelers. Less play-action, more pistol.

They changed up the basis of Sean Mannion's offense, as 84% of the Eagles' offensive snaps were in the shotgun and 64.2% involved motion — both were season-highs. A season-high 42% of plays were snapped between six and 10 seconds left on the play clock — far above the previous high of 24.2%.

Play-action percentage — the basis of Mannion's offense — was 8.0%. That's the lowest since Week 1 (5.7%). Shotgun play-action percentage was 14.3%, the highest since Week 1 (14.3%).

The Eagles ran "11 personnel" 82.0% of the time, the second-highest percentage in a game this season (Week 6 vs. Panthers, 83.6%). "12 personnel" was 12.0%, the second-lowest percentage this year (Week 9 vs. Giants, 9.1%). The Eagles didn't run "13 personnel" — three tight ends on the field — at all (despite having five tight ends on the active roster).

There were significant changes on how the Eagles ran their offense, from pre-snap motion, to when they snapped the ball, and the formations they used. Since Joe Milton became the starting quarterback, the Eagles were trending in this direction.

The results, unfortunately, have been more of the same.

The Eagles can't control the clock, they've been unable to stay on the field, and struggle to score points. This was with Saquon Barkley healthier (for a half at least) and DeVonta Smith back.

Sean Mannion's offense still failed to score 20 points and gain 250 yards. This was with the changes made — and how Philadelphia has been trending in that direction — over the last two weeks.

There have been some positives with the changes, but the results have been what we're accustomed to.

When looking at the offensive numbers after the first quarter, it's impressive the Eagles actually won the football game and snapped their losing streak. This also demonstrates how inept the Pittsburgh Steelers offense was on Sunday (or how well the Eagles defense played).

The Eagles had 86 yards of total offense over the final three quarters on 36 plays — an average of 2.4 yards per play. On their nine drives, the Eagles averaged 10.8 yards per drive and 0.75 points per possession.

The 3-and-out percentage was 50% (the Eagles lead the NFL in 3-and-out percentage). They had zero explosive plays while running 83.3% of the plays in shotgun and 60.5% of plays in motion.

Joe Milton was 6-of-15 for 50 yards in the final three quarters, averaging 3.3 yards per attempt and finishing with a 49.3 rating. Milton was 3-of-9 for 31 yards when blitzed (44.2 rating) and 1-of-4 for 10 yards on throws of 10+ air yards.

Milton finished 11-of-21 for 141 yards in the game, averaging 6.7 yards per attempt and finishing with a 73.7 rating. He was 4-of-7 for 87 yards on throws of 10+ air yards for the entire game (101.5 rating).

Saquon Barkley had 12 carries for 24 yards, 2.0 yards per carry. The Eagles run offense had 20 carries for 37 yards (1.9 yards per carry) with a longest run of seven yards. DeVonta Smith and Darius Cooper were the only two players to catch passes.

The Eagles averaged 3.5 yards per carry for the entire game (26 carries for 91 yards). Barkley had 17 carries for 75 yards, 4.4 yards per carry. Dallas Goedert was the only player other than Smith a Cooper to catch a pass (38-yard reception in the first quarter).

The numbers in the final three quarters are consistent throughout the year. The Eagles average 184.7 yards and 4.0 yards per play in the final three quarters — last in the NFL. The 12.6 points per game rank 30th in the NFL.

The second half numbers are poor as well. The 4.6 points per game in the second half are 32nd in the NFL, same with the 110.1 yards and 3.6 yards per play.

The Eagles did up the scoring average, but that was due to 53 and 55-yard field goals by Jake Elliott. They had just 73 yards of offense and averaged just 2.4 yards per play.

Hard to get excited about changes when the numbers remain the same — or worse.

This might be the biggest disappointment from Sunday. Throughout the season, the consensus of the offense's lack of ability to move the ball was due to the inconsistency and lack of communication on the offensive line.

The offensive line arguably played its best game all season, and the Eagles still couldn't move the ball.

Philadelphia's offensive line allowed just five pressures, a pressure rate allowed of 20.8%. The pressure rate allowed was the lowest in the 2026 season (previous low was 38.3% in Week 9 against the Giants). The sack rate was at 12.5%, the lowest since Week 7 against the Dallas Cowboys.

The Eagles offensive line also saw the Steelers blitz five-or-more rushers 54.2% of the time, the highest since Week 4 against the Los Angeles Rams (Markel Bell's first start).

As for Bell, he didn't allow a sack for the second straight game. Bell also allowed a 4.2% pressure rate, far below the 11.6% allowed in Week 8 and 10.5% allowed in Week 7. The rookie is improving.

Landon Dickerson allowed a season-low one pressure and a 4.2% pressure rate. Drew Kendall didn't allow a pressure.

The rushing yards per attempt on designed runs was 3.9, the highest since Week 7. This was with a yards before contact average of 0.23. The Eagles still couldn't move the ball and score points after the first quarter.

Milton has only made two starts for the Eagles, but the offense has been tailed to fit his skill set. That involves less play-action and more pistol.

The Eagles ran shotgun on 75.8% of the offensive snaps in the Week 9 loss to the Giants, Milton's first start. That number increased to 84.0% on Sunday. The play-action rate dipped from 13.6% to 8.0%, and the 13.6% rate in Week 9 was the highest since Jalen Hurts was the starting quarterback (Week 5 at Jacksonville Jaguars).

Based on the shotgun percentage in Week 9, the Eagles were considering making a switch on offense — or accommodating to Milton's style. This is a reminder Milton joined the team on October 16, and has only been in the offense for over a month.

Over the past three weeks, the Eagles have been in shotgun 79.3% of the time. That only trails the Cincinnati Bengals for highest in the NFL (85.3%). Play-action is only at 11.2%, 26th in the NFL. The Eagles are also running "11 personnel" on 81.0% of the plays, the highest in the league.

The Eagles altered their offense, yet are averaging just 15.0 offensive points per game (28th in NFL) and 217.0 yards per game (31st in NFL). The yards per play is at 3.7 (32nd in NFL).

No matter the changes, the offense is still bad.

There isn't much to get excited about this offense, and Sunday's win over the Steelers confirmed that.

After an exciting first quarter, the Eagles went dormant. They couldn't stay on the field, and went 3-and-out on 50.0% of the offensive possessions.

Yes, they scored six points in the second half — thanks to Jake Elliott and two 50+ yard field goals. They had 73 yards of offense and averaged 2.4 yards per play in that stretch. In the second half of games since Milton become quarterback, the Eagles are 29th in points per game (4.5), 31st in yards per game (86.0), and 32nd in yards per play (2.8).

The Eagles are 32nd in the NFL in points (4.6), yards (110.1), and yards per play (3.6) in the second half this season. There are arguments to be made the offense has only gotten worse.

The offense is the offense, no matter what changes Nick Sirianni or Sean Mannion make to the unit. There were encouraging signs on Sunday, and the Eagles still couldn't move the ball.

Hopefully, the offense can be better against three bottom-10 defenses in the NFL over the next three games. There's little reason for optimism.
