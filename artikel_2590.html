LaMelo Ball's steal sets up Wolves' go-ahead layup, then Trey Lyles makes game-saving block vs. Trail Blazers

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

While starring for the Charlotte Hornets, LaMelo Ball was far too often a defensive liability. The 25-year-old guard isn't even a month into his first regular season with the Minnesota Timberwolves, and they're already squeezing significantly more effort out of him on that end of the court.

That uptick in defensive intensity helped deliver the Wolves a 106-105 victory on Tuesday night at home against the Portland Trail Blazers.

With 8.1 seconds remaining, and Minnesota trailing by one point, Ball got a piece of an inbounds pass from Portland's Donovan Clingan, poking it free. Wolves guard Ayo Dosunmu corralled the loose ball and flipped on the accelerator. Rather than attacking the paint on a night he scored a season-high 30 points, the guard fired a pass down low to forward Jaden McDaniels, who quickly dropped in the go-ahead layup with 2.6 ticks to go.

With his team suddenly down one, Clingan connected on his next inbounds pass, as the big man hit star guard Damian Lillard in stride. Except Lillard's drive was denied by an elevating Trey Lyles. The 6-foot-9 veteran rejected the shot out of bounds.

Portland got one last chance with a second remaining on the clock. Robert Williams III couldn't handle the lob from Clingan; however, the ball ended up with Toumani Camara, whose last-second attempt was contested by Ball in the paint and off the mark.

Ball let out a triumphant roar as the buzzer sounded. He finished with 18 points on 7-of-17 shooting, but his defense down the stretch was pivotal. As for Lyles, he chipped in 8 points, 7 rebounds, 4 assists and that game-saving block in 31 minutes off the bench.

McDaniels' final basket gave him 22 points. He had a team-high three steals.

Altogether, the Timberwolves (11-4) held off a Trail Blazers (9-5) team that was fueled by a combined 48 points from Lillard (25) and his backcourt partner, Jrue Holiday (23).

Minnesota currently leads the NBA in defensive rating. It is in the top 10 of that category for the fifth straight season. This time around, Ball is part of that display, adopting the Wolves' defensive DNA.
