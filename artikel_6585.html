Butter Mochi Cake With Maple-Miso Pecans

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

For Shuai Wang, chef of King BBQ in North Charleston, South Carolina, mochi brings back memories of holidays with family. His other dessert love? Classic Southern pecan pie. This Hawaiian-style butter mochi cake draws on both, pairing a rich, chewy cake with a generous layer of maple-miso pecans.

The batter gets its signature bounce from mochiko, a distinct style of rice flour that gives butter mochi its chewy texture—not the fluffy crumb of a conventional cake. Coconut milk and melted butter add richness, while the pecans provide crunch. Let the cake cool completely before slicing so the texture has time to set.

The secret to the pecan topping is a little white or yellow miso, which brings savory depth to the maple syrup, much like the salt in salted caramel. The nuts need only a brief head start in the oven; they’ll continue to toast as the cake bakes.

Can I use regular rice flour instead of mochiko?

No. Mochiko, also called sweet or glutinous rice flour, has a different starch composition from regular rice flour. It’s responsible for butter mochi’s characteristic springy, slightly sticky chew. Regular rice flour or all-purpose flour won’t produce the same texture.

White and yellow miso are both good choices for this cake. White miso tends to be milder and slightly sweeter, while yellow miso has a somewhat more pronounced savory flavor. Either adds complexity to the maple-coated pecans without overwhelming their buttery, nutty flavor. Learn more about the different types of miso and how to use them.

Mochiko is naturally gluten-free, despite the sometimes confusing name glutinous rice flour. This recipe can be gluten-free, but check your miso: Some varieties contain barley or wheat. If you’re baking for someone who needs to avoid gluten, choose a miso labeled gluten-free and check your other packaged ingredients.

Place a rack in middle of oven; preheat to 350°. Lightly oil a rimmed baking sheet with vegetable oil. Whisk 2 Tbsp. pure maple syrup and 2 tsp. white or yellow miso (gluten-free if needed) in a medium bowl until smooth. Add 8 oz. raw pecans, coarsely chopped (about 2 cups) and 1 Tbsp. sugar and toss until evenly coated. Spread out on prepared baking sheet; bake until fragrant and matte looking, 6–8 minutes (nuts won’t be fully toasted). Let cool.

Lightly grease a 9"-diameter cake pan with unsalted butter and line bottom with a parchment paper round. Vigorously whisk 2 large eggs, 1¼ cups (250 g) sugar, and 1 tsp. Diamond Crystal or ½ tsp. Morton kosher salt in a large bowl until smooth. Add one 13.5-oz. can unsweetened coconut milk, well shaken, heated slightly if fat is solid, 2 tsp. vanilla bean paste or vanilla extract, and 6 Tbsp. unsalted butter, melted, slightly cooled, and whisk well. Add 1⅔ cups (254 g) mochiko and 1 tsp. baking powder; whisk vigorously until combined and batter is smooth and thick. Scrape batter into prepared pan.

Toss pecans to break up any clumps and scatter evenly over batter, going all the way to the edges (it may seem like a lot). Bake cake until sides start to pull away from pan, top is golden brown, and a tester inserted into the center comes out clean (some pecans will sink), 50–55 minutes. If pecans begin to brown too quickly, loosely tent pan with foil. Transfer to a wire rack and let cake cool in pan 15 minutes. Turn cake out onto rack; peel away parchment and discard. Carefully turn cake right side up and let cool completely (at least 1 hour).

To serve, slice cake and top with scoops of vanilla ice cream.

Do Ahead: Cake can be made 1 day ahead. Store, loosely covered, at room temperature.
