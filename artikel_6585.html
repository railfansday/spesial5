Dutch Apple Pie

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Dutch apple pie is the upgrade the fall classic needs. With a nutty, streusel topping, this cozy dessert will have you coming back for slice after slice. It’s also easier to make than the traditional version because there’s no need to cook the apple filling ahead of time. In fact, some of us will be swapping out our traditional apple pie recipe for this one for Thanksgiving this year.

Lightly flour your work surface, then roll out the chilled dough into a 12-inch circle. Carefully move it to a 9-inch pie dish. Gently lift the edges and let the dough settle into the pan. Make sure not to stretch the dough. Press it firmly into the bottom and sides. Pop it in the fridge until it’s cold (about 30 minutes), or freeze it for 10 minutes if you’re in a hurry.

Grab a medium bowl and whisk together the granulated sugar, flour, cinnamon, and salt.

In a large bowl, whisk together the egg, sour cream, and vanilla. Add the dry ingredients and whisk until the mixture is nice and smooth.

Move a rack to the center of your oven and preheat it to 350°. In a medium bowl, whisk together the brown sugar, flour, granulated sugar, walnuts, oats, and cinnamon. Add the butter and use a fork or pastry cutter to work it in until crumbly.

Spread the apples evenly in your prepared pie crust. Pour the filling over the apples, then sprinkle the crumble topping all over.

Bake the pie until the crust and crumble are golden brown and you don’t see any more moisture. This should take about an hour. Once it’s done, let the pie cool on a wire rack until it reaches room temperature, then pop it in the fridge for an hour before serving.

Because this pie has a sour cream and egg-based filling, store leftovers covered in the refrigerator for up to 4 days.
