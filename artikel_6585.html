26 Best Men's Scarves 2026, According to GQ Editors

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Are any accessories as essential, yet mysteriously overlooked, as the best men's scarves? First, they warm your neck (and face, and chest) like nothing else. Second, they put a style-forward, cheery, colorful bow on pretty much any outfit. And yet, these soft little miracles are all too often an afterthought in the menswear world. Even if you’re the type of guy to painstakingly lay your fits out the night before, chances are when it comes to your scarf game, you’re still reaching for one you picked up in a rushed pinch from Nordstrom Rack, or at a frigid college football game.

This is, of course, fine. But it’s also sort of a tragedy. Because when you pick up a truly great scarf, everything changes. Swaddling your neck in cashmere (or cashmere-emulating fabrics) doesn’t just provide a soothing all-day embrace through bracing winds. It also, like a necktie, creates an opportunity for a flash of personal style, right under your handsome face. Even better, because scarves use less fabric than coats and knitwear, they’re also a great way to own some of the best materials in our industry for far less scratch.

To help you pick the one you’ll soon be recognized by, I surveyed hundreds of scarves from across the fashion (and budget) spectrum, journeying from mall chains, to historic British manufacturers, to the buzziest makers today, and everything in-between. Below, the final 26 best men's scarves to knot up this winter—and next winter, and the one after that.

Parisian label Brut puts a Big Apple spin on the classic European football supporter scarf, featuring none other than Lady Liberty herself. A fitting French connection.

The bandana, neckerchief, foulard, whatever you want to call it has become an easy, even trendy winter accessory. Alex Mill’s is made from extra-fine merino wool, comes in 14 colors, and costs less than a hundo.

Another bandana-shaped style from the Banana, this time with a generous helping of cashmere for a little extra softness.

A smart tartan plaid as classic as the linings inside Barbour’s famous wax jackets, in a soft wool-cashmere blend. About as British as a rainy afternoon and a pint at the pub.

We cross the Irish Sea for L.L.Bean’s take, made from Irish-woven lambswool, known for its durability and natural water resistance. Plenty warm and handsome to boot.

Lost & Found’s Scottish lambswool scarf comes with pockets stitched into it. Perfect for stashing your cards, cash or other things a little less above board. We won’t ask.

On the GORP-ier side of things, this thermal fleece scarf balances warmth, stretch and lightweight comfort in a slimmed-down design that will both fit with your puffer and stay out of your way.

Quince has made a name for itself selling luxury materials at un-luxury prices. Case in point: this soft scarf made from 100% Mongolian cashmere, at a price you could get two with.

Another triangle scarf, here in a stunning sienna color that’s still right in Margaret Howell’s wheelhouse. Snug and sensible.

Lil’ brown checks, a fuzzy brushed finish, and Japanese cotton-wool twill woven in England. A big helping of understated charm.

A nice change of pace from the usual winter plaids, two-tone chevrons give this Coach scarf a little graphic punch, while a lightweight but warm alpaca blend keeps things cosy.

If you love pattern, fall and winter is primed for Polo Power. This Fair-Isle-esque design is easier to wear than the classic sweater, and will add some energy to your winter fits.

True to form, Kapital keeps things weird with a wild leopard-and-paisley design. The lightweight gauze also means you can wear it a dozen different ways, pretty much any time of year.

William Lockie’s scarves will set you back a few extra bucks, but they’ve been at it since 1874, so you're buying proven scarf smarts. In this case: Pure cashmere, traditional techniques, and even a pre-sale bath in local fresh water for added softness.

Todd Snyder isn’t reinventing the wheel here, this is just a beautifully brushed cashmere scarf. Classic, timeless, soft, and warm as they come.

Howlin’ often brings a little irreverence to its sweaters, and the same goes for its scarves. Here, throwback collegiate “fun” stripes show the brand’s take on the Ivy scarves of yesteryear.

Made from Italian wool, this Mfpen number gives you two checks for the price of one, thanks to a clever double-faced design. Twist it, drape it, wear it however you please.

All the cozy, old-school charm of an Aran knit, Percival takes the classic cream cable-knit sweater and turns it into a scarf. And at more than half off, it’s a bit of a steal.

She's pricey, but you’re paying for fine Scottish-spun cashmere materials and serious know-how with an entirely handmade and hand-linked scarf.

Printed scarves were the first things Drake’s made back in 1977, when it was still a small workshop. These archival designs are works of art you can wear. Wool, silk and good taste.

Missoni’s famous zigzags came out of swinging ’60s Milan, and they still have that groovy Italian charm. Funny how something so unmistakably of its time never seems to go out of style.

A statement scarf if there ever was one, with vibrant Yves Klein blue and an oversized logo patch that’s not shy about announcing itself. A lot to spend on yourself, maybe, but one hell of a gift.

There’s something dashingly medieval about a shearling scarf. Wrap it around your neck for a bit of Feudal-Lord-core. Beats a suit of armor.

NN07 mixes two old-school patterns into one elegant Italian-woven scarf, with a fuzzy brushed finish and blanket-stitched edges. Your granddad would approve.

This fashion-guy-approved cashmere scarf from Auralee is classic enough, but boy, those rich colours are doing something for me. It truly is all in the details.

By now, you may have noticed the Scots know a thing or two about wool. Johnstons of Elgin has been at it for centuries, and this beautifully woven cashmere scarf is further proof.
