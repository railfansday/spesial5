Georgia’s Bear Canister Debate and a Half Dome Rescue | This Week on Trail #2

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Bear canisters are coming to Georgia, and the Appalachian Trail community has some big feelings about it. On this episode of This Week on Trail, produced by The Trek, hosts Jess “Sassafras” and Katie “Double Dip” dig into the Forest Service’s new rule requiring hard-sided canisters on the entire Georgia section of the AT, and one of them walks in lukewarm and walks out ready to make everyone carry a bear can forever. Plus, hikers rig a makeshift rope to pull an engaged couple off Half Dome, a new listener segment debuts, a Redditor asks about packing out filet mignon, and Pattie Gonia sets off on a 500-mile fundraiser in drag.

Starting January 1, 2027, the US Forest Service will require hard-sided bear canisters year-round for overnight hikers on all 78.3 miles of the AT in Georgia, the Approach Trail, and a half-mile buffer on either side, which rules out Ursacks and food hangs. Jess walks through the order and the stat behind it: Georgia holds about 3.5% of the trail but more than 12% of AT bear encounter reports over the last decade. Katie, who carried a canister for the entire CDT, rates the inconvenience about a three and notes you can mail a can home from the post office without even boxing it up. Jess goes further, arguing that anyone this upset about carrying a canister for the first week of a six-month hike is being a total weenie.

The news drew more than 1,000 comments across The Trek’s Facebook and Instagram posts, and Jess brings the highlights, from an AI-generated image of a mangled canister to the theory that BearVault has the ATC in its pocket. The hosts point out the order came from the Forest Service, not the Appalachian Trail Conservancy, though the ATC is catching heat anyway. Katie says cost is the one complaint she sympathizes with and predicts rentals and used cans will fill the gap, while Jess, who works with The Trek’s AT survey data, says plenty of hikers are sleeping with their food instead of hanging it properly. Jess also worries about the ridge runners who will be stuck explaining a rule they had no say in, and Katie, drawing on her own trail steward work, explains why giving them a hard time only leads to stricter enforcement.

Katie recounts the October 12 rescue on Yosemite’s Half Dome, where a woman descending the cables slid down the slick granite and stopped just above a steep drop, and her fiancé slipped trying to reach her and ended up clinging to a ledge by his fingertips. With rangers miles away, nearby hikers emptied their packs and built a line from clothesline, carabiners, slings, and donated harnesses, then anchored themselves in a human chain while two of them worked down to the couple as snow began to fall. The hosts wonder whether they would have joined the chain, and close with a reminder of the first rule of backcountry rescue: don’t make yourself a second victim.

The show debuts a new segment where listeners submit a one-minute video defending a hill they will die on. First up is Caroline “Boy” from Maine, who argues that half the fun of backpacking is getting to stop: the town shower, clean clothes, a real meal, and pulling your shoes off at camp. Jess and Katie fully agree, and one of them goes a step further, suggesting the worst part of backpacking might be the backpacking.

A post in r/backpacking asked whether it’s a bad idea to pack a frozen, sealed filet mignon for a short overnight and drop it into store-bought pho. Both hosts say bring the steak. Jess recalls her first backpacking trip’s taco night, canned black beans and a broken can opener included, and Katie confesses to packing a travel-size “pepper cannon.” Katie also passes along a CDT trick from a hiker who froze Taco Bell and wrapped it in his sleeping bag to keep it cold on the way out of town.

After ribbing Pattie Gonia last episode, the hosts give her her flowers for her new fundraiser: backpacking and biking 500 miles in drag from San Francisco to Los Angeles to raise $5 million for 19 nonprofits. Jess and Katie admire the tenacity and say it’s a good reminder that hiking should be sillier and more fun.

Jess reads hater comments from her bear canister video, including a call to ban nose rings and a pile of corrections on how to say “Appalachian,” which the hosts understand to be a regional difference you hear both ways on trail. They also respond to a viewer who said the first episode sounded like a mean girl podcast, and invite gentler feedback.

The episode wraps with a kind note from Jack, who has followed Jess’s AT hike and Katie’s hikes and watches the show with his wife.
