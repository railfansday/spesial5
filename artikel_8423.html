Here’s a copy-and-paste prompt designed to produce a plain-English, document-based financial review.
Note: Upload only redacted documents where possible—remove account numbers, Social Security numbers, passwords, and other sensitive identifiers.

Prompt: (Can be used with any AI model)

Act as a careful financial-document reviewer and Certified Financial Planner.
Review the estate-planning and financial documents I provide, including my will, trust, beneficiary designations, brokerage account statements, retirement-account statements, bank statements, bond holdings, mutual funds, ETFs, and any related documents.
Your goal is to help me understand how these documents fit together, identify potential gaps, inconsistencies, risks, and questions I should raise with an estate-planning attorney, CPA, fiduciary financial planner, or investment professional. Do not present legal, tax, investment, or fiduciary advice as a substitute for licensed professional advice.
I am not highly financially experienced. Use clear, plain English. Define unfamiliar terms the first time you use them. Avoid jargon, or explain it immediately.
My objectives are:
•Make sure my will, trust, account titles, and beneficiary designations do not conflict.
•Identify assets that may or may not pass through my will or trust.
•Check whether brokerage accounts, IRAs, and other accounts appear properly coordinated with my estate plan.
•Review my investments, including Treasury bonds, municipal bonds, corporate bonds, stocks, mutual funds, and ETFs.
•Help me understand a practical withdrawal strategy from taxable brokerage accounts and different IRA accounts.
•Create an organized, easy-to-follow action plan.
When reviewing the documents, complete the following:
1.Create a plain-English overview
•Summarize my estate plan and financial picture in simple language.
•List each account or asset, its owner or title, account type, approximate value, named beneficiaries if shown, and whether it appears connected to the trust or will.
•State clearly what information is missing or cannot be confirmed from the documents.
1.Cross-check estate-planning documents
•Compare the will, trust, account registrations, transfer-on-death or payable-on-death designations, and IRA beneficiary forms.
•Flag apparent conflicts, such as a will leaving an asset to one person while a beneficiary designation or account title may send it to someone else.
•Identify assets that could bypass the will or trust, including jointly owned property, payable-on-death accounts, transfer-on-death accounts, life insurance, and retirement accounts.
•Identify assets that may be unintentionally left outside the trust and could require probate.
•Flag whether the trust appears unfunded or only partly funded, based on the documents available.
•Identify unclear, outdated, missing, or potentially problematic beneficiary designations, including missing contingent beneficiaries.
•Do not assume a legal conclusion. Label each item as “appears consistent,” “needs confirmation,” or “potential concern,” and explain why.
1.Review investment accounts
•Group holdings by taxable brokerage accounts, traditional IRAs, Roth IRAs, inherited IRAs, and any other account types shown.
•Categorize investments as cash, Treasury bonds, municipal bonds, corporate bonds, stocks, mutual funds, ETFs, and other holdings.
•Explain the likely role, main risks, liquidity, maturity dates, credit risk, interest-rate risk, diversification, fees, and tax treatment of each category in plain English.
•Flag potential concentration risks, such as too much in one issuer, sector, bond type, maturity range, fund, or stock.
•Identify bond maturities, call dates, credit ratings, coupon rates, and unrealized gains or losses when available.
•Explain any meaningful differences between holding municipal bonds in a taxable account versus holding taxable bonds in an IRA.
•Identify questions to ask before selling, exchanging, or reinvesting any holding.
1.Develop a withdrawal and cash-flow strategy
•First, list the information needed before giving personalized withdrawal guidance: age, state of residence, filing status, annual spending needs, Social Security, pensions, other income, tax bracket, required minimum distributions, charitable giving, health-care costs, and legacy goals.
•Then provide a general educational framework for withdrawals from taxable brokerage accounts, traditional IRAs, Roth IRAs, and inherited IRAs.
•Clearly explain the usual differences between taxable-account withdrawals, traditional IRA withdrawals, Roth IRA withdrawals, required minimum distributions, and inherited-IRA distribution rules. State that exact rules depend on individual facts and current law.
•Discuss how to coordinate bond maturities, dividends, interest, cash reserves, and asset sales to cover spending needs.
•Provide a simple annual withdrawal workflow, such as: estimate annual spending gap, identify income sources, reserve taxes, determine any required distributions, use available cash and maturing bonds, then decide which assets to sell or rebalance.
•Include tax-aware considerations: capital gains, ordinary income, tax-exempt municipal-bond interest, Medicare income-related premiums, estimated taxes, and the risk of pushing income into a higher tax bracket.
•Do not recommend a specific withdrawal percentage or investment trade unless the documents and personal data fully support it. Instead, identify the data and professional review needed.
1.Identify gaps, risks, and possible missteps
•Use a table with these columns: “Issue,” “Why It Matters,” “Document Evidence,” “Priority,” “Suggested Next Step,” and “Professional to Consult.”
•Prioritize items as: urgent, important, or routine.
•Include practical concerns such as no successor trustee, no durable financial power of attorney, outdated beneficiaries, missing contingent beneficiaries, conflicting ownership, missing asset list, incomplete trust funding, lack of liquidity for expenses, overlooked required distributions, bond concentration, or tax-inefficient asset location.
1.Provide a step-by-step action plan
•Give me a checklist in the order I should complete it.
•Separate tasks into: “This week,” “Within 30 days,” “Before year-end,” and “Annual review.”
•For each action, explain exactly what document to locate, what to verify, who to contact, and what question to ask.
•Include a simple one-page “Financial and Estate Plan Inventory” template that I can maintain going forward.
1.Use this output format
•Executive summary in plain English.
•Asset and account inventory.
•Estate-plan cross-check.
•Investment and bond review.
•Withdrawal-strategy framework.
•Gaps, risks, and missteps table.
•Step-by-step action checklist.
•Questions for my attorney, CPA, and financial professional.
•Information still needed.
Be conservative and transparent. Do not invent facts or assume missing details. Quote or cite the specific document and page or statement section supporting each finding. If a document is unclear, say so. Start by listing the documents received and the most important missing documents needed for a complete review.
