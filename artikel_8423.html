Finding The Hidden Door on the Camino

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Yesterday’s temperature hit nearly 89°F as we left the official Variante Espiritual route and climbed up the mountain to our albergue in the tiny farming hamlet of Silva. We were perched about 1.5 km above the Armenteira Monastery, which sat tucked in the valley cove below. Our albergue felt more like a quaint six-room boutique hotel—fully booked, though we somehow never saw another pilgrim.

There were no restaurants or stores in Silva, but we woke up to find a wonderful breakfast spread mysteriously delivered to the shared kitchen overnight by the owners. Fueled up on coffee, tea, pastries, and savory bites, we hit the trail at 9:00 AM.

The morning was cool, though the sun was warming up fast. Although we felt a slight touch of FOMO, we’ve already explored so many spectacular cathedrals and monasteries on this trip that we decided to bypass the descent down into Armenteira, saving ourselves the steep climb back out.

Our morning began with a peaceful walk through the forest up a steep, winding paved road to the hilltop village of Villar. Looking to our right, we could see the stone Monastery of Armenteira nestled deep in the valley cove below.

Looking back and down behind us, the landscape opened up dramatically:

Knockerz joked that this mountain range was just one giant PUD (Pointless Up and Down). The narrow, winding streets of Villar were flanked by high ancient stone walls shielding medieval houses and barns.

Following our custom Google Maps route, we aimed to rejoin the official Variante Espiritual in the high forest just before the mountain pass.

Entering the shaded forest, the path turned into a steep gravel track. Along the way, we stumbled upon four horses grazing freely alongside the road. Farmers here often turn their livestock out to free-range in the high mountain forests.

Soon after passing the horses, we officially rejoined the Variante Espiritual trail as it climbed up from the monastery. We pushed up to the highest point of the pass, where the forest canopy suddenly broke to reveal sweeping views of the Atlantic Ocean far below.

Right off the trail during our descent, we took a break to explore a fascinating historic site featuring ancient stone petroglyphs carved into the bedrock.

Continuing down through a recently clear-cut section of forest with wide-open coastal views, a Belgian hiker suddenly popped out of nowhere right in front of us. He had just scrambled straight up the edge of a sheer drop-off. He gave us a sheepish grin and admitted he had taken a “cheat route”—climbing straight up the clear-cut hill instead of following the long, winding switchbacks.

We took one look at his shortcut and decided we liked the idea. We dropped over the rim and scrambled straight down the mountain along rough logging tracks:

At the bottom of the slope, we safely reconnected with the official Camino trail.

We stepped out of the woods into an upscale residential neighborhood built directly into the steep hillside. Beautifully kept homes lined narrow alleyways paved with cobblestones. As usual, local Spaniards kept trying to point us in the opposite direction, convinced we were going the wrong way. Once again, we appreciated their deep-rooted tradition of looking out for pilgrims.

Reaching the coastline, we officially said goodbye to the Variante Espiritual and reconnected with the main Portuguese Coastal Camino.

The path shifted to a sidewalk running along a busy coastal road. Although we were hot and out of water, the cool salty ocean breeze refreshed our spirits until we found a small fruit market selling ice-cold water.

The official route drifted back inland toward the hills, but since the area was getting more developed and suburban, we chose to stick to the flat coastal highway to avoid another series of “PUDs” into the hillsides. We crossed a massive bridge over the river and entered the bustling city of Pontevedra.

Pontevedra is a large, vibrant city centered around a massive, beautifully preserved medieval core.

Navigating the narrow cobblestone streets, Google Maps brought us right into a lively plaza in the heart of the old town. According to the map, our apartment building was right there—yet we couldn’t see an entrance anywhere.

The app was pointing us directly toward an upscale outdoor bar and restaurant crowded with locals enjoying afternoon drinks. A table of well-dressed patrons looked up in utter perplexity as two sweaty, dirt-covered pilgrims wearing backpacks walked right up to their table.

The Discovery: Directly behind their table—hidden in plain sight—was a massive, 12-foot-tall, 6-foot-wide lime-green ancient wooden door.

I punched in the door code, the heavy latch clicked, and we stepped inside a stunning historic building. We climbed the original wooden staircase to our second-floor apartment. The room was breathtaking: 12-foot ceilings, exposed timber beams, stained-glass accents, and floor-to-ceiling patio doors opening over the plaza. It had the distinct historic charm of a classic Bourbon Street apartment in New Orleans.

After dropping our packs and taking long, hot showers, we put on our clean set of clothes and headed out to explore the medieval center.

We settled into a lively neighborhood tapas bar serving fresh pinchos (small, savory open-faced sandwiches):

After eating, the city quieted down for afternoon siesta. We returned to our apartment, threw open the balcony doors, and relaxed with a glass of wine, watching the quiet streets below.
