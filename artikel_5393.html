Novo Nordisk's departing CFO will lead Denmark's Lundbeck Foundation

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Karsten Munk Knudsen will succeed Lene Skole, who has led the Danish foundation since September 2014, on Jan. 1

Karsten Munk Knudsen, the departing chief financial officer of Novo, formerly Novo Nordisk, has been appointed CEO of the Lundbeck Foundation, the foundation said. He takes up the position on Jan. 1, 2027.

He succeeds Lene Skole, who has led the foundation since September 2014 and will step down at the turn of the year to focus on board work outside the foundation's ownership portfolio. Skole will remain CEO through Dec. 31, 2026, to ensure an orderly handover.

From Jan. 1, Knudsen will join the boards of H. Lundbeck, ALK-Abelló and Falck as an observer. At the forthcoming annual general meetings, he will take over from Skole as deputy chair of the three companies.

Knudsen has spent more than 25 years in the Novo Group. He started in 1999 as a business analyst at NNIT, then a Novo Nordisk subsidiary, and has held senior finance roles, including in the U.S. He has been CFO and executive vice president of Novo since 2018 and leaves that post at the end of 2026. He sits on the boards of Hempel and 3Shape Holding.

"In the Board's view, Karsten Munk Knudsen is exactly the right person to lead the Lundbeck Foundation into its next chapter and strategy period," Søren Skou, chair of the foundation's board, said in a statement. "He has deep insight into the life science industry, into capital allocation and into creating innovation-driven growth."

Knudsen said in the statement that the foundation "is a long-term and engaged owner of strong companies, and it invests in research that can make a difference for people with brain disorders." He added: "That combination of value creation and social responsibility motivates me greatly."

Knudsen is 54, and Luigi La Corte will succeed him as Novo's CFO, according to Bloomberg. The foundation owns the drugmakers H. Lundbeck and ALK-Abelló and holds a stake in Falck.

Novo set out its 2030 strategic ambitions at a Capital Markets Day in London in September, the same month it rebranded from Novo Nordisk to Novo. In the second quarter, Novo held roughly 39% of the obesity drug market, while Eli Lilly $LLY held about 61%, according to CNBC.
