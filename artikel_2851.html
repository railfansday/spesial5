Five Plays: Pitt Third Quarter

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

I can't get enough of the uptick in aggression that we're seeing from the defense. This is a tight formation by Pitt, and the defense answers with an 8-man box with one safety deep to account for a throw down the field. But it's not just the manpower in the box; it's the way they are attacking the play here that is so nice to see. Koen Entringer walks up to the line and immediately gets into the mix to make a play. Ben Perry attacks the outside shoulder of his blocker to set the edge in case this play is coming outside. The interior line is battling every block, which allows TJ Capers to identify the path of the runner so that he can shed his block to the right side.

Then you have Antonio Harris meeting this runner at the line and taking out his legs for a great tackle in space. I mention "havoc" plays a lot, but it's worth noting that the best run defenses make things look chaotic. They truly create havoc at the line of scrimmage, and that's what we see here. Pitt players aren't able to execute anything because of what Louisville is doing to them.

The third installment of Lincoln Kienholz doing things features a new possible best throw of the season.

Pitt's cornerback gives a different look at the mistake Antonio Harris made on Pitt's longest play of the game. He is supposed to be getting depth with the receiver to make sure he doesn't get behind him, but as he's getting depth, he doesn't shade towards the boundary. Tre Richardson is angling for the back pylon from the snap, and the corner loses him in coverage because he is taking a straight angle back. A rough moment for Shawn Lee, who is one of the best corners in the ACC. This was the first touchdown he gave up this year.

This was an underrated play by TJ Capers. You can see the slot receiver to the bottom of the screen running towards Capers at an angle to run behind him. Capers squares his hips before he starts to turn his shoulders to the inside. But right before the ball is thrown, he stops and jumps in front of the route. I don't know how he reacted so quickly to this receiver when he started this play facing the complete opposite way. But his movements make the receiver hesitate for just a bit, and the quarterback throws it where he thinks the receiver will end up. It's an ill-advised throw, but Capers is in the perfect spot to make this play.

The offensive line has gotten a lot of flak from me this season, but they deserve some real credit for how they've played during this winning streak. Pitt got a handful of negative plays in this game, but most plays looked more like this, where Kienholz gets to stand tall with no pressure in his face before he delivers a strike down the field. Kudos to these guys for getting better as the season has gone on.

Did you notice the subtle move Lawayne McCoy makes here right before he catches the ball? He cuts back towards the ball so that he can create the angle to get around the corner to get to the corner of the endzone. Very good stuff here.

I mentioned this on Mike's show on Friday, but Louisville hasn't really reinvented themselves on defense in the back half of the year. What they have done is change the way they bring a fifth rusher. Earlier in the year, Stanquan Clark would most likely be lined up on the line of scrimmage on this play, and he and the defensive linemen would straight rush up the field to try to create pressure.

I think the staff expected the edge rushers to win their one-on-ones, and they didn't need to be too creative to win up front. That wasn't the case, and they struggled to get sacks and pressures early this year. As you can see on this play, Clark just blitzes from the second level while timing up the snap. The defensive tackles slant to the right to pull blockers with them, and Clark ends up running right past the right guard and right into the backfield. They're only rushing four on this play, but the different way they bring the linebacker makes all the difference in the play.

This is how you fill an alley as a safety. Teach tape from TJ Banks.

It has been really nice to see the secondary shut down throws down the field over the last few weeks. It hasn't been perfect, but guys are doing their part to make sure that things are covered. On this play, Banks bites on a double move and ends up a full step behind his man. Kaleb Beasley does his job here as the deep safety to get on his horse and close on the ball right as the receiver is making the catch. This is what it's supposed to look like when everyone does their job.

Brian Brohm waited until late in the game to mix in a couple of screen plays, and it worked out perfectly for him. Gettting Isaac Brown into space with a couple of guys out in front of him to block is always a good idea. Pitt's overaggressive style of play creates the environment where these plays do the most damage. Even Pitt's very veteran defensive line doesn't sniff this out up front.
