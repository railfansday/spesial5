Utah Jazz vs Phoenix Suns: Player Grades

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

By my money, this was the most disappointing loss of the year thus far. Riding high after two road wins, the team looked exhilarated for the first time all year and the home fans were excited to finally have something to latch onto. You could feel how badly the crowd wanted to root hard for their team, but instead, their team simply decided to not show up. The Jazz only lost by 10 points tonight, but if you could only analyze the game based off of body language and effort, you would guess that it was more than that. They didn't hustle after loose balls, they made no attempts to patch their poor decision making, and they looked defeated the whole night. This was a bad loss, and the grades will represent that – not a lot of positives you can take from this one.

Note: These grades are more-or-less relative to the player. An A grade for Darryn Peterson is different than an A grade for Tony Bradley.

It is a make or miss league, and Ace Bailey missed. Honestly, that isn't too surprising; it's been the status quo up to this point in the season. One of the most underrated skills in the league, fascinatingly enough, is actually making shots – a reputation alone doesn't help quite as much. To this point, Ace has a limit on his ceiling until his jump shots go in at a consistent rate, which has not been the case in his collegiate or NBA career. He will rarely go 0-7, but a better shooter will have nights like this even less frequently. Additionally, his defense has become less and less impressive as the year has progressed; tonight, he wasn't nearly as loud as he was at the beginning of the year. Without event creation on defense, self creation on offense, or the ability to hit open threes, Ace really isn't providing anything. Will Hardy recognized this, benching Ace for the closing stretch. This team's potential is very tightly knit to Ace's, and games like this just won't cut it.

After heaps of well-deserved praise after his recent stretch, Mo Bamba had a major regression-t0-the-mean game tonight. He was essentially invisible; the Suns were attacking him relentlessly and he was unable to use his size to make them pay. He looked comfortable taking threes…perhaps too comfortable; I think I prefer when he takes them with defenders sagging off completely, instead of in the face of solid closeouts. Ultimately, this harsh grade was given in the light of his recent games. These were the minutes I expected we'd get from Mo when the Jazz picked him up initially, but because he's shown me more, I've come to expect more. So, while I've loved the Bamba experience thus far, this game, hopefully, acted as a return to reality for those already championing Bamba as the Jazz's answer at the center position.

This was one of the worst offensive games that I can remember from Keyonte. The missed shots were one thing, but the lack of effort and body language were really discouraging. Keyonte has taken on the mantle of leader, but the responsibilities of that title are not relinquished during games where his threes are not going in. It's fun to be a leader when you beat the Lakers in L.A., but it's essential to be a leader when you are battling the Suns in a slog two nights later, and you are simply not doing that when you nonchalantly let opponents steal offensive rebounds from under your nose. I restrained myself from delivering an F due to some genuinely solid defensive contests, but I am placing the majority of this game's ugliness squarely on Keyonte's shoulders.

Josh Green is Josh Green. He can't dribble or pass, which is detrimental in lineups where he is playing alongside 2-3 other players who also can't dribble or pass. But he'll hit an open corner three or two, and he is easily our best defensive option against any star scorer, ranging from 6'4 to 6'8. Devin Booker ended with 23 points, but he wasn't particularly efficient, partially because Green understood that the reason he gets minutes is to make life difficult for the bucket-getter that he's lined up against. The final ATO was his worst coverage of the night, but that did not define his night – we did not lose this game because of Josh Green losing Devin Booker at the end of the game.

Very impressive night from Jaxson Hayes, where he reminded the fanbase that, until this season, he was a far more established player than Mo Bamba for a reason. One underrated skill of his is his timing on his rolls to the rim – he plays it at just the right pace where the ball-handler is able to hit him right in the most sensitive pocket of the defense, and tonight the Suns didn't really have an answer besides fouling. The missed free throws were frustrating, and I don't feel like Hayes ever plays as a 7 footer on defense (more of a shot blocker than a rim protector, and tonight he wasn't even blocking shots), but providing a consistent and efficient offensive outlet is something that shouldn't be undersold.

People aren't too quick to offer Svi praise these days, but he provided one of the better combinations of shooting and effort on the team tonight. I get it, I get it, his turnovers are frustrating, especially for a player that really shouldn't be dribbling, but tonight he understood that someone had to make something happen, which helps me give him a bit of a pass for his 3 turnovers tonight. At the end of the day, if Svi can make jump shots, he's a solid role player – let's hope that his recent efficiency continues on throughout the season.

Maybe I should've spent more attention on what Okogie was doing on defense, but it didn't pop off the screen tonight like it sometimes does. Okogie is somehow an even more simplistic player than Green – if he doesn't have a small, not-too-quick guard to pester, and the shot's not dropping, he really isn't providing too much to the team. He doesn't take much away – he'll shoot when open, and bring intensity at the very least – but he didn't contribute much of anything to the team tonight.

Interesting game for Peterson. He went 0-7 from three, which could make you think that I'd penalize him the same way I did Ace and Keyonte, but Peterson was able to get his fingerprints all over the game tonight in a way that his other prestigious youngster teammates could not. Some of his passes tonight were legitimately inspiring, and gave me hope that his long-term position might actually be point guard – his one hand dart to Green in the corner after a shifty drive was particularly impressive. Additionally, even though he struggled when matched up with a shifty point guard in Gillespie, I still think he gave some serious defensive flashes, at the very least on the defensive glass. Ultimately, I think much of a player's value is based on what they can provide to a team when their shot isn't falling, and tonight, Peterson provided a bit of everything while Ace and Keyonte did not. Some nights will be off nights, but that can be overcome with skills that are not dependent on the variance inherent to jump shots – Peterson seems to already have a strong understanding of that fact.

The defense wasn't awesome, and the decision making wasn't always great, but when he is making his shots, Brice provides so much value to this team because we simply do not have a surplus of players who can do what he does. He is a versatile shot-maker who can succeed even when faced with contests, and that cannot be said for a lot of the players competing for his minutes. Until we get some other player who can fill this niche on the Jazz bench, I can't get behind the prevailing sentiment, which is to call for his immediate dismissal from the consistently active Jazzmen. Brice brings an NBA skill that can completely alter a team's fortunes, which he did tonight – without his efficient shooting, I think the Jazz would've been dead in the water.
