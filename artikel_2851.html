Knicks 112, Raptors 108 (OT): Scenes from our first bonus basketball of the season

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

There's trouble when Mike Breen calls the Knicks out. Right before tip-off, the lifelong fan and broadcasting GOAT called them a "mediocre" basketball team, then acknowledged that they know it's a problem and they're trying to fix it. We noticed it, too; New York had lost two straight and been outhustled by the outclassed Indiana Pacers on Friday.

For 40 minutes tonight, the Knicks proved Mike's case. They led after one because Toronto couldn't shoot, and much the same was true at halftime. Then the Dinos began to pull away, nearly doubling up the Knicks in the paint and beating them to the boards. The Americans trailed by seven to start the fourth and by as many as 14 before finally showing some fire and desire. They went ahead with less than a minute left, but Scottie Barnes (30 PTS, 9 RBS, -9) scored Toronto's last nine points to force overtime. The Knicks never trailed in the fifth frame, and a Brunson dagger with 33 seconds left put them ahead for good, 112-108.

Back from a groin injury, Barnes scored 10 of Toronto's first 13 points, hitting back-to-back threes to put the Raptors up 13-4. Scottie was merriment and light while the Knicks were a smear of discouragement. Birthday boy Karl-Anthony Towns (28 PTS, 17 RBS) pitched two passes into the front row in the first four minutes, and Jalen Brunson (28 PTS, 9 AST, 41 MIN) had three dimes and one basket in the quarter. The bench improved matters. A Mikal Bridges (5 PTS, 7 RBS, 32 MIN . . . +15!) steal and a Towns bunny sparked a 9-0 run that included five points from Landry Shamet (23 PTS, 30 MIN, +12) and a tying dunk from Andre Drummond, courtesy of Josh Hart (0 PTS, 5 RBS, 26 MIN, -15).

Allen Graves stripped Brunson and Jakob Poeltl laid it in at the other end to put Toronto up 17-14. Then the Dinos, hardly renowned for their shooting, came up blank for the final 3:45. Tyler Kolek fed Towns for the tying three and Miles McBride for the one that made it 23-17 at the break.

Shamet scored six quick points to put the Knicks up 32-22. He hit a three after Kolek picked off an RJ Barrett pass, then made all three free throws after Barrett fouled him on another attempt. Toronto answered with a 14-2 run. Poeltl picked Jordan Clarkson's pocket and finished at the other end, starting center Collin Murray-Boyles dunked back a shot Shamet had blocked, and Jamal Shead (19 PTS) scored eight points in 76 seconds to put the Raptors ahead 38-36. During Toronto's 10-0 stretch, the Knicks burned a full shot clock and missed four straight shots.

OG Anunoby stopped the run with two free throws, and Towns put New York back in front with a three. Barrett tied it at 42, but Brunson answered with a step-back three, his only field goal of the quarter. Bridges, who'd barely figured into the offense, set up that shot with 45 seconds left and hit his first basket, a 21-footer, with seven seconds to go. Barnes managed just two points in the second, and Barrett had as many turnovers as baskets (two).

The Knicks were lucky to lead 47-43 at the half. The shooting gap was small (44% to 41%), but Toronto owned the paint, 26-14, won the boards by four, and turned 12 Knicks giveaways into 16 points. The three-ball kept our heroes afloat (42% to Toronto's 26%). For those keeping track, Bridges was 1-for-2 for two points, with two rebounds, an assist, and a steal . . . but stay tuned for this:

On to Q3: the Knicks led 57-54 midway through the third before Toronto scored seven straight. Brunson's three cut it to 63-62, but the Raptors answered with an 11-0 run, including two Shead threes (the kid was cooking). Barrett had eight points and three assists, and Shead scored nine as Toronto won the quarter 31-20 and took a 74-67 lead into the fourth. Cap scored 13 of New York's 20 points, KAT had four, Bridges made one free throw, and Hart continued to deal with a devastating allergy to buckets.

On to Q4: Immanuel Quickley hit two threes from at least 29 feet to put Toronto up 84-71 with 8:55 left. The Knicks answered with a 12-2 run. Kolek found Anunoby for two quick buckets, and Towns picked Barrett's pocket to send Bridges the other way for a layup. Towns had 15 points and seven rebounds in the quarter, while Brunson went scoreless but handed out five assists.

Down five with under two minutes left, New York scored seven straight. Bridges grabbed the offensive board that set up Shamet's tying three, and Towns put the Knicks ahead with a hook. Towns then hit from 28 feet to make it 95-92 with 20 seconds left, but Barnes answered with a step-back three six seconds later. He had 11 in the quarter and put the clamps on Brunson as time expired.

On to OT: Shamet and Brunson scored seven apiece in the bonus frame. Quickley kept Toronto close with two early buckets, one off a Bridges turnover. Barnes whiffed on free throws with 1:47 left, banked in a three to tie it at 104, and lobbed to Murray-Boyles to tie it again at 106. Out of a timeout, Bridges found Brunson for the go-ahead fadeaway three with 33 seconds left. Anunoby hit both free throws to restore the three-point cushion, Barnes missed a chance to tie it, Towns grabbed his 17th rebound, and then he split a pair at the line to seal the win.

Mo is gassing up his recap. The Knicks stay over the border for game with the Raps on Tuesday. Rest up, Knickerbockers.
