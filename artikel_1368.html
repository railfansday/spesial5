Metasurface waveguides for all-day augmented reality glasses

Nature (2026) Cite this article

Augmented reality (AR) displays, originating from early optical see-through head-mounted systems1, have evolved towards lightweight glasses that seamlessly integrate digital information with the physical world2,3. However, their widespread adoption requires compact optical systems that enable bright, wide-field and comfortable viewing. Diffractive waveguides (WGs) offer a promising approach towards lightweight AR displays yet face inherent trade-offs among field of view (FoV), optical efficiency and eyebox size owing to limited degrees of freedom in light control. Here we show an all-day-wearable AR glass prototype based on spatially varying, interwoven metasurfaces that provide several grating vectors for multidirectional light control. The resulting dual-layer metasurface-based waveguide (meta-WG) achieves a 30° FoV and an 8 × 8-mm2 two-dimensional (2D) eyebox while maintaining an average luminance efficiency of 1,938 nits lm−1 (up to 4,225 nits lm−1) within a 1.5-mm-thick form factor. The prototype produces bright and stable virtual images under outdoor ambient illumination, demonstrating the compatibility of meta-WGs with practical wearable display requirements. By expanding the degrees of freedom for light routing beyond conventional diffractive WGs, our approach provides a scalable framework for balancing efficiency, viewing range and form factor, bringing high-performance AR glasses closer to practical consumer applications.
