Michael Carrick will not survive unless Manchester United change

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Sometimes comments, however over the top, hit home. Michael Carrick will desperately hope the remark from his former Manchester United team-mate Gary Neville is not one of them. That it does not stick. That it does not come to define his time in charge of United this season – if he lasts this season. Or be used in one of those highlight reels, with doom-laden music, to explain the story of this Premier League campaign.

On TV co-commentary duty for Sky, Neville referred to United as “blue mannequins” (they were wearing their away kit) and said that for a period after half-time it was the worst he had ever seen them play since he started watching, aged five. Neville is now 51. That is a lot of games to settle on this conclusion so strongly and, surely, inaccurately.

"They were absolutely mannequins!" Gary Neville says Michael Carrick has to stop defending the Manchester United players. pic.twitter.com/Pa4PqIoRbh

But so passive was their football that is the way he felt in the moment. For a club with United’s “attack, attack, attack” tradition – becoming something of a distant memory as seasons pass – that is a damning as it gets.

Neville’s Sky paymasters will be delighted by his emotive, insulting, headline-grabbing language. But he of course exaggerated a point to make a point. United, and Carrick, were, indeed, far too passive, lacking aggression, urgency and belief. They just do not press. United did, for too long, play like a team of dummies.

Against Fulham they almost played walking football until they conceded, desperation kicked in and they chased the game to salvage a late draw.

It begs the question: what is Carrick doing with this team? Yes, he may have been let down by no defensive signings in the summer – even if he must have bought into the plan to prioritise a midfield rebuild – but he is far from absolved from blame.He will cling to the response to Lisandro Martínez’s own goal, to striking a post twice in the first half from Matheus Cunha and Bruno Fernandes, to managing to avoid a third successive defeat in a week, which would have led to the crisis klaxon being sounded. Carrick can talk about how ridiculous it would be to question his position as head coach. But is it?

We now go into a three-week international break and the Premier League vacuum would have been filled by a debate over Carrick’s future, whether he is up to the job and another existential examination as to where United are heading. And who is to blame.

That still might happen. United did not lose. But they were not remotely good enough, as has been the case for most of the season. Their only league win in five was against newly promoted Ipswich Town, having lost to newly promoted Hull City. They have also drawn at Everton and now at Fulham, who started the day bottom of the table and remain winless. Apart from facing Manchester City, it has hardly been the most taxing of starts. Except they are down in 12th and have not played well at all.

It is not just that. Last year, United revamped their attack. Benjamin Sesko was absent through injury but are Cunha – despite his goal – Bryan Mbeumo and Marcus Rashford actually showing they are good enough? They, too, are passive and do not effect the play. Do not have that killer instinct. There is then an over-reliance on Fernandes. And if he does not do it, then who steps up?

That 15 minutes after half-time that culminated in the Martínez mishap was certainly woeful. But despite going close, United were also second best in the opening period, with Fulham’s 19-year-old academy product Josh King showing why he was so close to a senior England call-up. The forward ran riot, tormenting left-back Luke Shaw and was only denied by Senne Lammens. On more than one occasion, especially with a smart save with his right boot.

The goalkeeper was at the centre of Fulham’s goal, which was a shambles that summed up the state of United, who are just too easy to play against. And, so, Fulham’s captain Calvin Bassey was too strong for Cunha, who threw himself to the ground, looking for a foul, losing the ball from Kobbie Mainoo’s easy pass. The defender strode forward before Lammens parried his low shot, which then bounced off Martínez and back past the goalkeeper and into the net.

It was a Hammersmith End House of Horrors for United. To cap it all, Martínez then tried to blame Lammens who had, frankly, kept United in the game up until that point and could legitimately have told him where to go. And asked why he could not trap a ball and clear.

Fulham will certainly feel their own frustration. But with their new head coach Álvaro Arbeloa and an evolving team – if only striker Gonzalo García was more deadly – there are clear signs of optimism, having also just drawn at Liverpool.

United’s late equaliser was fortunate. Yes, they applied pressure but to no real effect until Cunha’s shot deflected off the boot of Fulham defender David Affengruber as he tried to block, to wrong-foot goalkeeper Bernd Leno, who had also been outstanding. It was credited to Cunha, his first of the season, and the relief from Carrick was understandably palpable.

But, once again, it was not up to the standard needed. And beyond the point, beyond the avoidance of defeat, that is the reality of where United are right now under Carrick who needs to step up, take more responsibility. And, like his team, be far less passive. Otherwise, he will not last.

Might these games offer less possession and more counter-attacking chances? That’s glass half full.

I was purely referring to the fact that they walked around the pitch. I’ve never seen a Manchester United team walk around on the pitch that much.

Michael has got to stop defending them. I get why he wants to defend them, he’s loyal, and there’s a long way to go into the season.

Both teams were the same, I called them mannequins. I’ve never seen United walk around so much out of possession, no alertness, no being on your toes.

I wish he could play those attacking players, but they are going to kill him defensively.

Neville goes on to call that period: “An absolute disgrace, nowhere near good enough.”

When you make some saves in the game you feel very focused. Unfortunately I couldn’t keep out the goal but that is football.

It’s the Premier League and you will have spells where you don’t have it all your own way. To expect total dominance for the whole game, I don’t think anyone gets that in this league.

I thought we should have won the game, I thought we created enough to win the game. The goal we did concede is one of them goals. The boys could have thought it is one of those days but I really like the reaction. We nearly won it in the end. We came to get the win of course, the way it panned out and the way we finished is the positive we take from it.

I think we did a good game. Obviously not an easy game for us, we were in the bottom position but we saw personality with the ball, we were brave. We had moments where we had to defend but in general we did a good game.

We wanted to control the transition and the counter attacks. We know they put too many people inside so we have to control the space behind our midfielders. They sometimes found the spaces, it is a difficult match against a big team and for me they will be near the top of the league by the end of the season.

Now we have to find the first victory. If you see our first five games we have already played Man Utd, Chelsea, at Anfield, at Sunderland. Now we have an October that will be so important for us.

There were so many things for them, you can see it in the foul by [Harry] Maguire. Every decision was for them. I knew after the game against Manchester City that they don’t like Manchester United to lose.

It’s hard to explain, I feel after the first half we always get a lot of chances and create, play well.

In the beginning of the second half they put in more energy than us and we cannot accept this. We always play better after conceding goal, and it cannot be like this.

I don’t think today was about final decisions, we were a bit unlucky, I hit the post, and Bruno hit the post. We need to create more and hope the luck comes and we score. We need to improve in every single position.

It was too slow, Fulham ran more and looked like they wanted to win more. Bassey just eased Cunha out the way [for Fulham’s goal] and looked like he wanted to take the game to Man United and Carrick will be thinking ‘we need to do a lot more’.

It was a game that was a real struggle for both teams. Both lacked confidence. There were challenges for both managers coming into this game, they both need the international break to start their seasons again.

The defensive work from both teams was awful and lacked effort. Carrick will be relieved going back to Manchester with a goal and a point.

The script pretty much writes itself these days. A flicker of optimism and hope of a fresh start giving way to the same old frustrations, complaints and inquests, the cumulative toll of which places an ever greater strain on a club mired in mediocrity but with little reprieve from the expectations that come with bearing such an illustrious name.

Michael Carrick says Pro Ref’s apology over the derby fiasco means little to Manchester United – but warned his players they cannot allow such setbacks to derail their season.

Chief refereeing officer Howard Webb formally apologised to United after the VAR officials Matt Donohue and Blake Antrobus failed to spot Enzo Fernandez was offside before wrongly allowing Erling Haaland’s winning goal to stand in Manchester City’s 1-0 victory at Old Trafford.

United were left shocked by the scale of the blunder, which came with 30 minutes of the game remaining and City already down to 10 men.

But Carrick has told his players not to become preoccupied with such injustices as they bid to overcome their poor start to the season before a crucial trip to Craven Cottage.

“It can have an impact in the moment of time, it can have a big impact, but we can’t let it have an impact on the rest of the season,” the United manager said.

“That’s for us to get back to winning ways as soon as possible. Listen, one-off things can happen, at the time it was a pretty big one, we know that. I don’t think I need to go over that.

“But yeah, I’ve got faith in it [VAR], it’s the system that we work towards and that we use so the apology doesn’t mean an awful lot.”

Carrick added: “I can appreciate and I respect it for them putting their hands up and saying we got that wrong, I think it happens - it does happen.

“But moving forward, yeah, we just have to deal with it, what’s thrown in front of us. And maybe we get one or two decisions on the back of how it balances out - percentages or law of averages or whatever that may be.

“But that was a pretty big one to take and had a huge impact on that game and that’s why it was particularly tough.”

Michael Carrick may have had a point when he said it was “ridiculous” to question his future this early in the season, but this afternoon’s game at Fulham will shape the mood at Manchester United for the next three weeks.

A tally of four points from their first four games of the season is a disappointing start, and Brighton added to the sense of gloom in the League Cup by becoming the first side in 50 years to win from 2-0 down at Old Trafford.

With Champions League football on the agenda, that competition was at the bottom of United’s priority list but the ragged nature of the performance prompted boos around the stadium.

The no-show at Hull and defeat to 10-man City also fall into the “it’s how you lose” category of defeat, while there are widening fissures between the fanbase and the club’s hierarchy over ticketing policy and Sir Jim Ratcliffe’s political interventions.

The mood has also been sour at Fulham in the first few weeks of the season. In fact, they start the game bottom of the Premier League with one point, but do have the incentive of knowing a draw would take them above Spurs.

Following reports of friction between some established players and new head coach Alvaro Arbeloa though, they produced their best performance of the season in a goalless draw at Anfield last week when they had chances to win the game.

Fulham made nine changes in the League Cup in mid-week, but still ran out 3-2 winners at West Ham.
