ways of growing: 20 artists explore plants as design collaborators in lisbon exhibition

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

20 practitioners utilize bacterial cellulose, living mycelium, plant-dyed textiles, and botanical chemistry to frame plants as active collaborators in design and art.

Haenke edited by christina vergopoulou November 16, 2026 5 min

On view at the Gallery of the Faculty of Fine Arts at the University of Lisbon (FBAUL) through November 28th, 2026, Ways of Growing / Modos de crescer presents works by 20 artists, designers, and researchers examining the intersection of plant biodiversity and material experimentation. Serving as the culmination of the two-year Free Radicals research initiative, led by Haenke (Czech Republic) alongside partners in Portugal, Spain, Armenia, Hungary, and Germany, the exhibition originates from three 2025 field residencies conducted in Galicia’s Courel mountains, a permaculture farm in central Portugal, and northern Armenia’s Lori Mountains.

Rather than treating flora solely as passive raw matter, the exhibited works engage botanical organisms, agricultural byproducts, and local ecologies as active design agents. Material and technical investigations span cyanotypes printed on kombucha-derived bacterial cellulose and toned with plant extracts, bio-composites pairing traditional Portuguese crochet with living mycelium, and textiles woven from chestnut-dyed linen. Additional installations integrate botanical chemistry and digital hardware, including olfactory sculptures formulated from eucalyptus ash and essential oils, plant-based analog film processing, acoustic transmission through organic substrates, and a multimedia system linking historic Armenian herbaria with Raspberry Pi microcomputers. Inaugurated during FBAUL’s 190th anniversary, the exhibition coincides with the release of Free Radicals: Attuning to Plants in Creative Practices (Onomatopee), a publication edited by Sasha Střelcová and Linsey Rendell compiling methodologies from 70 practitioners across design, ecology, and material research.

Elena Falomo’s research-led installation combines field recordings, geographical artefacts and storytelling to question which landscapes are prioritised in responses to climate changeEleonora Edreva’s textile-based poem is woven from vintage linen, chestnut dyes, local language and soundIvana Papić uses ecological fiction to question invasive-species narratives in her experimental video essay imagining a love story between a native chestnut tree and an introduced mimosaAlejandra Díaz-Guerra Acedo’s bio-based textile experiment combines traditional Portuguese crochet with living mycelium, allowing fungi to decompose cotton fabrics buried in contrasting soilsMaria Ilieva’s olfactory compositions trace the lifecycle of eucalyptus from fresh foliage and wildfire to industrial pulp processing and paper productionfood artist and chef Aly Beveridge explores how our proximity to land and ingredients create a bodily intimacy that allows us to reconsider our relationship to foodAdam Kvaček’s performative walk explored the familiar language of recreational tourism to investigate Portugal’s industrial eucalyptus landscapes
