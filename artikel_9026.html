Why BIM Still Doesn't Know What Your Building Is Made Of

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

DL: Because nothing connects a material's attributes to all the documents that describe it. The information lives across models, specs, PDFs, spreadsheets and manufacturer sites, and someone moves it between them by hand.

Take a porcelain tile selected early in design. It goes into the schedule under one name, the spec under the manufacturer's product line, and a keynote under shorthand typed before a deadline. Months later the contractor prices the spec, the tile has a sixteen-week lead time, and the approved substitute never makes it back into the drawings. Every document reflects a different stage of the decision, and nothing keeps them aligned.This results in costly errors and change orders.

Material intelligence, and search anyone can use

AD: What led you from BIM to material intelligence, and how do the two connect?

AD: What led you from BIM to material intelligence, and how do the two connect?

DL: I kept watching the same gap persist from the Revit days. BIM gave architects a coordinated model of geometry, then left them to make the highest-consequence decisions, the material selections, off the platform in PDFs and phone calls.

DL: I kept watching the same gap persist from the Revit days. BIM gave architects a coordinated model of geometry, then left them to make the highest-consequence decisions, the material selections, off the platform in PDFs and phone calls.

Material intelligence gives every product selection the treatment Revit gave geometry. We call it the Material Information Model: a single structured record carrying the material's full attribute set, connected to every document where it appears. Make the selection once and the schedule, spec, keynote and drawing all draw from the same source. Join that to the building model and you get what I call material-forward design, where choosing materials is part of designing rather than a chore that comes afterward.

AD: Performance, cost, availability and environmental impact pull in different directions. How can a system help architects compare them without suggesting one "best" material?

DL: The best material selection is not always what was chosen before. The right answer changes by project, client and region. What a system can do is make the trade-offs visible on a common basis. We normalized product data into one schema, so every product can be compared side by side, including HPDs, EPDs, certifications and code requirements. What it changes for owners and architects

AD: Where does the return show up, for owners and for the design team?

AD: Where does the return show up, for owners and for the design team?

DL: It comes from every stakeholder working from the same record early, when decisions are still inexpensive to change. Materials cost 50% of every building, yet owners rarely see those selections while they're being made. With one source, they can see what's selected, at what cost and against what standard, in real time. A choice that's over budget or off the owner's standard gets caught at selection, not in a change order months later.
