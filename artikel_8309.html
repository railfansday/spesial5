Te Araroa day 12: An exciting side-quest!

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

4.5km, Waitangi Holiday Park to Paihia Top 10

Last winter I did a week of maintenance on the tall ship R. Tucker Thompson, which is berthed in Opua. The crew told me to let them know when I was passing through on TA and they would help out how they could. I almost didn’t text, as I hate to ask for favours, but I figured maybe I could get my friends a tour of the ship and if I was really lucky perhaps I could sleep on board on the way through. I got a reply right away; they were sailing on Tuesday and did I want to join? How could I refuse that offer! Of course I said yes, so on Tuesday morning I found myself taking the ferry from Paihia over to Russell, where the trip would depart from.

A fun rainbow bench on the way to the ferry 🏳️‍🌈

Waiting in Russell for the ship to arrive

On the quay, I chatted to my fellow sailors a bit while we waited. As I boarded, I recognised the skipper Pete, because we had spent a day varnishing the fore mast together last winter. We set off from the quay, and I eagerly stepped up when they asked for volunteers to help set the sails. It was great to pull some ropes again! The plan was to engine sail to an island, where we would go ashore, have lunch, swim, and then sail back. Shortly after departing, the crew gave a safety briefing for climbing the rigging, and I got harnessed up.

One of the other passengers snapped some photos of me up the rigging

It’s been a while since I climbed the rigging on a ship under sail, and it was quite breezy. But I was pleased that I didn’t feel too scared, although I didn’t want to climb too high.

Once I got down from the rigging, other passengers started noticing dolphins close to the ship. They were swimming right alongside and leaping out of the water literally a metre from us. It was so cool to see that! I didn’t manage to get photos because I was scared to drop my phone over the side.

Soon we arrived at Motuarohia Island, which was a postcard-perfect Bay of Islands view. There was a track up to a lookout, which felt extremely easy for me without my pack for once – it felt like I was floating. The views were great and I stood up there for a while admiring them.

Some people were swimming in the bay, but unfortunately I hadn’t brought my towel across to the island with me. Eventually the crew brought the RIB back to pick us up and lunch was waiting on board. Most people were eating sausages, but the cook had prepared some falafels for me. I asked if there were any other veggies, and she said she didn’t think so. I left a couple of falafels just in case, and good thing I did! A German lady was in the next batch of passengers to come back from the island, and it seemed like she maybe hadn’t informed them in advance that she didn’t eat meat.

After eating lunch, some people swung off a rope from the rigging into the water. That was a bit much for me, but I was regretting not swimming earlier so I climbed down the ladder into the water. It was pretty chilly, but definitely refreshing.

Then we started heading back, and set all the sails – which of course meant pulling more ropes. All too soon, we were approaching Russell and taking down the sails. There was due to be a ferry back to Paihia shortly after arrival, which I just about managed to make. It was an awesome day out, and really cool to chat with all the crew, see the sights, and most importantly be under sail again! The weather was also much better than the forecast had made it sound.

Back in Paihia, I made a quick stop in the Four Square to grab a couple more snacks for the next section. There wouldn’t be a shop for a couple of days and I wanted to be sure I would have enough.

I hadn’t realised, but the next section of track was tidally dependant and the tide was now all the way in. I had to walk along the road instead, which did at least have a pavement but wasn’t the most scenic. After a bit of road walking, I reached some track which went through the bush above the shoreline. It was a nice walk, and felt easy after my restful day. Soon I arrived at the Paihia Top 10 holiday park, which has a special rate for TA hikers. I ended up between Georgia, who I met the previous night, and Harry, who I had last seen at the Krishna Sanctuary before Raetea Forest. It was a cool tent spot, right on the water, and I ate dinner, chatted a bit with Georgia, and went to bed.
