Painful Coventry defeat will send shudder of fear through Newcastle

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Newcastle United suffered a painful, chastening defeat, comprehensively beaten by Coventry City in a performance that should send a shudder of fear through Tyneside.

It was a dreadful night for the visitors, who conceded four goals for the second successive away game. The manner in which they fell apart was damning. Whatever the supposed style of play, it is not good to watch: long balls are pumped forward for short strikers to chase or compete for in the air against far taller centre-backs. Away from home, it is becoming ineffective.

Newcastle are too easy to create chances against and there is not much that gets you excited when they attack.

Matthias Jaissle’s side did create chances against Coventry, but are not clinical and do not inspire confidence they can be with the players they have. Defensively, they escaped earlier in the season, but are now being exposed and punished. “Not good enough in both boxes,” was Jaissle’s blunt verdict. “To concede eight goals in two away games is not good enough.”

Matthias Jaissle on this evening's defeat at the Coventry Building Society Arena. https://t.co/216NW6Laz8

Newcastle’s misery was in stark contrast to the joyous celebrations from Coventry. This was a thoroughly deserved first home win of the season for Frank Lampard’s side.

“It was an outstanding performance,” the manager said. “We knew we would have to suffer at times against a team like Newcastle. But we were really good at the basics, winning the second balls, winning the duels, those moments are critical. It’s not always about pretty patterns, it’s about work ethic.”

The win lifts Coventry level on points with Manchester United and will give them fresh confidence at this level. This could be the start of something special for them.

As for Newcastle, this raises uncomfortable questions about their summer recruitment, with so many expensive players on the bench or playing badly. It also means people will query whether Jaissle’s implementation of “vertical football” is working. The fact Lampard talked so openly about winning the duels to set up the win will anger the German.

Jaissle can point to two bad misses from one of the new arrivals, Matias Fernandez-Pardo, a £51m buy from Lille, in the first half. But his team are too easy to play through and the defence is an accident waiting to happen.

Even after Sven Botman’s stunning strike offered them a way back into the game, they capitulated at the back as Lewis Hall – who was off colour all evening – and Joelinton’s mistakes gift-wrapped a third for Ellis Simms. A fourth followed for Stephen Mfuni.

You can get a sense of what sort of mode a team are in within the first few passages of play and Newcastle were not sharp or precise. It was sloppy.

That was certainly true of goalkeeper Lukas Hornicek, who was off his line in no-man’s land trying to snuff out a through ball that he was not going to get. He stood up to deflect it away, but then rashly chased after it, diving at full stretch at the feet of Coventry’s Sidiki Cherif and failing to get the ball. Jack Rudoni coolly sent the Czech goalkeeper the wrong way from the spot.

Newcastle had been awful in their last away game at Leeds, another 4-1 thrashing, and there were many of the same faults on display.

It was not a good first half for Fernandez-Pardo up top. They are mystified in Belgium why he believes he is a centre-forward rather than a winger and he did not do himself any favours here. Newcastle’s best move of the game resulted in Lewis Miley playing in Jacob Murphy down the left. His cutback was perfect for Fernandez-Pardo to sweep home at the near post but instead of trying to make contact, he dummied it, thinking Harvey Barnes was better placed. He was not. It was a baffling piece of centre-forward play.

It had been the easiest 40 minutes Lampard’s side have had since they were promoted, but Newcastle still should have scored before the break.

Nico González spotted the run of Fernandez-Pardo through the middle. He wriggled clear of Bobby Thomas and sat down goalkeeper Carl Rushworth, but the Coventry centre-back got back to block his shot.

Newcastle pressed the self-destruct button early in the second half. A long throw into the box was half cleared. There was a half-hearted attempt to close Cherif down from Yoane Wissa and then a shot that somehow went through a crowd of three Newcastle defenders, including the legs of Botman, and nestled in the bottom corner.

A win for Newcastle United this evening will take Matthias Jaissle’s side up to fourth in the Premier League and vindicate the members of the club’s hierarchy who insisted they had appointed an elite manager to replace Eddie Howe.

It will also suggest they got their summer rebuild right and everything will point to an exciting future for a new look team.

Yet, should Newcastle lose against a Coventry City side – newly promoted to the top flight and yet to win at home this season – ripples of discontent will spread through the fanbase and everything about the summer, from managerial appointment to player recruitment will be called into question.

That may seem like an odd thing to write given how wildly different those reactions are but that is where Newcastle find themselves at the moment. We do not actually know if they are any good or what the expectations should be for a young team with a rookie Premier League head coach.

There remains an undercurrent of anxiety among supporters who have seen so many of their best players leave over the last 12 months and be replaced by young, emerging talent from across Europe.

The 4-1 thrashing at Leeds in their last away game was a painful reminder of the team’s vulnerability. They were bullied and outclassed.

Newcastle have not put together an impressive 90-minute performance since the opening day draw with Liverpool, yet that defeat to Leeds was also the only game they have lost. in all competitions. We will know a lot more about them after this. This is a game they are expected to win, so the ramifications of a defeat will be more severe.

When Brian Kilcline was at Notts County, the manager Jimmy Sirrel became a little perturbed by the nickname his young centre-back had carried since his school days. It might have been nice alliteration, but “Killer Kilcline” was not exactly the image he wanted his team to portray.

“Also the lads shouting, ‘get him Killer’ tended to alert referees,” Kilcline recalls.

So Sirrel decided he would put it to the County fans. He organised a competition to establish a better moniker for his foursquare defender.

“You know what they came up with?” Kilcline says. “Curly Wurly. Like the chocolate bar. It was to do with my hair, which was naturally curled. By the way, it was never a perm. But I mean, come on. I can’t imagine Curly Wurly would have intimidated anyone.”

So bunched is the table, though, that victory tonight would propel them up to fourth. Coventry, by contrast, are 19th after one win and four defeats from their opening five games.
