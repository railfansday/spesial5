Bear of the Day: DraftKings (DKNG)

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The house doesn't always win when prediction markets crash the party.

DraftKings (DKNG) is a Zacks Rank #5 (Strong Sell) that runs one of the largest online sportsbook and iGaming platforms in the U.S.

The boom that followed sports betting legalization has faded, and growth is slowing just as the company spends heavily to fight a new threat from prediction markets like Kalshi. After a wide third-quarter miss and a sharp guidance cut, analysts are lowering their estimates and the stock is trading close to 2026 lows.

Based in Boston, DraftKings (DKNG) got its start in daily fantasy sports in 2012. It has since expanded into online sports betting, iGaming (online casino), lottery services and, most recently, its Predictions platform, which offers event contracts.

DraftKings grew rapidly as state after state legalized online sports betting, creating a steady stream of new markets and customers. Much of that initial expansion opportunity has now played out, shifting the focus to gaining market share in existing states and generating growth from newer products.

DraftKings has a market cap of $9 billion and trades at 33 times forward earnings. It has Zacks Style Scores of “F” for Value, “B” for Growth and “D” for Momentum.

On November 6, DraftKings reported a third-quarter loss of 0.53persharebelowtheZacksConsensusEstimate-0.21. Revenue of $1.33 billion missed the $1.38 billion consensus, and the adjusted EBITDA loss widened to $316.9 million from $126.5 million a year ago.

The company also cut its fiscal 2026 outlook and revenue guidance fell to a range of $6.35 billion to $6.55 billion, below the $6.68 billion consensus. Adjusted EBITDA guidance was cut to $175 million to $275 million from a prior $700 million to $900 million.

Most of that EBITDA cut reflects spending on Predictions, but the core business slipped too. Core EBITDA guidance was lowered to about $900 million at the midpoint from roughly $1 billion. Management blamed customer-friendly sports outcomes for about 60% of that and heavier iGaming promotions for most of the rest.

The core numbers show a maturing business. Monthly unique payers rose 13% to 4.1 million, but average revenue per payer grew only about 3% to $109. Like-for-like handle grew 12%. These are respectable numbers, but they’re not the kind of growth that justified the stock’s valuation during the legalization boom.

Prediction markets are the new wild card, with platforms like Kalshi offering sports event contracts that operate outside the state-by-state sportsbook framework. This puts them in direct competition for the same bettors.

DraftKings has responded by building its own Predictions business, and it isn’t cheap. The company expects to put $650 million to $700 million of adjusted EBITDA into Predictions this year and another $250 million to $350 million in fiscal 2027. The business has grown fast, with more than 1.6 million customers so far this year, but it isn’t expected to turn EBITDA positive until fiscal 2028.

The regulatory backdrop adds another layer of risk. Management’s own underwriting allows for the possibility that sports contracts are shut down in July 2028, and it said investment levels remain sensitive to court developments. If prediction markets win in court, the competition intensifies. If they lose, much of that spending loses its purpose.

Analyst sentiment has turned decisively negative, with no upward estimate revisions over the past 30 days. Analysts have made two downward revisions to fiscal 2026 estimates and three to fiscal 2027.

The fiscal 2026 Zacks Consensus Estimate has fallen 46% over the past month, from $1.00 to $0.54. That now implies an 18% decline from last year's $0.66.

Near-term expectations have weakened as well. The fourth-quarter consensus has dropped 39% in the past 30 days, from $0.75 to $0.46.

Fiscal 2027 estimates have fallen from $1.75 to $1.43. While that still implies a substantial rebound, the Most Accurate Estimate of $1.33 sits below consensus, suggesting further downward revisions could be ahead.

The stock is down 50% on the year and trending towards its 2022 lows.  The moving averages are likely candidates for resistance until the fundamental story changes.

The 50-day is at $20.65 and the 200-day is $23.50 and should be used as selling points for those trying to play a bounce. Longer there is a clear road lower for the stock, with the next support level around the $11 level.

The easy growth from new state launches is behind DraftKings. Core results are being hit by bad sports outcomes and heavy promotions, while a costly push into prediction markets has wiped out most of this year’s EBITDA. With estimates heading lower and the Kalshi risk hanging over the industry, investors may want to stay away until the picture clears up.
