Barnsley have a far bigger problem than Daniel Stendel right now

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Daniel Stendel was the returning hero in the summer when he arrived back at Barnsley for a second spell as manager.

However, it has been anything but a success, with the club sitting in the relegation zone and in the midst of a long winless run.

The Reds are only above bottom-side Bromley, who have parted company with their manager, Andy Woodman, on goal difference. They have just two wins to their name, with three draws, and eight losses from their opening 13 matches of the league campaign.

Burton Albion, who shipped six in one match against Milton Keynes Dons, and Bromley, who saw Sheffield Wednesday score seven against them, are the only League One teams with worse defensive records than Stendel’s men.

At the other end of the pitch, it is no better. Barnsley have scored just 13 goals, with fellow relegation-zone side Peterborough United, AFC Wimbledon, and outliers Bradford City the only sides to have scored less.

Barnsley have claimed just two of the last 27 points available to them, and despite thumping wins against lower division sides in the FA Cup and EFL Trophy, have not won in league action since early September.

The statistics do not make for better reading either. The Tykes are 18th for xG conceded and have underperformed their xG created by more than five goals, according to FotMob.

The overall xG table places Barnsley 19th, four places higher than their current position, but that is still far below the hopes and aspirations of the Reds’ faithful.

Stendel was a promotion winning manager in his first stint at Oakwell, before the fickle nature of football reared its ugly head and the board sacked him following a winless run.

The German has enjoyed limited success since his 2019 departure, at Hearts in Scotland and Nancy in France, before returning to Hannover 96 to coach their reserve team.

Daniel Stendel's first vs second Barnsley spell (source: Transfermarkt)

The fixture list shows no mercy either, with Stendel having to prepare his side for a South Yorkshire derby against Dave Challinor and his Owls, before a trip to imperious Bradford City just days later.

The obvious answer might be to sack Stendel, after a nine-game winless run in League One.

But, that would only make the German a scapegoat for much deeper and more troubling problems. Barnsley have underinvested for years and failed to replace their departed stars.

A suitable replacement for Davis Keillor-Dunn, who is now at Wednesday on loan from Wrexham, was never sought, nor was it for David McGoldrick after his exit from the club more recently. Adam Phillips left in the summer for a fellow third tier side, with Cameron McGeehan adding a strong midfield option without adequately and directly replacing the skills that Phillips brought to the table.

Daniel Stendel is already under pressure at Barnsley after returning to the club in the summer

The Barnsley squad has seen repeated underinvestment by a board that has lost touch and damaged almost beyond repair its connection to the fanbase at the heart of the Tykes.

Removing Stendel and adding another name to the revolving door of head coaches and manager might be the easiest option, but it is far from being the solution to the gaping holes that have grown and been allowed to fester under sub-par direction.
