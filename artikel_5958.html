Etihad Launches U.S. Credit Cards With Elite Status—But Most Americans Should Skip Them

Etihad has launched two U.S. credit cards, including a $499 card that offers Gold status after a single Etihad purchase. That’s the sort of benefit that usually gets my attention. But I wouldn’t recommend either card to most U.S. consumers.

Qatar Airways status that comes the first year with their premium card can get you into American Airlines and Alaska Airlines lounges on domestic trips (and American Airlines international business class lounge access). Emirates status lets you book first class awards that non-status members can’t. Etihad’s status doesn’t do either of those things, and you can earn their miles faster with other cards.

The two cards launched November 20 through First National Bank of Omaha. They’re Etihad’s first U.S. cobrands, with annual fees of $99 and $499.

Here are the main differences in the offers: Benefit   Etihad Guest Visa Signature   Etihad Guest Premier Visa Signature Annual fee   $99   $499 Initial bonus   40,000 miles: 10,000 after first purchase, plus 30,000 after $3,000 in spending within the first three billing cycles   60,000 miles: 20,000 after first purchase, plus 40,000 after $7,000 in spending within the first three billing cycles Etihad purchases   3 miles per dollar   5 miles per dollar Partner airline purchases   2 miles per dollar   3 miles per dollar Other purchases outside the U.S.   2 miles per dollar   2 miles per dollar Other U.S. purchases   1 mile per dollar   1 mile per dollar The initial offers require applying by February 3, 2027. Both cards waive foreign transaction fees. The Premier card also includes Priority Pass, a Global Entry or TSA PreCheck application fee credit every four years, and 3,000 miles after the first Etihad purchase and at each card anniversary. The status rules are: $99 card: Silver after your first Etihad purchase within 90 days. Keep Silver with $20,000 in annual card spending or $3,000 in Etihad purchases. $499 card: Silver at account opening, then Gold after your first Etihad purchase within 90 days. Keep Gold with $40,000 in annual card spending or $8,000 in Etihad purchases. Otherwise, you get Silver. Both earn one tier mile per dollar spent. The cheaper card caps this at 25,000 tier miles per calendar year, while Premier has no cap. These are status credits, separate from the miles you redeem for travel. So you can spend towards Etihad status. I like bonusing non-U.S. purchases, but if that’s meaningful to you then the Atmos™ Rewards Summit Visa Infinite® credit card is far better since it earns unlimited 3 points for every $1 spent on eligible foreign transactions. And I value Alaska’s miles far more than Etihad’s. But Etihad Gold Isn’t Qatar Gold When Qatar Airways launched its U.S. cards, the premium card’s first year of Gold status was immediately useful even to someone who never planned to fly Qatar. Qatar Gold is oneworld Sapphire. That means lounge access when flying American Airlines and Alaska Airlines, including domestic trips. It even gets you into American’s business class Flagship lounges on an eligible same-day itinerary. Etihad isn’t in an alliance. Their airline partnerships don’t turn Etihad Gold into an Admirals Club membership or give you those oneworld benefits on domestic American flights. The Premier card’s Priority Pass membership is a big nothingburger. Priority Pass is a drug on the market. Everyone offers Priority Pass. And Emirates gave its credit cards a purpose when it restricted first class awards to elite members. You need Silver status or higher to book a first class Classic Reward. You don’t need an Etihad card’s status just to redeem miles for the award you want. Etihad status can help when you actually fly Etihad, especially in coach. Lounge access and other benefits depend on your tier. But someone who mainly redeems miles for premium cabins already gets many of the things status would otherwise provide with their ticket. I’d Earn The Miles With A Different Card You don’t need an Etihad credit card to earn Etihad miles. Capital One, for instance, transfers to Etihad Guest at 1:1, and Venture and Venture X earn two miles per dollar on ordinary purchases. Citi and Bilt also offer transfers to Etihad. Put $40,000 of unbonused spend on the Etihad Premier card and earn 40,000 redeemable Etihad miles, while satisfying the spending requirement to keep Gold. Put that same spending on Venture X and earn 80,000 Capital One miles, which can become 80,000 Etihad miles. Or you can transfer them to a different airline. This is why I separate a card’s initial bonus and benefits from whether it’s worth using for spending. An Etihad bonus might help someone finish off an account for a specific award. That doesn’t make the card a good place to put their continuing spend. The narrow case I’d consider is someone who regularly buys Etihad coach tickets, will use the status benefits, and can retain Gold through $8,000 of Etihad purchases rather than diverting $40,000 of ordinary spending. For someone who wants Etihad miles for an occasional business or first class redemption, I’d earn transferable points with a better card. I wouldn’t pay $499 and give up half my everyday mileage earning just for Etihad Gold.
