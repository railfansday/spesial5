World Kindness Day 2026: what it is and 7 ways to join in

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Clinically reviewed by Dr. Chris Mosunic, PhD, RD, MBA

The barista is ringing up the person in front of you when their card gets declined. You lean over, tap your phone, and cover the $4. They turn around, flustered, and thank you twice. The line keeps moving, but your morning feels a bit lighter.

Moments like this are what World Kindness Day is all about. Every year on November 13, it celebrates how acts of goodwill can brighten an ordinary day, for those receiving and performing them.

Read on for the story behind the day, what the research says about kindness and mental health, and some simple ways to take part, whatever your schedule looks like.

World Kindness Day is an unofficial global observance that encourages acts of kindness, big and small. In 2026, it falls on Friday, November 13. The day was first observed in 1998 and grew out of the World Kindness Movement, a coalition of kindness organizations rather than a single founder.

The coalition formed at a conference in Tokyo, and it included founding members from Japan, Singapore, Australia, Canada, Thailand, the U.K., and the U.S. They chose November 13 because it was the conference’s opening day and the 35th anniversary of Japan’s Small Kindness Movement. Today, classrooms, offices, and communities around the world honor the day.

You’ve probably felt it: you do something kind and your mood instantly lifts. Researchers have studied this effect, and here’s what they’ve found.

In a 2023 study from The Ohio State University, 122 adults with moderate to severe symptoms of depression, anxiety, and stress were assigned to one of three activities for several weeks. They either did acts of kindness, planned social activities, or practiced cognitive reappraisal, which means looking at a stressful event from a new angle.

All three activities helped, but kindness did more than cognitive reappraisal to ease depression and anxiety. The kindness group was also the only one that reported feeling more connected to others.

Other research points the same way. A review of 27 studies covering more than 4,000 people showed that performing acts of kindness gave people a modest but noticeable boost in their well-being.

Kindness may also lighten a hard day. In a two-week diary study of 77 adults, people tracked their stress, their mood, and how they helped others each day. Stress usually dragged positive emotion down, but on high-stress days when people helped more than usual, their mood held steady. In other words, a little extra kindness seemed to cushion the blow of a stressful day.

A note on the research: Most of this research is short-term and relies on self-reported mood, so we can’t say how long the benefits last. Kindness can support your well-being, but it doesn’t replace professional care. If anxiety, low mood, or stress lasts for weeks, it’s worth talking to a doctor.

You don’t need a grand gesture. These seven ideas fit into a regular day, whether you’re at home, at work, or anywhere in between. You can also adjust any of them to fit your time, energy, and budget.

There’s probably someone on your mind — a friend you know is having a hard time, a relative you haven’t caught up with lately, or an old coworker you miss.

Mention the moment you thought of them, like “Saw a dog that looked like yours and had to say hi. Hope you’re doing okay.” Add “no need to reply” at the end, in case they’re feeling down or overwhelmed and might not have the energy for a back-and-forth.

Leave a short review for a small business, or send a note to a service worker who made your day. Write what you appreciated, such as the waiter who kept checking on your table or the mechanic who explained the repair without talking down to you.

For a coworker, send a note to their manager, who may not otherwise hear about their good work.

A quick “love your shoes” is nice, but a more specific compliment shows someone that you’re really paying attention, and that you care. Praise someone for how patient they were with a difficult customer or how useful their point was in the meeting.

It takes only a few seconds, but noticing the details can mean more to someone than you might expect.

Look for small things that would make someone’s day better and take care of one: carry the heavy bag, refill the empty printer, or take on the household chore nobody wants to do.

When you spot a need early, you can spare someone the awkwardness of asking.

Buy lunch for your colleague, round up a tip, or add a friend’s drink to your order. Set an amount before you head out, so a kind impulse doesn’t turn into a money worry later.

If your budget is a concern, the next idea includes free ways to help.

Donate clothes you don’t wear to a shelter, give to a cause you care about, or sign up for a volunteer shift. When you donate, check what the organization needs first, since staff and volunteers have to sort through anything they can’t use.

Many organizations also offer remote tasks, like writing cards or tutoring online.

Kindness toward yourself counts, and it’s often overlooked. Notice how you talk to yourself when something goes wrong, then ask whether you’d say it that way to a friend. If you wouldn’t, here’s a quick way to try a kinder tone.

Try: A short self-compassion pause. Place a hand on your chest, take one slow breath, and name how you feel (“this is a lot right now”). Then say what you’d tell a friend in the same situation (“that sounds hard”).

The World Kindness Movement created the day to encourage kind acts around the world. For many people, it’s also an invitation to do the kind thing they’ve been putting off, like thanking a neighbor.

World Kindness Day in 2026 is on Friday, November 13. It falls on November 13 every year, so only the day of the week changes.

World Kindness Day is on November 13 because it was the opening day of the first World Kindness Movement conference in Tokyo, and the 35th anniversary of Japan’s Small Kindness Movement.

No single person started World Kindness Day. The World Kindness Movement, a coalition of kindness organizations from seven countries, launched the day in Tokyo.

No, World Kindness Day and Random Acts of Kindness Day are two separate observances. World Kindness Day is November 13, while Random Acts of Kindness Day is February 17. They share the same spirit, so you’re welcome to celebrate both.

Studies suggest kindness can be good for you. Acts of kindness have been linked to better mood, lower anxiety, and stronger connection with others.

In one diary study, stressful days usually made people feel less happy and upbeat. But on days when people helped others more than usual, that drop in positive feelings was smaller.

Kindness supports well-being, but it doesn’t replace care. Speak to a doctor if anxiety, stress, or low mood lasts for weeks.

If you’re feeling drained, it’s okay to start with yourself. A kind word to yourself counts, and some days it’s all you have energy for. When you have more to give, something small and thoughtful is plenty, like holding the door for someone or letting someone go ahead of you in line.

There’s no official color for World Kindness Day, so you can wear whatever feels good. Some schools and groups pick a color for their own events.
