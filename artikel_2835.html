New Devils Goalie Connor Hellebuyck Says Threats Made Against Family Led to Trade Demand: ‘Crossed the Line’

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

New Jersey Devils goalie Connor Hellebuyck told reporters that incidents that were "more than online death threats" toward his family led to his request for a trade from the Winnipeg Jets

Hellebuyck led Team USA to an Olympic gold medal over Team Canada and received boisterous praise from President Donald Trump

"I needed to be a good husband, a good father, and do what's best for my family and my career," the goalie told reporters on Tuesday, Nov. 10

New Jersey Devils goalie Connor Hellebuyck claimed there were incidents toward his family that "crossed the line" and were "more than online death threats," which led to his request to be traded from the Winnipeg Jets.

The drama and mystery surrounding the former league MVP's decision to force his way out of Winnipeg after winning the 2026 Olympic gold medal with Team USA has arguably been the biggest story surrounding the NHL this season.

"I don't want this to get out of hand," Hellebuyck, 33, told reporters during a press conference ahead of the Jets-Devils clash in Winnipeg on Tuesday, Nov. 10.

The 22-minute press conference wavered between tense and lighthearted as reporters tried to clarify allegations made by Hellebuyck in recent months about how he and his family were treated in Canada following this year's Winter Olympics.

Hellebuyck, a father of two, refused to discuss details about what allegedly happened but said his family experienced "a few moments that I don't want to see happen again."

The league's most decorated goaltender said those incidents, as well as his wish for "a change in scenery," led to his trade request in August. "I've alluded to this a little bit," Hellebuyck said Tuesday. "This is a very complex decision and a very difficult one. There's no one singular moment that wasn't important in all of this decision-making, and this is over a span of time."

Hellebuyck continued: "I will say, with all the uncertainty I was experiencing, there was, you know, some moments and some things that happened to my family that, for me, crossed the line and as a husband and a father, I felt made that decision very certain — that I needed to be a good husband, a good father, and do what's best for my family and my career."

Connor HellebuyckCredit: Gregory Shamus/Getty

The three-time Vezina Trophy winner led Team USA to a gold medal over Team Canada in men's ice hockey at the Winter Olympics in Milan back in February. Hellebuyck hinted that incidents following the Olympics led him to seek to move his family back to the U.S.

Hellebuyck also received criticism in Canada after he and other Team USA members accepted President Donald Trump's invitation to visit the White House following the Winter Games.

Trump, 80, also fawned over Hellebuyck following the 2026 Winter Games, saying he wanted to award the goalie the Presidential Medal of Freedom. The president's praise over the goaltender also came amid ongoing tensions between the U.S. and Canada over Trump's tariffs against the longtime American ally.

Hellebuyck said he's aware Trump's comments may have "put a target" on him among Canadians upset over the gold medal game and the country's political relations.

"Those are accomplishments I will never apologize for," Hellebuyck said of the Olympic fallout. "I know the sensitivity about what's going on between the U.S. and Canada, and I've never gotten into politics deep with anyone because I'm a hockey player, and I play hockey."

Connor HellebuyckCredit: Mark Blinch/NHLI via Getty

Hellebuyck refused to report to Winnipeg's training camp after requesting a trade in late August, leading to the team suspending him for the first part of the NHL season. The Jets eventually traded the Commerce, Mich., native to New Jersey on Oct. 23.

The drama surrounding Hellebuyck was reignited this week when the Devils announced he would be held out of his return to Winnipeg on Tuesday.

Jets coach Scott Arniel said earlier this week that Hellebuyck "gave up" on his Winnipeg teammates, while other teammates expressed disappointment about the Devils holding him out of Tuesday night's game.

Connor HellebuyckCredit: Steven Ryan/NHLI via Getty

Hellebuyck said he understood his former team's reaction to his trade request and was adamant that he didn't want the issue to "ruin the relationship" he had with Winnipeg fans.

Reporters asked Hellebuyck about why the Jets have said the goalie never told them about specific incidents he's since alluded to; however, the goalie again refused to elaborate on what allegedly happened.

Hellebuyck also avoided answering a reporter's question about whether there was "evidence" of the incidents he's alluded to that allegedly led to his trade request.

"I feel like if I answer that question, it's pinning someone against each other, and I don't want…I don't want this to be about that," Hellebuyck said before the press conference ended. "I've made my decision. I've made it clear, and I've sacrificed a lot to live by that decision. If that's not enough for you, then I don't…I don't know if I can have any other answers for that."
