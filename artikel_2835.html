Bucks Reacts Survey: Are your vibes still high?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Welcome to SB Nation Reacts, a survey of fans across the NBA. Throughout the year we ask questions of the most plugged-in Bucks fans and fans across the country.

Back in September, we gauged your enthusiasm for the season ahead, asking if you were more or less excited for this season than last. As it turned out, you were much more excited for this season. Since then, however, there's been a whole lotta losing. But has that been enough to kill your vibes, or, as they have for resident commenter Boom_Shakalaka, have the young Bucks done enough to fill your cup?

As it stands, the Bucks sit 15th in the Eastern Conference with a 2-16 record. In the process, they've earned the not-so-enviable records of worst start to a season in franchise history and second-longest losing streak in franchise history (13 in a row, prior to last night's win against the Toronto Raptors). Unsurprisingly, they also have the second-worst point differential in the entire league (-11.4 PPG), ahead of only the 2-16 and seemingly always-inept Sacramento Kings (-12.5 PPG). Yet, despite Sacramento's lengthy run of failures—only twice in the last 20 years have they had a winning record—the Kings have the better average attendance, attracting an average crowd of 14,345 people this year to the Bucks' 13,296 (which ranks dead last).

On a player level, there's been plenty to gripe about too. Last night aside, Myles Turner has been okay at best and woeful at worst, while Kyle Kuzma continues his Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde play to the ire of fans. Jaime Jaquez Jr. has cooled off considerably after some hot form early, and Ryan Rollins has been a shell of the player he was last season, at least on the offensive end. For all his talent, Kel'el Ware has been similarly underwhelming.

Despite the losing, there's been plenty for Bucks fans to cheer (okay, quietly bump their fists) about. Rookies Brayden Burries and Nate Ament are playing more often and looking even better than we could have hoped for—especially at this early stage of the season. Burries, in particular, has been outstanding, playing every game (including 10 starts) and putting up 11.4 PPG, 5.7 RPG, 2.5 APG, 0.9 SPG and just 1.4 TOV on .455/.313/.741 shooting splits. Beyond the numbers, he's looked every bit the seasoned pro, consistently one of Milwaukee's better rebounders and downhill scorers, and, at times, even looking like the team's best player. Ament, while not as spectacular, has arguably been even more surprising. He's played in 16 games (two starts) and has proven to be a reliable shooter, opportunistic rim finisher, and legitimately good on-ball defender on his way to averages of 7.6 PPG and 3.1 RPG on .453/.349/.750 shooting. Beyond those two, Kasparas Jakučionis has had more than a few moments—including a Brew Hoop MVB and Defensive Belt double—while Tyler Herro has largely been good and, on occasion, unbelievable. Others have had moments too.

Recently, despite the continually fluctuating and at times infuriating rotation, the Bucks have also looked much more cohesive as a whole. They had a very real shot at winning against the Portland Trail Blazers, should have won—or at least gone into overtime—against the Philadelphia 76ers, and did put it all together in an uplifting win over the Raptors in Canada. Sweetening that win was Turner's play. Where he'd looked out of sorts earlier in the season—perhaps most noticeably against the same Raptors team—he was decisive in this one: engaged on both ends; dynamic as a spacer and finisher inside; and a defensive presence, both as a rim protector and rebounder. That this performance came against the very team that could potentially seek Turner's services in exchange for draft capital makes it even sweeter. Keep it coming, big fella!

Taking all of this into consideration—the "little wins" highs and the lower-than-Lil-Jon-can-make-you-get lows—just how much are you enjoying this season?
