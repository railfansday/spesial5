SBI and Cambodia Plan a Yen Stablecoin Trial Behind Everyday Remittances

SBI Holdings and the National Bank of Cambodia plan to test a yen stablecoin for transfers between Japan and Cambodia. The proposed design puts JPYSC between financial institutions, leaving customers with a familiar way to send and receive money. That makes this a test of payment infrastructure behind the scenes, rather than a proposal that remittance users manage a new crypto wallet themselves.

SBI’s November 13 announcement describes a closed-loop proof of concept examining regulation, systems and commercial viability. The group will provide banking, remittance and token infrastructure, while Cambodia’s central bank supplies regulatory guidance and reviews the work. The proposal is intended to complement the country’s Bakong payment system.

The customer-facing distinction is significant. A sender usually wants to know the fee, exchange rate and amount the recipient can use. If the institutions change their settlement method without changing that experience, the technology succeeds only if it improves the complete payment journey.

A quicker transfer between two providers could still leave a recipient waiting if local payout or currency conversion is unavailable. The trial therefore needs to evaluate both the digital settlement leg and its connection to ordinary balances.

The June launch announcement identifies SBI Shinsei Trust Bank as JPYSC’s issuer and SBI VC Trade as its distributor. Initial use was limited to accounts at SBI VC Trade, with external-wallet transfers unavailable at that stage. That historical access condition should not be mistaken for proof that every proposed cross-border use is already operational.

The trust-based structure, discussed in NewsBTC’s earlier JPYSC coverage, distinguishes the token from a floating-price cryptocurrency. Its intended reference is the yen, while the rights and operational route depend on the issuer and service terms. A customer paying a Cambodian expense still has a currency-conversion question even if the intermediate settlement asset maintains its yen value.

NewsBTC has covered other proposed uses, including linking JPYSC with tokenized investment funds. A common settlement asset can serve different workflows, but each connection needs its own implementation and permissions.

SBI also disclosed a distinct agreement to test tokenized Cambodian government bonds. That experiment is planned on Canton Network, with the National Bank of Cambodia managing bondholder information. The announcement leaves service details and timing to further discussion.

The two projects should not be combined into a claim that investors can buy Cambodian bonds with JPYSC. Neither release establishes such a transaction or a technical bridge between Canton and Bakong. One examines remittance settlement; the other examines how a government-bond holding and transfer can be represented.

Tokenizing a bond changes its representation and transfer process; it does not remove the issuer’s repayment obligation or the investor’s need to understand it. A remittance settlement token performs a different task. The legal claims behind those assets remain central even if both use blockchain technology.

The remittance announcement schedules interim findings and a demonstration for the Black Swan Summit on November 15–16. It does not establish a commercial launch date, a customer tariff or achieved savings. Those remain questions for evidence from the work and any subsequent service announcement.

For a meaningful comparison, the partners would need to consider the amount delivered after all fees and conversion, the time until it becomes usable and the process when a payment cannot complete. A low blockchain transaction fee alone cannot describe those outcomes.

The proposed trial gives yen stablecoins a specific task: improve the settlement arrangement behind a defined payment corridor. Its value will depend on whether institutions can turn that arrangement into a dependable service for people and businesses moving money between the two countries.
