Where To Spot The Air France Airbus A318 Before It Finally Retires

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Air France's Airbus A318 was due to disappear from scheduled passenger service on November 8, but its retirement has been pushed back to March 27, 2027. The airline's remaining aircraft are still operating from Paris Charles de Gaulle Airport (CDG), with Marseille (MRS), Toulouse (TLS), Bordeaux (BOD), Nice (NCE), Milan Linate (LIN), and Lyon (LYS) offering some of the most frequent opportunities to catch the type.

Using data from the Simple Flying Flight Tracker, we analyzed 423 A318 flights between October 12 and November 8. The four aircraft visited 30 airports during those four weeks. One stopped appearing after October 20, although it has since returned to service. Their movements provide a useful indication of where and when to look, particularly for anyone planning a visit to Paris in the coming months.

The smallest member of the Airbus A320 family has already received several extensions to its service life with Air France. Last summer, the carrier extended planned A318 operations from November 8, 2026, until March 27, 2027. The revised timetable covers domestic French routes and a selection of intra-European destinations, with all the scheduled services operating through CDG.

However, March 27 is the final date currently shown in the published schedule rather than a confirmed farewell flight. Aircraft assignments can also change independently of the route timetable, meaning the presence of an A318 in a booking system does not guarantee that passengers will actually travel on one. For spotters, recent movements are consequently more useful than relying entirely on flights advertised several months ahead.

Air France has continued replacing its smaller A320-family aircraft with the Airbus A220-300. On November 6, the airline received its 60th A220, registered F-HOZJ, which subsequently entered passenger service on November 9. The new aircraft seats 148 passengers, compared with 131 on the A318, and is now used across much of the airline's European network. Nevertheless, the four remaining A318s continue to appear in daily operations.

The busiest A318 route during our four-week analysis was between CDG and Marseille Provence Airport (MRS), with 69 recorded flights in both directions. Of these, 35 departed Paris and 34 returned from Marseille. Toulouse-Blagnac Airport (TLS) came second with 47 flights, followed by Bordeaux–Mérignac Airport (BOD) with 42. Together, these three domestic connections accounted for 158 flights, or more than a third of the total activity recorded.

Nice Côte d'Azur Airport (NCE) was the fourth most frequently visited destination, with 28 flights. The leading international route was Milan Linate Airport (LIN), with 22, while Lyon–Saint Exupéry Airport (LYS) recorded 20. These six routes accounted for 228 of the 423 flights, making them the most useful starting points for anyone trying to photograph the aircraft away from CDG. The figures reflect actual recorded movements, not the number of A318 flights scheduled in the months ahead.

Marseille is particularly interesting because the A318 appeared on several different rotations rather than being restricted to a single flight number. AF7344 was recorded ten times from Paris during the analysis, while AF7336 appeared on eight departures. The late-evening AF7344 also operated with F-GUGP on November 21, according to subsequent flight history.

Despite their small number, the A318s were used across a geographically diverse network. We identified 30 different airports in 12 countries between October 12 and November 8, including CDG itself. France accounted for nine of these airports, while the aircraft also visited five in Italy, four in Spain, and two in Portugal. Outside these markets, the tracking data included destinations such as Amsterdam (AMS), Copenhagen (CPH), Warsaw (WAW), Munich (MUC), Prague (PRG), and Bergen (BGO).

The aircraft were not limited to routes typically associated with the French domestic network. Recorded international flights include Paris to Casablanca Mohammed V International Airport (CMN), Málaga (AGP), and Ibiza Airport (IBZ). Bergen Airport (BGO) also appeared in the data, although each of these destinations was served far less frequently than the main domestic routes. A route appearing once or twice in four weeks should not be interpreted as a dependable spotting opportunity.

Unsurprisingly, of the 423 recorded flights, 422 either departed from or arrived at CDG. The exception was a one-off flight between Bologna (BLQ) and Venice (VCE) on November 6. The French hub airport therefore remains the most obvious location for seeing one without having to predict its next destination, although the timing of a visit makes a substantial difference.

As each of the active A318s generally completes around five flights on a flying day, the aircraft make repeated appearances at CDG throughout the day. Our tracking data contains 213 departures from the airport during the 28-day period, equivalent to an average of approximately eight daily departures across the remaining fleet. These were not evenly distributed across the day, however, and several periods offered considerably more activity than others.

The most useful morning window was between 7:00 AM and 8:59 AM local time, when 49 A318 departures were recorded. Another 44 departures took place between noon and 1:59 PM, while 49 occurred between 6:00 PM and 9:59 PM. The later flights are less useful for photography during the winter months, although they provide additional opportunities for anyone primarily interested in watching or flying the aircraft.

Particularly at CDG, it is worth following the registration rather than concentrating on just one scheduled departure. An A318 arriving from Bordeaux, Marseille, or Milan may continue to an entirely different destination after its turnaround. Watching the aircraft's inbound sector can therefore help establish whether the same airframe is likely to operate the next flight, rather than waiting for a single flight number that may be assigned another type.

Four registrations appear in the four-week dataset: F-GUGM, F-GUGN, F-GUGO, and F-GUGP. The busiest was F-GUGM, which completed 146 flights and was recorded on all 28 days. F-GUGO operated 129 flights across 26 days, while F-GUGN recorded 102 flights across 21 days after first appearing on October 19. On days when they flew, each aircraft generally operated around five sectors, although individual daily totals varied.

F-GUGP followed a different pattern as the aircraft completed 46 flights during the first nine days of the period, with its final recorded departure on October 20, when it flew from CDG to Casablanca. It did not appear again in our data through November 8. Consequently, the four-week figures primarily reflect the activity of three aircraft during the second half of the period, rather than a consistently deployed four-aircraft fleet.

More recent data shows F-GUGP operating AF7344 from CDG to Marseille on November 21, departing at 8:15 PM and landing at 9:21 PM. Its return to flying demonstrates why a list of active registrations needs to be checked against current movements, particularly as the retirement date approaches.

The A318's wider route network may contain many possible destinations, but the availability of the actual aircraft will determine which services operate as planned. With only four registrations to follow, a single aircraft being unavailable can affect a sizeable proportion of the type's daily flying. That is also why an A318 shown against a route weeks in advance is not necessarily the aircraft that will turn up on departure day.

The easiest way to identify an A318 currently flying is to open the Simple Flying Live Flight Tracker and search for the aircraft type or one of the four registrations. F-GUGM, F-GUGN, F-GUGO, and F-GUGP provide a much narrower starting point than following every Air France flight from Paris. Filtering the map around CDG then makes it possible to see which aircraft are airborne and where they are heading.

To find out where Air France's last A318s will fly, the most useful approach is to check the recent history of the individual aircraft and then compare it with the airline's upcoming flights. The four-week data suggests beginning with Marseille, Toulouse, and Bordeaux, before considering Nice, Milan Linate, or Lyon. However, the registration assigned on the day is more important than how often a particular route appears in October or early November.
