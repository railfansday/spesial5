Are AI companies ‘glosslighting’ you with misleading jargon? This report says yes

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Ever describe an AI agent’s error as a “hallucination”? Fret over the threat of “superintelligence”? How about call an AI assistant your “co-pilot”? Beware: you may have been glosslit by the AI industry.

“Glosslighting” combines “glossary” and “gaslighting.” It’s for situations where industries develop lingo that intentionally misleads consumers to shape public perception—something that researchers at Simon Fraser University think the AI industry is doing in spades.

Every industry has its own specialized vocab, and there’s nothing inherently wrong with insider lingo. But with an industry like AI, where development is hotly debated and potential controversy lies around every corner, word choice around the industry takes on extra weight.

What is glosslighting?

For the study, researchers analyzed 30 of the AI industry’s biggest buzzwords, breaking down the ways that common language around the technology can control public opinion.

The study’s authors emphasize that while AI terms have been looked at in isolation, they’re more interested in AI’s “interlocking vocabulary” and the way it protects AI companies from taking accountability for the dark sides of their technology. 

“Behind the language of seamless intelligence are workers who classify traumatic content for low wages, communities whose water is consumed by server farms, and individuals whose lives are determined by probabilistic decision tools sometimes named, reassuringly, as ‘solutions,'” the authors write.

How AI jargon causes harm

The study’s authors group the terms by the way they cause harm, the truth about AI they obscure, and the questions they deflect. Those groupings include the following:

  • “Hallucination,” “co-pilot,” “robot rights,” and “agentic AI” humanize the technology, diverting attention from the people and companies making the decisions around them
  • “The cloud,” “data mining,” “scaling,” and “human-in-the-loop” hide the environmental and social costs of AI development
  • “Superintelligence,” “frontier AI,” “AGI,” “existential risk,” “smart city,” “AI superpower,” and “race to AI” shift focus to the future of AI, limiting attention on present-day concerns
  • “Democratizing AI,” “open AI,” “responsible AI,” “ethical AI,” “collaborative AI,” and “human-centric AI” let AI companies frame themselves as trustworthy
  • “Bias,” “ex-ante risk regulation,” “deep learning,” and “data subjects” paint some problems as technological or too complex for laymen to understand, shutting down debates about their usage before they begin

The study’s authors go on to recommend alternatives for many of these terms that either simplify communication, demystify confusing terms, or go more in-depth to provide greater accuracy to AI’s impact.

One author, assistant professor of computing science Nicholas Vincent, told Tech Xplore that “issues where there’s real complexity mean there’s a responsibility to do more to help people understand.”

“Everyone exposed to AI deserves accurate descriptions of what it is and how it’s used,” Vincent said. “As consumers get a little bit pickier, there’s going to be more demand for specifics. That is, ‘If you want me to buy your expensive AI product, you better have a pretty clear sales pitch for what it actually is.’”
