Supercomputer names the 3 EFL League One clubs that will get promoted

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

We're a quarter of the way through the EFL season, which gives us a good sample size to set more informed predictions about how the campaign will play out in all three divisions.

In League One, Huddersfield Town and Stockport County have been the two sides who have consistently impressed and deservedly led the way in the top two.

But below them, Luton Town have picked up a head of steam and some strong underlying numbers are starting to translate into results. They even took a lead at the Accu Stadium on Tuesday night against the league leaders, but were forced to settle for a draw.

Then, further down the table, Sheffield Wednesday have appointed Dave Challinor, and many believe that's the start of a resurgence at Hillsborough. Leicester City have the quality on the pitch and a system that's starting to settle under Russell Martin, and between them, the likes of Reading, Plymouth Argyle and Oxford United will be competitive, too.

So, it's all shaping up to be a very intriguing promotion race with a lot of sides who will believe they can go up, but who does AI think will be in the Championship next season? Football League World has taken a look to find out.

Winning the league, quite surprisingly in AI's eyes, are Oxford. The surprise mainly comes from the Yellows being nowhere near the top six in many critics' pre-season predictions.

There was logic to that reason, too, as Oxford weren't able to spend money and invest in the squad over the summer due to an embargo imposed on them after failing to comply with the EFL's SCMP regulations.

The Yellows were also going into the season with a novice head coach, too, after Matt Bloomfield was sacked in favour of ex-Arsenal and Wales midfielder Aaron Ramsey. Quite bluntly, no one really knew what to expect.

However, Ramsey has more than hit the ground running in the dugout, and Oxford's embargo, despite its frustrations, did mean that the vast majority of their squad that finished 22nd in the Championship and had a mid-table points tally under Bloomfield stuck around. The likes of Mark Harris, Michal Helik and Ciaron Brown have been great.

But also, Ramsey has done well to re-integrate some players many fans felt would never wear an Oxford shirt again. Siriki Dembele has been the main example of that, but Gatlin O'Donkor and Louie Sibley have contributed well, too.

There are questions about the overall depth at the Kassam. Teenagers Mo Missanga and Leo Snowdon are seeing more matchday squads than perhaps expected; albeit Missanga has been brilliant, but hopefully the embargo can be lifted in January, and Ramsey can bring in some reinforcements to help solidify what AI expects to be a title charge.

The current league leaders, Huddersfield, have been forecast by AI to slip from the summit but still secure automatic promotion.

That may be a disappointment right now, but given that the Terriers have failed to even finish in the top six in their two campaigns at third-tier level since their relegation from the Championship, it's a position they should more than take.

There's a clear argument that Huddersfield's squad is already Championship quality. It definitely is at the top end of the pitch. Joe Taylor and Derensili Sanches Fernandes already have 25 goals and assists between them through 14 games, which is quite frankly ridiculous.

But behind them, Cameron Humphreys has been one of the best midfielders in the division for a couple of years now, and it's mental that Huddersfield were able to keep him around for a second loan spell from Ipswich Town, and Archie Collins is more than thriving at a top-end League One club after leaving Peterborough United.

It says a lot that there are question marks over where Ryan Ledson fits in when he comes back from suspension, with the Terriers' club captain and someone with close to 200 Championship appearances patiently waiting on the sidelines.

And it's all being overseen by Martin Drury, an exciting, young manager whose attention to detail and demanding nature of a good performance can ruffle some feathers in the 'only results matter' camp, but it's more than working so far.

There may be a slight drop-off, but one that AI believes shouldn't be too detrimental to their promotion push.

After failing in the play-offs for the last two seasons, AI thinks that Stockport will finally get over the line and secure a second-tier return for the first time since the 2001/02 campaign.

There were some understandable concerns about the immediate future of the Hatters heading into this season, with influential CEO Simon Wilson and manager Dave Challinor moving on after the play-off final, but those have been dispelled very quickly.

There's an argument to be made that County are even better now under Jimmy McNulty than they were under Challinor, and that's no slight on the good work done at Edgeley Park over the previous five years.

They have so many different avenues of attack, whether that be patient, on the floor, working through the lines with Ollie Norwood and a reintegrated Lewis Bate in the pivots, or they can go long and hit Kyle Wootton, who remains one of the best target men strikers in the entire EFL, given how well he can hold the ball up and be a goal threat, too.

Stockport can sometimes get caught out defensively. It took them a little while to get to grips with their new out-of-possession play at the start of the season, and Oxford did showcase how to beat their high line, once they finally stayed onside.

But any worries of them fading away after back-to-back third-placed finishes seem to have dissipated already, and AI feels that it could be third time's the charm in terms of them earning promotion, winning ahead of the likes of Plymouth, Reading, Sheffield Wednesday, Luton and Leicester.
