'I thought I was watching The Karate Kid' - top ref rages at Rangers VAR escape

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Mark Clattenburg has called out Ryan Lee and John Beaton after insisting Rangers' James Penrice should have been sent off during the Scottish League Cup semi-final win over Aberdeen.

The former FIFA referee was left stunned after Penrice escaped punishment for a first-half challenge on Aremu Afeez before going on to set up Ryan Naderi's late winner as Rangers booked their place in the final at Hampden.

Penrice, operating in midfield alongside Nicolas Raskin, escaped punishment after missing the ball and catching Aberdeen's Afeez with a high, studs-up challenge near the halfway line.

Referee Lee opted against showing a card, while VAR official Beaton chose not to intervene.

The decision has come under intense scrutiny, with Clattenburg unable to believe Penrice remained on the pitch.

Speaking on the So Near, Yet So VAR podcast, he said: “I thought I wasn’t even watching football. I was watching my favourite, The Karate Kid.

“Listen, it’s more justifiable and easier to understand when the ball isn’t really that high. If you look at it again, the ball isn’t really there, and I think the player knows what he’s doing.”

Clattenburg went on to explain why he believes the challenge was impossible to defend.

He said: “I think he’s just decided he wants to catch his opponent high with his studs.

“And when you’re playing the ball, when you’re trying to play, you don’t go in like that. You would go in with your body. You would go in with your knee.

“You don’t go in with your studs and then make a clear movement with the studs towards an opponent’s ribs, hip, wherever we want to call it.

“I was surprised it wasn’t red. I’ve seen it in real time.

“For me, when it’s not a footballing action, then you can never support a player’s actions, and I think when a referee gives a red, then nobody can have any argument.”

Former SPFL referee Bobby Madden agreed with Clattenburg's assessment, with both officials adamant Rangers should have been reduced to 10 men.

The controversy was heightened when Penrice went on to have a decisive impact on the result, providing the assist for Naderi's 83rd-minute winner that secured Rangers' place in the final.

Aberdeen will feel particularly aggrieved given the match-winning contribution came from a player many believe should already have been dismissed.

Clattenburg and Madden are not alone in that opinion. Former SFA referee Des Roache has also argued that Penrice should have been sent off for the challenge on Afeez.
