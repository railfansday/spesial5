Trump administration has nominated four candidates to fill key SEC and CFTC commissioner seats.

New SEC and CFTC commissioners could shape U.S. crypto rules without CLARITY Act.

Democrats will take control of Congress after lawmakers begin their terms in January.

The CLARITY Act remains stuck after failing to advance in the Senate. Meanwhile, the Trump administration is moving to fill key positions at the SEC and CFTC. Four nominees will face Senate review this week.

If approved, they could fill vacant positions and help the SEC and CFTC carry out their work as lawmakers struggle to agree on new crypto rules.

The White House sent four nominees to the Senate, including Democrat Kristin Johnson and a Republican Steven Levine for the SEC. Meanwhile, Republican Nathan Anonick and Democrat Matthew Rowland for the CFTC.

The timing matters because both agencies are operating with limited staff. The SEC currently has only two commissioners, while CFTC Chairman Michael Selig is the agency’s only serving commissioner.

If confirmed, the four nominees would give both agencies more members to debate and vote on policy.

For crypto companies, that could be important because the agencies are now expected to play a larger role after Congress failed to pass a market structure law.

The connection between the new nominees and the stalled bill is direct.

Nathan Anonick, a Republican, serves as legislative counsel to Senate Agriculture Committee Chairman John Boozman. He also worked as a lead negotiator on the Agriculture Committee’s part of the CLARITY Act.

Matthew Rowland, a Democrat and CFTC attorney, has also worked on the CLARITY Act. He previously worked with Democratic senators Amy Klobuchar and Debbie Stabenow on the Senate Agriculture Committee.

That gives the two nominees direct experience with the same crypto legislation that is now stuck in the Senate.

The SEC nominees also bring different backgrounds.

Kristin Johnson, a Democrat, served as a CFTC commissioner from 2022 to 2025. She has focused on digital asset regulation, customer protection, risk management, and market integrity. She has also backed stricter rules around crypto custody and decentralized finance.

Steven Levine, a Republican, previously worked as counsel and deputy chief of staff to former SEC Commissioner Mark Uyeda. He later served as general counsel and senior adviser at the White House Council of Economic Advisers.

Levine would take the SEC seat previously held by Hester Peirce, who became a well-known supporter of clearer crypto rules while leading the SEC’s Crypto Task Force.

The timing of the nominations is also important. Democrats won control of both chambers in the November 3 midterm elections. However, Republicans still control the current Congress until the new lawmakers take office on January 3, 2027.

That gives Republicans a limited window to confirm Trump’s nominees before the change in power.

The Senate Banking Committee has scheduled Johnson and Levine for a November 19 hearing. The Agriculture Committee is handling the CFTC nominees, with the hearing schedule still developing.

If the nominees are confirmed, the SEC and CFTC could have a stronger role in shaping crypto policy while Congress remains divided over the CLARITY Act.
