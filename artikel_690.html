Gotham Film & Media Institute Names 2026 Winners of Focus Features Student Short Film Showcase (Exclusive)

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The five winners will each receive a cash grant and have the opportunity for their short to be shown on Focus Features' digital streaming platform.

The Gotham Film & Media Institute has selected the winners of its 2026 Focus Features Student Short Film Showcase.

The five winners of the program, presented in partnership with Decentralized Pictures, are Ana Alpizar of NYU, Shile Liu of CalArts, Nicolás Palacio of the City College of New York, Ravia Rasool of Columbia College Chicago and Qinting Wang of Columbia.

The winning filmmakers, who will be honored at the 2026 Gotham Awards on Monday, Nov. 30, will each receive a cash grant and have the opportunity for their film to be shown on Focus Features’ digital streaming platform and Decentralized Pictures’ DCP+ streaming platform. “One of the most exciting parts of what we do at Focus is discovering new talent. There’s something special about finding them early, when they are finding their voice and exploring what only they can do,” Focus Features vice chairman Jason Cassidy said in a statement. “This year’s filmmakers embody that spirit, bringing bold, personal stories and fresh perspectives from around the world to the screen. We’re proud to continue our partnership with the great team at The Gotham and to celebrate this exceptional group of burgeoning filmmakers.”

The showcase, open to MFA students who completed a short film in the last three academic years, seeks to provide a diverse group of emerging filmmakers with access to the film industry and support multiplatform distribution of their work.

The annual initiative, now in its eighth year, is part of The Gotham’s year-round support for independent film and media creators, providing career-building resources, access to industry influencers and paths to wider recognition.

The Gotham’s programs and initiatives include Gotham Week, Filmmaker magazine, The Gothams, Expanding Communities, the LAIKA x The Gotham Animation Accelerator, the Genesis House x The Gotham Shorts Fellowship and Gotham EDU, which includes the Colgate University Study Group, Industry Immersion at Cannes and the Film and Media Career Development Program.

“The Gotham has always believed that exceptional storytelling knows no borders, and this year’s Focus Features & The Gotham Short Films Showcase reflects that vision,” The Gotham executive director Jeffrey Sharp said in a statement. “This year’s cohort represents an exciting evolution of the program, bringing together filmmakers whose stories span Cuba, China, Colombia, Pakistan and beyond. As The Gotham continues to expand its support for creators around the world, we’re proud to help elevate these remarkable artists and celebrate the fresh voices shaping the future of filmmaking.”

The winning films were selected from submissions from 20 graduate film schools by a jury of filmmakers and curators including Lela Meadow-Conner, Arno Mokros and Kristin McCracken.

“The jury recognizes these five films for their distinct voices and artful use of the short film format,” the jury said of the selected shorts. “Coming from five different colleges and universities and filmed globally, the films showcase unique and fresh stories that we were excited to see on screen. We’re looking forward to these filmmakers’ future works.”

The bios for the winning filmmakers and loglines for their shorts, all provided by The Gotham, follow.

Bio: Ana Alpizar is a Cuban filmmaker based in New York City. Her short film The Fisherman (2017) premiered at the Sundance Film Festival. Her latest short, Norheimsund (2025), premiered at the Venice International Film Festival and was subsequently selected by Sundance, Clermont-Ferrand, Tampere, Palm Springs, and more than eighty other international festivals. Ana’s work has been recognized by the Sony Future Filmmaker Awards and the DGA Student Film Awards. She is also a recipient of the BAFTA BBC America Diversity Scholarship and the Princess Grace Award in Film and holds an MFA from NYU Tisch School of the Arts.

Logline: A girl’s long-distance romance with an older Norwegian man promises to pull her and her mother out of their austere life in Cuba, but her dreams are shaken when she realizes he isn’t as idyllic as he seems.

Little Lies Little CrimesDirector: Shile Liu (CalArts)

Bio: Shile Liu is a Chinese filmmaker and moving-image artist based in Los Angeles. Her work moves between narrative cinema, experimental documentary and video installation, often exploring childhood, family relationships, memory and the boundaries between reality and imagination. Her short film Cold Water (2021) was selected for the 52nd Tampere Film Festival in Finland and was a semifinalist at the International Moving Film Festival in Mexico. Her experimental documentary 24 (2025) screened at the Milwaukee Underground Film Festival, Golden Ger International Film Festival, and Beijing International Short Film Festival, with additional screenings in New York and Los Angeles. Shile holds an MFA in Film Directing from California Institute of the Arts (CalArts) and a BFA in Broadcasting and Television Directing from East China Normal University.

Logline: During China’s one-child-policy era, nine-year-old Jiejie has grown up hiding the existence of her younger sister, Meimei, who was sent at birth to be raised by rural relatives. When five-year-old Meimei returns, she fights to go back to the only home she knows. Jiejie decides to send her back, hoping to reclaim the life she had before.

One Early Morning, the Alligator Swallowed meDirector: Nicolás Palacio (City College of New York)

Bio: A filmmaker from the Colombian Caribbean, his work explores the space between fiction and documentary through visual poetry and cinéma vérité. His MFA thesis film, One Early Morning, the Alligator Swallowed Me, received the Chantal Akerman Student Prize and the National Board of Review Student Grant, and won best cinematography and best fiction film at the CityVisions Showcase. The film will have its world premiere in IDFA’s Short Documentary Competition. Following his 2020 debut feature, Lemonade, Lemonade, made as his undergraduate thesis film, he is currently in post-production on his second feature, Madera, which received a production award from Colombia’s Film Development Fund (FDC). The project was previously presented at the San Sebastián International Film Festival, Berlinale Talents Guadalajara and the Bogotá Audiovisual Market.

Logline: In the Colombian Caribbean town of Plato, a solitary fisherman drifts through riverside life shaped by the enduring legend of the Alligator Man, binding myth, landscape, and human solitude.

Silence on SetDirector: Ravia Rasool (Columbia College Chicago)

Bio: Ravia Rasool is a Pakistani filmmaker working across writing, directing, producing, and editing. Her work explores identity, morality and human relationships through intimate, character-driven narratives. Her film Silence on Set, set against the backdrop of the Pakistani Punjabi film industry in the early 1990s, reflects her interest in stories rooted in human complexity and social dynamics. Her work has screened internationally.

Logline: In the mid-1990s, as the Pakistani film industry chases spectacle, a socially conscious director takes on a commercial project under the promise of creative control. As production unfolds, he confronts a system that reshapes his vision, forcing him to navigate the distance between his ideals and the realities of filmmaking.

Her WeddingDirector: Qinting Wang (Columbia)

Bio: Qinting Wang is a writer-director and producer whose creative work spans China and the United States. She holds an MFA in film from Columbia University School of the Arts. Her short film Her Wedding was selected for the 2026 American Pavilion Emerging Filmmaker Showcase at Cannes. Her earlier film The Weak was selected for the China New Talents program at Interfilm Berlin. She also produced Delay, which won the Sonje Award for Best Asian Short Film at the 2025 Busan International Film Festival. Her films explore intimate human relationships and the social expectations that shape them, often through the perspectives of women.

Logline: On the day of her arranged marriage, a bride reclaims the ceremony meant to contain her.
