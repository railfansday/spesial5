DOC NYC Reveals U.S., International Competition and Audience Award Winners (Exclusive)

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Oscar-qualifying short jury award went to 'Memories of a Window' as the nonfiction festival recognized three short films in awards contention with The Louies, a $100,000 cash fund named after TV and media pioneer Louis Wolfson.

DOC NYC has revealed its 2026 award winners across a number of juried competitions as well as the documentary festival’s audience award.

Additionally, the festival presented prizes to a number of awards-hopeful short films.

The international competition award went to The Hummingbird Paints Fragrant Songs, and the U.S. competition prize went to Mai American.

The top prize in the Metropolis competition, dedicated to stories about New Yorkers and New York, went to The Darkest Light. The Archival Storytelling competition award went to Whiddy as the shorts competition jury prize, allowing the winning film to qualify for consideration in the Oscars’ documentary short category without a theatrical run, went to Memories of a Window. DOC NYC’s Audience award went to Kick, about American runner Nikki Hiltz.

In addition to the competition prizes, DOC NYC announced winners for a number of partner awards and pitch day projects and awarded a total of $100,000 to three U.S. short films in awards contention tihs season through its Louies cash fund, named after TV and media pioneer Louis Wolfson and sponsored by the Lynn and Louis Wolfson II Family Foundation. First prize and $50,000 went to Taliesan Black-Brown’s Listen. Second prize and $30,000 went to Laura Poitras and Rachel Lauren Mueller’s They’re Here, and third prize and $20,000 went to Hilla Medalia’s Outposts: Nothing Here is Yours, produced by Sheila Nevins.

Online screenings for the hybrid, 17th edition of DOC NYC continue through Nov. 29 with the juried award winners among the projects available to stream.

A complete list of the 2026 DOC NYC winners, along with the projects that received special mentions and runner-up status, as well as statements from the jurors, follows.

The jury selected from among eight new international productions in this section.

Grand Jury Prize: The Hummingbird Paints Fragrant Songs, directed and produced by Èlia Gasull Balada and Matteo Norzi, produced by Maria Altamirano. (U.S. Premiere)

Jurors’ statement: “The jury admired how patiently the film brings the viewer into its world. Through its central character’s art, we witness her power to uplift her community alongside the fragility of her circumstances and the challenges facing those around her. Sensitive cinematography and assured editing deepen our connection to the story, while the film trusts its audience to discover its meanings and perspectives.”

The Hummingbird Paints Fragrant Songs was a previous DOC NYC winner in 2024 as recipient of the $5,000 Pitch Day Award. The film is available online through Nov. 29.

Special Mention: Bugboy, directed by Lucas Paleocrassas, produced by Rea Apostolides and Yuri Averof. (North American Premiere)

Jurors’ statement: “We recognize Bugboy for its remarkable narrative assurance and the intimacy of its filmmaking. Patient character development and precise editing bring its carefully woven connections to fruition with emotional clarity. Most affecting is how fully the film draws us into its protagonist’s fascination with insects, making his changing relationship with his father and growing understanding of himself deeply felt.” Available online through Nov. 29.

Jurors: Mohammadreza Eyni and Sara Khaki (Oscar-nominated filmmakers of Cutting Through Rocks), Paola Matturo (Sundance Documentary Fund) and Nicole Tsien (producer of the Emmy-nominated and 2024 DOC NYC Audience Award winner, Slumlord Millionaire).

Films featured in the International Competition section: Lucas Paleocrassas’s Bugboy, Nolwenn Hervé’s The Cord, Miguel Eek’s Dryads, Maxence Voiseux’s Gabin, Èlia Gasull Balada and Matteo Norzi’s The Hummingbird Paints Fragrant Songs, Mahsa Karampour’s Into the Jaws of the Ogre, Jens Schanze’s Materia Prima and Adrian McCarthy and Brenda Morrissey’s Whiddy.

The jury selected from among seven new American nonfiction films in this section.

Grand Jury Prize: Mai American, directed and produced by Kevin Truong. (World Premiere)

Jurors’ statement: “Mai American brings freshness and intimacy to the intergenerational immigrant story. The director’s involvement across the filmmaking lends a distinct perspective, while fluid editing, inventive animation, and flashbacks bring his mother’s memories to life. The jury was moved by how this deeply personal film invites viewers to reflect on their own relationships with their parents, revealing art’s capacity to deepen understanding across generations.” Available online through Nov. 29.

Special Mention: The Newsreel: Characterization of Subversive Organization, directed and produced by Ivora Cusack and James June Schneider, produced by Annick Lemonnier. (World Premiere)

Jurors’ statement: “The jury admired the mastery of craft that brings this history of collective filmmaking to life with such urgency. The film raises timely questions about authorship, privilege, and who gets to tell the news. We were especially struck by the filmmakers’ commitment to a shared purpose beyond individual credit, offering a powerful example of collective action as today’s information landscape fractures.” Available online through Nov. 29.

Jurors: Sam Bisbee (Academy Award-nominated producer of The Perfect Neighbor), Michael Premo (award-winning filmmaker), Elizabeth Woodward (founder and CEO of production and distribution company WILLA).

Films featured in the U.S. Competition section: Hugo Perez’s #SOSCUBA, Wyatt Maw’s Ashland, Patrick Shen and Brandon Vedder’s Illuminations, Kevin Truong’s Mai American, James Longley’s Mohabat, Ivora Cusack and James June Schneider’s The Newsreel: Characterization of Subversive Organization, and Brandon Kapelow’s Under Fire.

The jury selected from among five films in this section, which is dedicated to stories about New Yorkers and New York City.

Grand Jury Prize: The Darkest Light, directed and produced by Simon Mendes, produced by Heidi Reinberg. (New York City Premiere)

Jurors’ statement: “Can music transcend one man? The Darkest Light is a film that centers the perspectives of survivors while holding the complexity of a family that has to live with the legacy of a man who was both abuser and advocate. It takes on one of the hardest questions in culture today: can we separate the art from the artist? Rooted in the Upper West Side, this distinctly New York story offers an emotionally stirring and generous exploration of accountability and repair. It refuses to offer easy resolution and was a film that stayed with the jury, leaving us with questions we’re still turning over.” Available online through Nov. 29.

Special Mention: The MAD World of Harvey Kurtzman, directed by Bart Simpson, produced by Ina Fichman and Aimara Reques. (International Premiere)Jurors’ statement: “The MAD World of Harvey Kurtzman is a vibrant, well-crafted portrait of an influential yet underappreciated artist. The jury appreciated the film’s strong dramatic arc and a bittersweet ending, reflecting the spirit of its protagonist’s own work in its form. The animation of New York and the context of the comic book industry give the story depth. It is a timeless piece of artist portraiture that brings Kurtzman’s legacy to a new audience.” Available online through Nov. 29.

Jurors: Jacqueline Handy (Director of Public Programs and the Margaret Mead Film Festival Director at the American Museum of Natural History), Christopher Radcliff (Academy Award-shortlisted filmmaker of We Were the Scenery), Hannah Rosenzweig (award-winning filmmaker).

Films featured in the Metropolis section: Simon Mendes’ The Darkest Light, Bart Simpson’s The MAD World of Harvey Kurtzman, Linda Saffire and Adam Schlesinger’s Make Work, Deborah Riley Draper’s Romare Bearden: A Life in Collage and Sabine Lidl’s Siri Hustvedt – Dance Around the Self.

The jury selected from among ten films in this competition, which celebrates Main Slate films of exceptional merit that employ non-AI generated, sourced material as their primary story driver. This award was developed in collaboration with Archival Producers Alliance and is co-presented by Footage.Net, Fox Archives, and Global ImageWorks.

Grand Jury Prize: Whiddy, directed and produced by Adrian McCarthy, co-directed by Brenda Morrissey, and produced by Maria Horgan, archival producer Adrian McCarthy. (World Premiere)

Jurors’ statement: “Whiddy showcases a beautiful use of archival. Each piece of footage and photograph retains the spectacular texture of the medium in which it was originally filmed. The way the archival elements dance on screen along to sounds and music is effective and unique. Also unique is the way the archival materials are treated with a burnt orange, reflecting the color of oil, mud, and fire, and serving as a metaphor for evil corporate greed. It’s clear that the archival producers not only did intense and thorough research of the subjects addressed in this film, but understood the specific visual language that so creatively tells this story. Everyone who worked on this is a true master of the craft.” Available online through Nov. 29.

Special Mention: Acting Like Women, directed by Cheri Gaulke, produced by Cheryl Bookout, Anne Gauldin, Meg Linton, and Liz Yale Marsh, archival producer Isabella D’Agnenica. (New York City Premiere)

Jurors’ statement: “The visual documentation is astoundingly fresh even if it is decades old, degraded, and dusty. It resonates deeply in 2026. The documentary archive of the growth of an art and political movement, of the time and place that allowed it to flourish, and the performances make for a critical document of time and place created from personal and private and commercial archive, and ephemera alike allows access to and understanding of a unique and exclusive community.” Available online through Nov. 29.

Jurors: Carl Deal (Academy Award-nominated filmmaker, Trouble the Water); Rachel J. Morrison (filmmaker and archival producer), and Sasha Waters (moving image artist).

Films nominated in the Archival Storytelling Competition: Cheri Gaulke’s Acting Like Women, Karen Hayes’ A Better Way: James Lawson, Architect of Nonviolence, Christopher Gruse’s Kenny: The Marxist Who Would Be Mayor, Bart Simpson’s The MAD World of Harvey Kurtzman, Ivora Cusack and James June Schneider’s The Newsreel: Characterization of Subversive Organization, Joss Crowley’s Pretenders, Joe Berlinger’s Primetime Preacher$, Deborah Riley Draper’s Romare Bearden: A Life in Collage, Adrian McCarthy and Brenda Morrissey’s Whiddy and Juan C. Dávila Santiago’s Zenón and the Rebel Boats.

Audience Award Winner: Kick, directed and produced by Julia Schwalb, produced by Samantha Bloom, Grace Zahrah, Alex Schmider and Zackary Drucker. (World Premiere)

On the road to the Olympics, American runner Nikki Hiltz kindles love and reconciles their identity as a trans nonbinary athlete with the undue scrutiny of a society that resists their place.

Audience Award Runners-Up (in alphabetical order):The Best Justice System in the World, directed and produced by Suja A. Thomas. (World Premiere) Available online through Nov. 29.

Mall Country, directed and produced by Brian Becker, produced by Carrie Weprin. (World Premiere) Available online through Nov. 29.

Under Fire, directed and produced by Brandon Kapelow, produced by Laurel Thomson. (World Premiere) Available online through Nov. 29.

What Happened to Danny, directed and produced by ManSee Kong, produced by Lorraine Ma, Samuel J. Rong, Jennifer Ngo, and Corinne Manabat Cueva. (World Premiere) Available online through Nov. 29.

Shorts Competition: All new short ﬁlms playing at the festival were eligible for the Shorts Grand Jury Prize, with the exception of Partner Events and Short List: Shorts selections.

Grand Jury Prize: Memories of a Window, directed and produced by Mehraneh Salimian and Amin Pakparvar. (New York City Premiere)

Jurors’ statement: “The jury was moved by this film’s evocative use of sound and mirrored images, which connect people witnessing a revolution from their windows. Working within extraordinary restrictions, the filmmaker transforms personal grief into a collective record of resistance. The result captures the profound experience of witnessing an uprising and enduring its losses, while preserving connections that reach beyond the frame.”

The 2026 winning short ﬁlm qualiﬁes for consideration in the Documentary Short category of the annual Academy Awards without the standard theatrical run (provided the ﬁlm otherwise complies with the Academy rules).

Special Mention: Replikka, directed by Piratá Waurá and Heloisa Passos, produced by Heloisa Passos, Mark Slagle and Yula Rocha. (U.S. Premiere)Jurors’ statement: “Replikka powerfully affirms documentary’s ability to preserve cultural knowledge and give it new life. The jury admired the precision of its images and music, weaving ancestral storytelling with the Wauja people’s ongoing resistance. Both ancient and distinctly modern, the film reflects the sacred site’s recreation through its own act of preservation, revealing the survival of heritage as an urgent and living necessity.”

Memories of a Window screens online as part of the Shorts: The People Vs. program; Replikka screens online as part of the Shorts: Keeping the Faith program; both are available through Nov. 29.

Jurors: Laura Nix (Academy Award-nominated filmmaker, Walk Run Cha-Cha), Molly O’Brien (Academy Award-winning filmmaker, The Only Girl in the Orchestra), and Marcia Rock (filmmaker and director of NYU Journalism’s News and Documentary program).

Subject Matter: DOC NYC partnered with Subject Matter to present a $30,000 grant from Subject Matter to Rare as F@k, directed by W. Zack Starr and produced by Sean Fine and Andrea Nix Fine, to support the film’s audience outreach and impact efforts, along with a corresponding grant of $30,000 to the nonprofit Runway of Dreams. Subject Matter additionally awarded a $30,000 grant to My NDA, directed and produced by Juliane Dressner and Miriam Shor and produced by Elizabeth Woodward and Hanna Gray Organschi, with a corresponding $30,000 grant to Harassment-Free New York, a nonprofit organization. Both films made their U.S. premiere at DOC NYC. Rare as F@k and My NDA are both available online through Nov. 29.

The Louies: To support three U.S. short films in awards contention for the 2026-27 season, DOC NYC presented The Louies, a new $100,000 cash fund named after Louis Wolfson, a pioneer in television and media, and sponsored by the Lynn & Louis Wolfson II Family Foundation.

First Prize ($50,000): Listen, directed and produced by Taliesan Black-Brown, produced by Sam Davis and Greg Moga.

Second Prize ($30,000): They’re Here, directed and produced by Laura Poitras and Rachel Lauren Mueller, produced by Yoni Golijov.

Third Prize ($20,000): Outposts: Nothing Here is Yours, directed and produced by Hilla Medalia, produced by Sheila Nevins and Yael Melamede.

Jurors: Violet du Feng (Academy Award-shortlisted filmmaker, Hidden Letters), Jaie Laplante (DOC NYC Artistic Director), and Joshua Seftel (Academy Award-winning filmmaker, All the Empty Rooms).

Listen screens online as part of the Short List Shorts: Legacies program; They’re Here screens online as part of the Short List Shorts: Dedication & Determination program; Outposts: Nothing Here is Yours screens online as part of the Short List Shorts: Divisions program; all are available through Nov. 29.

Bloomberg Green Docs: After hosting a DOC NYC screening for the top five films in their independently curated shorts competition, Bloomberg Green Docs announced the $25,000 grand prize winner How to Plant a Lake / Cómo plantar un lago, directed by Satomi Blair and CG Foisy.

Shine Global Resilience Award: A new award in 2026 is the DOC NYC Shine Global Resilience Award, honoring a film that highlights and celebrates the inspiring strength, creativity, and power of children with a $2,500 cash prize and strategic support and consulting from Shine Global. The winning film is Bugboy, directed by Lucas Paleocrassas and produced by Rea Apostolides and Yuri Averof. Bugboy is available online through Nov. 29.
