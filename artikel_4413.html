REO Reveals T4X, T4C, and S4C As a Trio of Simple, Cheap, American Trucks Under $30,000

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

A flatbed work truck, crew cab pickup, and SUV are all in the works from REO.

Texas-based startup (and resurrector of an old name) REO Trucks unveiled its revamped and renewed mission and previewed its first vehicle this past summer, but as of Monday, the American startup has some trucks to show us. Or, at least some renderings of some vehicles, alongside some real illustrations by frankstephenson design.

All three vehicles REO intends to release are detailed and pictured here. The T4X is the work truck; the T4C is the crew cab pickup; and the S4C is a family SUV. All three ride on the same steel ladder frame, and are even identical in every way from the B-pillar forward. It’s beyond the B-pillar where each, naturally, differ based on their specific use cases.

The T4X will be the first to go on sale (targeting late 2028) according to the brand, and it has a two-seat cab with a steel drop-side flatbed. Said bed measures 79.1 inches long and 70.6 inches wide. The tailgate has some neat features with integrated cup holders, recesses for phones or tablets, and bottle openers on either side.

The T4C crew cab trades some of the bed length for passenger space, as it seats up to five people. That said, the bed is still fairly long, at 54.1 inches in length.

Meanwhile, the S4C seats six, and notably has a shorter rear overhang than the trucks to help with the departure angle for off-roading. The cargo area can be accessed via a side-hinged tailgate, and the rear glass even drops down into the door.

These are the unique body style features REO is detailing for now, but they all have much more in common than they do differences. For example, each REO vehicle will be equipped with a naturally aspirated four-cylinder engine that the company says is designed for longevity, drive-ability and towing more than outright performance. A six-speed manual transmission comes standard, while a six-speed automatic gearbox will be optional. Every REO is also fitted with part-time four-wheel drive, allowing you to select 2WD or 4WD High/Low via a floor-mounted lever. A locking rear differential will be optional for those who want it.

Expect a bumpy ride, because in addition to the body-on-frame construction, all REOs will have solid axles front and rear. Disc brakes will be fitted up front, but drums are used in the back. You’ll steer with hydraulic assistance, and 17-inch steel wheels will be wrapped by all-season tires. Should off-roading be in your future, a “Ransom Package” will be optional, adding off-road tires with unique off-road wheels, upgraded dampers/leaf springs, and a 2.5-inch suspension lift. (You’ll also get orange accents that designate it as the Ransom Package model.)

Photos of the interior aren’t available, but REO says physical controls will “take prominence.” Unlike the Slate Truck, power windows come standard, but the seats are manually adjustable. There will still be screens, though, as REO says it comes with a digital cluster and display for navigation and media.

As of now, the T4X is slated to start at $21,500 and be sold in a direct-sales model (no dealers). The T4C is meant to starts at $25,000, and the S4C at $28,500. The company says it aims to assemble all three in Texas. While the T4X is meant to reach buyers in late 2028, neither the crew cab pickup nor the SUV have predicted on-sale dates yet; if you want one, REO is taking refundable $25 deposits.
