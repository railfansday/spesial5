Bitcoin flashes false recovery warning as $64 billion futures frenzy masks weak demand

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Derivatives accounted for 83% of tracked trading volume while ETF investors withdrew more than $1 billion ahead of the weekend selloff.

Bitcoin‘s recovery above $64,000 risks losing momentum as leveraged traders dominate market activity while institutional investors continue withdrawing capital.

The cryptocurrency staged a modest rebound after plunging toward $62,000 over the weekend, its lowest level since July. However, the recovery comes against a backdrop of weakening demand, with US spot Bitcoin ETFs recording substantial withdrawals and futures positions shrinking across major exchanges.

The clearest warning came from Wall Street before the weekend crash.

According to Farside Investors, US spot Bitcoin ETFs saw more than $1 billion in net outflows over the five trading sessions ending Nov. 13. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust accounted for about $908 million of those withdrawals, highlighting a retreat from one of Bitcoin's largest institutional investment channels.

With ETF demand retreating, leveraged traders dominated the weekend's market activity.

A CryptoQuant analysis showed that Bitcoin futures generated $64.11 billion in trading volume on Nov. 14, dwarfing the $13.36 billion recorded across tracked spot exchanges.

That meant derivatives accounted for nearly 83% of combined trading activity, with futures turnover almost five times larger than spot volume.

The imbalance was particularly striking on Binance, where $24.46 billion in Bitcoin futures trading exceeded the combined spot volume of every exchange tracked by CryptoQuant by 83%.

The disparity raises a troubling possibility for Bitcoin's recovery. Prices may stabilize as leveraged traders unwind bearish positions, but that buying can disappear quickly if investors don't return to accumulate the underlying cryptocurrency.

The weekend's derivatives frenzy coincided with a significant retreat in open interest, suggesting traders were reducing exposure as Bitcoin absorbed its latest losses.

Binance data showed that open interest in its coin-margined Bitcoin futures market dropped 7.3%, from 16.25 million contracts on Nov. 13 to 15.06 million by Monday morning.

The perpetual futures segment contracted even faster, declining approximately 7.8% over the same period.

That reduction removed more than 1.18 million outstanding contracts from Binance's coin-margined market, reducing the pool of speculative positions that had helped fuel the weekend's volatility.

Meanwhile, funding rates revealed another significant shift in trading conditions.

Binance's BTCUSD perpetual funding rate turned negative at -0.006022% on Nov. 15 as bearish pressure intensified. By Monday morning, the rate had reversed to 0.003593%, indicating that the earlier imbalance favoring short positions had eased.

Together, falling open interest and the funding reversal suggest Bitcoin's derivatives market has undergone a substantial reset.

They also leave room for a short-covering rebound, in which traders who bet on further losses buy futures contracts to close their positions, potentially pushing prices higher without attracting fresh spot investment.

However, the danger is that such buying is temporary.

Once bearish traders finish closing their positions, that source of demand disappears. Unless new buyers step in, Bitcoin could lose the support behind its recovery and become vulnerable to another round of selling.
