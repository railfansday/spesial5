Record hedge-fund leverage could soon spill over into Bitcoin

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Fed’s November report puts gross notional leverage at a Q1 2026 record in data starting in 2013. Losses or tighter funding could spread across markets.

Hedge-fund leverage reached a record in Q1 2026 on the gross notional measure, according to the Federal Reserve’s Nov. 13 Financial Stability Report. For Bitcoin, the risk is that losses or demands for additional collateral force leveraged investors to cut positions, spreading pressure across markets.

The record covers the series beginning in 2013. The report also describes reduced Treasury basis-trade activity alongside record or near-record leverage across equity, relative-value and macro strategies. That broader assessment is why a smaller basis trade alone cannot establish that exposure to financial stress has eased. The comprehensive leverage observation is months old; the report generally reflects information available by Oct. 28.

Gross notional leverage compares total exposure, including derivatives, with net asset value. Balance-sheet leverage compares gross assets with net asset value. Neither is a measure of net directional exposure after offsetting positions, so a gross leverage record should not be read as an equally large one-way market bet.

The Treasury basis trade pairs bonds bought with repo borrowing, secured against the bonds, and a short Treasury futures position. Shrinking that trade can reduce one source of financing demand, while other leveraged strategies remain exposed to losses or tighter funding.

Dallas Fed researchers argued on Oct. 16 that modest basis-trade retrenchment could reflect changing relative returns and the appeal of alternatives such as swap-spread trades. Those trades combine Treasury and interest-rate-swap positions. That is a possible explanation for strategy substitution, not proof of how every fund repositioned.

The financing mechanism matters more than the strategy label. The Fed’s stability report warns that material losses or worsening funding can generate spillovers, while higher volatility can constrain dealers’ willingness and ability to intermediate.

Bitcoin’s connection is conditional. IMF research published in January 2022 found stronger Bitcoin-equity co-movement and spillovers during 2020-21 than before the pandemic, with spillovers tending to intensify during market volatility. That historical evidence supports a route for cross-market stress; it does not establish today’s correlation or identify hedge funds currently selling Bitcoin.

The September dealer survey provides a counterweight. Conducted Aug. 11-24 and covering changes from June through August, it found client leverage, broad financing terms and market functioning basically unchanged on net, although some dealers reported wider equity collateral spreads.

Stable conditions can coexist with high leverage: two-thirds of the 15 dealers with at least a decade of hedge-fund business placed leverage above the midpoint of its past-decade range. These were qualitative assessments, not November measurements.

The warning signs to watch are tighter collateral terms and investors cutting positions across markets. Stable funding would weaken that case. A smaller Treasury basis trade alone cannot establish that Bitcoin is insulated from broader deleveraging.
