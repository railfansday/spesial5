If you're starting to notice that your skin is looking a little, well, tired, you're not alone. The visible signs of aging—I’m talking fine lines and wrinkles, dullness, even sallow skin—will strike us all sooner or later. Bummer. On the bright side, we're living in an age when there's no shortage of smart beauty devices and skincare products for these issues. From wrinkle-smoothing injections like Botox and fillers to anti-aging creams, you've got an arsenal of skin-boosting tools at your disposal.

But what if I were to tell you that there's one such solution that takes roughly 10 a day and can be done while you're relaxing in bed scrolling TikTok? Enter red light therapy.

Red light therapy has exploded in popularity lately because it's convenient, noninvasive, and can be done at home. It uses a specific spectrum of visible light that offers a host of beauty benefits—low-level laser light therapy can effectively treat scars, vitiligo, and psoriasis, research shows. It has an anti-inflammatory effect and can even help to smooth wrinkles, too. So, how does it work? We know that red light penetrates 2 to 3 millimeters into skin's dermis, where it's “absorbed by light-sensitive porphyrins in the mitochondria of skin cells, stimulating energy production in fibroblasts to churn out more collagen,” says dermatologist Ava Shamban, MD.

But the goodness doesn't stop there. Red light can also help fade dark spots and manage eczema, plus boost hair growth, says dermatologist Purvisha Patel, MD. These at-home devices can be expensive, but they're an investment you can use for a years to come: Many red light therapy tools are also equipped with blue lights, which are particularly effective at treating acne (say buh-bye to those period or menopausal breakouts) and other concerns. Bottom line: At any age—and for years to come—these red light therapy tools can be a beneficial addition to your beauty routine.

Ultimately, these are the devices that we’ll continue to use and would genuinely repurchase if we had to start our skincare routine from scratch. The best part? Our faves start at just $72, which is a major win for a category that easily ranges from $300 to $700.

I've got a great list of options for you, but if you find yourself in the market for a red light therapy tool, here are a few things to keep in mind.

There are several kinds of red light therapy devices to choose from, including panels, masks, and facial wands, and one may be a better fit for you based on your lifestyle and the area you're seeking to treat.

LED masks are the most common variety you'll find for home use because most are hands-free and contain a lot of diodes (a fancy way of referring to the individual lightbulbs), offering a powerful burst of light to treat the face, neck, or chest.

Maybe you're claustrophobic and the idea of wearing masks gives you the heebie-jeebies—that's where panels come in. They look like flexible bodyboards that you lie down under, and they come in a diverse range of shapes and sizes, each offering different levels of red light exposure to your face, décolletage, and back. These also help replace the need for a separate lip-specific red light therapy tool.

Wands are appealing when you want to target smaller specific areas (the crow's feet or smile lines, for example) and prefer to do your treatments on the go (you can walk around while using these).

Our eyes are the first place to show signs of aging, so you’ll want to take a cue from dermatologist Pooja Rambhia, MD, and use these affordable red light therapy eye tools. Red, deep red, and near-infrared wavelengths target fine lines, wrinkles, darkness, and puffiness early on, making them a smart preventive beauty move.

It's tempting to pick a device with the quickest treatment window, especially if you don't have loads of free time to sit back and chill with a red light mask. But remember: To treat more advanced signs of aging, a longer treatment time may be more beneficial. Look at the full picture before you base your purchase on the convenience of a quicker treatment. And for what it’s worth, almost all of our featured recommendations can be used in just three minutes, so even busy women can incorporate quick treatments into their routine.

You may notice the abbreviation nm on the outer packaging of many of these tools. This stands for nanometers and refers to the wavelength of the light that the device emits—an important metric to consider, says dermatologist Jody A. Levine, MD. "Red light typically falls between 630 and 660 nanometers, and is good for targeting fine lines and mild discoloration," says Dr. Levine. If you're looking for something slightly more powerful—to deal with deeper wrinkles, sagging, or stubborn pigmentary problems—go with a device that cranks up to the near-infrared range (between 800 and 850 nanometers) to penetrate deeper.
