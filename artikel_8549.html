How to Make a Budget in 5 Steps – Start With Take-Home Pay, Not Salary

Build your budget on take-home pay, the amount that lands in your account, rather than your salary. Set your savings first, list every expense from the last two or three months of bank and card statements, cut until the plan balances, and check it against your real spending once a month.

The gap between salary and take-home pay is big enough to break a budget in its first month.

Picture a single filer who takes the standard deduction, owes no state income tax, and has nothing taken out of each check for benefits. Their $60,000 salary is $5,000 a month, but federal income tax plus Social Security and Medicare taxes leave about $4,200 of it. A budget that plans to spend the full $5,000 runs about $800 a month over the $4,200 that arrives.

State income tax and money taken out for health insurance or a 401(k) shrink the check further, so read your take-home figure off the net pay line on your own pay stub.

Pick the tool you’ll open every month. A spreadsheet totals the numbers you type in, a budgeting app linked to your accounts pulls in your transactions, and pen and paper needs nothing but your statements.

Download your latest pay stubs and recent bank and card statements before you start, since steps one and three are built from them.
