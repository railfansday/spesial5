To distribute only realized capital gains to the beneficiary, that must be the only income the trust has and the trust must distribute a pro-rata share of every type of income in the DNI pool (ordinary interest, non-qualified dividends, qualified dividends, and capital gains).
I have an attorney and very knowledgeable CPA who disagree with you. Your state may have different laws. Maybe you need a better attorney to draft your next trust.
I don’t think state law is going to override IRC §§ 661(b) and 662(b).

Yes, there is an exception, but unless your attorney and CPA understand the two tests under Treas. Reg. § 1.652(b)-2(b) and your distribution qualifies, it’s not legit no matter what the trust docs say.

One requirement is that the income under the custom allocation has an economic effect independent of the income tax consequences. I don’t believe your usage meets that criteria. I think maybe they just see the exception and don’t realize that Treas. Reg. § 1.652(b)-2(b) applies.
