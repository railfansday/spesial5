Goldman Sachs Sees 52 More GW of Data Center Power in 2028. These 3 Stocks Look Best Positioned to Cash In.

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A $50 billion AI data center is worthless without one basic thing, and these stocks supply it.

By Neha Chamaria – Nov 25, 2026 at 2:06PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Bloom Energy ›

The artificial intelligence (AI) boom is rapidly running into a hard physical constraint: electricity. In a research note cited by Reuters, Goldman Sachs (GS -0.62%) projected that up to 52 gigawatts (GW) of additional data center power capacity could come online in the U.S. through 2028.

Goldman noted that U.S. data center power capacity and demand have already climbed nearly 30% year-to-date, accounting for more than one-third of the country's total electricity demand growth. It expects natural gas consumption to rise and is bullish on gas prices.

What's fueling this demand, and which stocks could win?

AI data centers need 24/7 stable, uninterrupted power, even when the sun sets or the wind dies down. That gives an edge to power sources that can run on demand, and natural gas is the biggest of them.

In 2025, natural gas made up 40% of U.S. utility-scale generating capacity, compared with 31.5% for all renewables combined and 13.3% for coal, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. When Texas set its all-time demand record on July 22, 2026, gas supplied 48% of the power and solar supplied 32%.

Speed matters too, as hyperscalers and data center operators can't wait years for a grid connection. Delays are costly. At an event in October, Nvidia (NVDA -3.91%) CEO Jensen Huang estimated that a 1-gigawatt AI data center can generate around $40 billion a year in revenue, and a three-month delay pushes back roughly $10 billion in revenue.

That urgency makes reliable fuel and fast power delivery critical, pushing developers toward natural gas. Three stocks could cash in, as they span the entire power value chain.

Energy Transfer (ET +0.70%), which sits along major natural gas corridors supplying power generation and industrial demand. It owns and operates nearly 140,000 miles of pipeline infrastructure, connecting major producing basins and markets.

Since the start of 2025, Energy Transfer has signed more than 9.5 million MMBtu (one million British thermal units) per day of agreements with hyperscalers, data centers, and power plants. These include agreements with Oracle (ORCL -5.13%) for three data centers, two of which are in Texas.

The company says it is in "advanced negotiations" with multiple customers to supply natural gas along its pipeline network, which includes Texas, Arkansas, Oklahoma, Ohio, Louisiana, and Illinois. These are also among the largest data center markets today.

Its new Hugh Brinson Pipeline entered full service in September ahead of schedule and carries Permian gas about 400 miles to the Dallas-Fort Worth area.

Energy Transfer earns primarily under long-term fee-based agreements and pays a steady, growing dividend. The stock yields 7.3%.

Bloom Energy (BE -2.25%) directly targets the power bottleneck by making solid-oxide fuel cells that provide modular, on-site power at facilities. Its fuel cells generally run on natural gas today, and growth has been explosive.

Third-quarter revenue surged 138.1% to a record $1.2 billion, with fuel cell system sales surpassing $1 billion in a single quarter for the first time. Bloom raised full-year 2026 revenue guidance for the third time in the year, projecting 122% growth. Management didn't give backlog numbers, but said it is growing faster than revenue.

Bloom Energy has secured massive partnerships, including a $25 billion financing deal with global asset management giant Brookfield Asset Management (BAM +2.60%).

GE Vernova (GEV -0.78%) makes large gas turbines, and almost 25% of all the electricity generated globally passes through its equipment. It also makes high-voltage transformers, switchgear, and control systems needed to connect data centers and buildings with the transmission lines.

While the industry lead times for some of these equipment now stretch into years, turbine buyers are paying up to book factory slots through 2032. Its latest agreement to acquire Trench Group for $9 billion is a calculated move to vertically integrate and secure parts supplies for transformers and other equipment.

Backlog hit $197 billion in the third quarter of fiscal year 2026. Until months ago, the company expected to hit $200 billion in backlog in 2028. Scarcity is giving the company incredible pricing power.

CEO Scott Strazik said full-year 2026 order pricing per kilowatt is on track to rise 50% from last year. With higher prices and volumes, you should expect bigger sales, margins, cash flows, and returns on capital.
