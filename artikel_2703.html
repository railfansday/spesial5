Ravens Discussion: Predictions for Week 11 matchup vs. Panthers

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Baltimore Ravens will face the Carolina Panthers on the road in Week 11, looking to snap a five-game losing streak and keep their slim hopes of a late-season turnaround alive. Here are Baltimore Beatdown's predictions for this contest.

At this point, it's difficult to pick the Ravens. They are coming off one of their worst losses in franchise history and haven't had life for weeks. Are the Panthers a great team? No, but if the last five weeks are any indication of what Sunday will be like, the Ravens will again be dominated in the trenches, commit costly pre-snap penalties, and turn the ball over in critical moments. The dismissal of Marlon Humphrey could help the team morale, but at this point, Humphrey really isn't moving the needle too much. Simply put, there are too many issues right now. Do I have any confidence that the Ravens will magically be able to run the ball, protect the quarterback, sack the opposing QB, clean up their pre-snap issues, and not the turn the ball over? No.

X-Factor: Mike Green. Green has been a bright spot on an otherwise underwhelming defense all year with last Monday being arguably the best performance of his young career. If Green can continue to stack games like that, the Ravens could have a future star on their team. Although Trey Hendrickson has been getting a lot of flak from the media, he's been getting consistently double-teamed and chipped which has allowed Green to take advantage of 1-on-1 pass rush situations.

I'm not sure I will pick the Ravens for the rest of the year the way it looks right now. This Panthers matchup might be the easiest remaining on the schedule but with no Nnamdi Madubuike and a drama-filled week with Marlon Humphrey, it just doesn't fill like the Ravens are in a great spot mentally to go on the road and find a win. The Panthers offense loves the middle of the field, something Jesse Minter's defense leaves available by design. Chuba Hubbard has been breaking tackles this season, something the Ravens struggle with. The Ravens offensive line doesn't seem likely to magically improve. If the Ravens win, its because they suddenly learned how to run into sacks against a bad offensive line and because Jacoby Brissett took advantage of an injured Panthers secondary room, that looks to get some guys back this week. I think this is easy win for the Panthers, the Ravens can't stop Hubbard and the Panthers bleed clock for a fast, low-scoring win.

X-Factor: Ja'Kobi Lane. Lane was a favorite target in Brissett's debut as a Ravens, who targeted Lane 11 times against the Chargers. Unfortunately, Lane only brought in three of those targets and had a really bad drop in the redzone. He was also tackled multiple times on plays that weren't flagged for defensive pass interference inexplicably. I don't think the Jacoby-to-Ja'Kobi connection is going away despite the low success rate. Brissett loves to push the ball down the field and Lane's skillset fits that well. A breakout game from Lane could drive a Ravens win and maybe flip momentum for the season.

The Baltimore Ravens have lost five straight games, with their last win dating back to October 11th against the Atlanta Falcons. This Sunday, Baltimore faces another NFC South opponent in the division-leading Carolina Panthers (4-5). Despite the Ravens' recent struggles and internal drama surrounding Marlon Humphrey, this adversity could be the catalyst for one final push to save their season. The Ravens have a favorable matchup against Carolina's offensive tackles. Rasheed Walker has surrendered 5 sacks and 38 pressures this season, while rookie first-round pick Monroe Freeling has allowed 5 sacks and 26 pressures. Defensively, Carolina has been a buzzsaw against the run, ranking 8th in efficiency and 2nd when stacking the box. However, the Panthers rank just 23rd against intermediate passes, an area Baltimore can exploit. Expect Mark Andrews, Zay Flowers and Ja'Kobi Lane to see a similar target share to last week.

X-Factor: Trey Hendrickson: Hendrickson has an opportunity to deliver his signature performance as a Raven. Carolina's struggles at both tackle positions, combined with a tight end room that lacks a reliable option to consistently chip him, create an ideal matchup. Expect Hendrickson to capitalize on these one-on-one opportunities and potentially take over the game as a pass rusher.

The Ravens' playoff chances could not be slimmer now after a fifth straight loss last week. However, there is still a mathematical possibility of postseason life — which would pretty much evaporate if they lose any of the next few games. The Panthers are a formidable opponent, but certainly beatable. The Ravens will need to take advantage of Carolina's weak offensive line and create positive plays on defense via the pass rush. Mike Green and Trey Hendrickson have advantageous matchups against both of the Panthers' offensive tackles.

X-Factor: Roquan Smith. The Panthers have been one of the top passing attacks over the middle of the field this season, which is Bryce Young's biggest strength. Smith's coverage in this area has been up-and-down, and he will need perform better in this game. The Panthers have multiple big targets who can catch in traffic. Smith will be targeted often and needs to avoid getting lost in open space. Also, can Smith step up as one of the veterans the Ravens need to be more of an impact playmaker? Let's see in this game.

It's hard to imagine the Ravens beating anybody after giving the Chargers their first win of the season. This defense holding both Chuba Hubbard and Tetairoa McMillan in check seems like a pipe dream at this point, especially without Nnamdi Madubuike up front. If the offense can finally figure out how to run the ball, then this team will have a legitimate chance, but I will believe they are capable of doing so when I see it. Jacoby Brissett cannot be asked to throw the ball close to 50 times again this week, but with how impossible running the ball has felt at times, he may be asked to shoulder a significant load once again in his second game with the team. That strategy worked out decently well against Los Angeles, but dropping back that many times behind this offensive line is a recipe for disaster over any extended period of time. It is time for Declan Doyle to pull out all the tricks to get Derrick Henry going this Sunday.

X-Factor: Travis Jones: Jones had a strong game against the Chargers and the big nose tackle will need to show up again this week for the defense to have a shot at limiting the damage from Hubbard on the ground. Jones will be needed to provide interior pressure as well in the absence of Madubuike.
