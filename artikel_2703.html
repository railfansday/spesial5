Stampede Blue Reacts results (Week 10): Colts fan confidence falls again, down to 17%

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

The latest SB Nation Reacts survey delivered another troubling update on the state of fan sentiment in Indianapolis, with just 17 percent of Colts fans saying they are confident the team is headed in the right direction. It is another slight drop from last week's 19 percent mark, and it reinforces just how little confidence remains in the current state of the franchise.

At this point, the result should not come as much of a surprise. The Colts have lost five straight games, fallen to 2-7 on the season and spent the last several weeks making major roster changes ahead of the NFL trade deadline. Quenton Nelson, DeForest Buckner, Keenan Allen and Josh Downs have all been moved, while other notable players such as Jonathan Taylor, Grover Stewart and Daniel Jones remained with the team despite speculation surrounding their futures. Taken together, it has been a clear sign that Indianapolis is no longer focused on salvaging this season, but instead trying to position itself for a longer-term reset.

That does not necessarily mean every fan opposes the direction the organization is taking. In fact, some of this low confidence may simply reflect how painful the current stage of the process looks rather than outright disagreement with the moves themselves. The Colts have finally started making the kind of difficult decisions that a struggling team often has to make, but that does not make the short-term product any easier to watch. Losing games, trading recognizable veterans and entering the second half of the season with significant uncertainty at quarterback, wide receiver and along both lines is not exactly a recipe for immediate optimism.

What this number really shows is that Colts fans are still waiting for a reason to believe. The franchise may have started its rebuilding process in earnest, but rebuilding alone does not inspire confidence unless there is a clear sense that the team knows where it is going. Right now, that part still feels uncertain. Chris Ballard is gone, the roster has been reshaped, and the next general manager will inherit a team with more draft capital, but also plenty of holes and no obvious path back to contention in the near future.

For now, the fan base remains deeply skeptical. Until the Colts start showing signs of a coherent long-term plan, or at the very least begin putting a more competitive product on the field, it is hard to imagine that confidence number rising in any meaningful way.
