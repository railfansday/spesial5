Mavericks vs Nuggets Recap: 3 things from the Mavericks 120-101 win against the Nuggets

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The Dallas Mavericks picked up their most impressive win of the season Tuesday night, defeating the Denver Nuggets 120-101 in Denver. This was an NBA cup group game, and the win kept the Mavericks undefeated in group play.

This was a dominant showing from the Mavericks in one of the hardest road environments in the NBA. Dallas led nearly the entire game, taking the lead with 7:30 left in the first quarter and never letting Denver get back on top.

Dallas' defense was the star of the first half, limiting the Nuggets to 39 points, the Nuggets worst first-half scoring mark of the season. In the first quarter both teams started a bit cold, but in the second quarter the Mavericks defense really took over. Dallas' swarming, aggressive style flummoxed a Nuggets team that wanted to slow the game down in the half-court. The Mavericks played the passing lanes well, with nine first-half steals, leading to multiple transition opportunities.

In the second half the Nuggets were more successful in making this more a half-court game, but Dallas answered every single rally attempt. Denver closed the gap to as little as three in the third quarter, but would never get it closer than that. Kyrie Irving woke up in the second half, and started a run to start the fourth quarter that truly put the game away. This was a balanced game from the Mavericks, as while Irving and Cooper Flagg had their highlight plays, they struggled overall with their shot.

This is a landmark win for a Dallas team on the rise. Let's go over our takeaways.

Dallas still has a pretty funky roster, imbalanced with so many bigs and forwards and weak on guards. So much of that talk in the summer and preseason reflected more on what that imbalance would mean on offense, but it was just as important to talk about what that meant for the Mavericks defense. Dallas doesn't have a lockdown perimeter guy — a point-of-attack defender, as the kids say. So how do you overcome that in a league dominated by elite perimeter ball handlers and high-tempo, spread pick-and-roll play? The answer: use all those athletic wings and bigs to play like absolute mad men.

Credit to coach Dusty May and his staff, they have imbued a defensive identity on this team that it has sorely lacked over the last few seasons. Dallas might not have defensive-stopper archetypes, but they have so many rangey wings and bigs — Cooper Flagg, PJ Washington, Zaccharie Risacher, Caleb Martin, Naji Marshall, Daniel Gafford, and Morez Johnson Jr. Those athletes have been unleashed, playing up and shooting the passing lanes to cause havoc. It hasn't always worked, and we've seen teams exploit this aggressive defense with spacing, shooting, and off-ball movement. But boy, when it works, and when the Mavericks are locked in with their aggression and rotating well, it's awfully impressive to watch. The Mavericks had the Nuggets in absolute hell in that second quarter where Dallas outscored Denver 34-22.

Dallas had 13 steals tonight, scoring 26 points off the 18 Denver turnovers. This also led to the Mavericks having an overwhelming 35-7 fastbreak points advantage and an absolutely preposterous 68-38 points in the paint advantage. Dallas made Denver look old and slow. Kudos to this coaching staff and a roster of players that have absolutely bought in.

Morez Johnson Jr.'s rookie season has been rough to say the least, with lower-body injuries during the summer and preseason seemingly zapping all of explosiveness. We've seen glimpses of Johnson's talents at various points of this young season, but they've been few and far between. Against the Nuggets Tuesday night, Johnson showed off why he was a top-10 pick this past summer.

Johnson finished with nine points, 11 rebounds, and a steal in 19 points. He led the Mavericks in rebounds despite being eighth in minutes played. He held his own against Nikola Jokic, keeping Jokic in check during the second half when the Nuggets were furiously trying to rally. Johnson punctuated this effort with a great dunk right at the end of the third quarter, showing some leaping ability that just hasn't been there for most of the season. Johnson also banked in a three-pointer, which was also fun and funny.

With Daniel Gafford unable to grab any defensive rebounds (only four in 27 minutes), Dallas is desperate for any rebounding help. Hopefully Johnson can build off this performance.

Kyrie Irving's final stat line doesn't seem great — 11 points on 5-of-19 shooting, and only 1-of-8 from threes. If you didn't watch the game, you'd think it was one of Irving's worst. And weirdly it both was and wasn't.

The shooting was as bad as it looked for spurts of the first half, but Irving willed himself to some big buckets in the second half and had some spectacular assists in the third quarter. Also Irving continued his off-ball brilliance, moving well off the ball and creating opportunities for himself and his teammates.

If you have a chance, rewatch the second half and focus on Irving. Whenever he doesn't have the ball, he's almost constantly on the move. Obviously there are times when Irving is off the ball and standing in the corner, but his constant motion without the ball causes so much trouble for opposing defenses. Irving's cuts toward the basket seemingly get him either a layup or free throw attempts almost every time, and that happened again against the Nuggets.

It's rare you see someone of Irving's talent be so comfortable and useful as an off-ball player. Irving relishes the dirty work of making cuts and moving, knowing that movement doesn't always directly benefit him. But it benefits his teammates, and it's clear Irving cares. It's just nice to watch, and this performance was another example of that.
