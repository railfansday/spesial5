NFL Monday Night Football preview: Joe Burrow, Jayden Daniels meet in big game for Bengals and Commanders

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Joe Burrow and Jayden Daniels have plenty in common.

They starred at LSU and each won a Heisman Trophy. They both were high picks in the NFL Draft and have had success in the pros. But, like their LSU legacies, Burrow has done just a little more.

Burrow comes into Monday night's game as the NFL MVP favorite with his Cincinnati Bengals at 7-2 and in first place of the AFC North. He is on pace for more than 5,000 yards and 50 passing touchdowns.

"I mean, it would be impossible to say that anyone is playing better," Bengals coach Zac Taylor said after Burrow threw for 409 yards and four touchdowns in the Bengals' 38-18 win over the Steelers last week. "I'm not watching every game ... but I bet the people that aren't talking about him aren't watching our games, because if you are, it's really hard to say that he is not the best player on planet Earth."

The Washington Commanders are trying to stay in the NFC East race at 3-5-1, but their entire season has been a challenge. Daniels has been good but not quite to the level he reached when he was NFL Offensive Rookie of the Year in 2024.

The game is about more than just the respective quarterbacks. The Bengals have improved around Burrow, protecting him better and with a defense that has improved since an offseason trade with the Giants for defensive tackle Dexter Lawrence II.

The Commanders' situation around Daniels hasn't been as great. The offense is thin on playmakers after Terry McLaurin's season-ending injury, though running back Jacory Croskey-Merritt could return from an injury on Monday night. There have been plenty of questions about head coach Dan Quinn's job security. The Commanders stayed alive in the NFC East race with a win over the New York Giants last week, but they don't have much margin for error anymore.

It is an interesting contrast between the teams, and with two star quarterbacks from LSU taking center stage, Baton Rouge will be tuning in.

Cincinnati Bengals quarterback Joe Burrow, left, and Washington Commanders quarterback Jayden Daniels talk following a preseason NFL football game Monday, Aug. 18, 2025, in Landover, Md. (AP Photo/Nick Wass)

Some results on Sunday dictate this answer. The Steelers lost to the Eagles, a bad loss that dropped them to 6-4 and two losses behind the Bengals in the AFC North race. Elsewhere, the Giants pulled off an upset win over the Jaguars to improve to 6-3-1, increasing their lead in the NFC East. The Commanders already dug themselves a hole early in the season and the Giants continue to win games. The Bengals are pushing for the No. 1 seed in the AFC and nobody in that race can afford bad losses, but Washington probably needs the win more. The Commanders need all the wins they can get to stay in the playoff picture.

The Bengals are coming along well on defense but still have issues covering tight ends. The 685 yards allowed to tight ends this season is tied for second-worst in the NFL, according to Pro Football Reference. The team they're tied with? The Commanders, so Bengals tight end Mike Gesicki could be a factor on Monday night, too. For Washington's offense to thrive, it needs some pass catchers to step up around Jayden Daniels. Okonkwo is having a solid season with 27 catches for 263 yards, but the Commanders probably need more from him on Monday night against a defense that is vulnerable against tight ends. Okonkwo has 15 targets the past two games, so Washington is trying to get him more involved. It's a good matchup for Okonkwo to have his biggest game of the season.

Just how good is Cincinnati's offense? That's basically what this handicap boils down to. Respected money pushed this line up from Bengals -4 to -6 (in Washington), but that's been the point of resistance. Joe Burrow & Co. have scored at least 31 points in five victories (4-1 against the spread), while the Commanders haven't lost by more than seven points in any of their past five games. Ultimately, both defenses are similar in yards per play allowed (Washington at 5.4 YPP; Cincinnati at 5.5 YPP), but I think the Bengals' offense (third in NFL at 6.2 YPP) is too potent for the Commanders to keep this within the number over the course of four quarters. — Ben Fawkes

Fantasy hopes are high as the Bengals and Commanders hold a 54.5-point total for Monday. Here are three themes to track from a fantasy perspective.

1. What is Jacory Croskey-Merritt ready to do? Washington's running game has been stuck in neutral all year (25th in DVOA), in part because of JCM's groin injury. He's missed the past four games, but he's also averaging a mere 3.6 YPC when active. Nonetheless, he's the best natural runner the team has.

2. Can anything stop the Cincinnati passing game? MVP favorite Joe Burrow has been too good to be true over his past five starts, piling up 20 touchdown passes and 1,624 passing yards (he's also rushed for a couple of scores). Ja'Marr Chase (WR4) and Tee Higgins (WR7) are both smashing this year, and TE Mike Gesicki has somehow scored six times on just 20 receptions.

3. Who emerges among the Washington pass catchers? Stefon Diggs is still getting by on guile and experience, but he's making only 6.5 yards per target. Antonio Williams has played better of late, and tight end Chig Okonkwo is a target earner, though he hasn't scored a touchdown yet. Jayden Daniels hasn't run much for a month, so he'll need to keep the offense moving with his arm. — Scott Pianowski
