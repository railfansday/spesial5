Mahomes has a new star and Cousins fights back tears - NFL Sunday Review

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

NFL Sunday Review - we reflect on Week 10 in the NFL as Emmett Johnson announced himself as a new star on the Kansas City Chiefs, Kirk Cousins capped a memorable return for the Minnesota Vikings and Christian McCaffrey dominated in the San Francisco 49ers' win over the Dallas Cowboys.

"There's more of the story to be written." Kirk Cousins could barely fight back the tears as he processed a day he thought might never come, a second chance he thought might never arrive. But football can't keep Kirko Chainz down, and suddenly the Minnesota Vikings have hope.

It is the easiest story in the NFL to get behind. A 38-year-old Kirk Cousins, written off more times than he can count, deemed deep into the back nine of his career, coming back to a hero's welcome to spearhead a playoff charge while lighting a fire under an offense in desperate need of inspiration. His inspiration.

He is football's lovable goof, with the unpretentious, unflashy dad clothes and a 'down with the kids' willingness to sling on Justin Jefferson's chains should it lift his teammates. He is also now Minnesota's great hope of rewarding defensive mastery with an attack capable of competing in January football.

The Vikings were limping behind Kyler Murray, limping behind Carson Wentz but sitting a coveted 6-2, and on Tuesday rolled the dice by trading to bring back Cousins from the Las Vegas Raiders.

It was three years ago that Cousins played the final game of his first chapter with the Vikings when he tore his Achilles tendon in Week Eight against the Green Bay Packers at Lambeau Field. On Sunday he was back where it ended - but could it yet be where it all started again?

Cousins, who had revived his starting career as mentor to No 1 pick Mendoza in Las Vegas, finished 21 of 31 for 207 yards, two touchdowns and one interception in his first game back as the Vikings stunned the Packers 30-10.

His start featured a perfectly placed sideline strike between two defenders to a reception-starved Justin Jefferson on third-and-second in the first quarter, and so too a high-level tight-window bullet to Myles Price for a 38-yard touchdown catch-and-run on third-and-23.

It would become Jefferson's best game of the season as he made eight catches from nine targets for 92 yards and two touchdowns, Cousins' reconnecting with one of the stars of the league having seen him suffer for the first half of the season.

Much of the debate and, at times, criticism around Cousins throughout his career has been the shortage of point guard ability as a needle-moving creator. The Patrick Mahomes' and Josh Allens and Joe Burrows and Lamar Jacksons of the world have unfortunately heightened that bar for all quarterbacks across the league. But what has never been in doubt with Cousins is the efficiency at which he feeds his best players, and at which he moves the ball and at which he can accentuate an offense's strengths without being spectacular. Spectacular isn't exactly needed when there is a Brian Flores defense foaming at the mouth in wait of their chance to go hunting on the sideline.

In one game the Vikings looked a more polished, more accomplished operation behind Cousins, and alongside that came another rampant defensive play that saw Minnesota sack Jordan Love eight times.

In the first half alone they produced five sacks, 12 pressures, one interception and one fumble recovery, including sacks on back-to-back plays at the end of the second quarter. Their sixth sack then arrived on the very first play from scrimmage after the break, followed by the third of their four turnovers when Dallas Turner recover a wayward snap.

They were frightening, and the perfect complement once Cousins had helped the Vikings get on top early. The impact was evidenced through the time of possession, Cousins' offense holding the ball for 17.55 minutes in the first half and 35.16 a week on since the Vikings mustered just 17 minutes and 28 seconds of possession against the Bills. What a difference some respite for one of the league's most potent defenses makes.

There was a nice moment in the second half when former New Orleans Saints quarterback Drew Brees paid tribute to Cousins' communication at the line of scrimmage, where he shifted personnel and changed the play like an orchestra conductor. It was long-lost control and assurance under center.

Cousins later executed a trick play that saw him pump fake left and the swivel to pump fake to his right before tossing to Gavin Bartholomew over the middle of the field for a touchdown. For Kevin O'Connell to dial up a play like that just days after Cousins' arrival is testament to the footballing brain now leading Minnesota's offense.

"It was emotional coming to this stadium three years ago, tearing my Achilles here. It's been a tough three years, just grateful for the opportunity to play here, grateful for my teammates and what they did to make this outcome possible.

"It's been a tough three years. There's more of the story to be written," said Cousins post-game, his voice cracking several times."

Cousins won just one playoff game in six seasons during his first stint with the Vikings, before signing a four-year $180m contract with the Atlanta Falcons in 2024, only to be bench for Michael Penix Jr in his first season. He was then released in March earlier this year before landing in Las Vegas as something of a bridge towards their new era under Mendoza.

What looked like a declining career hurtling towards its end is instead flirting with one final twist.

Patrick Mahomes' new friends are alright. The Kansas City Chiefs were without their best player this season in Kenneth Walker III on Sunday; it did not matter. The absence of last season's Super Bowl MVP paved the way for an increased role for fifth-round rookie running back Emmett Johnson, who capitalised emphatically with 22 carries for 101 yards and six catches for 70 yards and two scores through the air as Kansas City beat the Atlanta Falcons 35-13 in a five-touchdown day for Patrick Mahomes. Johnson showcased his menu of evasive manoeuvres in the open field with a clinic in tackle-missing jukes and the yards-after-catch threat primed to see him become one of Andy Reid's favourite weapons over the second half of the season.

There was also a debut for wide receiver Josh Downs, who had four catches for 53 yards and a touchdown in his first game since arriving via a trade from the Indianapolis Colts. It's been a long time since Mahomes and the Chiefs posed a consistent vertical threat but Downs could prove an answer to as much. Mahomes completed seven of 12 attempts on passes of 10-plus air yards for 206 yards and three touchdowns, while going three of six on attempts of 20-plus yards for 132 yards and two scores. The Chiefs offense is clicking.

Denzel Ward is playing at an All-Pro level. It is hard to be one of the best players on the league for so long on a team that has been bad for so long; Myles Garrett did it in Cleveland, and Denzel Ward is still doing it. He stepped up when the Browns needed him most on Sunday with a stunning diving pass break-up against Xavier Hutchinson to deny the Houston Texans with 30 seconds left in Cleveland's 27-24 victory. Ward allowed just four receptions for 16 yards from nine targets on Sunday while lining up against Nico Collins in man coverage on 10 of 11 routes, according to Next Gen Stats. Pat Surtain II may still hold the honour of top dog in the eyes of many, but Ward will feel he holds a strong argument as being the best corner in football.

There are encouraging signs for Fernando Mendoza. The Raiders ushered in a new chapter this weekend as No 1 overall Draft pick Mendoza led his side out against the Seattle Seahawks in his first regular season start following Cousins' trade to the Vikings. It started strongly with a touchdown run on his opening possession before Mike Macdonald dialled the pressure up a notch on defense in the second half as the Raiders offense suffered without star weapon Brock Bowers. He went 11 of 13 for 90 yards through the air in the first half, before managing just six of 16 for 53 yards following the interval. Mendoza finished the game 17 of 29 passing for 143 yards, zero touchdowns and zero turnovers while being sacked six times. Facing the league's best defense always beckoned as a baptism of fire, so the shortage of interceptions or apparent ball security issues was a plus point.

Brock Purdy is a top five quarterback in the NFL. For somebody so often pigeonholed as a 'system quarterback', he sure does do a lot of good outside of the system. Purdy continues to showcase an elite ability to extend plays and create outside of the pocket, perhaps best epitomised by the 74-yard McCaffrey touchdown catch-and-run that saw him side-step a near-certain sack, slide to his left, cut back inside and fire a dart to his open running back. Poise in the face of chaos and a ground threat that makes him dangerous off-script. Purdy finished the game 22 of 28 for 389 yards and three touchdowns with a 154.5 passer rating. He is in the MVP hunt and playing at a level among the premier play-callers in football. By now, though, he knows his true judgement comes around playoff time.

Christian McCaffrey: If you thought injury setbacks had zapped the juice in the 49ers running back, think again. McCaffrey went off for 223 scrimmage yards and three touchdowns to tear apart of hobbling Cowboys defense, which surrendered a franchise-worst 584 yards at home. McCaffrey's 161 receiving yards were the most by a running back in a game since Jamaal Charles' 195 in 2013, while he is the first 49ers player with 200 scrimmage yards and three scores in a regular season game since Jerry Rice in 1995.

Parker Washington: Jacksonville Jaguars head coach Liam Coen called him the best player on the field, and a man possessed. The Tennessee Titans could not live with Parker Washington as the Jags receiver took another major step closer towards a bumper new contract with a career-best seven catches for 152 yards and a touchdown in his side's 40-23 win. One play saw him put Alontae Taylor in a blender with another clinic in route-running, another play saw him levitate to haul in Trevor Lawrence's pass at the sideline, and another saw him race free for a 50-yard catch-and-run. His outstanding season continues.

Mike Vrabel: On more than one occasion on Sunday, the camera captured Mike Vrabel closing his eyes in excruciating thought at the sight of another setback for his Patriots, be it an injury or a pre-snap penalty. The game was over before it ever really began for last year's beaten Super Bowl finalists, Drake Maye departing after the opening drive through injury and a sputtering offense later losing AJ Brown in the latest disruption to what is threatening to become a nightmare maiden season in New England. They are 5-5 with the Buffalo Bills, Minnesota Vikings and Kansas City Chiefs coming up over the next month; their season is teetering on the edge.

Aidan Hutchinson: Perhaps it is unfair to kick a man when he is down, but it is hard to overlook a game-ending, potentially season-ending injury caused by a celebration. Lions edge rusher Aidan Hutchinson is feared to have suffered a serious knee injury after landing awkwardly during a post-sack celebration on the opening drive of New England's defeat to the Lions in Munich. It was the same play that saw Maye suffer his own game-halting injury, with neither returning to the field. His absence would leave Detroit without their star edge rusher heading into the second half of their playoff charge

Jahmyr Gibbs is a familiar resident in this slot, but he is impossible to ignore as the clear frontrunner for Offensive Player of the Year through 10 weeks. The Lions running back was at his scintillating best in Munich as he put up 252 scrimmage yards and two touchdowns to torch the Patriots defense, his 62 career touchdowns breaking Terrell Davis' record for most by a player through his first four seasons in NFL history. He is not only the perfect cocktail of speed and shiftiness, but one of the smartest crease detectors in football. He runs hard, he runs fast, he runs with high-powered intelligence as the focal point of Drew Petzing's Lions offense. Another clinic, this time on European soil, to extend his lead as the NFL leader in scrimmage yards.

The 49ers and the Cowboys go way back as one of the NFL's most storied rivalries between two of football's most prestigious franchises. They renewed their dislike to the sight of record numbers as they combined for a series-high 86 points, with San Francisco beating Dallas 46-40 to improve to 8-1 on the season. While the 49ers flexed their offensive power, in doing so there were more glaring concerns over the Cowboys and a defense ranked third worst in yards allowed. Dak Prescott threw five touchdown passes, three to George Pickens, as the Cowboys put up 439 yards of offense, only to come out second best. Purdy produced two scoring strikes to McCaffrey and one to Deebo Samuel.

"The second half was unacceptable; tackling was atrocious, you can't give up three explosive plays of over 65 yards for touchdowns and expect to win. Totally unacceptable defense in the second half," said Cowboys head coach Brian Schottenheimer.
