With that in mind, five nonprofit organizations were selected for this round of Mission Meow grants:

Community Cat Welfare Network in Pinehurst, North Carolina supports the humane welfare and stabilization of existing community cat colonies in central North Carolina through the provision of food, insulated winter shelters, veterinary care, humane population stabilization efforts, and public education.

Grant funding will provide 20 winter shelters for stabilized community cat colonies.

Through the work of Fix & Feed Feline Feral in Tampa, Florida, stray cats and kittens are humanely trapped and spayed or neutered, vaccinated for rabies, and returned to their colonies, which are managed by caring volunteers. The health of these feline communities is supported through health checkups, annual vaccinations, and specific health care as needed.

Grant funding supports TNR equipment and an incubator to expand their growing community program.

Lupa Legacy Animal Rescue provides medical care to homeless pets in central Oklahoma, with a focus on owner-surrendered pets and tame, stray cats and kittens from TNR efforts.

Grant funding will help provide TNR equipment and microchip readers to expand trapping efforts to a nearby trailer park.

Hands to Paws Rescue in Georgia initiates and supports programs that extend and elevate the standards of compassionate care for companion animals in the greater Augusta area by providing educational programs, direct care, and cooperative efforts to increase access to low-cost spay/neuter, rescue, fostering, and adoption of animals.

Grant funding supports TNR equipment to help expand their community cat spay/neuter transportation program.

TippedEars in Compton, California, is combating cat overpopulation through humane TNR. While in their care, community cats are sterilized, vaccinated, and treated for fleas.

Grant funding supports TNR equipment to create a trap-lending program for trained community members.

Each organization stood out in its own unique way, but they all shared qualities that are deeply important to Mission Meow. They are collaborative, community-focused, and committed to TNR, addressing cat overpopulation at its source. Many work with underserved communities and focus on community cats who could easily be overlooked. Most importantly, they understand that creating lasting change means not only helping the cats in front of us today, but also working to prevent more cats from needing help tomorrow.
