Virgil van Dijk and Andoni Iraola hit out after Liverpool denied 'clear penalty' against Arsenal - as Reds boss reveals he will tell Alexander Isak to 'dive on floor' in future

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

By BEN WILLCOCKS, ASSISTANT SPORTS EDITOR and LEWIS STEELE, FOOTBALL REPORTER

Published: 14:45 EDT, 1 November 2026 | Updated: 17:17 EDT, 1 November 2026

Andoni Iraola has revealed he will now tell Alexander Isak to 'dive on the floor' as he and Virgil van Dijk hit out at VAR after Liverpool were denied a 'clear penalty' during Sunday's 1-0 defeat by Arsenal.

The Gunners ended their 14-year wait for a Premier League victory at Anfield to return to the top of the table, keeping Liverpool at bay with a superb defensive performance after Kai Havertz opened the scoring in the 16th minute.

Just minutes after the German's opener, Liverpool striker Isak looked to have a golden opportunity to equalise when he rose to meet Bradley Barcola's cross, only to miss the ball after having his shirt pulled back by Arsenal defender Jurrien Timber.

Despite clearly tugging the Swede's shirt, Timber escaped punishment from referee Andy Madley, while VAR opted not to intervene and award Liverpool a penalty despite protests from Iraola and his players.

Sky Sports pundits Jamie Carragher, Daniel Sturridge and Ian Wright unanimously agreed Liverpool should have been awarded a penalty at half-time and suggested that Isak was denied the spot-kick because he failed to make more of the incident.

Andoni Iraola (right) and Virgil van Dijk (left) hit out at VAR after Liverpool were denied a 'clear penalty' during Arsenal's 1-0 win at Anfield on Sunday

Alexander Isak was blatantly pulled back by Jurrien Timber but was not awarded a penalty

Iraola thinks the game could have gone differently had Madley not missed Timber's shirt pull.

The Liverpool boss said after the match: ‘There are two situations and they are a little bit related. The Isak one is a clear penalty.

‘He's gonna head the ball in front of the goal, and they grab him where everyone sees the shirt. You don't need a lot, just to avoid Alex, because the ball just goes in front of him, and I don't see the reason why, probably because he doesn't dive or he doesn't go to the floor. It shouldn't be a reason not to concede a penalty.

Iraola also pointed to a similar situation where Cody Gakpo was tugged back, but the officials said the Dutchman would not get to the ball.

‘They say the ball was too long and Cody wouldn't have arrived to the ball, but then you're basically accepting that the first one is, is a pen, no?’ added Iraola. ‘So you cannot use the argument in both ways differently. So I think it's a, yes, it's a pen, the Alex one is.’

Speaking to Sky Sports after the match, the manager said: 'It's a problem we have with the game. Because he doesn't dive and go to the floor. The fact that you are even asking me if he should go to the floor, if he goes to the floor, they call the penalty 100 per cent.

'It should be like this but it is what it is. Next time, the managers should tell Alex to go to the floor, otherwise they won't call it.'

Van Dijk insisted it should not be on his team-mate Isak to make more of the incident

Asked if he will tell Isak to do that in future, the Spaniard replied: 'Yes, definitely.'

Reds captain Virgil van Dijk took aim at VAR too after the match, expressing his disappointment at the defeat.

'I think it was a clear penalty. I've seen it back and I've seen it being given. Today it wasn't meant to be,' he said.

Asked if Isak should have reacted more, the Dutchman added: 'We have 50 cameras. Why does he have to complain when there are so many cameras?

'Everyone has seen it. In the end it wasn't given and we lose the game unfortunately, so well done to Arsenal.'

Former Liverpool defender Carragher was furious that VAR had not intervened as he analysed the incident at half-time.

'One of the best goal-scorers in the league is pulled back seven yards from goal, and the pull back stops him heading the ball. Why does he need to make a meal of it? VAR, do your job,' he said bluntly.

Arsenal moved to the top of the table on 24 points with victory at Anfield, while Liverpool remained fifth after Brighton and Chelsea, who are third and fourth respectively, both won on Saturday.

Mikel Arteta hailed his side’s resilience to win at Anfield.

Asked if it feels better to do so as a player or manager, he said: ‘Now it feels better as a manager, it has been such a long time without this feeling. We are extremely proud, especially the conditions we arrived in after the defeat against Fleetwood looked like the end of the world.

‘But then you have to pick yourself up. Four days later you have to come to Anfield against this manager and this team.’
