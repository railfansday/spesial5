Robert Pattinson’s Biggest Box Office Hit Officially Comes Home With 90 Minutes of Extras

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Hannah covers horror, sci-fi, and games at Collider, where she writes news and features, reviews new releases, and interviews the people who make them. She’s helping build out Collider’s gaming coverage, with Pokémon as one of her specialties. If she isn’t writing about a monster, she’s probably watching one on a tape from her VHS collection.

Matt Damon has had a rough time getting home in Christopher Nolan’s movies. In Interstellar, somebody finally comes to collect him, and he immediately makes everybody regret it. The Odyssey gives him a much better reason to want the trip to end. As Odysseus, he has a wife and son waiting in Ithaca, with the aftermath of the Trojan War and a succession of gods and monsters between them. Nolan shot the journey around the world, making the sea itself one of the problems his cast and crew had to contend with.

Audiences followed him to the tune of $1.8 billion worldwide. According to NBCUniversal, that makes The Odyssey the biggest movie of Nolan’s career and the highest-grossing release in Universal Pictures’ history. The first movie shot entirely in IMAX has spent months giving people a reason to find the biggest screen within driving distance. Now the people who have already made that trip can finally spend some time looking at how Nolan pulled it off.

The Odyssey is available to watch at home as of November 17. The movie is available to buy or rent through digital retailers including Prime Video, the Apple TV app, and Fandango at Home, with 4K Ultra HD, Blu-ray, and DVD editions arriving the same day. Damon leads a cast that includes Anne Hathaway, Tom Holland, Robert Pattinson, Lupita Nyong’o, Zendaya, Samantha Morton, and Charlize Theron.

Four worlds and four completely different ideas of what a good life looks like. This is where you belong, what it would ask of you, and what you would get back in return.

You belong somewhere old enough that the mountains have names and somebody can still tell you who gave them. Middle-earth is for people who think the right thing is worth doing even when it will not be finished in their lifetime.

You want the sea, a crew, and absolutely nobody telling you where you are allowed to go. The Grand Line is the world for people whose main ambition in life is finding out what is over there.

You would rather be feared and useful than liked and useless. The Continent is a world where the monsters are the straightforward part of the job and the people are where it all gets complicated.

You want the myths to be real and loose in the actual world, and you want to find out at about thirteen that you were never anywhere near as ordinary as everybody kept telling you.

The home release includes more than 90 minutes of special features, with a collection of 12 production featurettes personally overseen by Nolan. The Trojan Horse gets a feature of its own, which feels fair for a prop that has to fool an entire city while the audience already knows what’s inside. Other pieces follow the construction of Troy in Morocco, the ships used for the voyage, and the stunt work with Damon and Holland.

The physical editions also include the IMAX prologue, along with a conversation in which Reece Feldman asks Nolan to explain what happens to the movie after it leaves the set. Ludwig Göransson takes viewers through the score in a separate featurette, including the instruments and vocals used to build it. Together, the extras give the production’s enormous scale some individual faces. Somebody had to make those ships seaworthy, dress those armies, and get the cameras into position before Odysseus could have the worst trip of his life.

The Odyssey is available now on digital and physical formats.
