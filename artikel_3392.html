David Ellison on Elon Musk’s Skydance Investment, Trump and ‘Rush Hour 4,’ and Whether He Would’ve Made ‘Digger’

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Though David Ellison has been around Hollywood for a while now, he’s undoubtedly our newest media mogul and still a fresh face when it comes to being the center stage headliner at fill-in-the-blank business media conference. There’s still a lot we don’t know about him and a lot of questions people have about his plans for Skydance, his politics, and his taste in movies.

So on Wednesday when he spoke with Andrew Ross Sorkin of the New York Times at the DealBook Summit on Wednesday, he had a chance to get candid about a whole range of subjects. It’s early days at Skydance, with changes to the C-suite coming daily, so he’s still keeping his cards a little close to his sport jacket. But we did learn a few things about what he envisions for Skydance. “One of our guiding thematics has always been where entertainment is heading,” Ellison said. “It’s that great Gretsky quote, skate to where the puck is going, not to where the puck has been, and that’s been what’s guided the company.”

Following a report that the world’s richest man had invested $1 billion into Ellison’s Skydance, leading many to think there’s some bigger picture brewing between Skydance and Starlink, Ellison did confirm that Musk is an investor and shareholder, and though he couldn’t comment on anything on which they may be collaborating, he’s welcoming the opportunity.

“He’s the greatest entrepreneur of all time,” Ellison said. “He doesn’t invest in a lot of companies, so to have his vote of confidence and to have him as a shareholder is an honor and a privilege, and if there’s ever an opportunity to collaborate in the future, from our perspective it would be a privilege.”

Sorkin did double back and ask if Ellison will still allow Alex Gibney’s “Musk” documentary to air on HBO as been planned. He said that HBO has a Pay-1 deal with A24, so of course they would honor that agreement, and they “believe in independence and we don’t believe in censorship of any kind.”

One problem: A24 didn’t produce “Musk” as Ellison said. It’s a Bleecker Street film. So we’ll put a little asterisk on that answer.

Tom Cruise’s mega flop “Digger” has the ironic and unfortunate distinction of being the final movie released by Warner Bros. Discovery. And Ellison was asked “how risky” he would continue to be in the context of how much money “Digger” stands to lose. Ellison started by saying that Cruise is “one of the seminal artists of our time,” that Skydance has made eight movies previously with him, and they’re making a “Days of Thunder” sequel before (hopefully) heading into making “Top Gun 3.”

Ellison then described a long story that involved sleeping on Hans Zimmer’s couch before landing on the age old, “what’s the budget?”

“I spent the last 16 years of my life on the filmmaking side of this business. I have lived on set, I have slept on Hans Zimmer’s couch, I have been in editorial rooms for 20 hours non-stop, I do understand the creative process of this business,” Ellison said. “The goal has to be in any film that you make is how do you reach the broadest possible audience for that particular story? There is no one size fits all approach. We will make franchise movies, we will make original stories, we will make genre pictures, we will tell stories aimed at audiences from every single walk of life. What is important is that you size the budget to the potential addressable audience.”

Sorkin pushed Ellison hard about what his politics are and who he voted for in the most recent election. He still did not get an answer, but he would not let slide some of the reports that President Trump himself asked about Paramount requesting that the studio make “Rush Hour 4.”

“We are working with Brett [Ratner] on ‘Rush Hour 4,’ which is a sequel to an incredibly successful franchise,” Ellison confirmed. However, he said the first time he heard about the prospect of that movie was when film chief Josh Goldstine walked into his office and mentioned it, not that it came from Trump, and that “there’s a lot of made up stories” out there.

“Rush Hour” director Ratner as a reminder was in 2017 accused of sexual harassment and misconduct by six women as part of a Los Angeles Times report, including actresses Olivia Munn and Natasha Henstridge, claims that he denied. His most recent project was this year’s “Melania” documentary that was released by Amazon MGM.

One thing Ellison has been working with President Trump on is his desire to get passed a federal tax rebate for film productions in the U.S., and he told Sorkin his goal is to see the rebate passed by the end of this year, at which point the new Congress comes into office and a bill has to be reintroduced. He said he had three meetings with the late Senator Lindsay Graham about the problems of film production leaving the country.

Ellison was staunch in pushing back on some data from the Tax Policy Center as shared by Sorkin that while subsidies do influence where productions choose to shoot, it’s not the same as creating measurable economic growth and that it just pulls workers away from other jobs and tourism.

“Categorically that’s just not true. When you actually look at the rebate in the United Kingdom, it has been a boom to their economy, same is true in Canada, same is true in Australia. All those other countries have those rebates because it pencils out,” Ellison said. “If you look at Texas, Paramount was a part of building a 31 percent rebate for all of Taylor Sheridan’s productions. We had a meeting down there with the deputy governor that said, based on their own independent audit, for every dollar that was invested by the state returned $4.70.”

He also stressed that it’s a misnomer that Tom Cruise is the one getting a tax break but that really it goes to helping union, working class film professionals who benefit.

“You are creating jobs, you are helping small businesses, and every other country that has done this in the past has done this because it is economically sustainable,” he added.

Ellison on numerous occasions in earnings calls and in other appearances has expressed his fondness for artificial intelligence and how he feels it’s going to transform Hollywood. He elaborated on that view a little more on Wednesday, calling it not just a tool for artists but a “creative unlock.”

“Going back to the days of Pixar, the lesson was always be wrong as fast as possible…we’re going to be living in a world where you can make the movie not 10 times but 20 times before it gets put out,” Ellison said. “We’re also living in a world where a $750K movie made $500 million because the cost of production has boxed out new voices.”

He’s referring to “Obsession” there, but he also gave the example of the original “Terminator,” which Ellison said James Cameron made for $4 million but today would cost $70 million. He felt the AI tools would help people to make those original stories, and he’s fully on board.

“The relationship we basically have with talent, our understanding of technology and how those two things are fused together, it’s a feedback loop,” Ellison said. “It is the art challenges the technology, technology inspires the art feedback loop. That is what is necessary to meet this moment in time and build the entertainment company of the future.”
