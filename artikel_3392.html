‘Artificial’ Trailer: Andrew Garfield Stuns in Luca Guadagnino’s Sharp, Shocking, Funny Portrayal of OpenAI CEO Sam Altman

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Luca Guadagnino takes on — and potentially takes down — artificial intelligence and one of its figureheads with his latest feature film, “Artificial.”

A swishy, swively, veering-toward-camp Andrew Garfield plays gay OpenAI CEO Sam Altman opposite an ensemble that includes Oscar-nominated “Anora” breakout Yura Borisov as his former chief scientific officer Ilya Sutskever, Monica Barbaro as erstwhile OpenAI chief technology officer Mira Murati, Ike Barinholtz as an outré Elon Musk, along with Cooper Hoffman, Mark Rylance, Jason Schwartzman, Chris O’Dowd, and Zosia Mamet.

Ahead of the film’s release from Neon on Christmas Day, watch the trailer below. Written by former “Saturday Night Live” scribe Simon Rich, “Artificial” dramatizes a two-decade artificial intelligence arms race. AI-pioneering professor Geoffrey Hinton makes a vanguard discovery that will change the course of humanity forever — with Sam Altman, Elon Musk, and more prominent Silicon Valley techbros racing to get in on the vision, inevitably leading to corruption that Guadagnino details with scary prescience. Especially as Ilya falls deeper and deeper under Sam Altman’s frighteningly charismatic sway.

As IndieWire’s David Ehrlich previously wrote, “Guadagnino is exploring how difficult it is to abandon the body in the first place — and not just the body, but the intangible essence that it contains. We couldn’t leave it behind if we tried, and this, in his own way, is Guadagnino trying just as hard to do that as Sam Altman ever did.”

“Artificial” was something of an industry hot potato for a sizable stint over the summer, with Amazon MGM Studios dropping the film (which it produced) allegedly over its own ties to artificial intelligence and Altman. Neon eventually picked up “Artificial,” marking the indie company’s first partnership with the “Call Me by Your Name” and “Challengers” director, whose last movie was “After the Hunt,” also at Amazon MGM Studios. “Artificial” surprise-premiered at the New York Film Festival to strong praise — the same week that Aaron Sorkin’s “The Social Network” sorta-sequel “The Social Reckoning” failed to generate much noise.

Neon will take the film into wide release over the Christmas holiday. It’s indeed one of the most crowd-pleasing (and also darkest) films of Guadagnino’s career. (Earlier in December, IndieWire Honors will celebrate the film’s cast at our December 3 event.)
