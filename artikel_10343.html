Several methods can help you deal with online threats and strengthen security. All are effective, but each has a trade-off. For example, the harder you make it for bots to affect your website’s performance, the more likely you are to frustrate real customers.

Those of you who browse online daily know that credential stuffing, fake signups & payment fraud keep growing. To prevent fraud, businesses install CAPTCHAs & hard blocks that treat bots and legitimate users the same, diverting them to your competitors.

IP geolocation maps an IP address to a physical location & network details, letting you spot suspicious traffic before it reaches sensitive actions. Used well, it’s an invisible, low-friction risk signal. In this guide, you will learn how to flag high-risk activity and apply soft geo-fencing with step-up authentication.

Geolocation makes several common attack patterns easy to spot:

The first is traffic bursts from data center ASNs. These are bots, as real customers don’t browse from cloud servers

Many accounts created from one IP or subnet. This is a clear sign of fake signups

Impossible travel, such as logins from two distant locations within minutes

Timezone vs. IP mismatch. A device’s clock disagrees with its apparent location.

Note: Geolocation is just a signal, not a verdict. VPNs, travel, & mobile carriers can all create false alarms, so it works best when combined with other signals. That’s exactly why a proportional, soft response beats a hard block.

In short, IP geolocation turns a bare IP address into context, helping you separate real users from automated or fraudulent traffic.

Not every IP geolocation anomaly deserves the same response. The simplest way to stay consistent is to define a clear risk-signal framework.

That can be a short list of checks. Each check can have a weight that reflects how strongly it suggests fraud.

With risk scoring, you can turn these checks into a decision. You just have to assign each signal a numeric weight.

For example, medium can be 10 & 30 can be high.
