Best Crypto-Friendly Banks: 5 Options for Crypto Investors and Businesses

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Which banks will support your crypto habits?

Finding a crypto-friendly bank in 2026 is not as simple as opening an account with a traditional financial institution. Some banks allow transfers to cryptocurrency exchanges like Coinbase, while others offer built-in crypto trading, digital asset custody, or specialized banking services for Web3 businesses.

The best option depends on where you live, whether you are an individual investor or a crypto business, and which digital asset services you need. Some providers also impose restrictions on specific exchanges, transaction limits, or crypto-related business activities.

In this guide, we compare crypto-friendly banks and fintech providers, examining their supported services, availability, fees, and limitations to help you find the right banking solution in 2026.

A crypto-friendly bank makes it easier for individuals and businesses to interact with the cryptocurrency market through traditional financial services. Depending on the provider, this may include allowing transfers to and from crypto exchanges, offering crypto trading or payment features, or providing banking services to eligible crypto companies.

Not all crypto-friendly providers offer the same features. Some let customers buy, sell, and hold digital assets directly, while others simply support transfers to selected platforms like Kraken or provide financial services to Web3 businesses. Some are licensed banks, while others are fintech companies that partner with banks to deliver their services.

Before exploring the best crypto-friendly banks, consider the main advantages and potential drawbacks.

If you’re looking for the best crypto-friendly bank to join in 2023, you’re in the right place! This list breaks down some of the top banks for cryptocurrency from a variety of fields, including traditional banking, business banking, and personal accounts.

Revolut remains one of the more accessible options for people who want to manage traditional money and cryptocurrency through a single financial app.

Revolut is a digital banking platform that combines traditional financial services with cryptocurrency features. In supported markets, customers can use its app to buy, sell, hold, and transfer cryptocurrencies, including Bitcoin and Ethereum. Available assets and features depend on the customer’s country and account eligibility.

Revolut also supports crypto withdrawals and deposits through selected external wallets and blockchain networks. However, transaction fees, supported assets, and withdrawal limits vary. Its cryptocurrency services are provided by separate legal entities from its banking services, and crypto holdings are not covered by standard bank deposit protection.

Wirex flips the script amongst the other ‘best crypto-friendly banks’ contenders. While other platforms began their story as traditional banks and slowly moved to cryptocurrency acceptance, Wirex set out to be the world’s leading crypto bank from day one.

Wirex is a global fintech platform combining traditional currencies, cryptocurrency services, and payment cards. Users in eligible markets can buy, sell, exchange, and hold supported digital assets, as well as use available payment services to spend their funds.

Wirex also hosts a cross-chain bridge, offers crypto-linked cards, and a Cryptoback rewards programme, which advertises rewards of up to 8% on eligible purchases.

Actual rewards depend on the card programme, subscription, and applicable conditions. Wirex is not a traditional bank; its services are provided through regulated entities and financial partners, depending on the customer’s location.

Mercury is a US-based financial technology company providing business banking services through partner banks. It is particularly relevant to eligible cryptocurrency and Web3 startups, DAOs, and investment funds that need traditional financial infrastructure to manage their operations.

Businesses can use Mercury to manage fiat deposits, payments, expenses, and transfers to supported cryptocurrency exchanges, including Coinbase and Gemini. However, Mercury does not hold cryptocurrencies or provide crypto trading services.

It supports most eligible US-registered businesses, including those founded by international entrepreneurs, but does not accept applications from crypto exchanges or money services businesses.

Monzo is a UK-based digital bank that allows customers to use their accounts with selected cryptocurrency exchanges.

Although it does not offer native cryptocurrency trading, customers can transfer money to supported platforms, including Coinbase, making it an option for investors who want to manage traditional banking and crypto purchases separately.

Monzo applies transaction monitoring and restrictions to cryptocurrency-related payments. For example, it currently blocks payments to Binance while allowing transfers to a range of other exchanges. Its cryptocurrency payment allowance is also limited, so customers should check the latest terms before making large transactions.

Ally Bank is a US-based digital bank offering traditional banking products, including checking accounts, savings accounts, and investment services. Unlike specialist crypto-friendly financial platforms, Ally does not provide direct cryptocurrency trading or cryptocurrency custody through its banking services.

Customers cannot transfer cryptocurrencies directly to Ally accounts, which accept transactions in US dollars. Ally Invest also does not currently support direct cryptocurrency trading, although customers can access certain crypto-related investment products, including cryptocurrency funds and publicly traded companies involved in the industry.

Ally is therefore better suited to customers looking for conventional banking and investment services rather than direct access to cryptocurrency exchanges or digital assets.

Choosing a crypto-friendly bank involves more than finding a financial institution that allows cryptocurrency transactions. The right option depends on your location, investment activities, and whether you need basic exchange transfers or more advanced crypto-related services.

Here are five important factors to consider:

Check whether the bank supports transfers to your preferred cryptocurrency exchanges, such as Coinbase, Kraken, or Binance. Some financial institutions restrict payments to specific platforms or apply additional security checks to crypto-related transactions.

Compare account maintenance fees, international transfer costs, currency conversion charges, and any limits on cryptocurrency-related payments. Some providers also charge separate fees for buying, selling, or withdrawing digital assets.

Look for providers with appropriate regulatory authorisation and transparent security policies. Check whether eligible fiat deposits are covered by a recognised deposit guarantee scheme and understand that cryptocurrency holdings generally receive different protections.

Crypto-related banking services are not available everywhere. Before opening an account, check whether the provider accepts customers in your country and whether its cryptocurrency features are accessible in your region.

If you operate a Web3 startup, blockchain company, or cryptocurrency-related business, check the provider’s business eligibility requirements. Some platforms, including Mercury, support eligible technology and Web3 companies but exclude certain activities, such as operating a cryptocurrency exchange or money services business.

Crypto-friendly banks and financial platforms make it easier to manage traditional money and cryptocurrency in one place. However, their safety depends on regulation, banking partnerships, and security measures.

An important distinction is that eligible fiat deposits may be protected by deposit guarantee schemes, while cryptocurrencies generally are not. Crypto assets also carry risks such as market volatility, custody issues, and potential losses.

Not every crypto-friendly platform is a licensed bank. Providers such as Wirex and Mercury may operate through regulated entities or partner banks. Before choosing one, check its licences, account terms, and what protections actually apply to your funds.
