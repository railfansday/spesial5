Bitcoin’s mining difficulty falls 9%, easing pressure on miners

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The November 14 adjustment reversed most of October’s difficulty increases. Expected coin earnings improve, but profits still depend on price, fees and costs.

Bitcoin’s mining difficulty fell 9.1% on November 14, reversing most of the extra computational burden added by October’s two increases. The completed reset increases the chance that a given amount of computing work earns a block reward, providing immediate computational relief for operating miners.

The adjustment took effect at block 975,744, timestamped 05:02:50 UTC. Difficulty dropped from 148.36 trillion to 134.82 trillion, according to Mempool’s adjustment history.

Bitcoin recalculates difficulty every 2,016 blocks to target an average interval of roughly ten minutes.

October’s increases had lifted difficulty by a combined 11.8% from its October 3 level of 132.72 trillion. The first increase, on October 16, was about 5.9%; the second, on October 29, added about 5.5%.

The November cut removed 86.5% of the increase accumulated across those two adjustments. Difficulty now sits just 1.6% above the October 3 baseline. The reset therefore unwinds most of that added burden without taking difficulty all the way back to its earlier level.

At unchanged block subsidy and average transaction fees per block, the old-to-new difficulty ratio implies 10% higher expected BTC earnings per unit of computing power.

The gain is larger than the 9.13% difficulty decline because earnings per hash move inversely with difficulty. Dividing the previous difficulty by the new level gives about 1.1004, meaning the same work has a higher expected reward under those fixed assumptions. It is a calculation of gross expected earnings, rather than a measured increase in a miner’s receipts or profit.

The completed change also supersedes the estimate discussed in CryptoSlate’s November 10 mining analysis, which described a projected 6.5% cut around November 13.

A CryptoSlate Bitcoin snapshot captured at 18:17 UTC on November 14 showed $65,863, down 4.3% over 24 hours. The page said its price had refreshed about nine minutes earlier, well after the difficulty adjustment.

Luxor’s hashprice methodology distinguishes BTC-denominated expected revenue, which depends on subsidy, fees and difficulty, from dollar revenue, which also depends on Bitcoin’s price. Meanwhile, Mempool’s block-fee data showed varying fees around the adjustment, so unchanged fees are an assumption rather than an observed constant.

For an operating miner, the reset improves the difficulty component of expected earnings. Whether that translates into a viable margin still turns on the value of the rewards and the cost of producing them, including electricity, equipment efficiency and other expenses.
