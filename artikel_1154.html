Arsenal's tifo for crunch clash with Man City will NOT mention Premier League guilty verdict as insiders lift lid on plans for new design ahead of Emirates contest

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Published: 16:30 EST, 26 November 2026 | Updated: 10:13 EST, 27 November 2026

Tifos are back at the Emirates. Those loud, passionate designs which last season galvanised Arsenal en route to a first Premier League title in 22 years return on Sunday for the visit of Manchester City.

An emotionally-charged fixture between the title holders and the team found guilty of the majority of 115 breaches of the league's financial rules by an independent commission (which is being appealed by Man City) awaits.

Still, it can be revealed that Sunday's display will be more restrained than initially considered.

Daily Mail Sport understands that a supporter-led group, established by the Arsenal Advisory Board (AAB) last season, initially raised an idea with Arsenal of referencing Man City's charges in the tifo. However, following consultation with the club, collectively the idea was dropped.

Instead the tifo will be done 'in the Arsenal way to inspire the fanbase', a source says. They added: 'It will galvanise and charge the fans in the right way.'

These matters can often be delicate. For example, it's understood a debate was had over the north London derby tifo ahead of Arsenal vs Tottenham in November 2025 at the Emirates, which involved a montage of club icons from past and present. And at the very top, positioned deliberately, sat Sol Campbell.

Sol Campbell, perched atop this Arsenal tifo for the derby game against Tottenham last season, moved between the clubs in a highly contentious transfer in 2001

What Arsenal offered on that PSG night in 2025 caused embarrassment – a solitary white cannon on a red backdrop

His 2001 move from Spurs to the Gunners remains one of the rivalry's deepest fault lines. Arsenal initially thought it could be too provocative but proceeded as the group were adamant on the imagery.

The reception when the tifo unveiled was immediate. The players felt it. The supporters felt it. Thierry Henry himself shared the image on social media with a simple verdict: 'What a tifo! Thank you, Gooners!'

As revealed in the book 22 Years: The Inside Story of Arsenal's Title-Winning Season, later in the season Mikel Arteta met up with the creators to say thank you and gifted each of them a signed, framed picture of the Tottenham tifo. Stuart MacFarlane, the club's chief photographer, was also there to take pictures of the group alongside Arteta. The Spaniard privately values the energy these displays create and embraces them.

It's understood Arteta checks the tifo designs before they are finalised. Much work goes into creating them, as reflected in the final proposal for Sunday's tifo which had to be confirmed and sent to the manufacturers nearly three weeks before matchday.

The artwork is printed on a square shape canvas which is then cut out and backed onto a paper-like material. That is cable-tied to a huge rectangular netting and tied to the roof with further cables and ropes.

The design along with the attached apparatus weighs around a couple of hundred kilograms, which is a serious amount. The lightweight fabric also has to be fire retardant and meet weight restrictions.

The work involved in putting the tifo together has seen volunteers invited to the Hale End academy this Friday to cable tie the tifo and get the design laid out on an indoor pitch.

Budgets have to be met, too, for the group are allocated an undisclosed amount to cover tifos for the whole season. Blowing the budget on one or two displays would rule out further spectacles later in the season.

The latest design builds on a growing trend which Arsenal began to grasp just over a year ago, the mission to properly create tifos initially starting in April 2025. Arsenal had unveiled their own tifo ahead of a Champions League semi-final first leg against Paris Saint-Germain — the first to be draped from the Emirates Stadium's roof in the arena's history.

For those confused by the term 'tifo', it stems from the Italian word tifosi, meaning passionate supporters. The designs are an attempt to galvanise fans, building an atmosphere with powerful messaging and provocative imagery to unsettle opponents.

Arsenal boss Mikel Arteta checks the tifo designs before they are unveiled

Mikel Arteta said he hopes Manchester City's guilty verdict for breaching Premier League financial rules does not overshadow Sunday's crunch clash against Arsenal.

Man City were found guilty of the large majority of charges for breaking the league's financial regulations between 2009 and 2018, in a period which saw them win eight trophies. The Manchester club insist they're innocent and are appealing the independent commission's verdict.

When asked about whether the charges will overshadow the match, Arteta said: 'I hope that it doesn't because it's a process that is still open, it's not resolved and I don't know what is going to happen.

'I think when we have two teams at the level that they both are at right now with the quality players that are on that pitch, it's a massive game and a big game to enjoy.'

He added: 'That (the match) is the only thing that we should put the energy and the focus in. There's nothing else.'

Meanwhile, Arteta said there is a chance David Raya will be fit enough to play on Sunday.

Raya appeared to be in agony as he was forced off in the 70th minute of Arsenal's 2-2 draw at Newcastle last Saturday, hurting his left shoulder in an accidental collision with William Osula.

There were initial fears the goalkeeper could be facing an extended spell on the sidelines, with Kepa Arrizabalaga playing in Tuesday's Champions League match against Borussia Dortmund.

Arteta said: 'We still have a few hours before the game. We're still trying to get everyone back.'

On if there's 'a ray of hope' that Raya will be available, he added: 'Yeah.'

What Arsenal offered on that PSG night was widely mocked – a solitary white cannon on a red backdrop. That was it. A sterile design for one of the biggest European nights the Emirates had ever hosted, at that time.

It's understood a club-produced design had been used over a fan-led alternative, mainly because there was limited time between the proposal and the game itself.

The episode proved to be a pivotal turning point, led by the AAB.

The AAB is the club's formal fan-engagement body, created in 2021 following the backlash over Arsenal's involvement in the wretched European Super League plot — a breakaway competition set to feature 12 of Europe's leading clubs. The subsequent supporter anger forced Arsenal to improve their communication with fans.

After the PSG tifo disappointment, the AAB helped create a new group for the start of the 2025–26 season focused on creating tifos. Arsenal wanted to learn from the error, and this was their way of putting fans back in control.

It's worked. The outcome resulted in a slew of eye-catching displays in the previous campaign from Campbell to Leandro Trossard's eye-goggles celebration for the 'history in our sights' tifo ahead of the Champions League quarter-final second leg against Sporting Lisbon — or the 'over land and sea' design with a huge ship for the semi-final second leg against Atletico Madrid.

By softening the parameters and letting fan representatives into the inner sanctum, Arsenal have utilised a marginal gain Arteta had craved ever since he took charge seven years ago — make the Emirates a fortress.

While there was an initial temptation from the AAB to reference Man City's guilty verdict in Sunday's tifo, the overall message will be unmistakably clear: Arsenal are now in charge.
