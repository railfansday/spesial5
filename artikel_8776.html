The RearVue 820 (Garmin’s latest cycling radar) sets a new bar for on-bike radar systems. It improves on the Varia RTL515—our longtime benchmark, which is also on sale—with increased detection range, same-speed vehicle tracking, longer battery life, and a brighter taillight. Plus, when paired with a newer Garmin cycling computer, the RearVue 820 will track the lane location and size of approaching vehicles.

Still using your phone to track your rides? A cycling computer is a safer, more accurate way to navigate, display key riding metrics, and capture data from devices like heart rate monitors and power meters. Garmin’s Edge 540 packs computing power, battery life, and function into an 80-gram package. It’s a great upgrade for any road, mountain, gravel, or fitness rider.

Wahoo’s Elemnt Bolt 3 is a favorite of our editors and test riders for its small size, accuracy, and ease of use. The Bolt features a 2.3-inch full-color display, 20-hour battery life, and an 85-gram weight. For simple operation and a lower price, the Bolt uses buttons instead of a touchscreen.

Keep your hot drinks warm on cold winter rides. CamelBak’s Podium bottles are popular among gravel and off-road riders due to their open/closed twist-lock cap. The Podium Steel is a vacuum-sealed insulated stainless steel version of the brand’s classic plastic Podium model. It’s a classy-looking bottle and available in several colors. Plus, CamelBak offers custom printing on these bottles through its site.

Last winter, I tested 8 of the top insulated bottles in winter conditions. Read about the testing and results: “Some Insulated Bike Bottles Promise 12 Hours of Hot Drinks. Which Ones Survived Our Experts’ Sub‑Freezing Tests?”

Cycling radars are a new technology that gets a lot of attention for keeping riders safe on the road. Mirrors have been around for generations and do a great job, never require charging, and don’t chirp endlessly in traffic. I added a Spurcycle mirror to my custom bike this summer. I love its simplicity and clean looks—it doesn’t look out of place, even on high-end race bikes.

“Flat pedals win medals” is an age-old bike saying. While many riders have switched to clipless pedals for road and gravel, many prefer flats for mountain biking and commuting. They’re intuitive, compatible with almost any shoes, and you never have to worry about unclipping too late. OneUp’s Composite Pedals are lightweight and durable—plus they’re available in two sizes and six colors.

Lazer’s Tonic Kineticore helmet performs much better than its $80 MSRP suggests—and at $60 (25% off), it’s a steal. At 240 grams, the Tonic is relatively light for its price. It features a modern road aesthetic and has comfortable, easy-to-adjust straps. Lazer offers the Tonic in four sizes, and four color options are on sale. It’s a solid pick for any road or gravel rider.

Wahoo reinvented indoor training when it released its original Kickr in 2012. Now on its sixth generation, the Kickr remains our top pick for high-performance indoor smart trainers. The Kickr is super accurate, folds easily for storage, and is stable during workouts. The latest version adds WiFi connectivity, which enhances connection speeds during Zwift rides and races.

Tired of taking your bike off the trainer every time you want to ride outside in the winter? Don’t want to wreck your nice bike with sweat and salt from indoor rides? If either sounds like you, an indoor training bike should be in your future. Garmin’s Tacx Neo Bike combines its excellent high-end trainer with a fully adjustable bicycle frame. It also features integrated controls for riding virtual worlds like Zwift and Rouvy, which you can set up like your favorite drivetrain.

The Neo Bike Plus uses a step-through type frame and has adjustable seat tube length, top tube/reach, and bar height. You can also change crank length. This makes it ideal for households with multiple riders sharing an indoor bike. It’s also great for testing new riding positions before transferring them onto your outdoor bike.

Tights are a must-have for riding in temps below 45 degrees. Pearl Izumi’s Quest tights feature a soft ThermaFleece lining, thigh pockets to hold your phone or snacks, and a single-density chamois pad. Men’s and women’s sizing is available.

Stay warm on winter rides and commutes with Pearl Izumi’s AmFib gloves. These gloves feature Primaloft insulation, AmFib material to block wind and rain, and synthetic leather palms for grip. Unlike many winter gloves, they’re touchscreen compatible. The AmFibs also have an extended cuff, so cold air doesn’t get into your gloves or jacket.

Cycling shorts with a padded chamois are a must-have if you’ll be on the bike for longer than 20 or 30 minutes. Pearl Izumi’s Attack shorts are a long-time rider favorite; they have a comfortable chamois pad and leg grippers so they don’t roll up. These are a great pick for trainer rides, pairing with tights on cold days outside, or wearing under pants or shorts for commutes or mountain bike rides.

We love bib shorts for longer rides because they don’t sag and tend not to bunch up. However, many bibs can be a pain when nature calls, and we need to make a pit stop. That’s what makes droptail bibs so great: You don’t need to remove your jersey or jacket; simply drop the tail.

Endura’s AllTrack Ride bibs use the Scottish brand’s DropSeat design for bathroom breaks and have its 600 Series chamois for extra comfort on gravel rides or rough pavement. Plus, they have thigh pockets to hold your cell phone or snacks while you ride. At this price, you should grab a pair (or two!) before they sell out.

It’s important to have a warm, thermal jersey in your closet when the temperature drops. On its own or paired with a light vest, this fleece grid jersey breathes well and keeps you from becoming sweaty and cold. On colder days, wear the Attack Thermal under a windproof jacket to keep your core insulated and cozy.

For riders who only go full coverage when absolutely necessary, this waterproof cycling cap will be right in their wheelhouse. It offers extra protection from rain, sleet, and wind without added bulk. It’s thin and fits comfortably under a cycling helmet.

The CTY 1.1, by REI’s Co-op Cycles house brand, is perfect for commuting, getting around town or campus, or leisurely weekend rides. REI equips the CTY with 40mm-wide, flat-resistant tires, a dependable Shimano 24-speed drivetrain, and disc brakes. The bike is available in five sizes (to fit riders from 5-foot to 6-foot-3) and two colors.

The Topstone 3 is a perfect entry point into gravel riding. It’s also a great upgrade for riders who need a dependable all-arounder for fitness, commuting, or even bikepacking. The Topstone features a high-quality aluminum frame and a carbon fork, equipped with plenty of mounts for racks, fenders, and bags. For climbing hills, the bike has a Shimano Cues 2x10-speed wide-range drivetrain and easy-to-maintain cable disc brakes for descents. At under $1,400, it’s a great deal for anyone looking for a versatile gravel or all-road bike.

Scott’s Speedster Gravel 30 says exactly what it is upfront: a speedy gravel bike ideal for racing, group rides, or fast rides on unpaved roads. Sharing design elements with Scott’s more expensive gravel race bikes, the 30 features an aluminum frame, carbon fork, 45mm-wide tires, and a Shimano Cues 1x10 drivetrain with sure-stopping hydraulic disc brakes. The frame uses a UDH hanger, so it’s future-proof if you want to upgrade to a lighterweight groupset once you get addicted to the spirit of gravel.
