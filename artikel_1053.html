Hiding remotes and unplugging TVs: How Celebrity Race Across the World cut Jeff Stelling off from football

Jeff Stelling has revealed that producers would unplug televisions and hide remotes during filming of Celebrity Race Across the World, which left the veteran sports broadcaster completely cut off from football and his beloved Hartlepool United.

Stelling is taking part in the latest series of the hit adventure travel show, teaming with his son Matt in a 4,660-mile race from the Malaysian city of Kuching to the finish line in Melbourne, Australia on a budget of £26 a day.

The former Soccer Saturday presenter, who ended over 30 years of service with Sky Sports in May 2023, is a passionate lifelong Hartlepool fan and serves as honorary president of the National League club.

But as he traversed the depths of South East Asia with no phone and limited technology, he was left completely in the dark about the club’s performance at the tail-end of the 2025/26 season.

“We couldn’t get any football scores at all because we’ve got no way [of finding them out],” Stelling told The Independent.

“If you’re within your checkpoints, if there’s a TV in your room, they [the producers] make sure it’s either disconnected or else they don’t give you a remote, so they make sure that that’s taken away. You’ve got no contact.”

The fourth series of the show, which aired its first episode on Wednesday, was filmed from February to April earlier this year.

During that time, Hartlepool experienced a mixed bag of results - going on their joint-best win streak of the season with three consecutive victories, only for that run to be ended by their heaviest defeat of the season: a 7-0 hammering at Wealdstone at the end of March.

Stelling says he was out of the loop for all of this, however, which allowed him to let his dreams run wild as the National League season approached its climax.

“For me, it meant I could fantasise that we were winning every game,” he added. “And then when we finished, I found out the truth that we hadn’t been winning every game.”

Stelling was speaking on behalf of Enterprise as he fronts the ‘Local Legends’ campaign, which strives to celebrate the unsung heroes that give their time for non-league clubs week in, week out.

Hartlepool, who have spent six of the last eight seasons in non-league, ended last season in ninth, 12 points adrift of the National League play-off places.

His agony without any knowledge of the Pools’ performance was not a shared experience with his youngest son, though, with Matt a Brighton supporter.

“Well obviously, my relationship with this son’s a bit stressed because he’s a Brighton fan!” Stelling joked, whose older son meanwhile follows his father’s lead in supporting Hartlepool.

“No, we’ve always gotten on really well,” he continued, “but it’s sort of challenging for both of us, but probably more for him, that you are going to spend six weeks, 24 hours a day, with your dad - often sharing a bed or sharing the same floor.

“Different generations have got different views of things, so inevitably, over that period of time, in the sort of intense situation that you’re in, there are inevitably going to be occasions when you’ve got to bite your tongue, on both sides.

“But it was brilliant. As I say, we were close before. We’re probably even closer afterwards.”
