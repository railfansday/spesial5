Premier League Clubs’ Transfer Spend Between GTA 5 and GTA 6

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Grand Theft Auto VI finally arrived on November 19, 2026, more than 13 years after GTA V launched on September 17, 2013, when the most valuable wonderkids in world football were a very different group. In the time between the two, Premier League spending has gone to unfathomable levels.

Top-flight clubs have now spent more than £2bn in each of the last five summer windows, and summer 2026 set a new record of £3.56bn, all on top of the annual wage bills they pay out every season.

Here is how much the Big Six spent between the two releases, from lowest to highest, and what that investment returned in terms of trophies.

Following the release of the latest instalment of the popular video games series, players have noticed more than a few familiar structures on the map

Arsenal matched Liverpool's outlay, but their trophies came in two distinct eras. Four FA Cups under Arsène Wenger and Mikel Arteta kept the cabinet ticking over, before Arteta delivered the Premier League title in 2026, the club's first in 22 years.

They came within a penalty shootout of a first Champions League too, losing the 2026 final to Paris Saint-Germain. Whilst the Gunners have rarely been the big spenders, their last big signing, Declan Rice for £105million, has been a perfect deal thus far and recent spending under former sporting director Edu helped build a squad that looks sustainable for the foreseeable future and years to come.

Liverpool's spending was relatively measured for most of this period. Jürgen Klopp built a side that won the Champions League in 2019 and ended a 30-year wait for the league title in 2020, before Arne Slot added another title in 2025.

The Reds were credited with an incredible spell of transfer deals where the likes of Mohamed Salah, Sadio Mané, Roberto Firmino were all captured for low fees relative to the market before exploding into world-class performers. Whilst there were big signings with Darwin Núñez, Virgil Van Dijk and others, the Reds have typically recouped strong money on top players, with Phillipe Coutinho's £142million sale a clear example of their shrewdness in the market.

Spurs sold Gareth Bale to Real Madrid for a world-record fee just before GTA V arrived, and they have been reinvesting ever since. They reached the Champions League final under Mauricio Pochettino in 2019, only to lose to Liverpool.

With Daniel Levy ensuring Spurs were financially frugal up until his exit last year, their spending habits rarely matched any of the big boys. However, their 2026 summer spending, which totalled nearly £400m was the highest in the club's history, in what was a true statement of change. Still, their only trophy came in 2025, when they won the Europa League. That works out at roughly £1.7bn for a single piece of silverware.

The hugely-anticipated game represents a long period of time, and football fans are throwing it back to 2013 looking at the most valuable wonderkids.

Manchester United are the cautionary tale. They broke the world transfer record to bring Paul Pogba back in 2016 and have rarely stopped spending since, yet they have not won the league since Sir Alex Ferguson's final season in 2013. Still a major force, they have been able to attract big names but they have failed to build a sustainable squad and setup capable of recapturing their former dominance.

Despite their overall struggle for power, there's still been two FA Cups, two League Cups and the 2017 Europa League to note, an outlay that only Chelsea and City have beaten. However, we can't ignore their transfer shockers, which include Pogba, Antony and Jadon Sancho, all of whom cost significant figures near to £100m and left for almost nothing.

Manchester City spent around £400m less than Chelsea and won twice as many trophies. Under Pep Guardiola, they won seven Premier League titles, including four in a row from 2021 to 2024, and finally lifted the Champions League in 2023 as part of a treble.

Guardiola has since moved on, and Enzo Maresca's rebuild brought another huge summer in 2026, headlined by Enzo Fernández's £125m move from Chelsea. However, the ongoing investigation into their 100-plus charges certainly puts a cloud over their achievements as their financials have been investigated since the 2010s.

No club has spent more since GTA V came out. Chelsea's outlay accelerated under BlueCo, who broke the British transfer record twice in six months in 2023 with Fernández (£106.8m) and Moisés Caicedo (£115m). Spend to win has been the motto since Roman Abramovich took over the club in 2003, and it has continued to be a mantra even after his departure. Another midfield addition has been touted for over £100million in the near future, too, with Bournemouth's Alex Scott.

The spending has brought silverware despite the many differing periods of instability. Chelsea have won the Champions League, the Europa League, the Conference League, two Club World Cups and two Premier League titles in that time, becoming the only club in football history to win every single trophy at club level across that period.
