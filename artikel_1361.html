Man City are failing to give their supporters something to stand behind as Enzo Maresca's side radiate uncertainty with meek display in Fulham draw, writes JACK GAUGHAN

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Published: 11:04 EST, 21 November 2026 | Updated: 14:16 EST, 21 November 2026

The bananas are disappearing now winter is coming, the fire and fury waning with each passing week. In legal limbo, Manchester City’s supporters need something – anything – to stand behind but increasingly their team refuse to provide that solace.

City were booed off at the end as Enzo Maresca ducked down the tunnel. Some of that could have been directed at referee Thomas Bramall, after Erling Haaland’s disallowed goal, but certainly a significant number were jeering what they’d just seen. That simply does not happen at the Etihad Stadium, even during the torrid run of two years ago.

But they are witnessing a squad who radiate uncertainty. Who take three touches when two will do; who always receive possession squarely facing their own goal; who do not create passing lines for their mates. And crucially, who are forever momentarily confused as to who exactly is picking up which runner and those split seconds make all the difference in this division.

There is just this strange state of unease around how they want to approach games at the moment, something that adversity can provoke and something that Maresca is adamant he can correct. A coach of his calibre should do given the time and freedom.

Yet when he spent the two-week international break studying clips and fashioning ways of improving, he cannot have envisaged a scenario whereby home fans were so incredulous at a lack of energy that they were roused to their feet when Haaland deigned to close down Bernd Leno and force an error.

Man City put in an underwhelming performance as they were held to a 1-1 draw by Fulham

Enzo Maresca's side are radiating uncertainty and look worryingly vulnerable at the back

Rodrigo Muniz made City pay as he put an impressive Fulham 1-0 ahead after 16 minutes

Fulham popped it about nicely and at will. Found pockets to hurt their illustrious hosts, who were again asking a centre half – in this case Marc Guehi – to charge into midfield. City did have more balance than at Nottingham Forest for including Ayyoub Bouaddi for Enzo Fernandez, yet Alvaro Arbeloa still aggressively pressed them in that area to some success.

‘I told the players that we will have the chance to beat them if we play with courage,’ said Arbeloa, who was indebted to the towering performance of defender David Affengruber.

Arsenal will have watched this grinning and could easily be racking up an unassailable lead once these teams meet in north London next Sunday. Unassailable with all the usual caveats about the appeal and what this season may or may not eventually mean.

City did create enough statistically but as with other games of late, that did not tell the whole story.

Calvin Bassey pirouetted through midfield, the heavily derided Gianluigi Donnarumma thwarting a number of strong Fulham chances, and City appeared somewhat lost and out of ideas.

Elliot Anderson equalised with a superb volley but City failed to create much after that

Maresca expressed his concern at City's lack of clean sheets after they struggled again

That is a worrying departure from recent defeats, where they attacked with the same abandon as that early season flourish when they might as well have hosted games at Madison Square Gardens and played with an orange ball.

Met with a Fulham who had not conceded more than once in the league since the start of September, City failed to create a meaningful opportunity in the last 25 minutes of a match they were desperate to win and should have been in the ascendancy of after Elliot Anderson’s volleyed equaliser after half time.

Maresca, though, is more concerned with a lack of clean sheets. It’s now none of those in nine games in all competitions for the first time in seven years, with Rodrigo Muniz opening the scoring on 16 minutes. A throw inside Fulham’s own third prompted Guehi to jump and lose his duel, City turned around and Oscar Bobb laid on for Muniz.

‘We’re conceding too many goals,’ Maresca said. ‘We need to avoid it. You need to win games 1-0. You need clean sheets. We’re not able to do it. But I can see many positive aspects. We started four months ago and not four years ago.

‘We’ve conceded almost 80 per cent of our goals at the start of the first half and second half. It’s something we need to improve. It’s a throw in at the corner flag and nobody is expecting they can create something.’

Maresca was also insistent that Haaland ought to have been awarded an equaliser before the break when poking in on the goal line, yet Bramall adjudged that Leno had the ball under control on field, with VAR not overruling.

Some hard luck, some profligacy with Haaland squandering a couple of big chances, but the day was summed up when in stoppage time, Jeremy Doku was penalised for a foul throw.
