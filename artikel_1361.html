Leeds Ditched Star Labelled ‘Worse Than Augustin’, Now He’s Scored 11 CL Goals

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Leeds United have made some excellent signings in recent years, none more so than current club captain Ethan Ampadu, as well as England duo James Trafford and Dominic Calvert-Lewin.

Ampadu has taken the jump from the Championship to the Premier League like a duck to water, while Trafford and Calvert-Lewin both have the quality to take Leeds to the next level after they consolidated their spot in the top flight in the first season after promotion.

Calvert-Lewin is even attracting interest from rivals Manchester United but, according to GIVEMESPORT sources, he is set to extend his contract to stay at Elland Road instead.

It's fair to say that, like many clubs, the Yorkshire outfit haven't always got it right with their transfer business.

There are already big doubts over summer addition Jean-Matteo Bahoya, who was viewed as an exciting signing but who has barely been picked by Daniel Farke, with the club already making a decision on terminating his loan spell in the January transfer window.

Bahoya isn't the only forward to be a flop, so far at least, at Elland Road in recent years, with Facundo Buonanotte and Jean-Kevin Augustin two more prime examples.

Leeds signed the former on loan from Brighton and Hove Albion in January of this year to boost their survival battle, but he played just 36 minutes of Premier League football between then and the end of the campaign, before returning to the Seagulls.

The story of Augustin is even more interesting, from a player who signed on loan from RB Leipzig, to one who ended up costing the Whites around £40m, despite playing only 48 minutes of football for them.

Leeds signed Augustin on loan with a £18m obligation to buy the player if they won promotion to the Premier League, although they argued that, because the Championship season had been interrupted due to the coronavirus pandemic and not completed until July, Augustin's contract should have reverted to Leipzig at the time they actually went up.

Leeds lost their appeal and agreed on a £15.5m compensation package with Leipzig, while they were also ordered to pay Augustin himself £24.5m in compensation because of the failed permanent transfer.

Augustin will undoubtedly go down as Leeds' worst ever signing for that reason and the number of minutes paid, but Leeds content creator Joe Wainman told GIVEMESPORT previously that Weston McKennie was 'arguably worse than Augustin'.

He said: "The mad thing is, I'm that vitriolic towards last season and the demise of the football club, with the poor acquisitions that we made and the coach that was in charge made in Jesse Marsch, that I would say with chest that Weston McKennie is arguably a worse signing than JKA, and JKA played about 15 minutes.

That's how strongly I feel about that. He seemed disinterested. We saw nowhere near the player that he's shown in the past, as you've mentioned.

That is a big statement that might have been in the heat of the moment, but there was no doubt that McKennie was a big disappointment at Elland Road.

Leeds signed the USA international on loan in January 2023 until the end of the season, with the option to make the deal permanent for ££30m.

Speaking on his arrival, McKennie when asked what he was going to bring, said: "Hard work, non-stop 90 minutes back and forth, tackles, trash talk probably and everything - funny moments and serious moments.

It's a dream come true, I've dreamed of playing in the Premier League for the longest time. To have the support system I have here, I'm ready.

Some Leeds fans would certainly question the work-rate they saw from the American in the final months of the season, as they were relegated from the Premier League and McKennie returned to his parent club.

Having failed to reach the levels he had done earlier in his career during his time in Yorkshire, it would have been a surprise to supporters that his career has continued on an upward trajectory, with the 28-year-old establishing himself as a key player for Juve.

McKennie scored nine goals and registered eight assists for the Serie A giants last term, including netting four times in the Champions League, taking his career total in the competition to 11.

McKennie may not be a well-loved figure at Leeds after the role he played in their demise that season, but he has shown the quality he has in his locker, which makes his Elland Road spell even more disappointing.
