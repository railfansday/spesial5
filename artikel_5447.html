Pony AI beat revenue expectations as its robotaxi business kept expanding

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Robotaxi services revenue rose 165.4% to $17.8 million, while the net loss widened to $136.3 million on trading securities losses

Pony AI reported third-quarter revenue of $44.6 million on Wednesday, up 75.2% from $25.4 million a year earlier, as its robotaxi business grew.

That topped the $37.5 million analyst estimate, according to Bloomberg. Bloomberg also said robotaxi revenue growth was the slowest of the year for the business.

Robotaxi services revenue rose 165.4% to $17.8 million and made up about 40% of the total, the company said. Fare-charging revenue increased by close to 400%. Robotruck services revenue rose 76.3% to $18.0 million, and intelligent solutions revenue was $8.9 million, compared with $8.6 million a year earlier.

The robotaxi fleet reached 2,768 vehicles as of Sept. 30. The company is working toward a year-end target of 3,500 vehicles.

"We remain on track to achieve our 2026 targets: expanding our Robotaxi fleet to 3,500 vehicles deployed in 20 cities worldwide by year-end, while growing full-year Robotaxi services revenues to 3.5 times the 2025 level," Chief Financial Officer Leo Wang said in a statement.

Gross profit rose 69.5% to $7.9 million. Loss from operations narrowed to $68.7 million from $69.7 million a year earlier.

The net loss widened to $136.3 million from $61.6 million, a swing the company attributed chiefly to declines in the fair value of its trading securities. Net loss attributable to Pony AI was $105.6 million, compared with $61.3 million a year earlier.

In China, the company said its previously separate operating areas in downtown Guangzhou and Nansha District now form a single contiguous area of more than 1,000 square kilometers (386 square miles). It added newly permitted driverless vehicles to its operations in Shanghai’s Pudong New Area and has begun deployment in Hangzhou, Chongqing and Hengqin in Zhuhai.

Overseas, Pony AI began what it described as Europe's first fully driverless test rides with invited passengers in Zagreb, Croatia, in September. The company is advancing deployments in London and Manchester, with road testing under way in Manchester.

Pony AI's U.S.-listed stock is down 72% year to date.
