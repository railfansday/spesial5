Lundbeck is exploring deals to expand into psychedelic medicines

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

CEO Charl van Zyl says the drugmaker has no active neuropsychiatry projects in its pipeline, and Rexulti loses patent protection in 2029

Lundbeck, the Danish drugmaker, is exploring deals to expand into psychedelic medicines, Chief Executive Charl van Zyl told The Wall Street Journal.

"We are mapping the space and we have quite active conversations around what is happening in this landscape," Van Zyl said, according to The Wall Street Journal. "There are some more late-stage companies and then there are also obviously more early-stage-profiled companies. We keep looking at all these options."

Lundbeck developed the antidepressants citalopram and escitalopram, according to The Wall Street Journal. In recent years it has moved its focus to migraine and uncommon neurological disorders. Van Zyl said moving into psychedelics would keep the company faithful to its heritage in psychiatric medicines while giving it a way to build options in a field where it has scaled back its own in-house research.

"It's true to say that in our pipeline we don't have active projects in neuropsychiatry," Van Zyl said.

Rexulti, Lundbeck's top-selling product, loses its patent protection in 2029. Van Zyl said the headway made in recent years means revenue should trace a valley-shaped curve instead of plunging abruptly when exclusivity ends.

The company's fortunes now hinge more and more on Vyepti, its migraine drug, and on medicines still in development, according to The Wall Street Journal. The most advanced is bexicaserin, a treatment for a rare form of epilepsy that Lundbeck gained when it bought Longboard Pharmaceuticals for $2.6 billion in 2024. Van Zyl said acquisitions are not the only pillar of the company's strategy, but it is nonetheless pursuing deals that could reinforce its pipeline.

"We continue to shop even if we're not hungry," Van Zyl said, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Van Zyl has steered Lundbeck away from neuropsychiatry since he became CEO three years ago, he said. In psychiatric illnesses, the underlying problem is frequently harder to pin down, and medicines are more likely to fail, he said.

Other large drugmakers have moved into the field. Eli Lilly $LLY agreed in July to acquire AtaiBeckley for roughly $2.8 billion in cash, with up to roughly $1 billion more tied to development and regulatory milestones. Johnson & Johnson $JNJ led a $100 million financing in Delix Therapeutics, which develops psychedelic-inspired depression treatments.

Spravato, Johnson & Johnson's depression treatment, is a nasal spray built on ketamine, a psychedelic-like drug, and it brought in nearly $1.7 billion in worldwide sales last year, according to The Wall Street Journal.

The Food and Drug Administration also held a public hearing in September on therapeutic uses of psychedelic drugs. It followed an April executive order from President Donald Trump directing the Department of Health and Human Services and the FDA to support psychedelic drug development.
