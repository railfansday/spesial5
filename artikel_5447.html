Activist investor is pushing Invitation Homes to keep selling off its houses

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Land & Buildings wants at least $2 billion a year returned to shareholders and says talks with the board have gone nowhere

Jonathan Litt's Land & Buildings Investment Management is pushing Invitation Homes to keep selling houses and to return more cash to shareholders, according to The Wall Street Journal. The activist hedge fund intends to mail a letter to Invitation's investors spelling out what it wants.

Land & Buildings says its discussions with Invitation's executives and directors failed to produce any progress on its worries, according to The Wall Street Journal, which reviewed the letter. The firm owned less than 1% of Invitation as of June 30.

The letter says Invitation should continue disposing of homes "one at a time" while its stock sits at a discount to net asset value, a figure Land & Buildings pegs at roughly $45 per share, according to The Wall Street Journal. Invitation Homes stock was at $26.85 at its latest close.

The firm also wants Invitation to hand shareholders $2 billion or more each year, in the form of buybacks or dividends. Among its additional demands are a 6% rebate on the down payment for renters who purchase the homes they lease, along with scaled-back acquisitions and development.

“The Board should say plainly that it will keep selling homes and returning capital for as long as the discount persists, even if that ultimately means liquidating the Company,” Litt wrote in the letter, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Land & Buildings argues that a new law will cap Invitation's room to expand, and that the company's cash is best spent repurchasing its own stock. Invitation Chief Executive Dallas Tanner has said the company can work within most of the regulations proposed to carry out the law.

Dallas-based Invitation is valued by the market at roughly $15.5 billion and held close to 86,000 houses in September. Its stock has lost over 30% across the last five years, according to The Wall Street Journal, which pointed to higher interest rates and the new law as factors.

In the third quarter, Invitation sold more than 600 homes, spent $245 million on stock buybacks and bought more than 200 homes, according to The Wall Street Journal.

The measure, H.R. 6644, became law on July 11 and will bar institutional investors from buying additional single-family homes without requiring them to sell existing holdings. It defines an institutional investor as one owning 350 or more single-family homes. Exceptions include build-to-rent developments and a program that lets investors buy homes to renovate and offer tenants a path to ownership.

Invitation was marketing 549 homes as of July, and it bought the homebuilder ResiBuilt earlier this year.

Land & Buildings, based in Stamford, Connecticut, concentrates on investing in listed real-estate firms. It has pressed for changes at firms such as Equity Commonwealth and First Industrial Realty Trust, according to The Wall Street Journal.
