Peltola widens narrow Alaska Senate lead over Sullivan as counting continues

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

The Alaska Senate race remains undecided with Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan and Democrat Mary Peltola are separated by fewer than 700 votes. Finn and Dunleavy discuss the impact of same-name candidate Dan J. Sullivan, who received thousands of votes, as the closely watched race remains unresolved.

Alaska's still too-close-to-call Senate race is expected to deliver another batch of ballot results Tuesday night after Monday's latest ballot drop expanded Democratic nominee Mary Peltola's lead over incumbent Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan to 2,517 first-choice votes.

Still current as of Tuesday midday ET, Peltola stands at 48.6% (106,321 votes) to Sullivan’s 47.5% (103,804) in the ranked-choice voting system that will kick in if neither candidate gets across the 50% threshold.

The controversial race, punctuated by the "Decoy Dan" scheme exposed by The New York Times, has Republican Gerald Heikes at 2.2% (4,865) and independent Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. at 1.7% (3,651 votes). Roughly 75–76% of the expected vote is in, with tens of thousands of early, absentee and questioned ballots still to be counted.

"After every vote is counted, it is clear that Mary Peltola will be elected Alaska’s next United States Senator," Peltola campaign manager Cheryl Bruce wrote in a statement Tuesday morning after that previous tranche of votes.

"Mary won today’s votes by a margin of 68-30, reflecting major support in key areas from Anchorage and Chevak. This overwhelming margin further strengthens Mary’s path to victory, and Mary is looking forward to continuing her relentless fight to lower costs and put Alaska first in the United States Senate after all votes are counted."

Monday's drop added nearly 5,000 ballots and marked a shift from the razor-thin, 637-vote Peltola edge that had held for days after Election Day.

The incumbent Sullivan and President Donald Trump have both suggested if Peltola does not get to 50% of the vote and ranked-choice voting (RCV) goes to rounds two or three that the Republican would absorb secondary voting strength in the system.

Under Alaska’s system, if no candidate reaches a majority of first-choice votes, the lowest finisher is eliminated and ballots transfer according to voters’ rankings until one candidate surpasses 50%. Right now, that would be the other Dan Sullivan, which might help Peltola break 50%.

The Official RCV tabulation is scheduled for Nov. 18 after the overseas absentee deadline.

The slow pace matches the timeline Gov. Mike Dunleavy described in an interview Sunday. He called the state’s process "very confusing" and "a slow system," pointing to significant ballot dumps expected Friday, Nov. 13, and Wednesday, Nov. 18.

Dunleavy has also continued an investigation into the "Decoy Dan" candidacy of Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., which some Republicans allege was intended to siphon votes from the incumbent; the governor has said the Division of Elections and the Department of Justice are reviewing related questions.

Democrat Scott Colom leads incumbent Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith, R-Miss., by about 2,500 votes (49.08%-48.81 %).
