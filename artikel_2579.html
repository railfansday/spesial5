Injuries dominate the Rams snap count review after 10 weeks

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

With the Los Angeles Rams taking a Sunday off in the midst of an abbreviated bye week, this edition of the snap count review will concentrate on a roster overview of the first 10 games 2026 season. Obviously with all the injuries, the comings and goings on the Injured Reserve List have had an undeniable effect on snap counts and play time percentage.

One of the interesting things about an 18 week roster review series is the ebb and flow of change. Many of the players seeing reps in the first five games have taken a step back, opening roles for new names/faces for the second five. And now, after the break, these next few games will see even more changes as the Rams roster evolves again. In a 17 week season, it is "survival of the fittest."

Which Rams do you think should see more reps during the stretch drive?

Players, their snap counts and play time percentage in bold.

Matthew Stafford (700, 98.6%), Stetson Bennett (10, 1.4%), Ty Simpson (0)

Stafford's 2026 stats are eerily on par with his career numbers, so why does it seem he's just not comfortable in this version of McVay's offense? The opening halves have been particularly frustrating. Problems against this next run of opponents will be magnified with slow starts much more difficult to overcome. There will be no late-season saviors for the roster, the L.A. high-paid talent has to lead. Staff has done it before, more than ever, it's imperative that he puts this offense on his shoulders.

Kyren Williams (467, 65.8%), Blake Corum (233, 32.8%), Ronnie Rivers (25, 3.5%)

Using both running backs on the field together may simply be a case of getting the best players on the field. Williams is still the clear RB#1 over Corum, but carries and touches are starting to even out. How the workload separates vs Green Bay will be interesting, Williams has been limited in practice with a turf toe, he is reportedly good to go for Thursday Night but seeing closer to a 50/50 split in reps should not surprise fans. Rivers has had a good long rest on the IR and definitely looked rested and bursty last week, both on kickoffs and running to close out the game. L.A. should look to use that to advantage.

Konata Mumpfield (317, 44,6%), Xavier Smith (89, 12.5%), CJ Daniels (52, 7.3%), Tutu Atwell (159, 22.4%)

IR: Puka Nacua (424, 59.7%), Davante Adams (327, 46%) Jordan Whittington (130, 18.3%)

PS: Alex Bachman (3, 1%), Brennan Presley (2, 1%), Tyler Scott

Dance with who you brung. It's simple, McVay and Stafford have to find a way to incorporate this group into the offense. Last week, tomato can opponent or not, they were involved and the offense appeared to run on all cylinders after a clunky start. The work was spread out, not so much in the number of players getting targets, but rather the balance, with almost all getting a work share. It was a good sign and the best way to utilize a thin room. Make the opposing secondary cover the whole field and account for every individual.

Colby Parkinson (375, 52.8%), Tyler Higbee (456, 64.2%), Davis Allen (367, 51.7%), Max Klare (30, 4.2%)

Certainly, the Rams use of multiple tight end sets has improved the run. While none of three are stellar blockers individually, they all work hard to gain position and help out in creating numerical mismatches. L.A. uses them as the pulling blockers as well. As receivers, they have not been explosive, but with a catch rate around 75 percent, offer a solid option. Although Stafford seems most comfortable targeting Higbee and Parkinson, of Davis' 13 catches, nine have went for first downs and three for scores. A decision looms for Terrance Ferguson, his 21-day window for interchange from IR to active roster closes on Wednesday. His ankle injury is well, but he's now fighting a hamstring problem. If L.A. does not activate him before the window closes, he's lost for the season.

Alaric Jackson (647, 91.1%), Warren McClendon (710, 100%), Kevin Dotson (704, 99.2%), Steve Avila (574, 80.8%), Beaux Limmer (139, 19.6%), Bill Murray (49, 6.9%)

David Quessenberry (67, 9.4%), Keagan Trost (0), Coleman Shelton (651, 91.7%),

IR: Justin Dedich (0), Dylan McMahon (13, 1.8%)

To date, it's been a strong performing group, ESPN rates the unit #2 in run block win rate and #7 for the pass, now fans will get to see how well the depth meshes. With wide receiver depth wafer thin, opponents will game plan to stop the L.A. run game. The pressure is on the line to overcome it, consistently creating space for the running backs and not allowing the offense forced to become one dimensional towards the pass. Individually, SIS Data Hub rates Dotson #1 and Shelton #2, with Avila and Jackson tied at #4. ESPN scores McClendon #7 in pass block and #10 in run block win rates. The IR window has been opened for Dedich and he's very likely to be available for the Packers game.

Ethan Evans, Brandon McManus, Joe Cardona, Joshua Karty

They have actually shown incremental improvement over the past two weeks. It should be noted that's not a high bar, the best descriptor for the unit's play in the first six week is shit-show. The good? Maximus Pulley, Grant Stuard, Shaun Dolac, and Damarion Williams are gamers in coverage. The bad? Rams gunners just aren't getting down field to pressure returners. The ugly? Josh Karty rates an active roster role for a specialty "corkscrew" kickoff, but cannot get in the regulated landing zone 50 percent of the time.

Aaron Donald (313, 51%), Braden Fiske (356, 58.1%), Tyler Davis (238, 38.8%), Ty Hamilton (135, 22%), Larrell Murchison (67, 10.9%)

IR: Kobie Turner (232, 37.8%), Poona Ford (153, 25%)

It will be hot news if Turner is ready to go on Wednesday, reports are of full participation in practice. The Packers have one of the NFL's poorest running attacks and starter Josh Jacobs is questionable. Rattling QB Jordan Love is paramount, he likes to take deep shots, so the Rams have to disaffect him with pressure in his face and keep him off his spot. Pass rush pressure is down this year from last, but going forward, having the full complement of interior players should change that trajectory.

Myles Garrett (94, 15.3%), Byron Young (489, 79.8%), Josaiah Stewart (395, 64.4%), Wesley Bailey (187, 30.5%), Desjuan Johnson (69, 11.3%), Darryl Peterson (20, 3.3%)

This next month of games is why you bring in and pay a Myles Garrett. No disrespect to Byron Young, who's returned a solid value and has worked to create an all-around game, but the L.A. defense needs its superstars to take it to the next level. Although it hasn't been a long break, hopefully Stewart's nicks and cuts are healed. A healthy Garrett will likely nibble away at his reps a bit, but he's played pretty well as a sophomore and a little more pass rush pressure may keep him on the field. The Rams have been smart in getting Bailey's feet wet, stay on the same protocol.

Nate Landman (458, 74.7%), Omar Speights (268, 43.7%)

It appears that DC Shula understands the unit's weakness covering passes. Opponents are game planning one-read, quick passes and the Rams are countering with more defensive back packages. Both Landman and Speights are down in their on-field percentage. While snaps are down, tackles, tackles for loss, and depth of tackles production have improved. Dolac has been trending towards more action in recent weeks, but it is still primarily mop-up work. He does show that, if pressed into extensive service, he should step right in and not miss a beat.

Quentin Lake (613, 100%), Kam Curl (571, 93.1%), Kam Kinchens (337, 55%), Jaylen McCollough (235, 38.3%), Maximus Pulley (0)

The Rams extensive use of "dime" coverages is a another example of getting your best players on the field. They are not a flashy group, just do solid assignment-based work. Secondary depth has allowed Lake to line up less in the slot on wide receivers. He is really quite good when tasked with coverage on tight ends and in run support. Getting better athletes on quicker/faster slot opposition makes the whole operation smoother. McCollough's use has been hit-and-miss, a handful of games at 40-60 percent of action and as many others with 20 percent or less.

Trent McDuffie (393, 64.1%), Josh Wallace (469, 76.5%), Emmanuel Forbes (212, 34.6%), Cam Lampkin (5, 1%)

PS: Damarion Williams (0), David Long, Nyzier Fourqurean

McDuffie, Wallace, and Forbes are all three at the top of NFL coverage rates. The three combined have allowed a catch rate of only 50 percent. McDuffie has been stellar from both the slot and outside, he also is very strong in run fits. Wallace has been a pleasant surprise outside, allowing the Rams to move McDuffie around. He's generally sticky, but his lack of speed shows when trying to stay attached on long crossing routes. Forbes has proven to be a solid CB#3. With Watson out, his workload will rise. If he can lock down a role outside, L.A. can use McDuffie in the slot and the pass defense should be stronger. Opposing teams have had more success attacking inside the numbers than out on the boundaries.

Injuries have certainly been a huge influence on the Rams season thus far and the worst by far has been self-inflicted, shooting themselves in the foot. The season opener was an ass kicking, but the other three losses were by a total of nine points. L.A. dominated the Broncos, but were did in by two special teams gaffes and an interception return. They did have a makable field goal attempt to beat Buffalo in OT. Those were losses to a pair of tough teams. The gut punch was coming out uninspired against a poor Arizona Cardinals team and allowing them to control the flow of the game game and pull off an upset. This could be the one that thwarts a playoff bid.

Now the Rams are in a must win situation and with the injury bug still still dug in like a tick, the Rams have to rely on their high-paid stars. There's still plenty of firepower remaining, but it's going to take them all to get this hay stacked in the barn before the storms. Stafford cannot wait until the second half to get tuned in, go with the hot hand at running back, and that "world class" pass rush has to earn its moniker. It's not just the players executing, the braintrust has to be on point as well. No more wasted timeouts and silly challenges, if the late-season games play to form, a lot will go down to the wire.

So for the rest of the regular season, the Rams are performing this high wire act on a razor blade, with slippery soles and no net.
