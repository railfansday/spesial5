Walmart Has Paid Investors a Growing Dividend for 53 Straight Years. Here's What $10,000 Starts You With.

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The retail giant is maintaining its title of Dividend King.

By Lawrence Rothman, CFA – Nov 21, 2026 at 5:20AM EST

Walmart (WMT +1.51%) grew into a popular shopping destination due to its ultra-low prices. Along with technology investments that have kept the company competitive with the likes of Amazon (AMZN -4.10%), it has regularly posted gains in sales and earnings over the years.

And it's still growing. In its most recent fiscal quarter -- fiscal 2026 Q3, which ended on Oct. 31. -- its adjusted revenue grew 4.5% year over year, helping drive a 4.8% increase in adjusted earnings per share.

In addition to investing for growth, Walmart rewards its shareholders with dividends. In fact, management has raised the payout for 53 straight years. That makes it a Dividend King, a title only earned by those rare companies with payout-hiking streaks of at least 50 consecutive years.

In February, the board of directors raised the quarterly payout from $0.235 per share to $0.2475 per share. With a payout ratio (dividends divided by earnings) of 37%, Walmart can easily afford that payment.

If you decided to invest just over $10,000 in Walmart's stock, you could purchase 78 shares. That would provide annual dividend income of about $77.

However, investors should note the less-than-average dividend yield. At the current share price, Walmart stock yields 0.8% compared to 1.1% for the average stock in the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%).

Walmart's stock also looks expensive right now based on historical valuation and compared to the S&P 500. It trades at a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 47, up from 40 at the start of the year. That's much higher than Walmart's 10-year median P/E of 32. Meanwhile, the S&P 500 currently has a P/E multiple of 24.

There's no doubt that Walmart is one of the world's most successful retailers, and it retains its competitive advantages by offering hard-to-beat prices. But its relatively low dividend yield and rich valuation mean I'd stay away from Walmart's stock right now.
