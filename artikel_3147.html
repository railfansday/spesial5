1 ETF That Can Turn $500 Monthly Into a $600,000 Portfolio That Pays Out $18,000 Annually

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Consistency can go a long way with this ETF.

Ask almost anyone how an extra $1,500 a month sounds, and I'm sure they'll say it sounds great. Ask them how it would sound to have it come in with no extra work, and I'm sure they'll tell you it sounds really great.

Getting to $1,500 in monthly dividends won't happen overnight for most investors, but history shows that consistently investing in the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF (SCHD +0.53%) can put you well on your way.

At its historical growth rate, $500 invested monthly into SCHD could grow into a $600,000 portfolio that pays out $4,500 a quarter, or $1,500 a month. Let’s take a look at how.

Since it began trading in October 2011, SCHD has averaged a 13.7% annual total return (including dividends). If it sustains that average, $500 monthly investments could reach $600,000 in about 21 years. Realistically, that could be tough to maintain, so we'll use a more modest 10.2%, its annual average excluding dividends.

With those returns, $500 monthly investments could grow to $600,000 in 25 years. And aside from having $600,000 to your name, one of the best parts is that you would've invested only $150,000 over that time, highlighting the power of compound earnings.

Right now, SCHD's dividend yield is 2.9%, but its average over the past decade has been around 3.2%. If we meet in the middle and assume a 3% yield, $600,000 invested in SCHD would pay out $18,000 annually ($4,500 quarterly).

Past results don't guarantee future performance, so we shouldn't assume SCHD will continue at its historical rate. However, its structure gives me confidence in its ability to sustain growth.

One of the best things you can do is take advantage of your brokerage platform’s dividend reinvestment plan (DRIP). When you enroll in a DRIP, your platform takes the dividends you're paid and automatically reinvests them in the stock or ETF that paid them instead of paying you out in cash.

Using a DRIP compounds the effect because the dividends you reinvest start earning dividends themselves and become shares that can appreciate over time. That's typically a better route than receiving relatively small cash payouts and will speed up the process of reaching the $600,000 mark.
