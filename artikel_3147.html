Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Peak freight rates and Panama Canal bottlenecks drove record results this quarter.

Dorian LPG Ltd. (LPG -6.78%) reported record quarterly financial results, characterized by peak freight rates and significant gains from opportunistic vessel sales. Management reported that the freight market hit new highs due to geopolitical conflict and logistical bottlenecks at the Panama Canal, which effectively shortened the global shipping supply. The company strengthened its balance sheet through a $368.4 million refinancing and the sale of four vessels since July 2026, generating substantial cash for fleet renewal and shareholder distributions. While Middle East exports have declined, management stated that U.S. production growth continues to absorb new tonnage, maintaining a constructive outlook for the VLGC sector.

The quarter ending September 30, 2026, has three headlines: escalating conflict in the Middle East, congestion at the Panama Canal, and record highs in the VLGC freight market. There are several aspects of the conflict in the Middle East that have had significant impacts on shipping and arbitrage economics, whether the initial collapse of the MOU, Houthi advances to the Red Sea coast, or the successful attack on the Saudi East-West pipeline. These aspects combine to create a backdrop for energy markets solving a shortage crunch for importing regions and explains energy markets acting with urgency. Meanwhile, the Panama Canal's critical role in U.S.-Asia trade cannot be ignored.

Record-high Panama Canal auction prices and severe delays were witnessed during the quarter amidst meteorologists concluding the coming El Niño season could be one of the most severe on record. These are the leading explanations for how the Baltic Exchange's BLPG3 calculated TCE climbed from about $82,000 per day on July 1 to about $198,000 per day on September 30. This nominal TCE is based on a roughly 60-day round trip rather than about 80 days if the ballast length is around the Cape of Good Hope, which increases the denominator for getting per-day earnings. Furthermore, the assessed TCE does not account for weather factors, delays, and other costs.

Increased delivered product prices after the MOU ceasefire and increasing freight rates incentivize solutions to increasing exports from the Arabian Gulf and vessels to chance transits through the Strait of Hormuz. Notably on oil tankers and VLGCs, Middle East exporters turned to own tonnage or willing ship owners to load at Arabian Gulf terminals and discharge to waiting vessels outside the Arabian Gulf. Additional seaborne tons of oil products and LPGs gave some relief to shortage-stricken countries in Asia and drove rates up.

However, because of irregular availability of vessels willing to transit the Strait of Hormuz, irregular scheduling to ensure a transit with least risk, and irregular supply of vessels to receive cargo in the Indian Ocean, sometimes with little notice, it is a logistically inefficient operation. Freight markets benefited from the inefficiencies, increasing vessel utilization. The requisite premiums for ship owners to take the risk of transiting the Strait of Hormuz acted as a locomotive on the reported rates. It should also be noted that while the shuttle services through the Strait of Hormuz have increased Middle East exports compared to the prior quarter, presently available data suggests export volumes are about half of pre-conflict levels.

The secondhand tonnage markets also benefited from the new trade, as Middle East exporting countries and risk-willing companies turned to purchase prompt tonnage to outright own vessels and secure a regular supply of vessels able to attempt transits through the Strait of Hormuz. The successful attack on the Saudi East-West pipeline to Yanbu by pro-Iranian interests had a significant impact on widening the arbitrage, as crude oil and LPG export capabilities were severely restricted. The Houthi alliance's capture of Mocha and Perim Island during September 10, 11 has further expanded their capacity to attack vessels in the Red Sea and drove up delivered prices in Asia.

Whereas several container alliances have committed to Red Sea transits, Bab-el-Mandeb is de facto closed to tankers and VLGCs because of expressed threats to Saudi origin cargo, as would be the case for Yanbu exports. The Houthi alliance's advance to the Red Sea coast happened immediately after a period of rising, record-setting auction prices at the Panama Canal. In August, there were 25 Neo-Panamax auctions with an average winning bid of about $2 million, with winning auction prices rising above $4 million five times. In July, there were 45 auctions with winning bids averaging about $1 million.

The reduced number of auctions reflected the reduced transit capacity and water management implemented by the Panama Canal authorities in preparation for El Niño. Through August and mid-September, the canal authorities issued multiple notices to the restrictions, adding planning uncertainty for ship owners and adding congestion when demand was peaking due to the elevated commodity markets. The new records set during this calendar quarter were primarily due to high demand from all shipping sectors, as seen the quarter prior, but it was the reduced transit capacity and ever-changing restrictions that made non-auction transits nearly impossible to accomplish. It should be noticed that the drought season in Panama has not yet commenced.

The additional costs associated with Panama Canal congestion translates to higher freight levels, and higher barriers to smooth canal transits incentivize sailings around the Cape of Good Hope. The longer transits from the U.S. to Asia absorbing vessel supply explain why VLGC loadings in the U.S. Gulf for this calendar quarter averaged about 102 a month, compared to about 113 a month the quarter prior, because North American production continues to grow. The Middle East conflict is therefore not the only point of attention for the VLGC market. The quarter ending September 30, 2026, mimics the prior quarter in that the Iran conflict has instilled urgency into worldwide commodity markets and exacerbated existing logistical inefficiencies.

Even with the VLGC market contributing to solutions for providing energy from the Arabian Gulf, knock-on inefficiencies have presented itself. While there are no obvious silver bullets to address congestion at the Panama Canal, past experiences and the company's flexible fleet will help overcoming those obstacles in the coming quarter, and we remain well-positioned to capture arising opportunities.
