Crypto trading still forces users to choose between convenience and control

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

A candlestick chart on a dark screen shows prices sliding lower, mostly in red candles.

As security concerns persist across centralized exchanges, a new generation of trading platforms is attempting to make self-custody easier without recreating the complexity of decentralized finance.

For years, cryptocurrency trading has involved a trade-off. Centralized exchanges offer relatively straightforward interfaces, but users must generally trust those platforms to hold their assets. Decentralized alternatives allow traders to retain control of their funds, although navigating wallets, blockchain networks, gas fees and bridges can make the experience considerably more complicated.

That divide has become increasingly relevant as security breaches and operational failures continue to expose the risks of centralized custody.

The challenge for developers is no longer simply to build decentralized trading infrastructure. It is to make that infrastructure accessible enough for users who have become accustomed to the convenience of traditional exchanges.

London-based cryptocurrency trading platform Byrrgis is among the companies attempting to address that problem. Its public launch on November 24 introduces expanded cross-chain trading functionality, token analysis tools and a simplified wallet experience built around a non-custodial architecture.

On centralized cryptocurrency exchanges, users typically transfer assets into wallets controlled by the platform. While this arrangement simplifies trading, it also means customers depend on the exchange’s security practices, financial stability and withdrawal policies.

Non-custodial trading changes that relationship by allowing users to retain control of their private keys and authorize transactions directly from their wallets.

However, self-custody introduces its own challenges. Users become responsible for protecting their wallets, understanding transaction approvals and navigating potential vulnerabilities in smart contracts.

It also does not automatically eliminate exposure to third-party infrastructure, particularly when trading involves cross-chain protocols or external liquidity providers.

The question is whether developers can reduce those operational barriers without introducing new forms of dependency.

“Security and control over funds should not come at the expense of a simple trading experience,” said Siraaj Ahmed, CEO of Byrrgis. “With our full public launch, we’re bringing together cross-chain trading, token analysis and portfolio management in one place, while allowing users to maintain control of their assets instead of handing them over to a centralized exchange.”

One of the persistent usability problems in decentralized finance is fragmentation.

Trading assets across different networks can require separate wallets, native gas tokens and bridging transactions. Users may also need to understand which blockchain holds a particular asset before executing a trade.

Byrrgis is attempting to abstract some of that complexity through a trading interface supporting Solana, Ethereum, BNB Chain and, according to the company, Robinhood Chain.

Its cross-chain routing system is designed to let users initiate supported swaps with a single transaction signature rather than manually moving assets between networks.

A feature called Universal Gas is intended to remove the need to maintain separate native gas-token balances for supported transactions.

These approaches reflect a broader direction in blockchain development known as chain abstraction, where the underlying network infrastructure becomes less visible to the end user.

Yet simplifying the interface does not remove the technical risks of cross-chain execution. Routing systems, liquidity providers and smart contracts can still introduce points of failure, even when users retain custody of their wallets.

Another challenge facing cryptocurrency traders is the amount of information required to evaluate unfamiliar assets.

Market liquidity, trading momentum and on-chain risk indicators are often distributed across multiple analytics platforms, making comparisons difficult for less experienced users.

Byrrgis has incorporated a feature called Scout Score, which assigns supported tokens a rating between 0 and 100 based on a combination of on-chain risk, market momentum and tradability indicators.

The scores update as market conditions change, offering a consolidated assessment alongside the platform’s token discovery tools.

Such systems may make complex information easier to interpret, but they also raise questions about transparency and reliability.

A numerical score can simplify research, yet its usefulness depends on how indicators are weighted, how frequently data is updated and whether the methodology can identify emerging risks.

A high rating should not be interpreted as a guarantee of safety or future performance.

Beyond cross-chain trading and token analysis, Byrrgis has introduced card-based cryptocurrency purchases, a redesigned wallet onboarding process and changes intended to improve swap execution.

The platform also includes tools for tracking holdings across networks and managing multiple positions from a single interface.

These additions illustrate a wider challenge for decentralized trading applications: competing with the user experience of centralized platforms while preserving the characteristics that distinguish self-custody.
