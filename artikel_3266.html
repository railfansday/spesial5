Here’s How Many Shares of Target You Need for $15,000 in Yearly Dividend Income

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Target looks like a reliable income stock, but it requires diving deeper into its tariff refunds.

By Jack Delaney – Nov 20, 2026 at 5:30AM EST

For an investor looking to generate $15,000 through dividend stocks, it's often to achieve a specific goal. It could be to replace part of a current salary or to cover increased living expenses in retirement.

Either way, it's about finding an income stream. Today, we'll look at how many shares of retailer giant Target (TGT +1.09%) it would take to generate $15,000 in annual dividend income.

To run through some quick math, Target has a forward annual dividend of $4.64 a share, or a forward yield of 2.91%. To receive $15,000 in yearly dividends, a shareholder would need about 3,233 shares. At an opening price of $165.93 on Nov. 18, buying that many shares would have cost about $536,451.

The real consideration, however, isn't how much Target will pay you in dividends this year. Rather, if you're going to invest that amount of money into the retailer, it's about whether those payouts are sustainable.

Target reported third-quarter earnings on Nov. 18 , with revenue rising 7.1% to $27.1 billion and earnings per share (EPS) of $2.65, beating Wall Street estimates of $2.07 a share. It also benefited from a $301 million pretax tariff refund, which added $0.50 in EPS.In Q2, tariff refunds added $1.65 per share to generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) and adjusted earnings. Also, the operating income margin rate of 9.6% benefited by 3.7% from tariff refunds.

Target's dividend appears to be reliably supported, as it's generating more cash and earnings than it pays out while keeping debt under control. In the first six months of 2026, cash provided by operating activities reached $4.5 billion, while a little more than $1 billion was paid in dividends. Target paid $2.30 per share in dividends in the first six months of the year, with diluted earnings per share of $5.83 during the same period. In 2026's Q3, Target also reported that its long-term debt and other borrowings were $14.2 billion, down from $15.4 billion in the prior-year period.

Target could become an attractive income stock for long-term investors, but it just needs to show it can keep improving its financial results without tariff refunds, which won’t last forever.
