Introducing the Waymo Accessibility Network UK: Co-Designing the Future of Autonomous Mobility

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Today, we’re launching the Waymo Accessibility Network UK to inform the future of autonomous mobility in the UK and enable a more independent future of transport. Whether it’s travelling to work, visiting friends, attending an appointment or simply enjoying everything a place has to offer, everyone deserves the freedom to travel with confidence and independence. Getting around should be about possibility, not barriers, and fully autonomous driving technology can provide more people with independent means of travel.

“Establishing the Waymo Accessibility Network UK is a vital step in ensuring our rollout of autonomous technology in London is inclusive, equitable, and aligned with local needs,” says Chris Ludwick, Waymo Product Management Director. “Working hand-in-hand with our UK partners allows us to refine, test, and deploy solutions that give passengers the freedom of truly independent travel.”

Waymo’s Accessibility Network provides a forum for leading British disability advocates and charities to engage with our team in a consistent, two-way dialogue, ensuring that diverse voices within the UK’s disability community actively shape how our technology is deployed on UK streets. Waymo’s Accessibility Network UK is an extension of our longstanding commitment to inclusive mobility and how we continuously improve our service based on feedback from the disability community. That feedback lives within our product today, helping inform features like audio cues and vehicle-finding tools.

Robin Spinks, Head of Inclusive Design at the Royal National Institute of Blind People (RNIB) says: “RNIB warmly welcomes the Waymo Accessibility Network, and we look forward to contributing alongside the blind and partially sighted community.”

London is a global leader in transport. But as we have been hearing from UK accessibility groups over many years, getting around can still present daily challenges, in particular for disabled and older passengers. For sight impaired Londoners commuting at peak times, finding the right vehicle on a busy street can be difficult. Guide dog users still face refusals in private hire vehicles. For older passengers and people with different physical or cognitive needs, even seemingly small aspects of a journey can make a significant difference. As we shared in our Vision for London, opening travel to all matters – and autonomous driving technology offers the opportunity to rethink some of these experiences.

The Waymo Accessibility Network will work directly with member organisations to support user research and participate in product testing. The feedback from members helps inform the passenger experience of our fleet and build upon Waymo’s current accessible design features, including:

We are honoured to collaborate with an exceptional group of national and community-based organisations that champion inclusion across physical, sensory, and cognitive disabilities. We welcome our 10 inaugural participants: RNIB, Re-Engage, AgeUK, Transport for All, Thomas Pocklington Trust, Guide Dogs UK, Sense, Motability Foundation, National Centre for Accessible Transport and Scope.

“Autonomous technology offers enormous potential to transform independent travel, but it must be shaped by disabled people from day one. By joining the Waymo Accessibility Network UK, NCAT hopes to ensure that disability-centred design sits right at the heart of the UK’s autonomous vehicle future.” says Clive Gilbert, Consortium Board Member at the National Centre for Accessible Transport and Head of Accessible Transport at Policy Connect.

“Thomas Pocklington Trust is delighted to be joining the Accessibility Network, along with other disability stakeholders. It is important that the voices of blind and partially sighted people are heard and that travelling in a Waymo can become a reality as the autonomous vehicle improves its accessibility, safety and other features. We want to build confidence for blind and partially sighted people, with independent travel on all modes of transport,” says Bhavini Makwana, Policy and Campaigns Lead at the Thomas Pocklington Trust.

Technology alone doesn’t remove every barrier. True accessibility depends on the streets we share, the vehicles we ride in, and the passengers we support. While Waymo can offer a major leap forward in independent mobility, we’ll always have more to learn. Bringing Waymo to the UK means doing so in partnership. As we work towards welcoming our first passengers in London, our goal is that Waymo becomes a trusted and valuable addition to the UK’s existing and iconic transport networks, and provides a new mobility option. Through Waymo’s Accessibility Network UK, we are ensuring that the people who experience barriers to mobility have a meaningful voice in shaping its future. We look forward to welcoming more UK organisations to the network as we work together to build safer, more inclusive streets for everyone.
