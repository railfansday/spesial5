Bush-appointed judge torpedoes Trump immigration rule, rips DHS for overstepping its authority

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A federal judge in Boston has blocked the Trump administration's $100,000 H-1B visa fee, stating it is an unauthorized tax without congressional approval. The policy, enacted last year, drastically increased the fee from $2,000-$5,000 to prioritize U.S. citizens. The White House plans to appeal the decision.

An immigration rule drafted by the Trump administration to plug an alleged gap in the visa system has met its end at the hands of a federal judge appointed by former President George W. Bush.

District Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV of Massachusetts ruled Monday that the Department of Homeland Security overstepped its authority by promulgating a rule that placed greater restrictions on visas for international students, exchange visitors and foreign journalists. His ruling represents a victory for the labor unions and university presidents that challenged the rule, and another loss in the Trump administration’s legal crusade to crack down on immigration.

The DHS rule would have replaced a longstanding policy by only allowing international students to study in America for a fixed timeframe, generally capped at four years, requiring them to apply for an extension if they wish to continue their studies. Previously, there was no fixed time limit for international students so long as they remained enrolled in a university and otherwise maintained legal status. The rule also would have imposed new restrictions on transferring schools, changing academic programs and pursuing additional degrees, while subjecting students to additional immigration reviews intended to combat fraud and national security threats.

"DHS acknowledges that there are legitimate reasons why a student may pursue another academic degree at the same or lower level as the one he or she has completed — for example, pursuing an M.B.A. after a J.D.," the judge wrote. "But the Final Rule would effectively prohibit such a student from entering the United States to pursue those studies. And the purported reasons given by DHS for doing so — protecting national security and preventing fraud — are not reasonably related to the nature and purpose of the F-1 visa program."

"Put bluntly, DHS is seeking to prevent or discourage the very activity — providing educational opportunities for foreign students in the United States — that Congress sought to permit and encourage," Saylor continued. "The statute simply does not grant DHS such broad and open-ended authority to undermine the F-1 visa program."

Saylor had already blocked the rule from taking effect in September, but Monday's ruling went further by striking it down entirely after finding that DHS violated federal administrative law.

Lawyers representing the department disagreed with the judge’s characterization, arguing that indefinite admission periods prevent immigration officials from periodically reassessing whether foreign nationals are complying with the conditions of their visas. Mandatory extensions, in the telling of the agency, would create additional opportunities to identify fraud, security risks and immigration violations — risks it says are substantiated by past cases of fraud and abuse in the student visa system.

"The performative outrage is incredible. Where was the outrage when foreign nationals were abusing the system, violating the terms of their student visas, and making a mockery of our immigration laws? If the media really cared about legitimate international students, they would be thanking President Trump for cracking down on the rampant fraud to ensure only those truly intending to study in the United States are given the privilege," a DHS spokesperson told Fox News Digital.

Saylor, however, further argued that DHS failed to establish how its new rule would constitute an improvement over existing systems meant to catch visa fraud.

"But DHS has not adequately explained how implementing an unreviewable, completely discretionary renewal system — after a foreign student has already been in the country for four years — meaningfully enhances the ability of DHS to root out cases of fraud, or how the benefits of such a system outweigh the immense costs of abandoning the existing system," he wrote.

Currently, DHS relies on the Student and Exchange Visitor Information System (SEVIS) to monitor student visa fraud. SEVIS is a federal database that monitors international students and exchange visitors, tracking their enrollment, immigration status and compliance with visa requirements through information reported by schools and program sponsors. The agency argued that the system is a "data-reporting system for schools" that is "not designed for performing all relevant fraud and security screening."

Saylor was identified as a Republican in a 2014 profile piece. The same piece noted Saylor had close ties to former Massachusetts Gov. Mitt Romney and that he was reportedly considered for a position in the Bush administration. He was confirmed by a bipartisan vote in the Senate, even winning the support of Democratic Sen. Ted Kennedy, a noted liberal, who praised him for his fair-mindedness and independence.

DHS did not tell Fox News Digital whether it intended to appeal Saylor's ruling.
