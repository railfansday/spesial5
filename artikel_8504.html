Decorating a Rental – 40 Ideas You Can Undo at Move-Out

You can decorate a rental your way without losing the deposit if every change either comes off clean or swaps back to the original at move-out. Choose peel-off, plug-in, and freestanding pieces, bag every knob or fixture you replace, and get the landlord’s written okay before you paint, drill, or touch the plumbing.

Bare walls, a glaring ceiling light, and dated cabinet knobs don’t have to last as long as the lease.

Each of the 40 ideas below passes the same move-out test. A rug, a lamp, or a tension rod comes off without a trace. A cabinet knob or a showerhead swaps back, because the original waits out the lease in a labeled bag.

Take a small kitchen with one 8-by-9-foot wall by the table and 20 cabinet knobs. Three rolls of peel-and-stick wallpaper at $25 each cover the wall, a $35 runner goes in front of the sink, and two $20 ten-packs replace the knobs, which go in a bag in a drawer. That’s $150. At move-out the paper peels off, the runner rolls up, and the old knobs go back on, so the landlord has nothing to take out of a $1,500 security deposit. Flat or freshly painted walls can lift with the paper, so test a small piece inside a closet for a week before you cover the wall.

Photograph every wall, floor, and fixture before you change it, since those pictures are your proof of the rental’s condition when you ask for your security deposit back.

Give every adhesive below the same closet test, from picture strips to backsplash tile, and remove each one slowly at move-out.

The Consumer Product Safety Commission’s Anchor It! campaign reminds renters as well as owners to anchor furniture to the wall, and it notes that patching the hole afterward takes minutes. Each anchor takes a screw, so get the landlord’s written okay before you put one in.

Put every original you swap out, from cabinet knobs to the showerhead, in bags labeled by room and taped inside a kitchen cabinet, so nothing is missing on move-out day.
