Dan Le Batard admits he deserves to be called a snitch after uncovering ‘White House’ story

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

If anyone wants to accuse Dan Le Batard of snitching on the 1990s Dallas Cowboys for their "White House," he's not going to argue it.

Former NFL wide receiver and current football analyst Andrew Hawkins was on The Dan Le Batard Show Tuesday morning. Hawkins also regularly contributes to Michael Irvin's sports podcast for Netflix, The White House, prompting Le Batard to express surprise about the show title.

"Michael Irvin, I am shocked that he has named his show The White House," Le Batard said ."For those of you who don't know some of this backstory, many years ago, when the Super Bowl was in Miami, I, showing no judgement at all that was good as news judgement, put in the 30th paragraph of a story that the Cowboys had a white house in Dallas that they called the White House to have parties with people who were not their wives."

The White House was known as the NFL's biggest party house in the '90s. Some have compared it to a brothel, Irvin has described it as a "safe place for camaraderie." Details on the house began to leak in 1996 after Irvin was involved in a hotel-room drug and prostitution arrest. But according to Le Batard, he put the Cowboys White House in the 30th paragraph of a story about sex and the Super Bowl decades ago.

The admission by Le Batard garnered unison chants of "snitch" from the rest of the show. An accusation Le Batard didn't even bother to deny.

"It's fair," Le Batard admitted. "It's actually fair commentary. But I put it in the 30th paragraph, I just tossed it in at the end."

"No one has ever called me a snitch before on that. And I deserve it. I absolutely deserve it," Le Batard added. "Pablo Torre is getting some snitch stuff, but that's not snitch stuff. What we just did there is snitch stuff. But it was the 30th paragraph."

Le Batard may have been one of the first to reference the infamous Cowboys White House in mainstream media, but it was only a matter of time before someone blew the roof off that safe place for camaraderie to expose it.
