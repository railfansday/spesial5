Nicolas Cage on his John Madden misfire: "I'm not Frank Caliendo"

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

This whole thing is antithetical to my identity.

That’s the one line that resonates most strongly in the new Madden movie. Because it’s exactly what John Madden would say if he saw it.

It’s bad. It’s very bad. Anyone who says it’s good has no taste and/or an agenda that involves the strategic application of lips to asses.

The biggest problem is Nicolas Cage as John Madden. Anyone who vividly remembers the unique charisma of John Madden knows instantly that nothing about Cage’s portrayal reflects the true nature, spirit, and demeanor of one of the most important and impactful figures in the entire history of the sport of football.

Cage, who seems to realize that his portrayal missed not only the bull’s-eye but also the entire wall on which the dart board was mounted, has defended the outcome by peering down his nose at the sports world.

“He was not on my radar,” Cage said of Madden, via Kirsten Chuba of the Hollywood Reporter. “I was growing up watching black-and-white Orson Welles movies and Fellini movies and Bruce Lee. I wasn’t interested in team sports whatsoever.”

“I’m someone that likes to stay out of his comfort zone and take a risk, I said yes, because that would be a transformation,” Cage explained regarding the decision to attempt to play the towering, iconic figure. “It would be something where I might learn a few things, taking on this character who is really nothing like me.”

The audience has learned that Cage’s interpretation is nothing like Madden.

“That’s what acting’s about,” Cage said. “I’m not an impressionist, I’m not Frank Caliendo. I lean more towards being a surrealist, meaning I like to get a concept of a personality and then dig deep and see what I can locate emotionally with my imagination and my psyche and filter it through the cadence and the appearance, within reason, of John Madden. I’m trying to give you an evocation, more or less, or an interpretation.”

The problem was it wasn’t even “within reason.” It was without reason. And the entire film was beyond unreasonable.

And I like Nicolas Cage. He was brilliant in Moonstruck. He won an Oscar for Leaving Las Vegas. And The Family Man remains a highly underrated holiday film.

Still, to quote another holiday film, “They can’t all be winners.” Cage’s role in Madden as John Madden is not.

I only met John Madden once. I watched him on TV for decades. I’ve heard plenty of stories about him from people who worked with him at NBC. And I’m confident that he would be livid about this film.

“That’s not me,” he’d say. “Not even close.”

Or, perhaps, “This whole thing is antithetical to my identity.”
