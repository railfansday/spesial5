Golden Fox: Lake City native helps US win Women's World Championships

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Nov. 16—HERNING, Denmark — Taylor Fox and the United States are golden again.

For the second time this calendar year, the Lake City native helped the U.S. senior national women's hockey team beat its biggest rival in the biggest game in international play.

Fox and teammate Megan Keller assisted on Hilary Knight's game-winning goal Monday at Herning Hockey Arena as the U.S. rolled to a 6-2 win against Canada in the gold-medal game of the 2026 Women's World Championships.

The win came nine months after Fox — then Taylor Heise — set up Keller for an overtime goal against the Canadians in the gold-medal game at the Milano-Cortina Winter Olympic Games in Milan, Italy.

This time, the Americans took care of things in regulation, and convincingly so.

Knight and Kelly Pannek scored twice, while Lacey Eden and Hayley Scammura scored once each for the U.S.

The Americans went 7-0-0 in the tournament and won all seven games by at least four goals. As dominant as they were in the Olympics — outscoring their opponents 33-2 and going more than 352 minutes without allowing a goal — they were arguably more impressive the past two weeks in Denmark.

The U.S. recorded four shutouts at these World Championships and allowed more than one goal in a game just once — Monday's game against Canada. Team USA outscored its seven opponents 56-4 en route to its second gold medal of the year.

As is her nature, Fox — who starred for Red Wing High School and the Minnesota Gophers before the PWHL's Minnesota Frost made her the first-ever No. 1 overall draft pick in league history — was somewhat quietly one of the best players in these World Championships. She scored just one goal, and was never named the Player of the Game, but she was named one of the Americans' top three players at the tournament, along with Knight and Caroline Harvey.

And, proving her playmaking ability, Fox had a tournament-high 13 assists, to finish second in the tournament in scoring, with 14 points. Harvey led the tournament with 16 points. Knight — who led all players at this WWC with nine goals — was third in the tournament with 13 points.

Canada opened the scoring in Monday's game, as Sarah Fillier scored 11:03 in.

It was all U.S. from there, as the Americans scored the next five goals — Knight in the first period, Pannek and Knight in the second, and Pannek, Eden and Scamurra in the third.

Fillier scored Canada's final goal, too, which cut its deficit to 5-2 with 16:28 left in the game.

U.S. goalie Aerin Frankel took care of the rest, stopping 18 of 20 shots.

Canada goalie Ann-Renee Desbiens made 21 saves.

Many of the players in the gold-medal game will now return to North America to quickly prepare for the start of the PWHL season.

Fox and the Minnesota Frost play their preseason opener at 8:50 p.m. Sunday against PWHL expansion franchise Vegas Vanguards in Henderson, Nevada.
