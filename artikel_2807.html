PFT's Week 10 2026 NFL picks

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

I have no explanation for the last two weeks. But I’ll use an unexpected run of success to provide a warning to anyone else who wakes up one day in the midst of a hot streak.

In Week 8, I went 13-1 in the straight-up-only picks exercise. (No more picks against the spread, for reasons I’ve previously explained.) For Week 9, I ended up 14-1.

That’s 27-2 over two weeks. (Which translates to 87-48-1 for the year, which doesn’t sound nearly as good as “27-2.”)

Now for the warning, specifically to anyone who has gambled or who will be gambling on sports. The worst thing you can do is get lucky. Because you’ll think that you’ve suddenly become good.

Then, you’ll let it ride until your luck runs out. That’s when it gets dangerous. Because you’ll fool yourself into thinking that it’s just a temporary setback, that your secret formula for picking winners remains viable, and you’ll chase your losses. You’ll keep digging that hole, confident that it’s just a matter of time before your sudden genius returns.

Eventually, you’ll find yourself in an untenable financial situation.

That’s how it happens for plenty of gamblers. They don’t start out losing and keep losing. They win enough to make themselves think they know more than they do, when the reality is that no one knows anything. And then the wheels come off. And then they get desperate to put the wheels back on.

Case in point: Last week, someone who has been part of the NFL universe for more than 30 years expressed amazement that the Cowboys were favored by only 4.5 points against the Colts. With the Colts in disarray due to multiple trades and poised to make even more trades, the Cowboys would win easily. It was, the person said, a “sure thing.”

There is no sure thing in gambling. Well, there’s one. If you ever let yourself believe you’re smarter than the house and you don’t snap out of it in time, it’s a sure thing that you’ll be filing for bankruptcy, eventually.

Can Dan Quinn and company come up with another way to screw things up in a key moment?

It’s desperation time for New England, like it was when they found a way to beat the Bills.

The Falcons are capable of winning every game. It’s just impossible to know when that version of the team is going to make an appearance.

Time is running out on Houston. And the Browns seem to be in the early stages of a backslide.

One of the craziest weeks in Vikings history ends on a high note, thanks to the Packers not being able to run the ball effectively.

With the uncertainty of the trade deadline over, the Colts can get back to focusing on trying to win football games.

If the Saints can put a full 60 minutes together, they could be very dangerous.

The Bills have a rough six-game stretch coming; they know not to fall victim to a trap game.

Liam Coen and Robert Saleh meet again. One has an elite roster. One does not.

The Cardinals still can’t figure out how to win a game at home.

It’ll take a little time to get Fernando Mendoza fully up to speed.

If it wasn’t already clear that the Cowboys are a long way from being elite, it will be.

Joe Burrow’s MVP case gets even stronger with a prime-time win.

Jacoby Brissett’s job is simple. Win a few winnable games so that the team will still be relevant when Lamar Jackson returns.
