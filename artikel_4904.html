Senate Passes Bill Banning Prediction Market Bets on War, Terrorism and Death

The Senate has passed a bill that would ban prediction markets from offering bets on war, terrorism, assassinations and deaths, sending the measure to the House of Representatives.

The Discouraging Exploitative Assassination, Tragedy, and Harm Betting in Event Trading Systems Act, or DEATH BETS Act, passed the Senate by voice vote on Wednesday, November 19, with an amendment, according to Congress.gov. The bill was introduced by Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA) in March, and is cosponsored by Sen. John Curtis (R-UT).

"With this bill, Congress is making it clear that these bets should be illegal," Schiff tweeted after the vote.

The bill would amend the Commodity Exchange Act to bar exchanges and clearinghouses registered with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) from listing or clearing event contracts that expressly reference terrorism, assassination, war or U.S. military operations.

It would also ban contracts that, at the time of listing, are "reasonably expected to resolve principally based on a death of an individual." The CFTC would be able to extend the ban to "any similar activity" by rule.

The bill would not affect the status of other types of contracts covered by existing CFTC rules on event contracts, such as those involving gaming, according to its text.

Prediction markets such as Kalshi and Polymarket let users trade on the outcome of real-world events, from elections and sports to geopolitics. Markets on wars and the fates of world leaders have drawn criticism from lawmakers, who have argued they let people profit from tragedy, and could reward insiders with knowledge of military plans. Polymarket users have been accused of insider trading after making $2.4 million on Iran-related predictions. Polymarket pulled a market on whether a nuclear weapon would be detonated after a public backlash.

Senate Democrats have raised concerns that people close to President Donald Trump could profit from war-related markets, and one senator has alleged White House corruption over Iran war predictions. Kalshi was also sued after refusing to pay out on a market tied to the death of Iran's then-Supreme Leader, Ali Khamenei.

The CFTC has also moved to curb such markets. In June, it proposed rules that would ban markets on outcomes that could come about through war or assassination, such as vaguely worded bets on when U.S. adversaries would be "out of office," which Kalshi and Polymarket had used to sidestep existing bans on war-related contracts.

War-related markets have also appeared elsewhere, including on Myriad, the prediction market operated by Decrypt's parent company DASTAN, where, where users traded on whether U.S. troops would enter Iran.

The bill's passage comes as prediction markets face growing legal pressure from states, which argue that some event contracts amount to unlicensed gambling. New York, for example, has sued Coinbase and Gemini over their prediction markets. The CFTC, which first sued three states in April to defend its authority over the markets, has now taken 12 states to court, most recently Louisiana.
