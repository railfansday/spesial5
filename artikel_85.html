Austria May Strip Former Foreign Minister Karin Kneissl of Citizenship

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The onetime foreign minister, Karin Kneissl, has lauded her new home for its “genuine freedom” and used it as a perch to attack her old one.

The Austrian authorities have taken initial steps to revoke the citizenship of a former foreign minister who moved to Russia — a country she has lauded for its “genuine freedom” — and who has repeatedly denigrated Austria while echoing Kremlin-friendly talking points.

Karin Kneissl made international headlines when she danced with President Vladimir Putin of Russia at her wedding in 2018, when she was her nation’s top diplomat.

Since then, she has angered many of her compatriots with regular barbs against her country of birth and its citizens.

That the Austrian state would even consider the extraordinary step of revoking her citizenship reflects a national weariness about the former foreign minister. It follows a prolonged national debate over whether Ms. Kneissl retained the right to remain Austrian after — both physically and by all appearances ideologically — turning her back on her homeland.

Ms. Kneissl, who served as foreign minister from 2017 to 2019, made her very public move to Russia in 2023, a year after Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine. She was hired to run a think tank in St. Petersburg.

Over the years, Ms. Kneissl, 61, seems to have taken pleasure in denigrating Austrians in interviews with Russian-friendly media. Earlier this year, for example, she repeated her claim that Hitler, who was born in Austria, was typically Austrian. She also said that her countrymen had acted like hyenas toward her.

Such comments served to bolster a push to strip her of her citizenship. The initiative has been led by the liberal party NEOS, which is run by the current foreign minister, Beate Meinl-Reisinger.

“Why does Kneissl continue to appear prominently as Austria’s former foreign minister when she so obviously despises and hates our country?” Yannick Shetty, a NEOS member of the Austrian Parliament, asked in January in response to Ms. Kneissl’s remarks.

On Monday, officials in Vienna confirmed that an inquiry into the former minister had been launched. They said they were looking at whether she had obtained a second citizenship and whether her conduct significantly harmed “the interests or reputation of the Republic.”

It remains unclear if the inquiry will actually lead to the revocation of her citizenship and, if so, how long it might take.

Ms. Kneissl was proposed for the post of foreign minister in 2017 by the far-right Freedom Party of Austria, though she was never a member of that party.

After leaving Austria in 2020, she went to France and Lebanon before moving to a little village southeast of Moscow in the heartland of Russia.

On Monday, Ms. Kneissl said she knew that steps were being taken to revoke her citizenship. In comments reported by the Russian state news agency Tass, she said that if Austria moved forward with the plan, she would end up stateless and need to apply for travel documents from the United Nations.

Christopher F. Schuetze is a reporter for The Times based in Berlin, covering politics, society and culture in Germany, Austria and Switzerland.
