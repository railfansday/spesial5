Forget ‘The Traitors,’ Alan Cumming’s 2-Season Apple TV Flop Demands a Weekend Binge

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Alan Cumming is one of the most versatile actors ever. He began as a Tony-winning stage actor and won the hearts of every millennial in films like the Spy Kids trilogy, Romy and Michele's High School Reunion, and Spice World. He then switched to television, where his role as shark lawyer, Eli Gold, on The Good Wife netted him three Emmy nominations. The role that Cumming is perhaps most known for right now is his hosting duties over on The Traitors, a show which has landed him six Emmy wins. Cumming might be crushing his over-the-top Shakespearean-like leadership, complete with a stunning array of colorful costumes, but there's one other binge-worthy project that never got to flourish properly that he shines in.

Schmigadoon! first premiered on Apple TV back in 2021. The parody musical comedy, created by Cinco Paul and Ken Daurio, is an inventive take on the musical art form. The first season, consisting of just six episodes, pays tribute to the Golden Age of musicals of the 1940s and 1950s. With a focus on Brigadoon, and nods to Oklahoma, South Pacific, and Carousel, the show follows two New York-based doctors named Melissa (Cecily Strong) and Josh (Keegan-Michael Key). They decide to go backpacking to try and fix their relationship problems, but on their trip, they discover the mystical town of Schmigadoon. The kooky village seems to exist in some ephemeral past, and there are all kinds of stereotypical, yet hilarious characters who have the tendency to break out into elaborate song and dance musical numbers. Melissa and Josh realize that they can't leave the town until they find true love and break the spell. The second season of Schmigadoon! tackles an entirely different era of musicals. Released in 2023, the six-episode season is aptly named Schmicago, and features parodies of musicals from the 1960s and 1970s, like Chicago, Cabaret, and Sweeney Todd.

Every one of these four started as a cast recording people played until the disc wore out, and every one of them eventually turned into a film. Here is yours, what it says about you, and the case for it being the best of the four.

You work like the clock is against you, because in your head it always is. Hamilton is three hours about a man who cannot stop producing, cannot stop arguing and cannot leave a single thing alone, and you recognised yourself in it immediately.

People made up their minds about you early and they were wrong. Wicked takes the most famous villain in American children's fiction and asks what the story looks like from her side, which is a question you have asked about yourself more than once.

You cannot walk past something unfair. Les Mis is a show about a man who spends his entire life trying to make one wrong right, and about everybody around him deciding what they are prepared to lose for other people.

You are not interested in saving it all for later. Rent is a year in the life of people with no money, no plan and a very clear sense that the time they have is the only thing they genuinely own.

Schmigadoon! is a completely unique show because it's so brilliantly written. There are plenty of fun storylines, and some of the plots that you might already love from these traditional musicals get turned on their heads in a delightfully tongue-in-cheek way. These homages to past memorable musicals are a tad irreverent, but they also focus on the elements that made these shows so popular in the first place. The series is truly at its best during the big-budget musical numbers that are sprinkled throughout each episode. With songs in Season 1 like 'Corn Puddin,' which landed the series an Emmy for Outstanding Original Music and Lyrics, "You Can't Tame Me," "I Always, Always, Never Get My Man," and Kristin Chenoweth's showstopping performance of "Tribulation," Schmigadoon! definitely features catchy, toe-tapping music that will have you wanting to sing along.

It's time to get on your feet and dance!!

Both seasons of Schmigadoon! offer a completely stellar cast of performers. Key and Strong are grounded anchors in this fantastical world, but there are a plethora of other talented actors including Chenoweth, Fred Armisen, Dove Cameron, Aaron Tveit, Ariana DeBose, Jane Krakowski, and Martin Short. Despite this stacked cast, Cumming is a true standout. In Season 1, he portrays the mayor of Schmigadoon, a closeted gay man named Aloysius Menlove who sings the amusing "Somewhere Love Is Waiting For You." Season 2 offers Cumming the chance to chew on even more scenery in an altogether different role as Dooley Blight, a Sweeney Todd rip-off. Through several dark, yet hilarious songs, Cumming manages to make the character even more memorable. He never fails to land each comedic beat, and his beautiful baritone nails every song he takes on.

Although the show was a hit with reviewers, Schmigadoon! was disappointingly cancelled after Season 2 and never seemed to gain the traction with audiences that it needed. In an interesting twist, the show has found new life in a stage musical also written by Paul that debuted on Broadway in April. The show won four Tony Awards this year, proving that the magic of Schmigadoon was transferable from screen to stage. Cumming definitely has his dance card full right now, with his recent role in the miniseries, Tip Toe, as well as his continued participation in the massive hit that is The Traitors, but Schmigadoon! is still certainly worth checking out to get another glimpse of Cumming's immense talents.

Schmigadoon! is available to stream on Apple TV in the U.S.
