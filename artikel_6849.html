How to Make Sure Your Sex Life Keeps Getting Hotter With Age

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

We get better with age—our sex should, too.

I’m excited to get older. I keep getting wiser, more sure of myself, and much more confident about speaking up for myself. All of these things are just bound to make the rest of my life better—including my sex life. And I plan on having sex—good sex—for as long as as I possibly can.

And, as it turns out, I can indeed do that, for a very long time.

Despite what you’ve heard from other people, neither men nor women have sexual “primes” in their 20s. Good thing, too, because think about how much unlearning of sexual stigmas and hangups happen in your young adulthood!

“In reality, women in their 40s and 50s often know what they want far better than they did in their 20s,” certified sex and relationship coach and sex educator Gemma Nice tells Cosmopolitan. “The shift is moving away from sex where the goal was just to look good or rush to penetrative sex and moves towards purposeful, sensory connection using more intuition.”

Even when people do hit the average peak for sexual desire—typically 30s and 40s for both men and women—it doesn’t just disappear afterwards. Sure, on average, sexual frequency decreases every new decade of age you enter, but that doesn’t mean your sex life is doomed after your 30th or 40th (or 50th or 60th!) birthday. There are ways to keep your sex life healthy, fulfilling, and hot the older you get. You just have to work at it.

The most inevitable part of aging is that our bodies will change. Now, with proper nutrition and exercise, you can make sure you’re staying as limber and flexible as possible. But some health issues affect our bodies regardless, and eventually there are some limitations to what you can physically do. Instead of framing this as a death sentence for your sex life, you have to learn how to adapt your sex life to your changing body.

When it comes to women’s health issues like perimenopause and menopause, your sex life can be directly affected: You might experience exhaustion, decreased libido, vaginal dryness, or other physical and emotional symptoms. There are workarounds to these, of course, including changing the time of day when you have sex, changing how you have sex, spending more time on foreplay, and lots and lots of lube.

“Menopause does change how the genitals respond but the clitoris doesn't lose nerve endings,” Nice says. “Reduced blood flow just means it takes longer and needs more direct attention.”

Sometimes this means changing up your sex position. Do I love being able to say that I’ve had sex in the Butter Churner position? Absolutely. Do I think that I’m going to really enjoy that move the older I get? Probably not.

Maghan Burge, nurse practitioner and founder of Braise—a platform that helps you design your room based on intimate wellness—says that your sex positions should adjust with your athletic ability. “Positions that were easy at 25 can strain knees, hips, and backs at 50,” she says.

She encourages taking your time and using support pillows and wedges that take pressure off joints. And of course, you need to get out of the mentality that sex is less “sexy” or “fun” just because you need to make some alterations.

“None of it is a sign that something's wrong,” Burge says. “It's maintenance, the same as stretching before a run.”

This maintenance can even be changing up your room so that when it’s play time, all you’re worried about is enjoying sex.

“Night sweats, broken sleep, and hip or back pain make bed the last place you want to be active,” Burge says. “Cooling sheets, a fan, breathable fabrics, and a body pillow (or a pillow between the knees for hip and spine alignment) sound unsexy, but people who sleep well and hurt less have far more interest in being close.”

If you’re with a long-term partner, there’s going to be a lot of talking needed to maintain a great sex life.

Even if there are kids, a mountain of responsibilities, and a packed schedule that seem in the way, Burge says to make an effort to talk more and earlier in the day. “Desire at 10 p.m. usually starts with feeling like a team at 10 a.m.,” she says.

Also, discuss what you both want your sex and intimate life to look like. If every night doesn’t have to end with sex, talk about what sort of nights you can have together to keep intimacy in tact. “Keeping touch and closeness in the routine, without a scoreboard, is what keeps the bigger nights happening,” Burge says.

One simple way to do this is through a somatic tool known as the lap-sit reset. According to Nice, this is where you straddle your partner while they sit cross-legged, look into each other’s eyes, and just sync your breathing for a few minutes. “It releases sexual tension from the pelvic floor, grounds the nervous system into rest-and-digest, and reminds your body that your partner is a safe, sensual space to be,” she says.

You’ll feel less like you need to “warm up” to sex if you’re keeping a physical connection with your partner throughout the week.

This is important with commitment-free hookups too. Not everyone is going to be married or in a long-term relationship when they’re older. Some of us will still be just getting it in when we can—and there is nothing wrong with that.

“When you stop being who you were back in your 20s and start to really know what you want from now, you will be really empowered to go and get that amazing sex you have been wanting for ages,” Nice says. “It can be the most liberating sex of your life.”

Things change as you get older, but we all need to collectively get out of the mindset that it’s for the worse. I plan on aging like fine wine—and I expect my sex life to follow suit.
