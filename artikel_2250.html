He played with Scott Parker at Charlton Athletic - Then he was jailed for £15m fraud

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Centre-back Richard Rufus was in the same Charlton Athletic team as Scott Parker, but despite an eleven-year career at The Valley, his post-retirement life has been one misstep after another.

The life of the professional footballer can be an extremely insecure one. All players are one serious injury from their career being over, while a downturn in form could mean that their next contract could be their last.

A lucky few make it through to their mid-to-late thirties without any mishaps, but there's also a number who don't even make it that far, and in the case of the former Charlton Athletic defender Richard Rufus, a series of injuries resulted in his career ending at just 29 years of age.

But Rufus's post-playing career has not been a good one. The former centre-back has repeatedly found himself in trouble with the law, to an extent that, despite having been inducted into Charlton's hall of fame in 2013, he's at risk of becoming better known for this post-career proclivities than he was for his talents on the pitch.

As a player, Richard Rufus was the definition of a one-club man. Born in Lewisham in 1975, he joined Charlton Athletic as a youth and progressed through the club's academy, making his debut for them as a 20-year-old against Wolverhampton Wanderers in April 1995.

He would go on to spend 11 years at The Valley, and the early years of his career suggested that he could go on to get to the very top of the game. He was selected six times for the England under-21s over the course of 1996 and 1997, and by this time he was an established face at the heart of the Charlton central defence.

In 1998, he was part of the Charlton team which returned to the top-flight for the first time since 1990, though his Premier League debut against Newcastle United in August 1998 only lasted 25 minutes before he was sent off, and Charlton were relegated back to what was then still known as the First Division again.

On this occasion, though, Charlton bounced straight back up at the end of the 1999-2000 season, winning the First Division title, with Rufus scoring six goals in 44 appearances for them. His team-mate at the centre of the Charlton defence for a while in the early 2000s, Gary Rowett, later stated that Rufus was "on the radar" for a full England call-up by this time.

Among the other players in the Charlton team of that time was Scott Parker, who would go on to hit considerable heights with West Ham United, Spurs and Fulham, and who's since made a successful career for himself as a manager.

But while the new century started explosively well for Rufus and his club, it didn't take long for injury problems to start mounting up. Rufus made 32 appearances for Charlton in the 2001-02 season, but the following season he was limited to just ten as the result of a knee injury.

The start of the 2003-04 season suggested that he had recovered from his knee problems, but behind the scenes the injury returned, and throughout the year he underwent a series of operations. It looked briefly as though he was back when he appeared on their bench for a League Cup match against Luton Town in September 2003 which Charlton won on penalty kicks.

But in truth, the injury hadn't fully cleared, and after one final operation carried out by the renowned orthopedic surgeon Richard Steadman in the USA failed, he was forced to retire from the professional game at the age of just 29 in the summer of 2004. In total, he made 254 appearances for Charlton over 11 years, scoring twelve goals. In 2013, he was inducted into the club's hall of fame.

Rufus's post-playing life has been blighted by a succession of run-ins with the law. The problems started in October 2013 when, having set himself up as a financial advisor, he was declared bankrupt with debts totalling £6 million. Among his creditors were the Pentecostal church of which he was a member, which lost £5m, the equivalent to half of a £10.1 million windfall they'd received from the sale of land in Hackney, as well as family members. Rufus had promised his church a 72% return on their investment, but they only managed to claw back £1.4 million from it.

From here on, things only seemed to spiral. By May 2015, his bankruptcy had been discharged, but he was in court again following an investigation into misappropriation of funds and the unregulated taking of deposits. It was established that Rufus had operated a £16m Ponzi scheme involving more than 100 investors, including members of his family and congregation members of churches he had attended, pocketing more than £3 million. He received a 15-year bankruptcy restriction order for his troubles.

The matter ended up being treated as a criminal matter, and in 2019 he was charged with fraud, money laundering and carrying out a regulated activity without authorisation. It took a long time for the case to come to trial - it wasn't heard until November 2022 - but in January 2023 the former player was convicted and sentenced to seven-and-a-half years in prison. It was heard that Rufus had used his former career as a professional footballer to project a status of wealth while living off the money that he had promised to invest. In total, he paid back £7.4 million of investors’ money and lost them £8.7 million.

Both Gary Rowett and Scott Parker have gone on to have successful careers in management. Rowett has managed at eight different EFL clubs, while Parker has won promotion from the Championship three times and has recently landed a plum job at West Ham United. In stark contrast to that, though, the sort of management that Richard Rufus went into once his playing career ended up being of a very different sort, and he's lost pretty much everything as a result.
