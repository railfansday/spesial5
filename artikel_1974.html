Newcastle United struggles can be summed up by one word

Under Eddie Howe, Newcastle United had a clear identity.

That was to be a pressing machine, giving teams no time to breathe at times and overrun them in midfield, thanks to physical players such as Tonali and Joelinton.

That was the expectation, almost a demand of the Newcastle United fans, as a Wor flags banner read ‘Get into them’.

Four months on though from Eddie Howe’s departure,  a clear sense of identity is yet to be implemented by Matthias Jaissle.

Jaissle arrived as an unknown to the Premier League, having arrived from Al-Ahli, it has not been smooth sailing so far.

As a club that qualified for the Champions League 18 months ago, is a mid-table finish expected this time around?

It must be stated that this is not the same Newcastle United team that finished top five and qualified for the Champions League in the 2024-25 season.

Having lost the likes of Bruno Guimaraes and Alexander Isak, to Premier League rivals Arsenal and Liverpool respectively. They have lost huge presences in the team and currently lack a talisman to influence a game as well as players such as Isak and Bruno G did multiple times.

Diving deeper, an argument can be made that Newcastle have not hit the same heights since the departure of Alexander Isak in 2025.

Late summer 2025 signings Yoane Wissa and Nick Woltemade struggled for goals, scoring just 14 goals combined between them last season in all competitions. This compares to Isak’s 27 goals in his final Newcastle United season,

The 2025-26 season saw Newcastle United finish 12th in the Premier League, seven places below where they placed the year before and a massive 17 points worse off.

In my opinion the reasons for this can be summarised into two points, which are lack of away form and dropping points from winning positions. They won just four away games in the Premier League and dropped a crazy 30+ points from winning positions. This suggests Newcastle United have for some time not been the same team that did so well in 2024-25.

Matthias Jaissle officially became Newcastle United Head Coach only 18 days before the start of the season. This gave him little time to bring in players to fit his system of ‘vertical football’ and work his football philosophy into the current players before their first match against Liverpool.

I think it can be said that Newcastle United did start the season well, with three wins and two draws in their first five games (in all competitions).

After those opening five matches including a Premier League win over Spurs and draws against Liverpool and Bournemouth, I believed that Newcastle would be in a battle for the European places. However, wins have been hard to find. Also a defeat in the Carabao Cup at Everton.

One of the main reasons for the struggles has been the lack of goals from Osula and Wissa, just two between them.  Meanwhile out on loan, Nick Woltemade has scored five goals so far for Juventus.

Looking at the wider stats, in the Premier League against Coventry and Everton, Newcastle had 36 shots in total but only eight of those on target, scoring just one goal.

Also, new signings have struggled. Fernandez-Pardo without a goal so far, whilst players such as Toure and Bamba are yet to start a Premier League match.

I think Newcastle United’s struggles can currently be summed up in one word – change.

Change in manager with new ideas, a change in system still being perfected and big change in the squad with a lot of younger players being brought in who are still adapting to the physicality of the Premier League.

There is though definitely something to build on, the 2-2 draw with Arsenal gave fans hope.

To answer the question of what the goal for the rest of the season should be, I believe it should be the idea of identity and growth. Allowing Matthias Jaissle to work on his system, find his best team and use this season as a transitional one.

A big challenge will be the big home fixtures against Manchester United and Sunderland, we might know a lot more after these games.
