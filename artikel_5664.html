NRG to sell 4.9 GW of gas plants to LS Power for $4B

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Let Utility Dive's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

NRG Energy CEO Robert Gaudette said the sale values one 1.2-GW Texas gas plant at $1,400/kW, while NRG is “developing 1.5 GW of new natural gas generation in Texas” for just above $1,000/kW.

NRG Energy has agreed to sell around 4.9 GW of gas plant capacity to LS Power, which approached NRG with an unsolicited offer of $4 billion — around eight times the plants’ annual adjusted EBITDA of $500 million, NRG President and CEO Robert Gaudette said during a Nov. 9 earnings call.

NRG plans for that $4 billion to go toward a $1 billion stock buyback and $2 billion debt reduction, with the remaining $1 billion providing “flexibility to reinvest in our long-term strategy through attractive contracted growth opportunities or further reduce debt,” Gaudette said. “We expect the sale to close in the second quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals.”

“One aspect of the valuation worth highlighting is Jack County, which carries an implied value of approximately $1,400/KW,” Gaudette said. “For perspective, we're developing 1.5 GW of new natural gas generation in Texas for just above $1,000/KW. That comparison demonstrates the value we're realizing from selected existing assets while developing newer, more efficient generation at a lower cost.”

Gaudette said that NRG’s project of building a 1.2-GW combined-cycle natural gas-fired power plant in Texas to power the data center project of an unnamed cloud and AI hyperscaler is “on track, and I fully expect approved agreements and an announcement by year-end.”

“We're also advancing additional project opportunities with the same customer beyond the initial 1.2 GW,” he added. “Beyond the initial project, our pipeline of opportunities exceeds the turbine allocations and [engineering, procurement, and construction] capacity under our original development agreement with GE Vernova and Kiewit.”

That development agreement is for 5.4 GW of turbine capacity, according to NRG’s earnings presentation.

In response to an analyst question about whether the results of the recent midterm elections present any risks to the 1.2-GW project, Gaudette said, “The political environment has actually strengthened this project and our projects.”

“The way that we've gone about developing generation to support data centers and this large load growth over time, the political environment is where we thought it would always be,” he added. “We've been very picky about the projects we do and very picky about who we do them with … I've actually talked to our customer since the elections. This project is going to happen.”

Gaudette noted that “prices in ERCOT are depressed,” which is built into the company’s 2027 guidance and long-term outlook, he said.

“Right now we're in a world where battery build and solar have kind of dominated the last two years of build, and demand hasn't been fast enough to absorb all of it,” he added. Gaudette called 2027 “weak,” but noted that the ERCOT region has a strong pipeline overall.

“When you look into the queue in ERCOT, it's not just data centers,” he said. “There's multiple gigawatts of manufacturing facilities, expansions of industrial facilities. Those are coming. Those have jobs. Those are going to hit the grid and sop up some of that excess capacity … NRG is very well positioned to capture value both on the retail side and the generation side as this market tightens and our customers need us more.”
