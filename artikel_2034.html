Arsenal’s Havertz penalty controversy sparks fresh call for change to football laws

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Arsenal’s controversial penalty claim against Hull City has raised new questions about how the offside rule is interpreted in football.

Former Premier League official Dermot Gallagher believes that whilst he understands why the officials made the decision, the result would have been different under the current rules.

Speaking on Ref Watch on Sky Sports, Gallagher said that the holding should have been the main focus of the incident involving Kai Havertz and Hull defender Nobel Mendy.

His comments have increased criticism of referee Farai Hallam and VAR official Michael Oliver. However, Gallagher also feels that the language used in the laws needs to be addressed to avoid this issue in the future.

The incident happened shortly before half-time during Arsenal’s 1-0 victory over Hull at the Emirates Stadium.

Mendy pulled Havertz’s shirt inside the penalty area, prompting VAR to recommend an on-field review. Hallam subsequently confirmed that the Hull defender had committed a holding offence.

However, he ruled that Havertz was in an offside position and awarded Hull a free-kick.

Gallagher believes that was incorrect under the laws of the game. He explained that being in an offside position does not automatically constitute an offence.

The player must be involved in play before the referee can give a penalty. In this instance, Havertz had not tried to play the ball or challenge an opponent before being pulled back by Mendy.

This means that the player was held first, so a penalty should have been given to Arsenal.

Gallagher said that the AR had first raised their flag for offside. He said that it was a great process with VAR, but the initial decision could have affected the outcome.

He recognised why the decision was made even though he felt Arsenal should have been awarded the penalty.

In terms of common sense, he said it does not look right to award the penalty, as Havertz was in an offside position and couldn’t legally contest for the ball.

But he emphasised that the officials must strictly enforce the laws of the game. Gallagher thinks there is some grey space here which can be addressed by having more specific wording.

This could be crucial in these types of situations as the difference between being in an offside position and committing an offside offence can have a big effect on whether a penalty is awarded.

It’s a small comfort for Arsenal after missing the chance to take the lead from the spot. But Martin Odegaard did eventually score the winner in the 72nd minute to give Mikel Arteta’s men three points.

The incident has exposed a wider problem for referees. Until the laws provide clearer guidance, similar decisions could continue to generate controversy.
