Judge says ICE ‘made a mockery of court orders’ with instant re-arrest policy

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Federal judge in Colorado finds immigration officials in contempt but takes no more action after they end practice

A federal judge said that the US Immigration and Customs ⁠Enforcement agency (ICE) “made a mockery of court orders” in Colorado and that the court found government officials in contempt ⁠for instantly re-arresting a ⁠man whom ​the judge had ordered released.

US district judge Charlotte Sweeney declined to take any further action because she ⁠said the officials had “cured the contempt by ceasing the practice at issue” as sworn by an official in court.

“ICE’s new ‘policy’ of ⁠immediately re-detaining petitioners following release made a mockery of court orders mandating petitioners’ ​immediate release,” the judge wrote on Wednesday, ‌according to court records in ‌the US district court for the district of Colorado.

At least 18 people ‌in Colorado have been held in detention longer than they should have been or have been re-arrested within minutes of their judge-ordered release in the past six weeks, CPR News, the news division of Colorado Public Radio, reported.

The report said that immigration attorneys estimated the number may be ‌higher given that some immigrants represented themselves.

The phenomenon of immigration detainees being released and then quickly re-detained has been noticeable in recent months and legal advocates say the trend is on the rise.

Sometimes a person is re-arrested days later, sometimes without even leaving the detention center and, according to advocates, often in violation of a federal court order.

ICE, which is part of the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), has been the face of ​Donald Trump’s immigration crackdown and deportation drive that rights groups say has violated free speech and due process rights.

Human rights groups also say the crackdown has created an unsafe environment, particularly for ethnic minorities, who have raised ⁠concerns about racial profiling.

The Trump administration has maintained that ​its actions aim to ​improve domestic security and are ​in compliance with the law. ICE and the DHS did not ​immediately respond to ‌a request ​for comment on what ​the judge said on Wednesday.

Democrats, who won majorities in the House and Senate in the midterm elections, now aim to hold the Trump administration accountable for its immigration policies.
