CDT Southern Terminus Closure Lifted, Border Monument To Be Relocated

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The Continental Divide Trail’s southern terminus and southernmost mile have reopened following a temporary construction closure, according to the Continental Divide Trail Coalition (CDTC). This section of the trail had been closed in late October so that construction crews could widen an access road and construct a second border barrier at the terminus.

The first mile of the CDT falls within the Fort Huachuca New Mexico National Defense Area (NDA), a military zone created in spring 2025 when roughly 110,000 acres along the US-Mexico border were transferred from the Bureau of Land Management into Army control. Hikers seeking to access the trail’s southern terminus must submit to a background check and obtain an Army permit. Noncitizens must also have a US citizen escort through the NDA who also has an access permit.

“These events are unprecedented in the history of our trail system: the transfer of a critically significant element of our national scenic trail into the Department of Defense control, the conversion of the CDT Southern Terminus into a military and construction zone of momentous scale, and trail management partnerships that include land management agencies and state governments, but also the US military,” the CDTC said in a press release.

The CDTC received word today from US Army officials at Fort Huachuca that the trail is once again officially open, although construction activities are ongoing and hikers may still encounter traffic, road closures, and delays in the area. “This closure came at a critical time for southbound hikers,” the organization said. “Completing the 3,100-mile trail is a monumental achievement, and the construction activities at the border created dynamic changes to the permitted NDA access in place during the past year.”

The press release also indicates that the construction of the second border wall means the southern terminus monument will need to be relocated to an area outside of the border walls, and that access to the monument may be temporarily prohibited during construction and relocation. “CDTC does not know the exact timing of the relocation but will provide updates as available,” the organization wrote.
