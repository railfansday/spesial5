Good morning. Stock markets around the world have fallen sharply as a rout in US tech stocks spread across the Atlantic after the Democrats’ midterms victory led to fears of a slowdown in the AI industry. A sell-off on Wall Street on Thursday was followed by sharp falls in Asian stock markets on Friday morning. The dip in investor sentiment has also reached London.

Susannah Streeter, the chief investment strategist at Wealth Club, said: “The tech sell-off has intensified. Big worries are colliding – the build-out of datacenters looks set to slow, which risks shredding the spending spree underpinning the boom, right at the time that investors have become super nervous about returns on this massive investment.”

What happened to the Nasdaq? The Nasdaq share index had its worst day in 19 months after slumping 5.5% on Thursday amid growing signs that the AI industry will face tougher regulation.

Reeling from the midterm results, the US president has attempted to secure the future of his gaudy redecorations at the White House by declaring his design of the Oval Office and Cabinet Room national monuments that should be preserved. Barack Obama recently said the redecoration looked like “Saddam Hussein opened a The Cheesecake Factory” but Trump claimed his proclamation would “make them into a landmark for the ages”.

What is happening with Trump’s “triumphal arch” project? A federal judge has halted construction work on the towering arch for 14 days, a significant setback for one of the president’s most prominent projects in his vision to remake Washington.

The law, known as Assembly Bill 1709, prohibits such platforms from offering children “psychologically exploitative features”. The office of the California attorney general said it was reviewing the lawsuits. “Our office is committed to defending California’s commonsense laws to protect kids online,” it added.

Paraguay’s rightwing president, Santiago Peña, resigned on Thursday after a political and financial crisis collapsed the South American country’s banking system. At the crisis’s centre is Ueno Bank, an obscure lender that expanded rapidly under Peña to claim 60% of Paraguay’s adult population as clients. Last Monday, the central bank ordered its resolution over insolvency, freezing 3m accounts – prompting executive arrests and massive street protests.
