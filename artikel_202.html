Waymo pulls driverless taxis from freeways across the US after California ban

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The autonomous driving company suspended driverless taxi rides on US highways days after California banned the vehicles on its own freeways

Waymo has stopped offering driverless taxi rides on freeways across the US indefinitely. The Alphabet subsidiary pulled its vehicles from highways nationwide after California banned them from freeways in response to recent traffic incidents.

Waymo had allowed riders to hail its robotaxis for freeway rides in the San Francisco Bay Area, Los Angeles, Phoenix, Miami and Austin. The California-based company offers rides on city streets in many other areas, including Las Vegas, Dallas and Denver. Those rides remain unaffected by suspended freeway service.

A Waymo spokesperson wrote in an email that the company has “temporarily restricted commercial freeway rides in all markets where we operate that service”, though it’s “continuing freeway testing with trained autonomous specialists behind the wheel”. The suspension was implemented last Wednesday, according to the spokesperson, but they didn’t say how long it would last.

Waymo’s voluntary suspension came the same day as the California department of motor vehicles banned the company’s robotaxis from the state’s freeways. The ban followed two traffic incidents, including one in which three Waymo driverless vehicles drove into a construction zone, a DMV spokesperson, Jonathan Groveman, said in a statement.

The construction zone incident occurred on 5 November, and the second incident, which involved California highway patrol vehicles, occurred the next day on a highway in San Francisco, according to Groveman.

Video of the 6 November incident circulated widely on social media last week. In the footage, a Waymo vehicle can be seen trying to bypass California highway patrol vehicles conducting a traffic break.

“The department determined that Waymo’s driverless freeway operations pose an imminent hazard to public safety due to recurring incursions by its vehicles into active construction zones where construction workers, vehicles and equipment are present,” Groveman said.

Groveman added that “similar incidents” previously occurred on 18 May.

The Waymo spokesperson said the “recent freeway construction zone encounters on US 101 in California” did not “involve any injuries or collisions”.

California’s restrictions bar Waymo from operating and testing its driverless vehicles on the state’s freeways, according to Groveman. He said the restrictions will remain in effect until “Waymo demonstrates to the department that it has taken effective corrective actions to address the deficiencies that precipitated the restrictions”.
