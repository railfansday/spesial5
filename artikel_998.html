Harry Kane is targeting the “business end of the season” after undergoing surgery on a torn ligament in his left ankle.

Bayern Munich said the operation on the injury, sustained in Friday’s 1-1 draw with Cologne, was a success but the 33-year-old is expected to be out for months as a result.

Kane wrote on Instagram: “The highs and lows of life! Obviously very disappointed to get this injury but as always it’s an opportunity to show resilience and improvement going into 2027.

“I’ll be back fresh and ready to go for the business end of the season!

“Tough times don’t last, tough people do” – my guy @edelman11 (Julian Edelman, three-time Super Bowl winner with the New England Patriots) told me this one!

“Thank you for all the messages! Alles gut.”

Kane sustained the injury when he was scythed down in the area by Cologne defender Joel Schmied, who reached Michael Olise’s low cross first but clattered into the Ballon d’Or winner’s left leg in his follow-through at the back post.

The England captain writhed in pain and, although he gingerly got back to his feet and initially soldiered on, it was clear he could not continue and he hobbled off the field alongside two physios after treatment.

After the match Bayern confirmed Kane had torn the syndesmotic ligament and “will therefore be sidelined for the next few months”.

Kane earlier this month signed a new deal with the Bundesliga giants until June 2029 – reportedly worth in excess of £400,000 a week, making him the highest-paid player in Europe before bonuses.

Since arriving from Tottenham three years ago, Kane has bagged a scarcely believable 156 goals in 163 appearances in all competitions, helping Bayern win a league and cup double last season.

He went into the international break having a drawn a blank in his four previous matches for Bayern but he scored twice for England, including from the spot in their 3-2 Nations League win over Spain.

But after being forced off after 20 minutes at the Allianz Arena, replaced by Tom Bischof, Kane could now be facing a battle to be fully fit for England’s two-legged quarter-final against Norway in March.
