Ranking 7 possible next jobs for Eddie Howe after Man Utd snub

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Eddie Howe has been passed over for the Manchester United gig, meaning the ex-Newcastle boss is still on the look for his next job.

Howe has only been out of work for a little under four months but it already feels like many of the opportunities he would have fancied have gone.

Mauricio Pochettino beat him to Old Trafford and so here are his possible options rated by how likely we think they are.

It was Howe’s decision to leave Newcastle but you do wonder if he regrets it slightly.

When he left St James’, there were potential vacancies at England, Manchester United and Spurs and yet all three of those options looked closed off for the time being.

And in terms of the kind of club that would appoint Howe, that second rung below the best of the best does seem the most likely.

Newcastle should be high in that group provided they find a way to make Champions League qualification more consistent and Matthias Jaissle’s struggles may have them calling up Howe to see if he fancies another go.

He probably does not but you can’t rule it out entirely.

Cracks are already starting to appear in the relationship between Oliver Glasner and the Forest hierarchy with him responding to a 2-0 defeat to Brentford at the end of October by saying he had made too many compromises since taking over.

He did not allude to what those compromises were but ignoring them brought an immediate result in the form of a 3-1 victory against Manchester City.

However, Glasner is quite a combustible character and Evangelos Marinakis even more so. It would not be unreasonable to suggest this may be a short partnership.

It would take a lot to sack Glasner with his mega bucks deal but if they do, Howe could be a candidate they go after.

From Howe’s perspective, there are not a huge amount of positives to go off. You would probably get paid a lot of money but the squad is not the best by any stretch of the imagination and you would be signing up for a relegation battle you suspect.

Howe probably thinks he is above that at this stage of his career.

Howe has long been linked with the England gig and he was reportedly in the running before Tuchel got it.

But with the German having survived the World Cup fallout, it feels unlikely that seat would be vacant until at least post Euro 2028 and that is a long time out of the game for someone like Howe.

He may well fancy the job but would probably take one in between.

If you can’t get a Premier League job, why not fill your coffers with a stint in Saudi?

Howe has already shown flexibility when it comes to moral objections to Saudi Arabia and so would presumably have no issue in spending a stint over there from that perspective. He would be joining the likes of Ange Postecoglou, Brendan Rodgers and the best-paid manager in the world, Simone Inzaghi.

The problem is that once you go to Saudi, you never really come back and Howe may swerve it to preserve his top-level career.

When Howe took a managerial sabbatical in 2020, he headed to Madrid to watch how Diego Simeone trained his Atletico squad, suggesting he at least acknowledges there is life outside of the Premier League.

For that reason and given the lack of immediate Premier League options, him taking a job in Europe is not completely out of the question.

In terms of the leagues that would suit him, the Bundesliga feels the second most natural fit behind the Premier League given it also plays high-intensity football and so maybe a team away from Dortmund or Bayern could be where he ends up.

We have seen plenty of English players have success in Europe but a manager doing so is still rare at this stage. You cannot deny though that if Howe does do it, it would make him a better coach.

West Ham have only just appointed Scott Parker but he does feel like the modern Neil Warnock in that he will get you up from the Championship but don’t expect him to keep you there.

This is not the Sullivan and Gold era of West Ham anymore either. A Gold is still in charge as following the death of her father, Vanessa Gold took over as chairwoman, but 43% of the club was bought by Daniel Kretinsky, a Czech billionaire who also co-owns Sparta Prague.

The new owners will have bought West Ham for what it could be rather than the Championship outfit it currently is and turning a lower-half Premier League club into one challenging for Europe is exactly the kind of task Howe did at Newcastle.

For Howe, it will be a question of whether he wants such a project again and he will want assurances that there is money to rejuvenate the squad but it does seem like an ideal job for him should Parker take them up.

The Toffees are sixth in the Premier League but there are still some sections of the Hill Dickinson crowd that are in two minds about David Moyes.

Obviously he has done very well to take them from the threat of relegation and into the top six but the football is not exactly the most thrilling.

Only Leeds have scored fewer of the teams in the top 10 and there is a desire for Everton to play more expansive football, something they have not really done well since Carlo Ancelotti.

Howe’s style is far more high-tempo than Moyes’ and so should be more pleasing for the fans and there are a lot of positives for the former Bournemouth boss to consider.

First of all, the club has the newest stadium in the Premier League, they also have a good squad if a little light, and you could put Everton in that sleeping giant category, even if they have been in that slumber for a long time now.

What he will want is assurances from the ownership. The Friedkin Group have indicated their willingness to sell the club and so Howe may want to wait until a new owner is identified but he could do a lot worse than the Everton job.
