Jordan James made a decision few players would consider before joining Wolves

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Wolves had an eventful summer after their embarrassing relegation from the Premier League last season, with Jordan James among the arrivals.

Many expected a huge turnover of players at Molineux, but whilst that didn’t happen, there was a refresh of the group, and Cesar Peixoto’s squad was boosted by some high-profile additions.

Bringing in Kieran Trippier and Raul Jimenez on free transfers caught the eye, and James was another arrival that was seen as a coup, as he was named Young Player of the Year in the Championship following his performances at Leicester last season.

Despite the Foxes’ struggles, James was outstanding, scoring 11 goals in 34 games for a side that were relegated to League One.

Dropping to the third tier meant James was not going to extend his stay at the King Power Stadium, so he returned to Rennes, and a summer of speculation followed.

There were plenty of links to Premier League clubs, with Fulham thought to be very keen, but it was ultimately Wolves who won the race for his signature.

Interestingly, James confirmed he had top flight offers, but he explained that he chose Wanderers as he didn’t want another year battling to stay up, having suffered relegations with Leicester and Birmingham in his relatively short career so far.

On that front, James’ move to Wolves has worked out, as Peixoto’s side are firmly in the hunt to win promotion, and there will be a belief in the group that they can not only go up, but fight for the title as well.

Yet, there’s no denying that James’ time at Wolves hasn’t gone as envisaged so far, as he has made just three starts from his 11 appearances in the league.

There have been flashes of his undoubted ability, including a superb goal against Stoke, but James has failed to establish himself as a key player under Peixoto.

To make matters worse, after making back-to-back starts for the first time under the Portuguese head coach, James was forced off after just 36 minutes with a hamstring issue against Cardiff at the end of October.

We know that the Championship is a marathon not a sprint, so there won’t be too many concerns about James’ lack of impact so far.

The 22-year-old will be hugely frustrated at the timing of his latest injury, but he will still expect to have a crucial role in Peixoto’s side over the course of the season.

It should be said that a disrupted pre-season meant James was starting behind the rest of his teammates, but, once he does get back up to speed, he will want to make a big impact.

The middle of the park has been a problem for Wolves, as Marshall Munetsi doesn’t always convince, so there is a feeling that James can force his way into the XI, and the prospect of him linking up with Andre and Fer Lopez will excite Old Gold fans.

James only joined Wolves on an initial loan deal, although a clause is in place for the Championship side to make it permanent.

It has been suggested a fee of £10m would be required to make it permanent, so many see it as a no-brainer for Wolves to trigger the option when you consider the age and potential of the midfielder.

After bouncing around several clubs at a young age, James will want a settled home, and after taking time to adapt, all connected to Wolves will hope he can kick-on and make that Molineux by firing the club to promotion.
