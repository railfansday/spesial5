He played Huddersfield Town & Luton - Then he was jailed over charity fraud

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

He made over 500 senior club appearances and played against both England and Argentina in the World Cup finals, but when Efe Sodje was found to have been siphoning off money from a charity in his name, none of that mattered in the slightest.

With his distinctive do-rag headband, Efe Sodje was a familiar face English football over the first decades of this century. Sodje had the sort of career that most EFL players could only dream of. Not only did he enjoy a twenty-year career which took in eleven clubs, but he was also recognised at international level.

Born in London to Nigerian parents, Sodje was picked for the Nigeria international team twelve times even though he never kicked a ball in the Premier League, and this included being selected for their national team squad for the 2002 World Cup finals, where he played against two former winners of the competition, both England and Nigeria.

Sodje brought his playing career to a close in 2015 at 33 years of age, but less than two years later he found himself in court on fraud charges and, after his conviction on those charges, ended up spending time at her majesty's pleasure.

Born in London in October 1972, Efe Sodje made his breakthrough in the senior game in the Football Conference (now the National League) in 1994. After three years at Broadhall Way, he left for newly-promoted Macclesfield Town in 1997, starting a spell in the EFL which would last for 16 years.

Sodje would make 303 appearances in the League over this time, during which he played for eight League clubs, including Luton Town, Huddersfield Town, Crewe Alexandra and Southend United. His longest spells came at Crewe, for whom he made 113 appearances, and Bury, for whom he made 212.

He won honours at six different clubs. In the non-league game, he was part of the Stevenage team which won the Football Conference title in 1996, but it was in the Football League that he picked up most of his silverware. He was promoted from the third tier with Southend United in 2006 - and was part of their team which knocked Manchester United out of the League Cup in November 2006 - and was promoted from the fourth tier no fewer than four times, with Macclesfield Town in 1998, Crewe Alexandra in 2003, Huddersfield Town in 2004 and Bury in 2011.

But he was perhaps best known for his surprise appearances for the Nigeria international team. While with Luton Town during the 1999-2000 season, he was called up to the national team squad for the 2000 African Cup of Nations, in which his country finished as runners-up to Cameroon, although he didn't play in the tournament.

But the bigger shock came two years later, when he wasn't just called up to the squad for the 2002 World Cup finals, but played against both Argentina and England, although his team was knocked out in the group stages of the competition. In total, Sodje - whose brother Sam also played international football for Nigeria - made 12 appearances for his country.

Sodje ended his playing career in mysterious circumstances, though. He played his last two seasons back in the National League with Barrow, and then back at Macclesfield. But when his departure from the game was announced in June 2016, by which time he was 42 years old, no reason was given for it.

Early in 2019, Sky Sports ran a story which may have explained Sodje's sudden departure from professional football. In September 2017, he - along with two of his brothers, Bright and Stephen - had been given jail sentences for their involvement in defrauding a charity which had been set up in their name.

It was reported that the three had siphoned off £63,000 which had been donated to the Sodje Sports Foundation, a charity ostensibly set up to provide aid for poor children in the Niger delta. Sky reported that, "The three brothers used their connections within professional football and business communities to secure support for their foundation and to give it an appearance of legitimacy."

Efe Sodje was imprisoned for 18 months, Bright Sodje for 21 months, and Stephen Sodje for two years and six months. As well as this, Sam Sodje - who himself made over 200 senior appearances for an array of clubs, including Brentford, Leeds United and Charlton Athletic - was acquitted, while Sam Sodje's brother Akpo fled the UK during the investigation, leading to the issuance of a European Arrest Warrant after he refused to return from Dubai over the investigations. He had previously been connected to a spot-fixing case while playing for Tranmere Rovers.

When handing down the sentences in January 2019, Judge Michael Topolski QC told the defendants: "You have brought shame upon yourselves and your family." Having been given the huge leg-up of the sort of sporting ability that many people would give anything for, it's impossible to avoid the conclusion that the judge was entirely right in his assessment of them.
