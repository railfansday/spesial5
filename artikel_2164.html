Dowman weaves his magic and new signing shines as Arsenal hit ground running

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Teenage sensation Max Dowman was the star of Arsenal’s first pre-season match, as he scored one goal and assisted another in a 4-1 victory over Girona.

Dowman, 16, was joined on the scoresheet by new signing Christos Tzolis, who struck with a low effort in his first game since his £34m move from Belgian side Club Brugge.

Established attackers Kai Havertz and Gabriel Jesus also scored for the Premier League champions, who were largely comfortable against opposition from the Spanish second tier.

Here are four things Telegraph Sport learnt from the match:

It was this time last year that Dowman first announced his remarkable talent to the wider world. Then only 15, he sparkled in pre-season and made it clear to Mikel Arteta that he was ready to impact matches at the highest level.

A year on, Dowman is now an established first-team player. He is the youngest ever Premier League winner and the competition’s youngest ever scorer, and he looks set to make even more of a mark this season.

In this friendly against Girona, Dowman created Arsenal’s opening goal with a gorgeous defence-splitting pass for Havertz. He did so after receiving the ball under pressure and spinning into space. Dowman then scored Arsenal’s third with a powerful low drive from the edge of the box.

Dowman is in competition with Bukayo Saka and Noni Madueke for a place on the right wing, which means this summer represents a huge opportunity for the teenager. After playing for England at the World Cup, Saka and Madueke will both miss most of pre-season.

Dowman, therefore, has a serious chance of going into the new campaign as the starting right-winger. The Community Shield on August 16 must be a target in his mind.

“We know what he’s capable of doing,” Arteta said. “We want to be more consistent in terms of the performances and the actions that he can deliver, but I think today he had a really good game.”

Arsenal are close to completing a deal for Newcastle United captain Bruno Guimarães but, for now, their only outfield signing of the summer has been Tzolis. This was the Greek winger’s first match in front of supporters and he immediately demonstrated why Arsenal were willing to pay £34m to buy him from Club Brugge.

With Tzolis, it is all about the final product. Goals and assists. He struck 22 times for Club Brugge last season, and registered 29 assists.

How did he produce such impressive numbers? The answer could be seen in his first-half goal against Girona. Receiving the ball on the left wing, Tzolis quickly accelerated towards the penalty area and unleashed a low shot. It was perhaps a more direct approach than Arsenal would usually have seen from Leandro Trossard, the player Tzolis has replaced, and it caught Girona off-guard as the ball deflected into the net.

“I’m very pleased with him,” Arteta said. “First of all, the way he has adapted to the club, to the team. The connection he’s already generating with the boys. I think you’re going to love the player.

“A player who’s very direct. He’s got the goal between the eyes. He has a lot of similarities with Leo [Trossard] in a lot of the things that he can do and the spaces he can occupy. And then the work rate that he has... He’s a boy that comes with so much hunger. He wants to prove himself, it’s a dream for him. And we are here to help him.”

Havertz was one of the few Arsenal players who benefited from playing at the World Cup. His injury issues mean that, unlike most of his team-mates, he did not end the 2025-26 season in a state of exhaustion.

After returning to the pitch in the spring, Havertz enjoyed an impressive end to the campaign for Arsenal. He then performed well at the World Cup, scoring three goals for Germany.

He remains one of Arteta’s favourite players, not least because of his versatility. He started in attack in the crucial end-of-season games in May, but played in midfield against Girona. His low finish, from Dowman’s pass, represented his eighth goal in his last 12 matches for club and country.

Girona’s goal, scored by Arnau Martínez, was perhaps the only alarming moment of this comfortable pre-season victory. Martínez was unmarked from a left-wing corner when he powered a header into Arsenal’s net.

For a group of players who pride themselves on their set-piece prowess, this was a disappointing moment. It also perhaps showed Arsenal’s reliance on their two first-choice centre-backs, William Saliba and Gabriel Magalhães, when they are defending crosses into their box.

Gabriel arrived at Arsenal’s pre-season training camp a few days ago, so he should be in good shape by the start of the new campaign. Saliba, though, will be missing for months with a back problem.

Without Saliba’s height and power, will Arsenal be as effective from set-pieces in the opening chunk of the campaign? Coventry City, Arsenal’s opponents on the first night of the season, will undoubtedly look to test the champions in these situations.
