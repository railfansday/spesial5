Bitcoin Has Traded Below Its 200-Week Average Only 8.4% of the Time. History Says This Is When Patient Investors Get Paid.

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Past trends show that bearish moments are usually the best times to buy this cryptocurrency.

By Neil Patel – Nov 21, 2026 at 8:19AM EST

In typical fashion, Bitcoin (BTC -2.19%) is taking investors on a bumpy ride. The world's first and most valuable digital asset has had short spurts of positive momentum this year. But in the past month, the price has tanked 28% (as of Nov. 21), with a five-day drop of 9%.

Bitcoin is currently trading nearly 14% below its 200-week moving average (WMA). According to River, a Bitcoin financial services firm, the leading cryptocurrency has only been in this territory 8.4% of the time in its entire life. It's a rare occurrence, indeed, and it shows that bearish sentiment is ruling the narrative right now.

History says the bulls should stay patient. This is when investors get paid.

The 200 WMA smooths out Bitcoin's wild price swings. It tracks the average closing price over roughly four years, which coincides with the investment community’s view of the digital asset's full market cycle. This is why many long-term investors treat it as a baseline.

As of Nov. 21, the 200 WMA sits at $68,130, and Bitcoin is trading about 14% beneath it. According to River, Bitcoin purchased during past periods when it traded below its 200 WMA returned an average of 120% over the following year. The subsequent three-year gain is 582%. At those averages, $10,000 would have grown to $22,000 after 12 months and $68,200 after 36 months.

It all sounds so easy, but investors shouldn't take those figures at face value. Because Bitcoin has rarely traded this low relative to its long-term average, the sample size is small. Many of those episodes came when Bitcoin was a niche experiment a decade or more ago. At a $1.2 trillion market cap, the cryptocurrency is unlikely to compound at the same pace as in its early years. Still, the direction of the pattern is hard to ignore.

The recent drop also needs context. Every past Bitcoin bear market included several rallies of 25% or more. This time is similar. That last surge occurred in the back half of August and September.

However, depth is the noticeable change. So far, this downturn has been much more muted than prior ones. Bitcoin is 53% below its peak now.

There are many takeaways. A smaller fall suggests a more mature asset with steadier holders. It could mean the bottom isn't in yet, and it might leave less upside going forward.

Every bear market brings its own reason to declare Bitcoin dead. The past down cycle in 2022 saw major exchange failures that harmed the industry's reputation. Before that, there were worries about government intervention.

Today's fear is more technical in nature. Critics argue that advances in artificial intelligence and quantum computing could break Bitcoin's cryptography. This would undermine the network's security. That's a real concern for any industry in 2026 and beyond.

There's no reason to panic yet. The real threat is likely many years away, and the Bitcoin community, fully aware of how technology is rapidly evolving, can adapt. Network updates can bolster the cryptographic setup over time.

There's also a track record worth noting. The Bitcoin network has operated for over 17 years with virtually no outages. That's a remarkable streak for any piece of financial infrastructure.

The smart money is paying attention. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) filings show that many of Wall Street's biggest companies added to their Bitcoin positions last quarter. But the leading cryptocurrency probably still accounts for less than 0.1% in most of their clients’ portfolios, River says. That's a very tiny percentage among powerful companies with trillions of dollars in assets under management. A modest allocation bump could bring in demand and translate into substantial buying pressure.

Of course, Bitcoin is no sure thing. It's volatile, and past performance offers no guarantees.

That's where dollar-cost averaging comes in. Investors commit a fixed sum at regular intervals, whether the price is up or down. You avoid market timing, and in periods like the current one, a fixed sum gets you more Bitcoin.

Just remember to never invest more than you're willing to lose, and keep your eyes on the long term.
