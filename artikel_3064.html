Victor Wembanyama’s historic night set the tone for the season

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

While the number of precincts reporting was rising on Tuesday night, I realized that watching election returns is similar to watching the NBA Finals. How you feel about the event, and how you will remember it, depends on who you are rooting for.

If you are a Republican and a Spurs fan, Tuesday night could've felt like watching the second half of Game Four of the 2026 Finals, seeing a lead bleed away and feeling powerless to stop it. You probably had trouble sleeping afterwards, as we all did last June, before spending the next day trying to understand what went wrong.

If you are a Democrat and a Spurs fan, Tuesday night could've felt like Game Five of the 2014 Redemption Finals, seeing your side turn around an early deficit, and then run away with things as the night went on. You may have had trouble sleeping that night, but for a different reason than your Republican neighbor or friend.

The important thing is that all of us have been on both sides. Our side has won, and our side has lost. And the same is true for the fans of the other teams. Regardless, they still remain our friends and neighbors, whoever they support. Sports is not politics, and politics isn't sports. The stakes aren't remotely similar, but the people we share our lives with are.

All Spurs fans, regardless of political leanings, certainly enjoyed the night after the election. I played in my normal Wednesday night basketball game, so I had to record the Spurs-Mavericks game, which started at 6:30 p.m. Los Angeles time. Because the game was on ESPN, my recording missed the first several minutes of the Spurs game while the Pistons-76ers game was finishing. When ESPN finally switched from Detroit to San Antonio, Mike Breen was saying that Victor Wembanyama already had 10 points. It turns out that he had his first blocked shot 38 seconds into the game, and scored his tenth point 3 minutes and 48 seconds in. While I missed seeing all of that, this was clearly the start of something special.

And so it continued throughout the first half, leading to this screenshot as the first half ended:

Look at that line: 29 points, 15 boards and 5 blocks. If someone had asked you how Victor did against Dallas, you could have told them, "Great! He had 29 points, 15 boards and 5 blocks!" Then you could've enjoyed following that up with "and that was only the first half".

How about this; Wemby's halftime numbers were better than anyone else's entire game. For the Mavs, Kyrie Irving had 29 points for the game with 7 boards and 5 assists, but shot 9 for 27 from the floor. Naji Marshall had 22 points with 5 boards, 3 assists and 1 block. Neither measured up to Victor's first half. The Spurs' second leading scorer, Dylan Harper had 21 points (8 less than Victor's first half), 5 boards (10 less than Victor had at halftime) and zero assists (although no one on the Spurs other than Victor could make a shot – as we will discuss later).

Of course, Victor played after that remarkable first half, and the Spurs needed him to. When I was coaching, we had an expression when one player was playing well while his teammates were not – "I am sure glad we brought that guy." In the second half, Victor scored 23 points, grabbed 9 rebounds, and blocked 3 more shots. While not as unworldly as his first half, those second half stats were also better than any of his teammates' entire game. And of course, Victor's final line – 52 points, 24 boards (9 offensive) and 8 blocks — was truly historic.

The one thing Victor did not do well Wednesday night was shoot three-pointers – he went 1 for 8. But even that awful number was still better than most of his teammates. Including Victor's 1 for 8, the Spurs shot an incredible (in a very bad way), 5 for 36 from three. Yuck. The Spurs best two shooters, Devin Vassell and Julian Champagnie, went 0 for 6 and 0 for 5, respectively. De'Aaron Fox was 0 for 2 and Keldon Johnson 0 for 3. And while Stephon Castle was a bit better than Victor from three (but still only 2 for 9), he was still 6 for 20 overall. And with all that Victor was doing offensively, he only had one turnover – less than Fox, Castle, Harper, and Johnson.

Not surprisingly, Victor's eight blocks were more than the rest of the Spurs combined. In fact, he had more blocks than the rest of both teams combined. And he had more steals than every other Spur other than Vassell – they both had two. "I am sure glad we brought that guy," indeed.

One other thought that came to me during the game. We all know that Victor's already abundant competitive juices flow even more when he goes up against Chet Holmgren – see, for instance, the 2026 Western Conference Finals. After last night's game, there's at least a possibility that Victor has been reading about how he and the Mavs' young star Cooper Flagg will be competing over the next decade to be the best player in the NBA and the so-called "face of the league". Based on last night's performance, Victor certainly has the lead in that race – and those articles "spurred" him on during Wednesday's game.

I am glad the game was nationally televised for all hoops fans to see. I am especially glad that I remembered to record the game so that I could see it when I got home after Wednesday hoops. I wouldn't skip the weekly game with my crew, but not being able to watch Victor's memorable performance would've been a loss indeed – for me and for basketball fans of all political persuasions.
