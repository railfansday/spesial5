Tallulah Willis: I Stayed Out Past My Bedtime to Celebrate My Mom at the Baby2Baby Gala

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The whole endeavor began as it usually does, with a search for the perfect dress. Turns out it would be a yellow Carolina Herrera column-shaped confection (if you care), teetering on yellow satin pointed peekaboo-toe platforms, with my first press pass thudding around in my corresponding yellow duchess satin clutch.

Stylist Brad Goreski and his assistant Clarke, who work with my mother, Demi Moore, sourced the ensemble after finding Mom’s beautiful black ruffled two-piece (adorably also Carolina Herrera).

Around 6:50 p.m. on Saturday, my older sisters, Scout (in cocoa-brown beaded Elie Saab) and Rumer (in whimsical archival Vera Wang), florist Eric Buterbaugh, Brad, my mom and I are about to leave her house for the Baby2Baby Gala, an annual fundraiser for the non-profit that dedicated to providing diapers, clothing, and other basic necessities to children and families in need across the country.

Demi is taking photos against a pale brown backdrop when I hear her say, marginally worried, “Yes, the outfit is amazing, but they didn’t give me pockets.” I cannot fault her. A pocket, particularly in an evening gown, creates an entirely different architecture for one’s night.

When we make it to the Pacific Design Center in West Hollywood, Rihanna is blasting and a gorgeous electric General Motors car, part of the silent auction, is parked out front. I’m holding the bottom of my dress to avoid any “yard sales” on the carpeted floor when I hear, “Is it you? I hope it’s you!” It’s my very new, lovely friend Jackie Tohn, whom I met at a Nobody Wants This premiere party.

Inside, beautifully dressed people laugh and chat. I spot Miranda Kerr, who is both a Baby2Baby board member and major donor, and pass Nara Smith leaning over a dolled-up Elsa Hosk while “Espresso” blasts from overhead speakers.

Table 4 is front and center. Some could say the best seats in the house. Fitting, as Baby2Baby is honoring my mother with the Giving Tree Award tonight. The scene around our table is a frenzy of joyful energy. I make a beeline for Paris Hilton to thank her for the facial I received at her spa this week, while Dancing With the Stars alums Rumer and Jenna Dewan immediately pull out their phones to compare pictures of their kids.

Then I spot Sofia Richie Grainge and her husband, Atlantic Records CEO Elliot Grainge. I snake through throngs of elegant people and poke Elliot’s back twice. “Elliot, it’s Tallulah! We went to Wildwood together!” He instantly remembers me. “Tallulah was the cool girl in class,” he tells Sofia. I blush, but don’t feel the need to correct him.

Scout approaches and says, almost incredulously, “Hi, I’m Scout…you guys just signed me at Atlantic Records.” Elliot grabs her shoulders. “We’re so happy to have you. Are you happy with us?” She can’t say “yes” fast enough.

The program begins with Keke Palmer, who completely owns the stage, telling hilarious stories about her son before shifting into heartfelt candor about the gift of watching him grow. Kerry Washington thanks the sponsors and announces Baby2Baby’s new permanent headquarters, “a promise to millions of children. Not just for today, but for decades to come.”

Then Kelly Sawyer Patricof and Norah Weinstein, the organization’s CEOs, come to the stage, sharing the work—no, the magic—they have created over 15 years. Their first gala in 2012 raised $1 million. Last year’s raised $18 million. To date, they’ve distributed an astonishing 850 million essential items to people in need across all 50 states, responded to over 150 disasters, developed their own diaper production line during COVID, and expanded into mental health support for new mothers. The mission remains the same, but the scale is impossible to comprehend.

Now Rumer, Scout, and I are all crying during a short video about two families impacted by Baby2Baby. The feeling is palpable for the rest of the room.

As the live auction begins, I catch my mom discreetly reading over a printout of her speech. I don’t feel nerves coming off her, but a desire to match the elegance and integrity surrounding us.

Andrew Garfield takes the stage and speaks directly to my mom about the woman he knows. “She is a miracle,” he says.

Then it’s Demi’s turn. Holding her gleaming award in two hands, she thanks Andrew for carving out time amid his Artificial press tour. My mother adores Andrew, a feeling shared across our family.

Alone on stage in her Carolina gown (sans pockets), she asks everyone to breathe. “Inhale. Exhale.” That breath, she says, is the relief Baby2Baby provides when a mother no longer has to choose between dinner and diapers. “Your child matters, you matter, and you are not alone.” She leaves us with the idea that the essence of this work is to be remarkably human. Baby to baby, mother to mother, soul to soul.

Then Cardi B takes the stage and the atmosphere shifts with a jolt. Everyone is on their feet dancing. The official after-party begins, but I’m turning back into a pumpkin in towering satin stilettos. It was a remarkably beautiful night, and I’m left with the overwhelming desire to do more—to show up for someone who may not have the ability to show up for themselves or their precious loved ones.

But for this on-scene reporter’s inaugural correspondence, it was ultimately Baby2Baby2Bedtime.
