It's easy to write off every issue in the modern industry as someone else’s fault, but that’s not realistic; every part of the industry is struggling, inside and out.

Arkane Lyon is one studio that's been in hot water for a long while, struggling to find a buyer before Microsoft closes its doors. Following hopeful news as more buyers step up, a French union with ties to Arkane staff has accused Microsoft of attempting to sabotage the development of the studio’s current project, Blade, by barring hires for over a year.

The development of Marvel’s Blade has been absolutely hectic, between internal shifts in the game’s vision and an early announcement that put heat on Arkane far before they were ready. Now, STJV, the French union representing certain members of Arkane Lyon’s staff, has made a major accusation, claiming the game is coming along fine, despite Microsoft’s interference.

According to STJV in a direct quotation, “Today, as work on Blade is going fine, and the level of quality of the game to be expected is on [par] with what Arkane has accustomed its players, workers have to face the harsh reality: our studio might get closed because, and only because, Microsoft decided to, even if that means sabotaging the game it funded.”

This all still has yet to be confirmed, as no direct response from Microsoft has been released, despite the accusations levied by STJV. Alongside the biting remarks, the union has dropped a series of demands, including a say in who buys Arkane, and a larger payout for the staff if no buyer steps forward.

Despite all of this swinging out, Arkane is still eager to release Blade, staying optimistic that the title will release in the following years if Arkane is allowed to keep their doors open. STJV keeps the argument practical, framing the studio’s progress as a financial incentive for both parties. “Even this closure itself is done against any financial logic, as closing Arkane today would probably be more expensive than finishing the game,” says STJV.

Arkane’s aim to finish the game despite their wild situation is comforting to see, and gives me real hope at the quality of their Blade title, as long as it manages to release. The whispers of big studios like Ubisoft’s interest make it clear more than just Arkane want to see the title released, and as the months continue to tick by with the company still open, those whispers just get louder.

Arkane is a studio that would feel like a serious loss for the larger gaming industry if they closed their doors, even after stumbles like Redfall. Dropping into the mainstream scene with hits like Dishonored and Prey, Arkane Lyon is infamous for worlds that feel so lived-in, even after devastation has raked across them.

They truly do feel like the perfect team to work on Blade, as their mastery of immersive sims and wild powers leaves massive room for the exact type of action Blade is perfect for. It really does feel like every production surrounding Blade is hexed, if the canceled films and Arkane’s own pains are anything to look at.

I’m very eager to hear a response from Microsoft, as STJV has levied some major accusations that could really disrupt how third parties and internal studios interact with them. At present, we only have STJV’s word to go off of, so take these accusations with a grain of salt until more comes out.
