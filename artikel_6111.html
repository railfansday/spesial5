The Best Places to Go in Europe in 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Hoping to discover some of the best places to go in Europe in 2027? No wonder: 2026 was a bumper year for European travel, from the Winter Olympics fueling a surging obsession with the Dolomites to the total solar eclipse bringing hoards of umbraphiles to the Balearic Islands, and celebrity weddings adding to an ever-growing love for Sicily. But as we edge closer to the end of the year, there are a few clear-cut destinations we’re most looking forward to visiting (and recommending to you, our fellow travelers) for the 12 months to come.

We’ve asked our expert contributors from all around the globe to nominate the spots that are on the up—the places that are on their radar thanks to a flock of hotel openings, major cultural moments, talk of new flight routes, or concerted conservation efforts taking root. These nominations make up the Best Places to Go in Europe in 2027—and we promise they are worthy of your precious vacation time and hard-earned spending money.

From untamed wilderness in the Albanian Alps to the world’s oldest art fair (celebrating a big birthday in 2027) in Cologne, off to everyone’s latest favorite coastal coolcation destination in Denmark, this is our guide to the latest and greatest in Europe in 2027. Happy travels. —Olivia Morelli

Go for: untamed wilderness and peaceful pockets

The Albanian Alp's village of Theth is a patch of pastoral living—and fantastic hiking trails.

Local bed-and-breakfasts and guesthouses welcome trekkers, who do both day treks and through hikes connecting small towns.

As Albania’s allure continues to grow, travelers flock to the Adriatic beaches of the south. But in the northern alpine region, the scenery offers a different type of drama—jagged snow-dipped peaks, turquoise rivers, hut-to-hut hiking—and in place of shiny seaside hotels, Albanian-owned wooden B&Bs welcome alpinists for home-cooked meals after long, satisfying days on the many surrounding trails. This area was largely isolated during the communist era, given its strategic location along the borders with Montenegro and Kosovo, which means that it has been slower to open its arms to the world (that’s also why you’ll stumble into countless abandoned bunkers from this period on your hike). The feeling here is that you can still show up to a guesthouse without a reservation, you can still camp wherever you find a spot, and, yet, there is also the sense that if you aren’t seeing this place now, or within the next couple years, you’ll miss the precious moment when it still feels like a secret to be let in on.

Start your trip in the charming city of Shköder, known as the gateway to the Alps, where bikepackers cycle through the city, and restaurant patios spill over with guests enjoying glasses of Albanian wine. From there, many travelers plan stops in the villages of Theth and Valbona (a stunningly scenic one-day through-hike links the two of them), or embark on ambitious multi-day journeys on routes like the Peak of the Balkans, which traces a two-week-long loop crossing into Kosovo and Montenegro. Infrastructure improvements have made roads and trails to access this region better in recent years, with much more where that came from. What was once only known by residents and those who purchased their paper guides now exists online, meaning it is also possible to base yourself in, say, the Eden-like Valbona Valley, and embark on a new route each day. Increasingly, there are elevated places to stay that preserve the soul of this place, opening in a slow trickle like Valbona Resort and Spa (2024) and SeSam Villas in Theth (2025). There may be folklore calling these the Accursed Mountains, but only because they are so vast and dramatic that their geography has historically kept intruders out. For hikers, that means a literal playground, with Albanian hosts leading the way. —Megan Spurrell

Go for: the next World Expo centered on play, and a booming hotel scene to match

Belgrade is set to host the World Expo in summer 2027, and a flock of new hotels will be ready to receive the travelers it is sure to bring.

All the world’s coming to Belgrade next year—and, it seems, coming to play. The Balkan city will host the next edition of the World Expo, a spiritual successor to the World Fair where nations come to exchange their latest and greatest cultural achievements and boldest ideas for the future. From May 15 to August 15, 2027, Belgrade plans to welcome over four million visitors to explore the theme of “Play for Humanity: Sport and Music for All." Its status as a host city feels especially symbolic as the Balkans emerge as a leader in technology and innovation across Europe. To meet the occasion, the city’s hotel scene is stepping up, with 15 new openings slated to cut the ribbon by the time the program kicks off. The crown jewel in the portfolio is the Ritz-Carlton, Belgrade, which will occupy a prime spot on the Danube riverfront. Other big names to watch out for include the InterContinental Belgrade and the Swissôtel Belgrade. In total, these projects will add roughly 1,500 to 2,000 new hotel rooms to the city. The shiny, new threads go well with the old in a place as historic as the Serbian capital, which lays claim to grand sites such as the Church of St. Sava, one of the largest Orthodox churches in the world, and the Museum of Yugoslavia, which has a sprawling eight-acre park complex. —Kat Chen

Go for: a coveted summer destination at its sweet spot

A slew of luxury openings like Aman and Mandarin Oriental have arrived in Bodrum over the past few years, with NG Sign Bodrum and Bvlgari on the horizon.

For the past few summers, the glittering Bodrum peninsula has welcomed a wave of luxury openings, transforming this once-sleepy harbor town into one of the Aegean's most coveted corners. Aman, Mandarin Oriental, and homegrown Maçakizi were early champions of the scene and remain firmly at the heart of its appeal, but a new generation of hotels, restaurants, and beach clubs has since made Bodrum one of Europe’s most sought-after summer escapes.

Mayfair and Knightsbridge restaurant favorites Zuma, Bagatelle, and Novikov have all made the journey east in recent years, accompanied in 2026 by oh-so-chic arrivals from Dubai, Paris, and Marbella (Gaia, Gigi Rigolatto, and Maison Revka). Together they have helped recast Bodrum as a playground for the international jet set, bringing with them a distinctly cosmopolitan, see-and-be-seen energy.

This summer also brought the long-awaited arrival of the adults-only hideaway Oku, the latest in the brand's collection of design-led retreats in Ibiza, Kos, and Andalusia. Throw in annual beach-club takeovers from the likes of Jacquemus and Alo, as well as boutique openings by brands like Cartier, and it seems there’s barely a name in luxury that isn’t lining up to plant its flag here.

Enjoy it while it lasts. In 2028, all eyes will return to the peninsula as Bvlgari opens its sprawling hillside resort above yacht-speckled Paradise Bay, giving Europe's luxury travel crowd fresh reason to return. —Sophie Prideaux

Go for: Irish hospitality and spectacular sporting scenes

The 46th Ryder Cup will take place at Adare Manor in County Limerick.

Designed by golf course architect Tom Fazio, Adare is nestled upon 840 acres of parkland.

Home to natural wonders, ancient castles, lively pubs, and famously friendly locals, Ireland has long captivated travelers. While the well-trodden tourist trail takes in perennial favorites, 2027 will see another destination step into the spotlight. County Limerick, on the west coast, is gearing up to host one of the world’s greatest sporting events: the 46th Ryder Cup. Between September 17 and 19, the tournament will take place on the pristine fairways of the prestigious golfing estate Adare Manor. Designed by renowned golf course architect Tom Fazio, the championship course is set within 840 acres of magnificent parkland surrounding the estate’s 19th-century Neo-Gothic castle—a fairytale destination worth visiting at any time of year.

Beyond golf, Limerick has a rich sporting heritage. Thomond Park, home of Munster Rugby, is one of the best-supported grounds in the world, with match days bringing a buzz to the city. Beyond the sporting calendar, there’s plenty to discover. Highlights include the 13th-century King John’s Castle, the Hunt Museum for its art collection including works by Picasso and Renoir, and Lough Gur, an idyllic lake beside Ireland’s oldest and largest stone circle. Limerick also makes an excellent base for exploring the Wild Atlantic Way. Around an hour northwest of the city, Lahinch is widely regarded as one of Ireland’s best surf beaches, while the spectacular Cliffs of Moher rank among the country’s most beloved landmarks. With world-class golf, a proud sporting heritage, fascinating history, and easy access to some of Ireland’s most spectacular scenery, Limerick is set to have its moment in 2027. —Camilla Hewitt

Go for: a city break that’ll stay with you long after you’ve left

In Cologne's Old Town, the charm is in the details, like this whale lantern outside Brauhaus Sünner im Walfisch .

If you visit for Cologne's exuberant carnival, say as the locals say: “Morje es, wat do drus mähs!” (“Tomorrow is what you make of it!”)

Let’s be honest: Cologne doesn’t necessarily win you over at first sight. It is not a conventionally beautiful city; rather, it is a feeling. Even locals will tell you that the city gets under your skin. Anyone who has experienced Cologne’s exuberant carnival spilling onto its streets and into its clubs and pubs is likely to fall for the city—and its people—for good. The motto for the so-called fifth season in 2027 is “morje es, wat do drus mähs!” (“tomorrow is what you make of it!”). Multiple crises or not, the people of Cologne refuse to let anything dampen their spirits.

And while folk culture is a major part of Cologne’s appeal, the city’s high culture is equally compelling. In September 2026, the opera house on Offenbachplatz finally reopened after 15 years of renovation—an occasion eagerly anticipated by many. Just 160 yards away is Kolumba, the museum designed by Pritzker Prize-winning architect Peter Zumthor. Its lofty, lime-washed rooms and the gentle light filtering through the narrow gaps between walls and floor provide a quietly dramatic setting for the art—and a welcome respite from the bustle of the city. Zumthor built the museum around the ruins of the former St. Kolumba church, incorporating the Madonna in the Ruins chapel by Gottfried Böhm. The work of two exceptional architects brought together in one place: that alone is reason enough to come.

The Museum Ludwig is another perennial draw. In 2026, it recorded its highest-ever visitor numbers thanks to its exhibition devoted to the late Japanese artist Yayoi Kusama. Art, in Cologne, is very much a capital-A affair. Every autumn, the international art world descends on the city for Art Cologne, the world’s oldest contemporary art fair, which celebrates its 60th anniversary in 2027. The 200th anniversary of Beethoven’s death is being marked in equally spectacular fashion. Throughout the year, the Cologne Symphony Orchestra will welcome a stellar line-up of renowned conductors and ensembles. The musical celebrations culminate in October, when pianist Igor Levit will take on all 32 of the great composer’s piano sonatas in a single evening.

The city’s famously warm and open-hearted inhabitants are just as welcoming when it comes to gastronomic newcomers. Julia Komp, who became Germany’s youngest Michelin-starred chef in 2016 at the age of 27, knows this well. Today, her restaurant Sahila is firmly at the forefront of Cologne’s culinary scene. Other top tables include the intimate 20-seat Onda on Händelstrasse; Vunky on Eigelstein, specializing in plant-based fine dining; Toki, the city’s first kaiseki restaurant; and the recently opened Kluth, whose six-course surprise menu, “et kütt wie et kütt” (“it is what it is”), takes its name from a quintessential Cologne saying. —Ilona Marx

Go for: rugged landscapes and cinematic coastlines

Sailing yacht Orient Express Corinthian's itineraries will set sail to Bonifacio, Calvi, and Saint-Florent in 2027.

Hotel Basgi Basgi is a favorite among design lovers in Corsica—a grow that is increasingly looking to the island.

When Simon Jacquemus chose Phare de la Pietra, a 19th-century lighthouse on a rocky Corsican peninsula, as the backdrop for his Spring 2027 fashion show this summer, it was easy to see why. The designer, long inspired by the interplay of place, light, and raw emotion, described his show Le Bonheur (meaning “happiness” or “bliss”) as “a cliff that I add colors on top. An impressionist painting.”

It's true. Corsica is a place where cinematic elegance meets rugged natural beauty. It's why the island was once named Kallisté, meaning “most beautiful,” by the ancient Greeks. Here, dramatic mountains meet wild hinterlands and fiery red rockfaces jut against turquoise waters, making for breathtaking hiking through UNESCO-listed landscapes such as the Scandola Nature Reserve.

The island long slipped under the radar compared with neighboring Sardinia and the French Riviera. But Jacquemus’ arrival, coupled with a flurry of recent smart hotel openings, suggests that may not be the case for much longer. Leading the charge is A Mandria di Murtoli, a 10-room retreat spread across five restored stone buildings in the Ortolo Valley, which is a little sister to epicurean Domaine de Murtoli, where farm-to-table living is the order of the day. Elsewhere, Hotel Mylos on the west coast and Basgi Basgi in the north are drawing an increasingly design-conscious crowd.

Adding to the island’s momentum is the maiden voyage of the Orient Express Corinthian, a sailing yacht with 2027 itineraries calling at Bonifacio, Calvi, and Saint-Florent, opening up Corsica's fishing ports, medieval citadels, and spectacular coastline to a new wave of travelers. —Ashleigh Spiliopoulou

Go for: a coolcation destination with Michelin status

Newly-opened Betty Beach brings street food and brewery offerings to the beachfront of the Danish Riviera.

The 60-mile so-called Danish Rivera is dotted with fishing villages, seaside resorts, and beaches.

With rising summer temperatures across Europe, the Danish Riviera has emerged as the continent's favorite coastal coolcation destination. This 60-mile coastal stretch in Northern Zealand brings travelers the aesthetic appeal and culinary credibility of Copenhagen, but in a relaxed setting of fishing villages, sandy beaches, forests, and historical seaside resorts that remain largely undiscovered by international visitors.

The Louisiana Museum of Modern Art in Humlebæk marks the southern end of the riviera, but many visitors staying in Copenhagen don't venture any further north to seaside towns. Many of the region's charming seaside hotels date back to the late 19th century. But a new generation of stays is rejuvenating the landscape with contemporary design. Cori in Hornbæk brings barefoot energy to a beautifully repurposed former hospital, and Hôtel de Ville in Gilleleje transforms an old town hall into an eclectic boutique spa hotel.

Exceptional gastronomy abounds, with Michelin-starred standouts such as Jordnær, The Samuel, and Parsley Salon representing New Nordic Cuisine at its finest. For something more casual, recently opened Betty Beach combines beachfront dining, street food, and a brewery for sun-kissed imbibing. Denmark's first official wine route launched last year in North Zealand and Odsherred, with nine wineries offering tours and tastings of cold-climate grape varietals such as Solaris and Rondo. —Amber Gibson

Go for: wild Austen-esque adventures and spa stays galore

Dartmoor's vast landscape, made of granite tors, walking paths, and moorland, brings the drama of Sense and Sensibility to life.

Dartmoor has always lent itself to drama. Wild ponies pick their way through heather, granite tors loom out of the mist, and the ever-changing weather plays a starring role. Now it’s getting its close-up as the backdrop of Georgia Oakley’s new Sense and Sensibility adaptation in theaters in September 2026. The film was shot around Princetown, Tavistock, and Gutter Tor, and its release coincides with Dartmoor National Park’s 75th anniversary. As the Austen connection pulls focus to Dartmoor, 2027 is the year to stay a little longer and explore a little further.

It’s unusually special in the depths of winter, when the weather gets wild. Picture misty mornings walking the ancient tracks and climbing tors, and afternoons yomping across moorland in conditions that demand retreating somewhere cozy for a hot bath and a roaring fire. For a hands-on experience, book one of the free conservation days running on selected Sundays in 2027 to learn more about Dartmoor’s prehistoric archaeology, wildlife, and rare species while helping to preserve its future. Don’t miss Tavistock, the old market town on Dartmoor’s western edge. Its handsome Pannier Market is bursting with antiques and curiosities, ideal for a spot of treasure hunting. For a change of pace, visit 11th-century Buckfast Abbey, where the Benedictine monks still make their famous tonic wine to a secret recipe.

To feel like a main character in your own period drama, book a room at Boringdon Hall. This show-stopping historical hotel on the edge of the moor combines roaring fires and old-world grandeur with one of Devon’s best spas. Its Michelin-starred Àclèaf restaurant retained its star in 2026, a quietly heroic achievement in a world obsessed with shiny new openings. There is nothing like spending the wildest part of the year getting wind-beaten on Dartmoor, then retreating for a spa treatment, proper grub, and a fire. —Zoe Cook

Go for: a hidden culture hotspot on the Iberian Peninsula

Évora, Portugal's prettiest city, is now home to the Vermelho by Christian Louboutin hotel.

Évora's artisan traditions that helped earn the city its title as a European Capital of Culture.

Although there’s really no need to do so—Évora is impressive in its own right—we sometimes refer to this little historic city as the Seville of Portugal. A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1986, its center comprises an imposing Roman temple, known as the Temple of Diana; a Gothic cathedral; the eerie Baroque Cathedral of Bones, next to the Church of St. Francis; and the Renaissance Palace of King Manuel I.

In 2027, Évora will turn even more heads as one of the European Capitals of Culture. But Portugal’s prettiest city remains, overwhelmingly, a peaceful place, where at many times of the year you can stroll around in near silence. Then there’s the food. Restaurants such as Tua Madre, A Cozinha do Paço, and Forno da Telha allow you to discover recipes deeply rooted in the land and tradition. Don’t leave without trying porco à alentejana (clams and pork stew), ensopado de borrego (lamb stew), and a variety of local soups. And bread—plenty of bread—with superb olive oil. There’s also wine, of course, produced on the herdades (traditional estates dotted throughout the Alentejo region). After so much eating, you’ll need to get out and about. Which is perhaps why we find the concept behind Évora’s status as a European Capital of Culture so charming—the motto is based on the Alentejo’s cultural way of being: vagar, or to wander. —David Moralejo

Fuerteventura's volcanic landscape offers a stirring contrast to its surf beaches—and a unique draw for travelers headed to the Canary Islands.

Boutique stay Casa Montelongo is one of several design-forward gems, this one tucked away in the charming village of La Oliva.

The second-largest and oldest of the Canary Islands, Fuerteventura is quickly establishing itself as one of the archipelago’s leading destinations. First came Paradisus by Meliá Fuerteventura, followed more recently by Zel Fuerteventura (the lifestyle brand created by Rafael Nadal in partnership with Meliá Hotels International). Set side by side, the two resorts form the Atlantic’s first wellness hub, set within a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. Their privileged location on Sotavento beach completes the experience, with stretches of white sand and crystal-clear waters synonymous with the island.

Fuerteventura is also recognized as one of the world's best destinations for water sports, particularly windsurfing and kitesurfing. The area surrounding both hotels is home to René Egli by Meliá, a renowned water sports center. And beyond its coastline, the island continues to stand out. Charming villages such as La Oliva are home to a new generation of boutique projects, including Casa Montelongo, one of the area's hidden gems. With the spectacular Corralejo Dunes, traditional Majorero cheese, and dramatic volcanic and desert landscapes contrasting with idyllic beaches, Fuerteventura is emerging as the ideal destination for travelers seeking a holiday centered on sport, wellness, relaxation, and reconnection. —Maria Ángeles Cano

Go for: a vibrant city finally getting its moment in the spotlight

Beyond Liepāja's beaches, converted historic and Soviet-era buildings are a reason to visit this cultural hub in 2027.

Plenty of the most seasoned travelers have never heard of Liepāja. That’s about to change: the Baltic coastal city has been named a European Capital of Culture in 2027, bringing a year-long program of public art, performances, and festivals with an allocated $11 million in funding. Curious visitors casting their gaze towards the little-known city will discover art-nouveau architecture, an atmospheric former naval district, and one of the longest urban beaches in Northern Europe.

It’s certainly smaller and considerably less visited than Riga, but the repurposed historic and Soviet-era spaces are strangely wonderful. Wiktorija, a former cinema dating from 1913, now operates as a multi-use cultural venue hosting everything from poetry evenings to rock concerts. Take a guided tour around Karosta, the former Tsarist and Soviet naval district, to explore the history of landmarks like the striking St. Nicholas Naval Cathedral. The Great Amber Concert Hall, meanwhile, is home to the Liepāja Symphony Orchestra, the oldest in the Baltics, which is already expanding its program ahead of 2027. Rooted firmly in the present rather than the Soviet past are businesses like MO Liepāja in Julianna's Courtyard, a cluster of restored industrial buildings along the Trade Canal now housing restaurants, bars, and creative outposts such as local surfboard maker Drtyboards. —Amber Gibson

Go for: a charm-packed city break, with new hotels and gorgeous coastal scenes to boot

Naples will host the Louis Vuitton 38th America's Cup next summer.

As for where to rest your head? Rocco Forte Hotels will open a 46-suite stay in the 16th-century Palazzo Sirignano.

Gritty and pretty, chaotic and cultured, ancient and contemporary, Naples is one of Italy's most exhilarating cities. In 2027, the Campanian capital will host the Louis Vuitton 38th America's Cup (July 10 to 18), an event that has accelerated the regeneration of the industrial Bagnoli waterfront. Away from the waves, the hospitality scene is evolving, too. Rocco Forte Hotels will open its first Naples property, a 46-suite retreat occupying the 16th-century Palazzo Sirignano, restored by architect Michele Bönan, and complete with a rooftop pool looking out over the Bay of Naples and Mount Vesuvius.

But Naples has never needed another reason to visit, with masterpieces such as Caravaggio's The Seven Works of Mercy and Giuseppe Sanmartino's Veiled Christ, and the antiquities of its National Archaeological Museum, whose forthcoming second site, Mann II, will bring thousands of artifacts out of storage. The city’s contemporary art scene continues to evolve at the Madre Museum, which recently welcomed internationally acclaimed curator Eugenio Viola as its new director. Nearby, Pompeii and Herculaneum agricultural parks remain as compelling as ever. And then, of course, there’s the warmth and spirit of the Neapolitan people.

Da Attilio, Concettina ai Tre Santi, 50 Kalò, Diego Vitagliano, and Pizzaria La Notizia are practically compulsory for pizza, but there’s more to the city’s rich food scene. Donna Teresa and Mimì alla Ferrovia are among the beloved mainstays serving some of the finest traditional Neapolitan cuisine. —Jaclyn de Giorgio

Parga is the kind of Grecian destination every traveler hopes to find: easy to reach, yet seemingly under the radar. New hotels in 2026 and 2027 add to the appeal, without blowing its cover.

Glittering Ionian water, pine-clad hills tumbling into a colorful harbor, and a Venetian castle above it all: Parga is undoubtedly picturesque. Yet this little corner of the Greek mainland remains surprisingly under the radar. Its old town is made for slow wandering, leisurely lunching on seafood or traditional Greek mezze at waterfront tavernas, and exploring seriously beautiful beaches such as Valtos, a gold sand bay with award-winning Blue Flag credentials. Inland, meanwhile, the Acheron River makes a heavenly setting for horseback riding, rafting, canyon walks, and swimming.

Part of Parga’s appeal is how easy it is to reach: Tiny-but-mighty Preveza Airport is only an hour away, making this feel like a secret without the schlep. Parga Beach Resort, directly on Valtos Beach, makes a particularly excellent base. Its new beachfront suites, launched in late summer 2026, sleep up to two adults and four children. A regular water taxi nips around the bay to Parga town, turning supper in that spectacular harbor into part of the adventure. The 2027 opening of Varko Bay nearby will add another high-end stay, with villas, a spa, sandy beaches, and a private marina. —Zoe Cook
