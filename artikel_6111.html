82% Of Americans Fly Once A Year Or Less—And Don’t Know The Rules

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Last Thanksgiving, if you saw an open aisle seat on Southwest Airlines, you were welcome to take it. Do exactly what you learned a year ago and this Thanksgiving you’re the guy holding up the aisle because the passenger behind you has a boarding pass for your seat, and you’re getting scolded by a flight attendant. Travel rules change! Boarding at Southwest has changed! In YouGov’s November 17–19 poll of 1,092 U.S. adult citizens, 34% said they fly less than once a year, 32% said never and 16% said once a year. That’s 66% who fly less than annually (including never) and 82% who fly once a year or less. Just 34% said they’d flown in the past 12 months and 65% said they hadn’t. Who’s Actually In The Seat Next To You? In 2015, then-American Airlines president Scott Kirby said 87% of American’s customers flew them just once in the previous year and generated about half their revenue. Kirby was explaining why American needed basic economy. For every 100 American adults who flew in 2017, 24 took just one trip, and all 100 averaged 5.3 trips apiece. In the 1970s and 1980s, roughly half the air trips Americans took were for business. Personal trips already accounted for about 70% before the pandemic and 74% in 2025. My line when business travel dried up during the pandemic was that “all airline passengers are Spirit Airlines passengers now.” Mask rules drove most of the reported trouble. Of 5,981 unruly passenger reports the Federal Aviation Administration received in 2021, 4,290 involved masks. But losing the regulars sprinkled through coach mattered too. What It Costs To Go See Family In 1977, before deregulation, 25% of U.S. adults flew that year and 63% had ever flown. I’ve written about how much less work it takes to buy a ticket. Between 1995 and 2025, the average domestic fare fell from $618 to $387 in 2025 dollars, a 37% drop excluding bag and seat fees. Fee-inclusive costs are down, too. In a separate YouGov poll earlier in November, 18% of people with family incomes under $50,000 had flown in the past year, compared with 66% at $100,000 or more. Family travel multiplies the cost of tickets across each person while driving costs are constant. AAA expects 72.9 million Americans to drive this Thanksgiving holiday and just under 6 million to fly domestically. Say you book a 40 minute connection. You don’t know whether that’s a good idea or not, and may not even know or understand the connecting time, just the departure and arrival times. And if your regional jet on the first segment valet tags your bag, you’re waiting for it on the jet bridge, too. My status gets me on early enough to find space for my bag. When a flight cancels, I’m looking up alternatives on my phone while I’m in line for an agent. I still have days when I wonder how anybody who does this once a year manages to get where they’re going.

The person who took their laptop out at security on the way there and left it in the bag on the way home may have followed instructions both times. It depends on the scanner in their lane.

Say you book a 40 minute connection. You don’t know whether that’s a good idea or not, and may not even know or understand the connecting time, just the departure and arrival times. The airline ends boarding 15 minutes before departure. You’ve got 25 minutes even if you’re on time, some of which you’ll spend getting off the first plane. And if your regional jet on the first segment valet tags your bag, you’re waiting for it on the jet bridge, too.

I wound up on Southwest’s first day of assigned seating because American went through three itineraries trying to get me to Washington and I finally bought a ticket to Baltimore. At the gate, Southwest called Group 3 to line up under a sign that still said Group 1. Where, exactly, was the passenger who stopped to read the sign supposed to stand?

I’m home this Thanksgiving. I traveled the week before and will travel again the week after. I like to avoid ‘amateur day’. And when I do travel I try to go where other people aren’t. My favorite Thanksgiving trip was Chennai (I wanted to have Thanksgiving dinner with Indians), and I had to explain to CBP on arrival back in the states that we’d done that because it was cheaper booking Lufthansa first class (on points) than going to visit my wife’s family.
