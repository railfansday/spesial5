inside ZHA’s royal theater in morocco, white curves meet crystalline caverns

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

new photographs by hufton + crow reveal the sweeping riverside architecture and faceted interiors of ZHA's théâtre royal de rabat.

Zaha Hadid Architects (ZHA) has released new photographs of the Théâtre Royal de Rabat in Morocco, revealing the completed architecture of its first realized project in Africa. Captured by Hufton + Crow, the images trace the theater‘s sweeping white exterior along the Bouregreg River before moving inside, where intersecting staircases lead toward a dramatically faceted auditorium.

Originally announced in 2010 as the Grand Théâtre de Rabat, the project has followed a long path to completion. Construction began in 2014, and the building received a ceremonial inauguration in October 2024. Its first public performance followed in April 2026, ten years after the death of Zaha Hadid.

Situated between the historic cities of Rabat and Salé, the theater forms part of a wider cultural development program initiated by King Mohammed VI. Its low, undulating profile draws from the tidal landscape of the Bouregreg, with broad white surfaces that rise above the riverbank and extend toward the surrounding park.

The theater’s exterior by ZHA takes its shape from the movement of Morocco’s Bouregreg river. The architects sculpt long, layered bands which sweep across the building to rise over the main auditorium and descend toward the entrance plaza. From above, the theater appears almost continuous with the landscaped ground, its curved silhouette stretching across a broad site of lawns and paved walkways.

The smooth appearance conceals a complex concrete structure. A system of structural ribs supports the curved envelope, while molded glass-fiber-reinforced concrete panels form the exterior skin. Narrow bands of glazing interrupt the white surfaces, and integrated lighting follows the building’s edges, tracing its geometry against the river at night.

Extending eastward from the main building is an open-air amphitheater with room for 7,000 spectators. The landscaped grounds connect the performance spaces to the waterfront, while environmental systems respond to the local climate. Heat-recovery equipment works alongside a thermal labyrinth that pre-cools incoming air, and rainwater collected below ground supplies the site’s irrigation system.

Inside the Théâtre Royal de Rabat, the fluid forms of the exterior continue through a vast, triple-height foyer. White staircases cross through the space at different levels, while curved overhead surfaces seem to peel away from the walls. Against the dark, angled glazing along the perimeter, the pale interior takes on an almost carved appearance.

The foyer is conceived as a public gathering space as well as the main circulation route through the building. Visitors move between overlapping balconies and broad stairways, with views opening across the central void. The photographs capture the scale of these interiors particularly well, from the narrow passages between sculpted walls to the open floor below.

At the uppermost level, a restaurant overlooks the Bouregreg Valley and the historic medina. The space continues the building’s sculptural language, with long horizontal openings that frame views of the surrounding city. Across the public areas, the architecture maintains a continuous sense of movement from the entrance to the upper floors.

Beyond the smooth white interiors, the 1,821-seat main auditorium introduces a dramatic change in geometry. Its walls and ceiling break into angular, crystalline surfaces inspired by muqarnas, the intricate three-dimensional vaulting found in traditional Moroccan and Islamic architecture. The forms project over the seating and around the stage, creating a dense composition of folded planes.

These faceted surfaces also serve an acoustic purpose. Developed with acoustic engineers at Arup New York, the hall is designed to accommodate opera and other large-scale productions, with sound distributed across its multiple seating levels. The angular interior responds dramatically to theatrical lighting, as different surfaces catch color and shadow during performances.

The complex also includes a smaller, 250-seat Studio Theatre for experimental productions and rehearsals. Retractable seating allows the room to shift between a flat-floor configuration and a traditional proscenium stage. Together with the riverside amphitheater, the performance spaces accommodate everything from intimate workshops to major public festivals.
