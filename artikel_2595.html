Bucks vs. Heat Player Grades: Jakucionis and Herro continue good and bad form as Giannis dominates

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

On a momentous night when the Milwaukee Bucks and Giannis Antetokounmpo faced each other for the first time, the Miami Heat pulled off a 137-130 win. Frankly, the final score flatters the Bucks, who spent most of the game down ~20 as Giannis diced them up from all over the court and finished with a near triple-double. As for interesting interactions between former teammates on both sides, there really wasn't much to report. Tyler Herro got booed pretty mercilessly, while the reaction to his other three former Heat teammates was pretty lax. Giannis barely interacted with anyone from the Bucks (other than a brief joking interaction with Jericho Sims after Sims fouled him). Frankly, it looked a little awkward for everyone involved. Read our full summary of the game here and catch a six-minute audio recap on the Bucks+ podcast, Bucks In Six Minutes, below.

24 minutes, 8 points, 4 assists, 1 steal, 4/11 FG, 0/5 3P, 0/1 FT, -18

Herro was awful, yet again. Insipid on defence and unable to do anything with the Heat's quick, physical guards as they hounded him up and down the court. I was desperately hoping for a snarling, trash-talking Herro who gave the Heat the business, but he went out with a whimper with the team looked better with him on the bench.

23 minutes, 10 points, 2 rebounds, 5 assists, 2 turnovers, 3/7 FG, 1/4 3P, 3/4 FT, -8

Jaquez was fine. He had his usual backbreaking turnover or two, but otherwise he was kinda just out there and made little impact, positive or negative.

30 minutes, 26 points, 5 rebounds, 1 assist, 2 steals, 1 block, 3 turnovers, 9/16 FG, 2/5 3P, 6/10 FT, -18

Credit where it's due: Kuzma was fantastic. Milwaukee would have lost this game by at least 30 if not for Kuzma's work on both ends. He was about as good as anyone could have asked for as one of two primary defenders on Giannis, and his ability to keep the scoreboard ticking—even if it came drawing a BS foul—was crucial in slowing the Heat's barrage of scoring runs.

21 minutes, 4 points, 5 rebounds, 4 assists, 3 blocks, 2/5 FG, 0/2 3P, -15

Turner didn't do anything that stood out as actively detrimental to the team, but he wasn't a major plus either. Based on our lowered expectations for the big man this season, I would say he played fine. They kept Turner away from Giannis as much as possible, and I thought he did a solid job as a weak-side rim protector.

17 minutes, 11 points, 3 rebounds, 1 steal, 5/7 FG, 1/2 3P, -15

I mean, Ware got most of his when the game was, for all intents and purposes, over, scoring four of his five buckets in the second half. Still, I liked the way he ran the floor and kept his hands ready. I will say that whenever Giannis had a chance to go at Ware, you could almost hear his inner monologue saying, "BBQ chicken."

25 minutes, 20 points, 7 rebounds, 1 assist, 2 steals, 3 turnovers, 6/9 FG, 3/5 3P, 5/6 FT, -14

Burries was great, especially in the third quarter, when he was the only guy who wanted to play with a spark, scoring 12 points on 4/6 shooting, including a sequence where he scored a layup, stole the inbounds pass, and flipped it to a streaking Kuzma for another layup. I thought his defence was a major plus too, as he fought over and under screens on the Heat guards.

25 minutes, 20 points, 3 rebounds, 5 assists, 2 steals, 0 turnovers, 6/8 FG, 3/5 3P, 5/6 FT, +11

Jakucionis with his second consecutive great game. Once again, his pace and burst were far more useful than Herro's finesse. KJ's second-half line is insane: 5/5 from the field, 3/3 from three, and 5/6 from the charity stripe with four assists, two steals, and no turnovers. He made several great passes out of the pick-and-roll, showcasing his ability to create a two-on-one and read the help defender.

17 minutes, 7 points, 4 rebounds, 1 assist, 3 turnovers, 1/5 FG, 0/3 3P, 5/5 FT, +9

Ament wasn't overly involved in the first half, missing both of his three-point attempts. However, there were some encouraging signs in the second half. One facet of Ament's game that's evolving is his feel. They had him sprint up to set middle and side ball screens to see if he could catch it on the slip when the defence was switching, and he got a nice scoring opportunity doing exactly that. Interestingly, they also used Ament a bit as a passing hub at the top of the key as screening actions occurred around him; he hit LeVert on a nice dime while playing that role.

16 minutes, 1 point, 2 steals, 0/5 FG, 0/3 3P, -19

17 minutes, 4 points, 6 rebounds, 2 assists, 2 turnovers, 1/3 FG, +12

Sims was the other primary defender on Giannis (along with Kuzma), and he was solid enough. Attacked the rebounds hard and even made some nice passing reads.

14 minutes, 11 points, 3/5 FG, 3/5 3P, +17

Green got most of his opportunities off roll-and-replace actions, where the point guard (mainly Jakucionis) created a two-on-one in the pick-and-roll, forcing Green's man defender to rotate over and tag the roller, leaving him open for a split second from three.

Obviously, the game plan centred on defending Giannis. I have always said that the first step to stopping Giannis is having a defender who can at least trouble him one-on-one, and then you're able to help opportunistically as opposed to every time down. The Bucks don't have that guy, so their plan was basically to turn GA into a passer, and he wrecked them doing exactly that. There's nothing you can do about that, but I at least liked that Jenkins chose to take something away and didn't let him score and pass; he shot just four times in the first half. I also suspect Sims started the last game to prepare for this one—and that starting won't be regular—but I could be wrong.

Inactive: Kam Jones, Cormac Ryan, Pete Nance, Ousmane Dieng, Bogoljub Markovic, Ryan Rollins

The Bucks do not have a passable Giannis defender on their roster. Kuzma and Sims did their best and competed admirably, but neither was enough to really make him work.

Jericho Sims still has a major issue with illegal screens.

I hate to say it, but the Heat's GTA-themed court looked stunning (see below). This was a one-night thing; we won't see it ever again.

Unlike last season, when they were among the top teams in shooting percentage, the Bucks this season cannot seem to find a rhythm, and it is frustrating. Shot 15/43 in the first half last night and rank 29th on the season (42.9%).

Giannis did not shake hands with any members of the Bucks at the end of the game. A few nights before, he said this was "just another game."

The Bucks are back in action tonight as they take on the Portland Trail Blazers at home. Watch at 7:00 p.m. CST on MY24.
