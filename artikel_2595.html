As Vikings flash complementary football 'for one of the first times' in Kirk Cousins' return, Fernando Mendoza sums up Raiders debut: 'It's good to get punched in the face'

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Facing third-and-23 in the first quarter, Kirk Cousins dropped back.

His offensive line was slowing the Packers' rushers threatening from his left.

Second-year undrafted free-agent receiver Myles Price was crossing the field from his right.

So Cousins wound up and hit Price on a crosser, Price bouncing off Packers cornerback Brandon Cisse and racing the 14 remaining yards to the end zone.

The 38-yard touchdown marked the first touchdown reception of Price's career and the first in Cousins' return to the Vikings after a Tuesday trade from the Las Vegas Raiders made him the first quarterback to start for two different teams in back-to-back weeks, per Fox.

The timing that play required? The poise in the pocket just four days after Cousins had arrived at the Vikings' practice facility wondering what code he needed to input to enter the building?

"That's probably the [touchdown] that you're thinking, 'Is there enough time on task … ?'" head coach Kevin O'Connell said after the Vikings beat the Packers, 30-10. "We're kind of in a four-by-one look right there. They're dropping somebody in. Myles runs right into the hook area kind of behind there and you've got to get that ball thrown with pretty elite timing and you're really throwing it into a spot with some trust that Myles is going to go get it because inevitably the corner may fall off.

Improving to 7-2, the Vikings showed their first glimpse of what a high-ceiling offense might look like beside one of the best defenses in the league. Brian Flores' unit was the star of the show yet again, sacking Jordan Love eight times and securing four takeaways. The Vikings contained the Packers to 10 points and 220 yards just one week after Green Bay embarrassed the Patriots to the tune of 45 points and 478 yards.

Add in 312 yards and 30 points on offense after the Vikings averaged 266.8 and 18.3 before Cousins' arrival?

"Obviously there's a lot to build off of from this," O'Connell said. But "for one of the first times this year, I thought we played complementary football."

Expect the ceiling only to rise as Cousins acclimates to his reunion with a team that deemed the remainder of his season worth a third-round pick and a $10 million salary hit.

And the Raiders team that parted with Cousins?

Rookie first overall draft pick Fernando Mendoza started off strongly before the No. 1-ranked Seahawks defense stifled the Raiders in the second half of a 21-13 loss. Mendoza spoke to the opportunity the unraveling could provide.

"It's good to get punched in the face, because now it's a great opportunity to grow and learn and to really unveil all the deficiencies that I have in my game and as a future hopeful leader of this offense …" Mendoza said. "There's a lot of things I need to improve on in that field, and I'm excited to watch the film and get back to work."

Fernando Mendoza felt the Seahawks' heat in his first NFL start this season.

When former Dallas Cowboys head coach Jason Garrett suggested a recipe to onboard Mendoza last week, it seemed ideal but unrealistic.

"Quiet the game down for him, try to get the running game going early, high-percentage throws early, move the quarterback," Garrett said Wednesday on an NBC conference call. "Try to get him to be 5-for-5 [passing] before he even thinks about it to get into the flow of the game."

As an aspiration, that sounded great. But was a Raiders team that ranked among the league's worst in both rushing and defense really going to be able to support a new quarterback that way? How much could the passing game withstand the loss of Cousins, who ranked fifth in passing yards and 11th in passer rating when he left for Minnesota?

Credit Mendoza and Raiders head coach Klint Kubiak's early game plan: Mendoza not only started 5-for-5 against Seattle; he completed seven straight passes before his first incompletion. Las Vegas' early offense was balanced and steady, Mendoza operating from under center and running bootlegs off play action.

During the first half, Las Vegas kept Seattle's defense honest: Mendoza scrambled for a yard on third-and-1 to keep his first drive alive and later for 8 yards on third-and-5 near first quarter's end. And as if in ode to his fourth-and-4 national championship highlight, Mendoza tucked and ran for a 10-yard touchdown to give the Raiders an early 7-0 lead.

"Got to give the Raiders a lot of credit in the first half," Seahawks head coach Mike Macdonald said afterward. "I thought they had a good plan, and they made it challenging for us in the first half. But our guys did a great job adjusting and sticking together and finding a way to win in the end."

The Seahawks' adjustments, paired with the loss of two-time Pro Bowl tight end Brock Bowers to an ankle injury, derailed a promising start.

With the help of defensive-back blitzes that Macdonald said resulted from in-game looks, the Seahawks sacked Mendoza four times in the second half after just two in the first. Seattle's pressure disrupted the timing of Mendoza's passing game and shuttered the Raiders' run game: After converting 7-of-10 third-down attempts in the first half, Las Vegas went 0-for-7 in the second half. The Raiders followed up a 62-yard rushing performance in the first half with just 3 net yards in the second. Ten first downs in the first half preceded just two across quarters three and four.

And after Mendoza opened the game 11-of-13 for 90 yards passing, in addition to notching the rushing touchdown, before halftime — he managed to complete just 6-of-16 attempts for 53 yards and no touchdowns in the second half.

"It just felt like such a weird game," Raiders tight end Michael Mayer said. "I don't really know how to describe it. Felt like we started out pretty good, and then I really don't know what happened after that."

Kubiak and Mendoza each said they hold themselves to higher standards than they reached in the loss.

"There was a lot of sacks there," Mendoza said. "We should have had half as many if not even less than that. So I need to mature as a quarterback, throw the ball away, throw more accurate balls on third down and making sure we can move the chains to give our defense time to recuperate on the sideline and to really get it going in the second half."

The Vikings and Raiders' playoff hopes are trending in different directions.

Minnesota's chances of advancing to the postseason improved to 90% with its Sunday win, per Next Gen Stats, even as it sits in second place in a crowded NFC North. The Raiders' chances fell to 10% after their loss, their 4-6 record paired with their remaining schedule projecting worse chances than also-currently-out-of-it AFC contenders in the New England Patriots, Baltimore Ravens and Houston Texans.

A similarity between the two teams whose fates will be tied this season by their shared Cousins custody: the expectation of evolution as the offensive cast settles into its roles.

In Minnesota, Cousins reunited with a handful of teammates including receiver Justin Jefferson, whom he said "felt like old times watching Justin do what he just did." But plenty of teammates, and parts of the Vikings' playbook like the run concepts assistant head coach Frank Smith implemented this season, remain new. Cousins is no stranger to new play-callers: "I've changed coordinators 11 times," he said after the game. But there were times during his quick turnaround week when Cousins said he told O'Connell and Co., "You know what, I would be more comfortable doing this."

"You can't just roll the ball out there and ask the quarterback to have all the answers while they're still digesting their in-flight meal from when they came here," O'Connell said.

With just two full-speed practices after Cousins' arrival, O'Connell said he did not think Cousins took a full-speed rep of the double-pump 20-yard touchdown on which he hit Gavin Bartholomew in the fourth quarter.

"I think it was just either a walkthrough or maybe a jog or something, but it looked the way it's supposed to look…" O'Connell said. "Kirk just couldn't have executed better."

Kubiak, meanwhile, was kicking himself for some decisions he implemented with Mendoza. Those rollouts that worked for Mendoza in the first half? "Should have done more of that for sure," Kubiak said of shifting the plan.

The Raiders' resilience to losing Bowers, whose 676 yards and six touchdowns receiving both lead Las Vegas?

"I thought overall when we lost Brock we definitely saw a big change in production," Kubiak said. "So I look at myself. I have to do a much better job of moving the ball and spreading the ball around. …

"Overall, we've got to play better. We've got to support him better."

With just one game down, and a schedule that will lighten in December, the Raiders will have more chances. Perhaps Cousins' summation of his day applies to both teams — as well as to how NFL history will ultimately remember this rare in-season trade of a starting quarterback.

"There's just so much football ahead," Cousins said, "and this story is going to get written much later than now."
