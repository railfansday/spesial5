J.J. McCarthy knows he can be better despite being more than enough for Giants so far

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

J.J. McCarthy sailed passes long and wide. He missed open receivers by several yards. He struggled with pressure, bungling multiple opportunities to put away the Jaguars, who dared the 23-year-old to burn their Cover Zero defense.

When Jacksonville head coach Liam Coen curiously opted to kick a 26-yard field goal to cut its deficit to five with less than four minutes remaining, he was banking on the Giants being unable to pick up a first down for the fifth straight drive.

"As an offense, we took that personal, for sure," McCarthy said after the 23-18 win. "We can't get into that position. We can't give them that confidence. … The next time we get in those situations where we're up, we have to finish the job.

"As an offense, we're going to do everything we can to make sure we close that one out a little bit earlier. Starting with myself, there's a lot of throws I want to get back."

McCarthy went 0-for-9 on throws of at least 20 yards. He had a stretch of nine straight incompletions, as the Giants managed only three second-half points.

But at the end of his most erratic game with the Giants, McCarthy (18-of-38, 173 passing yards, with one touchdown, no interceptions) stood in the pocket with a pass-rusher bearing down on him and delivered a 19-yard pass to Malachi Fields between multiple defenders, helping seal the team's biggest win of the season.

Fans greet Giants quarterback J.J. McCarthy (18) as he walks off the field after a game at MetLife Stadium on Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026. Corey Sipkin for NY Post

"He threaded that needle right past that linebacker. That was a big-time throw," head coach John Harbaugh said. "That's a confident guy, and he's been in a lot of huge games. And we're going to start hitting some of those deep passes. We wanted to take some shots. We wanted to go after them downfield, and we did, and those are going to start happening. We'll start making those plays for sure."

Despite McCarthy's uneven effort, the Giants (6-3-1) improved to 3-1 with the third-year quarterback as the starter.

New York Giants quarterback J.J. McCarthy (18) throws a pass during the first half against an opponent in Brooklyn, N.Y., Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026. Corey Sipkin for NY Post

Since debuting with 243 passing yards, one touchdown and one interception in the fourth quarter of the loss to the Saints. McCarthy has averaged 211.2 passing yards and 22.5 rushing yards in four starts, with two touchdowns, one interception and a 60.3 completion percentage.

McCarthy may never resemble the top 10 draft pick the Vikings expected to become the face of their franchise, but the former Michigan star — who was acquired two months ago for a 2027 fifth-round pick — has kept the Giants afloat without Jaxson Dart by riding a dominant defense and largely avoiding devastating mistakes — a formula the Wolverines used to win a national championship with McCarthy in 2023.

"J.J. [came] in mid-season, but you just can tell by his leadership how much he wants to win, how much he's a competitor and how many people that he brings around with him," said Malik Nabers, who caught his first touchdown from McCarthy on Sunday.

In the first half, McCarthy led the Giants to 20 points against the league's No. 2 scoring defense. The quarterback, who was the team's leading rusher before going into victory formation, put the Giants in position to blow the game open late in the third quarter — leading by 14 and driving in Jacksonville territory — before Cam Skattebo fumbled and Jaguars safety Antonio Johnson ran the other way with the ball.

McCarthy then made perhaps the day's most important play, sprinting across the field to bring down Johnson, preventing a defensive touchdown that could have swung the game.

"In a weird way, I look forward to those moments to put some contact out there and make a tackle," McCarthy said. "At the end of the day, you have to do your job as a quarterback. When everything goes wrong, try to find a way to make it right."
