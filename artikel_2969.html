Eagles locker room rallying around rocket-armed QB Joe Milton

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Eagles locker room rallying around rocket-armed QB Joe Milton  originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

If you happened to be in South Philadelphia on Wednesday afternoon and have supersonic hearing, you might have already known the Eagles have a new starting quarterback.

"He zips it. He zips it," receiver Darius Cooper said shaking his head. "You can hear it flying in the air."

The rest of the world found out the Eagles made the decision to bench Tanner McKee and go with Milton from ESPN less than 2 hours after head coach Nick Sirianni failed mention a thing during his Wednesday press conference. And neither Milton nor McKee were available to reporters after Wednesday's practice.

But despite some janky attempts at obfuscation from a couple of starting offensive linemen on Wednesday, the team knows who is starting against the Giants on Sunday afternoon. It's Milton.

Dallas Goedert said he and his teammates found out the news in a meeting minutes before the public heard.

"We were kind of hoping that it would stay under wraps," Goedert said. "It would be a competitive advantage and they wouldn't find out until Saturday. But it didn't take long for people to know."

Milton, 26, was as sixth-round pick out of Tennessee back in 2024 and has played in games with the Patriots and Cowboys, but this will be his first start. The Eagles just signed him off the Cowboys' practice squad on Oct. 16. It took him a week to get bumped up to the backup job and three weeks to become the starter.

After Jalen Hurts was lost for the season with blood clots in his lungs, the Eagles gave McKee three starts and the offense has been struggling. So now they're turning to Milton and hoping he provides a spark. We'll see if Milton can do that on Sunday, but he did seem to inject some juice into the locker room to kick off this week.

"He's a real good athlete. That stands out," Goedert said. "He's got a cannon of an arm. Today it seemed like he was really accurate, got through his reads good, made check downs when he needed. But it'll be exciting for him to go out there. I know he's excited to get on the field and play. Going to do whatever I can to help him succeed."

Milton is 6-foot-5, 236 pounds and he is elusive in the pocket. That elusiveness will help an offense that has been giving up too many pressures, especially against the blitz, all season.

But the thing everyone always brings up about Milton is his rocket of a right arm. That was no different after Wednesday's practice.

"He can spin it," tight end Eli Stowers said. "You feel it when he throws it to you and it hits your hands pretty quick. When he lets it go down the field, it's pretty good."

"It's kind of perfect for a pass catcher," Goedert said. "The balls that are in the air for a long time when you can think, those ones are the scariest ones. The ones that get on you and you just have to catch it, those are the easy ones. It seems like he's been putting the ball in a good spot and I'm excited to see what he does Sunday."

Goedert on Wednesday was surprised to find out that Sunday's game will be Milton's first-career NFL start. Goedert said he thought he read that Milton had already started six or seven games; that's not the case. Milton has been in the NFL for two seasons and he's played in five games and has thrown 53 passes — but he'll make his first start in a few days.

Despite that inexperience, Milton apparently doesn't lack for confidence. Goedert didn't even seem to question the thought that Milton had started games before.

"He's got a lot of swag to him. He's confident," veteran receiver Hollywood Brown said. "He's excited for this opportunity as well. That was his whole reason to come here, to get a chance like this. He's excited for it.

"It's a testament to the journey he's been on. He's probably been that confident his whole life. So for him, it seems natural. For all of us, we're rallying around it, trying to get a W."

Even though Milton has shown confidence, the Eagles are going to try to help him as much as possible on Sunday. Cooper said he wants to help give Milton tips on where their motions are coming from, Goedert said he wants to be a reliable outlet, Brown said he wants to be able to create after the catch. The Eagles are likely without DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks again, so any little thing will help.

Milton didn't sign with the Eagles until Oct. 16, but he immediately fit in. Stowers said that Milton has been "a light in the locker room" since his arrival and praised the quarterback for making an attempt to get to know all of his new teammates on a personal level. That goes a long way, especially now that Milton will lead them into a game on Sunday.

"I'm excited for his opportunity," Cooper said.
