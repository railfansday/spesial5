If Stocks Keep Sliding, This Is the 1 ETF I'm Continuing to Buy

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Nasdaq and S&P 500 have entered correction territory.

By Dave Kovaleski – Nov 23, 2026 at 10:15AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Ea Series Trust - MarketDesk Focused U.s. Momentum ETF ›

Since reaching an all-time closing high of 27,599 on Oct. 6, the Nasdaq Composite index has dropped about 17% to roughly 22,914 as of Nov. 20. The S&P 500 has also plummeted since it hit an all-time high of 7,818 on Oct. 6. The large-cap benchmark has shed about 11% since then, falling to about 6,982.

It is already a correction for both indexes, and they could be on their way to a bear market, which is a 20% drop from recent highs.

If stocks keep sliding into bear market territory, I'll continue to buy one exchange-traded fund (ETF) that has outperformed the major indexes and should continue to do so in a bear market: the MarketDesk Focused U.S. Momentum ETF (FMTM +2.19%).

The MarketDesk Focused U.S. Momentum ETF has dropped about 11% since Oct. 6, but for the year, it is up about 10.5%, trading at around $35.50 per share as of Nov. 20. That is far better than ETFs that track the Nasdaq or the S&P 500.

The Invesco QQQ (QQQ +7.16%), which follows the Nasdaq 100, is down 1% year to date, while the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO +4.57%) is up 1% this year.

The outperformance is greater over the trailing 12 months. The MarketDesk Focused U.S. Momentum ETF is up nearly 16% over the past year, while the VOO has gained 4% and the QQQ is up 2%, as the chart above indicates.

What I like about this ETF is that, while it is a momentum fund, it starts with quality and liquidity screens to make sure stocks are stable growers. In a bear market scenario, this is critically important.

The all-cap ETF, which includes stocks with at least $1 billion in market value, screens stocks for profitability, looking at net income, return on assets, and cash flows; operating efficiency, examining gross margins among other metrics; and balance sheet quality, scanning debt levels, current ratio, and other factors.

Then, from this universe, it looks for stocks with momentum, that is, stable price appreciation over the previous six months. The stocks are then ranked by consistency and the quality of price appreciation. In addition, the ETF is equal weighted, which provides additional stability during down markets.

Finally, it is rebalanced monthly, so it is constantly seeking the most stable growers and weeding out those that may be prone to volatility.

It only holds about 32 stocks, so it is concentrated, but it is also well-diversified. The three largest holdings currently are Tradeweb Markets, Reinsurance Group of America, and Okta.

When markets get volatile and negative, this ETF is built to not only find the stocks that are outperforming but also seeks to identify the growers that can best navigate choppy markets.
