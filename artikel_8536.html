Cap Each Stock at 5% of Your Portfolio – Trim Winners Once a Year

Keep any single stock to about 5% of your whole portfolio, every account included. Check once a year and trim any winner that has grown past that cap, since a rising price pushes a stock over it without another purchase. Then if one company collapses, you lose about 5% of your portfolio, not years of savings.

Say you have $200,000 invested and put $10,000, the full 5%, into one company. Then it triples while the rest of your portfolio stays flat.

Your stake is now $30,000 of your $220,000, about 14%. A collapse would cost you $30,000 instead of the $10,000 you first put at risk.

The cap doesn’t apply to broad index funds, since one fund spreads your money across hundreds of companies. If you also own shares of one of the biggest companies in your fund, though, count the part of your fund invested in that company toward the cap, along with your own shares.

Figure each stock’s share of everything you have invested, your IRA and 401(k) included, when you buy it and once a year after that. At each yearly check, sell enough of any stock over 5% to bring it back to 5%, and put the proceeds into your index funds.

Sell where the tax is lowest. If you hold the stock in an IRA, sell those shares first, since selling inside an IRA triggers no tax at the time. In a regular brokerage account, sell a losing investment in the same year, and its loss offsets your gain, as long as you don’t buy more of it within 30 days on either side of the sale.

Set a lower cap of 1% or 2% for speculative stocks, such as companies that don’t make a profit yet. If you won’t read a company’s quarterly results, skip single stocks and keep your money in index funds. Your employer’s stock is a separate case, since your paycheck depends on the same company.

Run your first check this week, and start with the stock that has gained the most since you bought it.
