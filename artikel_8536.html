Fund pages, brokerage order screens, and 401(k) menus all assume you already know the vocabulary. Learn about 20 core investing terms, from expense ratio to limit order, before you open an account or pick funds, so you know what you’re buying and what it will cost you.

Two funds can hold the same 500 stocks and still charge you different fees. On a $10,000 balance, a fund with a 0.05% expense ratio costs $5 a year, while one at 0.75% costs $75.

Neither fund will ever send you a bill. The fee comes out of the fund’s own assets, so the only sign of it is a balance that grows a little more slowly.

Many of the terms below name a choice that costs or saves you money, like picking between those two funds. The order type you choose sets the price you pay, and how long you hold an investment before selling can change the tax rate on your gain.

Between two funds that track the same index, the one with the lower expense ratio leaves you more of the same return, so check that number first. For any word on a fund page or order screen that isn’t on this list, the SEC’s free glossary at Investor.gov has a plain-English definition.
