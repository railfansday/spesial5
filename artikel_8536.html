Get AT&T Fiber if it reaches your address, since every plan has uploads as fast as its downloads, no annual contract, and no data cap. Its new-customer discount of $15 to $25 a month ends after a year, so compare its price after the discount with the cable plan at your address before you sign up.

AT&T Fiber is AT&T’s fiber-optic home internet, sold where its network reaches in parts of 32 states. AT&T lists 300 Mbps (megabits per second) at $35 a month plus taxes for the first year with AutoPay and paperless billing, and at today’s prices it costs $50 after that, though prices can vary by address.

Households AT&T Fiber reaches should take it over a cable plan with the same download speed and price once both promotions end, since its uploads match its downloads. Light users and anyone whose address gets only AT&T Internet Air, its 5G home internet with typical downloads of 90 to 300 Mbps, should price the cable plans at their address first.
