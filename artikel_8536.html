(Shrug) They are two good actively managed balanced funds. Sometimes Wellington has done better, sometimes Dodge & Cox. I don't think it's meaningful to try to judge which is "better." Morningstar gives Wellington a "gold" analyst rating and Dodge & Cox Balanced only a "bronze," for what that's worth.*

Morningstar classifies Wellington's stock exposure as "large blend," Dodge & Cox Balanced as "large value." But (shrug) you can interpret that to mean that Wellington is guilty of style drift--or that the managers have been engaged in factor timing, and since it's actively managed, why not?

According to Bogle, the Wellington Fund experienced a "rise," (1929-1966), a "fall" (1967-1978), and a "resurrection" (1979-date).
