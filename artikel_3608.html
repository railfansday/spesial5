Bob Dylan Meets Pope Leo in a Black Hoodie and Polka-Dot Tie

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Yes, I was there. Not at his meeting with the pope, of course, but at the controversial Bob Dylan concert held at the Unipol Dome in Milan.

The legendary singer-songwriter has a discography that stands the test of time, but his recent decision to perform in near darkness hasn’t exactly been celebrated. With a black hood pulled up that almost completely conceals his face and only a few dim lights illuminating the stage, the impact of seeing one of the greatest artists of the 20th century was greatly diminished. From the stands, I couldn’t even zoom in on my phone because it was sealed in a bag at the entrance.

Eventually, I accepted it. Dylan intentionally created this experience, expressing his desire to distance himself from the audience. Far from a technology-forward performance, the Nobel Prize laureate, whose face has become a central figure in modern history, sang nonstop from under a hood for more than an hour and a half.

Dylan has worn the hood for at least the last two years, likely because he wants the audience to focus on his music rather than on him as a public figure. But does it have the same effect when meeting with the pope, especially when he isn’t performing?

The singer was received by Pope Leo XIV at the Apostolic Palace, dressed as if it were a stop on his tour: black sweatshirt, hood up, black gloves, black pants, and a loosely tied polka-dot tie. The ensemble was completed by an umbrella—also black—being used as a cane.

Andrea Monda, editor in chief of the Vatican newspaper L’Osservatore Romano, and Reverend Antonio Spadaro, an official from the Vatican’s Ministry of Culture, which sponsored this meeting, as it had done in July with Patti Smith, escorted Dylan. The two Americans came together in a tableau that is destined to become iconic.

The contrast bordered on the surreal: The Pope's characteristic white robes and Dylan's reclusive black hood. As always, the memes were quick to follow. Some compared Dylan to Palpatine from Star Wars, while others saw a parallel to the final encounter in the game Assassin’s Creed II.

It’s amusing to see them visually contrasted, because their personalities overlap in many ways (and not just because of their American origins). Pope Leo wrote the foreword to a book on disarmament, quoting a 1963 protest song by Dylan titled “Go Away You Bomb.” As for Dylan, he has embarked on a spiritual journey over the years that has brought him closer to the Christian faith.

Pope Leo XIV meets Bob Dylan on November 4, 2026, in Vatican City, Vatican.

“I’m a religious person,” he said in an interview with the Wall Street Journal—“I read the scriptures a lot, meditate and pray, light candles in church. I believe in damnation and salvation, as well as predestination. The Five Books of Moses, Pauline Epistles, Invocation of the Saints, all of it.”

So, is wearing a hood in the presence of the Pope a provocation? Probably not: If anything, it’s consistent with how he has made his recent public appearances.

The brief, 10-minute meeting was private, during which the two exchanged gifts. The Pope gave Dylan a rosary. The musician reciprocated with a signed copy of Chronicles–Volume One, Dylan’s autobiography. The hood stayed on throughout the meeting, turning a gesture of artistic consistency into something that, to outsiders, might look like a defensive maneuver.

Perhaps this is the intended paradox: Dylan has spent his career evading labels, yet precisely when he tries to hide, his goes viral. In many ways, it’s an act of loyalty to himself. At 85, with a career that has left an indelible mark on music and politics, he continues to defy conventions. At least he took off his gloves to shake the Pope’s hand.
