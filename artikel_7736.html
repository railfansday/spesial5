The Best Paperback Releases of the Month: November 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Here are 12 of the best novels to come out in paperback this month, as selected by the CrimeReads editors.

Con Lehane, The Red Scare Murders (Soho Crime)

“Con Lehane spins his tale using the tried and true masculine vernacular of the hard-boiled genre. He’s convincing because he knows New York, and he knows the history of the labor movement. We don’t have many other books in this vein.” –Northwest Labor Press

Alissa Lee, With Friends Like These (Atria)

“An immersive novel that delivers suspense with style and intelligence.” –Kirkus Reviews

Nalini Singh, Such a Perfect Family (Berkley)

“Even genre veterans are likely to be fooled by this devilish puzzler.”–Publishers Weekly

Hazell Ward, The Game Is Murder (Berkley)

“There are puzzles for the reader to solve, frequent authorial intrusions, dozens of sly, knowledgeable references to detective fiction classics and unexpected twists galore…” –Washington Post

Kate Belli, The Gallery Assistant  (Atria)

“A haunting, post-9/11 psychological thriller that combines the glittering facade of New York’s art world with the messy, painful aftermath of trauma.” –The Seattle Times

Stuart Neville, Blood Like Ours (Hell’s Hundred)

“A deadly, bloody story that will keep you reading until the end. It’s got early Stephen King vibes and we’re all for it.”–Belfast Telegraph

Phillip Margolin, False Witness (Minotaur)

“Margolin once again has proven his expertise when it comes to the law and the courtroom. He has penned yet another legal thriller that satisfies on many levels.” —Bookreporter

Emma Stonex, The Sunshine Man (Penguin Books)

“A raw, gritty psychological thriller that will leave readers breathless.” –Booklist

Orlando Murrin, May Contain Murder (A John Scognamiglio Book)

“A wickedly delicious mystery spiced with unexpected secrets and intrigue.” –Kirkus Reviews

CJ Farley, Who Knows You By Heart (William Morrow)

“An era-defining novel…a very entertaining and ambitious mash-up of genres: thriller, science fiction and romance.”–New York Times Book Review

Viola Van de Sandt, The Dinner Party (Little, Brown)

“A powerful, provocative study of female rage unleashed. Franca’s intoxicating tale is often darkly funny despite the subject matter.” –The Scotsman

Celeste Connally, Revenge, Served Royal (Minotaur)

“Bridgerton meets The Great British Baking Show. Lots of plot twists, and lots of fun.” –Kirkus Reviews
