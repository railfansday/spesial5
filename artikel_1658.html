Tottenham: Roberto De Zerbi confirms new Dejan Kulusevski plan ahead of Sunderland trip

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Your weekly briefing on Tottenham, featuring team news and expert analysis from Sam Tabuteau

Roberto De Zerbi has revealed that Dejan Kulusevski is closing in on his first Tottenham start in more than a year and a half.

Kulusevski made an emotional return to fitness last month, coming off the bench for his first appearance in 526 days following a serious patella injury as Spurs beat Coventry 3-1 at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium.

The Swedish playmaker has since seen his minutes managed, with cameo appearances against Chelsea, Crystal Palace and Leeds before the international break.

Kulusevski was subsequently called up to Sweden’s squad for their Nations League fixtures against Bosnia and Poland earlier this month, playing a combined 54 minutes for Graham Potter’s side.

Spurs remain cautious about risking Kulusevski. But the 26-year-old’s performances over the international break have now convinced De Zerbi that he is nearly ready to start for the first time since Spurs’ 2-0 defeat to Crystal Palace on May 11, 2025.

“Listen, I am thinking to start maybe not now, but I think we are very close to start the game. He is working well. I watched his games in the national team just to understand the physical condition,” De Zerbi said in his pre-Sunderland press conference on Friday afternoon.

“He played the first game as a winger and the second game as a No.10 and especially in the second game he played very well, but we have to be good to understand the right moment to put him on the pitch. I think medical staff, physical staff, they are working very well with him and we can’t lose any time, but we can’t take any risk because he is a special player for us.”

Spurs have paid extra attention this season to managing the minutes of players with a history of injuries, with Dominic Solanke, Destiny Udogie and James Maddison all having faced spells out of the side in a bid to minimise the risk of them suffering long-term issues.

Udogie returned to the starting lineup for the first time since October’s 1-1 draw with Manchester United as Spurs beat Ipswich 2-0 last weekend.

Solanke, meanwhile, made just his sixth league start of the season, getting on the scoresheet in the second-half to seal the three points.

Asked about the pair, De Zerbi admitted that while they were making progress, Udogie would need to remain under close supervision from the club’s medical staff over the next three to four weeks.

“They’re working well also this week, both,” De Zerbi continued.

“Dominic Solanke, I think, played very well in the last game against Ipswich, and also Udogie.

“I think we have to manage Udogie better than Solanke because Udogie needs to stay with the medical staff, focusing on him for another three, four weeks, and then if he goes over this four weeks, I think for him it’s not a problem.”
