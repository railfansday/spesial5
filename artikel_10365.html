The problem with strong passwords isn’t understanding why you need them; it’s keeping track of a different one for every account.

Proton Pass does that work for you. When you sign up for a website, its password generator can create a strong password that’s completely unique. Save it in your vault, and Proton Pass can fill in your credentials when you return.

That’s useful whether you’re creating an account for a single purchase or signing into a service you use every day.

Unique passwords also help limit the fallout from a data breach. If a retailer exposes your login credentials, using a different password elsewhere means those same credentials won’t unlock your other accounts.

With unlimited logins and encrypted vaults that sync across devices, Proton Pass lets you keep those details together as you move between your phone and computer.

Proton Pass is available for practically every platform, from Chrome and Firefox on the web to native macOS, Windows, and Linux apps to, of course, iOS and Android. This makes it trivially easy to access your passwords, passkeys, and API credentials wherever you find yourself.

Black Friday shopping often leaves something behind long after the packages arrive: a steady stream of promotional emails from stores you barely remember visiting.

Proton Pass includes unlimited hide-my-email aliases, giving you an alternative to handing out your real address every time you create an account.

An alias is an extra email address that forwards messages to your normal inbox. You can give it to a shopping website and still receive your order confirmations and delivery updates, without sharing the underlying address you use for everything else.

If that alias starts receiving spam, you can switch it off. Your real email address stays the same, and you don’t have to update all your other accounts.

It’s a practical way to put some distance between your primary inbox and the websites you’re trying for the first time. A separate alias for each service also gives you more control: you can stop messages arriving through one address without disrupting the others.
