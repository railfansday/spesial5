New York City Council is expected to ban stores from using personal data to set prices

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The bill would let the city fine retailers up to $1,000 per violation and take effect one year after enactment

The New York City Council is expected to vote on Thursday on a bill that would forbid businesses from relying on algorithms and personal data to quote shoppers varying prices for identical goods, according to The New York Times.

Council Speaker Julie Menin, the bill’s sponsor, said the votes needed to pass it are comfortably secured. The Council’s Consumer and Worker Protection Committee has its vote set for 11 a.m. ET, with the entire Council convening later in the day, according to amNewYork.

"When corporations use algorithms and personal data to charge different people different prices for the same products, it's unethical and worsens our affordability crisis," Menin said in a statement, according to The New York Times. Mayor Zohran Mamdani said in a statement that he supports the move. "New Yorkers' personal data should never be used as a weapon against their own pocketbooks," he said.

The bill, Introduction 891-A, would empower the Department of Consumer and Worker Protection to look into violations and fine offenders as much as $1,000 for every infraction, according to amNewYork. Every consumer or transaction involved could be tallied as its own violation. The bill would also bar companies from gathering, selling or passing along personal information for use in surveillance pricing.

Routine sales, coupons, rewards for loyal customers and breaks for specific categories of people, among them veterans and seniors, would stay legal, according to amNewYork. The bill carves out some activities already under government regulation, such as pricing for insurance and credit.

Qualifying small businesses would be told of an initial violation and given 30 days to demonstrate that they have fixed the problem, according to amNewYork. The bill was revised after a June 17 hearing at which retail, hotel and technology groups warned it could restrict rewards programs and discounts. The effective date moved from 120 days to one year after enactment. The bill does not include the private right of action that the department's commissioner, Sam Levine, had requested.

Josh Gold, a spokesman for Uber $UBER, said the ban would hurt small restaurants. Uber Eats lets restaurants send targeted discounts based on consumers' searches and abandoned shopping carts, and the city ban would not allow that practice, according to The New York Times. "If enacted, this law will make it harder for small restaurants with limited marketing budgets to reach customers, putting them at a disadvantage against national chains," Gold said.

The state Legislature passed a similar ban, the One Fair Price Act, in June. It remained unsigned by Gov. Kathy Hochul as of Wednesday, according to amNewYork. Connecticut, Maryland, New Jersey and Seattle have approved similar measures, according to The New York Times.

Seattle's City Council approved a policy in September barring large grocery retailers from using artificial intelligence and personal data to set prices for individual shoppers. That policy still allows openly advertised loyalty discounts for broad groups, such as seniors or veterans, and standard coupons.

If the Council passes the New York bill and Mamdani signs it, the ban would go into force a year after it is enacted, according to amNewYork.
