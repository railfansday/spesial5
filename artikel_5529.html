Trump fired White House cyber director, tapped Pentagon official as replacement

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Kirsten Davies, the Pentagon's chief information officer, will serve as acting national cyber director after Sean Cairncross's exit

US President Donald Trump speaks in the Oval Office of the White House in Washington, DC on July 14, 2025. (Andrew Caballero-Reynolds/AFP via Getty Images)

President Donald Trump is replacing White House national cyber director Sean Cairncross with Kirsten Davies, the Pentagon's chief information officer, who will take over in an acting capacity, according to CNN. Administration officials confirmed the change on social media on Thursday, and a White House official told CNN that Cairncross is leaving the administration.

Cairncross was fired, according to CBS News. A source in the White House told CNN that Trump "fully supported firing Sean Cairncross from his position as he lacked the strong cyber credentials to perform the job."

White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller pushed for the change, according to Axios. Two sources described the departure as amicable.

Cairncross said in a statement to CNN that he is "incredibly proud of our work to strengthen America's cybersecurity, protect our critical infrastructure, confront foreign threats, and position America to lead the world in Super Intelligence." He has held the post since August 2025, according to CBS News.

Davies thanked Trump, Miller and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth in a social media post on Thursday, according to CNN. “Our opportunity is to dominate the cyber landscape for generations to come ... but only if we modernize and drive resilience into the foundations our great nation runs on,” she wrote. Hegseth and Secretary of State Marco Rubio congratulated her.

The Office of the National Cyber Director is responsible for coordinating strategy and policy on cybersecurity throughout the federal government. A permanent director must receive a nomination from Trump and approval from the Senate, according to CNBC. The Senate will be run by Democrats starting in January, which leaves little time to put forward a nominee, according to CBS News.

Davies already won Senate approval to serve as chief information officer at the Defense Department, according to Axios. She advises Hegseth on information technology and cybersecurity. Prior to entering government, she held a position at Unilever, where she oversaw cyber resilience efforts worldwide.

The change follows a run of U.S. technology outages that the administration has attributed to patching and system upgrades. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton and Rubio, who is also national security adviser, said the administration sees no indication of a coordinated cyberattack or state-sponsored activity behind the incidents.

The outages include a halt to flights across the country, a worldwide Microsoft $MSFT Outlook failure and, reportedly, a blackout of the classified communications network used by the National Security Agency, according to CNBC. Democrats have objected to how the administration has handled the situation, according to CNN.
