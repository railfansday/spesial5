Republican senator opens door on possibility of a presidential bid, in shift from past position

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Sen. Josh Hawley, R-Mo., warns that the House’s failed attempt to impeach War Secretary Pete Hegseth is just the beginning on ‘Jesse Watters Primetime.’

Following the GOP’s widespread midterm losses, Sen. Josh Hawley, R-Mo. — who in September indicated to the Washington Examiner that he had "no intention" of running for president in 2028 — is now leaving the door open to a White House bid.

"After this most recent election, I think everybody that cares about the future of the party, the future of the country, has a lot of thinking to do," the senator said to The Associated Press when asked about his presidential aspirations. "So I’ll just say I have a lot of thinking to do."

Democrats delivered a midterm election drubbing and will control the House and Senate majorities next year.

"I was alarmed by the results of the election, and I’m concerned about the direction of both my party and the country," Hawley told NBC News when asked whether he is thinking about running for president. "And I want to do my part there. I’m trying to think through what my part is. But I certainly want to do my part for the future."

"Listen, I don’t want to give you a misconception that, for sure, I have plans or intentions," he noted, according to the outlet. "What I said to you is the honest to goodness truth, which is just that I’ve got four years here to serve in the Senate, and I’m just trying to think about where I am and what’s in front of me, what can I do to help the country go forward."

"I'm concerned about what comes next for the party. So I don’t know what my role is in that yet, but I’m trying to give it some serious thought, and I’m no further along than that," he said.

Hawley's comments mark a notable reversal, as he previously shot down the idea of pursuing the presidency in 2028, telling the Washington Examiner in September, "No, I have no intention of doing so, period."

Hawley, who has served in the Senate since early 2019, won reelection in 2024, meaning his current term will not end until early 2031.

Hawley's campaign did not immediately respond to Fox News Digital's request for comment on Friday.
