Crusader Kings publishers Paradox announce CEO swap, say they're "in a strong position at a time when much of the industry is facing major challenges"

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

"My plan was never to be CEO for life", says man clearly not embracing the grand strategy despot spirit

Paradox Interactive, publishers of umpteen strategy genre stalwarts including Crusader Kings, Europa Universalis, and Hearts of Iron, have announced plans to switch overlords early next year. Fredrik Wester will make way for Mattias Lilja, as the deputy CEO takes the throne.

While the move's been announced this week, Lilja will properly become boss from January 4th, making this a case of getting a new figurehead in place as the calendars swap. Departing CEO Fredrik Wester isn't going far, at least for now, as he'll "continue focusing on the company's long-term development as a member of the board".

"My plan was never to be CEO for life," Wester said. "The goal was to build the foundation before passing the baton on for the next chapter in the company’s journey. Since I returned as CEO, we’ve streamlined our strategy, rebuilt our pipeline and developed our commercial offering. Mattias has had a central role in laying this foundation, and now is the right time to hand over."

"The Board wants to thank Fredrik for his visionary leadership and outstanding contributions over these years," stated chairman Håkan Sjunnesson. "As a result, Paradox is in a strong position at a time when much of the industry is facing major challenges." He added that Lilja is "more than ready to assume the role and lead Paradox towards new gaming experiences that last for decades".

Lilja himself declared that Paradox have "a unique DNA that has made us one of the greats in deep and engaging strategy and management games" and will be "stepping up the pace". "We will make further investments in our core business, push the boundaries of our game design, work even more closely with our loyal players, develop more and better tools and services that benefit both modders and players, and continue to sharpen our ways of working and our organisation, with player value in focus," he said.

As Wester alluded to, he previously served as Paradox CEO from 2009 to 2018, with a brief stint as executive chairman of the board sandwiched between that and his latest stint as CEO since 2021. Lilja has been deputy CEO since 2024 and chief of staff since 2021, having originally joined Paradox as a producer in 2009.

The past half decade has been a very mixed bag for Paradox. Their big strategy flagships keep ticking along, albeit with the question of whether their next DLC will feel worth it hovering overhead near-permanently. Outside of that, however, the likes of Cities: Skylines 2, Vampire: The Masquerade Bloodlines 2, and cancelled Sims competitor Life By You have made for a track record heavy on questionable decisions and games which either flopped hard or failed to blow away expectations. Wester acknowledged back in 2024 that the company had "made the wrong calls in several projects, especially outside of our core".

With the likes of post-apocalyptic strategy game Afterworld currently in the works, it'll be interesting to see if Lilja's tenure can see Paradox finally start to nail not just their nerdy bread and butter, but also games that don't involve staring down at a big map.
