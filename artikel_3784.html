How to find a Guild in WoW Forever

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

No Guild Finder (so far) isn't stopping WoW: Forever players from finding (or even making) the perfect Guild

Guilds return in World of Warcraft: Forever, and Blizzard wants to recapture the excitement of hopping into the game and dragging your guildmates to the occasional raid, a good leveling session, or the plain old shenanigans. While guilds are already a staple in most MMOs and other versions of WoW, it’s still refreshing to see Forever return with a classic iteration of a staple social experience.

But since Forever is indeed a remake of Classic, it also comes with the sheer difficulty of finding a Guild you could be a part of. There’s really no way to tell if a Guild is available for applications, or maybe if a Guild is strictly friends-only. Then again, there are creative ways where you can score a Guild membership for your character.

Historically, WoW players have always been able to find Guilds through the Guild Finder, launched back in Cataclysm, as well as the improved Guild & Communities Finder back in 2019. Forever being a remake of the OG WoW means going back to a time where none of these tools existed, so it’s back to “manual” searching for WoW: Forever players.

As of writing, there are no WoW: Forever add-ons specifically to help find Guilds in the game. Sites like CurseForge do have a plethora of add-ons that help with Guild management, but that’s after you make your Guild.

Cataclysm launched the original Guild Finder back in 2011, so players had around seven years of finding guilds with no built-in tool to help them. Therefore, WoW: Forever players can also resort to old-fashioned ways of finding prospective Guilds.

Even without the Guild Finder, WoW: Forever players have a plethora of social avenues to form communities and find like-minded players for their Guilds. Maybe you don’t want to be part of a Guild. Maybe you found some Guilds taking the game too seriously and you want something chill, or some Guilds might be “too” chill for your liking. That’s probably when you should consider making your own Guild.

If you want to create your own Guild, it has to be something certified by your Faction as 100% legit. This is quite unlike other MMOs where you can just make a Guild easy peasy, or just pay some currency to get the privilege. Instead, you need to get something called a Guild Charter.

To do this, head to your capital city. Ask a guard to help you locate your Guild Master. From here, you can ask them for a Guild Charter, and they’ll sell it to you for 10 Silver.

Be careful if you’re doing this purchase if you’re low-level, since that 10 Silver is already the current life savings of other characters. But once you’ve decided you really want to become a future guild founder, then proceed with the purchase and type in your Guild Name.

This won’t stop there, though. Before your Guild actually becomes legit, you need to find 10 Signatures first.

Unlike other MMOs, WoW: Forever takes a page from Classic’s rulebook and demands Signatures from other players to co-found your Guild. As with Classic, you also need 10 (!!!) Signatures in your Guild Charter. And if you’re not playing with friends who are ready to be your co-founders, it’s not easy to convince random people to just hop in and join someone’s Guild.

Once you find a player willing to help, you just right-click their name and ask them for their Signature. A prompt will appear on their end where they can sign your Guild Charter.

When you finally get your Signatures, you must rush back to your capital city and turn in the signed Guild Charter. Only then do you get your guild. But first things first, how exactly do you find Signatures?

After you create your Guild, you practically have access to a mini-Faction within the Forever experience. As the leader, you (and a select few you appoint) have access to some of the Guild’s core settings. You can adjust these at any time, but the basics you should pay attention to are the following:

You might recall the suggestion to go to Discord to find a guild to join, or maybe even find potential guildmates. That’s because you can actually connect your WoW: Forever account to your Discord account as well. Doing this is pretty simple, and it’s all done from your settings:

First, you need to connect your Discord to your Battle.net account. To do this, go to your Discord account and click “User Settings.”

Under “Connected Apps,” you will see “Add a new connection,” and you can click the Battle.net icon there.

It will open Battle.net, where you can click “Continue,” and it will send you back to Discord, where you can “Authorize” the connection.

After that, you will be redirected to your settings, where you can confirm the connection.

Back in your WoW: Forever session, go to your “Options” and find “Social” under “Gameplay” or via the search.

It will show the Enable Discord Functionality toggle. Connect so you can link your Discord to your specific WoW account.

Below that setting is an option to choose how your name is displayed in the chat connected to Discord. It could be your Discord Global Name or your Last Online Character Name.

This part is best done if you’re the owner of the Guild, so you can set this up from scratch. Over in the “Guilds & Communities” tab, you can go to your “Guild Control” and check out Discord Settings.

From there, you can choose which Discord Server you want to connect to your Guild. And after that, you can select which specific channel you want to add to your Forever Guild.

When you link the two, the Discord channel overrides the Guild Chat by default. But there’s an option to separate the Discord chat as a separate channel from the Guild Chat.

Now, all your messages in WoW: Forever in that specific chat can be seen live on Discord. Likewise, messages from Discord will be seen back in WoW: Forever, with a nifty Discord icon to denote that the message has been sent from Discord specifically but read in-game.

While it’s possible to just separate your Discord chat from your Guild Chat, it might be economical to fix things over on Discord so that you have a dedicated guild channel for in-game players. You can lock that channel as view-only to non-players, so only guild members can actually chat there.
