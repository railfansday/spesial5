Trump could avoid Dems' ‘extreme’ amnesty push by signing immigration reform before new Congress

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Sen. Ruben Gallego, D-Ariz., says he's willing to work with Rep. Maria Elvira Salazar, R-Fla., to pass her immigration reform agenda in the U.S. Senate.

House Republicans are optimistic that President Donald Trump could sign a bipartisan immigration reform bill before Democrats take control of Congress.

They warn that if the president waits until January it could result in legislation containing more "extreme" language that opens the door for amnesty.

"Yes, it's going to happen under this Congress," Rep. Don Bacon, R-Neb., told Fox News Digital, pointing to the country's labor shortages and Republicans' declining support among Hispanic voters as reasons to pursue immigration reform. "I think it's a win-win for a country, we get a much-needed workforce while providing some accountability for misbehavior."

Bacon's remarks come after Trump signaled during a recent "60 Minutes" interview that he would be open to immigration reform, potentially creating an opportunity for lawmakers to advance the Dignity Act, a bill introduced by Rep. Maria Elvira Salazar, R-Fla., in 2025.

The legislation, which now has over a dozen Republican co-sponsors, would provide certain undocumented immigrants who meet eligibility requirements with renewable legal status and work authorization, without establishing a direct pathway to citizenship.

However, Bacon warned that delaying the legislation until the 120th Congress, when Democrats will control both chambers, could result in a more expansive immigration package.

"Unfortunately, I think the Dems will make it more like an amnesty bill," Bacon told Fox News Digital. "They'll probably go to another extreme."

Rep. Marlin Stutzman, R-Ind., added to Fox News Digital that "there is no better solution on the table that will get support from both parties while simultaneously standing in the way of the Democrats' looming amnesty-ridden citizenship-for-all alternative."

"The best way to see the work the administration is doing continue into the next two years while Democrats are in control of both houses is to pass a Republican-led immigration plan before January," Stutzman continued.

Salazar previously told Fox News Digital that Trump's willingness to discuss immigration reform could create an opportunity to advance her legislation before Republicans lose control of Congress.

"I think that this is a very good window to concentrate on something that even the president said that he was willing to work with the Dems, which is immigration," Salazar said.

The bill's original sponsor also expressed hope that Trump could make immigration reform a defining part of his presidential legacy, comparing the opportunity to Abraham Lincoln's role in ending slavery.

Senate Democrats., like Sen. Ruben Gallego, D-Ariz., told Fox News Digital that he would be willing to work with Salazar on pushing for the immigration bill.

"I'm here to try to bring as many protections as I can to these families." Gallego told Fox News Digital. "Let's get it going, we're willing to work with anybody to try to keep these families together."

The White House did not respond to Fox News Digital's request for comment.
