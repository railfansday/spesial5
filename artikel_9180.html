Layli Long Soldier’s Quilted Poetry

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The Lakota poet reflects on the visual art in her new book, 10 art titles we’re reading this month, and a biography of Beverly Buchanan sets the record straight.

The days have been cut short here in North America, swiftly and cruelly. But if there’s anything a brutal 4:50pm sunset can teach us, maybe it’s that turning to a good art book can be just as nourishing as a trip down Museum Mile. For our last monthly list of the year, our editors and critics recommend new art books that make the season bright: a glimpse into Rosa Luxemburg’s secret herbarium, Salman Toor’s latest catalog, essays by Celia Paul, who Natalie Weis says “writes much like she paints.”

Layli Long Soldier is another figure who blurs the boundaries between text and art, uniting them in her new poetry collection We. Artist Dyani White Hawk, her longtime friend and colleague, interviewed her about the shape of her poems, her monumental quilts, and how Lakota philosophy guides her work. Among many insights about art and grief that stopped me in my tracks, a favorite is: “I think creating is equivalent to love. It can be received or it can be dismissed, but we give it because we must.”

More below, including a biography of polymath Beverly Buchanan and artist Martha Wilson on living through the Culture Wars at the center of Isaac Butler’s new book.

—Lakshmi Rivera Amin, associate editor, books

Reading Layli Long Soldier’s newest publication, We (Graywolf Press, 2026), is a visual experience. She leads us across the page through poetry and storytelling, but her work is far more than ideas, statements, and wordplay. Even when just text on the page, it is both literature and visual art. Through this bridge, it creates deep connections to our innermost experiences — at times validating our own humanity, or providing a window into another’s. Fittingly, the title has two meanings: the English “we” and the Lakota “wé,” meaning “blood,” a shared lineage and life source. | Dyani White Hawk
