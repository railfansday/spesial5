Going Greenwood

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

I see light side of the darkness and have a pair of contrasting encounters on the trail.

On the bright side, I get plenty of time to do what I call sleep. I am in my tent at dark, which is quite early with sunset before 6:00PM. I get comfortable in my quilt and put on a podcast until I feel sleepy. That usually doesn’t take too long when I hike more than 20 miles in a day. I don’t have much to do until it gets light out, which is around 6:30AM. I’ll have breakfast and wait until it feels right to get back on the trail. The extra time comes in handy since I often don’t even get to my car camping spots until 11:00PM or so. One night of about 6 hours of sleep offset by one of 9 or more. It all balances out. Now, if I can just find a way to get the extra sleep in the van and less sleep in the tent I’ll be in business.

In spite of the extra time in my tent, I was still slow to get up. It is mostly laziness seeping in, but when I have “only” 20.8 miles to hike, my sense of urgency in the morning wanes. After filtering a fresh liter of water to work through on my hike out of camp, I made my way onto the trail just before 8:30AM. It would be a straight shot back to my van to close out another 2-day backpacking stint. I was already thinking of the town food waiting for me later in the day.

For anyone curious, I just looked up the amount of daylight today in New York is 10 hours and 33 minutes. It was 11 hours and 18 minutes on February 27th in Georgia, the day I began my hike. I still have almost a month left to hike and already I am working with 45 minutes fewer minutes of daylight every day.

After following the ups and downs of a few hills, the trail plunges into the Vernon Valley and back through the marshy area. I knew that the Stairway to Heaven waited for at the end of the 2-mile stretch of flat trail. The fun wouldn’t stop there as I’d be heading back into New York and the same rocks that tested my ankle yesterday. The game plan for today was to take it easy on the ankle to not upset it beyond the point of mild tenderness. That was no problem on the boardwalk over the marsh, but it would be tricky on all the rocky terrain that lay ahead.

Yesterday’s sunshine had given way to an overcast day. I even thought it might rain, which might be a problem for two reasons. First, the trail would become even more slippery, that would be rough. Second, I had been leaving my poncho behind because every time I’ve checked the forecast there had been a 0% chance of rain in the coming days. I hoped that would hold true as I crossed Route 94 and entered the domain of the Stairway to Heaven.

There were still a few day hikers on the trail, and even some section hikers. One couple I talked to were hiking to the Pennsylvania border, I told them I might run into them tomorrow, keeping my trail name alive even late into the season!

Marching back up the Stairway to Heaven proved to be much less stressful than hiking down. I could easily keep my body weight over my feet and avoid spots that looked like they might be tricky. At the viewpoint, more people were sitting and soaking in the view than yesterday, but I wasn’t one of them. I took my picture and shot back down the trail, eager to get back to the van. I had been doing the hiker math all day, if I kept up a good pace I could be back at the van in the 4:00 hour.

Crossing back through the marsh in Vernon Valley.

Crossing back through the marsh in Vernon Valley.

I was clear of the day’s big climb and things were looking good so far. I was in my zone, so much so that a deer bolting away from me startled me a bit. It stopped a short distance up a hill and turned around to size me up. It did the thing that deer do – stare blankly without moving a single muscle. I decided to have a little fun, I played a song on my phone and began to dance to see if I could solicit any kind of movement whatsoever. I was unsuccessful. My next encounter was decidedly more exciting.

I had last seen the “Flintstones” Pebbles, Bambam, and Sans at the Connecticut/Massachusetts border. I hadn’t caught up with them on my subsequent hikes, so I assumed they were well down the trail and hiking further ahead every day. That changed as a hiker emerged from a rocky switchback just ahead. Pebbles! She and Bambam had spent a few days visiting New York city while Sans hiked ahead. She was now hiking on her own, still determined as ever to stay on the AT. She shared a story about a bear encounter a few days earlier. Apparently she scared the poor bear so much that it dropped it’s fish while fleeing. It did sheepishly return to retrieve the fish, keeping one eye on Pebbles. She still had the seating pad that I collected for her in Massachusetts and was in good spirits, especially after hearing that, from what I had hiked in New Jersey, the trail ahead was considerably less rocky. I don’t think either of us are looking forward to “Rocksylvania”.

This deer is about to hear some music and watch me dance.

Soon I popped back up onto the rocky ridge above Greenwood Lake. Like yesterday, planes were flying overhead every few minutes. The day had brightened up a little but still looked like a gloomy late-October day. I suppose that isn’t inappropriate given the holiday we have just around the corner.

I hadn’t taken a water break or stopped except to catch up with Pebbles during the day. I had enough energy in my tank to keep cruising ahead. When the trail turned off the ridge I knew I had just 2 more easy miles to Route 17A. When I reached my van I went straight for my jug of water. I knew right where I’d be driving, the grocery store in Warwick. The first order of business was getting my fill of town food, followed by a quick session of filling my food bags for the final 2-day backpacking stint of the week. I grabbed my laptop bag and got comfortable in the seating area to knock out yesterday and today’s blog posts. This is by far the best blog writing routine I have had so far. Hike 2 days, then knock out the  2 posts start to finish, leave myself as little as possible to finish up on Sunday so I can relax. Only a few more weeks of this hectic routine!
