‘The Murder of JonBenét Ramsey’ Puts Family’s Former Boulder, CO, Residence in the Spotlight Once Again

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Nearly 30 years after 6-year-old JonBenét Ramsey was found dead in her family’s home in Boulder, CO, the tragic case is regaining attention with the release of a new Netflix limited series.

“The Murder of JonBenét Ramsey” tells the story of the cold case through a lens that seeks not to solve the mystery, but to tell the story of those who were involved—particularly as the 1996 murder occurred in a decade marked by high-profile crime.

The 1990s "ended with Columbine and started with the Menendez brothers, the Susan Smith murder, the OJ trial,” showrunner Richard LaGravenese said in a statement. “I was fascinated by the challenge of doing something that was a murder mystery without an ending, without being solved.”

To portray the effects of the media coverage that surrounded the case, the limited series will focus on Patsy and John Ramsey (played by Melissa McCarthy and Clive Owen) as well as a group of local and federal investigators (brought to life by Garrett Hedlund, Will Patton, Shea Whigham, and Alison Pill).

“They all had different points of view for all different reasons,” LaGravenese said. “It was an incredible story to tell from all these perspectives.”

The Ramsey family’s former home was at the center of the case, and although a handful of interior and exterior shots in the series’ trailer strikingly resemble the recognizable Tudor, it has been reported that “The Murder of JonBenét Ramsey” was filmed in Calgary, Alberta.

And while a few of the memorable details of the home—such as the black-and-white checkerboard tile floor in the kitchen—also make an appearance in the trailer, much of the home has been updated in recent years. Listing photos from 2024 show that the kitchen was entirely renovated.

The Ramseys purchased the home for $500,000 in 1991, and moved to Atlanta in 1997 following JonBenét’s death. They sold the home for $650,000 in 1998, with profits from the sale pledged to the JonBenét Ramsey Children’s Foundation.

The 7,240-square-foot abode changed hands again, for $1.05 million in 2004. Numerous attempts to sell the home since then have come to naught. In 2008, it was listed for $2.68 million; the price was later reduced to $2.29 million in 2009, $2.3 million in 2011, and $2 million in 2014.

Nine years would pass before the home was listed again, asking $6.95 million in March 2023. After a series of price adjustments, the property was taken off the market in February 2025.

JonBenét was found dead in the wine cellar in the basement of the home in the early hours of Dec. 26, 1996. Hours before her body was discovered, the family uncovered a ransom note in the home, which asked $118,000 for the return of their daughter.

Police determined she had been murdered in the late hours of the night on Christmas, and a later autopsy ruled the cause of death as strangulation.

In the following months, police investigated a number of suspects, including Patsy, John, and their son, Burke Ramsey—who were not cleared until July 2008, two years after Patsy died of ovarian cancer.
