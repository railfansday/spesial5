Rapid Recap: Bucks 116, Raptors 111

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Milwaukee Bucks won—yes, won!—their second game of the season in a back-and-forth, tense matchup against the host Toronto Raptors. The Bucks rode a fourth-quarter surge to cement a commanding lead over the Raptors that they just couldn't overcome. Tyler Herro scored 32 points, and Myles Turner posted a 27-point, 11-rebound double-double. Scottie Barnes led the Raptors with 31 points.

Jakob Poeltl scored the game's first points when he grabbed an offensive board and muscled off Kyle Kuzma to convert the and-one. The Raptors quickly jumped out to a 14-4 lead, helped by hot three-point shooting, but the Bucks responded by scoring seven straight points to cut Toronto's lead to four. Jaime Jaquez Jr. helped the Bucks take the lead with a three-pointer with four minutes remaining, but that was by no means permanent. After Collin Murray-Boyles and Tyler Herro missed back-to-back dunks, the Bucks and Raptors traded leads before Brayden Burries tied it up at 28 with a pull-up three at the end of the first.

Both sides reverted to their usual offensive selves at the start of the second: clunky and inefficient in the half-court. Jamal Shead, who shoots south of 28% from three this season, connected on a three to push Toronto's lead to 33-31, but Myles Turner immediately fired back a triple to preserve the Bucks' one-point edge. Because of each side's offensive struggles—or, depending on how you look at it, defensive success—the lead remained small for both sides. Turner then regained the flow he had in the first quarter, clicking from long range and preying on Toronto's lack of interior size. Toronto held an edge to close the half, with buckets from Murray-Boyles and Immanuel Quickley extending their edge to four, 53-49, at the long break.

The game remained tight to open the second half, with Turner continuing his hot shooting, splashing a three to tie it at 55, his fourth bomb of the game. However, Toronto built a seven-point lead with Poeltl's and-one layup as the Raptors found success getting to the line and drawing fouls, especially on Turner. That, along with Barnes' high-flying athleticism and midrange prowess, helped Toronto build a slight buffer. However, it wasn't enough, as the Bucks came roaring back; Herro's floater gave the visitors a 73-72 lead, and although Jamison Battle quickly retook it with a pull-up three, it felt like the Raptors wouldn't easily make it out of Scotiabank Arena with a win. Nate Ament took the lead on a driving layup with 2:07 to go, and the Raptors struggled to respond, largely because of their second-half shooting struggles. Kel'el Ware jammed home a putback dunk in the waning seconds, giving the Bucks an 85-80 lead at the end of the third.

The momentum began to shift towards the hosts as the fourth got going. Allen Graves knocked down a wing three, cutting Milwaukee's edge down to one, and the Raptors took the lead when Barnes found Poeltl for a short floater. Although the Bucks went on a long field goal drought, they got into the bonus really early in the quarter, which let them keep their noses in front. That said, Ja'Kobe Walter lit Scotiabank Arena on fire when he hit a long three to tie the game at 98, but Kuzma immediately responded with a triple of his own to quiet them back down. Turner then went on a personal 6-0 run with a wing triple and an and-one on Poeltl, fouling him out of the game—huge! The Raptors couldn't overcome that lead, even as Turner fouled out as well. The Bucks kept hitting big shots, including Burries' acrobatic layup and a dagger Herro jumper, while Toronto flatlined on offense. Milwaukee ends its 13-game losing streak!

The Bucks had their best shooting night of the season so far, making 14 three-pointers on 50% shooting. Coincidentally, this game also saw the fewest three-point shots (28) the Bucks have attempted all season, highlighting the Bucks' paint dominance over Toronto's frontcourt.

Myles Turner has firmly put the starting center conversation to rest today. Despite the fouling concerns and turnover issues, Turner looked like the stretch five Milwaukee dreamed of when they paid him $106 million over four years. He gave the Bucks elite spacing in the half-court offense, letting slashing guards like Herro and Burries feast inside. He was also an imposing interior defender, and his two blocks and constant vertical challenges won't be ignored.
