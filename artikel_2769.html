Fantasy Football 2026: Waiver wire, Week 11 edition

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Welcome to the Fantasy Football Waiver Wire for Week 11.

The waiver wire was a feeding frenzy last week. A fresh round of injuries, a few NFL trades that impacted rosters, four teams on bye, and a lot of attractive free agents on offer were chum in the water, and managers bit.

Well, buckle up, fantasy players, because Week 11 is the worst bye week of the season. It's the only one with six teams on bye, and you're going to hear terms like "byepocalypse". On top of that, there's less to choose from this week, in part because so many players got scooped up last week (examples: Emmett Johnson, Kaleb Johnson, and Tank Dell). But that doesn't mean there aren't players to add who can help you. I'll do my best to identify them here.

The Process: Each week, I try to identify the top Waiver Wire targets and evaluate the short- and long-term prospects of those players. I also do my best to reveal which players who are coming off a big week are fool's gold. Finally, I look at streamers who might help you in a pinch (this is usually more important during bye weeks).

Some players discussed won't be available in your league. I try to list players who are available in more than 50% of leagues. But what does that even mean? A 10-team league with six bench spots will have a much larger Free Agent pool than a 14-team league with eight. Most leagues fall somewhere in between. This isn't "one size fits all." By way of example, I won't list Kirk Cousins, Emmett Johnson, Kaleb Johnson, Antonio Williams, Jordyn Tyson, Tank Dell, or Ryan Flournoy, among others who probably aren't available in any of your leagues.

A slight change this week: this column is dropping AFTER the Week 11 Monday night game (LAC@BAL).

WEEK 11 BYES: Falcons, Browns, Packers, Rams, Patriots, Seahawks

Don't look now, but Cam Ward is starting to look more like a No. 1 draft pick and less like a lemon. He's been putting up fantasy points (46 of them over his last two games), and doing it without his No. 1 receiver, rookie Carnell Tate. Tennessee has scored at least 23 points in four straight games. We saw flashes last season, but Ward hasn't shown that he can be a consistent producer. His upcoming schedule is exceptional, with games against Dallas, Washington, Las Vegas, Indianapolis, and Detroit in the next month and a half. He's a nice add in 2-QB leagues if he's available, or if you just lost Drake Maye and need emergency help.

Fernando Mendoza (who I recommended last week) had his moments in his debut. Let's give him a break, since it was against Seattle, and next week he gets the equally tough Broncos (and he might be without Brock Bowers), rested off a bye. Not an easy way to start a career. But I think he can help you out if you need a second quarterback in a Superflex down the stretch. He's not afraid to run, which is a plus.

Need a Week 11 streamer? A lot of people do with the big bye week. It's not a great week for that. Ward is your best bet, against that atrocious Dallas defense that gave up 46 more points in Week 10. Jacoby Brissett at the Panthers is in play as well. He attempted 49 passes on Monday night while throwing for 340 yards. And I'll actually mention Justin Herbert, who gets the Jets at home.

Beyond those let me show you our bargain basement. Geno Smith (assuming he's able to play this week) is at the Chargers. J.J. McCarthy has a tough home matchup against the Jags. Baker Mayfield or Jalon Daniels at the Lions isn't terrible. Kyle McCord could have the gift of extended garbage time against backups at Buffalo, and especially with the Bills set to play the Chiefs on Thanksgiving night. That's a lot to talk yourself into. Speaking of which, Joe Milton wasn't good in his first start against the Giants, but he did run for 64 yards on nine carries and the Eagles could finally get some weapons back coming out of their bye. They host the Steelers, whose defense never got off the plane in Cincinnati on Sunday night. Hey, it's a brutal week and desperate times and all that…

Emari Demercado will be a hot add this week, as he has a good shot to be the lead back for the Texans on Thursday night in a plus-matchup against the Colts. Houston hasn't run the ball effectively all season, but with Woody Marks unlikely to get out of concussion protocol on a short week and David Montgomery iffy to return to action, this is a no-brainer, even if it's a short-term pickup. But don't go crazy with a FAAB bid.

Seth McGowan may have been dropped by many speculators after Jonathan Taylor didn't get traded. McGowan should be rostered. He saw a season-high 12 carries in Week 10 and scored a TD for the second straight week. He's part of the offense.

I can't imagine many people dropped Kyle Monangai, but since he never went on IR it's possible that some managers needed the roster space. He's a priority add if available, and a great streaming option this week against the Saints, who allow the most fantasy points to running backs. David Montgomery is probably still rostered in most places (people are stubborn with their fourth- or fifth-round picks, and he did score thrice in Week 1), but I'd look to add him if he isn't.

Backs who should be rostered: Kaelon Black, Kenneth Gainwell, Justice Hill, Samaje Perine, Dylan Sampson,and Israel Ibanikanda (only because the Cowboys have no depth at the position).

The Breakfast Club, a/k/a/ the "don't you forget about me" list: Jonathon Brooks, Jacory Croskey-Merritt, R.J. Harvey, and Tank Bigsby. Most of these are still eligible for an IR spot.

Xavier Hutchinson tops the list this week. The Texans have a lot of bodies at the position (and especially once Marvin Harrison, Jr. returns), but for now Hutchinson is operating as the No. 2 receiver and he has scored in three straight games. The Texans are airing it out (because, again, they can't run the ball and their vaunted defense is struggling), making Hutchinson an attractive option.

Pat Bryant may have made it through last week's waivers because Denver had a bye in Week 10. Bo Nix favors him, and it's unclear if Jaylen Waddle will be back in Week 11. Even with a healthy Waddle, Bryant has value. He's right there with Hutchinson for me.

Caleb Douglas made the list last week, and I'll add feature him again, even though Kyle McCord doesn't inspire a ton of confidence. Douglas is the receiver to roster from Miami. He's getting good target volume and topped 70 yards in each of the last two games. This week's game at Buffalo should have extended garbage time, as discussed above.

Need depth? I'll repeat a bunch of names I've listed in recent weeks, together with some new ones: Keenan Allen, Konata Mumpfield, Malachi Fields, Tre' Harris, Laquon Treadwell, Jordan Watkins, and Jakobi Meyers.

Is Brissett going to turn Mark Andrews into Trey McBride? No, of course not. But Andrews got onto the fantasy radar on Monday night with significant volume and production (8-82-0 and he caught all of his targets). Given the lack of quality at the position, he's worth a pickup as the Ravens don't have any reliable receivers after Zay Flowers, and you know Brissett likes to get ball to his tight end.

I'll just keep running back the same names. Mike Gesicki, Darren Waller, Tyler Higbee, and Pat Freiermuth are all boom-bust options who can get you a TD (and maybe a little more) if you hit it right. Chig Okonkwo offers more consistency. Brevyn Spann-Ford is interesting, given how much Dallas throws. Terrance Ferguson is a stash if you've got the space. Michael Mayer should see a bump in volume if Bowers misses time.
