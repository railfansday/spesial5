Karl-Anthony Towns gave the Knicks a way to start late possessions

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Karl-Anthony Towns scored 17 points before halftime in Minnesota and finished with 22. The Knicks led by 15 at the break and won by three. You can enjoy Towns' return to Target Center and still come away asking why New York had such a hard time finding another easy trip late.

Towns added 12 rebounds in the 102-99 win, his 15th double-double in 15 games to start the season. There's a good reason the Knicks keep putting the ball in his hands. He can start a possession outside the paint and still get a big defender moving toward the rim before New York has committed to a shot.

Minnesota held New York to 12 points in the fourth quarter and the Knicks didn't score in the final 3:26. That's a team number. It doesn't tell us which shots Towns should have taken or prove he went missing. It does tell me I'd want one easier way to get two threats going at once when the next game gets tight.

I'd start with Towns catching above the lane and Jalen Brunson coming toward him. If the defender is up on Towns, he can make the handoff and roll behind him. If the big is back, Towns can face up or use the space to pass. The other three Knicks shouldn't have to stay planted while they find out which defender gives them something. One cut from the weak side is enough to turn a helping defender's head.

The timing matters more than whether it ends in the play Brown drew up. Start with enough time to reject a bad handoff and go back the other way. A center catching with seven seconds left has fewer options than a center catching while two defenders are still deciding who owns the next action.

In the official box score, Brunson had 21 points and seven assists. Leave him an option to control the last part of the possession if the first action doesn't produce a clean look. The alternative is putting him in a spread pick-and-roll from the start, which makes sense when the opposing guard can't stay in front. The Towns touch gives New York a way to get there with the defense already having to account for somebody else.

The team's pregame notes had Towns at 14 straight double-doubles before tipoff. Those numbers don't all come from the same coverage. Neither should the Knicks' late offense. In the next close fourth, I'd want to see Towns get that first touch with time to make a decision, before a missed shot or a ticking clock makes the next one for him.
