Inside 2 clutch moves by Texas football staffers in road win over LSU Tigers

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Steve Sarkisian shrugged off Arch Manning's leap into his arms late Saturday night in Baton Rouge, then put up two fingers. He should have instead formed a zero with his hand after Manning's game-winning touchdown pass to running back Raleek Brown.

The initial motion from Texas football's head coach signaled he wanted the Longhorns to attempt for a two-point conversion after taking a 22-21 lead over LSU with three seconds remaining. But his initial hand motion wasn't followed by a true two-point attempt. Instead, Manning took the snapfrom center Connor Robertson and immediately kneeled down, ceding any possible points in its eventual 22-21 victory.

How the hell did the Texas Longhorns win that? The hard way.

Texas could have lost to 23-22 if it turned the ball over and allowed an LSU defensive score on the two-point attempt. The chance of that event occuring was slim. But Sarkisian still opted to avoid the chance altogether, making a heady decision in the process. What changed Sarkisian's mind after his initial signal to go for two? He credited information from two Longhorns staff members in particular.

Video from Saturday night showed a man in a white hoodie and baseball hat speaking in Sarkisian's ear shortly after Manning's celebration. The man in question: John Michael Jones, Texas' director of player personnel. Jones also moonlights as an analytics expert, the on-the-sideline voice of a multi-member Texas analytics staff.

"(Jones) really manages a lot of the analytics for me," Sarkisian said Monday. "I get the information from analytics, I usually have a conversation with Mike, and then at times I may go converse with (defensive coordinator Will Muschamp). There's a lot of dialogue that goes into it, and then ultimately I make the call on whatever it is."

Sarkisian said Jones provides input on a variety of situations, including kneel downs, timeout usage, penalty acceptance and 10-second runoff decisions. The kneel down after Texas' last-minute touchdown was an especially prudent decision, one Sarkisian says came from Jones and Michael Bimonte, the Longhorns' assistant quarterback coach and co-passing game coordinator.

"I was excited, too, don't get me wrong. In your mind, we just won the game, but there are still three seconds," Sarkisian said as he explained his post-touchdown jubilation. "But below five seconds, with the lead, you take the knee to not even put the ball in harm's way at all. I thought both Mike Bimonte and John Michael (Jones) were very clear that this was a take-a-knee situation.

"I was already saying 'go-for-2', in my mind it was safe. They already went to, 'it's below five (points), this is a take-a-knee, be-safe-situation."

The Austin American-Statesman was unable to reach Jones or Bimonte for comment as of Monday.

Jones and Bimonte aid Sarkisian each Saturday. They also lead a key element of the Longhorns' weekly preparation.

Texas holds weekly "situational-masters meetings," per Sarkisian, a labor of love for Bimonte in particulaer. The offensive staffer "scours" the web to find examples of smart football decisions across the NFL, college football and high school football, and he then presents his findings to Texas coaches and players. There's also a presentation of "anti-situational masters," moments, in which Bimonte shows Texas players and coaches a collection of avoidable mistakes.

Sarkisian said Texas places an emphasis on raising the "football IQ" of its players. Jones and Bimonte appear to do exactly that for Sarkisian himself, who was convinced to make the proper decision on Texas' final offensive play at LSU.
