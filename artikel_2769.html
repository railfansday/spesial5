Game Preview: The Suns have a Cooper Flagg problem to solve on Black Friday

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Who: Phoenix Suns (9-8) vs. Dallas Mavericks (11-7)

Where: Mortgage Matchup Center — Phoenix, Arizona

I hope everybody had a memorable and delicious Thanksgiving. Today? We're back to basketball.

The Phoenix Suns, after going 4-1 on their most recent road trip, return home to face the Dallas Mavericks, a team that finds itself at 11-7 and riding a four-game winning streak after starting the season 7-7. It's a battle between two teams in close proximity in the standings. Dallas currently sits 1.5 games ahead of Phoenix as the eighth seed in the Western Conference, while the Suns, at 9-8, occupy the 10th seed.

Games like this carry a little extra weight, and it has little to do with the NBA Cup. For context, the Dallas Mavericks currently lead Group A in the Western Conference with a 3-0 record and a +36 point differential. They've already clinched the group and punched their ticket to the knockout rounds. Phoenix, meanwhile, sits at 1-2.

So why does this result matter as it pertains to the NBA Cup?

If you recall, the reward for advancing in the NBA Cup is an opportunity to win a relatively meaningless trophy while adding games against difficult competition to your schedule. Some willingly embrace that reality, subscribing to the iron sharpens iron methodology and pointing to the fact that the New York Knicks won both the NBA Cup and the NBA championship last season. That's fair.

But some teams, like the Phoenix Suns a season ago, really didn't have any business being there, and all they ultimately did was add two losses to their schedule. Meanwhile, if you're a quality team that misses out on advancing in the NBA Cup, there's a possibility that the two games added to your schedule come against lesser competition, presenting an opportunity to add a pair of wins to your overall record.

If the Suns win tonight, they'll finish group play at 2-2 and could find themselves matched up against another team that finished with a similar record. So there are immediate implications as it pertains to positioning in the Western Conference standings, as well as potential implications for their overall record, knowing that two games could be added to their schedule against competition you might deem beatable.

Dallas is led by sophomore phenom and former number one overall pick Cooper Flagg, who is averaging 24.3 points per game on 48/32/85 shooting splits, adding 6.7 assists and 6.3 rebounds. He's young, athletic, and has an affinity for getting to the rim. He ranks ninth in the NBA with 9.5 attempts per game at the rim, so he's going to force the issue. The Suns need to be ready for it.

Dallas also has Daniel Gafford, who currently leads the league with a 69.5% field goal percentage.

My point? Keep an eye on the interior of the Suns' defense in this one. The Mavericks are a relatively average three-point shooting team, attempting 37.8 threes per game, which ranks 14th in the league, and connecting on 34.4% of them, good for 20th.

Phoenix needs to be mindful of both the perimeter and the interior, but more importantly, they need to be smart about how they defend Cooper Flagg. That assignment will most likely fall to either Dillon Brooks or Miles Bridges, and Flagg is clearly more athletic than both of them. I'd almost prefer Bridges on him to introduce another level of physicality, as he has a greater ability to move players off their spots. Brooks' agility, however, might give him the advantage in that matchup.

Ultimately, that's what I'll be watching tonight. Who's guarding Cooper Flagg, and how effective are they in doing so?

The Mavericks like to get out and run, and their 18.6 fast break points per game rank second in the NBA. They're also eighth in the league with 19.4 points off turnovers per game. If you're careless with the ball against a team that ranks sixth in the league with 9.4 steals per game, they'll take it and run. The key here? Ball security. No need to give a team like Dallas a chance to cherry-pick points.

Dallas also ranks seventh in the league with 16.6 second-chance points per game. I think you know where I'm going with this one.

The Suns' rebounding has been abhorrent through the first 20% of the season. And when you're playing a team like the Mavericks, who possess plenty of size and can continually bring more of it off the bench, there's going to be no relenting in that aspect of the game. Box out, rebound, rinse, repeat.

We saw that fight against the Houston Rockets, the same fight that defined the Suns' identity a season ago. The hope is that they bring that same focus and energy tonight and channel some of it into a little give-a-shit on the boards.

This is one of those games that's going to be a fight, as you have two teams that find themselves in the same neighborhood of the Western Conference standings. And that's essentially what we have right now: three distinct neighborhoods. You have five teams within 1.5 games of the lead, the sixth through 11th seeds separated by 2.5 games, and then it falls off a cliff. The 12th seed sits five games behind the 11th.

After a lengthy road trip, Phoenix is back home in front of a Black Friday crowd that will include me and my family, who are in town from Fresno. The hope is that the crowd isn't suffering from that post-Thanksgiving malaise and will be loud and proud in support of a team coming off an impressive road trip.

I think that'll be the case. The Suns will feed off that energy, and Jordan Goodwin will hit five three-pointers in this one. Suns win.
