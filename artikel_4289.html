PlayStation Adds Major Feature to Marvel’s Spider-Man 2, ARC Raiders, and More

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Daniel is a lifelong gamer with a love for various genres and gameplay styles who enjoys exploring games and playing from various angles.

A big component of what makes the PS5 an attractive console, besides just being able to play particular games you can’t play elsewhere, is the console’s various built-in graphical perks. Since the PS5 first launched in 2020, its hardware power has remained one of its definitive perks, with the PS5 Pro in particular taking matters further with the inclusion of PlayStation Spectral Super Resolution, better known as PSSR.

In this list, we discuss some of the best PS4 games to play with the PS5 Pro's Image Quality Enhancements option.

Even though the PS5 already has a few years under its belt, the brand isn’t just resting on its proverbial laurels and letting its existing hardware carry it. New advancements in graphical technology are coming out all the time, and even games that looked pretty stunning when they first dropped like Marvel’s Spider-Man 2 can always look a little bit better.

To that end, PlayStation has added support for one of its up-and-coming graphical frameworks, Quick Spectral Super Resolution (QSSR), to several more games in the PS5’s library following its initial launch last month exclusively in Marvel’s Wolverine and Ghost of Yotei. The newly-upscaled games include Marvel’s Spider-Man 2, Control Resonant, ARC Raiders, The Finals, No Man’s Sky, and Hell Let Loose: Vietnam.

In case you forgot, QSSR is a proprietary AI upscaling framework created in tandem between PlayStation and AMD. This framework came about from Project Amethyst, an ongoing developmental effort between both companies that also gave rise to PSSR. According to the official announcement of the newly-upscaled games on the official PlayStation news blog, QSSR provides “sharper details, cleaner edges, and more stable images than traditional upscalers,” and since it’s built on the same platform as PSSR, it’s easy for games that already use that framework to incorporate it.

QSSR is a proprietary AI upscaling framework created in tandem between PlayStation and AMD.

In that news post, PlayStation got quotes from all of the improved games’ developers on how QSSR has affected their respective titles. Insomniac Games’ Mike Fitzgerald said that, after incorporating the tech into the launch of Marvel’s Wolverine, the studio decided to go back and add it to Marvel’s Spider-Man 2 as well. “We’re excited for players to swing back into our 2023 title and see QSSR’s exciting image stability and sharpness benefits in both Fidelity and Performance modes,” he said.

Sony has introduced a brand-new AI-powered upscaling technology called Quick Spectral Super Resolution.

Remedy Entertainment’s Erik Jansson said that “QSSR gives Control Resonant a much more stable and detailed image, and we’ve worked closely with Sony to have it ready in an update coming in December.”

Embark Studios’ Mikael Linderholm said that QSSR support will be coming soon to ARC Raiders and The Finals, and said that his studio is “very happy to see Sony’s continued investment in PlayStation technology translate into meaningful improvements for all players.”

While I’m personally of the belief that graphics alone don’t automatically make a game good, it is still pretty interesting to see the degree to which graphical fidelity can be improved, especially as we start to run up against the proverbial limits of realism.

Obviously, the use of AI in games is a feisty topic, particularly in presentation elements like voice acting. In the specific use case of graphics, some efforts like NVIDIA’s AI-powered DLSS5 and its widely-mocked “yassify filter” on character models is probably not the optimal way to be going about this. Rather than trying to generate and overwrite existing graphics with slop, something like QSSR is closer to the ideal, taking what’s already present and merely smoothing out the rougher edges of the environmental rendering process.

PlayStation has promised that this tech will be coming to more games, ending its news post by saying, “Developers are just getting started, and we’re excited to keep working with them to bring QSSR to more games on PS5 in the coming months.”
