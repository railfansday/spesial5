Meet the Biggest Ralph Lauren Fan in France

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Some people collect works of art; others collect clothes. When I heard about a Parisian who had amassed thousands of Ralph Lauren pieces, my curiosity got the better of me and I wanted to learn more. Séverin Naudet, a former digital adviser to François Fillon when he was France’s prime minister, lives on the fifth floor of a beautiful Haussmann-style building. The tall, dark-haired 50-year-old appears not to age. Since leaving his role with the government in 2012, he has started a digital marketing company and is now also exploring opportunities in luxury and wellness.

At his home, he displays part of his wardrobe, and I realize that the rumors about Naudet are true. He shows me dozens of pairs of sneakers, tuxedo jackets and velvet shoes, pants and shirts, more jackets, and then there are also mugs, books, and pillows. In the bedroom, the linens bear the American designer’s label, while tableware from Ralph Lauren’s Burleigh Collection, carefully wrapped up and placed in a Carrefour bag, is ready to be taken to his house in Brittany, where “more sailboat-inspired” pieces are stored. There are also sorbet-colored swimsuits piled on the plump armrests of a club chair. “About 20 years ago I donated all my other clothes to a charity. Since then, I’ve worn nothing but Ralph Lauren,” he explains, barefoot and dressed in jeans and a Polo shirt, next to a jacket from Denim & Supply, a Ralph Lauren brand that existed from 2011 to 2016 and is now coveted by collectors. Naudet’s Ralph Lauren pieces represent more than just a wardrobe; they embody a way of life that embraces every facet of the American brand founded in 1967—from preppy cool to sophisticated, Gatsby-style chic, and including many nautical pieces that connect to another of Naudet’s passions: sailing.

A selection from his collection of sneakers and dress shoes

But how does one fall in love with a brand to the point of making it part of every facet of their life? “As a teenager, I had body image issues. I had bowlegs, and I didn’t feel comfortable in my own skin,” he explains. Walking into a Double RL store changed everything: “I tried on a pair of Low Straight jeans and it was magical—they were perfectly cut, elegant, and stylish. I almost felt sexy. I had straight legs and a nice butt,” he says with a laugh.

His connection to the American brand speaks volumes about that magic formula that is the goal of retailers around the world: combining storytelling with emotional attachment. “Polo was the cure for a certain melancholy that was the result of a gloomy childhood. Ralph Lauren instilled in me a spirit of joy, where yellows, pinks, and sky blues, stripes and madras fabrics, evoke summer, the sea, and sailing,” explains the man who has amassed several thousand Ralph Lauren items.

As retail experiences increasingly take place online, there’s nothing Naudet loves more than going to a physical store. Walking into one is like stepping into “the family home I never had,” he explains. “A Ralph Lauren flagship store can feel like visiting a house in the Hamptons, a Parisian hotel for a perfect Christmas, or a New York apartment with a view of Central Park.” Ralph Lauren locations are must-visits on the entrepreneur’s itineraries. In Manhattan, he always visits the West Broadway store. He knows, of course, the ones in London and Los Angeles, as well as Manda in Brussels, where he goes in search of vintage pieces. His favorite store, however, remains the one in the mansion on Boulevard Saint-Germain in Paris, where every year he celebrates Christmas at its Ralph’s restaurant.

One might call him a “perfectionist,” but he prefers “meticulous.” He will go to lengths to repair clothes, even if it requires tracking down the original fabrics or finding an expert to repair braided leather straps. He’s also interested in the story behind the brand and how design choices and corporate strategies have made it what it is today. “Ralph Lauren is a marketing genius, and his advertising is never simply advertising. He works instead like a filmmaker; he tells a story. All you need is an image, even without a product. He created a family that we all dream of being a part of by, for example, choosing his wife, Ricky, as his muse. Their marriage has lasted 60 years!” the former adviser shares. And at a time when the world is wondering and worrying about the future, Ralph Lauren and his mythologies are calm and soothing: “In a daily life saturated with screens, images, and now artificial intelligence, Ralph Lauren continues to offer a world that is profoundly real, tactile, and inhabited.” The proof? In a fashion world sometimes tinged with gloom, Ralph Lauren posted a 15% increase in revenue for 2025, surpassing $8 billion for the first time. And in the first quarter of 2026, growth continued strong at 14%.

He quotes from memory Tocqueville’s description of New England in 1835 as “a region left to the dreams of the imagination, which one ought to abandon to the free trials of innovators.” Ralph Lauren is, for Naudet, the brand that stands above the fray, its founder a dreamer embodying “the finest qualities of the United States, from Tocqueville to the present day.” We ask if, as the ultimate embodiment of the American dream, Ralph Lauren, 86, would make a good president? Naudet, an expert on the inner workings of power, smiles: “He possesses at least three key qualities needed to be a good president: He understands the times, he has broad appeal, and he inspires people to dream. The rest is part of the mystery of democracy, and America is a great democracy.”

If the self-made New Yorker has ever wondered who his biggest fan in France is, Vanity Fair has found him. Before our conversation ends, Naudet tells me that recently, as he does every year around this time, he bought two pairs of Heritage Straight jeans, and also spotted a leather jacket lined with shearling that he wanted. It’s “admittedly a bit pricey,” but he’s certain it’ll be perfect once the temperatures drop.
