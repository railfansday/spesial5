Scott Bessent has changed his tune on Nvidia leadership as President Trump says chipmaker should ‘rehire’ Huang

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Trump administration has officially backed Jensen Huang’s return to Nvidia—a little over a week after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the AI giant needed a “dramatic shake-up” of leadership. Huang and Nvidia, the company he cofounded in 1993, are currently subject to intense scrutiny after it emerged that Huang had conducted an extramarital affair with a woman believed to be a Chinese spy. The company and its leadership are now facing questions from Congress and U.S. law enforcement about their compliance with export controls on specialized chips to China.But olive branches are now being extended to Huang, not only from within the AI community but now also at the highest level of government.

President Trump wrote on Truth Social this morning: “I hope the board of Nvidia rehires Jensen Huang. It would be the best thing for the company, and also, for the U.S.A.”

The president has been lenient toward Huang, despite congressional concerns that Huang’s relationship with an alleged Chinese asset was a national security risk. Nvidia has said it does not believe U.S. or company secrets have been compromised. Despite that, Trump said he is a “brilliant person” who made some “bad decisions.”

Bessent also took a softer approach toward Huang in an interview over the weekend. Bessent had previously said Nvidia directors needed to look externally for a candidate to lead the company, but sought to recast that stance: “Jensen is on the outside now,” he told CBS yesterday.

“I said they should go to the outside, but he’s on the outside now,” Bessent added. “But I think what we need to see—because the other thing I said was, we will see which level of the business knew about this. Was it at the lower levels? Was it the middle levels? Did it go all the way to the top?

“And I have confidence that the Nvidia committees are moving very quickly on this and will come up with some very fulsome and robust answers.”

Bessent had previously been one of the sharpest voices on the scandal, saying his department “needed answers” on whether Nvidia knowingly routed its products through China. The Treasury secretary has also placed blame at the feet of a fellow cabinet member, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick. Bessent previously told CNBC he had been in favor of putting Megaspeed, a Chinese company associated with the woman Huang conducted an affair with, on the “entity list,” which would impose restrictions on the business. The Singapore-based company is currently under investigation by the U.S., Jay Clayton, director of national intelligence, confirmed this weekend.

The obstacles facing Huang—beyond any potential return to the company he built—are significant.

Alongside Nvidia’s internal investigations into his conduct (reports have since surfaced alleging Huang had affairs with two Nvidia employees), the billionaire is also set to appear at numerous congressional hearings.

Last week, the House Committee on Foreign Affairs confirmed that Huang and Nvidia would appear before it, as well as the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party and the House Intelligence Committee, for transcribed interviews.

Sen. Tim Scott of the Senate Banking Committee also confirmed that Huang would be appearing before his committee and is hoping to have the hearing before Christmas.

That aside, speculators are gaining confidence in Huang’s return, following support from the likes of Tesla CEO Elon Musk, Nvidia staffers, and now the executive branch.

Huang’s odds of being the next CEO of Nvidia—taking back the role from interim CEO and former CFO Colette Kress—are at 16% on Kalshi, 4% behind Arm’s current CEO, Rene Haas. Over on Polymarket, bettors give a 36% chance that Huang is back at Nvidia by the end of next year.
