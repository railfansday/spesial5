Lighter recovers $5M after $10M exploit linked to compromised API keys

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Lighter restored withdrawals after recovering approximately $5 million in USDC, while blockchain analysts traced $10.4 million in funds moved during the exploit.

Update 20:40 UTC: This article has been updated with details of Lighter’s $5 million fund recovery, the restoration of withdrawals and the cause of the exploit.

Decentralized derivatives exchange Lighter said it had restored withdrawals and recovered approximately $5 million after an attacker exploited compromised trader API keys in an incident involving more than $10 million in withdrawals Monday.

Blockchain analytics firm Bubblemaps said about $10.4 million in USDC had been withdrawn from the platform. It tracked funds being swapped into Ether (ETH) and Wrapped Monero XMR1, a token traded on Hyperliquid.

Lighter initially said its protocol and smart contracts were unaffected. The exchange activated Guardian mode, temporarily pausing withdrawals and new pool creation while its Security Council worked to secure the platform.

The security incident follows a string of high-profile cryptocurrency exploits this year, including the $6.3 billion Binance hack on Oct. 29, the industry’s largest to date.

Lighter and Bubblemaps identified wallet ‘0xb3f’ as the attacker’s address. Blockchain data platform Bitquery separately traced 10,393,057 USDC leaving Lighter’s Ethereum contract at 10:11 am UTC. It said most of those funds were converted into Ether through CoW Swap within minutes, followed by a series of 160 ETH transfers to wallets linked to XMR1 purchases on Hyperliquid.

In subsequent analysis, Bitquery identified a total of 1,920 ETH transferred through 12 wallets, each receiving 160 ETH (about $290,000) before routing the funds through LI.FI toward Hyperliquid.

The 12 Hyperliquid accounts purchased approximately $3.46 million in XMR1 tokens. Another 3,845 ETH, worth about $7 million, remained in the original wallet as of 11:55 UTC on Monday, according to Bitquery’s latest snapshot.

Bitquery also identified similar fund movements following a separate $6.45 million Safe wallet theft at 11:35 UTC on Monday, about 84 minutes after the Lighter withdrawal, as well as a Kava exploit two days earlier.

All three involved funds routed through LI.FI into XMR1 purchases on Hyperliquid, but Bitquery found no direct transfers linking either of the other incidents to Lighter. The firm cautioned that similar transaction routes alone are weak evidence of a common attacker.

In a postmortem published Monday at 18:20 UTC, Lighter detailed how the attacker obtained private API keys through a trader’s custom signing software, which reused cryptographic nonces, allowing the keys to be calculated from publicly available signatures.

The attacker used the compromised keys to create a public pool, transfer funds into it and withdraw them through an attacker-controlled account.

Lighter said its Guardian security mechanism temporarily paused withdrawals, allowing its Security Council to return approximately $5 million in USDC to the affected trader. The exchange said its protocol, smart contracts and official software development kits were unaffected.

Lighter has since required wallet signatures for transfers to public pools and made API-key signatures non-public. It also plans to introduce IP allowlists and configurable permissions for API keys.
