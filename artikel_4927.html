SBF’s 'The Altruists' Plot Twist: FTX’s Anthropic Shares Would Be Worth $43 Billion

Sam Bankman-Fried had an eventful Thursday—at least on the internet.

Anthropic went public, Netflix released “The Altruists,” and Crypto Twitter imagined the fortune his collapsed exchange might have made by keeping its AI investment.

At Anthropic’s November 19 closing price of $97.17, FTX’s former shares would have been worth $43.28 billion. At the $111.50 opening trade, their theoretical value would have reached $49.66 billion.

That money never belonged personally to Bankman-Fried. The bankruptcy estate sold the shares to repay creditors after FTX’s 2022 collapse.

The former CEO is serving a 25-year sentence for fraud after using FTX customer funds to make investments and cover losses, plus got hit with $11 billion in forfeiture—a fraction of what his single Anthropic investment would have returned today.

A company linked to Alameda, FTX’s sister company, committed $500 million to Anthropic in October 2021. The money converted into roughly 44.54 million preferred shares during the AI startup’s 2022 Series B, about 13.6% of the company.

Bankruptcy administrators sold the position in two batches in 2024 for approximately $1.34 billion. Buyers included an Abu Dhabi-linked fund and Jane Street, Bankman-Fried’s former employer, as filings showed.

At Thursday’s closing price that investment would have produced a total of nearly $43.28 billion—roughly 32 times what creditors’ administrators actually collected.

Sadly, that fortune is hypothetical. Later financing diluted FTX’s percentage ownership, the shares carried restrictions, and the estate needed liquidity to compensate customers who lost money when FTX went belly up. A judge approved the sale in February 2024.

Tiffany Fong, who rose to Twitter fame after interviewing SBF just before he was arrested, shared an AI-generated video of a crying Bankman-Fried drinking in a car alongside the $40 billion-plus calculation. Another account posted a crying-man meme about him watching the listing from prison.

FTX and Alameda’s portfolio extended far beyond Anthropic. A leaked investment spreadsheet showed roughly $5.4 billion allocated to around 500 bets. These are some of the most important ones:

Alameda put $200,000 into Anysphere, maker of the AI coding tool. The estate sold the stake for the same amount in 2023. SpaceX acquired the company at a $60 billion valuation in 2026. If the original holding was about 5%, its undiluted value would be near $3 billion; subsequent dilution makes that an upper-bound mark.

The estate sold roughly 25 million to 30 million locked SOL tokens near $64 each in 2024, Bloomberg reported. At SOL’s January 2025 high of $293.31, that tranche would have been worth a bit less than $9 billion.

Bankman-Fried’s vehicle spent about $648 million on Robinhood shares in 2022. Authorities seized 55.27 million shares, which Robinhood repurchased for $605.7 million in 2023. At the stock’s 2025 intraday high of $153.86, that block would have been worth approximately $8.5 billion.

Alameda’s $1.15 billion Genesis Digital investment would imply around $290 million at a 2026 transaction valuation. By contrast, $40 million in drone maker BRINC could mark near $109 million at a funding estimate, and $20 million in Anchorage Digital about $31 million at its 2026 valuation.

A 461-position reconstruction puts $5.32 billion in total investments at roughly $59.5 billion on today’s valuation for those companies and assets—about $54.2 billion in hypothetical gains. The model assumes all investments were held and imputes values for illiquid positions.

That’s good for a 1,018% return, and proof SBF knew how to pick his bets. But even the best investments can't justify gambling with other people's money without their consent. He made that bet, too—and lost.
