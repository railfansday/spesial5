50 Haunted Buildings That Show Every State Has a Ghost Story

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

From old hotels and historic homes to former prisons, every state has a building with a past and a few lingering ghost stories.

Spooky season is right around the corner, and what better way is there to celebrate than to learn a little history and some fun facts about some of the most haunted places across the country?

Each state is home to unexplainable stories, whether that’s at historic hotels, residences, prisons, and more. Many of the buildings still welcome visitors today, giving anyone interested in old architecture and local lore a chance to see them firsthand.

Learn about famously haunted buildings from every state.

Built in 1837, Selma’s St. James Hotel has spent much of its life welcoming travelers, some of whom say the historic hotel has a few guests who never checked out.

The Red Onion Saloon opened in Skagway in 1898 during the Gold Rush, and stories about former occupants still follow the historic building today.

Built in 1895, the Victorian-era Rosson House is one of Phoenix’s best-preserved historic residences and has inspired local stories about strange sounds and unexplained happenings.

The Crescent Hotel has overlooked Eureka Springs since 1886, and its unusual history has helped make it one of the state’s most famous spots for ghost stories.

Built in San Diego in 1857, the Whaley House served as both a family home and courthouse before becoming known for reports of strange sights and sounds.

The Stanley Hotel opened in Estes Park in 1909, and its grand halls have since become nearly as famous for ghost tales as for overnight stays.

Samuel Clemens and his family moved into Hartford’s Mark Twain House in 1874, and visitors today occasionally report footsteps and other odd happenings inside.

Built in the 1850s on Pea Patch Island, the former Civil War fort has a long history as a military prison and is now a popular setting for stories of unexplained activity.

The famous pink hotel opened in 1928 and is associated with stories about its founder, Thomas Rowe, whose ghost is reportedly seen around the property.

Since opening in Americus in 1892, the Windsor Hotel has hosted generations of guests and gathered its share of ghost tales along the way.

Built in 1882, ʻIolani Palace was the residence of Hawaii’s last ruling monarchs. There are stories about apparitions of Queen Liliʻuokalani and unexplained sounds, including reports of a piano playing.

The Old Idaho Penitentiary opened in Boise in 1870, and its years as a prison left behind plenty of stories about strange noises and shadowy figures.

Opened in 1858, the former prison has a dramatic history and is now open for tours.

This 19th-century inn is associated with the “Blue Lady,” reportedly seen in rooms and hallways and sometimes accompanied by the smell of cherry tobacco.

Built in 1868, the Villisca Axe Murder House became infamous after the 1912 killings there and later gained a reputation for unsettling experiences inside.

The current Eldridge Hotel building opened in Lawrence in 1925, continuing a hotel tradition on the site that stretches back much further and includes several ghost tales.

Louisville’s Waverly Hills Sanatorium opened in 1926, and the former hospital has since become closely associated with stories of strange figures and unexplained sounds.

Built in St. Francisville in 1796, The Myrtles now welcomes overnight guests, many of whom arrive already familiar with the home’s long haunted reputation.

The Kennebunk Inn began as a private home in 1799, and stories about former residents and workers have followed the property through its later life as an inn.

Baltimore’s Lord Baltimore Hotel opened in 1928, and guests over the years have shared stories about unexpected elevator rides, unexplained noises, and unusual sightings.

The Lizzie Borden House, built in Fall River in 1845, became famous after the 1892 murders and now attracts visitors interested in both the case and the home’s ghost stories.

The iconic 1887 hotel has long been associated with reports of apparitions and unexplained activity.

These former sandstone caves date to the 19th century and have accumulated stories of ghostly activity.

King’s Tavern dates to the 1700s and began serving travelers in Natchez in 1789, with ghost tales becoming another chapter in the building’s long history.

The former prison operated for 168 years and is now open for history and paranormal tours. Its enormous cellblocks and long institutional history add to its spookiness.

Fort Benton’s Grand Union Hotel opened in 1882, and its long life on the frontier has produced more than a few stories about strange sounds and sightings.

The Argo Hotel was built in Crofton in 1912 to serve railroad passengers, though today its history also includes stories of unusual encounters reported by guests.

Opened in Tonopah in 1907, the Mizpah Hotel is best known in ghost lore for a mysterious figure commonly called the Lady in Red.

The Mount Washington Hotel opened in Bretton Woods in 1902, and one of its best-known stories involves the supposed appearance of a former owner’s wife.

The 1923 hotel is associated with the “Lady in White,” reportedly seen wandering the hallways and accompanied by stories of singing, laughter and unexplained activity.

The current La Fonda on the Plaza opened in Santa Fe in 1922, continuing a long history of lodging on the site and adding a few ghost stories over the decades.

The Morris-Jumel Mansion, built around 1765, served as George Washington’s headquarters during the Revolutionary War and later became known for reports of ghostly figures.

Asheville’s Grove Park Inn opened in 1913, and its best-known ghost story centers on a mysterious guest remembered simply as the Pink Lady.

The 1880s mansion built by the Marquis de Mores is one of North Dakota’s most distinctive historic buildings. It has also been included in the state’s modern haunted-location lore.

The Ohio State Reformatory opened in Mansfield in 1896, and visitors to the former prison often hear stories about strange sounds and figures inside its cellblocks.

Oklahoma City’s Skirvin Hotel opened in 1911, and a century of guests has produced a steady collection of stories about unusual noises and sightings.

Completed in Portland in 1914, Pittock Mansion now operates as a historic house museum, where visitors have occasionally reported unexplained sounds and movement.

Philadelphia’s Eastern State Penitentiary opened in 1829, and the abandoned prison blocks have inspired ghost stories for decades.

Newport’s Belcourt was completed in 1894, and the Gilded Age mansion has become associated with stories about strange sounds and unexpected figures inside.

Charleston’s Francis Marion Hotel opened in 1924 and has since gathered stories of unexplained footsteps, flickering lights and a mysterious figure said to linger inside.

The Bullock Hotel opened in Deadwood in the 1890s, and local stories claim former owner Seth Bullock still makes the occasional appearance.

Nashville’s Ryman Auditorium opened in 1892, and more than a century of performances has also produced stories about mysterious figures and sounds after the crowds leave.

The Menger Hotel opened beside the Alamo in 1859, and generations of guests have contributed stories about former visitors who supposedly still wander its halls.

Built in Salt Lake City in 1901, the McCune Mansion has served several purposes over the years and has also become known for reports of ghostly figures inside.

The Green Mountain Inn has welcomed guests in Stowe since 1833, though some visitors say footsteps and tapping sounds continue after everyone has settled in for the night.

The 1927 hotel has a long history of celebrity guests and Prohibition-era stories, along with reports of apparitions and unexplained activity.

Built in Port Townsend in 1892, Manresa Castle began as a private home before becoming a hotel with stories about unexplained sounds and sightings.

The Trans-Allegheny Lunatic Asylum began receiving patients in Weston in 1864, and today its tours cover both the former hospital’s history and the ghost stories that followed.

Milwaukee’s Pfister Hotel opened in 1893, and guests over the years have reported unusual sounds and sightings in the historic hotel.

The current Occidental Hotel in Buffalo dates to the early 1900s, and one of its best-known stories involves the ghost of a young girl seen around the property.
