7 Holiday Decor Trends Designers Say We're About to See Everywhere

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The pros are leaning into handmade holiday style.

Pictured above: A holiday-ready space by designer Marie Flanigan.

Christmas is just around the corner, and as the holiday spirit makes its way into our homes, it’s a good time to give your seasonal decor a refresh. This year, on-trend doesn’t have to mean brand-new—designers love decorating with heirloom pieces collected over time.

However, if you’re in the mood to try something a little different, here are seven trends on our radar for a chic Christmas.

This winter, our decor is taking a note from European culture—again. Swedish folk art is emerging as a hot winter trend in both holiday and home decor. Defined by bold red hues, tulip motifs, and S-scrolls, the style blends cozy-core with the heritage style craze.

“European folk influences are so big right now,” says designer Johanna Lyle of Jo Lyle & Co. “Think Swedish Dala horses, quilt motifs, and decorative stitching. I think we will see those continue into holiday decor.”

When it comes to her tree decor, interior designer Francesca Grace waves a hand at the carefully contrived, and she’s not alone. “My ornaments are collected from my travels, so the tree is really a collection of memories. I love unpacking them and remembering where each one came from. They don’t all match, and I wouldn’t want them to,” says Grace. “I let the ornaments lead. That mix feels much more personal to me, and it gives the tree something new to say as the collection grows.” This year’s trends hinge on antiqued, inherited style, with one-off pieces that channel bold character and hardly ever come with a matching set.

Interior designer Marie Flanigan likes to add a fresh twist to her seasonal decor with dried oranges and pops of cranberry red.

“We've seen such a beautiful resurgence of dried citrus in holiday decorating over the past few years,” Flanigan explains. “And I think cranberries are ready for their moment to shine. Their rich red hue feels especially festive, and I love the idea of incorporating them into fresh greenery, draping them across a mantel, or pairing them with dried oranges for a more layered look.”

If there’s one classic Christmas element Grace hopes makes a comeback, it's multicolored lights. Inherently nostalgic, these bold-hued bulbs were a ’90s staple.

“I’m ready for a little less restraint on the tree. Those reds, blues, greens, and yellows immediately bring back the feeling of Christmas as a child. They also pull together a collection of ornaments that might otherwise seem completely unrelated. White lights can be beautiful, but I want my Christmas tree to have some mischief,” says Grace.

The classic red-and-green combination may take the title for the most iconic holiday palette, but this year, color trends are going complementary. While base colors stay warm and slightly rusted, patterned pieces are taking on cooler tones. This year, we’re also trading traditional plaid for Scottish tartan.

“Warm crimsons, rich browns, tactile fabrics, and blue-green plaids make a beautiful holiday palette,” says Elizabeth Bennett, cofounder of the interior design firm Kobel + Co.

In a total rejection of mass-manufactured everything, handmade felt pieces are taking holiday homes by storm. The style has even coined a new holiday aesthetic: #Kitschmas, and, according to Lyle, it’s here to stay. “As AI continues to homogenize design, things made by humans will feel more special and important. Handmade and crafted items create stories around decor that feel meaningful and can be treasured year after year,” she says.

Moody browns are steadily emerging as the new neutral as we inch closer to the new year, and lucky for our winter design schemes, the hue really is as versatile as it seems. Paired with powdery blues, bold crimsons, and even minty greens, chocolate brown grounds nearly any palette and matches every metallic hue.

“I’m particularly drawn to chocolate brown as an unexpected foundation for holiday color palettes. It's incredibly rich and versatile. I love the idea of moving beyond traditional red and green and allowing the existing colors of a room to inform the holiday decor. You can create something incredibly festive through tonal layering, subtle metallic accents, and variations in material,” Flanigan explains.
