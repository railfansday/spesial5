EA hires another former Call of Duty boss to take the lead on all Battlefield Studios' projects

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

EA has made some notable leadership changes within its studios group, and in who oversees the Battlefield franchise. Byron Beede, previously Battlefield's general manager, has been promoted to group general manager.

Beede announced the change on LinkedIn, saying that he now oversees EA's shooter and action-RPG portfolio, seemingly occupying the role of the late Vince Zampella.

In this stead, EA appointed Sam Cooper as Battlefield's new boss. Cooper, who also revealed his new job title on LinkedIn, previously had a stint at EA Sports, but he's mostly known for his work at Activision, where he spent the last five-ish years as Call of Duty's global product management and head of live service.

While this might seem like fairly unremarkable corporate news - executives move between rival companies all the time, after all - it reinforces a narrative that EA continues to keenly replicate Activision's Call of Duty model.

That particular push is years-old now, and it kicked off in 2021 when EA hired Byron Beede, former boss of all of Call of Duty at Activision, to lead the next evolution of Battlefield. So far, that decision has given us the very successful Battlefield 6 and its free-to-play, COD: Warzone-like battle royale mode Redsec.

To facilitate that, EA rearranged several of its studios to serve under one banner dubbed Battlefield Studios, replicating Activision's model of locking several teams around the world into developing and supporting a single game / franchise. Ripple Effect, Motive, the iconic Criterion and, of course, series creator DICE are all the Battlefield franchise's lead and support studios.

Many players argue - and I would agree - that Battlefield and Call of Duty games are more alike today than they've ever been. The user interface, the emphasis on limited-time events, the garish store cosmetics, the COD-like battle pass system - complete with a more expensive option, are all signs of that transition. And that is to say nothing of controversial changes to longstanding Battlefield tenets, such as the class system, intended to make Battlefield 6 appeal to a wider audience.

The effects of that leadership change likely won't materialise until the next Battlefield title, but it's a curious one to keep in mind as EA leans even harder into its biggest franchises following its $55bn private equity and Saudi Arabia's PIF-funded buyout.
