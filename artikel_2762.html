Las Vegas Raiders-Denver Broncos FEED Topic: Kolton Miller, DJ Glaze vs. Nik Bonitto, Jonathon Cooper

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Seattle Seahawks managed to get six sacks on Fernando Mendoza in his starting debut last week, and pressure on the quarterback will be another major key to the game for the Las Vegas Raiders' offense in Week 11. The Denver Broncos present the same problem but in a different way than Seattle, one that will place a greater emphasis on the Raiders' offensive line, especially tackles Kolton Miller and DJ Glaze.

Led by head coach Mike Macdonald, the Seahawks defense uses a creative scheme to help manufacture pressure and create confusion for opposing quarterbacks. To a certain extent, they're able to work around or neutralize the offensive line by attacking running backs in pass protection or forcing quarterbacks to account for free rushers by throwing hot. Last week was a perfect example, as top cornerback Devon Witherspoon led the team with two of the six sacks.

Meanwhile, Broncos defensive coordinator Vance Joseph isn't afraid to blitz, but that's to help create one-on-one opportunities for their defensive linemen, especially edge rushers Nik Bonitto and Jonathon Cooper.

Bonitto is coming off back-to-back Pro Bowl seasons, where he registered 27.5 sacks and 133 pressures, per Pro Football Focus, in 2024 and 2025 combined. This season, he's recorded 4.5 sacks in nine games and ranks tied for 19th among edges with 30 pressures.

Meanwhile, Cooper notched 18.5 sacks and 112 pressures in the two previous seasons to give the Broncos a quality No. 2 pass-rusher on the edge. In 2026, he's only participated in three games due to off-field issues in the offseason and has just 10 pressures and 0.5 sacks on 77 pass-rush snaps. So, Cooper's numbers are down as a consequence of his actions, but his track record on the field speaks for itself.

On the other side of the trenches, Miller has an impressive resume in pass protection but has been regressing this year. With 32 pressures on 379 opportunities, per PFF, his 8.4 percent pressure rate is the highest it's been since the 2018 first-round pick's rookie year (11.0 percent). But Glaze is trending in the opposite direction, going from 8.3 percent and 8.0 percent pressure rates in his first two seasons, respectively, to 4.0 percent (15 pressures on 379 opportunities) this year.

With the Raiders shifting from Kirk Cousins to Mendoza last week and seeing their playoff hopes rapidly decline over the last roughly two months, we've reached the point in the season where it's time to start looking at building for the future. Part of that is making sure the rookie quarterback can stay upright enough to make some plays and build confidence heading into next year. The other part is evaluating the team's needs before the offseason gets going.

It would be remiss to talk about the Broncos' pass rush and not mention Zach Allen, who currently leads all defensive tackles with 38 pressures and has 4.5 sacks. However, it feels like a foregone conclusion that Las Vegas will have to address its guard situation this spring. Meanwhile, offensive tackle is still up in the air, and this weekend will be a good litmus test for Miller and Glaze.

Is the veteran left tackle past his prime, where it's time to find a new blind side protector? Is the third-year pro's growth legitimate? How they fare against one of the NFL's best edge-rush duos over the last few years, Bonitto and Cooper, on Sunday will go a long way toward answering those questions.

So, for this week's game day discussion on The FEED, we'll be keeping tabs on how well Miller and Glaze can protect the edges to keep Mendoza clean in Denver. The game kicks off at 1:25 p.m. PST and will air on CBS (check your local listings). I look forward to talkin' some ball with y'all!
