Lauren Conrad’s Laguna Beach Mansion Lists for $26.5 Million—Plus 5 More Luxury Homes for Sale

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

For many Millennials, MTV’s Laguna Beach occupies a sacred place in the cultural canon. The reality show, which aired for three seasons in the mid-aughts, centered around an upscale Southern California enclave, where high school cool girl Lauren “L.C.” Conrad and her clique of telegenic friends had pool parties, bonfires—and plenty of drama. The backdrop for much of the series was Conrad’s opulent family residence, a blufftop Mediterranean-style compound designed by her architect father, James Conrad. Though the Conrads sold the 11-acre estate in 2008, it just hit the market again for the first time since then, priced at $26.5 million.

In other real estate news, the former home of screen legend Dorothy Dandridge, the first Black woman nominated for Best Actress at the Academy Awards, is now on the market for $5.5 million. And in New York’s Hudson Valley, the classical-infused retreat that ELLE Decor A-List architect Gil Schafer crafted for his own family is also available for $6.5 million. Check them all out below.

This house needs no introduction for fans of MTV’s hit reality show Laguna Beach. The six-bedroom mansion is where series star Lauren “L.C.” Conrad lived with her family, including dad James Conrad, the architect who designed the residence in 2004. Inside the 8,000-square-foot home are travertine floors, wood-beamed ceilings, and stone fireplaces, while the grounds come with an expansive pool court and sweeping Pacific Ocean vistas. When L.C. moved to Los Angeles to star in The Hills, her parents parted with the 11-acre estate, selling it for a reported $11.9 million in 2008. It’s now listed for $26.5 million with Sotheby’s broker Richard Trey Dewey.

ELLE Decor A-List talent Gil Schafer of the firm Schafer & Company is known for infusing stately homes with classical elegance, drawing from architecture styles of a bygone era to create residences that look like they’ve been standing for generations. For his own house in New York’s Hudson Valley, Schafer took cues from Greek Revival farmhouses to create a modern sanctuary with an old soul. Dubbed Middlefield, the 4,000-square-foot manor sits on 45 acres designed by another ELLE Decor A-Lister, landscape architect Deborah Nevins. Schafer parted with the property just last year for $6.18 million, nearly double his asking price. It’s now back on the market, listed with Ashley Whittaker of Compass for $6.5 million.

Trailblazing movie star Dorothy Dandridge made history as the first Black woman to be nominated for a Best Actress Oscar, for 1954’s Carmen Jones. Now, Ricarda Olander of Christie’s is selling Dandridge’s former Los Angeles residence for $5.5 million. The 1920s Mediterranean-style home spans roughly 4,400 square feet and comes with four bedrooms, five baths, and a series of outdoor areas designed for entertaining.

Perched atop a cast-iron SoHo landmark built in the 1870s, the glass-walled penthouse owned by soccer star Thierry Henry and model Andrea Rajacic just hit the market for $25 million. It’s the second time the couple has tried offloading the sprawling five-bedroom home, which they first put on the market in February 2025 for $26.75 million. Now listed with Sotheby’s broker Joshua Judge, the 5,600-square-foot condo has 4,600 square feet of private outdoor space thanks to a network of terraces across four different floors. Other perks include 14-foot ceilings, two deeded parking spaces, and an indoor bamboo garden.

From a one-bedroom cabin in rural Tennessee to a 63-acre Nashville compound, Dolly Parton’s homes reflected her humble beginnings and subsequent rise to the top of the entertainment industry. In the mid-1980s, a few years after the country music legend starred in her first Hollywood film, 9 to 5, she and her husband Carl Dean bought a property in Southern California. Now, just weeks after Parton’s death at age 80, their Los Angeles estate, which was rebuilt in 2009, according to the listing, just hit the market for $8 million.

Add this to your “Wi$h Li$t.” Taylor Swift’s former Beverly Hills bungalow just hit the market for nearly $8 million—and it’s brimming with pop star lore. Swift acquired the mini-compound in 2011, fresh off releasing her third album, Speak Now, when she was just 22 years old. At the time, she had a pair of Nashville residences to her name, though her property empire has since expanded exponentially.
