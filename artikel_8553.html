Selling your old phone? Get instant offers from buyback sites, then check what the same model sells for on a marketplace like Swappa, where selling it yourself usually pays more. Before it leaves your hands, back it up, sign out of your Apple or Google account, and factory reset it.

Deleting your photos isn’t enough. The Federal Trade Commission says to back up the phone, take out the SIM card and any memory card, and then reset it to factory settings, which erases your contacts, texts, photos, and browsing history.

Sign out of your Apple Account or Google account before that reset. On an iPhone, Apple’s own steps have you sign out first, and the erase then turns off Find My and Activation Lock, the lock that keeps anyone else from setting up your phone. On Android, Google says removing your account turns off the protection that would otherwise stop the next owner from setting up the phone.

Save that wipe for last, after you’ve compared offers and picked a buyer. A buyback site, kiosk, or trade-in pays a quoted price within days or on the spot, while selling the phone yourself takes photos, messages, and shipping but usually pays more.

Swappa’s own numbers show the size of that gap. The 128GB iPhone 13 sold there for $234 on average in its November figures, while the best trade-in value it lists for one is $134, for an unlocked phone. After Swappa’s 3% seller fee and PayPal’s processing fee, a sale at that price still nets about $80 more.
