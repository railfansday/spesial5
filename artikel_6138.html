This year’s gas prices are high enough to dent Thanksgiving travel plans, according to new research from AAA.

Higher fuel prices are pushing more travelers to stay home this Thanksgiving, according to a new report from AAA.

The auto club projects 81.5 million Americans to travel at least 50 miles from home between November 24 and 30, down 0.1% from 81.6 million last year.

It’s a small dip, but excluding 2020, it’s the first time since the 2008 recession that AAA has reported a decline in its Thanksgiving travel projection. (Older projections measured a shorter travel window and are not directly comparable.)

AAA projects 81.5 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles from home between November 24 and 30, down 0.1% from 81.6 million last year — and, excluding 2020, the first decline in AAA's Thanksgiving projection since the 2008 recession. Higher fuel and airfare costs are the pressure points: the national average gas price is near $4 a gallon, a level AAA says has never been exceeded on Thanksgiving, while Hopper puts average round-trip domestic Thanksgiving airfare at $402, up 31% year over year. AAA, whose members skew older and more affluent, reports average round-trip tickets booked by members at $800, up 17%. Car trips are forecast to fall 0.3% and domestic air travelers 0.2%, while trains, buses, and cruises gain about 4%. AAA spokesperson Aixa Diaz said the decline was smaller than the auto club anticipated, with nearly a quarter of the U.S. population still planning to travel. Bank of America Institute has tracked robust cruise spending and renewed strength in hotel spending growth this year, but said in a November 9 report that fewer travelers are trading down from premium to lower-cost options, which — alongside slowing airline transaction growth — could signal that more budget travelers are giving up trips entirely.
