Manchester United owe other clubs £416m in outstanding transfer fees for their signings in recent years.

United’s financial figures show the amounts they still have to pay after recent spending sprees, which will be a factor in their finances for years to come.

The Premier League club, who are themselves owed £75m by other clubs for players they sold, have also confirmed the cost of paying new head coach Mauricio Pochettino’s buyout clause was £7.58m.

United committed a minimum of £148m in the summer to new signings Youri Tielemans, Andrey Santos and Carlos Baleba after agreeing deals worth more than £200m in 2025 – for Bryan Mbeumo, Benjamin Sesko, Matheus Cunha and Senne Lammens – and 2024, when they signed Matthijs de Ligt, Leny Yoro, Manuel Ugarte, Joshua Zirkzee and Noussair Mazraoui.

With fees staggered over the course of their contracts, United are still paying for purchases made by former managers Michael Carrick, Ruben Amorim and Erik ten Hag.

According to the club’s first-quarter financial report, they will have to pay £235m in the next year and a further £114m in the next year, with the remainder over up to five years.

To offset that a little, United will receive £27m from other clubs in the next year.

United’s long-term net debt stands at £577m, while as of 30 September, they owed a further £207m in a revolving credit facility.
