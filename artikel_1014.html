Michael Carrick’s future as Manchester United manager is in severe doubt after overseeing the club’s worst start to the Premier League season in 40 years that has left them in the relegation zone, with Mauricio Pochettino looking set to replace him.

A second Old Trafford comeback of the week, this time battling from two goals down twice to rescue a 4-4 draw with Aston Villa on Sunday, might not be enough to save Carrick’s job and he is at risk of being sacked after just 10 games of the season and with his side 18th during the international break.

United ended a nine-game winless run when they came from two goals down to beat Roma in the Champions League last week but they have not won in eight attempts in the Premier League and have just one top-flight win this campaign.

Carrick survived crisis talks with the United hierarchy after defeats to Como and Bournemouth last month and was given time to turn results around, but speculation over a change begun the day after Sunday’s chaotic draw and The Independent understands contact was initially made with United States boss Pochettino over succeeding Carrick on Monday.

The Old Trafford hierarchy, including Jason Wilcox, Omar Berrarda and Sir Jim Ratcliffe, are set to meet in Monaco today to discuss Carrick’s future as the club are now close to agreeing a deal with Pochettino should they decide to move on from the current boss.

Michael Carrick said he still expects to be working with Manchester United over the international break and insisted he will not walk away.

United sit 18th in the table and they are now eight league games without a win – a club record in the Premier League era – with their only victory of the season back in August against newly-promoted Ipswich.

Asked on Sky Sports if he would quit if he felt it was the right thing to do for the club, Carrick said: “That’s not going to happen.”

Then, speaking in his post-match press conference, Carrick expressed confidence his side could still turn things around this season.

“One hundred per cent,” he said. “I think there’s more than enough. Goals change games and we’ve got to deal with it and it’s something we have to improve on drastically. But there’s more than enough, we’re just making it too difficult for ourselves at the moment in certain times…

“I’m quite clear with what direction the club needs to go in. We’ve just got to win games.”

Man United could break the £100m barrier for spending on hiring and firing managers since Sir Alex Ferguson’s exit in 2013.

To sack Michael Carrick, a estimated sum of £5m is expected, while a $7m release clause exists in Mauricio Pochettino’s contract, though that figure could rise to £7.4m total, when including assistant coaches and support staff under Pochettino at US Soccer, reports the Telegraph.

United’s dramatic comeback against Aston Villa raises more questions around the direction of the club, explains Miguel Delaney, with Carrick’s demise exposing a more worrying truth about the club.

Gary Neville has claimed the Manchester United “don't have a clue what to do” as they consider the future of Michael Carrick over the international break.

"Michael could leave, Michael might stay, we don't know, the ownership don't have a clue what to do,” Neville said on NBC.

"None of it has worked because if you recruit badly, if you've got poor performance department, poor recruitment department, you've not got leadership that is strong right through the spine of the football club you will fail.

“Every organisation in the world will fail if you've not got an aligned leadership from top to bottom - your board and your ownership right the way through to the person who is stood on the pitch trying to play football.

"There are real issues within that football club, you've got people doing those jobs who have never done those jobs before in senior positions."

The United hierarchy, including chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox, met minority owner Jim Ratcliffe in Monaco today in a planned meeting to discuss United’s start to the season.

The direction of the club, and whether to stick or twist with beleaguered manager Michael Carrick, will have been top of the agenda.

The Independent’s Chief Football Writer Miguel Delaney reports that Manchester United are close to an agreement with Mauricio Pochettino to become their next manager, with Carrick expected to make way.

For his part, Carrick took training as usual on Tuesday and Wednesday.

Mauricio Pochettino is widely expected to be announced as new Man United manager, but the club do face a problem getting the Argentine out of his United States contract.

United must strike a deal with US Soccer, with a solution required over the United States’ next two international games in particular, while the club’s hierarchy is meeting at a Monaco summit to finalise a decision over the club’s manager.

Compensation, worth an initial $7m (£5.3m), will be a formality due to the clause in Pochettino’s existing contract, that runs until the 2030 World Cup.

While additional compensation will be required for Pochettino’s trusted coaching staff, including long-time assistant Jesus Perez.

The United States face Concacaf Nations League fixtures against Haiti during the current international break, with an away match on 14 November and a home match in Cincinnati on 18 November.

US Soccer confirmed that planned player media access on Wednesday was called off in observance of Veterans Day, removing any opportunity for Pochettino’s squad to be asked about reports regarding his prospective departure.

The former Tottenham, Paris St Germain and Chelsea boss signed a contract extension to stay on as USMNT head coach until the 2030 World Cup only three months ago, meaning US Soccer would be owed compensation if he agrees to join United.

Pochettino has spoken with United regarding the post several times over the years, including in 2022 when the club chose to hire Erik ten Hag instead.

Peter Schmeichel has accused Manchester United of "disrespectful" treatment towards Michael Carrick as rumours linking Mauricio Pochettino to the club persist.

Former United keeper Schmeichel believes the root problems lie above Carrick, arguing the head coach should be allowed to sign players in the January window.

"I absolutely do feel sympathy for him. I think the way this is playing out is very disrespectful to him," Schmeichel told The Overlap Daily.

"There are so many things that have been going on at Manchester United literally since Sir Alex (Ferguson) and David Gill left the club. And it’s never been corrected. It’s not been the correct people, not the right people in charge, and slowly it’s just gone worse.”
