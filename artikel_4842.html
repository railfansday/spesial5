I Am Turning 40, and Refusing to Apologize for It

Posted November 12, 2026 | Reviewed by Michelle Quirk

Yes, I celebrate the month. (Don’t @ me!) It was actually something I started doing in college. My birthday (the actual day) falls close to Thanksgiving, so many of my friends would be leaving campus right around the time I was getting ready to celebrate. I also had a roommate whose birthday was close to Christmas, so we started celebrating early.

The trend caught on (whether it caught on or I pushed it, I will never know). This worked well until my 21st birthday, which, as we all know, cannot legally be celebrated early—at least not if you want your special bar drink in hand.

If you’re rolling your eyes and thinking I appear a bit arrogant, bear with me for a moment while I explain. While celebrating and having fun with friends was, of course, fun, these celebrations actually served a deeper purpose for me—although I didn't know it at the time. When college ended, I found myself alone again, thrown back into a world where my only source of support—my family—was unsafe and unstable. Home was scary.

Celebrating myself became a way of honoring my own journey. It was a way of recognizing the strength it took to navigate the year, and the safety I was learning to create within myself. It became a "Yay, Kaytee, you made it another year. You did it." Years later, I still have that same message, but it has become something deeper.

This was almost 20 years ago. This month I turn 40. An age where we, especially women, are told to stop celebrating. An age we are taught to dread. All month, I’ve received messages like these from others, including my own family:

“I bet you’re not excited about this birthday.”

"I was going to send you a card, but I imagine you don't want the reminder."

“I bet you won’t celebrate the whole month anymore.”

And I can’t help but wonder: Why? Why are we so negative about aging? We mourn when people die young, yet somehow shame those of us who are fortunate enough to keep living. We grieve the years someone never got to experience, while simultaneously treating the years we do get as something to fear.

I refuse to participate in this ageism. I have always celebrated my birthday, and I always will. Not just in the traditional, celebratory sense (I mean, cake is always nice), but as a way of honoring myself and all that I have become. I am always stumped at how much it bothers people that I dare embrace myself as an aging woman.

I am proud of who I am today. I love myself more today than I ever did when I was young. I no longer self-medicate or mistreat my body in the ways I had to to survive my childhood. I am happier and healthier now than I ever have been before. I have come so far and learned so much since that 20-year-old college student who celebrated her birthday with her roommates for a week straight, in one of the few places where she felt safe (but was unable to verbalize this at the time). I wish I could go back and tell her that someday it won't be so scary, and she won't have to put on a fake persona of strength to continue the denial. I would tell her not only that she would survive, but how much she would survive, and how much of a life she would eventually build for herself. I think she would be pretty damn proud, too. (And maybe a bit relieved to see a light at the end of what felt like a long, dark tunnel.)

Happy birthday month to me. And to all of us who have survived and are surviving. I hope you find a way to honor and celebrate yourself, too.
