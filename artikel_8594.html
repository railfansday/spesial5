Unused Bank Accounts Go to the State – Move Money Yourself Once a Year

An unused bank account can lose money to inactivity fees, and after three to five years without activity from you, the bank must turn the balance over to your state as unclaimed property. Make a deposit or withdrawal yourself at least once a year, or move the money to an account you use and close the old account.

The accounts at risk are the ones you rarely think about, like a savings account from your first job, a second checking account, or a child’s account no one checks.

An account can earn interest every month and still count as inactive, because banks and state law look for activity you start, such as a deposit, a withdrawal, or contact with the bank. Some states also count a login, and some don’t count a recurring transfer you set up, so make the deposit or withdrawal yourself.

Until the money goes to the state, the bank can also charge a recurring inactivity fee if your account agreement allows it, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency notes. Say a $1,200 savings account from your first job earns no interest and charges $5 a month after a year without activity. In a state that waits three years, $120 in fees comes out before the bank sends the remaining $1,080 to the state. In one that waits five, the fees reach $240 and the state gets $960.

The state holds that money for you until you claim it. To find an old account that may already be there, search every state you’ve lived in.

An account you don’t need is better closed. Move its balance to the account you use, then close the old account, so the bank can’t charge it a fee or send it to the state.

For an account you keep, update your address with the bank. Before the money goes to the state, the bank usually has to try to reach you, and some banks mail that notice to the last address they have. Then look up in the account’s fee schedule how long it can go without activity before an inactivity fee starts.

Set a calendar reminder to make a deposit or withdrawal yourself before that point and at least once a year, even $5 moved to your main account.
