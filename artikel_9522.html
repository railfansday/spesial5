An Optimizely vulnerability put malicious code into scripts used by major websites. The public record now includes a direct Zoom security notice, a Salesforce advisory, an Optimizely incident statement and responses from Equifax and IKEA to Reuters. That is evidence of company acknowledgment, not proof that every website visitor was notified.

On November 16, attackers used a vulnerability in Optimizely Experimentation to add malicious JavaScript to customer projects. The code was served through legitimate Optimizely snippets on prominent websites. This is not evidence that attackers separately penetrated the networks of all 178 organizations.

Report URI primary investigation | Independent BleepingComputer coverage

The payload changed valid wallet addresses in page content, clipboard interactions and some outgoing requests. Researchers traced roughly $7,040 in receipts to identified wallets by November 16, but the total attributable loss remains uncertain.

At 13:43 UTC, the attacker added code designed to capture card numbers, expiry dates and CVVs entered into page-accessible forms. Third-party isolated payment iframes were not directly accessible.

On certain older iOS versions, the malware attempted to deliver Coruna and DarkSword exploit chains. No confirmed number of successful device compromises is publicly established.

The response: what companies actually said

Optimizely publicly acknowledged that an unauthorized party exploited a vulnerability in Experimentation and added malicious JavaScript to customer projects. The vendor said it fixed the vulnerability and removed injected code. Original vendor notice

Zoom also published a direct notice on its Security Bulletins page: it acknowledged the Optimizely incident, said it disabled Optimizely across production environments as a precaution, and said it had not identified an impact on customer data at that time. Primary source: https://www.zoom.com/en/trust/security-bulletin/

Reuters obtained responses from Equifax (it stopped using Optimizely and found no evidence its own systems were compromised) and IKEA (it was assessing potential impact). These are statements to a reporter, not evidence of visitor notification. Reporting methodology for the 178-entry register

What the public record shows, as of November 17

The public record establishes responses from Optimizely, Salesforce, Zoom, Equifax and IKEA. Optimizely says it notified affected business customers; it is not established how many of the 178 organizations notified their own website visitors. The remaining companies must not be described as silent or non-notifying merely because a public notice was not found.

Cynet described an endpoint detection that traced attacker connections to an Optimizely-served script, with CNN and Zoom examples. StepSecurity analyzed the crypto clipper and card skimmer. SOCRadar published a separate attack walkthrough. SecurityWeek and The Hacker News reported the vendor response and technical timeline. These are different evidence types; syndications of the same Reuters dispatch should count as one source.

Independent security corroboration beyond Report URI

Reuters reported that Zoom, HSBC and CNN had not immediately responded to requests for comment at the time of its initial report. A nonresponse to Reuters is not proof the companies did not privately contact customers. Their social posts and status channels need continued checking.

Zoom, HSBC and CNN: Reuters requested comment

Reuters reported that IKEA was assessing potential effects on its digital services and customers. This is an on-record response through Reuters, not evidence of customer notification.

In an updated Reuters dispatch, Equifax said it had stopped using Optimizely and found no evidence that its systems were compromised. This is a company response conveyed by a news agency, not an independently located Equifax social post or direct consumer notice. The distinction is essential.

Salesforce posted an original security advisory acknowledging unauthorized changes to Optimizely scripts on certain Salesforce-branded sites and said it was disabling those scripts. Salesforce said it would notify affected customers if it identified unauthorized access to their data. That is conditional notification language, not confirmation of individual visitor notifications.

Optimizely posted a statement on its official LinkedIn company page acknowledging exploitation of the Experimentation signup flow, removal of injected code, revoked tokens and reset access. It explicitly said it had notified all affected Optimizely customers. That means the vendor says it notified its business customers; it does not prove that those businesses notified their website visitors.

A social-media post, a status notice, a spokesperson response, and a customer notification are four different kinds of evidence. Only the last establishes that customers were directly informed. The following reporting trails add distinct sources rather than 178 copies of one research article.
