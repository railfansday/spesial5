WeRoad is entering South Korea without selling a single trip directly, letting NOL own the customer. NOL handles bookings, payments and customer service, while WeRoad runs the trips. If it works, it could become the template for how Western tour operators get into Asia.

Adventure travel company WeRoad is entering South Korea through a partnership with travel and leisure platform NOL Universe, using the platform to reach younger travelers as it expands beyond its established European and English-speaking markets.

The partnership will let South Korean travelers book WeRoad’s small-group trips through Yanolja-owned NOL’s app and website. The first seven itineraries will go live in early December, with departures starting in 2027.

Direct sales remain Italy-headquartered WeRoad’s biggest revenue channel globally, but the company is taking a different route in South Korea. It is letting NOL manage the customer relationship rather than setting up its own sales operation.

“We might leave the South Korea market covered by the partnership with NOL and never go direct,” Andrea Lamperini, chief strategy officer at WeRoad, told Skift.

WeRoad is entering South Korea through a partnership with NOL Universe, the Yanolja-owned travel and leisure superapp, rather than building its own sales operation there. South Korean travelers will be able to book WeRoad's small-group trips through NOL's app and website, with the first seven itineraries going live in early December and departures starting in 2027. NOL owns the customer relationship before and after the trip, takes the booking and payment, and handles customer service in Korean, WeRoad Chief Strategy Officer Andrea Lamperini said. Direct sales remain WeRoad's biggest revenue channel globally, but Lamperini said the company might leave South Korea covered by the NOL partnership and never go direct. The deal gives WeRoad access to NOL's roughly 5.6 million monthly active users and is its first partnership with an East Asian company and with a superapp rather than a traditional travel agency. NOL Chief Business Officer for Global Oliver Libutzki said the company chose to partner with a top-tier player rather than build its own group travel product, citing increased Korean demand for short, multi-day tour packages sold as modular activities without flights. WeRoad is now looking at similar partnerships for 2027, with interest from potential partners elsewhere in Asia and in Australia.
