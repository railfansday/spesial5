An inspiral of a star inside a common envelope with its giant companion

Nature volume 659, pages 628–633 (2026) Cite this article

The formation of compact stellar binaries requires rapid orbital tightening of their progenitor binary stars1,2. This is theorized to happen through common envelope evolution (CEE)3, when the stars orbit in a loosely bound, shared convective envelope, triggering an inspiral due to frictional drag4,5. However, this inspiral in such a system has never been observed directly. Here we present observations of AT 2025abao, a transient eruption in the Andromeda Galaxy, which originates from the CEE of an approximately 7M⊙ asymptotic giant branch (AGB) star with its companion. Extensive pre- and post-eruption spectroscopy is best explained as an approximately 0.7M⊙ companion inspiralling within the convective AGB envelope that powers a seven-year period of gradual brighteningone of the first direct observations of frictional drag energy dissipation induced by CEE. This phase is radiatively efficient, consistent with predictions for convective transport in CEE5,6,7,8. The subsequent merger of the companion with the primary core powers the ejection of the outermost 10% of the stellar envelope at speeds about 10× the primary escape speed, and empirically constrains the fraction of dissipated orbital energy available to unbind the convective common envelope4,9,10,11, 0.01 ≲ α ≲ 0.4. This end-to-end characterization using multi-survey observations demonstrates a path towards mapping the landscape of CEE outcomes with upcoming surveys.

a, The quiescent binary system comprising a  ≈ 7 M⊙ AGB and a  ≈ 0.7 M⊙ star, surrounded by an extended dust shell from mass loss from the binary following Roche Lobe overflow of the primary. b, The precursor brightening phase that began in 2018, marking the onset of CEE, powered by frictional drag on the inspiraling companion lasting for  ≈ 7 years.
