One home AI feature has emerged as highly controversial in 2026, and it’s a video doorbell/security cam capability to scan and recognize faces. As a series of recent lawsuits against companies like Ring and Nest allege, facial recognition can scan and save facial data from people who can’t consent. That includes delivery people, strangers passing by on the street at the right angle, distant family members visiting and even your own children.

If you want to prevent your app (and company) from collecting facial data on people, it’s important to double-check that any “familiar faces” technology in the settings is turned off. You usually have to opt into more advanced AI features like this, and they usually require a subscription fee to enable, so it’s unlikely to start gathering data by accident.

Technically, familiar-face technology can be used to automatically unlock a smart lock when the right person approaches, or only alert you if a security camera recognizes an unfamiliar face. But smart homes offer so many alternatives to these tricks, it’s really not necessary for the average user.
