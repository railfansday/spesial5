Prediction: These Will Be 3 of the Best-Performing Financial Stocks of 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The strongest financial stocks of 2027 may come from three very different corners of the industry.

What does it take for a financial stock to succeed? Well, it all depends on the stock and the company behind it. A trading platform needs active investors. A brokerage needs a customer base that trusts it with more of their hard-earned savings. A digital bank needs loyal customers who remain loyal after their initial deposit.

Robinhood (HOOD +2.19%), Charles Schwab (SCHW +0.64%), and SoFi (SOFI +1.02%) each have a path toward a strong 2027. However, how these stocks perform next year will largely depend on what happens in the coming months.

Imagine a new investor. Someone excited about increasing their savings through investing. They download the Robinhood app and plan their first trade. Robinhood's challenge is not only to attract more of these customers, but to keep them active after the excitement of the first few trades wears off.

For 2027 to truly be Robinhood's year, the company will need to build on this encouraging progress and show that revenue growth isn't simply dependent on market excitement. Risks for 2027 include a trading slowdown, weakness in the crypto market, and new regulatory measures.

Overall, with a price-to-sales (P/S) ratio of more than 15, Robinhood's stock remains more highly valued than its five-year average P/S multiple. However, it is far below its five-year high of 35, reached in late 2025. In other words, Robinhood stock might not be cheap, but it's certainly been more expensive. If the company can build on its business momentum during the next 14 months, 2027 could be a banner year for the company and the stock.

To understand Charles Schwab, think of a megamart. Once consumers enter, there are multiple sales opportunities. In the same way that a big-box store might first attract a customer to buy groceries and then sell them a television, Charles Schwab can offer brokerage customers more than just access to trading markets. The company is positioned to parlay its brokerage business into lending, advisory, and other services. In 2027, Schwab's task is to turn those deeper relationships into higher revenue and greater profits.

For example, the company reported $7.2 billion in third-quarter revenue, up 17% from a year earlier. Net income rose even faster, up 23% to $2.9 billion. As CFO Mike Verdeschi said, "Deeper client engagement continues to broaden and strengthen our revenue model." In other words, Schwab has found that expanding the ways it serves clients has created more opportunities to earn their business.

Looking ahead to 2027, the plan is simple: Schwab must continue executing its strategy to expand its business and deliver additional services to existing clients, driving up revenue and profitability. The biggest risk to this plan is a market decline, which would reduce the value of customers' assets and perhaps force customers into more risk-averse assets (e.g., cash), hurting Schwab's profitability.

Much like Robinhood and Charles Schwab, SoFi has a similar game plan for 2027. It all comes down to relationship building. A customer might first encounter SoFi through a lending relationship, but the company doesn't want things to stop there. As we've already seen, a larger customer base becomes more valuable not simply because it is larger -- it can also become more profitable as the company sells additional products to each existing customer.

Take Chief Executive Officer Anthony Noto's recent commentary from the company's third-quarter earnings release: "Our focus is simple. We are adding more members, who are using more products, and generating more revenue per product to create the most value for our shareholders over the long run." The numbers back up his optimism. SoFi's products-per-member figure increased to 1.56, up from 1.47 one year ago. Total revenue climbed by 44% to $1.39 billion, and net income soared 73% to $241 million.

Granted, not every new customer relationship results in additional product sales, and SoFi faces risks. In particular, a weakening economy or rising inflation could affect borrowers' ability to repay loans. That would be bad news for SoFi, as customers missing loan payments would eat into the company's profits.

To recap, each of these three financial stocks could soar in 2027 if they execute their strategic plan and market conditions are favorable. Robinhood's goal is to gain the loyalty of its fast-growing customer base; Schwab needs to earn more from its existing customer base; and SoFi's challenge is to build relationships across its business offerings.

On a valuation basis, SoFi stands out as the most affordable at a 3.9 P/S, while Robinhood's 15.3 multiple is the highest.

Much remains uncertain about 2027. However, each of these three companies has demonstrated solid strategies, built around a proven business model. If they can continue to execute, this trio might top the list of 2027's best financial stocks.
