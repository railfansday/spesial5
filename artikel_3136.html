BigBear.ai vs. Innodata: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

One is built on federal and defense contracts, the other on training AI models for big tech, and their financial profiles diverge sharply.

By Sara Appino – Updated Nov 20, 2026 at 5:29PM EST

10 stocks we like better than BigBear.ai ›

Choosing between these specialized AI plays requires balancing high-stakes government work against commercial data engineering growth. Investors must decide if BigBear.ai (BBAI -8.67%) or Innodata (INOD -8.91%) better suits their portfolio needs.

BigBear.ai provides complex decision intelligence tools for federal and defense agencies, while Innodata helps major tech firms train generative AI systems. Both companies operate in the rapidly evolving world of artificial intelligence but serve different markets. This comparison examines their financial health, customer concentration, and valuation to help you decide which stock fits your strategy.

BigBear.ai offers decision intelligence solutions that combine data from different sources to help leaders make faster choices. It serves critical sectors like national security, cybersecurity, and global supply chains. According to its latest annual report, a specific contract accounted for nearly 51% of consolidated revenue. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $142.1 million. This represented a revenue growth decline of approximately 1.5% compared to the prior period. The company reported a net loss of roughly $180.2 million during this same time frame.

Following its early 2026 debt conversion, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.0x, meaning the company carries virtually no debt relative to its equity. The current ratio, which measures the ability to pay short-term debts with short-term assets, is close to 1.8x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was negative at nearly $46.3 million.

Innodata operates as a global data engineering firm that prepares and evaluates data for large-scale AI systems. The company serves several high-profile technology leaders, with one customer in its digital data solutions segment accounting for nearly 58% of total revenue in 2025. This focus on the best small cap tech stocks in the data sector highlights its role in the generative AI boom.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $350.2 million. This reflects a revenue growth increase of nearly 46.9% over the previous year. The company also achieved a net income of roughly $48.5 million during this period.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio is approximately 2.7x. The debt-to-equity ratio is 0.0x, indicating a strong balance sheet with no total debt. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was nearly $35.6 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 23.8% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

BigBear.ai faces significant risk due to heavy revenue concentration with government customers who can terminate contracts for convenience. Scaling its sales efforts remains a challenge while managing long and unpredictable sales cycles. It also faces intense competition from Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%) and Amazon (AMZN -4.10%). The company also navigates complex AI deployment risks and recent financial volatility including litigation over accounting errors.

Innodata relies heavily on a single customer that contributed nearly 58% of its 2025 revenues, making it vulnerable to contract changes. It competes against well-resourced firms like Accenture (ACN +1.19%) and Cognizant Technology Solutions (CTSH +3.11%) in the data engineering space. The business also deals with geopolitical risks from its global workforce and ongoing legal challenges regarding securities class actions.

Innodata appears to offer a more attractive value proposition based on its lower P/S ratio, which measures market value against sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

I'd go with Innodata. AI models are only as good as the data they learn from, and Innodata helps big tech companies create and test that data. Its sales have grown year over year for 13 straight quarters, and it makes money.

Last quarter, sales jumped 53% and management raised its growth outlook, though the stock has fallen sharply this fall. There are risks: Two customers make up most of its sales, margins narrowed, and one program is winding down. But Innodata is adding new customers and work from a large cloud company, and expects to replace the lost program by early next year.

To give BigBear.ai its due, it does high-stakes work for national security and border security customers with contracts that are difficult to replace, and its backlog is growing. But it's still losing money, and government contract sales can be difficult to predict. That uncertainty is where Innodata stands apart: It is already profitable, with plenty of cash to keep investing as demand for AI data grows.
