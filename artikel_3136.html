Altria Yields 5.9%. Here's How Many Shares You'd Need for $5,000 in Yearly Dividend Income.

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The cigarette giant has reliably raised its payouts annually for decades.

By Lawrence Rothman, CFA – Nov 22, 2026 at 4:14PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Altria Group ›

Altria Group (MO +0.25%) has a remarkable dividend history. It has raised the payment 61 times over the last 57 years. That puts the company firmly among the Dividend Kings — a small group of stocks with active payout-hiking streaks of at least 50 consecutive years.

The company has prioritized dividends, and management targets mid-single-digit percentage increases annually. It met that target this year by raising the quarterly payout by 4.7% to $1.11 per share.

That annualizes to a payout of $4.44 a year, which at Friday's closing share price of $74.87 gives it a dividend yield of 5.9%. For comparison, the S&P 500 index yields 1.1%.

What if you're targeting a specific amount in annual dividends — say, $5,000? To get that, you'd need to purchase 1,126 shares for about $84,300. Of course, that assumes Altria Group's dividends will remain constant, which they haven't, obviously.

But before income investors make a decision about purchasing this high-yielding Dividend King, they should take a deeper look at Altria Group's underlying business fundamentals.

The maker of tobacco and e-vapor products has been watching its product sales volumes decrease for some time. That's particularly true for its core cigarette business, which falls under its smokeable tobacco group.

In the first nine months of this year, smokeable tobacco products' revenue, excluding excise taxes, increased 3.6% year over year. However, this increase came entirely from the company increasing the prices it charges. The volume of cigarettes and other smokeables it sold dropped 2.7%, primarily driven by cigarettes' 2.8% volume decline.

Relying on a regular cadence of price increases on smokers to offset volume declines isn't a recipe for long-term success. While Altria management remains committed to paying and raising its dividends, it can't fight weakening business fundamentals forever. While the dividend seems secure for now, the company's relatively high 89% payout ratio suggests that may not be the case forever, unless the business finds other ways to raise its revenues.
