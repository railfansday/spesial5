Here's How Much $10,000 Invested in Altria Stock Could Pay in Dividends Over the Next Decade

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

It remains one of the S&P 500's premier dividend stocks.

By Stefon Walters – Nov 20, 2026 at 7:40AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Altria Group ›

Altria (MO +1.73%) is one of the market's marquee dividend stocks. In fact, many investors would tell you that Altria's dividend is the only reason to invest, even though the stock price surged more than 33% this year (as of Nov. 18). If Altria continues its impressive run, it'll be its best-performing year since its 2008 Philip Morris International (PM +0.95%) spinoff.

Altria's stock price run won't be the norm, but its dividend is as consistent as it gets, with the company being a Dividend King (a company with at least 50 consecutive years of dividend increases). It routinely offers one of the higher yields among S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.45%) companies and could be a good income play for patient investors. Here's how much it could pay out in dividends over the next decade with a $10,000 investment.

At Altria's price at the time of writing, $10,000 would buy about 130 shares, so we'll use that for our example. At Altria's annualized payout of $4.44 ($1.11 quarterly), those shares would pay $577.20. With a single $10,000 investment, you would've received close to $7,260 in cash payouts over a decade. The real magic, though, happens when you reinvest your dividends back into the stock, giving them a chance to earn dividends on them. In this scenario, had you reinvested dividends from year 1, your final investment value would easily be thousands more than $17,260 (original $10,000 investment plus $7,260 in cash payouts).

Altria is uniquely positioned as the country's largest tobacco producer, leading an industry at a crossroads. On one end, you have declining smoking rates in U.S. adults. According to the CDC, 42.4% of U.S. adults smoked in 1965. Now, that has dropped to 9.9%.

When you're the country's largest cigarette producer, that decline has a noticeable impact on business.

On the other end, tobacco users are very loyal and like what they like. Someone who smokes Marlboro Red or Black & Mild cigars (two of Altria's most popular brands) will typically buy them, even if prices go up. Of course, consumers have financial limits, but for the most part, this dynamic has given Altria the pricing power to offset some of its volume decline.

Altria hasn't done a great job establishing a smoke-free alternative to compete with popular brands like Philip Morris International's Zyn, but it isn't time to ring the alarm yet. Altria has enough time and more than enough resources to make something happen.

If you're looking for a good income investment, Altria is a buy. It knows its appeal is its dividend, so it's as shareholder-friendly as they come. I don't expect that to change in the foreseeable future.
