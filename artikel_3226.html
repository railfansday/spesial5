AMD vs. Nvidia: Which AI Chip Stock Is the Better Buy on the Dip?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

AMD and Nvidia both got hammered this month. Only one is the better bargain.

By Prosper Junior Bakiny – Nov 21, 2026 at 3:30AM EST

The semiconductor industry is experiencing a sell-off, and even some of the sector's leaders, including Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -13.25%) and Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%), haven't escaped the bloodbath. Both companies have faced headwinds in recent weeks and have lost significant market value as a result. AMD's shares have dropped 21% over the past month, while Nvidia's are down 40%. Which of these two stocks is a better buy right now?

AMD was the subject of a short-seller report by Hunterbrook Media alleging that the semiconductor leader may have violated export restrictions, since some of its firmware ended up at a blacklisted Chinese server manufacturer. However, Hunterbrook Media itself acknowledges that it doesn't know whether AMD itself supplied the software.

Meanwhile, Reuters has said it couldn't independently verify these claims, while AMD has denied them. So, this may be a giant nothingburger, after all. Yet, the stock fell because of the short-seller report, which arguably creates an attractive entry point.

AMD is capitalizing on the agentic AI boom, thanks to its market-leading CPU (central processing unit) servers. Third-quarter revenue jumped 45% year over year to $13.4 billion, while data center revenue soared 97% year over year to $8.5 billion.

AMD's non-GAAP earnings per share (EPS) were $2.09, up 74% compared to the year-ago period. AMD sees its revenue jump by more than 80% in 2027, while it expects its server CPU total addressable market to grow at a compound annual rate of more than 50% and reach $220 billion by 2030. That paints a very bright picture for the company's future.

Nvidia is going through a transition. The company's co-founder and longtime CEO, Jensen Huang, was involved in a scandal involving extramarital affairs, including with an alleged Chinese spy.

Huang stepped down as a result, a big blow to the company, given what he had built up to that point. Nvidia's board has appointed its CFO, Colette Kress, as interim CEO while it continues its search for its next leader. Despite all that mess, the company's financial results remain excellent.

In the third quarter of its fiscal year 2027, which ended on Oct. 25, Nvidia's revenue increased by 103% year over year to $115.8 billion. The company's data center revenue soared 110% year over year to $107.6 billion.

Nvidia's non-GAAP EPS was $2.68, up 111% year over year. Nvidia's quarterly update blew past expectations, and its guidance was impressive as well. The semiconductor leader expects $130 billion in revenue (at the midpoint) in its upcoming fourth quarter, representing an almost 91% year-over-year increase. And Nvidia expects its revenue to grow about 75% year over year in its fiscal year 2028, which will cover most of the calendar year 2027.

The loss of Huang is a problem, but Nvidia has many advantages that remain intact, including technological expertise, deep relationships with many corporations it does business with, and its CUDA ecosystem, which provides a wide moat from switching costs. With the right leader at the helm, it could still beat the market over the long run.

Nvidia currently generates higher revenue and profits, and is growing both metrics faster.

It also boasts higher gross margins. However, Nvidia's search for a new CEO introduces significant uncertainty. AMD's short-seller-related problems aren't nearly as dire, in my view.

Further, AMD's revenue growth is accelerating, particularly within its all-important data center segment, while Nvidia's revenue growth could decline next year. Does that make AMD the better buy?

We'd need to look at valuation as well, and in that category, Nvidia is the winner, as seen in their respective price-to-earnings (P/E) ratios above. Nvidia might be facing tougher headwinds right now, but the market has adjusted for that by giving the company much lower valuation multiples. Nvidia is far too cheap at current levels, in my view, and while I'd also buy AMD's shares right now, Nvidia's look even more attractive.
