Wizards Practice Notes: Kyshawn George, Brian Keefe discuss growth amid 7-12 start

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

WASHINGTON — The Washington Wizards have followed an eight-game losing streak with three wins in their last four games.

They've shown positive signs amid a 7-12 record. But inconsistency, in both effort and production, has derailed a promising start.

After a furious second-half comeback fell short in a 131-116 loss to the Boston Celtics on Tuesday, Brian Keefe said his squad needs to play with that energy and effort for a full 48 minutes. He furthered that sentiment after Friday's practice.

"You're trying to build consistency. I loved our response … I thought Boston played really well to start the game, but you're trying to be the best version of yourself and grow that consistency of things that you're doing well," Keefe said. "I think that's what we looked at the last couple of days is how we're going to get back to being more consistent and starting the games in a good position."

One of Washington's many inconsistencies is its rotation, which is a revolving door apart from the starting five and Bilal Coulibaly. Some nights, Tre Johnson and Bub Carrington are benched for a full game or an entire half. Other nights, Will Riley and Deandre Ayton play just five minutes.

Keefe said it's not uncommon for rotations to fluctuate, even this far into a season. He believes in the Wizards' depth and its ability to eventually produce.

"We're learning what fits," Keefe said. "The team is evolving in certain things that we're doing. So I don't think it's that unusual, but we want to use the depth … [The depth] has helped us contribute to winning."

This is meant to be a "season of growth" for Washington. It hopes its young players will evolve in close, meaningful games. Three of their last four contests have been so-called "clutch games" — contests that are within five points with five minutes remaining in regulation.

They've won two of those three games, in large part because of Kyshawn George's two-way production. The third-year wing is averaging 16.7 points on 40.7% 3-point shooting this season while headlining the Wizards' perimeter defense.

George said those reps in meaningful situations have helped build his confidence.

"Having played two seasons already, [I] had a couple of these situations last year. And just now knowing what we're looking for as a team," George said. "First of all, I think the most important part is trusting what's called, and then just making the simple play. I've done the reps. [It's about] just trusting myself, trusting my team."

George has ascended as one of Washington's cornerstone players next to AJ Dybantsa. He's emerged as a three-level scorer with two-way impact in only his third season.

This season feels different, though. He's been trusted to get the Wizards good looks in close games. And he's delivered. It's a trait he believes he's always possessed.

It's only now that the world is seeing that skill as he moves into a larger on-ball role.

"I've had the opportunity to do it," George said of his bigger role amid a breakout season. "First of all, I think that's a big one. I didn't get the opportunity [in previous seasons] to show that I will be able to do that. And then just the work I put in every single day translates to the game and the trust that my teammates have for me to have the ball in my hand."

Brian Keefe gave updates on the following players:

Reese Beekman, who's missed the last 23 days with an AC joint sprain, is "getting close" to a return.

Tristan Vukcevic, who was diagnosed with a hip impingement on Oct. 1, is "progressing," but Keefe didn't have a timeline for his return. Keefe said Tuesday that Vukcevic has not experienced any setbacks in his recovery.
