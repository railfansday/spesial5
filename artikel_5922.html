Citation’s majority stake in cruise seller World Travel Holdings is a bet that operators will keep needing middlemen.

Many cruise passengers have booked through World Travel Holdings without ever knowing it.

The Florida-based company, which bills itself as the world’s largest cruise agency, runs online travel agencies like Cruises.com, franchises more than 2,000 largely home-based travel agencies under Dream Vacations and CruiseOne, and powers private-label cruise booking sites for airlines, hotels, and other partners.

The company is not public, and most of its financials are not disclosed. But regulatory filings in the United States and United Kingdom show its franchise network is expanding and its subsidiaries’ profits are rising as more people turn to cruises. That kind of growth has attracted private equity.

In May, private equity firm Citation Capital made a “strategic investment” in World Travel Holdings, gaining exposure to record-setting cruise demand without the cost of owning ships.

World Travel Holdings, the Florida-based company that calls itself the world's largest cruise agency, is now majority-owned by private equity firm Citation Capital, which made what it described as a "strategic investment" in May. Co-CEO Jeff Tolkin confirmed Citation holds a majority stake and said he and his brother Brad were asked to stay on for "at least" five years; the brothers retain significant ownership, lead daily operations, and sit on the holding company board alongside Citation's Lydie Hudson and Dan Routh. The company is private and discloses little, but U.S. franchise filings and UK Companies House records reviewed by Skift show the business growing: annual sales rose from $1 billion in 2021 to $2.36 billion in 2025, and CruiseOne, the subsidiary behind the Dream Vacations and CruiseOne franchises, earned $7 million on $30.4 million in revenue in 2025, a margin of about 23%. WTH also took on new debt around the close, including roughly $24 million in loans committed by Andalusian Credit Co., according to an SEC filing. The deal follows a run of transactions for cruise sellers — Hays Travel buying Victoria Travel Group, Flight Centre agreeing to buy Iglu, and Trip.com Group taking a majority stake in e-hoi — signaling that buyers see cruise distribution as a growth business in its own right, and putting more of the industry's sales pipeline under owners focused on returns.
