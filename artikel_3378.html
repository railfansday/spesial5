8 Movies Guy Ritchie Almost Directed But Never Made, Including Sherlock Holmes 3

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Guy Ritchie has built a career around fast-talking criminals, elaborate schemes, and action sequences that rarely unfold as expected. After making his name with British crime movies like Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels and Snatch, he made his way into major Hollywood productions. While Guy Ritchie's filmography spans several genres, some of the movies he was attached to direct could have taken his career in entirely different directions.

Over the years, Ritchie has been attached to several high-profile movies that ultimately moved forward without him or never reached the screen. While some seemed like natural fits for his filmmaking style, others offered opportunities to explore new genres.

Not every abandoned project represents a missed opportunity, especially when another director eventually delivers a successful movie. However, several of Ritchie's unrealized films give an interesting glimpse at the creative directions he considered pursuing. Some have remained stuck in development for years, while others underwent substantial changes after he departed. From crime thrillers and comic book adaptations to a long-awaited return for Sherlock Holmes, these eight movies demonstrate how much of a director's career can be shaped by the films that never happen.

Guy Ritchie helped reinvent Sherlock Holmes for modern audiences with 2009's Sherlock Holmes, starring Robert Downey Jr. as the brilliant detective and Jude Law as his partner, Dr. John Watson. Ritchie's interpretation combined Holmes' sharp intellect with stylized action scenes, quick-witted exchanges, and an entertaining dynamic between the detective and Watson. He continued this approach in 2011's Sherlock Holmes: A Game of Shadows, which brought Holmes face-to-face with his greatest adversary, Professor Moriarty, played by Jared Harris.

However, Ritchie never returned for the planned third installment. In 2019, Rocketman director Dexter Fletcher was announced as his replacement, but the sequel subsequently encountered numerous delays. Interestingly, Ritchie hasn't ruled out returning to the franchise. During a May 2026 interview, he expressed interest in making another Sherlock Holmes movie, although his involvement remains unconfirmed. If Ritchie does return, the third film could recapture the elements between Holmes and Watson that made his first two installments so entertaining.

After directing Disney's successful live-action Aladdin remake in 2019, Guy Ritchie was set to tackle another animated classic. In June 2022, Disney tapped him to direct a live-action adaptation of 1997's Hercules, which follows the son of Zeus as he attempts to reclaim his place among the gods. Unlike Aladdin, which largely followed the original film's story, the proposed Hercules remake appeared to be taking a different approach.

Joe and Anthony Russo, who were producing the film through their AGBO company, told Variety that they envisioned a more experimental musical influenced by modern audiences and TikTok. However, the project has struggled to gain momentum. Ritchie was reportedly no longer directing the remake by January 2024, although the Russo brothers subsequently indicated that he remained involved. His current status is unclear, and as of late 2026, Disney has yet to announce an official cast or release date (via People).

Guy Ritchie nearly reunited with Jake Gyllenhaal for Road House 2, the planned sequel to Amazon MGM Studios' 2024 action remake (via TheWrap). Directed by Doug Liman, the first film starred Gyllenhaal as Dalton, a former UFC fighter hired to work as a bouncer at a Florida Keys bar. However, Dalton soon finds himself fighting off a group of criminals determined to drive the bar's owner out of business.

In April 2025, Ritchie was announced as the sequel's director, setting up a reunion with Gyllenhaal after their collaboration on 2023's The Covenant. Having previously directed action-heavy crime films like The Gentlemen and Wrath of Man, Ritchie seemed like a natural fit for the project. However, he exited the sequel just three months later, with no official explanation for his departure (via TheWrap). Nobody director Ilya Naishuller took over, and the film has since completed production.

Before returning to Sherlock Holmes, Guy Ritchie was preparing to direct a live-action adaptation of DC Comics' Lobo (via Variety). The character is an alien bounty hunter known for his violent tendencies, dark humor, and love of motorcycles. In September 2009, Warner Bros. announced Ritchie as director, with a script by Don Payne. The proposed film would have followed Lobo to Earth as he hunted four dangerous fugitives. Given Ritchie's work with volatile criminals like Brick Top in Snatch (2000), Lobo's brutality and unpredictable behavior could have been a great fit for his darkly comedic approach to violence.

However, the success of 2009's Sherlock Holmes changed Warner Bros.' priorities. In January 2010, producer Joel Silver told the Los Angeles Times that the studio wanted Ritchie to focus on a sequel rather than Lobo. Ritchie then directed 2011's Sherlock Holmes: A Game of Shadows, while his proposed DC adaptation was never developed.

Layer Cake (2004) seemed like an obvious fit for Guy Ritchie, especially after Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels and Snatch. Like those films, it explores London's criminal underworld, but its protagonist approaches business differently. Daniel Craig plays an unnamed cocaine dealer who treats drug trafficking like a legitimate profession, avoiding unnecessary violence and trouble. Just as he's preparing to retire, his boss gives him two final assignments that threaten to unravel his carefully controlled life.

Matthew Vaughn, who produced both Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels and Snatch, originally developed Layer Cake for Ritchie to direct (via Phase9). When Ritchie passed on the project, Vaughn decided to direct it himself. His version took a more restrained approach than Ritchie's earlier crime films, focusing on Craig's performance and the escalating consequences of his character's decisions. Layer Cake marked Vaughn's directorial debut, followed by films such as Kick-Ass and Kingsman: The Secret Service.

Sgt. Rock would have taken Guy Ritchie into a different corner of the DC universe. Rather than dealing with superheroes or supervillains, the film would have followed Frank Rock, a World War II infantry sergeant who leads a unit known as Easy Company. The character's stories focus on ordinary soldiers fighting their way through dangerous missions, often under overwhelming odds. Ritchie was linked to an adaptation that would have kept the original wartime setting, with Bruce Willis reportedly considered for the lead role. Given his experience with ensemble casts, the soldiers' competing personalities could have been an interesting part of his version.

Ritchie's version ultimately fell through, and by 2009, Warner Bros. had brought in Francis Lawrence to develop a new adaptation set in the future rather than World War II (via Los Angeles Times). Ritchie was reportedly reconsidered in 2011, but he never returned as director. Decades of attempts to bring Sgt. Rock to the screen have followed, including a version from Luca Guadagnino that was shelved in April 2025 (via TheWrap). However, DC Studios co-CEO James Gunn subsequently confirmed that the project was still being considered, just without Guadagnino attached.

A pirate adventure might seem like a departure for Guy Ritchie, but the betrayals and double-crosses in Robert Louis Stevenson's Treasure Island have plenty in common with his earlier crime films. The story follows Jim Hawkins, a young boy who discovers a treasure map and joins an expedition in search of buried gold. Among the crew is Long John Silver, who earns Jim's trust while secretly planning a mutiny. Silver's shifting loyalties would have fit naturally into one of Ritchie's stories, where even the most charming characters often have their own agendas.

In June 2012, Warner Bros. attached Ritchie to direct a new adaptation, with Alex Harakis writing the screenplay. The studio reportedly envisioned a stylized action blockbuster similar to Ritchie's Sherlock Holmes films. However, his version never reached production, and no official reason for its disappearance was announced.

The Dirty Dozen (1967) stars Lee Marvin as Major John Reisman, an officer tasked with turning 12 convicted soldiers into a unit capable of carrying out a dangerous mission behind enemy lines. Their constant fighting and disregard for authority create nearly as many problems as the mission itself.

Guy Ritchie was linked to a remake in 2007 alongside producer Joel Silver and publicly expressed interest in directing it. However, the film never moved forward. David Ayer was later brought in to develop a different version, leaving the original remake plans involving Guy Ritchie unmade.
