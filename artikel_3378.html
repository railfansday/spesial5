‘The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth’ Throws Netflix’s Oscar Campaign a Curveball

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

From a purely awards-hunting perspective, there’s a great argument to be made that Netflix could have pushed back its late November release “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” until next year. Historically, the perception of the embattled studio is that its had issues being distracted by too big an awards slate to pin down its Best Picture frontrunner. Remember in 2023 when it seemed like the streamer did not realize until the Oscar shortlists (in mid-December!) that “All Quiet on the Western Front” was its best bet at Best Picture? The Edward Berger film went on to win four Oscars, including Best International Feature. This year, Netflix is running their best awards campaign yet with “La Bola Negra,” which the studio acquired out of Cannes, where its helmers, Los Javis, won the jury prize for Best Director. Come the fall festivals, it became the first Netflix film to win the People’s Choice Award at TIFF. Since then, it has fronted Netflix’s experiment in expanding the theatrical distribution of its awards slate, doing admirable business in key markets. The consensus among prognosticators is that the film is not only a lock to receive a Best Picture nomination, but is also sure to win the Oscar for Best International Feature on behalf of Spain.

If Netflix wanted to secure a second Best Picture nomination, with “Cliff Booth” out of the picture, the team could put all its energy into Lee Chang-dong’s “Possible Love.” Not only is the near-three hour film, which won the Grand Jury Prize at Venice, another Best International Feature contender (on behalf of South Korea), but it is one of this season’s most critically lauded films. If enough critics’ groups get behind it in the coming weeks, a la “The Secret Agent” last year, then a second Best Picture nomination for Netflix at the 2027 Oscars becomes highly plausible.

Somewhere along the way, “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” became something of a puzzle for Netflix. The streamer had no choice but to follow through with putting it out around Thanksgiving, because it was already set to replace the IMAX screens that had initially been secured for Greta Gerwig’s “Narnia” film. So how does a company not run an awards campaign for the sequel to a recent Best Picture nominee, directed by a three-time Oscar nominee, written by a two-time Oscar winner, and starring Brad Pitt in the role he just won a Best Supporting Actor Oscar for?

What reads as a home run on paper is more of a double or triple in execution. “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” is made by people who know what they are doing, and that expands past director David Fincher, screenwriter Quentin Tarantino, and star Pitt.

Cinematographer Erik Messerschmidt, editor Kirk Baxter, production designer Donald Graham Burt, set decorator Jan Pascale, and composers Trent Reznor and Atticus Ross have all won at least one Academy Award. Costume designer Trish Summerville was nominated for an Oscar for her work on “Mank.” Even if there is a low bar for getting the Academy excited about a late-’70s period piece set in Hollywood, “Cliff Booth” easily clears it.

But we are in a spectacle-heavy year, with “The Odyssey” and “Project Hail Mary” in the awards conversation, and “Dune: Part Three” and “Werwulf” on the horizon. Even “Digger” is a formidable contender in the craft races. So if the feeling is that “Cliff Booth” is more of a hangout movie than a cinematic event, then the focus of its awards campaign should be on categories that are more willing to embrace that, like Best Production Design or Best Costume Design. (Not so much Best Sound, which “Once Upon a Time in Hollywood” did actually receive nominations for.)

In terms of the acting performances, again, Pitt is great as Cliff Booth, but there is no rush to nominate him for a performance he already won for. And there are plenty of other Best Actor contenders, like Matt Damon and John Malkovich (who have never won) and John Turturro and Andrew Scott (who have never even been nominated for an Oscar before).

The top performance of the film is with Cliff’s love interest, played by Emmy winner Elizabeth Debicki, who actually does have a shot at her first Oscar nomination, given the love for her performance in “Wicker” as well, and the lack of current consensus in the Best Supporting Actress race.

Everyone else in the film, from Scott Caan to Yahya Abdul-Mateen II to Carla Gugino, is very additive to the project but does not necessarily cross the threshold into Oscar-contender territory. In that way, it is similar to Tony Gilroy’s “Behemoth!,” another awards hopeful from this season.

Ultimately, “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” is entertaining enough to garner support from certain branches of the Academy, but falls too late in the year, in too few theaters, to truly become the last-minute surprise blockbuster that would get as many as 10 Oscar nominations, something its predecessor “Once Upon a Time in Hollywood” did handily.
