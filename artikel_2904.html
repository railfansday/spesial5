2 Dividend Stocks to Buy in November and 1 to Leave on the Shelf

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

In a volatile market, you want stocks that have reliable businesses so you can sleep well at night.

By Reuben Gregg Brewer – Nov 26, 2026 at 6:25PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Altria Group ›

Altria's (MO +1.95%) 5.9% yield is very attractive, but don't rush out and buy the stock. You may be better off with McCormick's (MKC +1.67%) 4.1%, or even Procter & Gamble's (PG +0.44%) 2.5%. Here's why two of these stocks could be buys in November, and one is likely best left on the shelf.

Altria isn't at any risk of going bankrupt. And its dividend doesn't look particularly risky, either. For more aggressive dividend investors, it may be worth a closer look given its well-above-market 5.9% yield. However, you have to ask yourself why that yield is so high before you buy the stock.

The answer is that Altria's combustible tobacco business, largely cigarettes, accounted for $5.4 billion of its $6.1 billion in revenues in the third quarter of 2026. That's 88% of the top line, which is a big problem when you consider that the number of cigarettes and cigars the company sold fell 2.9% in the quarter to 16.2 billion. In the third quarter of 2021, the company sold nearly 24.5 billion, indicating that this is a long-term trend. To be fair, Altria is working hard to diversify its business away from smokeable products, but given the uncertainty in the market today, thanks to geopolitical conflict, inflation, belt-tightening consumers, and AI volatility, why take on that kind of business risk?

At the other end of the risk spectrum is Procter & Gamble, one of the world's largest consumer-staples makers. The company sells paper products, toiletries, and makeup, with its brands typically positioned at the high end of the market. While there is some trade-down risk in that market position, P&G has long focused on leading the consumer staples sector with innovation. The goal is to produce products that consumers are willing to pay more for because they get more from them.

Right now, P&G's 2.5% yield is well above the market's 1% and the consumer staples average of 2%. So you aren't skimping on yield to own this well-run company. That said, its price-to-sales and price-to-earnings ratios are roughly in line with their five-year averages, so you aren't getting a bargain price, either. However, given that P&G was able to increase its organic sales growth by a respectable 4% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027 despite increasingly cost-conscious consumers, conservative dividend investors should probably give it a closer look.

McCormick's risk profile is a bit harder to define, but the risk-versus-reward balance is likely tilted toward reward. The historically high 4.1% yield is a key part of the equation, indicating the stock is cheap today. That's backed up by the P/S and P/E ratios being below their five-year averages. As with Altria, however, you have to ask why McCormick's yield is so high and its valuation so cheap.

The answer is that McCormick is in the middle of acquiring Unilever's (UL +2.48%) food business. That will roughly double the company's size and require it to take on significant debt. Those are risks, but the two companies' spice- and flavor-focused portfolios are complementary and offer cross-selling opportunities. And McCormick has a strong acquisition history, including meeting its post-merger deleveraging commitments. If you can handle a little uncertainty for a significant increase in yield, you may want to take a risk on McCormick as November draws to a close. Unlike Altria, whose business is contracting, McCormick's business looks set to get much bigger.

Altria's core business has been in decline for years. While the company has managed to maintain a growing dividend during this period, most long-term dividend investors are probably best off avoiding it. Conservative types would be better off with an industry stalwart like P&G. And if you are willing to take on a little more risk, McCormick's pending acquisition could be an opportunity to buy a historically well-run company while it is attractively priced and offers a well-above-market yield.
