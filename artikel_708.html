Mariko Tamaki Writing Sexy Vampire Thriller ‘Royal Blood’ for Skybound, Image Comics (Exclusive)

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Rossi Gifford is drawing the ongoing series that will debut early next year.

Mariko Tamaki grew up reading the vampire stories by author Anne Rice and became enthralled by the mix of horror and brooding romance.

“I’ve always loved a heartfelt monster story, and to me she is the queen of the soulful vampire,” the award-winning graphic novel author and creator said via email to The Hollywood Reporter.

Now Tamaki, who has spent the last several years working on DC’s YA graphic novels and is known for her queer coming-of-age story Laura Dean Keeps Breaking Up with Me, will channel some of that love into her own romantic vampire comic book series. She is set to write Royal Blood, an ongoing original horror series from Skybound and Image Comics. Tamaki is working with artist Rossi Gifford (Batman: Bad Seeds – Streets of Gotham) on the series, with the duo acting as co-creators along with Glenn Geller, Skybound’s president of TV.

Colorist Miquel Muerto (an apt name for someone who also works on Something is Killing the Children) and letterer Becca Carey (Exquisite Corpses) are on also part of the creative team for the comic, which is due to arrive in shops and digital platforms on Feb. 24, 2027. The book is intended for mature readers.

Hearts will skip, and many will stop entirely, in this story that Geller described as “Shakespeare with fangs.” Royal Blood is centered on a prince named Oliver, who is struggling to understand his place in the royal family, and a vampire, Charlotte, who was sent to destroy the royal family. Tamaki called it as “a sexy spy story, a vampire story, and an impossible love story.”

The initial idea of the series came from Skybound’s Geller, who posited, “What if a vampire spy infiltrated the royal family by seducing the prince?” Geller came up with the concept, title and main character names and showed it to his Skybound colleagues. Skybound Editorial loved the idea and wanted to make it a comic, reaching out to Tamaki, whom they thought would be a perfect fit given her track record.

Tamaki has gained a strong reader following thanks to a body of work that focuses on relationships, identity, and coming-of-age stories, LGBT or otherwise. Her graphic novel Laura Dean won the 2019 Harvey Award for best children or young adult book and was nominated for the Harvey for book of the year. It won three Ignatz Awards and three Eisners, including one for best writer and best publication for teens. More recently she wrote DC’s YA graphic novel I Am Not Starfire as well as the mini-series Zatanna: Bring Down the House.

For Royal Blood, Tamaki pitched a more fleshed out version of the idea and wrote a few scripts. Gifford was then brought in as artist, adding to what became the final form of the series. All three are working together on the ongoing series.

Tamaki listened to Annie Lennox’s “Love Song for a Vampire” plenty while writing the comic. The song appeared Bram Stoker’s Dracula, Francis Ford Coppola’s adaptation of perhaps the most famous vampire story of all time. It’s a movie she loves and notes the costumes by Eiko Ishioka as a standout.

Like the Rice novels and the Coppola movie, this comic is intended to have an undercurrent of passion running through it.

“It is so sexy, I can’t even tell you,” said Tamaki. “It is sooo sexy. I wrote it a little sexy and then Rossi Gifford made it sooo sexy.”

Gifford is a Toronto-based artist and character-designer who is coming off of working on DC’s Batman: Bad Seeds – Streets of Gotham. Tamaki and Skybound found a smoldering aspect to her work that made her the choice to draw the series.

“With Mariko’s work, which I’ve followed for years, at its heart, we have all the ingredients for a compelling series: engaging characters, a thrilling plot, horror, drama and a spicy love story,” said Gifford in a statement.

Royal Blood will be the latest title in Skybound’s line of horror comics that famously began with The Walking Dead. This year it published Corpse Knight, written by The Conjuring franchise director Michael Chaves, and continues to put out re-imaginings of iconic horror properties with the licenses of Creepshow and the Universal Monsters line.
