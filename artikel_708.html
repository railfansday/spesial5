Two goals in 15 games - what's going on with Gyokeres?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Viktor Gyokeres has scored twice in all competitions this season

A Premier League trophy and 21 goals in his debut season - on paper things have worked out perfectly for Viktor Gyokeres since his Â£64m transfer to Arsenal.

But, even with those achievements, it has been a tough start to the current campaign for the 28-year-old striker.

The Swede has started just one Premier League game this season and is yet to score in his eight league appearances.

He has started three games in the Champions League, two in the Carabao Cup, and has scored just twice in 15 games in all competitions. Not the numbers he would have hoped for after last season's glory.

It is not just this season that has seen Gyokeres suitability questioned for Arsenal though.

Even though he scored more than 20 goals in his first season in all competitions as Arsenal ended their 22-year wait for a title, his all-round game had its critics.

It has been near enough a straight battle for the number nine role between Kai Havertz and Gyokeres since the Swedish international signed.

And Gyokeres was thrust into leading the line for Arsenal quicker and more frequently than planned after injury kept Havertz out for long periods last season.

Although the Swede may be the more natural finisher, Havertz's all-round play is considered superior with the German also one of Arteta's most trusted players for big games.

Gyokeres, a "win-now signing", was bought for his ability to find the net but also for how he could open up spaces for his team-mates, something they have praised him for.

But playing with his back to goal and linking up play is not natural to Gyokeres and his failure to hold up the ball or win duels with centre-backs has led to fan frustration.

Arteta has spoken about how the striker has been working on improving his hold-up play and connections, and there were hints of it in the Champions League win over Slavia Prague where Gyokeres assisted the opener and scored the winner in arguably his best game of the season.

A big reason that Gyokeres is not starting games is that Havertz is fit.

Last season, Havertz suffered with knee and hamstring issues which meant Gyokeres was the only option Arteta had.

Had Havertz been fit, he would have played a lot more and the fact that he started the Champions League final and the key match away at Manchester City in last season's title race back this up.

This season Gyokeres has just six starts and Arteta says that his gametime and situation is similar to other members in the squad.

"We have rotated the team a lot and used the competitions as well to give minutes to players and exposure because they are all starters in my opinion," Arteta said.

"Viktor's case is one of many that they have been in the same situation where they play more in the Premier League or more in the Champions League which is not bad, eh?"

Arteta is open with his players about their standing in the squad and said that Gyokeres has not approached him directly about a lack of minutes.

"We have conversations with every player to make sure that they understand what we are doing and to gather information about the way we are feeling and so far it has been very positive with Viktor and the rest of the team," he said.

The signing of Gyokeres is an interesting one in its own right.

The Arsenal recruitment team had done a lot of due diligence on signing, now Manchester United forward, Benjamin Sesko.

It is understood work on that deal had been done but a change of direction to sign Gyokeres happened after Andrea Berta became Arsenal sporting director.

Some around Arsenal have always thought that Gyokeres is not the usual forward that the Gunners would go for and his predicament of a lack of game time was always likely to happen, even if he did play a big part in them ending their trophy drought.

Gyokeres ended last season with 14 league goals but those strikes actually only won Arsenal two points on their way to winning the league.

That is because, although his penalty in the 1-0 win at Everton was the only goal of the game, Arsenal would have come away with a draw, so that strike factually only accounted for two points and not three.

But Gyokeres did put the Gunners ahead five times last season in the league which shows that, among the frustrating performances, he had plenty of impact.

Max Dowman stole the show with his goal in the 2-0 home win over Everton, when he became the youngest scorer in Premier League history, but it was Gyokeres' goal in the 89th-minute which put Arsenal ahead and although it did not lead to a point score on its own, it set them on the way.

Gyokeres also scored in the 4-1 north London derby win, putting Arsenal ahead when Kolo Muani had equalised after Eberechi Eze's opener.

It is unlikely that Gyokeres will be sold in January as he continues to get games in the Champions League and is a senior player who can score goals in big moments.

But if his lack of minutes in the league continue, Gyokeres, who is a driven and ambitious individual, will not want to be the player who is left out of the starting XI in the long term.
