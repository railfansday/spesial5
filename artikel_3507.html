If you want to know what it looks like when a trillion-dollar tech bubble violently collides with physical reality, just look at yesterday. $2.5 trillion in tech valuations were wiped off the board as Wall Street panicked about AI’s leverage — punishing tech giants for incinerating cash on infrastructure that isn’t generating immediate profit.

Simultaneously, as we saw on Wednesday, the American voter rejected data center expansions in 40 out of 41 local ballot measures. The hardware hoarders are losing their grip, and the panic is palpable. The era of scrutiny is here, and I want to show you two possible timelines of where this could go.

But first, we have to go back over a couple things.

When faced with this stock crash, AMD CEO Lisa Su said, “We are a data center first company.” While I’d love to throw my hands up and get angry at this, I have to turn the BS detector on myself and address a brutal truth: she’s right. This market crash is a sentiment correction, not an overnight fab cancellation.

The big three memory foundries — SK Hynix, Samsung and Micron — operate on long-term agreements. 2026-2027 is already locked in at sky-high prices, and on top of that, enterprise AI accelerators consume roughly 3x the silicon wafers of standard consumer DDR5 memory.

As long as those massive AI server chips are shipping, conventional RAM capacity remains deeply suppressed.

So, if Wall Street is slashing valuations and data center permits are being blocked, why aren’t memory foundries pivoting back to consumer PC hardware right now?

Hyperscalers are also starving for memory, and receive around 60-70% of what they ask for. If Microsoft and Meta get spooked by the market and pull back their massive order growth by 10% or even 15%, it wouldn’t trigger a supply chain pivot. It just means the foundries are a little less overwhelmed. Demand still vastly exceeds total factory capacity.

If foundries are still frantically trying to fulfill enterprise contracts, they have zero financial incentive to redirect back to consumer memory.

For them to actually panic and pivot, hyperscaler orders have to crater. A simple market correction like this won’t do it. But a sweeping wave of municipal zoning bans? That changes the math entirely.

Top of the Democratic agenda on day 1 needs to be a pause on advanced AI development, regulation of this out of control industry, and a ban on artificial superintelligence.November 4, 2026

If tech giants are legally barred from breaking ground on new server farms, they are forced to cancel their existing silicon orders because they physically have nowhere to plug the chips in.

We are not getting a massive surplus of GPUs and memory kits tomorrow morning. But the midterms and the stock market correction did put a concrete ceiling on the shortage. So let me take a stab at how I think this is going to unfold.

The stock rout has cracked the leveraged AI market for now, but contract prices remain firm. There is zero immediate relief, so don’t expect some banger Black Friday deals on RTX 5090s.

Memory shortages will hit their tightest consumer squeeze through early 2027. PC makers will fiercely defend a ceiling of 16GB-24GB on premium laptops and handhelds. If you’re hoping for 32GB in sub-$1,500 machines, shelve that hope through most of next year.

As hyperscalers face sustained stock and regulatory pressure from the midterm fallout, corporate boards will force company CFOs to look at their data center infrastructure plans again.

Instead of writing blank checks for server racks, the pace of subsequent purchase orders will slow. And for you reading this, I envision that PC hardware prices will finally stop climbing and plateau.

PC builders (Apple, Dell, Asus, Lenovo, etc) will gain massive leverage during mid-year procurement negotiations — putting an end to double-digit quarter-on-quarter price hikes.

A typical memory wafer run takes 3-4 months. Once the AI server orders officially ease in Q2, foundries will finally redirect their wafers back to consumer products. This is when the true price relief begins.

Standard desktop DDR5 kits will experience price drops, and holiday 2027 laptops will finally start normalizing 32GB unified configurations without severe margin penalties.

We aren’t completely out of the woods, and we still have to survive a brutal winter. But in my mind, those 2028 doom-and-gloom shortage predictions are officially dead.

The hyperscalers just hit a political and financial ceiling, which may lead to a peak in memory pricing this spring rather than running unchecked into next year.

Hold off on your PC upgrades — the local AI renaissance could be scheduled for the second half of 2027.
