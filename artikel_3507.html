Xbox Head of Next Gen Finally Explains What Project Helix Is: ‘It Is Not a PC’

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Xbox wants Project Helix to have access to a unified games library

To celebrate 25 years of Xbox, the company released a special publication that let fans look back at its past and glimpse its plans for the future. The digital magazine features a conversation with Jason Ronald, Xbox Vice President of Next Generation, who provides an inside look into the creation of Project Helix, Xbox's next-gen console.

Ronald underlined that game preservation is a key element in the creation of Project Helix. As he sees it, owning a new console doesn't mean you have to leave behind your previous-gen games. Therefore, it's important to ensure that Project Helix is backward compatible with all previous Xbox consoles, allowing fans to continue to have access to and play their games. This way, game libraries will move alongside players wherever they go, rather than being left behind.

Nowadays, it's not as common for players to stick to a single platform, which is why games across all platforms are being taken into consideration. Xbox wants to break barriers and give players access to a unified gaming library. Ronald says that "Helix is a console that is also capable of playing games from your PC. It is not a PC that can also play console games." The goal is to make it easy and intuitive, whether you're a "hardcore gamer or a power user who wants to have that deeper level of customizability across settings and performance options."

To make this happen, Xbox is taking inspiration from ROG Ally and Xbox Mode, both of which were steps in the direction that Xbox is headed. It seems that access to a unified games' library, multiple storefronts, portability, and PC-like navigation are all features Xbox would like to include in its next-gen console. Feedback on the ROG Ally has played a big role in creating a prototype for Project Helix.

"The feedback that we've gotten from players has fundamentally informed and altered our plans for how [a unified library] actually shows up in a future console. Players shared where we met the mark and areas we needed to improve or approach differently. So absolutely, we view the Ally as a precursor to what we're doing with Helix."

Xbox wants to put "players at the center" in its next generation of consoles, and building Project Helix around feedback is certainly one way of doing it. There is one important question that remains on fans' minds: will Project Helix have a disc drive?

If being able to play games from each generation is such a crucial focal point for Xbox, then being able to use discs should go beyond the disc-to-digital program. While game digitization is a great option to have, it is limited to Xbox One and Series X/S consoles and still only leaves players with a digital license, which is not equivalent to game ownership.

Affordability is another major consideration in the creation of Project Helix. While console gaming used to be an affordable entry point into the world of video games, the component crisis has definitely changed this.

"It's definitely a challenging time, but as a system and a platform designer, it's also one of the most exciting times, because we have to invent entirely new technology to continue to advance the state-of-the-art while also continuing to make things accessible and affordable."

It seems that Xbox is working hard to find a way around these hurdles to deliver the best product it can. Adding a disc drive would likely conflict with the system's affordability, but it would certainly win points with fans disgruntled by the imminent all-digital future.
