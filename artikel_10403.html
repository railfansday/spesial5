I love YouTube on TV, but we desperately need better watchlist controls

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Managing a YouTube watchlist shouldn’t be as frustrating as it is, especially on the most-used app in my life. Google’s premier streaming app has too many issues with resurfacing content; in my opinion, this is now a major problem that needs to be fixed.

Aside from my YouTube watchlist gripes, it’s no secret that moving from a web browser to the TV is ultimately still a frustrating experience.

Lots of things I love about watching videos on my laptop or PC just don’t translate well to when I’m sat on my sofa holding a remote trying to find something to watch after wrapping up for the day. One thing I’m getting annoyed with is how YouTube scales on TV and Android TV boxes because you’re given less choice and arguably poorer recommendations for content due to the constraints in place.

Understandably, you can’t compress the layout too much on a TV as you’re typically sat a few feet away and all aspects of the interface need to be legible. I would still like to tweak it, though, if that were possible.

Maybe we could get proper sections for content, like how Netflix and other streaming platforms offer categorized areas where you can search for “comedy” or “horror”. I’m almost certain that, with Google’s insane investment and massive AI push, where every video is already analyzed hundreds of times, that wouldn’t be too difficult to do.

The problem now is that the sidebar menu is just too broad. Gaming covers basically everything. Then each line of videos accounts for one title, but requires search and previous watch data to populate. I find myself often fighting with recommendations on my TV, whereas on desktop it’s not as much of a problem.

That leads me to my biggest problem with YouTube, and it’s the, frankly, embarrassing watchlist or “Watch later” controls.

I can’t fault that it works, but right now the watchlist function is like something out of the early 2000s due to the lack of flexibility that I touched on above. As my YouTube consumption has increased 20x over the past four or five years, managing what I want to come back to or might have missed the first time around is starting to become a bit of a chore.

I’ve tried using playlists to save things, but things get messy very quickly because of how cumbersome it is to a) create a playlist on TV and b) how playlists are organized by default.

Google TV isn’t perfect, but it’s where I keep my biggest TV and movie watchlist outside of YouTube. Yes, YouTube is mostly for user-generated content, but I would take something as basic as Google TV’s interface baked into YouTube somehow. Add in categorization and auto-clear.

The Watch Later section has become an unwieldy place that I never really want to go unless I desperately have to. I’m constantly losing videos that I hoped to come back to or have an affinity for.

Videos don’t clear automatically, nor do you know when they were added. Yes, I’ll give credit that you can sort by a few options, but that’s the bare minimum. The process of moving things around is slow and takes multiple taps. Watch Later is also hidden compared to other services. It’s under your account, then beneath “Liked videos,” which is below any you may have uploaded yourself.

On a TV, the process is so annoying I just avoid it altogether. You go to the sidebar menu > Library > Watch later. It should be front-and-center on a TV. It’s almost unforgivable in my eyes because even your watch history is given greater prominence. I simply cannot understand that decision.

I’m not always a huge fan of AI being shoehorned into apps and services, but this is one of those areas that has been left to rot for a while and could get a big uplift from adding in a little bit of Gemini. Even if it were just an enhanced search function or more tools for organizing.

With YouTube TV effectively taking over regular broadcast television and being the world’s biggest streaming platform, I’m shocked by how so many areas of the platform remain in a consistently neglected state.

A little bit of spit and polish, and maybe I’ll start using this section again. For now, though, I’ll stick to texting myself links of stuff I want to watch. The ball is in your court, Google.
