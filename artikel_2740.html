Jahmyr Gibbs in Hall of Fame company with a long list of statistical achievements

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Lions running back Jahmyr Gibbs had another phenomenal game on Sunday, and in his fourth NFL season he has hit a long list of statistical milestones that only Hall of Famers have reached before him.

In Sunday’s 35-10 win over the Patriots, Gibbs carried 22 times for 153 yards and two touchdowns, and added seven catches for 99 yards. After the game, the NFL distributed a list of Gibbs’ stats that show him either equaling or exceeding the accomplishments of some of the greatest running backs ever to play the game. Among Gibbs’ milestones:

Gibbs has the most touchdowns in NFL history in a player’s first four seasons. He has now scored 62 touchdowns (50 rushing and 12 receiving), breaking the previous record of Hall of Famer Terrell Davis, who had 61 touchdowns in his first four seasons.

Gibbs’ 50 career rushing touchdowns make him the third player in NFL history to run for 50 touchdowns before turning 25. The other two are Hall of Famers Barry Sanders with 52, and Emmitt Smith with 50. Gibbs doesn’t turn 25 until after this season, so he’s a near-lock to move ahead of Sanders and into first place on the all-time list.

Gibbs and Hall of Famer LaDainian Tomlinson are the only players in NFL history to have multiple games with at least 250 scrimmage yards and two rushing touchdowns before turning 25.

Gibbs, Tomlinson and Hall of Famer Eric Dickerson are the only players to have four games of at least 200 scrimmage yards and two rushing touchdowns in their first four seasons.

Gibbs has topped 1,200 scrimmage yards and 10 rushing touchdowns in each of his first four seasons. The only players who have done that before are Tomlinson, Dickerson, Hall of Famer Earl Campbell and future Hall of Famer Adrian Peterson.

Gibbs and Amon-Ra St. Brown have played 24 games together in which they’ve both scored touchdowns, surpassing Bills Hall of Famers Thurman Thomas and Andre Reed, who both scored in 23 games, for the fourth-most games in which running back-wide receiver teammates both score. All the players ahead of Gibbs and St. Brown are Hall of Famers: Emmitt Smith and Michael Irvin did it 32 times for the Cowboys, Lenny Moore and Raymond Berry did it 31 times for the Colts, and Edgerrin James and Marvin Harrison did it 25 times for the Colts.

Only three and a half seasons into his NFL career, it’s too early to put Gibbs in the Hall of Fame. But everything about the way he has played so far indicates that some day he’ll have a bust in Canton.
