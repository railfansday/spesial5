3 takeaways from Kansas State's stunning loss to SDSU in Casey Alexander's debut

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

MANHATTAN — The Casey Alexander era at Kansas State started with a dud.

Kansas State tipped off its season with a 68-66 loss to South Dakota State on Tuesday, Nov. 3, at Bramlage Coliseum. It was the Wildcats' debut under Alexander, who was hired by the university this offseason, replacing Jerome Tang.

The Wildcats paid the Jackrabbits $90,000 to play the game. SDSU redshirt-freshman Davis Kern finished the night with 24 points.

Here are our takeaways from opening night.

Trailing 35-28 going into halftime, the Wildcats knocked down their first four 3-point attempts and looked like they took control of the game. A 16-3 run featured K-State making its first four 3-point attempts, along with multiple extra-effort plays from Pape N'Diaye and Jaylen Alexander. K-State led by as many as seven with 11:39 left.

A 9-0 run after the 8-minute mark flipped the score from a four-point K-State lead to a four-point Jacks lead. After that, it was back-and-forth until SDSU's Kalen Garry drilled a triple to go up by six with 1:40 left.

After a pair of Isaiah Abraham free throws with 39 seconds left to cut the Jacks' lead to two, the Wildcats forced a turnover, but Dezdrick Lindsay couldn't get a game-tying layup to fall with 7.5 seconds left.

The Wildcats got one more chance after a missed SDSU free throw. Brandon Rechsteiner's game-tying floater at the horn couldn't fall.

Alexander had the most efficient offense in college basketball last season at Belmont, including the nation's best 3-point shooting percentage.

That didn't carry over into the Wildcats' opener, at least in the first half. K-State finished the first 20 minutes by making 3 of 15 shots from beyond the arc. Every player who touched the floor seemed to have the green light if they had an open look.

Out of halftime, the Wildcats stuck with it. They made their first four 3-point attempts of the second period, with Brandon Rechsteiner making two, to quickly erase a seven-point halftime deficit. After that, they came back down to earth.

K-State finished 8 of 28 from deep, with Rechsteiner going 4-for-11.

The Wildcats didn't exactly instill confidence that they will be better than projected. South Dakota State is trending toward a better season than it had a year ago, but the Wildcats should beat an upper-half Summit League team.

Some sloppiness appeared, notably with a shot-clock and five-second violation. SDSU redshirt-freshman Davis Kern got hot in the first half, making the first three 3-pointers he threw up. SDSU also had a 26-22 advantage in the paint.
