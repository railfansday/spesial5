Kirk Cousins’ successful debut vs. Packers proves Vikings can win Super Bowl with him as QB1

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Kirk Cousins' successful debut vs. Packers proves Vikings can win Super Bowl with him as QB1 appeared first on ClutchPoints. Add ClutchPoints as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

In a somewhat surprising development, Minnesota Vikings' quarterback Kirk Cousins hit the ground running despite a short time to prepare. And he seems to fit with the team nicely. His successful debut against the Packers proves the Vikings can win the Super Bowl with him as QB1.

The Vikings struggled on offense for most of the season with Kyler Murray and Carson Wentz trying to solve the issue at quarterback. But against Green Bay on Sunday, the Vikings dominated on both sides of the ball in a 30-10 beatdown.

There's little doubt that the Vikings have a Super Bowl-level defense, so the addition of Cousins gives the team a real shot to make postseason noise in 2026.

Cousins threw for 207 yards and two touchdowns in the win over the Packers. And the great re-debut left him emotional, according to a post on X by Lindsey Thiry via NFL.com.

"It was emotional," Cousins said, his voice cracking halfway through his answer. "Coming to this stadium three years ago, tearing my Achilles here, it's been a tough three years. I was just grateful for the opportunity to play here today and grateful for my teammates and what they did today to make this outcome possible."

Cousins said it was a difficult week, according to startribune.com.

"The process [of getting ready] this week certainly was difficult," Cousins said. "Got in late Tuesday night. I didn't know what my code was to even get in the building on Wednesday."

The Vikings seemed to have a swagger on offense they haven't had all season. It seemed to be an instant buy-in by all of the players. And it looked like an instant chemistry between Cousins and the offense, according to a post on X by Alec Lewis.

"Vikings center Blake Brandel on QB Kirk Cousins's impact: 'His professionalism and leadership cannot be overstated. It's one of those things where the stuff that he's able to do might not show on the stat sheet, but it empowers guys and makes a difference in the offense.' "

One thing Cousins did that Murray and Wentz couldn't do was make Jefferson an integral part of the offense. The veteran receiver had nine catches for 74 yards. That marked the highest number of receptions for Jefferson this season.

And head coach Kevin O'Connell said Cousins made a big difference overall, according to startribune.com.1680

"He knows about Justin, he knows about Jordan [Addison], he knows about T.J. [Hockenson]," O'Connell said. "But some of these [young players], he wanted to hear about them, and then he started pouring into those guys: pouring into the relationship, pouring into, 'Hey on this route here, I just want you to be here,' whatever it is. People were incredibly comfortable with him. I think the huddle really operated efficiently."

This is looking like the real deal. It's both sides of the ball. And Cousins acknowledged the defense. He said it was special to have Andrew Van Ginkel on his side.

"[Van Ginkel] is unbelievable," Cousins said. "I've played against him. Practiced against him in '23. It's different watching him turn the ball over like that. It's a different brand of football when you have a defense like that."

And the plan turned out to be sound and solid. Their stout man coverage, pressing Christian Watson and Matthew Golden, helped the Vikings record four of their eight sacks of Jordan Love. Plus, they did it without linebacker Blake Cashman, one of their best defenders. It wasn't lost on safety Harrison Smith.

"I mean, 'Cash' has been probably our best player," Smith said. "We obviously want him out there, but we have guys who know what to do. We can build it how we have to."

This team is for real, now. And any mention of being in the Super Bowl conversation is warranted and fair.

However, the Vikings can't rest on what they've done so far. That's the observation of right tackle Brian O'Neill, according to startribune.com.

"We have not arrived," O'Neill said. "We've had one game in the past three weeks of pretty good, decent offensive football. There is a lot more on the bone for us."

However, O'Neill acknowledged Cousins has made a quick difference.

"He was running the show and in command. Man, he was dealing."

If Cousins keeps producing, and the Vikings' defense maintains its level of play, there could be plenty for Vikings fans to get excited about in the playoffs.
