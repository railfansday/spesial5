Bitcoin nears repeat of chart pattern that preceded 2023’s $25K retest

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Bitcoin’s 21-day moving average fell below its 200-day average, and analyst Keith Alan expects a second bearish crossover by Sunday.

Bitcoin (BTC) could repeat a pair of bearish moving-average crossovers that preceded its retest of $25,000 in September 2023, according to analyst Keith Alan.

New analysis from Keith Alan, cofounder of crypto trading platform Material Indicators, compares this week’s Bitcoin chart with a similar setup in 2023.

On Bitstamp’s BTC/USD daily chart, the 21-day simple moving average (SMA) crossed below its 200-day equivalent on Thursday. The move last occurred in early November 2025, around one month after Bitcoin’s latest all-time high of $126,200, in the early stages of the current bear market. The 50-day SMA crossed below the 200-day SMA later that month, forming a conventional death cross.

Alan expects the 50-day SMA to cross below the 100-day SMA by the end of the week.

“We’ve only seen this happen once before in Bitcoin’s history, and it should not be ignored,” he summarized in a post on X.

BTC/USD one-day chart with 21-, 50-, 100- and 200-day SMAs. Source: Cointelegraph/TradingView

The only other time in Bitcoin’s history that the two pairs of moving averages both produced a so-called “death cross” in the same period was between August and September 2023. BTC/USD retested support near $25,000 on Sept. 11.

Alan describes this double bottom as the start of a rally that took Bitcoin to its 2023 high of $44,700 in early December — an increase of 80%.

“One occurrence is by no means a large enough sample to consider a reliable pattern, or expect similar returns/drawdowns, however the timing, placement, and candle characteristics have enough similarities that we can’t ignore it either,” he said.

BTC/USD one-day chart with 21-, 50-, 100- and 200-day SMAs. Source: Cointelegraph/TradingView

Previously, Cointelegraph reported on Bitcoin losing support at the 200-week SMA for the first time since mid-August. The trend line typically functions as support during bull-market retracements, but it can act as resistance when Bitcoin rebounds from below it.
