If You Had Invested $5,000 in Amazon Stock 20 Years Ago, Here's How Much You'd Have Today

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

It's more than just the largest company in the world by sales.

By Jennifer Saibil – Nov 20, 2026 at 6:20AM EST

Amazon (AMZN -1.71%) has made a comeback from trailing the S&P 500, gaining more 18% year to date versus the index’s gain of about 7% (as of Nov. 19). Investors seem to have forgiven its huge capital expenditures on artificial intelligence (AI) since the results have been coming in, and they indicate that what Chief Executive Officer Andy Jassy has been saying about the incredible opportunity has been true all along.

Amazon has been a fantastic investment for investors who've stuck with it through thick and thin. If you'd invested $5,000 in Amazon stock 20 years ago, here's what it would be worth today.

Amazon has displaced Walmart (WMT +2.79%) as the largest company in the world by sales, and it's nowhere near slowing down. It's still growing by double-digit percentages despite its size, driven by its "day one" approach to growth.

Back in 2006, it was a completely different company. Amazon Web Services started in 2002, offering Amazon data storage to clients. But it officially launched as a cloud services provider in 2006. Since then, AWS has become the world's largest cloud provider, with a 28% market share, according to Statista.

Amazon was already an e-commerce superstar at that time, and few people likely saw this opportunity coming. But if you had enough confidence to invest just $5,000 20 years ago, you'd have more than $600,000 today.

Can Amazon still reward investors? Probably not the same way, but I believe it still can. AWS reported a 39% year-over-year sales increase in the third quarter, its highest growth in about five years, on quadruple the underlying sales. Management is also projecting growth to accelerate to 40% in 2027 and 2028. At less than 22 times trailing 12-month earnings, the stock has room to rise.

The stock should reflect that growth over the next few years, but given its nearly $3 trillion value, the stock isn't likely to gain another 12,000%.
