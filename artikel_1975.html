Club adviser updates on Thomas Frank getting 'premium contract' at Crystal Palace

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Thomas Frank is in no position to demand a lucrative contract in order to take charge as Crystal Palace's next manager.

That is according to former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who has doubts about whether the Dane is the right fit for the role.

The Eagles sacked Pierre Sage on Thursday and are now hunting for a swift replacement, with Frank one of the clear favourites for the job.

However, Wyness is concerned that Frank may have merely been part of this "machine" at Brentford, and may struggle to replicate his feats at Selhurst Park.

The 53-year-old has been out of work since he was sacked by Tottenham back in February, but that could soon be about to change.

He is a vastly experienced Premier League manager at this stage of his career, but Wyness does not expect him to be holding out for a lucrative deal at Palace.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that Frank was allowed to thrive at Brentford by their ability to consistently replace key players who departed.

He does not feel that Palace are capable of matching that model any more, following the exit of ex-sporting director Dougie Freedman back in 2025.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained that Frank is not well-placed when it comes to potential negotiations with Palace.

"I don't know if he's got the chance to be extra lucrative. I know that people would say he's probably the only one with this mid-level experience, and he's had success in the past," he said.

"My question about Frank would be his biggest success came at Brentford, where he was really a cog in a very well-run machine. It wasn't to my view that it was the Thomas Frank show; there was a whole background at Brentford that helped him do really well.

"And I don't know if that's there at Palace anymore without Dougie Freedman. So, you're taking a gamble on Frank to a degree, but it does seem he seems to be the front runner and the favourite to go forward.

"I don't think he deserves a premium. I do rate Frank as a manager, but as I say, you've got to consider how much he was helped in that Brentford machine as well, and whether it's there at Palace, and what problems he has to come in and solve.

"Is it man management? Is it a bad squad overall? Is it something that the manager can't just fix? That's what you've got to diagnose. You've got to try and pick the right manager for those problems."

Wyness told Football Insider earlier this week that Palace would be taking far too big a risk by appointing Anthony Barry as Sage's repalcement.

The England assistant coach has emerged as a surprise target for the South London outfit, having also worked with Thomas Tuchel at both Chelsea and Bayern Munich.

Wyness believes that "everybody rates" Barry within footballing circles, but feels that he is too inexperienced to get a Premier League job at the moment.

The 40-year-old has an excellent reputation at the top of European football as a coach, but has never been a manager before at senior level.
