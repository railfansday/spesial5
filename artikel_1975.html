Finance Guru raises 'question marks' over PIF's Newcastle future amid 'amazing' claim

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

PIF's future as Newcastle United owners is not guaranteed despite new audacious plans away from St James' Park.

That is according to former Manchester City financial adviser Stefan Borson, who exclusively told Football Insider that the owners' "appetite" for sport should be questioned.

PIF's future remains a talking point on Tyneside, and was especially major during the summer transfer window, as key players left on big-money deals.

Eddie Howe also stepped down as the Magpies went in a completely different direction, causing fans to raise questions over their ambitions.

However, the latest reports state that Newcastle's owners have been in advanced talks over plans for the building of a £1.3billion super stadium.

The news has certainly split opinion, and while it may suggest further investment from PIF, it does not completely rule out another takeover.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Borson has been cautious about ruling out PIF's exit, just because of new stadium plans emerging.

"I'm not saying that they are going to sell, but I do have a concern about the appetite of PIF to sport," he explained.

"They've moved the strategy a little bit away from sport. But personally, I don't think it changes much, right?

"Because right now, it is some nice drawings, some discussions with the sorts of people that you would want to discuss it with on viability.

"It's not impossible that PIF and the Reubens decide, 'Actually, this is an amazing opportunity to build in Newcastle,' and they do that and they fund it themselves with the banks that they have amazing relationships with.

"And make no mistake, Newcastle is not just PIF. The Reuben family are incredibly wealthy as well and very much involved at Newcastle and have stood their corner on every fundraising from memory.

"It's not impossible that they do decide to do this as part of a bigger project, but likewise I don't think it means there's no way that PIF would look to exit the situation. I think there are still question marks."

All signs currently point towards an entirely new stadium being built, rather than developing the famous one already standing on Tyneside.

This is obviously going to raise some concerns, with Newcastle having played their since 1892, but change is sometimes inevitable in the sport.

Borson has already told Football Insider that redeveloping St James' Park could end up being more expensive than building a new stadium altogether.

Newcastle's owners are incredibly smart, and if there is a cheaper way of doing business, you would expect them to go down that route instead.
