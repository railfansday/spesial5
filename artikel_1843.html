Celtic reveal plans for Sorensen, Van den Berg, Sinisalo and Europe

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Celtic’s historic European dream hinges on remaining unbeaten in their next two Europa League ties in Portugal and Cyprus as they seek to build on the win over Besiktas before the international break.

This, at least, is manager Martin O'Neill's view ahead of Thursday night's clash against Torreense, who have already amassed twice as many points as Celtic. The Portuguese second-tier side might be the most likely source of the win O'Neill feels is required. He has challenged his team to secure four points from the next two away fixtures.

The 3-2 win over Besiktas earlier this month breathed some life back into the Parkhead club’s European campaign. "It gives us a chance, you know," said O'Neill.

Celtic are currently sitting 32nd out of 36 teams in the standings. They will have to break new ground to finish among the top 24 and secure a place in the knockout stage. No team has managed to do so after opening with three defeats in the new European league format.

After Torreense in Leiria on Thursday Celtic travel to meet Omonia Nicosia in their final European game before Christmas. They play Marseille and Union St. Gilloise early in the new year.

“You'd have to think that we'd need to get four points in the two games to give ourselves a chance so that you've got two left over, including a home game, which will not be easy," said O'Neill. "It would be nice to carry it into January and still have a chance.

"We've taken enough criticism for not doing well enough in Europe and rightly so," he added. "It's the aim of the football club, it's what we've always said." O’Neill recalled a sponsors’ event last week where someone mentioned the manner of the Champions League qualifying exit. “Sometimes you feel as if we've never really got over the LASK thing,” he said.

Although much is at stake, O’Neill is still confident that bit-part players to date such as Oliver Sorensen and Joel van den Berg can play a part in the European effort.

Both were signed on the last day of the summer transfer window and were included in the European squad. Sorensen has failed to make an impression despite starting the opening two Europa League defeats against Ferencvaros and Benfica while Van den Berg has been sidelined with an ankle injury.

The Dutch midfielder made his belated debut as a substitute in Saturday’s 4-1 win over St Mirren. Sorensen, meanwhile, has not featured in the last two matchday squads due to a family bereavement. Celtic face a period where auxiliary players will be required to step up.

“This is a chance, with a bit of luck, for the likes of Sorensen and Van den Berg,” said O’Neill. He challenged Van den Berg to kick on following his cameo at the weekend. He hopes Sorensen, too, can begin to nail down a regular place in the side.

"He has, by his own admission, found it difficult,” said the manager “I think that he also arrived late as well too, trying to get to know people, trying to get his position, trying to get up to speed with what we do.

"And whatever we do, I bet you it'll be something really different to what he was doing at Parma. But now it would be up to him.”

Viljami Sinisalo has been waiting for his turn back in goal. O’Neill still hasn't made up mind about who will stay at No. 1.

Sam Johnstone is clearly in possession of the gloves at present even if it would not be a surprise if Sinisalo is given a match before long, perhaps as soon as Thursday. “There’s still good competition,” said the manager. “Viljami wants to be the keeper here, which is great.

“He will play again and at some stage you have to make you’re mind up who will stay as no.1. I don’t think there’s a phenomenal amount between them at this stage.”
