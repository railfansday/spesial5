How the MLS Season and Play-Offs Work (Explained)

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Major League Soccer — commonly known as MLS — is only growing in size and reputation. The United States is almost always at the forefront of every sport, particularly at the Olympics, but they've always struggled to break into the elite in football.

In recent years, they have tried to catch up with the best leagues in the world by signing some of the greatest players of all time. This included Lionel Messi joining Inter Miami in 2023. The former Barcelona star signed an initial two-year deal, with an option of a third, at $12m per campaign, whilst Apple TV also pay him $60m a year to play in the division. Although it was a signing for David Beckham's side, it was technically a major coup for the whole division — and it brought more fans to it.

Due to this, we have decided to outline the system and format of the MLS play-offs. It might initially seem complicated as it's not the same as most European leagues. The league finishes in a one-off cup match to decide the champions, highlighting how American sports always want to create as much drama as possible for their fans.

The United States' biggest soccer league has grown in recent years, especially with the introduction of football legends, like Lionel Messi.

The MLS consists of 30 clubs from the USA and Canada, with the teams split into the Eastern and Western Conferences. Unsurprisingly, teams situated on the west coast of America play in the Western Conference, whilst the same trend follows in the east. Every team plays 34 matches, with 17 at home and 17 away. The regular season lasts from February to mid-October with 18 of the 30 clubs advancing to the play-offs at the season's end.

Clubs face each team in their own conference twice – once at home and once away – and play six games against teams from the opposing conference. Once the conferences are complete, the Supporters' Shield is awarded to the club with the best regular season record across both conferences, while the top nine teams in each Conference progress to the play-offs.

During the regular MLS season takes place, teams also compete in the US Open Cup. It is the country's oldest soccer tournament, with 80 teams competing in a knockout competition to determine the champion. Some fans have described the US Open Cup as the American equivalent to the Carabao Cup.

In December 2023, MLS teams attempted to withdraw their teams from the competition, which has been contested every year for well over a century. Their withdrawals were retracted when proposals to field its developmental MLS Next Pro teams were rebuffed by U.S. Soccer. Now, the teams competing in the CONCACAF Champions Cup do not send squads, but the other clubs have to.

Although form in the conferences is important, it only matters until clubs have qualified for the play-offs. After that, the records are meaningless as the play-offs take centre stage in October. The top seven sides from each conference automatically qualify for the MLS play-offs.

But the sides that finish eighth and ninth in the two conferences compete in the wild-card round of the play-offs, with the winners advancing to the playoffs to face the first-place teams from the two conferences. The second placed teams will host the seventh placed teams, and the third placed teams will host the sixth placed teams, and so on and so forth.

All rounds are single-match elimination games hosted by the higher seed, except for the first round, which is contested under the best-of-three stipulation. The best-of-three ruling means that to advance to the next round, teams must win two matches out of a potential three against their designated opponents. If one team wins the opening two matches in the series, there will be no need for a third decisive match. But if the teams win one match each, the third in the series becomes a dramatic, decisive encounter for a spot in the semi-finals. The best-of-three matches are win-only, with no draws or extra time. Matches that end in a draw after 90 minutes go straight to penalties.

The first round winners then advance to the Conference semi-finals and then the final – the winner of the final will then advance to the MLS Cup final, which sees the final-winning Eastern side play against the final-winning Western side. The final is played at the ground of the team with the best record in the regular season.

Some have branded the format controversial, as it does not necessarily reward the most consistent teams. A club could win every single match in the main bulk of the conference round, but then get immediately knocked out in the first round of the play-offs. It's completely different to the Premier League, one of the hardest competitions in the world to win, which sees the team that accumulates the most points over 38 games crowned champions.

The MLS Cup brings together the best of US soccer, as Lionel Messi and co aim to lift the prestigious prize, that comes with plenty of added rewards.

Every season, five MLS teams are guaranteed to qualify for the CONCACAF Champions Cup, which is North America’s equivalent to the UEFA Champions League. However, despite three Canadian teams competing in the MLS, they can not qualify for the continental competition through the domestic league.

The first spot goes to the MLS Cup winners, who receive a bye to the Round of 16. A further two spots are given to the winners of the Western and Eastern Conferences. The fourth and fifth spots are given to the next-highest-ranked teams in the Supporters' Shield table.

If a team qualifies through multiple berths, e.g., winning their conference and the MLS Cup, or a guaranteed spot is taken by a Canadian MLS team, the Champions Cup spot is re-allocated to the next best US team in the overall table. Canadian MLS clubs also play against other Canadian clubs in the Canadian Championship – their annual cup competition – for one spot in the CONCACAF Champions Cup.

Including Landon Donovan, Clint Dempsey, and Christian Pulisic, the best USMNT players of all time have been ranked.

Alongside other competitions, MLS takes a break during the middle of the season for the Leagues Cup tournament, where every club from MLS and Mexico's Liga MX take part in a World Cup-style tournament.

From 2025, the format was adjusted to increase the number of inter-league matches. 36 teams compete, comprising the top 18 teams from MLS and the top 18 teams from Liga MX. Each team plays three matches against clubs from the other league and the top four teams from each league advance to the quarter-finals of the knockout stage. The knockout stage consists of single-elimination fixtures to determine the overall winner of the Leagues Cup. The quarter-final matches are therefore guaranteed to be MLS vs Liga MX fixtures.

The North American continental tournament features both the MLS and Liga MX teams, but how financially lucrative is it?

In most leagues across the world, it is relatively easy to build a squad, but that's not the case in MLS. Each club makes its own decisions on how it builds its roster and spends its money, but it must do so within the guidelines set forth by the league. Among the league rules are maximum salary budgets that prevent clubs from paying players over a certain amount of money, though exceptions exist, as in the case of "designated players," or the Beckham Rule as it is colloquially known.

Up to 20 players, occupying roster slots one to 20, count against the club's salary budget of $5,470,000 and are referred to collectively as the club's senior roster. Meanwhile, the salaries of players on the supplemental roster (slots 21-30) do not count toward their salary budget.

Finally, one of the most important stipulations comes under the ruling attached to the designated players, which gives clubs the freedom to sign players who may demand a higher salary than the budget rules allow for. It helps the league grow, and big name signings like Lionel Messi, Thierry Henry, and David Beckham, who was the league's first designated player, have been signed by MLS clubs under the ruling without breaking budget rules. Clubs are allowed to sign three players under the rule as long as the club bear financial responsibility for the amount of compensation above each player's salary budget charge.

In 2024, a designated player who was at least 24 years old during the league year carried the maximum salary budget charge ($683,750) unless the player joined his club after the opening of the secondary transfer window. In that case, the budget charge was $341,875. As part of this, clubs may trade a designated player as long as they remain responsible for all the out-of-pocket costs, but they can only trade one per year. Due to these rules, most players who ply their trade in the MLS are Canadian or American, but the specific rules have allowed the division to grow.

Teams face a short, 14-match mini-season and an MLS Cup as the Major League Soccer calendar shifts to match other major football leagues.
