Keith Wyness reveals Everton takeover 'verified' timeline

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Everton's takeover could potentially take seven months from the start of November to be completed in full.

That is according to former Toffees chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who has been involved in several attempted takeovers at Premier League clubs.

The Friedkin Group are reportedly in the process of weeding out the "serious buyers," having already received interest from several different parties.

Wyness outlined that the process of verifying each individual candidate can often take up to six weeks alone, with many more stages to come after that.

The Scottish businessman previously told Football Insider that Everton can expect to have a new owner in place by next summer, and he still feels that is a realistic timeline.

However, the club have several hurdles that they must overcome before they can even get close to that stage, with several arduous months on the horizon.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that Everton's timeline for an end-of-season deal remains realistic.

The boardroom expert has indicated that the Friedkins will wait on proof of funds from each interested party before progressing through their due diligence and naming a preferred bidder.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained exactly how many stages there are to the process of selling a club.

"I've been around a few Premier League takeovers; it'll take between five and seven months overall for the whole process," he said.

"You're going to need probably four to six weeks right now for the verification and the whole thing to get sorted out, depending on how quickly that gets done.

"Then you're going to need proof of funds. Then you're going to go on to the due diligence and that sort of area. And then you're going to get into a preferred bidder stage, where you get into the whole owners and directors test from the Premier League.

"The whole thing can take between five and seven months. That's the timeline I'd be looking at, and that fits in with what we said of trying to get a new owner in before next summer, which I think would be everybody's goal both from the buy side and the seller side."

Wyness previously told Football Insider earlier this week that Amanda Staveley's chances of buying Everton have "increased" now that Michael Dell is out of the running.

The American businessman had been heavily linked with the Merseyside outfit, but recently distanced himself from a potential takeover.

Wyness feels that Staveley's chances have grown as a result, but believes that she needs to make her backers known publicly if she is to have a genuine chance of striking a deal.

The former Newcastle director is renowned for her ability to build consortiums and generate financial backing, but it is unclear who she is allied with at the moment.
