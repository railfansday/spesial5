First Systemic Therapy for Nonsegmental Vitiligo Gets FDA Green Light

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

— Upadacitinib reduced facial and total body area affected by 40-50% at 76 weeks

The FDA has approved upadacitinib (Rinvoq) for nonsegmental vitiligo (NSV), the first systemic therapy approved for the condition.

The approval allows use of the JAK inhibitor in patients 12 years or older, according to an announcement from AbbVie. Other approved therapies, including JAK inhibitors, are topical and typically used to treat specific areas of skin depigmentation.

"For the vitiligo community, approval of the first systemic treatment option for nonsegmental vitiligo is an important development in advancing care," Richard Huggins, MD, of Henry Ford Health in Detroit, said in the announcement. "This approval reflects growing recognition of vitiligo as a serious autoimmune disease and the true burden it places on those living with the condition."

Principal support for the approval came from the phase III Viti-Up clinical program comprising two identically designed, randomized, placebo-controlled trials involving adults and adolescents with NSV. The co-primary endpoints in both trials were clinically meaningful improvement in facial repigmentation (F-VASI 75) and total body repigmentation (T-VASI 50) after 48 weeks.

The results showed that about a fourth of patients on the JAK inhibitor achieved F-VASI 75 at 48 weeks in both trials versus 6-7% of the placebo group. Rates of T-VASI 50 at 48 weeks were 20% with upadacitinib and 6% with placebo. After 76 weeks of follow-up, about half of upadacitinib-treated patients met F-VASI 75 criteria in the two studies combined, and 40-45% met T-VASI 50 response criteria.

"The Viti-Up clinical program is particularly impactful because it demonstrates the potential of a systemic treatment to achieve robust facial and total body repigmentation," said investigator Seemal Desai, MD, of Innovative Dermatology in Dallas and president of the Global Vitiligo Foundation. "We know that repigmentation takes time, and the significant improvements observed at week 76, with facial and total body response rates doubling or nearly doubling from week 48, are especially encouraging and reinforce the value of a treatment approach that targets the disease from within."

Neither clinical trial had reports of major adverse cardiovascular events, venous thromboembolic events, gastrointestinal perforations, active tuberculosis, lymphoma, non-melanoma skin cancer, or opportunistic infections other than herpes zoster. Overall, safety data were consistent with the known upadacitinib safety profile.

The recommended upadacitinib dose for NSV is 15 mg. The NSV indication adds to existing approvals for the JAK inhibitor that include rheumatoid arthritis, atopic dermatitis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis, non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis, giant cell arteritis, ulcerative colitis, and Crohn's disease.
