The Best Foods To Buy At Costco In November

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Our editors were blown away by one game-changing snack.

The holiday season is on the horizon, and Costco is celebrating with plenty of festive foods in every corner of the warehouse. Aside from the obligatory pumpkin pie, you can find party-approved appetizers, cozy bakery treats, and plenty of fall-forward snacks.

I spend all year keeping tabs on the latest and greatest products to hit Costco shelves. And as someone who writes about Costco for a living, I have a hot take: The food at the warehouse right now is the best it’s been all year.

Here’s what’s available this November—there are some treats you don’t want to miss.

The viral sensation has officially hit Costco. The Dot Cakes is a bakery on Long Island, New York, that exploded in popularity this summer thanks to its signature dessert: a single-serving cake in a cup covered in a generous layer of nonpareil sprinkles. Now, instead of waiting in long lines or trying to recreate it at home, Costco members can buy a six-pack of the real thing.

Each box includes both chocolate and vanilla cake, and both flavors are perfectly nostalgic. They taste like the best-ever birthday cake from your childhood, and the crunch of the sprinkles makes the experience even more fun.

It admittedly takes a lot for us to get excited about grapes—but these are worth the hype. Shine Muscat is a premium variety from Japan, famous for its large, sweet, seedless fruit. Many of the ones sold in the U.S. are imported from East Asia, including these Korean grapes now available at Costco.

I’ve had the pleasure of trying the grapes in smoothie form at a Costco food court in Seoul, but the real thing is even better. Our editors couldn’t stop eating them and compared the flavor to a plum or lychee. And at $10.99, they’re a fun upgrade that costs only a bit more than standard table grapes.

Want takeout flavors without takeout prices? Costco is now selling a noodle stir-fry kit that can feed the whole family for around $15. Each tray includes crispy breaded chicken pieces, yakisoba noodles, vegetables, and a sweet-and-savory sauce.

The meal is super easy to put together: Just crisp up the chicken in the oven or air fryer, then heat everything else in a pan. Costco is generous with the chicken and noodles, so the kit can provide at least four substantial servings. But if you need just one reason to add it to your cart, do it for the sauce. It’s just sweet enough with the slightest hint of spice.

Cabot Creamery has already combined its cheddar with popcorn, but this fall-forward version balances the sharp, salty flavor with a bit of sweetness. Each bag includes two types of popcorn: one seasoned with Cabot’s famous Seriously Sharp Cheddar and another with a maple syrup-flavored coating. Both are delicious on their own, but together? Magic happens.

The salty-sweet combo really works here, reminding us of those large popcorn tins with three different compartments, except everything’s mixed together. Your holiday movie marathon needs a bag of this.

Fans of the Kirkland Signature peanut butter pretzels need to grab this sweet treat. Jif’s Snappers combine mini pretzels, the brand’s creamy peanut butter, and a drizzle of milk chocolate. We love that there’s a higher peanut butter-to-pretzel ratio than with Kirkland Signature's version (and the PB is much softer). This snack has everything: It’s salty, sweet, crunchy, creamy, and impossible to put down.

Step aside, Snap, Crackle, and Pop. The brand Cloud 9 has upgraded the cereal-based sweet with flavors inspired by mango sticky rice. Each bar combines puffed rice with dried mango, coconut milk, and shredded coconut. And the Delish team agrees: This is arguably the best snack Costco has sold all year. It’s loaded with mango, lightly sweet, and full of interesting textures. Buy a bag and thank us later.

In just a few years, Swedish candy went from an uncommon novelty to a permanent fixture in the candy aisle. Now, Costco carries a Rolling Pin variety pack that includes sour gummies and foam candy (a Swedish staple with a marshmallow-like texture).

This 30-ounce bag features a wide variety of chewy textures and fruity flavors. If you’re just now dipping your toes into the world of Swedish candy, this is a great (and affordable) way to start.

Need fall breakfast inspo? Look no further. The newest addition to the Costco bakery is this maple pecan coffee cake, which is loaded with cream cheese, pecans, and maple flavor. The cake is topped with a crumbly pecan butter streusel and drizzled with the same icing Costco uses on its danishes.

Each coffee cake is $15, but it’s arguably worth much more given its sheer size. This thing is a behemoth—it’s tall and absolutely loaded with toppings. There’s also a thick layer of filling in the center that tastes nearly identical to pecan pie filling.

Baked brie is a party showstopper, and Costco now carries a kit with everything you need in one convenient package. Stonemill Kitchens’ kit includes puff pastry, a wheel of brie, chopped pecans, and caramel apple cranberry chutney. You’ll need to assemble it yourself before you throw it in the oven, but the instructions couldn’t be simpler. The puff pastry even comes with a sheet of parchment paper to protect your baking sheet!

And the results are straight-up showstopping. The melty, creamy brie pairs perfectly with the pastry and toppings. The chutney, despite the caramel in the name, isn’t too sweet—it adds just the right amount of festive holiday flavor. It’s so good that News and Features Editor Amanda Mactas even did a happy dance after taking a bite.
