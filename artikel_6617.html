Buying Your First Pair of Selvedge Jeans? Start Here

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

We’ve got bad news for white couches: Crunchy, indigo-dyed, raw selvedge jeans are back. People might say they never left, but as someone who literally edited a daily web series about denim fades from 2015 to 2018, I can assure you that across the almost-decade since, the category has taken something of a back seat as jeans with interesting washes and nostalgic cuts (Our Legacy’s Third Cut, GAP’s 90s Loose) surged in popularity.

But while it, ahem, faded from the public eye, the raw denim market was still tended to by the true believers among us. And we owe them a great debt, because their stewardship means we’re not just picking up where the last trend left off. Instead, we’re jumping into an elevated timeline. The best makers in the world have only gotten better, the cuts have gotten more diverse, and a whole bunch of stuff that was once incredibly hard to find in the United States is now readily available.

So available, in fact, that the selection can be overwhelming. Which is where I come in! Because while there are now thousands of pairs of raw denim jeans to choose from, I can tell you that only a precious few specific cuts have consistently dominated Subreddits, YouTube roundups, and my text message history for the best part of this century. Ten of them are below, starting with five approachable icons, and moving through to five more legendary deeper cuts. Let’s get faded.

The company behind Sugar Cane, Toyo Enterprise, has been making hard-wearing garments since the mid-’60s, but Sugar Cane didn’t come around until 1975. Ever since, they’ve been making some of the most considered, sturdy and timeless jeans and workwear on the planet in Japan, all of it aging more beautifully than a fancy barolo.

If you’re looking to dip your toes into the Sugar Cane pool, the 1947 model brilliantly recreates one of the most well-loved iterations of the most well-worn model on the planet (we won’t name names, but they’re in here too), and comes in a four-season, 14.25oz. selvedge denim fabric that will produce some preposterously good fades.

For anyone who’s fallen in love with a pair of old old vintage jeans, and wondered what they felt like when they came off the production line, it’s hard to get closer than a pair of Fullcounts. The brand was started in 1993 in Osaka, Japan, with the express purpose of reverse engineering the wonder of those vintage pairs, down to the all-cotton threads and long-staple cotton.

They sure figured it out, and still make everything in Japan. And while the classic 0105 is an absolute knockout, the Wide Straight iteration gives you a little more comfort with the same break-in potential. And if you’re just getting yourself into the raw selvedge game, a little comfort never hurts.

The only Canadian entrant on this list—partly because Henry’s hasn’t restocked yet—Naked & Famous has been around since 2008. And as you might expect from a brand whose hallmark fit is called the Weird Guy, they’re a little quirky. In a good way! For not only are they rigorously dedicated to sourcing the sickest raw denim on the planet, but that denim often has some funky detailing as well, like a green weft or some galaxy-like imperfections.

The True Guy cut is technically a relaxed straight (don’t confuse that for baggy) and this particular pair has kevlar woven into the fabric, for those into literal bulletproof denim.

If you’ve ever spent time on denim message boards, you’ve probably seen the name “Iron Heart” associated with some of the heaviest denim known to man. (If you’ve never been, steel yourself before entering.) And while it is true that the Japanese brand pushes the definition of heavyweight to its absolute limit, it also makes a damn good pair of regular jeans.

In fact, the mid-weight fare is as good as any on the market, will produce some HOF-worthy fades, and won’t require a title fight to soften up. Within that weight class, the straight leg of the 634S-142 is the kind of timeless silhouette that should outlast most trend cycles. (Also, for the record, “mid-weight” is a relative term. This is the lightest-weight denim that Iron Heart will entertain.)

While the US quite literally invented jeans as we understand them (denim as a fabric is another story, for those keeping very detailed score), you’d be forgiven for thinking otherwise, considering the lack of US-made options on this list. But thankfully, 3Sixteen is doing the work of like six companies at this point, sourcing the world’s finest denim—we don’t really make that here anymore—and stitching the 5-pockets up back home in the USA.

Every cut they make has a legion of fans, but the semi-recent, semi-baggy RS is the one tearing up the charts at the moment. And for great reasons. It’s got the aforementioned room, has a top block (re: the area that houses the waistband, fly and pockets) that doesn’t get all bunched up and funky, and a higher rise that’s flattering on just about everyone. As a result, it’s a pair that both denimheads and denim rookies appreciate with the same kind of zeal.

When it comes to details that will turn a denimhead into a living meme—Vince McMahon Progression, Guy Turning Around, you pick—few brands can execute them like Oni can. From the denim itself, which is milled in Japan and feels more handmade than small-batch sourdough, to the period-accurate details like flat-felled seams and linear bar tacks, every pair starts as a work of art and develops into a wholly separate work of art as they’re worn.

The only issue, really, is finding them, because hand-making just about everything isn’t exactly a scalable process. When they’re around, (and they currently are!) they’re worth more than just a gander, particularly the Wide Straight, which has a loose, straight leg and a healthy, fairly forgiving rise. This pair in particular is made with lighter-weight 12oz. denim, but don’t mistake that for lightweight denim—it’ll hold up year round, and could always handle some longjohns during a cold-snap, if you feel so inclined.

Those familiar with the Osaka 5—the loose collection of denim brands that sprouted up in Osaka in the ‘70s, ‘80s and ‘90s and redefined the very notion of raw denim specifically and the idea of what “jeans” could be, more broadly—will know that Warehouse was the fifth and final member, founded by two alums from a different member, Evisu. And for roughly 30 years the brand’s pumped out intricately detailed, vintage-indebted jeans that some of the pickiest people on the planet have sworn by, even going so far as sourcing American cotton to keep the look and feel as close to the source material as possible.

This pair in particular is based on one of the most popular pairs of 501s Levi’s ever made, the 1966 model, which had a straight leg and a high rise and a slightly twisted seam after a few washes, giving it a slow-developing signature—all present and accounted for here in 2026 via Warehouse.

These aren’t for the faint of heart. Let’s get that out of the way. 21oz. denim is almost armor, especially if your previous denim reference point is something with a wash, a rinse or some stretch. But for anyone looking for a challenge, absolutely electric fades and a pair of jeans that might outlast horseshoe crabs, look no further.

That’s because Samurai, another Osaka-based brand (but not part of the Osaka 5, for whatever it’s worth), makes some of the hardest-fading, hardest-wearing, most-revered jeans anywhere, and that includes the ultra-heavyweight numbers. And who knows, all that extra (fabric) weight might be nice with winter incoming.

Levi’s offers some great raw selvedge 501s at a pretty reasonable price, a pair that will age well and (with proper care of course) last for years. The 501, however, isn’t a standardized cut. In fact, it’s tweaked every few years to reflect the prevailing sentiment of the time a little bit better. And as a result, different eras have different hallmarks—for instance, the 1947 is a bit slimmer, the 1954 is the first pair with a zipper and the 1901 is the first pair with two back pockets.

Levi’s Vintage Clothing, a sub-label under the broader umbrella, still makes a bunch of these pairs and while the aforementioned are killer, the 1933 feels paradoxically relevant to 2026: it’s got a healthy, wide leg, a healthy rise and a couple cheeky details to differentiate it from the rest of the pack, like a back cinch and suspender buttons.

We couldn’t make this list without including a pair from the first member of the Osaka 5, in part because it’s hard to imagine any of this would be possible without Studio D’Artisan and in part because the brand still makes some of the best jeans in the world. Founded in 1979, Studio D’Artisan kicked off the obsession surrounding raw selvedge denim and bygone detailing, doubling down on craftsmanship and material quality in an era defined by synthetics and acid-washing. And they never really strayed, ultimately adding some more timeless staples to the denim assortment, all of it made in Japan with commensurate care. The SD-801 is a classic straight leg fit, made in Japan from a rope-dyed natural indigo 15oz. denim fabric and armed with a host of cheeky details, like a selvedge hit at the coin pocket and a goat leather patch at the back.

Of course, trying out any new look comes with the eternal question: How do I style these things?

Well, one of the best things about raw selvedge denim is that it wears well with pretty much everything. With that in mind, don’t overthink it. But if you do grab yourself a pair of elite-tier jeans and feel like upgrading the supporting cast around it, we have some recommendations for you. For instance, a pair of sturdy leather shoes—whether some Tyroleans, a work boot, or the like—has a multiplying effect beneath some rope-dyed indigo. The same goes for a vintage-inspired, well-proportioned t-shirt or hardy flannel. Of course, as is the case with the jeans above, not all of these items are created equal. The below eight, however, have the GQ seal of approval.
