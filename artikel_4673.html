How Wisdom Helps Us Weather Life's Storms

As we get older, life has a way of testing us. We experience losses, disappointments, health scares and illness, changes in relationships, professional uncertainties, and the painful realization that often not everything in our lives will unfold as we had imagined. Adversity does not politely step aside as we enter the second half of life. In fact, some of life's most difficult challenges arrive when we might have expected to be more settled. And yet, something interesting happens as we age: We often become better equipped to face these challenges.

This is not because we are less vulnerable, or because difficult experiences hurt any less, but rather because we have developed a skill that can be enormously protective: perspective. Perspective allows us to understand that a difficult moment is not necessarily a permanent position.

We begin to understand that a difficult moment is not necessarily a permanent state.

When we are younger, adversity often feels all-consuming. A professional setback, a relationship ending, or a personal disappointment may feel like it threatens our entire future. This is often due to a lack of internal evidence and lived experience showing us that things can change for the better, that pain can and will soften, and that life can surprise us again.

With age comes a different type of knowledge. We have lived through difficult periods before. We have experienced moments we perhaps weren't sure how we would manage, and then disocvered that we did live through them. We have watched circumstances shift, relationships evolve and change, and seemingly unsurmountable problems become more manageable with time.

These invaluable experiences become part of our emotional psychological inheritance. They remind us that we have survived uncertainty before.

Experience teaches us what deserves our energy.

One of the gifts of growing older is becoming more discerning. We may spend less time trying to control what is truly beyond our control and more time focusing on the things we can influence. In doing so, we come to understand that not every disagreement requires a resolution, not every disappointment requires an explanation, and not every relationship can or should be repaired. We become more aware of where our energy is going and whether it is being invested in something meaningful.

The second half of life offers us the opportunity to cultivate a different kind of strength.

One of the greatest advantages to growing older is that we have a larger emotional landscape from which to draw. We have learned that life can be painful and beautiful, sometimes simultaneously. We understand that happiness is not a permanent state and that hardship does not define a life.

We become better at holding two truths at once: Something can be genuinely difficult and we can still find reasons for hope.
