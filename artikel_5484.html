Russia's October oil revenues jumped about 65% as Iran war pushed crude past $100

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Export earnings reached $21.2 billion, the highest since May, as Urals and ESPO blends topped $100 a barrel

Russian oil producers brought in nearly $21.2 billion from selling crude and refined products abroad in October, a gain of roughly 65% over the same month a year before and the best monthly tally since May, according to Bloomberg.

Crude supplied over 80% of that sum, while Ukrainian strikes on refineries weighed on the volumes of refined products produced and shipped, according to Bloomberg. Russia's Urals and ESPO blends climbed above $100 a barrel in October amid fighting in the Middle East, where tankers were being hit almost every day around the Strait of Hormuz and Houthi forces were bombarding Saudi energy facilities. Russian barrels never have to pass through Hormuz on the way to their biggest customers, China and India, which helped sustain demand.

The International Energy Agency put Russian crude production at 8.73 million barrels a day in October, up from 8.52 million in September. The agency said ongoing strikes on Russia's energy facilities are steadily squeezing the diesel the nation can send to global buyers.

Crude exports were unchanged from September, standing at 5.33 million barrels daily, which is about 4% higher than a year before, according to Bloomberg. Exports of petroleum products rose to 1.49 million barrels daily, their strongest level since June yet roughly one-third lower than 12 months before.

Russia's federal budget receives oil money through taxes on producers rather than directly from exports, according to Bloomberg. Net oil-levy income for the federal budget came to about $10 billion in October, trimmed by subsidies that underwrite fuel supplies inside Russia, according to Bloomberg calculations drawing on Finance Ministry figures.

Drone strikes launched by Ukraine during the first half of October cut into throughput at Russian refineries. The strikes eased later in the month when Kyiv paused them for two weeks, according to Bloomberg. Ukraine has gone back to hitting major refineries, among them Kirishi, which is geared toward exports, and Omsk, Russia's biggest. The renewed campaign could push waterborne crude shipments up this month, so long as the export terminals escape damage.

President Donald Trump declared an energy ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine on Oct. 11, days after saying Moscow would supply more than 4 million tons of diesel to global markets under a deal with Russian President Vladimir Putin. The Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control granted a general license covering certain transactions in Russian-origin diesel, valid through April 7, 2027, according to CNBC.
