History Shows: Wayne Gretzky and Warren Buffett Can Teach Us About Investing

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Wayne Gretzky and Warren Buffett might be an unlikely team, but they shared a winning attitude that can help you make money in the stock market.

Wayne Gretzky is one of the most iconic figures in hockey. During his 20-year career in the NHL, Gretzky played in 1,487 games, scored 894 goals, and delivered 1,963 assists for a total of 2,857 points -- about 1.92 points per game.

Gretzky was so good at putting points on the board because of his uncanny ability to be in the right place at the right time. He seemed to anticipate where the puck was going to bounce next and put himself in position to capitalize on opportunities.

What can Gretzky's hockey career teach us about investing? More than you might think. Legendary investor Warren Buffett found inspiration from Wayne Gretzky. In a 2008 op-ed for The New York Times, Buffett quoted Gretzky, who famously said, "I skate to where the puck is going to be, not to where it has been."

There's a great lesson here about investing. Let's look at what Buffett meant by investing based on Wayne Gretzky's hockey skills -- and why stock market history shows that this "Gretzky-style" investing approach is a winning strategy.

Warren Buffett. Image source: The Motley Fool.

It's understandable to get caught up in negative daily news headlines. On any given day, it might seem like the stock market is going to crash, that the world is ending. But remember: Stock markets are about the future, not today.

Just as Wayne Gretzky was great at anticipating the next move of the puck on the ice, investors try to anticipate the future. They allocate capital to stocks or sectors based on expected future earnings growth.

Stock investors are constantly evaluating what's likely to happen next -- which companies are poised to keep growing, which industries are about to benefit from positive economic changes, and which countries around the world are due for a stock market surge. They want to put their money into the places where "the puck is going to be" -- not where the action is happening right now.

What does this mean for you? Take a long-term approach to investing. If you're putting money into the stock market today, hopefully you're ready and able to leave that money alone for the next five years, 10 years, 20 years, or more. You're sending that money on a long journey into the future. Even if the stock market crashes tomorrow, you're making a long-term bet that your investment will grow in the long run.

Most of the time, that's a good bet. The S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) has delivered 9.98% annualized returns over the past 98 years, even with terrible crises and crashes along the way such as the Great Depression, World War II, and the 2020 pandemic.

No matter how risky and dangerous the world seems on any given day, the future of the stock market might be brighter than you imagine. It pays to be optimistic about stocks.

Gretzky's famous quote is also a reminder to avoid trying to "time the market." In his Times op-ed in 2008, Warren Buffett wrote:

Just like a hockey puck bouncing around an NHL ice rink, markets move fast. The S&P 500 can crash, but it can also bounce back even faster. If you're waiting on the sidelines, trying to time the market and choose the "right time" to invest, share prices might shoot back up faster than you can buy in.

If you miss out on the "best days" of the S&P 500, you might miss out on a big chunk of future wealth. Fidelity research found that, based on historical S&P 500 returns from the end of 1987 to the end of 2025, an investor who missed the five best days of returns over those 38 years would have seen 38% lower overall returns.

Instead of trying to choose exactly the right day to buy stocks, it's better to put your money to work as soon as you can. "Time in the market" beats "timing the market."

How should you invest based on this lesson from Buffett and Gretzky? Buying a low-cost S&P 500 index fund is often a good long-term bet.

The Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO -3.42%) has delivered annualized gains of about 14.8% in the past 16 years since the fund's inception in September 2010. Even if the next few decades are not so fruitful and the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF only delivers the long-term stock market average return of 9.98%, that's still enough to get richer in the long run.

When you buy a low-cost index fund like VOO, you are not picking stocks or choosing individual sectors. You're making a long-term bet that the stock market itself will put your money to work in great companies that will profit from the economy of the future. In this way, investing in an S&P 500 ETF can put your money "where the puck is going to be."
