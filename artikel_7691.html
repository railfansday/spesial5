The Supreme Court and what constitutes cruel and unusual punishment

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Type to search articles, cases, and authors.

Earlier this month, the concept of cruel and unusual punishment briefly captured the country’s attention after the state of Tennessee on Sept. 30 tried and failed to execute Christa Pike. Pike, who was sentenced to death for the 1995 murder of Colleen Slemmer, survived being injected with two lethal doses of pentobarbital. As she recovered from her injuries, including arm burns, at a Nashville hospital, commentators debated whether it would be cruel and unusual – and therefore unconstitutional – for Tennessee officials to seek to execute Pike again.

This debate drew attention to a range of past Supreme Court rulings on the death penalty, including a case in which the court allowed a second attempt at executing someone who, as a result of a mechanical problem, survived electrocution. However, it also highlighted a number of unresolved issues in this area, including how the constitutional analysis may change if a state is found to have ignored warnings that an execution attempt may go awry.

Kelsey Dallas is SCOTUSblog's managing editor and the primary author of the SCOTUStoday newsletter.
