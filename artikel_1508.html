Striker Che Adams says Scotland boss Sebastien Pocognoli is big on the small details... 'he loves his FaceTime calls'

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 17:30 EST, 15 November 2026 | Updated: 17:30 EST, 15 November 2026

No matter how history judges his time in charge of Scotland, no one will ever accuse Sebastien Pocognoli of leaving a single stone unturned.

Intense, intelligent and highly demanding, the Belgian has already shown himself to be big on the small things which can combine to make a significant difference.

Che Adams discovered this for himself in September, even though an injury prevented him from being part of the new man’s first get together in La Manga.

‘I heard from the manager the last time when I wasn’t in the camp,’ he revealed.

‘He loves a FaceTime call. I think he’s taken a few of the boys by surprise when they’ve been out walking their dogs and stuff. I think KT (Kieran Tierney) was out for a walk when he phoned him.

‘He just calls out of the blue and has done it to a few of the boys. It’s unexpected and it’s different because we’ve never experienced something like that. But it’s good.

‘In the last camp when I was out injured, he was on asking how my injury was, how long I’d be out for and if I still wanted to be part of it.

‘The camps haven’t changed much, but the manager is definitely more intense. I think it’s the European style of play and thinking. It’s going to take time for him to understand the boys, understand us as players and understand us as a squad.

‘But he’s really bought into it and wants to help us do well.’

Hang on a minute. A manager who’s more intense than Steve Clarke? You could be forgiven for thinking that’s simply not possible. But Adams feels it is very much the case.

‘With Steve you could have a laugh and you can do that with this gaffer as well,’ he explained.

‘But he’s a lot more intense in terms of detail off the pitch telling you where to be and what to do.

‘This gaffer has good ideas, a good approach to the game.

‘He is with the times, really. Everyone’s buying into it and I definitely think we’ll see the benefits.’

John McGinn believes it could be as long as 18 months before it all comes up roses. But, in football, you rarely get that long.

Starting in St Gallen tomorrow, the Belgian could do with some green shoots emerging.

‘He’s a few years younger (than Clarke) and has a different philosophy on how he wants his team to play,’ added Adams.

‘It just takes time to click, but it’s up to us as players now to show we can do what he wants.

‘We have been together for a number of years now, so we know each other’s games.

‘You could see on Friday us starting to put the manager’s things in practice. We won a lot of balls high with our pressing.

‘He said to us after the game that it’s about the killer moments and we probably didn’t capitalise on them enough. But it was a win and the first one for the gaffer at Hampden.

‘For every manager they need time, but you saw spells there where the movements and patterns of play were very good.

‘It was very important to get the win and build up the confidence.

‘You’ve also got to keep in context things like that it was Luke Graham’s first start for us, but he had the confidence to play those passes in the build-up.

‘So, you have to credit the manager for putting the belief in the young players who have come in as well.’

The comfort for Pocognoli throughout a time of transition is that he can still depend on the old stagers who were central to the success under his predecessor.

On the night when Andy Robertson joined Kenny Dalglish on 102 caps, McGinn claimed a double to put himself within six goals of the scoring record jointly held by Dalglish and Denis Law.

Having just turned 32, there is every chance that the Aston Villa captain can soon claim his own slice of history.

‘It’s amazing for John, he’s such a great person and he deserves it,’ said Adams.

‘Everyone can see what he’s done for club and country, he puts his heart on his sleeve and gives everything.

‘It’s a pleasure to play with him. I really hope he does do it - and I think he will.

‘Everyone is so pleased for him, he’s the best person I’ve met in football. When I came into the squad I didn’t think we’d have people who would achieve these kind of things.

‘I have loved every single minute of it. We have done some great things here.

‘We have been to three major tournaments now, people are making personal achievements as well. But as a team and as a squad it’s about taking that next step now in tournaments.

‘It’s a pleasure to play with these boys. They’re legends of football.’
