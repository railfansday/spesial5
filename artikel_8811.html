Nick Walker is expected to hit the Arnold Classic stage in March, and if this heavy upper-body workout is anything to go by, “The Mutant” could be unstoppable in Columbus, OH, next year.

With the Arnold Classic now on his mind, the 2026 Mr. Olympia winner is hoping to top last year’s second place and rule this stage like he did last September at the Olympia. And, to get there, it appears that The Mutant is willing to push past his own superhuman limits. This was evidenced by a recent Instagram reel, in which Walker recorded a brutal but brilliant upper body session for his 2-million-plus followers.

With Olympia runner-up Samson Dauda speculated to enter, and Andrew Jacked confirming that he will try to defend his 2026 Arnold Classic trophy, Walker understands that each and every rep counts.

The 2026 Mr. Olympia found the ultimate success this time around with his outer sweep.

Walker’s video shows some seriously heavy sets, with the aim of reaching failure, so you should conduct some feeler sets to find the correct weight for you. For added safety, The Mutant is also spotted by IFBB Pro, Andy Paredes. “I’m not big on partners, but homie trains hard,” quipped Mr. Olympia in his IG caption.

The session started with the seated incline dumbbell press, a move that builds the shoulders, upper chest, and triceps. “Best chest on stage,” encouraged Paredes as Walker got ready to leave it all on the gym floor. “No weaknesses this year.”

Next up was the seated chest press machine, hitting the entire chest, shoulders, and triceps. Using the machine means that Walker can target these muscle groups effectively, while a traditional bench press requires the recruitment of the core for stabilization.

For his third exercise, The Mutant upped the anti with his chest dips, adding a plate suspended from his waist by a chain. And, if you are wondering what separates a chest dip from a triceps dip, it’s all about the positioning. Notice that Walker’s head and chest are forward, whereas triceps dips are characterized by a straighter torso. This way, the big man is activating the chest and anterior deltoids at the front of his shoulders

Moving on to the standing lateral raise machine, The Mutant turns his attention to the lateral (side) and rear deltoids, providing his upper body with a broader topline. Notice how he raises and then lowers the handles in a slow and controlled motion for added time under tension.

For his final exercise, Walker continues to build winning arms with standing barbell bicep curls, blasting those biceps and engaging the core to remain stable. Despite the heavy load, the 2026 Mr. Olympia once again concentrates on quality muscle contractions and time under tension.

To replicate these reps for yourself, aim for 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps on each exercise. The added weight on the chest dips is optional, but be sure to go heavy, taking in at least one feeler set to avoid injury. This may be a brutal workout, but the muscle building benefits could be brilliant.
