Ethereum Price and $1.16B Selling Threat: North Korea’s Binance Hacker Moves $137M

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Ethereum price is facing another challenge as a North Korea-linked hacker moves $137 million in ETH. The massive transfer comes as ETH USD struggles to defend $1,800 after falling from a $1,840 high yesterday.

Wallets linked to the October Binance hack still reportedly hold $1.16 billion in Ethereum. Another $1.19 billion sits in Bitcoin, leaving the attacker with a staggering crypto reserve. With 74,881 ETH suddenly dispersed across multiple addresses, we have another reason to watch the Ethereum price.

The latest transaction is part of a much larger operation that has kept blockchain investigators watching the stolen funds. According to Arkham data, the attacker dispersed 74,881 ETH worth $137 million within an hour.

The movement followed the disposal of most of the attacker’s stolen altcoins. The remaining stash is now heavily concentrated in Bitcoin and Ethereum, with 632,000 ETH and 18,362 BTC still sitting in associated wallets.

That leaves $2.35 billion in crypto potentially moving through the laundering pipeline. The timing could hardly be worse for Ethereum as its price has already suffered a sharp correction, with the latest sell-off pushing ETH below $1,800 after trading near $1,850.

The October 29 Binance attack was one of the largest crypto thefts ever recorded. The FBI attributed the operation to North Korea’s TraderTraitor group, identifying more than $6 billion in stolen virtual assets.

Investigators said the attackers converted stolen assets primarily into Ether. They also warned that the funds could move through decentralized finance protocols and instant swap services during subsequent laundering stages.

Now, the latest 74,881 ETH transfer shows the operation is entering another significant phase.

The sheer size of the remaining Ethereum position is particularly concerning. If the attacker continues fragmenting these holdings, thousands of smaller transactions could make tracking and intervention increasingly difficult. Cross-chain swaps could also move funds beyond Ethereum’s immediate ecosystem.

The market now has another potential source of selling pressure to monitor. Even a fraction of the remaining stash entering liquid markets could become a significant event.

Ethereum’s technical picture was already looking uncomfortable before the latest hacker activity emerged. ETH recently plunged toward $1,712 before staging a partial recovery. The rebound struggled to regain momentum, leaving the Ethereum price below $1,800, down 14% over seven days.

Institutional demand has offered little immediate relief. U.S. spot Ether ETFs recorded $398.9 million in weekly outflows between November 9 and 13, adding another source of pressure.

Now, the North Korean hacker’s enormous Ethereum holdings introduce another uncomfortable variable. The $1,800 level has become an important psychological battleground. Losing it again could expose ETH to another round of panic selling, particularly if traders begin pricing in the possibility of additional stolen funds entering the market.
