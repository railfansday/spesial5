At the 2026 Breakpoint event, Brennan Watt, CEO of Anza, gave a walkthrough of Solana’s wins this year, while sharing what is in store for 2027.

According to Watt, 2026 was a major year for Solana as it migrated its consensus to Alpenglow, slashed the block times in half from 400 ms to 200 ms while doubling capacity.

The Alpenglow upgrade went live on mainnet on October 21, 2026, cutting transaction finality from about 12.8 seconds down to roughly 150 milliseconds. It replaced TowerBFT with Votor, a faster path to reaching agreement.

Solana recently transitioned to 1.0, marking the network’s graduation from “mainnet beta” to “mainnet” and retiring the beta designation it has carried since launch. Solana 1.0 recognizes the progress on network performance, resilience, and developer experience.

According to Watt, Solana is eyeing significant improvements in 2027 across four categories. First is Resilience, which involves simplifying the protocol, reducing special cases, and making it safer to change.

“So, we're going to go ahead and simplify the protocol, replacing legacy abstractions and interfaces, pushing more of the execution on chain, and modernizing some of the wire formats. And all of this, you know, enhances resiliency, but it also allows us to move quicker and be more agile and deploy things and evolve the protocol more quickly,” Watt stated.

Second is Market Structure, which centers on Alpenglow, which aims for faster block distribution, and Constellation, which aims for clearer ordering rules and more competition among validators.

“Several important Alpenglow extensions are coming,” Watt stated. Alpenglow rolls out in two phases: first is Votor, its voting protocol, which is already live on the platform; second is Rotor, a single relay layer planned for a later release that will replace Turbine, the protocol that spreads a block’s data across the network.

Third is Performance, where network compute capacity doubles and transactions use 80% fewer compute units for token accounts. Last is Expressivity, which involves vector processing for models running fully onchain and an increase from 64 to 96 accounts per transaction.

Watt also spoke about improvements in onchain development: “The cost of storing data on Solana came down by 27% this year. And just in the coming two weeks, we're going to bring this thing down by another 7x.”
