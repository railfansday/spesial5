Adult children of emotionally abusive families may dread spending time with their family, especially during the holidays. If this applies to you, instead of feeling constantly victimized by family members during holiday gatherings, try the following suggestions that may help you feel more in control of what occurs.

Hopefully, the above suggestions will help you take care of yourself when you are in your parent’s, sibling’s, or other abusive family member’s presence, but if you find that you aren’t able to follow these recommendations, please don’t chastise yourself or think of yourself as weak. Instead, treat yourself with self-compassion and remember that you sustained a great deal of damage growing up in your emotionally abusive household, and this damage may still be preventing you from acting on your own behalf. Give yourself credit for the changes you have been able to make and trust that by continuing in your efforts to heal, you will eventually feel empowered enough to stand up for yourself and take care of yourself in the ways you deserve.

And remember: You may feel obligated to spend the holidays with your family, but you have an even more important obligation to protect yourself and your children from dangerous and abusive family members.
