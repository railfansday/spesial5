Are Scotland still too reliant on 'old guard'?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Scotland slump to 4-0 defeat in Switzerland

As Zeki Amdouni arrowed in second-string Switzerland's fabulous fourth goal, the body language of dejected Scotland duo Andy Robertson and Ryan Christie was telling.

The captain's record-breaking 103rd cap played out in cruel fashion. Suited and booted for the occasion, with commemorative boots and a personalised armband, there was no cause for celebration as Robertson was, yet again, wheeled out afterwards to make sense of the 4-0 scudding in St Gallen.

So too were John McGinn and Kieran Tierney. Billy Gilmour, who has also surpassed the half-century caps landmark, also did a bit of talking. Substitute Christie was spared this time.

After a result of this kind, rounding up a humdrum maiden campaign for manager Sebastien Pocognoli, it's necessary for the experienced heads to front-up.

When he named his first squad, so much kudos was thrown the Belgian's way for breaking away from the norm and bleeding in the stars of tomorrow.

But such young and bright lights were only allowed to shine on the bench, at best, as Pocognoli turned to the tried and tested.

As former Scotland international Leanne Crichton said on BBC Scotland's Sportscene: "There's a reliance still, clearly, on that key core of Scotland players.

"And, when you listen to it post-match, they sound dejected, they don't sound that confident about what is going on. It is a difficult place to be."

Pocognoli's abject Scotland suffer 4-0 Nations League defeat in Switzerland

St Gallen was a mighty troubling place for the Scots on Monday night.

The calm, picturesque city was invaded by coral shirt and kilt-clad Scotsmen from Sunday, but they - and their heroes on the pitch - couldn't wait to get out of the place after a poor showing at the Sitterstadion.

A narrow half-time deficit turned into a drubbing on the final whistle, at which the Scots remained goalless despite a handful of hopeful situations.

McGinn, the scorer of Scotland's past three goals, directed a weak header goalbound before the break, while Scott McTominay - nearly one year on from his Hampden heroics against Denmark - missed a couple of glaring opportunities.

"If McTominay is not operating at his highest level for the national team, there is an impact on results," Crichton said.

"If McGinn doesn't produce the goods on an evening, we're in trouble.

"If Gilmour and Ferguson in the middle of the pitch [don't perform], if Christie doesn't come on with an impact, we are [too reliant].

"And that's where you need to breed a different confidence in a different group."

Kieron Bowie, who many hoped would be in from the off, came on just before the hour mark and got right in about it, as is the striker's style.

Winger Ben Gannon-Doak, dropped for Aaron Hickey, did come on after the wing-back ended up going off with an apparent hamstring injury before Kristi Montgomery was handed a debut with five minutes to go.

Wide-men Findlay Curtis and Josh McPake were left on the bench, again. Robbie Ure didn't make either matchday squad this camp.

All three natural wingers excite supporters, even if they have only been handed a matter of minutes, but Pocognoli's system does not suit them.

That said, the players insist they're invested in the project.

"We have to believe that we're going in the right direction," Robertson added after a beyond-deflating defeat.

"Of course, there's going to be humps in the road, but when I look back on Steve Clarke's opening six games - I can't remember all the results but - I don't think everyone was shouting and screaming about Steve Clarke's Scotland after the first six games.

"We're trying to get a team together and we're trying to build a good atmosphere off the pitch.

"People wanted change within the squad. We've done that change and now we need to try and integrate the squad.

"We need to get close as a squad again, integrate the young players, make them feel a part of it, make them step up - it's for us to try and help them do it."
