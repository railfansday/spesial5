Arsenal get the best and worst of Gyokeres on frustrating night for Arteta

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Mikel Arteta may prefer to imagine his Arsenal team as a remorseless winning machine, although on nights such as these they can still demonstrate a familiar unreliability when it comes to the main purpose of it all.

There were 30 shots over the night from the English champions, only four of which were on target, and in the end their winner never came. Even in the 95th minute, Arteta’s players were still conspiring to foul it up. This Champions League game might have been decided in Arsenal’s favour within 20 minutes. By half-time it should have been a formality. Yet, as chance after chance slipped away, there was always a possibility this would end differently.

The leaders of the Bundesliga were traumatised at times by the ferocity of the Arsenal press, and there were times when Borussia Dortmund will likely have no idea how they survived. Even so, they did just that and, liberated in the second half, Maximilian Beier scored an improbable equaliser in his team’s best period. There were still 40 minutes for Arsenal to put this one to bed and they found many ways not to do so.

It was notable that the man who put Arsenal ahead in the ninth minute with a right-footed thump from space on the edge of the Dortmund box was the same striker who would miss the best chance to win it. That is the Viktor Gyökeres dilemma. In the space of a single game he can look like a potential match-winner and also the last man his manager would wish to see arriving on the end of a perfect Martin Odegaard cross.

So it proved after 75 minutes when Gyökeres thrust himself at an Odegaard ball into the box that just needed guiding inside a post, yet planted it decisively wide of the mark. On the touchline Arteta tried very hard to hide his despair and failed to do so.

It ended that way, with a heroic defensive performance from the Dortmund defence, including one memorable moment when substitute Serhou Guirassy’s determination to block a cross prompted a double ricochet off his own post. One could make the argument that there were times when it was “unbelievable”, as Arteta later said, that the ball did not find its way in. Although there were also moments when the reasons were self-evident.

“There’s an element of luck,” the Arsenal manager said, “and there is an element of us [not] finishing better.”

In fifth place in the league phase table, dropped points at home do not carry the same jeopardy in the group stage that they once did, yet even so there was a truth for Arteta to chew upon. This is a well-honed, big-game Arsenal Champions League side that he has built. One that reached the final last season and may well go close again, but as ever they seem to be doing so in spite of the great goalscorer that so many of their nearest rivals have at their disposal.

Arteta sounded that warning after defeat in the final in Budapest at the end of May and, while changes have been made, it would be hard to argue that they have been fundamental. This was a good opponent – the leaders of the Bundesliga with many exports from English football who have been reconditioned for the German game. Those include Fábio Silva, Felix Nmecha and Jobe Bellingham, who had a second-half goal narrowly disallowed for offside.

After a chastening first half in which they found themselves in the teeth of the Arsenal press, Dortmund re-emerged as a much more effective attacking force. They found a way to break the Arsenal press by targeting Christos Tzolis and the run from the teenage French defender Joane Gadou that followed was exceptional. That created the goal for Beier, one of four shots on target from Dortmund – the same number as Arsenal.

Dortmund, managed by the West German-born Croat Niko Kovac, did not always have Arsenal’s polish in possession but they certainly had the youth in their legs to match the English champions. In defence their captain Nico Schlotterbeck and Waldemar Anton will not have many better games. They might have been fortunate at times but they will also point to a penalty that was not given, when Gyökeres handled in his own area at the start of the second half.

Gyökeres’s first-half goal was certainly among the best he has scored for the club, invited to shoot by a Dortmund defence that simply stood off as he reached the edge of the area. He will not catch many as full as he did this one. More of a dig than a swish but with the useful consequence of packing a lot of power and a dip that meant goalkeeper Gregor Kobel saw it but could not pick out its flight.

Asked about the later Gyökeres miss, Arteta preferred to talk about other aspects of his striker’s performance. “The way he held up the ball, the way he threaded in behind and stretched the pitch,” Arteta said. “There was a lot to like in that performance and that’s the Viktor that we needed.”

Even so, there was some of the Gyökeres the Arsenal manager does not need as early as the end of the first half when he launched a wild shot over from Noni Madueke’s pass. Arteta was without David Raya, who has a shoulder injury and there were no guarantees that he will be back for the Manchester City game on Sunday.

There were some good moments for young Bellingham against Myles Lewis-Skelly in the first half and then a goal disallowed after the break. That came after Beier’s equaliser when Dortmund were at their counter-attacking best. In the final stages, Arsenal pressed hard for the winner and, although there were lots of shots, it would be hard to say there were lots of chances. The one that got away was the one that fell to Gyökeres.

Arteta would later call it “a brilliant performance”. It was hard to shake the feeling from some of the caveats he added that, like everyone else, what he really thought – above all – was that his team should have won.

By Yannick HuberOn Tuesday, all eyes will be on Ethan Nwaneri. The Champions League clash between Arsenal and Borussia Dortmund marks the 19-year-old’s return to the club where he grew up, where he became the Premier League’s youngest player and from whom he joined Dortmund on loan in the summer. It would be hard to imagine a more poignant setting. But for Nwaneri, facing Arsenal is about more than a reunion. It is also an opportunity to finally make his mark at Dortmund.

So far his time in Germany has not gone according to plan. Nwaneri arrived with a considerable reputation and high expectations, but has become less prominent in recent weeks. His playing time has diminished and he has frequently had to settle for a place on the bench.

But there are several reasons to believe that, of all games, this meeting with his former club could give his Dortmund career fresh impetus.

Mikel Arteta was an Arsenal player the last time they played Borussia Dortmund in the Champions League, and tonight he will have to make do without first-choice goalkeeper David Raya.

Arsenal’s manager struck an upbeat note when describing the shoulder or upper arm injury Raya sustained at Newcastle, and it appears he has avoided a long-term absence.

Even two weeks out, however, would mean Raya missing Premier League fixtures against Manchester City, Brentford and Spurs – possibly Arsenal’s most testing week of the season. Arteta did not rule out a return to face City on Sunday.

Kepa Arrizabalaga is expected to deputise against Dortmund, who are top of the Bundesliga and have won three of their four Champions League league phase games. They won at the Emirates in a pre-season friendly, and Jobe Bellingham and on-loan Arsenal attacker Ethan Nwaneri are among their ranks.

These Champions League fixtures can feel secondary in importance to Premier League fare because of the competition’s new format, but a top-two finish in the league phase is a serious incentive.

As Arsenal discovered to their benefit last season when they “won” the league phase, the top two teams are rewarded with home second legs all the way to the final.

Arsenal are currently third on 10 points and face Real Madrid at the Emirates next, the team above them in second, so victory this evening is essential to set up that occasion.

Arteta has tended to make between four and six changes for these games, and Ben White, Piero Hincapie, Myles Lewis-Skelly, Christos Tzolis and Viktor Gyokeres are players pushing for starts after starting on the bench at Newcastle.

Also among the substitutes at St James’ Park was William Saliba, who is stepping up his return from a long-term back injury and could play his first minutes of the season tonight.
