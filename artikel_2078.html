Ref Watch: Three Horatiu Fesnic blunders in Crystal Palace vs Hoffenheim

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Correspondent AUTHORITY Football journalist with experience at Reach PLC and PA Media; University of Central Lancashire graduate. FOCUS Fan-engagement content and match analysis across the Premier League and Championship. THE INSIGHT Oliver utilises a network of club and industry contacts to deliver verified reporting and fan-first content. He provides the human stories behind the results to ensure fans get the full picture as it happens.

Crystal Palace's worrying form continued as they forced a draw out of Bundesliga side Hoffenheim in their latest Europa League clash.

Pierre Sage's job will remain under threat after his side had to wait until the final minutes for Daichi Kamada to rescue a point on home turf.

Hoffenheim took the lead early on with a rapid counter-attack that ended with Daniel Prass sticking a first-time shot under Dean Henderson.

Kamada popped up with the equaliser in added time at the end of the match as he pounced on Tyrick Mitchell's header across the box to squeeze his strike past Oliver Baumann.

Referee Horatiu Fesnic made some hugely questionable decisions throughout the game, including a red card for Adam Wharton in the second half.

Football Insider have picked out three of his obvious blunders from the game in South London.

Wharton picked up his first booking of the night in the 58th minute after a clearly mistimed tackle on Hoffenheim's Wouter Burger.

He certainly deserved a yellow for the tackle in isolation, but referee Fesnic should have blown his whistle second prior for a foul on Eagles star Pino.

The little Spaniard had been hacked down by Leon Avdullahu in the middle of the pitch before the ball eventually found Burger and he was clipped by Wharton.

Later events would mean that Fesnic's initial mistake proved to be costly for Palace and Wharton.

Wharton was soon walking off the pitch after receiving his second yellow card in the 77th minute.

Fesnic felt like he elbowed Burger when jumping for the ball and did not wait to pull the red out of his pocket.

Replays showed that it was simply not the case, as the England midfielder did very little to hurt his opponent and definitely did not swing his arm towards him.

Palace saw red, and Hoffenheim were mightily lucky not to also go down to 10 men in the second half.

Croatian striker Kramaric was booked in the 68th minute for wasting time after kicking the ball away, and should have been given a clear second yellow just eight minutes from time.

The ex-Leicester City frontman clearly caught the ball with his arm as he tried to stop Palace from clearing it away.

It was a definite booking, with his arm in a very unnatural position, yet Fesnic failed to even spot the offence.
