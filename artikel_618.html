U.S. details investigation into Megaspeed as Trump downplays Chinese spy reports

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The U.S. Department of Justice and U.S. Department of Commerce have just put out a rare joint statement detailing an open investigation into the Singapore-based tech company Megaspeed.

According to the statement, U.S. agencies opened an investigation into Megaspeed in 2024 for "potential export control violations" after noticing suspicious activity. The Department of Commerce requested records from Nvidia regarding sales to Megaspeed in May 2025. (The export of advanced AI chips is highly regulated to protect national security, and Nvidia makes many of the chips that power the U.S. AI industry.)

One month later, Nvidia put Megaspeed on its "no sell" list. That same month, U.S. agents and Singaporean law enforcement conducted an in-person investigation at Megaspeed and interviewed Megaspeed CEO Alice Huang.

Following this on-site action, the Department of Justice "took the lead role in the criminal investigation of Megaspeed," according to the statement.

"While we don’t typically comment on ongoing investigations, it is in the public interest to provide the facts and address inaccurate information circulating in public reporting," the joint statement says. Last week, Nvidia cofounder and CEO Jensen Huang resigned from the company after he admitted to an affair with Alice Huang, who U.S. Intelligence alleges is a spy for the Chinese government. Alice Huang reportedly pushed for Jensen Huang to lobby the Trump administration to loosen regulations regarding Nvidia chip sales to China, which the former Nvidia CEO eventually did, according to the New York Times.

After Jensen Huang stepped down, a number of his supporters within the U.S. government and tech industry have rallied to his side and spread doubt regarding the accuracy of the allegations that Alice Huang is a spy.

In recent statements, President Donald Trump downplayed the spy allegations, in particular.

"I don’t know that she is a spy," President Trump said on Monday, according to CNBC. The president also signaled that he was open to Jensen Huang returning to Nvidia. "Usually, what happens with that is that any woman coming to the country that’s a beautiful woman that comes from China, they say she’s a spy. I don’t know that she was a spy at all."
