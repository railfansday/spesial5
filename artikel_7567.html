RELATED ARTICLEThe New York Times will launch a local newsletter pilot in the Twin Cities next monthSophie CulpepperJuly 9, 2026This year’s State of Local News Report from Medill was grim. But in the Twin Cities, Neal St. Anthony writes, a rich local news ecosystem offers a more hopeful picture. The metro area of more than 3 million people is home to The Minnesota Star Tribune, public radio powerhouse MPR, local TV stations, and multiple successful nonprofit startups, from nearly two-decade-old MinnPost to Sahan Journal (whose founder, Mukhtar Ibrahim, also launched The Somali Brief this fall). Axios has published an Axios Local newsletter for the Twin Cities since 2021, and this summer, The New York Times launched its own Twin Cities local newsletter.

RELATED ARTICLEWith Washington Post Local diminished, other news sites step up their D.C. coverageSophie CulpepperMarch 5, 2026The question this ecosystem raises, according to St. Anthony, is “whether the Twin Cities represents the potential future of local news in metros across the country: a robust ecosystem with no dominant mass-market player but more news outlets tailored to smaller, niche audiences with narrower but deep interests.” (That description brings to mind other urban media markets where a diffuse ecosystem of smaller, newer players have stepped up as legacy media pulls back.)

“There is a healthy degree of vitality in local news in the Twin Cities. The story elsewhere is ‘news deserts’ and little original journalism,” University of Minnesota professor Benjamin Toff told St. Anthony. While local institutions like the Star Tribune have survived, he added, “it’s not immune to industry forces, including the veritable disappearance of print.”

A few highlights from St. Anthony’s piece for Medill:

On MPR: After the 2025 rescission of federal funding for public media, Minnesota Public Radio lost about $6 million. But MPR, like other public broadcasters, was able to turn the rescission into a highly successful fundraising campaign. Its 2026 fiscal revenue rose 5% to $119 million, and it grew to 172,000 paying members, a record.
