Bolton Wanderers are giving Wrexham real transfer envy with £0 signing

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Sam Dalby is fast becoming one of the more underrated strikers in the Championship today at Bolton Wanderers, which must be a real gut-punch to those associated with Wrexham.

The 26-year-old was brought into the EFL with the Red Dragons, but was a mere squad option during their National League and League Two promotion campaigns, to the point where the club decided to loan him out in the final year of his deal in the 2024/25 term.

At that point, Wrexham were a League One side, and showing no signs of slowing down. Many felt that they were rapidly outgrowing Dalby, but the towering frontman was about to prove those critics wrong.

Indeed, a brilliant loan spell at Dundee United in the Scottish Premiership made Wrexham second-guess their decision that summer, and when they tried to offer him a new deal, it was Dalby's turn to turn them down.

Now he's at Bolton, thriving, and those at the Racecourse are regretting not using him as much while he was their player.

Looking at Dalby now, it's easy to blame Wrexham for mishandling him. But it was never as easy as that. The frontman arrived at the Racecourse as a 22-year-old after a nine-goal campaign at Southend United.

He wasn't exactly pulling up trees beforehand, and he was joining a side that had Paul Mullin leading the line and Ollie Palmer beside him. It was always going to be a struggle to dislodge those from the starting eleven at National League level.

Dalby started just nine times in the 2022/23 campaign, but did get off the bench another 32 times. He was rarely seen as a sporadic fringe player, just not a regular starter.

That changed a little bit in League Two, though, especially in the first half of the season. Both he and Palmer rotated alongside Mullin, and he started 14 league games that year.

The only issue was that he only scored once in League Two that year, and when Wrexham went out and brought in Jack Marriott and Steven Fletcher midway through the season, he became even more of an afterthought than he was in the National League, only making two appearances in the final 13 games.

Hindsight is a brilliant thing, because there weren't many against the idea of loaning Dalby out in the summer of 2024.

After barely featuring in League Two, his minutes were only going to decrease in League One, and it seemed a given that the then-24-year-old would just see out his final 12 months under contract at Wrexham before moving on the following summer.

However, after scoring 10 goals by January 2nd in the SPL with Dundee United, who weren't expecting to be in the midst of a push for Europe in their first season back in the top flight, Wrexham quickly realised that they may have made a mistake.

As stated in their retained list at the end of the 2024/25 campaign, they offered Dalby a new contract in January of that season with the hopes of extending his time at the club so they could, at the very least, profit from this breakout year.

The striker turned that down, though, and after ending the campaign with 15 goals in 35 league games, helping Dundee United finish fourth and qualify for the qualification rounds of the UEFA Conference League that year, he became a free agent.

Dalby had more than earned the four-year deal handed to him by Bolton ahead of last season. The Trotters had eyes on the Championship and, initially, the then-25-year-old was seen as a squad player to help that happen.

Like at Wrexham, Dalby was a victim of a red-hot striker in the opening months of the 2025/26 campaign, as Hull City loanee Mason Burstow scored seven in his first 11 games. In fact, the towering frontman started just four league games before mid-January, and Bolton picked up just one point in those.

From the 1-0 derby win at Wigan Athletic on January 17, something switched, and Dalby became practically undroppable. Even if Burstow started games, it was often as a two with the 26-year-old, who would be the hold-up man to bring others into the game.

He scored 10 league goals in just over 2,300 minutes, but crucially, missed the last two games through injury, which put his status in the play-offs in jeopardy.

However, that became quite the masterstroke for Steven Schumacher during their run to Wembley and their subsequent promotion. Dalby came off the bench in all three play-off matchups and changed the game in each.

Against Stockport County in the final, he came on with a little under an hour remaining, with the score at 1-1, and two minutes later, Bolton took the lead after his deft touch sent Amario Cozier-Duberry's away, and Kyle Wootton deflected the cross into his own net.

He then made it 3-1 with an overhead kick, and was, effectively, the game-changer in Bolton's promotion to the Championship.

Even despite his excellence in the play-off final, it was almost universally agreed that Bolton would need a new striker in the summer. Max Dean seemed to be the favourite, but after his move broke down, the Trotters could only really bring in more supporting members for Dalby.

The 26-year-old has thrived as the starting striker this year, though. He's continued to be one of the best linkmen in the EFL and has more than a handful of goals to show for it, too.

And, crucially, at this moment in time, he's scored more league goals this season than anyone in the Wrexham squad. For what is his first season in the second tier, it's not been bad going so far.

Now, he's one of Bolton's most important players, and one that you would imagine needs to remain fit for the Trotters to stand a chance at establishing themselves in the Championship, which isn't something anyone associated with Wrexham would have imagined back in the summer of 2024 after loaning him out to Dundee United.
