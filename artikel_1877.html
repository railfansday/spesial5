Crawley Town chief has his say on whether 3pm football blackout is still relevant

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Premier League and EFL are set to hold serious conversations about the blackout’s future next year with pretty much every TV slot now exhausted.

Removing the blackout – which is a rule that prevents live football matches from being broadcast on television or streaming services between 2.45pm and 5.15pm on Saturdays – could have a detrimental effect on attendances and finances, especially clubs in the lower tiers like Crawley Town.

The blackout is under threat and television rights deals for the Premier League, EFL and Scottish Premiership are all up for renewal in 2029-30 and last year, Premier League chief executive Richard Masters said there were no plans to remove the blackout – introduced in 2003 by UEFA as Article 48.

But this summer there appeared to be a shift in emphasis, with Masters saying there would be talks with the EFL ahead of the renewal of TV rights. Prime Minister Andy Burnham has urged caution and suggested the health of the football pyramid should come first.

The only leagues that apply Article 48 are England, Scotland and Northern Ireland. No other major country qualifies for the blackout - it is a tradition that is unique to football in the United Kingdom.

CEO Allman gave his personal opinion – and not necessarily that of the club – about the situation and what it would mean if it ended.

He said: “My personal opinion is that the 3pm blackout currently works well in protecting the current football pyramid. I hear the arguments about it not being in place in other countries, but there is no pyramid like the UK pyramid so am not sure it is a fair comparison.

"We see notable increases in attendances during international breaks as well as drop offs for Tuesday night games for example where they are available on SkySports+ so in general, whilst removing the blackout, would arguably increase the exposure of clubs like Crawley, would levy that with actual attendances dropping off, which whilst having the obvious financial implication, will also have impacts on atmospheres and the general match-day feel.”

So it’s clear whether he would oppose the ending of the blackout? “I wouldn’t be in favour - as I say, I think the pyramid is what stands us apart from other countries and sports, and with that, would encourage fans to go out and experience the full pyramid, rather than looking for more Premier League games on TV.”

He added: “Whilst I understand the argument of increased coverage, I wouldn’t levy that against supporters being in the stadium, buying a pint, eating a burger, cheering their team on and in turn, financially supporting the club to allow us to kick on across the board.”
