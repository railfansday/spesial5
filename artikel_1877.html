‘Complaining never helps’ - Fabian Hurzeler issues clear message to struggling Brighton striker Promise David

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Brighton striker Promise David is “in contention” to feature against Craiova as he looks to kick-start his Albion career following a difficult start to life on the south coast.

The Canadian international joined the Seagulls on a season-long loan from Belgian champions Union Saint-Gilloise in August.

The deal is believed to have an option to buy for around £20m but has so far Promise has struggled to make an impression in Fabian Hurzeler's first team.

David has totalled just 23 minutes of Premier League football without scoring and didn’t make the squad for the defeat at Hull.

In the Conference League the 25-year-old featured against Kauno Zalgiris and Jablonec but was not in the squad for the win at Getafe.

However, Hurzeler has indicated that the striker remains firmly in his thoughts ahead of the meeting against the Romanian league leaders at the Amex Stadium.

"He's a contender, like all the other players," said the Brighton head coach to Sussex World.

The Brighton boss also stressed that David must continue adapting to the physical demands of his methods, particularly the intensity required during training.

"It's getting step by step closer to the demands and the things we ask of him, especially regarding intensity," he explained.

Hurzeler compared David's situation to that of new arrival Femi Azeez, insisting that players outside the starting XI must take responsibility for forcing their way into contention.

"Tobi [Promise] has to use every training session, no matter how long we're on the pitch, to be at his highest level. He needs to do extra work in the gym and extra work after training.

"If you're a non-starter, that's what I demand from the players. You shouldn't be happy with the situation. No player should be happy about being a non-starter and not playing in games."

Promise arrived at Brighton with an impressive goalscoring record. He scored 41 goals in 82 appearances for Union Saint-Gilloise, helping the Belgian club to their first league title in 90 years.

He also has four goals in 18 appearances for Canada, including a goal at the 2026 World Cup.

Hurzeler believes the responsibility ultimately rests with the player to make the most of his opportunities.

"You need to take ownership and work as hard as possible because that's the only thing you can control," he said to Sussex World.

"You can complain about the situation, but I've learned one thing in life: complaining never helps. It's about doing the hard work and taking ownership of your own journey and your own process."
