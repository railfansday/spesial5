Amex Gold Card Review: Benefits & Points For Those Who Like To Eat

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The American Express® Gold Card is one of the most rewarding cards out there if you’re looking to earn Amex Membership Rewards points.

The Amex Gold Card is an absolute powerhouse if you spend a lot on food, whether it’s at restaurants or supermarkets (or a combination of both). While the card has a somewhat steep annual fee, the good news is that it offers credits that can largely help offset it. We’re also currently seeing an exceptional welcome offer, making it the ideal time to pick up this card. Anyway, enough of an intro, let’s get into my in-depth Amex Gold Card review.

Here are my thoughts on the major pros and cons of the Amex Gold Card:

For someone looking to earn Membership Rewards points, the Amex Gold Card is one of the most rewarding cards out there. To start, let’s take a look at the welcome offer on the card, along with the eligibility requirements and fees. After all, these are important factors people consider when applying for a card.

The Amex Gold Card has an improved welcome offer: You may be eligible for as high as 100,000 Membership Rewards® Points after spending $8,000 in eligible purchases on your new Card in your first 6 months of Membership. Welcome offers vary and you may not be eligible for an offer.

The way this works, you can apply for the card, and then find out your offer amount. Then you can decide whether you want to accept the offer or not. That’s an unconventional approach for a welcome offer. At least Amex has the “apply with confidence” feature, so there won’t be a hard credit pull unless you accept the offer.

Based on my latest miles & points valuations, Membership Rewards points are worth 1.7 cents each. So if you are eligible for a bonus of 100,000 points, those are worth $1,700, by my valuation.

This is a huge bonus, and on top of that, you have a full six months to complete the minimum spending requirement, which should be easy enough for many people to accomplish, as that translates to roughly $1,333 per month in required spending. Personally I’d only apply for the card if I got a welcome offer of at least 60,000 points (or ideally 80,000 points), but everyone has to decide for themselves what they’re willing to “settle” for.

The welcome bonus on the Amex Gold Card isn’t available to those who have or have had the card, or those who have had the Premier Rewards Gold Card in the past. Furthermore, those who have or have had an American Express Platinum Card® (learn more) may also not be eligible for the welcome offer on the card.

However, if you have had one of these cards before, you’re still eligible for the card, but you’re just not eligible for the welcome offer (which is “once in a lifetime”). In some circumstances, it could still be worth getting the card in those situations, if you’d benefit from it on an ongoing basis.

Let me emphasize that you’ll want to apply for the Amex Gold before you apply for the Amex Platinum. That’s because if you have the Amex Gold, you’re still eligible for the Amex Platinum offer, while the inverse isn’t true.

See this post for all the rules on applying for Amex cards, and see this post for details on getting approved for the Amex Gold Card.

The Amex Gold Card has a $325 annual fee. There is no annual fee for up to five additional cards. Meanwhile, the annual fee for six or more additional cards is $35 for each card (Rates & Fees).

Note that Amex Gold Card authorized users don’t receive some of the same benefits as the primary cardmember, but their spending does count toward the rewards that the primary cardmember earns.

The Amex Gold Card has no foreign transaction fees (Rates & Fees), so you can use it for purchases globally. That’s especially useful when you consider that you can earn 4x points on dining abroad, all with no foreign transaction fees.

What makes the Amex Gold Card so valuable is the bonus categories it offers, in the form of Membership Rewards points. The bonus categories include the following:

This is an outstanding return on spending. Based on my valuation of Amex points at 1.7 cents each, that’s like an 8.5% return on eligible prepaid hotel stays, a 6.8% return at restaurants and at U.S. supermarkets, and a 5.1% return on eligible flights.

Now let’s take a closer look at just how those bonus categories are defined.

The Amex Gold Card offers 5x Membership Rewards points on prepaid hotels that are booked through Amex Travel. As a general rule of thumb, this isn’t worth it. That’s because you’re usually not earning loyalty points with your preferred hotel program when you book through Amex Travel, so that’s quite an opportunity cost.

With the Amex Gold Card you’ll earn 4x points on the first $50,000 spent every calendar year at restaurants worldwide, and after that you’ll earn a single point per dollar spent.

Generally speaking, restaurants are defined pretty broadly, and the category includes everything from fast food to cafes to fine dining. This even includes delivery and takeout, given the popularity of that nowadays.

Just note that convenience stores, nightclubs, and restaurants located within other establishments won’t typically qualify for bonus points. Furthermore, eligibility for bonus points is determined based on how the merchant chooses to be categorized. There’s a chance that some merchants may not be categorized as a restaurant, even though you might consider them a restaurant.

For dining, this card even beats the 3x points that the Chase Sapphire Preferred® Card (learn more) and Chase Sapphire Reserve® (learn more) offer — this is one of the best rewards cards for dining purchases.

The Amex Gold Card offers 4x points on the first $25,000 spent every calendar year at U.S. supermarkets, and after that you’ll earn a single point per dollar. This excludes superstores and warehouse clubs, so don’t expect you’ll be able to earn those bonus points at Sam’s Club, for example. This is one of the best rewards cards for grocery store purchases.

The Amex Gold Card offers 3x points on airfare, with no limits on how many bonus points you can earn. This only applies when booking directly with an airline, through the Amex Travel App™ or through amextravel.com. Furthermore, charter flights and private jet flights are excluded.

Officially, booking through online travel agencies (except Amex Travel) wouldn’t earn you bonus points, though in practice, it may still sometimes code for 3x points. That’s because often online travel agencies still have the airline bill you directly when you book flights through them. This is one of the best rewards cards for airfare purchases.

The Amex Gold Card also offers 2x points on prepaid car rentals and cruises booked through Amex Travel. This bonus category doesn’t make or break the card, but it’s a nice extra, especially if you already make these bookings occasionally.

The Amex Gold Card earns Membership Rewards points. This is one of the major transferable points currencies, and is a favorite points currency for many.

The best way to redeem your Amex points is to transfer them to one of the Membership Rewards airline or hotel partners. Points can be transferred to about 20 partners, consisting primarily of airline loyalty programs, but also of some hotel loyalty programs.

The reason this represents such a good deal is that you can get outsized value toward first and business class travel. These tickets are often disproportionately expensive if paying cash, while they can be a good deal on points. On top of that, Amex often has transfer bonuses, which can stretch your points even further.

While you can redeem Membership Rewards points toward the cost of merchandise or gift cards, or to pay off your statement, these aren’t good uses of your points. You’ll typically get less than 1.0 cent of value per point, so that’s not how I’d redeem them.

In general, I’d recommend earning Membership Rewards points if your primary interest is transferring points to partner programs.

In particular, the Amex Gold offers statement credits for eligible purchases, which could save you up to $424 per year, more than offsetting the annual fee. One of the great features of Amex cards is access to Amex Offers, which offers savings or bonus points on purchases with all kinds of retailers. There’s huge value to be had in getting as many Amex cards as possible, so that you can get these offers on multiple cards. For staying two or more nights at select luxury hotels, you can receive extra benefits through The Hotel Collection. Benefits include a $100 hotel credit and a room upgrade, if available. This is different than Amex Fine Hotels + Resorts®, which requires having the Amex Platinum Card.

Holding the Amex Gold Card entitles you to a complimentary upgrade to Hertz Five Star status (enrollment required). This gets you an upgrade to a higher car class on rentals when available as well as a 25% bonus on all Hertz rentals. If you already rent cars through Hertz, this is a nice extra. It’s one of the more recently added benefits on the Amex Gold Card, too, since it was introduced in celebration of the card’s 60th anniversary.

The Amex Gold Card is a great card for anyone who spends a lot of money on food (whether at restaurants or supermarkets), and who is looking to earn Membership Rewards points. Furthermore, the card potentially offers over $400 in annual credits, between the dining credit, Resy credit, Dunkin’ credit, and Uber Cash.

I’d argue that if you value earning Membership Rewards points, if you spend a lot on dining, and if you can use a decent number of the card’s credits, then picking up this card is a slam dunk. It’s hard to beat this kind of return on dining and supermarket spending, and it’s a great complement to some other Amex cards.

The great welcome offer that’s available is the icing on the cake, and makes applying for the card all the more tempting. I’d recommend confirming you’re eligible for a welcome bonus on the higher end of the spectrum with the current offer of as high as 100,000 points, especially because you can only get an Amex Gold Card welcome bonus once.

I will say, the Amex Gold Card might not be for you if you don’t spend a lot on dining, if you don’t live in a major city where you can easily use the credits, if you value airport lounge access as a card perk, or if you’d rather earn cash back than travel rewards, since those are all potential considerations with the card.

The Capital One Venture Rewards Credit Card (learn more) is one of my favorite cards for those looking for a simple strategy, and who don’t want to focus on a bunch of credits, maximizing bonus categories, etc.

The card has a $95 annual fee, and offers a minimum of 2x miles per dollar spent, making this one of the best cards for everyday spending. The reasonable annual fee plus the flat 2x miles is ideal for someone who doesn’t maybe spend a huge amount in categories that are otherwise eligible for bonus points.

The Amex Gold Card is one of American Express’ best rewards cards for earning points. With 4x points on dining and supermarkets, earning Membership Rewards points is easy.

The card also offers some credits that are potentially useful, which for many will help offset the annual fee. The card offers up to $120 dining credits, up to $120 in Uber Cash annually, up to $100 in Resy credits annually, and up to $84 in Dunkin’ credits annually — a total of $424 in annual credits if you take advantage of all of them. If you don’t yet have the card, it’s seriously worth considering.
