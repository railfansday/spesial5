Happy Wednesday! Bitcoin has risen to around $74,500, though options traders are raising the odds of the price falling below $70,000 by Christmas.

Also in today's newsletter, Bitmine reports a $6.3 billion annual net loss, stablecoin supply drops $14.2 billion in its largest contraction since the 2022 bear market, and more.

Meanwhile, Pyth Network adds NYSE stock and ETF data to its marketplace, and Deutsche Bank launches its digital asset custody service and signs an asset management deal.

Bitcoin options traders now see a greater chance of the cryptocurrency falling below $70,000 by Christmas than climbing above $80,000.

Tom Lee-led Bitmine reported a net loss of more than $6.3 billion for the fiscal year ended Aug. 31, compared with a $348.6 million profit a year earlier. Nearly $6.23 billion of the loss came from unrealized declines in the value of its digital assets.

The supply of major stablecoins fell $14.2 billion over the past 30 days, the largest decline since the 2022 crypto bear market, according to Glassnode. Total stablecoin supply now stands just below $280 billion, its lowest level since September 2025.

OKXICE, a joint venture between OKX and NYSE parent Intercontinental Exchange, plans to launch its tokenized securities venue on Nov. 23 under the SEC's five-year innovation exemption, which allows such venues to operate without registering as traditional exchanges.

Bitcoin Core disclosed three security vulnerabilities on Tuesday, including a high-severity bug that could prevent affected mining nodes from creating valid block templates following a blockchain reorganization.
