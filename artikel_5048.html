Bitcoin now looks cheap by one measure after $1 billion in ETF outflows

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Bitcoin’s valuation signal looks cheaper, but five completed ETF sessions lost $1 billion. A durable recovery still needs buyers.

Bitcoin's latest on-chain valuation alert has arrived after five consecutive sessions of net redemptions from US spot exchange-traded funds, highlighting a lower valuation before the demand evidence confirms that buyers are absorbing supply.

CryptoQuant analyst Darkfost reported on Nov. 15 that Bitcoin's market-value-to-realized-value ratio, or MVRV, had fallen below 1.2. The ratio compares Bitcoin's market value with the value of coins at their last-moved prices. He called it the third trigger of his valuation preset during the 2026 bear market. Yet five completed ETF sessions through Nov. 13 recorded combined net outflows of $1 billion, according to Farside Investors.

The alert identifies a gap between lower valuation and returning demand. A lower aggregate valuation does not show whether new buying can absorb coins being moved at a loss.

CryptoSlate’s Bitcoin page showed $65,461 in a snapshot captured at 10:44 UTC on Nov. 15. The page said its price had refreshed about five minutes earlier.

MVRV divides market capitalization by realized capitalization, which values coins at the prices when they last moved. It compares today's aggregate market value with an on-chain cost proxy.

Darkfost identified earlier alerts in February, when Bitcoin had crossed $60,000, and June to July, when it reached $57,000. He also said the preset is adjusted over time to reflect MVRV's long-term decline.

That adjustment matters to the historical comparison. The post does not supply a threshold-change history or reproducible backtest showing what followed each signal. Three alerts within a bear market do not establish three durable bottoms.

CryptoQuant's methodology guide discusses MVRV below 1 as a possible-bottom region. That is distinct from Darkfost's adjusted sub-1.2 alert.

Unlike the specific holder-cost cluster examined in CryptoSlate's Nov. 14 coverage, MVRV covers Bitcoin's aggregate valuation. Both use last-movement information, which records historical positioning rather than current buying.

A separate CryptoQuant note from XWIN Japan, available on Nov. 15, cited a spent output profit ratio, or SOPR, near 0.98. The note did not identify the variant or exact observation time, limiting comparisons with other SOPR readings.

SOPR compares the aggregate dollar value of spent outputs when they move with their value when created. Below 1 means the measured outputs moved at an aggregate loss. It does not establish that every transfer was an exchange sale.

Adjusted SOPR excludes outputs younger than one hour, while long- and short-term-holder versions separate older and newer coins around a 155-day boundary. Those series need to remain distinct when assessing whether loss realization is easing.

Demand offers a separate test. Farside Investors recorded $185 million in net outflows from US spot Bitcoin ETFs on Nov. 13. That capped five consecutive sessions of net withdrawals from Nov. 9 through Nov. 13.

These are dated fund flows, not Sunday transactions, and ETFs represent only one channel of Bitcoin demand. Still, continuing redemptions leave that channel withdrawing capital rather than supplying the recurring inflows that would strengthen a recovery case.

Continued losses and repeated ETF redemptions would weaken that interpretation.

Nvidia's scheduled Nov. 17 results provide a near-term risk test. The company plans to release results around 1:20 p.m. Pacific and hold its call at 2 p.m. Pacific.

Nvidia’s results could affect appetite for risk assets, including Bitcoin. The test afterward is whether spot buying and ETF inflows return alongside improving spent-output profitability.
