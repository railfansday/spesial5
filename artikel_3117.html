Dario and Daniela Amodei ring the Nasdaq bell as Anthropic starts trading

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Wall Street rallied into the Anthropic IPO debut, and Needham started coverage at buy. Staff can sell 10% of their shares today, as protesters outside called to freeze AI.

Anthropic co-founders Dario Amodei, CEO, and Daniela Amodei, president.

Anthropic co-founders Dario and Daniela Amodei rang the Nasdaq opening bell on Thursday, alongside key employees. The company now trades under the ticker ANT, Bloomberg reported. The siblings run Anthropic as CEO and president.

.@AnthropicAI $ANT Co-Founders @DarioAmodei and @DanielaAmodei take the @NasdaqExchange podium as the company marks its initial public offering. https://t.co/eMxJO65kaF

Wall Street rallied into the debut. The Nasdaq Composite rose 1.5% just after the 9:30am ET open, and the S&P 500 gained 1%, CNBC reported.

Perpetual futures pointed to a 25% gain on the $83 Anthropic IPO price, according to CNBC. Earlier, grey markets implied about 27%. Before the open, Alphabet rose 4.1%, Amazon 2.4% and Broadcom 2.9%. Amazon owns nearly 15% of Anthropic and Google about 14%, Bloomberg reported.

“It’ll be an important barometer for investors’ AI appetite,” Callie Cox, director of investment strategy at Plancorp, told CNBC.

Cox also warned that hot IPOs rarely perform well straight out of the gate.

Analysts at firms outside the deal have started coverage. Needham rated the stock a buy, with a $150 price target. Across five buy ratings, the average target is about $116, according to Bloomberg’s data. Seaport Global’s Jay Goldberg started coverage without a rating or a target.

Growth is the question for some investors, Bloomberg’s Rebecca Torrence reported. Anthropic’s revenue reached $16.3bn in the first half of 2026, up about 1,300% on a year earlier. Third-quarter revenue grew 47% on the second quarter, down from 144% the quarter before.

The bills are large too, after compute costs rose to 58% of revenue last quarter. Anthropic has signed about $900bn of compute deals, according to its filings. Nearly $71bn falls due in the next 12 months, Business Insider reported.

Current and former employees can sell up to 10% of their shares on IPO day, BI’s Stephen Council reported. Another 35% frees up early next year. After that, every employee must sell on fixed, pre-planned schedules, a rule most companies apply only to executives.

Some are already planning where the money goes. Current and former Anthropic staff have asked lawyers and family office consultants about setting up their own investment firms, CNBC’s Hayley Cuccinello reported. Altss, a private markets data firm, expects 15 to 20 new single-family offices from Anthropic wealth over three years.

“Don’t build the Taj Mahal of family office structures when you may have years potentially to realize the position,” consultant Kirby Rosplock told CNBC.

Demand reaches beyond the US. Chinese retail investors want in despite steep pricing and regulatory barriers, the South China Morning Post reported. A 28-year-old consultant in Hangzhou told the paper the price was too high, but she would still buy a small amount.

Protesters unfurled banners outside the Nasdaq in Times Square at 9am, calling to “stop tech oligarchs” and “freeze AI”, BI reported.

Doubts also come from inside. One former employee, who left before the IPO, told The Verge’s Hayden Field the current pace of AI development feels “untenable under normal market structures”. On Wednesday, scientists and tech figures signed the MisAnthropic Statement, which says Anthropic is not a safe AI company.
