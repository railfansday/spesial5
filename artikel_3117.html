Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Offshore push and a pending Saltire deal shape the quarter's outlook.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Drilling Tools International Corporation (DTI +1.42%) reported sequential improvements in revenue and adjusted EBITDA, citing a recovery in North American rig counts and the initial contribution from offshore technology investments. Management stated that the company is currently focused on its international growth strategy, highlighted by the pending acquisition of Saltire Energy, which is intended to increase the Eastern Hemisphere's contribution to approximately 40% of pro forma revenue. The company reported that it has raised its capital expenditure outlook to fund specialized tools for new offshore contracts in Europe, which began generating full run-rate revenue in September. Management indicated that it is prioritizing deleveraging the balance sheet following the Saltire transaction, with a target of reaching 1x net leverage within two years of closing.

On U.S. land, our own activity rebound trailed the initial rig addition slightly. This 2- to 3-month lag is not uncommon in our business, but our activity caught up as the quarter progressed, and the number of rigs running DTI tools has grown alongside the broader rig count, supporting higher tool utilization in the largest part of our business.

In Canada, activity rebounded sharply following the seasonal spring breakup, with the average rig count up more than 40% from the second quarter. These investments are backed by long-term rental agreements, and we expect them to generate attractive returns. In the Middle East, activity was relatively flat in the third quarter as the regional conflict persists. And we expect modest improvement there in the fourth quarter.

Our Deep Casing Tools and Drill-N-Ream product lines continue to contribute to our growing Eastern Hemisphere story.

As I mentioned at the outset, last month we announced a definitive agreement to acquire Saltire Energy, an Aberdeen-based international downhole tool rental company with an established presence across the North Sea, the Middle East, Asia Pacific, and other key Eastern Hemisphere growth markets. This is a significant transaction for DTI and marks the beginning of an exciting new chapter for our company. For those of you who were unable to join our call last month, Saltire's business closely mirrors our own. Like DTI, Saltire rents the mission-critical downhole tools operators depend on, including bottom-hole assembly components, pipe and tubulars, and pressure control equipment, and it does so in markets where we have long wanted to be at scale.

These are two highly complementary businesses with similar operating models, distinct geographies, and limited customer overlap. Saltire dramatically accelerates an international growth strategy we have been executing for years. We have deliberately grown our Eastern Hemisphere business from about 8% of revenue in 2024 to 14% in 2025 and approximately 19% in the third quarter of this year. Saltire takes that to approximately 40% of pro forma revenue in a single step.

It grows our rental fleet from approximately 63,000 tools to a combined fleet of more than 100,000, more than doubles our addressable rig market, and gives us an established distribution network to bring technologies like ClearPath, Drill-N-Ream, and Deep Casing Tools to Saltire's international operator, OFS, and national oil company customers. Just as importantly, the combination leaves DTI well balanced across the global drilling market.
