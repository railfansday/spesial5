Broadcom vs. Taiwan Semiconductor: Two AI Powerhouses, but One Offers a Better Value for 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Broadcom and Taiwan Semiconductor are two major beneficiaries of the AI boom, but their contrasting valuations and business models could make one a more compelling investment.

The race to power artificial intelligence (AI) has turned the spotlight on the infrastructure layer of the technology world. Deciding whether to invest in Broadcom (AVGO +3.07%) or Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM +1.55%) requires understanding two very different roles in the chip ecosystem.

Broadcom designs specialized connectivity chips and owns a massive enterprise software portfolio, while Taiwan Semiconductor acts as the primary factory for the world's most advanced processors. Both companies are critical to the global supply chain, and their financial profiles offer distinct advantages for investors.

Broadcom is a leading semiconductor and infrastructure software company, designing custom AI accelerators, networking chips, and connectivity solutions while providing enterprise software through VMware. Its position in the AI supply chain has become an increasingly important growth driver, although customer concentration remains a significant risk, with its five largest end customers accounting for approximately 40% of annual revenue.

In FY 2025, Broadcom generated $63.9 billion in revenue, an increase of 23.9% year over year, while net income reached $23.1 billion, resulting in a profit margin of 36.2%. AI semiconductor revenue surged 65% to $20.2 billion, supported by growing demand for custom processors and networking infrastructure. Meanwhile, infrastructure software revenue increased 26% to $27 billion, providing another substantial source of earnings growth beyond semiconductors.

Broadcom ended fiscal 2025 with a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.8x and a current ratio of 1.7x. Free cash flow reached an impressive $26.9 billion, demonstrating the company's ability to generate substantial cash despite its significant debt obligations. However, stock-based compensation represented approximately 27.5% of operating cash flow, an important consideration for shareholders given the potential for dilution.

Taiwan Semiconductor is the world's leading semiconductor manufacturer, producing advanced chips for companies across AI, consumer electronics, automotive, and high-performance computing. Its manufacturing expertise and technological leadership have made it a critical supplier to the global semiconductor industry, with growing demand for advanced processors supporting continued expansion.In FY 2025, Taiwan Semiconductor generated approximately $122.4 billion in revenue, an increase of 35.9% in U.S. dollar terms. Net income reached $55.2 billion, resulting in a profit margin of 45.1%. Growth was supported by strong demand for advanced manufacturing technologies, particularly those used in AI and high-performance computing. The company's most advanced manufacturing processes accounted for 74% of wafer revenue, up from 69% in 2024, highlighting the increasing importance of its leading-edge chip production.Taiwan Semiconductor also maintains a strong financial position, with a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.2x and a current ratio of 2.5x. Free cash flow reached approximately $32 billion in 2025, demonstrating its ability to generate substantial cash despite the significant capital expenditures required to expand manufacturing capacity.

Broadcom faces significant customer concentration risk, with its five largest end customers accounting for approximately 40% of annual revenue. The company also relies heavily on Taiwan Semiconductor for chip manufacturing, exposing it to supply chain disruptions and geopolitical tensions. Additionally, its substantial enterprise software business introduces integration, regulatory, and competitive risks that could pressure growth and profitability.Taiwan Semiconductor faces more direct geopolitical risks given its manufacturing concentration in Taiwan. Maintaining its technological leadership also requires substantial capital investment, which could pressure cash flow if demand weakens. Furthermore, its exposure to the cyclical semiconductor industry leaves it vulnerable to shifts in AI infrastructure spending and broader economic conditions.

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing currently offers a lower valuation than Broadcom when comparing their Forward P/E, which uses future earnings estimates, and P/S ratio, which evaluates market value against sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

Broadcom and Taiwan Semiconductor are both well-positioned to benefit from continued investment in AI infrastructure, but their valuations and financial performance make one more attractive heading into 2027.Broadcom's custom AI chips, networking solutions, and enterprise software businesses provide substantial growth opportunities. However, its valuation reflects high expectations, with shares trading at 27.9 times forward earnings and 17.5 times sales. Its reliance on a handful of major customers also introduces risk if AI spending slows.Taiwan Semiconductor offers a more compelling combination of growth and value. The company delivered stronger revenue growth and higher profit margins in 2025, yet trades at a lower 24.2 times forward earnings and 13.9 times sales.Geopolitical tensions surrounding Taiwan and the company's substantial capital requirements remain important risks that investors need to keep in mind. However, even after accounting for these concerns, Taiwan Semiconductor offers a more compelling balance of growth, valuation, and risk, making it the better buy for 2027.
