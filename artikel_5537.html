Why aren’t mortgage rates lower after massive stock sell-off?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Market moves that typically lower yields, including weaker oil and AI equities, have not pushed mortgage rates down. The piece points to persistent hawkish Fed rhetoric as the main factor holding the 10-year yield near 4.94%.  AI Summary

AI stocks have sold off and oil prices are lower, but the 10-year yield is at 4.94% and mortgage rates are still abouve 7.30%. So what gives?

The Federal Reserve has been worried about oil prices driving inflation since the Iran conflict began and has noted that the trade war inflation has mostly been passed through. Now AI stocks are being slammed, with moratoriums on data centers coming in the near future as the anti-AI backlash grows. In this environment, you could expect the 10-year yield and rates to fall, but that’s not what’s happening.

I talked about this on the HousingWire Daily podcast with Editor-in-Chief Sarah Wheeler, but with today’s market action, I need to revisit this question with some charts. Let’s go over each part of this storyline.

Oil prices were very volatile earlier this year and bond yields started moving in synch with them — especially after the first MOU deal broke off. Now that oil prices have fallen, you might expect at least a few Federal Reserve members to say that this could be a positive, but they haven’t.

In fact, when oil was trading between $68-$82, no Fed members said anything positive, and Cleveland Fed President (and Fed hawk) Beth Hammack talked about how lower oil prices were were actually bad for inflation because people could then spend more money.

In any case, so far, lower oil prices haven’t helped the 10-year yield get below 4.70%. If oil can trade between $67-$82, that would help a lot, but the last time that happened was when we announced a deal with Iran in June.

I have used the CoreWeave stock price as an example of the problems with the AI companies. Since CoreWeave had some financing issues, the markets have soured on AI, and as you can see in the chart below, the company’s stock price has been crushed. This has helped keep the 10-year yield below 5.35% for sure and has assisted in lower yields this week. But we are still elevated versus what we saw after the MOU deal was announced.

The Fed has made AI a huge inflation talking point, and they need to see much more pain in the AI sector before changing their tune.

If we were getting all this news about AI and oil prices four weeks ago, I believe the 10-year yield and mortgage rates would be lower today. But all this stock drama and falling oil prices came in a 14-day period where the Fed hawks came out every day talking about more Fed rate hikes, even a 50-basis-point hike. I mean, look at how the U.S. dollar hasn’t been fazed by the market drama.

For the past two months, I have focused on two key levels for the 10-year yield: 5.35% and 5.15%. Both those levels held and yields have fallen from these points. So what lower oil prices and falling stocks have done is made sure bond yields don’t go much higher and for now, let’s take that as a victory. However, I do understand why people might be scratching their heads on why rates aren’t lower with lower oil prices and falling AI stocks.

The Fed hawks are the main reason bond yields aren’t lower; they want to make sure that the bond market doesn’t get relaxed, even with lower oil prices and falling stocks, and they have done their job well. They really want to take back all the insurance interest rate cuts from last year and because of this, the 10-year yield is still at 4.95%. If the Fed members weren’t so hawkish over the last two weeks, bond yields would be lower and we would have already had sub-7% mortgage rates.
