Thanksgiving Is the Worst Day of the Year for Cooking Fires—Is Your Kitchen Prepared?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

If you’re hosting loved ones this Thanksgiving, now is the time to get your kitchen ready. Otherwise, a nice holiday meal could turn into a costly and dangerous ordeal.

According to the latest data from the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA), about 1,139 home cooking fires were reported to U.S. fire departments on Thanksgiving Day 2024.

That’s a 321% increase over the daily average, making Turkey Day the leading day of the year for home cooking fires.

“People don’t ever set out to leave the kitchen unattended, but a minute is all it takes for a grease and oil fire to take over, and there’s a greater risk of that happening on Thanksgiving when every burner is going,” says Payton Kashani, personal injury attorney, president, and managing partner of Sunset West Legal Group PC in Los Angeles.

By preparing your kitchen ahead of time, you can reduce the risk of fires and enjoy a delicious and safe celebration with family.

The NFPA says that unattended cooking is the leading cause of home cooking fires. And that’s quite common on Thanksgiving Day.

Many dishes are usually being cooked at once on the stove, oven, and various countertop appliances. Add in a house full of guests that bring constant interruptions and you’re left with a risky situation.

“Thanksgiving fires aren’t usually about cooking appliances going wrong. They tend to happen in those few minutes when someone steps away to greet a guest at the door. Distractions like this can wind up causing serious burns with long, painful recoveries that shake up a family’s normal life,” explains Amanda Demanda, founder of Amanda Demanda Injury Lawyers in Miami.

Grease and oil fires are particularly concerning because there are so many dishes cooking at the same time.

Additionally, despite the fact that the NFPA strongly discourages the use of oil-based turkey fryers due to fire and burn risk, many people put a frozen or wet turkey into hot oil and cause a fire.

“Fires can also stem from flammable items that get left near the stove, like kitchen towels or food packaging. Loose sleeves may get caught up in the mix, and when things catch on fire, many of the injuries that arise happen from people trying to fight the fire themselves,” adds Demanda.

Before Thanksgiving cooking gets underway, prepare your kitchen and anyone who’s going to be in it or near it.

When you’re frying, boiling, or broiling, stay in the kitchen and set timers so you’ll know when dishes are ready. Once they’re done, check that all burners and your oven are off before you leave the room.

Keep everything that can catch fire well away from the burners, oven, or any other appliance that generates heat. These include towels, paper and plastic bags, oven mitts, wooden utensils, and food wrappers.

“If you’re going to wear long sleeves, make sure they’re close-fitting rather than loose,” says Demanda.

Demanda recommends keeping a lid on standby.

Your fire extinguisher should be within reach as well. Just make sure it’s always near the stove and everyone in your home knows how to use it.

In addition, skip the task of oil-frying your turkey if possible. If you like the taste, buy it already fried or use an oil-less fryer instead.

“In the event you do use a traditional fryer, keep it away from your house, place it on a flat, even surface, and ensure [the turkey] is fully thawed,” says Stephanie Fox, national spokesperson at American Red Cross.

Lastly, direct kids and pets out of the cooking area.

“Kids and pets running around your kitchen are a burn injury waiting to happen, so make sure you have a spot for guests to gather away from your cooking zone,” adds Demanda.

Rich Repich, home services expert at Cinch Home Services in Boca Raton, FL, recommends setting up appetizers and drinks in a different room so guests don't crowd the cook and giving them a separate table to leave any used dishes.
