Meta, Apple, and Signal are pushing Canada to rewrite its data surveillance bill

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Bill C-22 would let police and the spy agency compel companies to keep and hand over user metadata, and Signal has threatened to leave

Meta $META Platforms, Apple $AAPL and Alphabet $GOOGL are lobbying in Ottawa to rewrite Canada's lawful access bill before it passes, according to Bloomberg.

Leaders from Meta, Signal Messenger and Tailscale are scheduled to air their objections before a Senate committee on Monday, according to Bloomberg. The committee stage is the final legislative obstacle standing between the bill and possible enactment.

Bill C-22, titled the Lawful Access Act, 2026, completed third reading in the House of Commons on June 18. The Senate finished second reading and referred the bill to committee on Oct. 22.

The bill would empower the country's intelligence service and police to order electronic service providers to keep certain metadata and then disclose it, according to Bloomberg. Providers could be made to keep users' metadata for as long as half a year. Some firms could also be directed to decrypt protected data, though only where they designed the encryption themselves and have the means to reverse it.

Lawmakers revised the bill earlier this year to state explicitly that firms may not be compelled to build a "systemic vulnerability" into their software, according to Bloomberg.

Signal has warned it will exit Canada if the bill becomes law as currently written, according to Bloomberg. Swiss tech firm Proton has said it would reconsider its plans to grow there.

Public Safety Minister Gary Anandasangaree has charged that some critics, among them large technology companies, are spreading misinformation. "Bill C-22 does not authorize indiscriminate surveillance and does not permit or compel companies to introduce systemic vulnerabilities or so-called 'backdoors.' Any claims suggesting otherwise are incorrect," Simon Lafortune, the minister's spokesperson, said in a statement, according to Bloomberg.
