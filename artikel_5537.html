Nantucket Brokerage Must Pay $1.3 Million to Buyers Who Couldn’t Expand Their Home as Desired

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

A Massachusetts judge and jury have ordered a Nantucket real estate firm to pay nearly $1.3 million to a couple after finding that one of its agents misrepresented whether a home could be expanded to fit their family of eight.

The yearslong legal battle centered on a twp-bedroom, 1,200 square-foot property purchased by Dr. Mary Murray and Jeff Ment. Murray, an obstetrician, and Ment, a lawyer, live in Westport, CT, but spend summers with their six children in Nantucket, MA.

Their kids “all love to be there. Every single one of them loves to be on the island, and we love to be with them,” Murray tells Realtor.com®.

Their real estate agent, Melanie Gowen, helped them find rentals in previous years, and in 2020, she represented them in their purchase of 151 Polpis Road for $1.41 million.

Murray says the home wasn’t ideal, but in the ultracompetitive Nantucket market, where the median listing price is $5,995,000, they felt lucky to buy anything at all.

And Murray says the family was reassured after Gowen, an agent with William Raveis Nantucket, told the couple they’d have no problem building an addition to accommodate their six children.

“She said, ‘Listen, I know it’s the smallest house, but it’s a great buy. The best part about this is it’s an undersized house on an oversized piece of property that you can double,’” Murray says.

According to the couple, Gowen said she went to the island’s building department and confirmed expansion was possible. She’d even sketched out the addition and sent a video of the planned build, Murray says.

In Nantucket, rules govern how many bedrooms a home can be zoned for. But Ment says Gowen openly discussed how a homeowner could get around those rules by calling an addition a den or a living room, then adding a bed.

“In writing, she had told us, ‘I know you can't call anything a bedroom, but you can do what's always done on Nantucket and call it some other room and use it as a bedroom,'” Ment says.

In early 2021, Murray and Ment began planning the addition and contacted the town about a septic upgrade and expansion and learned that “absolutely not another inch or square foot can be added to the property,” Murray says.

“That's when everything started to crystallize that it wasn't quite as advertised,” Ment adds.

Without an addition, the home was too small for their family and essentially useless to the couple.

“She tells us we're incorrect, that of course it can be doubled,” Ment says. “We say we don't think we're incorrect, but, you know, go ahead and do what you can do.”

She then called back with her colleague Kenny Hilbig on the line.

Hilbig told the couple, “Turns out it can't be expanded due to a deed restriction. And we're going to work with you to figure this out,” Ment recalls. Hilbig initially suggested that they might be able to “trade” their property for another home.

“He knew of some other properties that were coming on the market," Ment says. "He thought we could basically trade a house somehow.”

But that idea didn't come to fruition. Hilbig then suggested that the brokerage William Raveis Nantucket might give the couple a bridge loan to purchase a third home. The company nixed that option as well.

Murray and Ment claim Hilbig then told them the only way they could recoup anything from the brokerage directly was to sue.

So in June 2021, Murray and Ment filed a lawsuit in Norfolk Superior Court. The couple sued Gowen and William Raveis for misrepresentation, negligence, breach of contract, breach of implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing,  breach of fiduciary duty, and violation of the Massachusetts Consumer Protection Act, otherwise known as 93A, for willfully and knowingly making unverified misrepresentations in trade/commerce and failing to make a reasonable settlement offer after receiving a formal demand letter.

The lawsuit alleged that “Since Dr. Murray and Mr. Ment had been working with Ms. Gowen for so long, they trusted her, her opinions, recommendations, referrals, and relied on her island knowledge. Ms. Gowen had represented that she possessed experience both building and designing homes on the Island of Nantucket. Ms. Gowen held herself out as not only a real estate agent but as an interior designer and property developer.”

In January, a jury awarded Murray and Ment $1.28 million in damages.

Following the verdict, William Raveis and Gowen asked for a judicial review of the jury verdict. In June, a Superior Court judge upheld the verdict and the award.

Then in October, William Raveis and Gowen filed an appeal.

“We obviously disagree with the outcome of the case, which is why we have filed an appeal,” wrote William Raveis' lawyer, Ray Cashen, in an email to Realtor.com. “The fact that Mr. Ment testified at trial that even he does not believe that Ms. Gowen ever lied to him severely undermines the jury’s verdict and the Court’s decision on the Chapter 93A claim. In addition, eleven days before the sale was completed, the plaintiffs were provided with the exact document that prevents expansion of the home, and they had two lawyers review that document (including Mr. Ment). They apparently did not understand the restrictions contained in the document, yet somehow expected their real estate agent to recognize its impact.

“Moreover, they always knew that the home could not be expanded to contain more than two bedrooms due to septic restrictions,” the email continues. “In fact, the plaintiffs attempted to have the sellers of the home waive the two-bedroom septic restriction, but the sellers refused and these plaintiffs bought the house anyway, then later sold the house for several hundred thousand dollars in profit. There was no actionable misrepresentation and we feel confident that the Appeals Court will see things our way.”

In 2021, Ment and Murray sold the home at 151 Polpis Road for $1.95 million, which was $540,000 over the price they paid the prior year. They then purchased a five-bedroom property on the island for $3.1 million.

Murray says they took out a home equity loan and had to borrow against their 401(k) accounts to raise the money for their new Nantucket home.

“We are way more in debt but also have a house for our family now, which is great,” she says.

When asked why they didn’t hire a lawyer to review Gowen’s claims before purchasing the home, Ment says: “We never asked our lawyer because we already knew from Melanie, who said she was at the building department. We hired a guy to close the deal. We didn't hire a guy to assess the expandability because in our minds, that was already resolved.”

Murray and Ment hope others can learn from what they went through. “Should you trust a real estate agent to tell you certain things about a property? And should you fact-check them or back-check them if they tell you something? Or would it be better if they just don't tell you anything and tell you to do all the research?” Ment asks.

“I always tell my patients, you should get a second opinion," Murray says. "If you're not sure about what I tell you, please get a second opinion. I want you to feel really confident. Really, it’s the same with this.”

Gowen did not respond to a request for comment from Realtor.com.
