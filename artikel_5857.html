Mullin took government plane to LSU-Texas football game with Americans stranded in wake of FAA outage: report

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin discusses the aftermath of the FAA's nationwide ground stop and outages affecting several of the nation's largest banks on 'Hannity.'

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin flew in a government plane to attend a college football game in Louisiana on Saturday, just one day after the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) lifted a nationwide ground stop that continued to impact travelers through the weekend.

Just 24 hours after Mullin stood next to Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy at a White House podium to tell the nation why the FAA had issued a ground stop, prohibiting all planes from taking off, the Homeland chief was in Baton Rouge, Louisiana, to watch the Louisiana State University Tigers take on The University of Texas at Austin Longhorns, according to a report from the New York Post.

Mullin, like other cabinet secretaries, cannot fly on commercial airliners due to safety concerns, so his trip was taken on a government plane, though government sources reportedly stressed to the Post that he paid for the travel expenses himself and that no special exceptions were made for the secretary.

"Secretary Mullin is required by law to travel on a government airplane. This is to ensure access to secure communications and to comply by his constitutional duty of continuity of government should a national emergency arise," a Homeland Security spokesperson reportedly told the Post.

"Any flight not related to his service as DHS Secretary he pays for personally," the spokesperson reportedly added.

The Post also reported that Mullin spent at least part of the game in the private box of Ben Bordelon, the CEO of Louisiana-based shipping company Bollinger Shipyards.

Bollinger has a number of government contracts, including a recently obtained $2.2 billion contract to build four icebreaking ships for the United States Coast Guard.

"Secretary Mullin abides by all ethics requirements," a DHS spokesperson also told the Post.

The LSU game was the second football game of the day for Mullin, who had also attended the Coast Guard Academy vs. the U.S. Merchant Marine Academy annual matchup at Audi Field in Washington, D.C., earlier on Saturday.

The previous evening, he had been shoulder to shoulder with Secretary Duffy, explaining to the nation that a technical glitch with a piece of FAA network infrastructure equipment likely prompted the systems failure that led to the ground stop.

"What it appears to be right now is, as Secretary Duffy was going in and putting patches into the system, they were putting new equipment and new technology and that new equipment, new technology, just wasn’t — it was overwhelming the older system," Mullin said Friday.

The ground stop ultimately lasted roughly five hours and impacted more than 1.5 million travelers on Friday alone.

Despite the ground stop's lifting on Friday, travelers still felt the ripple effects through Monday.

Fox News Digital contacted the Department of Homeland Security, the FAA and Bollinger Shipyards for further comment.
