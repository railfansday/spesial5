18 Practical, Real-Time Business Applications For Digital Twins

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Digital twins are virtual models of physical assets, systems or processes that use data to reflect how their real-world counterparts operate. By integrating live data and predictive analytics, these models can move beyond visualization to help businesses anticipate changes and evaluate potential responses before acting.

That capability could help organizations make faster, better-informed decisions as conditions change. Here, members of Forbes Technology Council share operations where they believe digital twins are ready to play a more direct role in real-time business decisions.

EV charging infrastructure is ready for this shift. Digital twins can combine real-time data on energy demand, charger utilization and grid capacity to help operators optimize power distribution, predict maintenance needs and reduce downtime. This transforms digital twins from virtualization tools into decision engines that improve efficiency, reliability and customer experience. - Hossein Kazemi, ChargePoint

Early design and investment decisions are where the twin models a problem space rather than an asset. Scope the question; map the suppliers, rules, technologies and customers as connected systems; research each from cited sources; and put numbers on how they push on each other. When a supplier fails or a regulation shifts, rerun the model to trace knock-on effects and feedback loops before anyone commits. - Christopher Dean, Digital Tactics Ltd.

There are warehouse use cases. The first concerns better labor route optimization. One challenge for labor in a warehouse is the long walks and wasted steps taken throughout a shift. A digital twin of a warehouse can offer a better route for specific tasks as they are presented to labor. The other use case is dynamic slotting. As demand and fulfillment patterns are constantly changing, where you slot inventory can dynamically adapt, leveraging that digital twin. - Guy Courtin, Tecsys Inc.

Optimize production scheduling. Digital twins can use live factory data to test how a machine failure or material delay affects production, then recommend resequencing work. Measure value through less downtime and more on-time orders—not richer visuals. The trade-off is maintaining reliable data and validated models before trusting their recommendations. - Gopichand Mannava, State of Connecticut

One use case is maintenance and integrity planning in process plants, but only where the twin is built on structured engineering data, not just 3D. Many operators still film assets by helicopter and have engineers zoom into the video. Once piping and instrumentation diagrams (P&IDs) become a connected model of equipment and lines, the twin can show which corrosion loop a failure touches. That’s a decision, not a picture. - Andrew Babkin, Azati

I see data center operations as a strong use case. A digital twin can combine live telemetry on power, cooling, capacity and workload demand to inform real-time decisions. This moves the twin from a visual model to an operational decision layer, helping teams respond to changing conditions before they affect performance. - Natasha Bryan, AlphaRidge

For me, it’s cyber defense. I’ve sat in too many war rooms where teams argued over which system to patch first while the clock ran. A digital twin of your network and external attack surface ends that guesswork. When a new vulnerability drops, you see in minutes what’s reachable, how an attacker would move, and what breaks if you patch tonight. Add live threat intelligence, and it shows what’s being targeted right now. That’s when a picture becomes a decision. - Kumar Ritesh, CYFIRMA

Industrial operations are ready. A digital twin can use live sensor data, asset history and AI to predict failures, optimize maintenance windows and test operating choices before they affect production. The business outcome is concrete: less unplanned downtime, longer asset life, lower maintenance costs and more predictable throughput. - Swati Deepak Kumar (Nema), Citigroup

Critical infrastructure operations, particularly power grids, are now ready for decision-driven digital twins. By combining live telemetry, asset conditions and demand forecasts, digital twins can help operators anticipate failures, evaluate interventions and prioritize maintenance. The breakthrough isn’t seeing the system. It’s knowing when to act, what to protect and who remains accountable. Human oversight must govern consequential actions. - Rajjie Sarmey, FutureProof CXO™

In healthcare, digital twins can help with operations like patient flow and capacity management in hospitals. By combining real-time electronic health record (EHR) data, clinical notes, imaging and predictive AI, digital twins can help anticipate patient deterioration, length of stay and discharge readiness. This enables hospitals to dynamically optimize bed allocation, staffing and care coordination. This has the power to reduce costs and delays, all while improving patient outcomes. - David Talby, John Snow Labs

Digital twins are ready to transform field service and critical asset operations from visualization into real-time decision-making. By combining the Internet of Things, AI, weather, traffic and operational data, they can predict failures, optimize dispatch, prioritize maintenance and dynamically reroute resources. Their greatest value emerges when they move beyond monitoring conditions to continuously recommending and enabling the next best operational action. - Hemant Soni, CAPGEMINI AMERICA INC.

Claims triage is ready. A digital twin of your claims operation, fed by live intake, staffing and vendor capacity, can tell you this morning which claims will breach service levels by Friday. That’s not visualization; that’s a staffing and routing decision made before the breach. We run triage on yesterday’s reports; the twin moves it to today’s reality. - Mayur Khandelwal, EXL

There’s retail and out-of-home media placement. A digital twin of a mall or city district that ingests foot-traffic sensors, weather and campaign data can tell a brand in near-real time which screens or activations deserve budget today, not next quarter. Once the twin is tied to spend decisions with a measurable outcome, it stops being a pretty model and becomes an operating tool. - Agung Dwi Sandi, rankpillar Group

Identity verification for real‑time identity assurance is one such operation. User digital twins built from verified documents, behavioral patterns, device signals and credential history can continuously compare live activity against the “trusted identity model.” When the twin detects drift—abnormal actions, agent‑like behavior and so on—it can trigger step‑up verification. That’s a digital twin not just visualizing identity but actively governing it. - Henry Patishman, Regula

Engineering delivery planning is where digital twins could help teams make better decisions in real time. A live model of an engineering team’s workload, skills, dependencies and delivery timelines could show what happens when an urgent customer request comes in or a critical engineer becomes unavailable. Managers could test different ways to reassign work and see which deadlines would slip before changing the actual plan. - Elton Chan, Second Talent

Game floor layout in family entertainment centers is one use case. Moving a machine is often a hunch, followed by weeks of waiting on a live floor. A digital twin of each game, fed by live play data, shows who plays it, at what hour and beside which machine. Managers see how play would shift before anything moves. Because the twin updates with every game played, the layout becomes a decision managers can revisit each week. - Vivek Thomas, AISensum

Commercial satellite operations can benefit from digital twins that use incoming satellite data to test changes before commands are sent. If a satellite shows signs of overheating, operators can assess whether to delay an imaging job or shift it to another satellite. That turns a virtual model into a business tool: protecting expensive equipment while managing customer deadlines, service reliability and revenue. Models must be validated against performance. - Shelli Brunswick, SB Global LLC

Renewable energy asset performance management is ready for digital twins to inform real-time decisions. Twins of solar farms and wind sites now integrate SCADA telemetry, weather forecasts and degradation models to predict output, flag underperformance and optimize maintenance scheduling. Operators act on live insights rather than lagging reports, improving availability and extending asset life across distributed portfolios. - Nitin Agarwal, Luminace
