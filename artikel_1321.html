Slow and dull Man Utd are just not good enough

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Manchester United are like John Toshack’s duvet. You fix one problem – being far too vulnerable to conceding goals – only to create another – lacking the threat to score. It is all very Meh United not Man United. The indisputable fact is they are just not very entertaining to watch. They are slow and dull.

In the end it also points to another thing. The thing highlighted in midweek by Lisandro Martínez after the Champions League defeat at Atlético Madrid: they do not have the strength in depth to compete.

What they do have appears to not be fit for purpose. Certainly Michael Carrick, the under-pressure manager, is struggling to get the best out of the resources he has.

“It’s like a duvet,” Toshack, the former Real Madrid and Wales manager, famously said of football. “If you pull it up, your feet get cold. If you pull it down, your shoulders get cold.”

There will have been times in the last few days, with the intense criticism and scrutiny over his future, when Carrick will have simply felt like dragging that duvet up over his head and keeping it there.

Defeat by Leeds United would have been unthinkable. It would have put Carrick under potentially intolerable pressure in what appears to be a pivotal week, with Como away in the Champions League and a home Premier League fixture against Bournemouth in the coming days.

Was this a lifeline or the tightening of the noose for Carrick? Time will soon tell. The relief of avoiding defeat was tempered by the reality of it now being just one win in their opening seven league games – at home to newly promoted Ipswich Town – and they are down in 13th place. It is six games without a win in all competitions.

That is unacceptable and is their fewest number of league victories in a top-flight campaign since 1986-87. Back then they sacked their manager in November and brought in a certain Alex Ferguson.

The hard truth is Carrick needs to start collecting some “Ws” in those results columns. United are already 14 points behind leaders Manchester City and five outside the probable Champions League places. Qualifying again for that tournament must be the minimum expectation.

Ruben Amorim, Carrick’s predecessor, lost his job within 24 hours of the same 1-1 scoreline at Elland Road last season – dismissed after Martínez-like explosive comments again aimed at director of football Jason Wilcox. Carrick will not suffer the same fate after this, but the ice is becoming increasingly thin.

We have seen this movie before at United. A slow start to the season, a period of drift, falling out of contention, no reaction on the pitch, backing from the hierarchy that becomes increasingly unconvincing. And then a change.

Can Carrick, who genuinely does still retain support at the highest level, alter that pattern? Does he have it in him? Is he too passive like his team? Carrick said this draw felt like a “fresh start”, but on this evidence that comment can be classified as wildly optimistic.

Having directed his criticism at the United hierarchy, and specifically Wilcox, the former Leeds winger who was here, over a lack of summer signings, Martínez apologised and was named in the starting XI. But the Argentine only lasted to half-time, withdrawn injured with a tight thigh, and sat watching the second half with his hood up over his head.

What United did sign in the last window was three midfielders – at a cost of £155m – and all three started: Carlos Baleba alongside Andrey Santos with Youri Tielemans in the No 10 role, which meant Bruno Fernandes was pushed out to the left, from where he constantly wanted to come in-field, with Amad Diallo on the right.

Carrick talked of having a “fresh spine” as well as that fresh start. But it did not make for a potent combination even if, further forward, Bryan Mbeumo played through the middle and scored one. He should have scored another.

The way the usually undemonstrative Carrick punched the air in his technical area showed the pressure he felt. It was something to build on. To gain control.

Leeds had started well but did not really pose much of a threat – beyond forcing Lammens to save well from Nico Elvedi’s downward header – and maybe the earlier kick-off subdued the crowd. This was far from a bear pit.

But after the break, Leeds were comfortably the better side. The nature of their equalising goal, with Dominic Calvert-Lewin too strong and alert for first Baleba and then Mbeumo to fire in from a corner, exposed the enduring softness at the heart of United.

And that must be addressed by Carrick, especially as he appeared to sacrifice attacking intent by shoring up defensively. But that duvet is looking far too inadequate right now. Or Carrick is making it appear so.

Michael Carrick says he has put on “the headphones” this week in an effort to drown out the noise, but admitted Manchester United have to stop their struggles from snowballing.

United sit just two points above the relegation zone after a 1-1 draw at Leeds left them with just one win from their opening seven Premier League matches.

Carrick claimed the result felt like a “fresh start” after a week in which Lisandro Martínez publicly apologised for criticising Jason Wilcox, the United director of football, and the Old Trafford board.

But the United manager acknowledged their position “doesn’t look too good” and that they must start winning soon to stop the club from entering another doom cycle.

“Listen, we’re not accepting it as everything’s great by any stretch, so I’m realistic with that,” said the United manager, who claimed “the headphones have been on this week”.

“But sometimes you do struggle, sometimes there’s a bit of it where you have to find a way through it. We haven’t really had to go through that yet [before now] and we have to find a way through it.

“There has been a cycle at times here over a period of time when it can go in different directions. We’ve got to be stable enough to keep doing the right things for the right reasons as a group, and we’re more than capable of winning football matches.

“I understand the situation doesn’t look too good if you look at it from a certain way, but I’ve no doubts we can go on a good run.”

United face Como in the Champions League in Italy on Wednesday before Bournemouth visit Old Trafford four days later.

Asked if he would be putting the headphones back on this week ahead of two more pivotal games, Carrick added: “Nah, it’s always quiet for me.”

Martínez was substituted at half-time at Elland Road with a tight thigh just days after his tirade at club executives and Marcus Rashford was left out for “purely tactical” reasons. “Nothing personal, the front players were doing a good job on and off the ball,” Carrick said. “Everyone is ready for Wednesday.”

Speaking to Sky Sports, he said: “I think we could’ve played with a bit more quality in the final third, particularly in the first half. We were getting good quality in the box but just not enough numbers.
