Ryan Giggs Named Two British Stadiums With the Best Atmospheres He’s Seen

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

No player in the history of the Premier League has made more appearances for Manchester United than Ryan Giggs and he 'won the lot' as their proud Stretford End so eloquently put it.

During Giggs' legendary career, he became the most decorated footballer not only to wear a United shirt but also in English football. The Welshman got his hands on 34 major trophies, 13 league titles and was part of the team that won the treble in 1999.

The unfortunate thing about time in football is that the heroes of yesteryear are quickly forgotten, and the younger generation certainly don't seem to realise just how incredibly talented Giggs was. Most weren't even alive when he glided his way through Arsenal at Highbury to score the most famous FA Cup goal in history and many may have even missed his exploits in Moscow against Chelsea in 2008.

Giggs carved out a career only one can dream of and that meant travelling the globe to play against the very best teams on the planet. He experienced some of the most hostile crowds in world football but the atmosphere at two stadiums particularly left their mark on him.

Giggs played in some of the most heated rivalries in British football, including feisty clashes with Liverpool at Anfield. As enthralling as those fixtures can be, they pale in comparison to the derby between Celtic and Rangers.

Back in 2018, Giggs admitted that he wished to have sampled the atmosphere of an Old Firm Derby having spent his entire career at Old Trafford. Speaking to the Daily Record, he said: "I was talking to Declan John last year about coming up for the Old Firm but he has gone now. It’s something I’ve always wanted to experience."

Giggs added: "There’s no Welsh players in Scotland at the moment unfortunately but it’s something I am definitely keen to do. I’ll get there at some point because everyone I speak to who has been there says it’s unreal. I have played in Glasgow."

The former United winger played seven times against Celtic and Rangers in Champions League competition, stepping out on the Ibrox and Parkhead pitches. Those outings seemed to whet his appetite to attend a derby game:

"Those were amazing atmospheres, probably up there with the best atmospheres I played in."

Giggs even recalled a trip to Turkey but insisted Glasgow was in a league of its own: "I remember going to Turkey in the early days against Galatasaray and it was ferocious but going to Glasgow surpassed that for intensity. I played at Ibrox in the Champions League when Phil Neville scored and a couple of times against Celtic."

He added: "We drew one and lost one. I managed to score in the draw. The noise was incredible. When that Champions League music went on and the crowd got behind the team, it was amazing. You didn’t just have to face 11 men, you had to face those passionate crowds as well."

Giggs's former United teammate Wayne Rooney not only wanted to taste an Old Firm Derby but to do so as a Celtic player. The former England striker revealed he had wished to have played for the Bhoys at some stage of his career.

When Giggs hung up his boots in May 2014, he didn't step into punditry like many of his former teammates nor did he leave Old Trafford. He instead took up an assistant coaching role, first as a player-manager in David Moyes' team before taking charge as caretaker manager and eventually assisting Louis van Gaal.

Some expected him to become permanent boss but instead he's watched Ole Gunnar Solskjaer and Michael Carrick try their luck in the dugout. He did, however, land the Wales national team job, spending four years in the role before resigning in 2022.

Giggs' personal life has meant he hasn't been back in football management since, but before he managed his country, he landed on Celtic's radar. In 2016, the Scottish club were looking to replace Ronny Delia and the Welshman was a shock name added to their managerial shortlist.

The Manchester United legend named one famous British stadium as having the best atmosphere he ever experienced as a player.

That came while he assisted Van Gaal and The Daily Mail claim that he had reservations about moving to Scotland. If he had decided to take charge at Parkhead, he'd have been able to experience the Old Firm Derby as a manager, potentially even coming up against his former Liverpool foe Steven Gerrard, who became Rangers boss two years' later.
