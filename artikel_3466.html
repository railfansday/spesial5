‘Ghosts’ Season 6 Is Taking an Unnecessary Risk With Its Release Formula

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Comedy fans have been wondering when Ghosts will return for Season 6, and now they have their answer. Following a pair of holiday specials, the supernatural comedy will return in January 2027 in its usual time slot. However, there's a major change to its release schedule: the show will now air twice-weekly. In addition to its regular Thursday slot, Ghosts will also air episodes on Mondays following Eternally Yours. While this is an attempt to build a block of supernatural comedy programming, it might be a majorly wrong move on CBS's part.

For starters, shifting to a twice-weekly schedule will disrupt the series' steady momentum. Part of the fun of watching Ghosts is seeing how Jay (Utkarsh Ambudkar) and Sam (Rose McIver), alongside Kyle (Ben Feldman), run a bed-and-breakfast full of ghosts. Though the holiday specials and certain episodes have been two-part stories, they've usually aired on a single night. Shifting that status would erase the steady pacing that's made Ghosts a breakout series, and in an age when it's one of the few network series still commanding a steady audience, that could be a death sentence.

Instead of one night to sit back, relax, and catch up on new episodes, the schedule shift for Ghosts means fans risk missingan episode. That could lead to falling behind on the series and missing key developments or a genuinely funny moment — or worse, both. While streaming services have given fans more access to certain series, part of the joy of watching Ghosts lies in catching it live. Doubling the number of episodes could lead fans not only to fall behind, but eventually to skip watching altogether and wait for the entire season to drop on Paramount+.

That's a lapse that CBS can't afford. Ghosts's viewership has fluctuated over the years, as it started out strong but has decreased slightly with each season. Shifting to two episodes a week could hurt those ratings, and given that Ghosts is still one of CBS's highest-rated series, it feels like a risky gamble. Ghosts is also preparing to take some of its biggest swings yet, as Rebecca Wisocky recently told Collider that the upcoming Christmas special will be a major game changer:

"Lots of people are in peril. The stakes are perhaps the highest that they have ever been. And I don't say that lightly."

If Ghosts lives up to Wisocky's words, this won't be a season to miss. Not only will the show hit its milestone 100th episode, but the new dynamic between Jay and Kyle makes for a fresh start. Doubling up on episodes risks burning through these interesting developments, but CBS might have a good reason beyond filling up its schedule.

Some people might ask why Ghosts is doubling up on episodes, and the answer lies in another upcoming event that dominates CBS's schedule. "March Madness" will see the network air college basketball games throughout March and April, a period when multiple series usually go on hiatus until the tournament ends. If Ghosts does a double-header of its own, it could build up to a mid-season hiatus and air the rest of its episodes through May, when its season finales usually debut. This could also mark a shift back to single episodes, depending on how viewers receive Ghosts' twice-weekly episodes.

Ghosts stands out in the modern television landscape not just because it's a great comedy, but also because it sticks to a set of rules most TV shows have abandoned. It's managed to (mostly) stick to the 22-episode order that most audiences feel is outdated, and has won over an audience thanks to its mix of humor and supernatural tropes. By altering the release schedule, CBS is losing sight of what makes the series worth watching.

Seasons 1–5 of Ghosts are available to stream on Paramount+.
