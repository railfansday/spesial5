Hike to the End of the World Begins

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

After a restful zero day in Santiago de Compostela, we set out on the Camino Finisterre toward what ancient pilgrims believed was the “end of the world.”

Our albergue stay last night turned out just fine. Despite a few online reviews mentioning sewer smells and bed bugs, our room was clean and bug-free (or so we thought at the time—more on that in the coming days!). The streets below echoed with late-night partygoers, but having logged 500 miles, we slept soundly. It was a crisp, 40°F morning as we packed up to begin the next leg of our journey.

At 8:00 AM, we wandered out past the majestic Santiago Cathedral as the city slowly woke up. Our first order of business was finding breakfast, which led us to a cozy café packed with locals.

I enjoyed two cafés con leche while Knockerz had tea served in a charming cast-iron teapot. The owner brought out complimentary pastries and cakes with our drinks, and the entire bill came to less than $8!

Once we left the city streets behind, we crossed a small river flowing past an old mill and entered a dense forest. The atmosphere shifted almost instantly. Compared to the crowded, touristy energy of the final 100 kilometers into Santiago, the trail to Finisterre felt quiet, authentic, and peaceful.

The trail soon began a gentle climb through a thick eucalyptus forest, where the crisp scent filled the air like a spa. At the crest of the hill, we spotted a small tour bus and about a dozen people gathered near a lookout.

As we chatted with them, we were surprised to discover they were the American tour group that had paid for the Botafumeiro swinging ceremony during yesterday’s mass!

We thanked them again for making that unforgettable experience possible.

Their guide pointed out that historically, this spot was the final point where pilgrims could look back and see the spires of the Santiago Cathedral. From here on, pilgrims turned their focus entirely toward the Atlantic coast.

Warmed up from the climb, we shed our outer layers and plunged back into an ancient, shaded woodland trail.

Emerging from the forest, we followed a paved country road that wound downhill toward the valley. We had already covered half our daily mileage in under two hours!

At the bottom of the hill lies Ponte Maceira (near Aguapesada), a picturesque medieval village built along the Tambre River. The star of the village is a stunning 200-foot-long Roman stone bridge featuring multiple arched spans over the rushing water.

Crossing the river brought us to the hardest challenge of the day: a steep, 700-foot elevation gain over just half a mile. We paced ourselves and made it to the top without much trouble. From there, the trail wound through quiet countryside hamlets along traffic-free paved roads, offering a very chill, laid-back vibe.

Before long, we strolled into Negreira and located our apartment in the center of town. Having an in-unit washer/dryer and an induction stove felt like luxury! We threw in a load of laundry and headed out to run a few errands.

After exploring town to find compression socks for Knockerz’s shin splints, we stopped by a small grocery store for supplies. We spent the evening cooking a homemade meal, finishing our trail chores, and relaxing. Our first day on the Camino Finisterre was wonderfully low-key—a refreshing change of pace after the busy finish in Santiago.
