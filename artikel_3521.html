Pokémon Officially Gives Pikachu The Ultimate 30th Anniversary Redesign

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

This year is a big year for anniversaries of some of our biggest franchises, and Pokémon is just one example. It has been three decades since the first video games, Pokémon Red and Pokémon Green, released in Japan on 27 February 1996, after Satoshi Tajiri came up with the idea and teamed up with Ken Sugimori to create the original designs of some of our favorite 'mons.

Now, 30 years on, and Pokémon is one of the biggest franchises in the world, and with its tenth generation expected to begin next year with the launch of Pokémon Winds and Waves for the Nintendo Switch 2, the Nintendo universe shows no signs of slowing down.

With over 1,000 Pokémon making up the Pokédex, some faces are more familiar than others, and as the franchise's mascot, nobody is more recognizable than Pikachu. Designed by artist Atsuko Nishida with help from Ken Sugimori, Pikachu was released internationally in North America in 1998 and has become the face of the franchise ever since. As such, they have had many different designs, and its 30th anniversary redesign is no different.

To help celebrate 30 years of Pokémon, and of course, Pikachu, Pokémon Co. has announced a 6-centimeter solid gold Gholdengo. Not only that, but Pikachu will also be standing by their side. With the Gholdengo statue consisting of 730 sheets of gold leaf and standing at a life-size 3.9 feet, Pikachu will not be far behind with 200 sheets of gold leaf and measuring just about 20 inches tall. However, the slightly small catch here, is that the Gholdengo statue comes in at just under $10k.

From September 30th to October 5th, 2026, the pair of statues were available to view on the eighth floor of Yokohama Takashimaya, Japan, but if you wish to see them in the flesh now, you will have to fork out on that eye-watering price tag. However, there is no denying that they will make for the ultimate collectible. What says 30 years of a franchise more than gold-clad figures that cost an arm and a leg to purchase?

Over on r/pokemon, fans discussed the brand-new collectibles, including the amount of gold used and what it means for that price tag, with one user sharing, "A fitting metaphor for how materialistic this franchise has become lately." Another agreed, adding, "TPC really is doing anything except working on refining their games, aren't they? At this point, Pokémon isn't a game license anymore, it's just a franchise of products. And yeah, no Pokémon player will be able to afford this." Another user had a handy tip, sharing, "If you buy $8,000 of gold and pay someone $1,000 to cast this, you can get two."

As we gear up for a brand-new Pokémon era, we can only imagine what the next thirty years will have in store for us. Now, however, we can look forward to the launch of Pokémon Winds and Waves, which is just as exciting as a gold-clad Gholdengo and Pikachu, but a lot cheaper.
