Strategic Bitcoin Reserve Bill Clears House Committee, Heads to Floor

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A bill that would write the U.S. Strategic Bitcoin Reserve into federal law is now eligible for a vote on the House floor, after the House Financial Services Committee formally reported it on Tuesday.

The American Reserve Modernization Act of 2026, H.R. 8957, was filed with amendments as House Report 119-894 and placed on the Union Calendar as Calendar No. 789 on Nov. 24, according to congressional records. The committee had voted 28-21 to advance the bill at a Sept. 16 markup.

Rep. Nick Begich, an Alaska Republican, introduced the bill on May 21. It now lists 23 cosponsors, including Rep. Jared Golden, a Maine Democrat, with Reps. Byron Donalds and Maria Elvira Salazar of Florida added on Nov. 24.

The reported text directs the Treasury secretary to set up a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve and a separate Digital Asset Stockpile within 180 days of the law taking effect. Bitcoin owned by the federal government would move into the reserve, while other digital assets would go to the stockpile.

The centerpiece is a lock-up. Treasury would have to hold all bitcoin deposited in the reserve for at least 20 years after enactment, and during that period no reserve bitcoin could be “sold, swapped, auctioned, encumbered, or otherwise disposed of for any purpose.”

After the 20 years, the Treasury secretary could recommend selling up to 10% of the reserve in any two-year period. Two years before the holding period ends, Treasury would have to send Congress recommendations on whether to keep holding or begin a gradual release.

Federal agencies would have 60 days after enactment to give Treasury a full accounting of the bitcoin and other digital assets they hold or have seized. Until the reserve is set up, agencies could not sell those coins except for national security, when required by law or a court, or to return assets to crime victims.

The bill also creates a Proof of Reserve system. Treasury would publish an annual report on total holdings, transactions and control of private keys, checked by an independent auditor with expertise in cryptographic attestations, while the Government Accountability Office would provide oversight.

States could choose to store their own bitcoin in segregated accounts inside the federal reserve, keeping title to the coins and the right to withdraw them.

The committee rewrote the bill before reporting it. Begich’s introduced text asked Treasury and Commerce to study funding bitcoin purchases with surplus remittances from Federal Reserve Banks or a revaluation of the Fed’s gold certificates, as well as with tax payments, tariff revenue and gifts.

Those options are gone from the amended study, which now looks at swapping other digital assets in the stockpile, forfeitures and settlements, and partnerships with states or private entities. The amended version also states that nothing in the study authorizes borrowing, pledging government assets as collateral, new taxes or deficit spending to buy bitcoin.

The committee also amended the bill’s title to drop a reference to offsetting costs with Federal Reserve resources, and removed a section that would have required the Exchange Stabilization Fund to report its bitcoin holdings.

Bitcoin Magazine reported on the bill’s bipartisan debut in May and on the full text revealing the 20-year lock-up in June. The reserve itself already exists under Executive Order 14233, signed in March 2025, but an executive order can be reversed by a future president, which is why Begich and his cosponsors want it in statute.

Setting up the reserve has been slow. In July, a report said Treasury and Commerce were competing over who would control it.

Begich’s bill has not received a floor vote. If it passes the House, the Senate would still need to act before it could reach the president’s desk.
