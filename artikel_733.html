Hollywood Still Faces Weekend Disruptions After FAA Lifts Flight Ground Stop

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The Motion Picture Academy's Governors Awards, the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame induction ceremony and numerous other events could still face delays amid flight cancellations

UPDATED: In the wake of a four-hour long flight ground stop, Hollywood can still expect a weekend of disruptions after the Federal Aviation Administration lifted the shutdown Friday evening. Travel plans were thrown into question for talent, executives and buyers headed to and from Los Angeles and New York during one of the busiest stretches on the industry calendar.

The FAA says it grounded flights “due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety.” It marks the most extensive disruption to air travel in the U.S. since the federal government mandated a total shutdown of all activity after the 9/11 attacks in September 2001. Some flights resumed immediately, but hundreds of others were canceled while travelers scrambled to make new plans. From the Motion Picture Academy’s Governors Awards to an array of premieres, screenings, concerts, among myriad other events, Hollywood’s plans as film award season kicks in to high gear were thrown into total disarray. The same is true for innumerable professional and college football and basketball games. CNN estimated that some 1.5 million travelers were left stranded in airports, on runways or on trips that inevitably had to be extended amid the ground stop.

The cause is not yet clear, but an FAA spokesperson says a communications problem affected the Air Route Traffic Control Centers, which handle flights moving across the country. Preliminary reports indicate it is not a cyberattack, according to law enforcement officials and media outlets. The rare ground stop, which was announced just after 1 p.m. PT, led to more than 7,800 flights across the U.S. being delayed by Friday evening, according to the air travel tracking service FlightAware.

On Sunday, the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences is set to hold its 17th Governors Awards at the Ray Dolby Ballroom at Ovation Hollywood, where honorary Oscars go to eight-time nominee Glenn Close, legendary Director Ridley Scott and Floyd Norman, Disney’s first Black animator. Killer Films producers Christine Vachon and Pamela Koffler, longtime champions of independent film, receive the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award.

The evening doubles as one of the season’s most important campaign stops, with contenders working a room filled with the Academy’s governors, three from each of its 19 branches, representing everyone from actors and directors to artisans and executives.

Variety has reached out to the Academy for comment.

The grounding will also affect buyers and sellers at the final days of the American Film Market, which runs Nov. 10-15 at the Fairmont Century Plaza in Century City. With international sales agents, buyers and financiers due to fly home this weekend, many of them could find themselves stuck in Los Angeles.
