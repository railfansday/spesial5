At Least Robert Pattinson Looks the Part…According to Ian Fleming

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Bond author commissioned a sketch of 007 in the 1950s — and it bears an uncanny resemblance to a certain British actor currently being fitted for a tuxedo.

Well, it took nearly 70 years, and there were plenty of detours along the way, but Ian Fleming finally got the James Bond of his dreams — at least when it comes to jawline and nose shape.

None of the previous 007s — not Sean Connery, George Lazenby, Roger Moore, Timothy Dalton, Pierce Brosnan, or even Daniel Craig — looked anything like how the character’s creator envisioned him. In Fleming’s mind — at least according to the SMERSH dossier occasionally referred to in his books — Bond was about six-foot tall, 168 pounds, with black hair and blue eyes. For Pattison, that’s check, check, check and check. But for the actors who proceeded him, not so much. Connery and Lazenby had dark hair but brown eyes. Craig had blue eyes but fair hair (controversial coloring that inspired more than a few cheeky headlines when he was cast: “No Mr. Blonde, They Expect You To Dye”). Moore had blue eyes but his hair was shades too light. Brosnan came closest, with black hair and blue eyes. But it turns out Fleming had other criteria aside from basic coloring and measurements.

In fact, the author commissioned a portrait of Bond in the 1950s, years before the movies were launched, when a British newspaper purchased the rights to serialize the novels as a cartoon strip. To help the paper’s illustrator nail down the look, Fleming hired an unknown artist to sketch 007’s face according to his highly specific instructions (“I think we should avoid giving Bond an American-shaped head and a duck’s haircut,” he wrote in one note).

The result was a long angular face with heavy brows, a narrow nose, sharp cheekbones, thin lips and a firm, squared-off chin that looks uncannily like… Robert Pattinson.

The funny thing is, the newspaper’s illustrator, John McLusky, pretty much ignored Fleming’s ideas and drew his own Bond with a much more rugged appearance — narrower eyes, a more expansive jawline, and a broad, cleft chin. In other words, a face that looked uncannily like… Sean Connery, who’d be cast in the part a half-decade later.
