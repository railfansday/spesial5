Valley Central football champions thriving with 'second life'

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

A whirlwind of events has taken Valley Central's football program from the depths of playoff defeat to a second life in the New York state tournament in mere weeks.

"It's unreal,'' first-year head coach Kyle Schneider summed.

Valley Central proved the beneficiary in a player eligibility case, securing a forfeit win over Middletown in the Section 9 semifinals and stunning Newburgh in the final, only two weeks after getting crushed by the same Goldbacks. Not only was it the Vikings' first sectional title, but it was an invitation to the first round of the state tournament on Saturday as they face powerhouse Christian Brothers Academy of Albany.

"Is it unbelievable? I think so,'' said team co-captain Collin Bishopp, not doubting the validity of the championship, only the circumstances leading to it.

The Vikings got off to a promising start, winning their first two contests and four of their first five. A win over a depleted Monroe-Woodbury squad on Oct. 17 had Valley Central at 5-1. Schneider admits the team "hit the wall'' in consecutive setbacks to Pine Bush and Newburgh and the Vikings were drubbed 48-15 by unbeaten Middletown in the Section 9 Class AA semifinals.

Oh well, nice season, tough finish, see you at the team banquet. But then the rumors began to swirl at school on the following Monday. It was discovered that Middletown had used an ineligible player and would have to forfeit the playoff win, sending VC to the final against Newburgh. Call it a second life.

"As soon as I found out, I texted my defensive coordinator, 'You can't kill what's already dead,''' Bishopp said. "I heard it all week, we were playing with the house's money.''

The Valley Central coaches swapped out their first game plan from the loss to Newburgh on Oct. 30 and came up with a new attack that proved more effective the second time around. Basically, the Vikings figured out NFA's most common repetition on offense and the VC defense slanted toward it every time. "Our energy gets amplified every time we make big plays and big stops,'' Bishopp said.

VC got out to an early lead and made it stand up for a 16-6 victory.

Bishopp said he went and hugged the school superintendent. The bus ride back to school was joyous, to say the least, and the community rallied to greet them upon their arrival. Congratulations were abound when classes resumed on the Monday following.

Schneider took over a program that has always seemed to be a step on the outside of the postseason and was 2-8 a year ago. The Vikings had appeared in the postseason only twice in the last 20 years, bowing out in the opening round in 2015 and 2017.

"I knew it was probably going to be kind of a one step at a time,'' he said.

But Schneider inherited a team with some older players and the key was they all came up as part of a winning stretch in the Orange County youth league and an unbeaten junior varsity team, and those bonds proved valuable.

"Really, this team has been together a long time,'' said co-captain Mike Giannoni. "So this has been a long process.''

Giannoni said Schneider and his staff helped change the culture. On the first day of training camp Schneider presented cards with five words as part of a motto: Communication. Accountability. Respect. Discipline. Selflessness. Those points are addressed every day by the coaches. The players bought into the new message right away. "I think that motto really helps us stay together and stay focused,'' Giannoni said.

"I think coach has done a phenomenal job. I think he really stepped up,'' Bishopp said.

No matter the outcome against CBA on Saturday, these Vikings can celebrate seven wins, the most since the 1968 team won the Orange County League title at 8-0 and the 1982 team went 8-1, finishing a game back in the OCL American Conference.
