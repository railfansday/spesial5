NBA team grades: Report card for all 30 teams after the first month

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The first month of the 2026-27 NBA season is in the books, and it's time to take stock of what's happened so far. Let's hand out some early grades.

It might be too soon to draw sweeping conclusions, but there's been enough basketball played to make preliminary judgements about the contenders falling short of expectations and the surprising teams surpassing what most experts anticipated. Evaluating each team's performance goes beyond its record, with roster construction, player development and overall trajectory factoring into whether a season is trending in the right direction.

Injuries to several stars and key contributors have shaped the league conversation through the opening month. Some teams have responded well to the adversity. Others have already dug themselves a hole that could prove difficult to escape. As a result, the grades cover a wide range of outcomes: six teams earned an A from USA TODAY Sports through the season's first month, while 10 received a D or F.

Here are grades for all 30 NBA teams now that the first month of the 2026-27 season is complete.

NBA PANIC METER: Which teams should worry after first month of season?

Records as of games played on Nov. 20. Preseason record projections via USA TODAY Sports.

The Hawks are right in the thick of the Eastern Conference playoff race again with guard Nickeil Alexander-Walker leading their backcourt, but there have been some troubling early signs. Jalen Johnson's production has dipped across the board from last season's breakout campaign, veteran closer C.J. McCollum is sidelined for at least another month and Atlanta has benefited from the NBA's second-easiest schedule to date. The Hawks' issues are flying under-the-radar, but they've struggled to show they belong among the East's top contenders when facing the NBA's best teams thus far.

After thriving without Jayson Tatum during much of last year's regular season, the Celtics haven't missed a beat in their first games since trading Jaylen Brown this past offseason. Tatum has elevated his playmaking, and Paul George looks rejuvenated as his new running mate in Boston. The Celtics have the NBA's best NET rating as of Nov. 18 and even got the better of Brown in their first matchup against the Philadelphia 76ers.

The Nets' roster is a mess because they've used seven first-round draft picks the past two years and none has demonstrated franchise-player potential to this point. Their top three guards (Egor Demin, Nolan Traore and Mikel Brown Jr.) haven't played much together yet due to minutes restrictions and skillset overlap. Their past four losses have come by an average of 24.5 points, and Julius Randle was spotted yelling at his teammates on the bench before calling their Nov. 18 loss to the Chicago Bulls "one of the worst games I've been a part of." After years of stockpiling assets, the Nets' rebuild still doesn't appear to have a clear path forward.

The Hornets perhaps expected to take a step back after trading LaMelo Ball this past offseason, but their start has muted much of the momentum generated from last year's encouraging run to the playoffs. Kon Knueppel lasted just nine minutes in his season debut before suffering a hamstring injury, and he's one of several key players to have missed games due to injury. The team, meanwhile, has the worst offensive rating in the league after finishing with the NBA's fifth-most efficient offense last season. Brandon Miller is shooting less than 35% from the field as the top offensive option, and Naz Reid, the primary acquisition in the Ball deal, was benched four games into the season by Hornets coach Charles Lee for "not meeting Hornets' standards." Just one month into the season, nearly everything that could have gone wrong for Charlotte has.

The Bulls are starting to emerge from a 1-9 start to the season, though their recent surge is happening right as No. 3 overall pick Caleb Wilson was diagnosed with a broken hand that will keep him out for the next few months. Wilson showed intriguing flashes in his early appearances, but it's still unclear whether Chicago's core featuring Josh Giddey and Matas Buzelis is viable long-term. The Bulls feel closer to escaping the bottom-half of the Eastern Conference than they have been because of Wilson, but his injury means they'll have to wait longer to figure out exactly what they have.

The Cavaliers are off to a rougher-than-expected start to the season, and it's particularly concerning because their starters are the reason they're scuffling along. James Harden is showing his age more regularly, struggling to finish at the rim and get to the free throw line. Evan Mobley has already suffered a toe injury. Peyton Watson, the team's main offseason acquisition, looked completely out of sync before missing action recently with a back injury. Rookie Meleek Thomas and forward Mario Hezonja have been bright spots off the bench, but Cleveland's core has failed to resemble the group that reached the Eastern Conference finals last season.

Cooper Flagg appears to have taken another step forward in his development after winning Rookie of the Year, and his fit alongside Kyrie Irving has been better than many expected under new coach Dusty May. They've positioned the Mavericks as a legitimate play-in contender despite playing one of the tougher schedules in the league through 15 games. Their early success together strengthens the case for Irving to remain in Dallas beyond the NBA trade deadline. With a first-year NBA coach and a 19-year-old franchise star, the Mavericks are outperforming expectations through the season's first month.

The Nuggets remain a threat in the Western Conference playoff race, but there are some early signs that Denver's reign as a legitimate title contender could be waning. Nikola Jokic's 3-point shot has largely disappeared from his repertoire, a development that began after he returned from a wrist injury last season. His points, rebounds and assists per game have decreased compared to recent years. Jamal Murray's strong play has been offset by relatively uninspiring starts for veterans Aaron Gordon and DeMar DeRozan. It's no coincidence the team has gone from first in the league in offensive rating last year to 10th as of Nov. 19. If Jokic can't return to his MVP form and cover some of the roster flaws like he so often has over the past five years, it's hard to envision this group being a championship contender.

The Pistons were a trendy regression candidate in the East, but they've already got wins over the Boston Celtics, Miami Heat, Philadelphia 76ers and San Antonio Spurs through the first month of the season. Cade Cunningham is back in the MVP conversation, and Ausar Thompson is becoming one of the league's most disruptive two-way playmakers. Early injuries to Jalen Duren and offseason addition Isaiah Joe have taken a little shine off Detroit's strong start, but guard Daniss Jenkins is beginning to blossom into the secondary perimeter scoring option Cunningham has lacked in previous seasons.

The Warriors got an unexpected gift when rookie Yaxel Lendeborg quickly emerged as the leading NBA Rookie of the Year candidate and helped solve the team's void on the wing with Jimmy Butler sidelined. They reeled off eight-straight wins with Lendeborg serving as Steph Curry's sidekick. But Golden State dropped five games in a row after Curry suffered an oblique injury on Nov. 11, a development that has exposed Draymond Green's limitations and the lack of offense creation on the roster beyond Curry. Even so, Curry's eventual return and the prospect of Butler coming back gives Golden State more intrigue and upside than most expected entering the season.

The Rockets have looked like NBA title contenders through the season's first month, with the return of point guard Fred Van Vleet helping jump start the league's second-most efficient offense. Houston's turnovers are way down and its 3-point shooting is among the best in the NBA at the moment. Alperen Sengun, meanwhile, is on pace to break his career highs in points and assists as part of the Rockets' deep front court, and Kevin Durant remains one of the NBA's elite scorers. This appears to be one of the NBA's most complete rosters, blending ascending young talent with veterans who have extensive playoff experience. Considering the Rockets also possess the assets to make another significant move before the trade deadline, they may be San Antonio's biggest threat in the Western Conference.

The Pacers were hoping to rebound from last season's injury-induced tank, but they lost star Tyrese Haliburton to a hamstring injury after just four games. Indiana is one of the worst shooting teams in the league after one month, and it's wasting another strong start from Pascal Siakam. The lack of shooting has complicated Ivica Zubac's transition to the lineup, with poor spacing creating fit issues for a player Indiana spent considerable assets to acquire. He's on pace for his worst statistical season in six years. The Pacers entered the season expecting to rejoin the Eastern Conference contender tier, but they look much closer to a lottery team than a playoff threat right now.

The Clippers are in rebuilding mode and off to an uneven start, with the expectation they could be sellers at the trade deadline. The good news is that Darius Garland, the centerpiece of last year's James Harden trade, appears to have rediscovered his All-Star form after an injury-riddled 2025-26 season. The development of first-round pick Keaton Wagler alongside Garland is likely more important than the results the rest of the way, and he's shooting better than 40% from 3-point range since Nov. 2. The Clippers are one of the league's better shooting teams overall, but they've offset that strength by ranking among the NBA's most turnover-prone teams. They're likely headed for the lottery, but that's exactly where most anticipated them to be.

The Lakers are off to a fantastic start with Luka Dončić a leading MVP candidate and on pace to surpass Michael Jordan for the highest scoring average (38.1) since the 1976 NBA/ABA merger. Walker Kessler is also providing the dominating interior presence Los Angeles hoped for when it traded for him this offseason and gave him a huge contract. Kessler is averaging a double-double, while Austin Reaves is scoring more efficiently than at any point in his career. If Dončić continues to play at this level, the Lakers will be in the NBA title discussion come playoff time.

The Grizzlies have been the biggest surprise of the 2026-27 season thus far, vaulting toward the top of the Western Conference standings when many expected them to begin a rebuild after trading away Ja Morant, Jaren Jackson Jr. and Desmond Bane. Instead, the team's recent draft picks, particularly Cameron Boozer and Cedric Coward, have helped spearhead a balanced and remarkably deep roster that already looks like a playoff contender. A recent injury to veteran Ty Jerome looms large, but Cam Spencer has filled the void admirably thus far. Memphis owns the NBA's best offensive rating as of Nov. 20. Few saw that coming.

NBA observers were in panic mode when the Heat began the Giannis Antetokounmpo era with four losses in a row, but Miami is 9-2 since then with an increasingly potent offense built around pace and space. Antetokounmpo is leading the Heat with the fourth-highest scoring average in the NBA (28.2 points), and Davion Mitchell leads the NBA in assists per game. Only the Spurs' starters have a better NET rating than the Heat lineup featuring Antetokounmpo, Bam Adebayo, Klay Thompson, Mitchell and Pelle Larsson among groups that have played at least 65 minutes together. One month into the season, Miami appears to have already discovered the lineup it can trust most when playoff games are on the line.

The Bucks were expected to be bad this season after trading Giannis Antetokounmpo, but they're off to the worst start in franchise history with the league's worst record and own the worst NET rating in the Eastern Conference as of Nov. 20. With veterans like Myles Turner, Kyle Kuzma and Gary Trent Jr. taking up salary cap space after being acquired in prior seasons, this rebuild appears to only just be beginning. Milwaukee entered the post-Giannis era without a clear franchise cornerstone, and the first month of the season has done little to change that. It will be painful for Bucks' fans this season.

The Timberwolves have done a fine job keeping pace in the competitive Western Conference after losing Anthony Edwards to a knee injury that required arthoscopic surgery. LaMelo Ball, Ayo Dosunmu, Jaden McDaniels and Rudy Gobert have formed a well-rounded core that could be dangerous if Edwards returns before the playoffs. There is no timetable on his recovery, though Edwards told reporters on Nov. 19 he is planning to to come back this season. Minnesota's ceiling ultimately depends on his health.

The Pelicans got off to a bad start, won four games in a row and now appear headed nowhere fast once again. Dejounte Murray's recent trade request brought renewed attention to one of the NBA's most confusing roster situations. New Orleans hasn't been able to win consistently despite Zion Williamson enjoying one of the healthiest stretches of his career, and Trey Murphy III is increasingly viewed as one of the league's more attractive trade targets. Jeremiah Fears and Derik Queen are intriguing young players, but both have contributed to the defensive issues that have left New Orleans among the NBA's worst teams on that end of the floor. Murray, meanwhile, is owed enough money that moving him won't be simple. If this start continues, Joe Dumars would have to consider a roster overhaul.

The Knicks deserve the benefit of the doubt after a sluggish start coming off an NBA championship, though the early-season struggles of Josh Hart and Mikal Bridges, as well as the ongoing contract situation involving Karl-Anthony Towns, bear watching. Jalen Brunson and Towns continue to form one of the NBA's most potent duos, and New York remains firmly positioned in the Eastern Conference playoff picture thanks in large part to one of the league's best defenses. The Knicks proved last season they can reach another level when the games matter most.

The Thunder are doing their best to stay afloat in the crowded Western Conference standings playing without Jalen Williams, Chet Holmgren and Cason Wallace due to injuries. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander remains one of the league's best players, and OKC's deep reservoir of first-round picks is paying off with younger players like Ajay Mitchell and rookie Aday Mara successfully stepping into larger roles. The Thunder have survived a difficult opening month and came out looking capable of remaining in the playoff mix. But bad injury luck could prevent them from contending for a title this season.

The Magic hoped to emerge as Eastern Conference contenders, but they finished the opening month of the season below .500 and lost star Paolo Banchero to injury after just two games. A recent four-game winning streak that included a victory over the Boston Celtics showcased the potential of a group built around Franz Wagner, Desmond Bane, Anthony Black and a healthy Jalen Suggs. But the franchise is still no closer to determining whether Banchero and Wagner can thrive together as a long-term foundation. Until Banchero returns, that question will remain unanswered, and Orlando appears destined to be part of the crowded pack of Eastern Conference teams battling to avoid the play-in tournament.

After considerable preseason hype and past stumbles out of the gate for LeBron James-led superteams, the Sixers finished the first month of the 2026-27 season tied with the Spurs for the NBA's best record. James and Jaylen Brown have blended in well with Tyrese Maxey, Joel Embiid and VJ Edgecombe, though the team's starting lineup had a surprising -4.9 NET rating playing together through 16 games. It speaks to some of the unexpected depth Philadelphia enjoys, with Dean Wade, Anfernee Simons and Kentavious Caldwell-Pope forming a versatile rotation. One month into the season, the 76ers look like the team to beat in the Eastern Conference.

The Suns have rebounded from a rough start to the season despite playing one of the toughest schedules in the league, and Devin Booker remains among the NBA's top 12 scorers. But their two most-used lineups have been outscored by an average net rating of 15.5 points per 100 possessions. Jalen Green is having the least productive season of his career, and centers Oso Ighodaro and Khaman Maluach have looked overmatched while replacing the injured Mark Williams. As currently constructed, Phoenix looks capable of reaching the play-in tournament. But unless the Suns can find an upgrade before the trade deadline, their ceiling appears to be a first-round playoff exit.

The Trail Blazers have taken a step forward with wins over the Lakers and Thunder already. Damian Lillard is providing a perimeter scoring punch, and his presence hasn't come at the expense of Deni Avdija. It's turned a lackluster offense into one with a top-10 NET rating thus far. It remains to be seen how Ja Morant's return from a hamstring injury affects the rotation, but early returns suggest Portland's crowded backcourt can co-exist. This might not be a legitimate title contender, but the Trail Blazers look as feisty as they have at any point since Lillard led them to the Western Conference Finals in 2019.

The Kings appear to be the most dysfunctional team in the league, with a core of expensive veteran players who don't seem interested in remaining in Sacramento but lack significant trade value. Rookie Darius Acuff Jr. has been a bright spot, on pace to average the most points by a first-year player since Michael Jordan. But the Kings haven't translated that production into wins and they have the league's worst NET rating. Meanwhile, last year's breakout rookie, 23-year-old center Maxime Raynaud, has yet to play due to injury. This directionless franchise is no closer to finding its way.

The Spurs have looked every bit like a team on a mission to avenge last June's NBA Finals loss, highlighted by a recent 12-game winning streak. Victor Wembanyama is an MVP candidate once again, and the returning core of Dylan Harper, Stephon Castle and De'Aaron Fox has made the absence of free-agent acquisition Tobias Harris largely an afterthought. A recent hip injury to Harper does not appear to be serious, and San Antonio is deep enough to withstand his absence if he misses additional time. One month into the season, the Spurs look like the favorites to win the NBA title.

After emerging as one of the NBA's pleasant surprises last season, the Raptors have stumbled out of the gate this year. Kawhi Leonard has yet to play because of a knee injury, and Scottie Barnes has also missed time. The team's defense has gone from a strength to a weakness, particularly in close games, and Toronto ranks among the NBA's worst rebounding teams because of a wing-heavy roster that lacks a reliable center. The Raptors traded for Leonard with championship aspirations in mind, but the first month of that pursuit has been anything but encouraging.

The Jazz's hopes of emerging as a playoff contender after a lengthy rebuilding have been decimated by injuries to Lauri Markannen and Jaren Jackson Jr., as well as a disastrous start that featured a 12-game losing streak. Keyonte George played more like a rising star in two recent Utah wins and, along with rookie Darryn Peterson and 20-year-old Ace Bailey, gives the franchise a promising young core. But the Jazz expected to compete for a postseason berth and that might not be possible after an opening month that could hardly have gone worse.

The Wizards remain near the bottom of the Eastern Conference standings, and their decision to give Trae Young a $212 million contract extension is already drawing scrutiny. Young is shooting a career-worst 30.4% from 3-point range, and Washington ranks as the NBA's second-worst 3-point shooting team. Opponents have also targeted Young defensively in crunch time. The team's struggles are particularly concerning because Anthony Davis is healthy and playing well, while rookie AJ Dybantsa has shown encouraging flashes despite struggling to shoot from outside since returning from injury. Washington may still have enough talent to reach the playoffs in the East, but its biggest investment is underperforming.
