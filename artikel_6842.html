Most Black Friday Sales Are Fake. These Ones Aren’t.

The Internet is a deep, dark place this week, but we found where the deals are worth it.

How do you even define a good deal anymore? The whole point of Black Friday was for retailers to make us wake up at the crack of dawn the day after Thanksgiving, wait in line, and then fight someone over a TV that was 50-percent off at Sears or Best Buy. How did they do that? They would sell a few products (door busters) at a loss just to physically bring people into the store. Those deals were good, and everything else, which you would buy because you were already there, was where the money was made.

That is now a thing of the past. Lines and physical altercations aside, in the past 20 years there has been a real swing in the favor of big retailers. First, we created Cyber Monday (though most people do their Black Friday shopping online already). Then the deals started a week earlier. Now, they last all month. And instead of having door busters to get you in the door, retailers just have very aggressive online marketing programs. In fact, it turns out that most Black Friday deals aren’t even deals at all. In a recent study, it was found that one in three Amazon Black Friday deals provided no true savings at all—prices were marked up earlier in the year, only to be brought back down to a standard retail value. The big retailers like Amazon and Walmart are the most to blame for these tricky strategies, but you should still be judicious when looking at any so-called Black Friday sales.

The good news? Black Friday is still filled with great deals—you just have to know where to look and what to expect. A lot of big wins revolve around private sales at more niche, direct-to-consumer stores. For example, Filson, one of our favorite outerwear brands, is having a 25-percent-off sale for Black Friday. That’s not the 75-percent-off you might have wanted, and there are other times that Filson goes 25-percent off. But the best strategy these days is to think about holiday shopping: Just buy the things you already would, but do it while they’re on sale now.

You should be especially skeptical of tech products. The increase in all tech prices has made it easy for those brands to push a 10-percent-off sale when products where 10-percent-off the current price. This is the sort of stuff we’ve been watching as the sales roll around.
