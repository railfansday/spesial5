If you’ve already taken a lap through our gift guide and still need more ideas, we’ve got you. All three of us editors here at Climbing have selected our top picks for gifts this year, from gear we’ve got our eyes on, to accessories in the climbing zeitgeist right now.

Some of these editor gifts picks make perfect mini gifts for stocking stuffers or for that climber partner who you want to thank for giving you the perfect soft catch this past year. Other picks make a bigger impact, from a super lightweight harness to a great gym bag. Most of our picks are great for a variety of climbers, whether it’s your new boyfriend who you hope to convert into a climber, or your mentor who’s been at it for eons.

We hope these gift ideas make your holiday shopping as painless as a splitter hand crack so you can spend more time training or climbing, and less time scrolling through endless product pages and catalogs.

Originally designed and hand-sewn by Nate Barton—the Missoula-based climber who ran the meme account @beta.sprayer until 2024—the Lolo Hoodie V1 ($169.99) is a functional midlayer for cold-weather climbing and the flagship product of the fledgling brand Lolo Hardware. Its large, zippered chest pocket was originally intended to help ice climbers warm up their hands between pitches.

Having pretty much lived in this hoodie for the past three winters, I’ve found the chest pocket far superior to any sort of pant pocket for storing my phone, gloves, and snacks. Plus, with my items against my chest, I never have to worry about my phone freezing or my car keys accidentally slipping out during a chimney. From Joshua Tree in January to Canyonlands in November, this is a comfy and durable hoodie for the coldest days and nights of climbing.

It’s 2026, folks: If you’re still using mined chalk instead of seawater-sourced chalk, this is your call to finally make the switch. Climbing’s feature on the hidden environmental impact of chalk showed how the vast majority of magnesium carbonate comes from mines in China, where magnesite factories poison the water and soil of nearby residents.

After breaking a cross-loaded carabiner in a scary trad fall, I now always add some sort of tape or rubber band to keep cam carabiners in place. I wrote about my DIY solution a couple of years ago, but now I simply slide on Avant Climbing’s Flip-Stop Cam Carabiner Stabilizers ($22.50 for a 10-pack). The Flip-Stop makes for easy cam clipping when mega pumped. But unlike my rubber band hack, it also keeps the cam sling open for clipping a long draw into. Crucially, like a quickdraw’s dogbone, the Flip-Stop prevents carabiners from rotating onto the dangerously weak X-axis. Nothing ruins the holidays like a pair of broken ankles.

The new Arc’teryx Alpha SL Harness ($180) may weigh less than the gift wrap paper you’ll hide it in. At an absurdly light 78 grams (2.7oz), the Alpha SL is for those going ultralight in the mountains, whether that’s an alpine rock romp or a big ski traverse. This harness is also surprisingly comfortable to rappel in, thanks to its uber-stiff Aluula-composite construction. And it fits a range of body types thanks to its adjustable leg loops and five waistbelt sizes. Just don’t set it down unattended on a glacier—it’ll blow away in the slightest gust of wind.

The Patagonia Airshed Pro Pullover ($169) might have been built for mountain running, but I wear mine year-round on all sorts of objectives, from sunny rock climbs to cold north faces. The Airshed Pro is a pseudo-windlayer: decently breathable for high-output activities, yet stills blocks the wind and has a water-repellent coating. The result is a shirt equally at home skiing on a glacier or rambling up a gusty rock ridge.

A note on sizing: Since the Airshed Pro was originally designed for runners, the cut is quite slim. I size up to facilitate a wider range of motion.

I have been loving the Black Diamond Solution Hoody ($299) as a light fall belay jacket and even for cold rock climbing when I’m too chilly to shed my jacket. The Solution manages to be super lightweight, movement-friendly, and breathable, yet warm. This is largely because the specific PrimaLoft insulation used in this jacket is made with technology developed by NASA for astronauts. Here on earth, it also works great. It honestly does feel like being wrapped in warm air.

The pocket real estate is also generous, and it’s nice that the whole thing packs into the left hand pocket so you can easily stash it in your pack without a cam or crampon tearing into the insulation. I’m also looking forward to using this as a mid-layer below a shell for ice climbing this winter.

Unless you’ve been living under a rock (which many of our readers hopefully have), you’ve heard about the new film about the weed plane that crashed into a Yosemite lake in 1976. What followed was a tragicomic climber gold rush. Now, to mark the 50th anniversary of the incident, you can sport one of several new t-shirts or hats ($29.95-$44.95) memorializing this infamous moment in Yosemite climbing history. All proceeds support the Yosemite Climbing Association and the Yosemite Climbing Museum.

Have I used one? No. Do I desperately want one? Yes. As a 5’2” climber, I’m often just a few inches short of being able to make the clip from a good stance. I’ve heard wonderful things about the stiffy Kong Panic Draw ($54.95-$59.95) that lets you clip bolts otherwise out of reach. Maybe 2027 will be the year I finally gift myself one! If there’s a short or easily panicked sport climber in your life, I bet they would be stoked to get this as a present. Or you could make one for them DIY-style yourself.

The oversized, durable Metolius Climbers Tote ($64.95) has become my go-to gym bag. It easily fits my personal gear plus a 60m rope, or four sets of personal gear (shoes, harness, and chalkbag for me, my husband, and my two kids). Despite having stored weathered climbing shoes zippered inside for too long, this bag miraculously doesn’t stink yet either. It stows away nicely in gym cubbies (whereas my previous Cotopaxi tote would sort of spill out) and is easy to carry by hand or shoulder. This is a great mid-sized gift for the climber in your life, or you could fill it with other gear you’re gifting for a bigger impact.

Note on price: While this tote isn’t on sale at REI, co-op members get 20% off one full-price item through Nov. 30, so this is a good one to save on.

I receive a lot of climbing books and galleys to review and consider for coverage. Only some of them are so good that I can’t put them down. That’s how I felt about Erratica, a new collection of true stories from Moab-based, life-long climber Brian Laidlaw. Rare is the read that both keeps you attentive and entertained, yet also evokes deeper reflection. With Erratica, Brian manages to do just that. Pick up a copy ($20) at Moab’s Back of Beyond bookstore, or find one online anywhere books are sold.
