Russian governor detained in rare criminal case against regional leader

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Andrei Chibis's wife rejects the allegations and he is expected to appear in court on Thursday

The governor of Russia's northern Murmansk region has been arrested on corruption charges in the first detention of a regional leader for five years.

Andrei Chibis, 47, is suspected of embezzlement and misappropriation of more than a billion roubles ($12.2.m; Â£9.3m) set aside to build an Arctic highway, as well as a further 39m roubles from the Murmansk construction budget.

Chibis is reported to have been detained as he arrived on a visit to Moscow on Monday and was taken in for questioning.

He denies wrongdoing but has made no public comment. His wife Yevgenia Chibis said "anyone who knows our family understands how ridiculous this is".

In a post on social media, she said her husband sincerely believed in the Murmansk region and thanked people for their words of support: "We really need them right now."

Chibis worked for several years as deputy minister for construction and housing, before becoming governor of Murmansk in 2019.

Murmansk is a key military point for Russia in the Arctic and is home to the Russian navy's Northern fleet.

The port of Murmansk is home to the Russian navy's Northern fleet

Chibis was re-elected two years ago after surviving a knife attack and is considered an active supporter of Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine. Governors are seen as representing the Kremlin, and Vladimir Putin removed several in the months after the war began in February 2022.

However, until now no regional governor has been arrested since the start of the war, even though plenty of high-ranking officials have been detained in that time.

The Murmansk governor is one of a number of officials suspected of corruption by the Russian interior ministry's investigative department which, according to reports, alleged that a criminal group was set up in 2022 to embezzle money from the regional budget and move it abroad, including to Kyrgyzstan.

Another part of the case involves alleged fraud surrounding the rebuilding of a motorway between the two Arctic towns of Apatity and Kirovsk, which are 15km (9.3 miles) apart.

According to Russian news agency Interfax, a court will decide whether Chibis will remain in custody at a hearing on Wednesday.

He has been sanctioned by both the UK and EU, which accuses him of involvement in the illegal transportation of Ukrainian children to Russia and their adoption by Russian families.
