U.S. and China are launching a superintelligence risk dialogue ahead of Trump-Xi talks

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the channel will let either side alert the other to serious incidents involving its models

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced a new dialogue with China on superintelligence on Tuesday, days before President Donald Trump is due to meet Chinese President Xi Jinping. Bessent spoke at a National Committee on U.S.-China Relations event in New York.

Bessent said the dialogue, which began at Trump's direction, is meant to address risks both countries face and to keep either side from misreading the other's systems. He said the two sides anticipate sharing their approaches to vetting advanced models prior to launch, as well as to making sure human judgment stays in charge of the technology.

Bessent said the U.S. and China are also building a dedicated line of communication through which either nation can warn the other about grave incidents tied to its superintelligence models. He said new areas of risk management will need attention as the technology advances.

Bessent said he will join Trump in Shenzhen next week, where the two leaders are to meet before a fourth encounter in Miami. It would be their third meeting this year, after Trump's May trip to Beijing and Xi's September visit to Washington. The Shenzhen meeting falls on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation Leaders' Meeting on Nov. 18-19, according to CNBC.

Bessent offered no pledge to hold back progress in the technology, according to CNBC. The Trump administration has mostly pushed back on wider curbs on how AI is built, fearing that tighter rules would hand China an advantage, CNBC said. In an interview released Sunday, Trump said he does not mind guardrails but does not want the U.S. in "a deep second position" behind China, according to CNBC.

China’s Foreign Ministry dismissed calls in September to slow frontier models, according to CNBC.

Xie Feng, China's ambassador to the U.S., also spoke at the event. He said joint efforts by the two countries are "indispensable" to improving AI governance, according to Bloomberg.

The dialogue follows the first AI-focused talks between the countries' top trade negotiators, which China's Commerce Ministry confirmed on Sept. 24. Both governments had agreed at the May summit in Beijing to establish intergovernmental AI talks. Bessent said in September that the two countries had settled on creating an AI dialogue, according to Bloomberg.
