Dow futures rally 683 points as the benchmark heads for its best week since April 2025

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

S&P 500 futures rose 1.7% and Nasdaq-100 futures advanced 2.4% ahead of a Friday session scheduled to close at 1 p.m. ET

Futures tied to the Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 683 points, or 1.4%, on Friday as the index headed for its biggest weekly gain since April 2025, according to CNBC.

S&P 500 futures rose 1.7% and Nasdaq $NDAQ-100 futures advanced 2.4%. Technology stocks led the move: Nvidia $NVDA stock gained about 3%, Meta $META Platforms stock rose more than 3% and CoreWeave stock added 5%.

Entering Friday's session, the Dow was up 4.2% for the week. The S&P 500 had climbed 3.9% and the Nasdaq had gained 4.1%. U.S. markets were closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving and are scheduled to close at 1 p.m. ET on Friday.

The week began with a large rally. Nvidia stock jumped 31% on Monday after the company reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, according to CNBC. The Dow climbed by upward of 1,400 points that day, a jump of about 3%. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite each posted their best day since April 2025.

Nvidia and other AI-related stocks gave back part of those gains during the following sessions, which weighed on the S&P 500 and Nasdaq. Sentiment toward AI stocks was already soft before Huang's departure, with investors fretting over how the buildout is funded with borrowing, hints that capital outlays are cooling, and the prospect that data center construction will decelerate following Democratic midterm victories, according to CNBC.

Some on Wall Street tie the recent weakness to the prospect of Federal Reserve interest rate increases, according to CNBC. This week's personal consumption expenditures price index data bolstered traders' bets that the Fed will raise rates several times in the months ahead.

"Most of this stuff really comes down to the Fed. That's the intermediate term factor," Warren Pies, co-founder of 3Fourteen Research, told CNBC's "Closing Bell." Pies said "many" of those his firm had consulted voiced fears of a "replay of 2018," a year in which the central bank tightened policy with four rate increases.
