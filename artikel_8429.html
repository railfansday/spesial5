Tanggal: 2026-11-24

I'm in a similar situation and find this difficult to model. My mom is 91 and I have no idea how long she will live. She is in memory care, but I could see her living to 100. The biggest problem is timing. Half of her money is in IRAs and half is taxable. Her RMD percentage is getting higher each year, but one of her IRAs is invested in dividend stocks and it typically returns 10% to 15% each year. So it is still growing even with her RMDs. My Roth conversion window is from now to 10 years from now. There is a sweet spot where her IRAs will be small enough to not wreck my Roth conversion plan. If she dies tomorrow, further Roth conversion are probably toast or piddling. If she dies 6 to 7 years from now, Roth conversion would probably still make sense (although I don't think I can convert inherited IRA money, it is the RMDs from it that wreck things and I could choose how much over 10 years to RMD).

One other thing to consider is can you take a lump sum from your pension instead of monthly payments? If you're going to have a windfall of mostly taxable money you can use to live on, you may be able to do significant roth conversions of the pretax money you already have. A pension eats in to your roth conversion space. I took my pension as monthly payments. Its nice, but it is kind of big and eats up most of the 12% tax bracket. When I retired I didn't have much in taxable, so I chose the monthly payment pension option.

It would probably be best if she would just start gifting money to everyone now. But she isn't comfortable with that (memory care issues aren't helping her reason).
My MIL is gifting money to us now and this isn't even coming close to drawing down the inheritance. Plus she is gifting to charity. She is firmly in the slow go years and my mother is firmly in the no go years of retirement.
