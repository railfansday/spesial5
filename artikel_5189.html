Mystery $106 Million Crypto Theft Hits EU Entity Days After Binance Hack

Another nine-figure theft has hit crypto, just days after the biggest heist in the industry’s history.

An unnamed entity in the European Union is suspected of losing more than $106 million from several of its accounts on centralized exchanges on Sunday, pseudonymous onchain investigator ZachXBT said Monday in a post to his Telegram channel.

The funds were withdrawn as USDC and USDT stablecoins by what he called an “illicit actor.”

ZachXBT published 11 Ethereum addresses tied to the theft.

Instant exchanges let users quickly swap one cryptocurrency for another, often with little identity verification, while Monero hides senders, receivers and amounts, making it a popular tool for obscuring stolen funds.

Swapping out of USDC and USDT also matters because the companies behind those stablecoins can freeze them.

ZachXBT said the culprit remains unidentified, but that he could say “with a high degree of confidence” that it isn’t Lazarus Group, citing multiple laundering indicators. Lazarus is the umbrella name often used for North Korea’s state-backed hackers. ZachXBT didn’t name the victim.

That sets the theft apart from the record-setting $6.4 billion Binance hack, which the FBI has attributed to TraderTraitor, a North Korean hacking group.

In that case, stablecoin issuers moved within minutes. Tether froze stolen USDT on Ethereum in 37 minutes, and Circle froze all $823 million in stolen USDC, helping lock up about 44% of the haul, according to blockchain analytics firm Elliptic.

North Korea’s launderers have since spread the remaining funds across about 12,000 wallets. It’s unclear whether any funds in the EU case have been frozen.

The incident adds to an already unprecedented week for crypto security. Beyond the record-breaking Binance hack, Brazilian exchange Mercado Bitcoin just today reported halting withdrawals after a $40 million hack of its own. Likewise today, BTCPay Server told merchants to switch off their servers amid reports of hot wallet attacks.
