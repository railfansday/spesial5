Why Helmerich & Payne Sank Today

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The oil and gas equipment vendor saw headwinds due to the war in Saudi Arabia.

By Billy Duberstein – Nov 19, 2026 at 4:04PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Helmerich & Payne ›

Shares of oil and gas equipment vendor Helmerich & Payne (HP -7.71%) fell 7.7% on Thursday.

HP delivered its fiscal fourth-quarter earnings report today. While adjusted earnings results beat expectations, the conflict in the Middle East led to a big impairment charge, with management noting subdued activity there that should continue through 2027.

In the third quarter, HP's revenue grew roughly 8% to $1.09 billion, while adjusted (non-GAAP) earnings per share rose to $0.36, up from a slight loss in the year-ago quarter. Both figures beat analyst expectations.

However, HP's GAAP bottom line fell into a large loss, as the company took a $610 million impairment charge on assets in the Middle East. It appears management deems some of its equipment in the region either damaged or unprofitable to maintain due to the war in Iran and the related conflict between the Houthis of Yemen and Saudi Arabia.

Going forward, management noted recent suspensions of conventional drilling activity in Saudi Arabia, and projected fewer active rigs in the country next quarter, with "the potential for further activity reductions over the course of the year."

Even amid geopolitical turmoil, the broader market was down today, possibly due to fears of further interest rate hikes. The Federal Reserve's minutes show officials remain concerned about inflation, which oil and gas product prices have driven to a large extent. Higher interest rates could slow growth or potentially cause a recession, which may hurt oil and gas demand going forward, even as the Middle East conflict threatens supply.

Ultimately, Helmerich & Payne’s stock looks cheap after today's decline, with roughly a $5 billion enterprise value and trailing adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization) of $913 million, implying a 5.5 multiple. However, that cheap valuation is due to the cyclical and unpredictable nature of global conventional energy investment, which is highly volatile, as this year has shown.
