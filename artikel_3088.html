AI server makers raise prices up to 45% after deals are signed, FT reports

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Trading firms such as Jane Street are paying the higher AI server prices as memory costs soar. XTX Markets has taken Dell to court over a $549m deal.

Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro are raising prices for Nvidia-powered AI servers months after buyers signed their contracts. Nikou Asgari reported the price rises for the Financial Times. The higher prices come when the equipment is ready to ship, according to people familiar with the matter.

“It is disconcerting when you get a 25, 30, 45 per cent [price increase],” one of the people told the FT.

GPU servers cost at least 20% to 30% more, one person said. CPU nodes, where memory makes up much of the cost, have tripled in price or more.

Large trading firms are among the buyers hit, including Jane Street, Optiver and Hudson River Trading, the FT reported. After the big cloud providers, they are some of the largest buyers of computing equipment, and they are building their own data centres for trading research. Jane Street has also signed with CoreWeave for cloud computing.

Most are agreeing to the new prices because they need the equipment. Contracts now carry an indicative price that can rise sharply by delivery, another person told the FT.

“We’ve never said no,” the person said. “At the end of the day, the compute is valuable to us and we’ll pay for it.”

Suppliers are largely passing on memory costs, the FT reported. Memory prices have risen more than 400% since the start of 2024, according to JPMorgan research cited by the paper. SK Hynix has said the memory shortage could last until 2030.

One fight has reached court. In June, British trading firm XTX Markets sued Dell in Ireland, according to court filings seen by the FT. The two had agreed a fixed price of $549m for servers, and XTX paid an extra $5m for price certainty.

Dell later told XTX it would have to pay at least $62m to $70m more, the filings say. Dell said it could not absorb the rising memory costs, according to XTX. The case is ongoing.

HPE and Supermicro declined to comment to the FT. Dell and Lenovo did not respond. Supermicro raised its revenue forecast by $4bn in October on AI demand.
