Walmart+ Members Spend 4 Times As Much As Non-Members. The Retailer’s New $120 Bundle Could Unlock More Growth.

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The perks just became sweet enough that one of its businesses could begin stealing a rival’s coveted customers.

By James Brumley – Nov 25, 2026 at 2:30PM EST

For most of the industry's existence, retailing was simply a matter of offering the right product at the right price in the right place at the right time. Shoppers would find your store, and gladly give you their money.

The internet has changed the increasingly competitive retailing landscape, however. People are empowered, with access to many choices. Simply opening a store's doors isn't enough. In this environment, the key is building an ecosystem that makes your stores the top-of-mind choice for meeting consumers' shopping needs.

Walmart's (WMT -0.28%) ecosystem just got a serious upgrade. Investors should take note, as it offers some much-needed growth potential.

In October, Walmart combined the benefits of Walmart+ (like free shipping) with membership in its club-based warehouse retailer Sam's Club. Subscriptions that individually would normally cost $98 and $60 per year, respectively, can now be combined for an annual cost of only $120.

The real opportunity with this offering, however, is a chance to poach some of the affluent paying members of Sam's Club's rival Costco Wholesale (COST -0.45%).

Walmart was already doing pretty well with this crowd, to be clear. With even higher-earning households feeling the pinch of lingering inflation, most of the company's market share gains since 2022 have come from America's more affluent households.

The proverbial prize here, however, is a portion of Costco's 84.1 million paid memberships. Costco's average member lives in a household with an average annual income of over $100,000. The typical Walmart customer's comparable income is just over half that amount.

Sam's Club alone isn't apt to encourage much defection. If Walmart can add value to its Sam's Club offering by adding the convenience of Walmart stores' wider and less-bulk inventory selection at an annual price in the ballpark of Costco's membership fees, it's got a chance of winning over at least some of these coveted higher-spending customers.

The average Costco member spends about $3,000 per year, by the way, versus roughly half that for Sam's Club members, and just a little more than that for the average Walmart store customer.

Most Costco members aren't apt to make such a switch, to be clear. However, it wouldn't take many defectors to make a noticeable difference in Walmart's top and bottom lines, now that paid access to both sets of perks is affordably accessible.
