‘I’m Entering My Third Bodybuilding Competition at 52. Here’s How I Build Muscle in Midlife.’

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

“Strength, resilience, and the courage to pursue goals don’t have an age limit.”

When I was 20 years old, I thought I’d reached the peak of my fitness journey: I became one of India’s first female bodybuilders, and was even acknowledged in India’s national and world records book. It was an incredible honor, and I took immense pride in the discipline, strength, and athleticism it took to get there. Little did I know, though, that some of my greatest fitness accomplishments would come decades later, in my 40s and 50s.

Growing up, I always loved playing sports recreationally, and that passion for movement and competition eventually led me to bodybuilding. But at 24, I got married and moved to the United States with my husband; shortly after, I gave birth to my daughter at 29. With all these big life changes, fitness fell by the wayside. My life was centered around giving to my family, and somewhere along the way, I stopped prioritizing myself. Over the next two years, I became chronically exhausted, lost muscle, gained weight, and didn’t feel like myself physically or mentally.

Then, when I was 33, I decided to make a change. I thought back to the discipline and structure I once had with fitness and realized how much I missed that part of my life. I wanted to get back to those habits and rebuild the strength I once worked so hard for, so, I began making time for fitness.

My husband started watching our daughter after work while I began doing full-body workouts using light weights, focusing on consistency rather than intensity. I already had a strong foundation in strength training from my bodybuilding days, so I created most of my own workouts. But I also reached out to my former coach in India, who helped program some of them.

At first, the workouts were hard, and I was sore every day. But mentally and emotionally, I felt amazing; for the first time in years, I was committed to doing something just for myself. Starting then, I promised myself that I would never stop prioritizing my health again.

Over the next year, I worked my way up to hour-long workouts and continued lifting heavier weights. As a result, I gained significant muscle and lost more than 20 pounds. But the physical changes were only part of the transformation—I had more energy during the day, slept better at night, and felt more confident and comfortable in my own skin. Every workout reminded me that I was getting stronger not just physically, but mentally and emotionally, too.

As the years went on, I kept growing muscle and losing weight by strength training four to five days a week. Still, there was always a voice in the back of my head telling me to try bodybuilding again. So, at 48, I entered another competition. I worked with a coach who helped me gradually push my limits by lifting heavier weights, adding more challenging strength-training exercises, and increasing the reps and sets of my workouts. I also followed a strict plant-based, protein-forward diet (I became vegan for ethical reasons when I was 38). So, I ate lots of tofu, tempeh, lentils, beans, fruits, vegetables, whole grains, nuts, and seeds. The work was demanding, but it was incredibly motivating to watch my body transform through discipline and consistency.

Stepping back on stage again was a deeply rewarding experience, and I felt so proud of the hard work and dedication it took to get there. Most importantly, it was so empowering to show my teenage daughter that strength, resilience, and the courage to pursue goals don’t have an age limit.

I took four years off of bodybuilding since life got busy and I wanted to give my body a break from competition prep. But about a year and a half ago, I decided I wanted to challenge myself again and see what I could accomplish in this new decade of my life. My training approach didn’t change too much since the last competition—I just kept building as much muscle as possible.

In April 2025, I began my “bulk” phase, a period focused on growing muscle by increasing my calorie intake and lifting heavy. I followed a structured workout plan with a coach five days a week through two lower-body days and three upper-body days. During each session, I usually did five to seven exercises, typically for four sets of six to eight reps with heavy weight for muscle growth. I used a mix of free weights and weight machines, and some of my staple moves were pull-ups, leg presses, leg extensions, squats, lunges, chest flies, and lat pulldowns. I also intentionally incorporated pauses at the top or bottom of certain movements to make each exercise more challenging.

Training for a bodybuilding competition also means entering a “cut” phase, which involves reducing body fat so muscle definition can show on stage. So, in January of this year, I reduced my calorie intake while continuing to strength train with heavy weights. After my strength-training sessions, I also did 45 to 50 minutes of cardio on the stair stepper or elliptical. Additionally, I finished every workout with a core circuit to improve muscle definition that includes moves like hanging leg raises, situps, and bicycle crunches.

Recovery has always been non-negotiable for my workouts, too. I lightly stretched after every session, and on my off days, spent at least an hour doing deeper stretching with resistance bands and a foam roller to maintain mobility and reduce my risk of injury.

I’ve learned that strength isn’t just about what happens in the gym. Every little habit works together as a system: how I train, eat, recover, sleep, and take care of myself mentally and emotionally. When those pieces are in balance, I have more energy, feel stronger, and perform better.

But instead of trying to change every one of my habits overnight, I took small, manageable steps, like dedicating just 20 minutes to myself at the gym. Once that became routine, I built on it by focusing more on nutrition and recovery, then eventually, I set my sights on returning to the bodybuilding stage. When I treat my body and mind as a whole, I’m able to gain strength sustainably, which allows me to keep doing what I love.

The simpler my workouts are, the more consistent I can stay with them. That’s why my coach and I work on foundational exercises like squats, lunges, presses, rows, and pull-ups. But to continue challenging my muscles in different ways, we use simple adjustments like tempo, pauses, and stance variations.

For example, while doing a squat, keeping my toes pointed forward targets my quads and center glutes. But turning my toes slightly outward helps me feel more activation in the outer glutes and inner thighs. Small adjustments like these allow me to keep progressing with simple and effective movements without constantly reinventing my routine.

For years, I was so focused on caring for my family that I stopped making time for myself. Getting back into strength training changed that. I realized that taking care of my health isn’t selfish—it’s necessary, and making that commitment has given me more strength, energy, and confidence in every part of my life. By scheduling my workouts like any other important commitment, planning ahead, and staying consistent even when life gets busy, I’ve found a way to prioritize myself while still being there for my family.

Today, strength training is about more than building muscle or preparing for a competition. It’s one of the ways I continue to invest in my health, challenge myself, and pursue goals that bring me joy. I’ve learned that you don’t have to be where you once were to start again. You just have to decide that you’re worth the time and effort, take the first step, and keep showing up.
