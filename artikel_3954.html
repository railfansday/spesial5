PlayStation defeats £2 billion UK lawsuit that alleged Sony forced consumers into "excessive and unfair prices"

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

12.2 million gamers can be wrong, apparently.

Sony has defeated a major £2 billion UK class action lawsuit concerning the price of games on the PlayStation Store (thanks, TheGameBusiness). The judgment came after a 30-day Competition Appeal Tribunal that took place in London, where PlayStation users claimed Sony had removed "all actual and potential competition" in relation to selling digital games on the console.

The case, brought by Alex Neill, who said she represented roughly 12.2 million gamers, accused Sony of applying "excessive and unfair" pricing on games that appeared on the storefront accessible by PS3, PS4 and PS5 consoles - the PlayStation Store. In a judgment published by the London’s Competition Appeal Tribunal today (17th November), the case was rejected. The judgement noted that market definitions used to define Sony's conduct were "unsafe", and that consumers "would be likely to make informed choices when purchasing consoles" and games on the hardware, too.

"Potential PlayStation console purchasers had both access to sufficient information and an incentive to consider that information in order to make informed purchasing decisions at the console level, especially in the case of high-value users who spend significant amounts in the digital gaming aftermarket," read the judgment.

The lawsuit made the argument that, upon purchasing a PS5 or other PlayStation console, you were then trapped within its ecosystem, and forced into paying the prices determined by Sony. The Tribunal disagreed, however, and ruled that Sony competed within a wider 'systems market' alongside Xbox, Nintendo and PC, for example. Furthermore, the Tribunal found no evidence that the existence of other digital storefronts would have actually brought any PlayStation Store pricing down.

Interestingly, the Tribunal used the argument that consumers can also opt and buy physical games instead of using the PlayStation Store - something that is changing as of the start of 2028. That did not seem to have any bearing on today's decision, though. It's also worth noting that this case did not seek to interrogate the ownership status of digital games - something that's been at the heart of the movement rallying against Sony's decision to kill off physical media. Earlier this year, spokespeople from Sony's legal team argued "reasonable consumers" already know they don't actually own their digital games.

The London Tribunal has a fairly long history. Back in 2022, it began as a case that attempted to sue Sony to the tune of £5bn for "ripping people off" on PlayStation Store. Sony argued the case brought against it in this trial was "flawed from start to finish". This isn't the only situation where we're seeing consumers pursue legal action against the platform holder. Last year, PlayStation was sued in the Netherlands over the so-called "Sony tax" that highlighted artificially inflated prices on the Store, and sought to represent more than 20,000 participants that felt short-changed by the company.
