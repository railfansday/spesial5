Mini Review: Shrek 2 Remastered (PS5) - Far Far Away Gets an All-Star Makeover

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

I believe, I believe, I believe this is a good time

Version Reviewed: PS5 (Standard) / European

While DreamWorks' tales of an ogre and his mates are hit or miss, there's no denying that Shrek 2 Remastered is a winning overhaul of a mid-2000s gem.

It comes from an era when movie tie-in games were all the rage, whether that meant half-arsed Harry Potter adventures or excellent efforts like X-Men Origins: Wolverine.

Shrek 2 is a thoroughly joyous time, adopting a Marvel Ultimate Alliance-style formula for its antics. Shrek, Fiona, and Donkey form the core team you'll swap between, with familiar faces like Lil Red and the Gingerbread Man joining the squad from time to time.

Each character packs special abilities, whether that's Donkey's 'Burro Blast' or Fiona's power to slow down time, Matrix-style. It's incredibly silly, and never commented on by our delightful cast. We wouldn't have it any other way.

Across a variety of locales from the titular movie, you'll squash, splatter, and swashbuckle through puzzles that take advantage of everyone's unique abilities.

Whether that's using Shrek's strength to hurl chickens into a pen for a fast food chain, or Donkey's agility to sneak up on a goblin that poops gems in a museum, there's plenty of variety.

'Hero Time' moments also spice things up, ranging from QTEs involving Fiona singing (badly) to birds, to Donkey riding Elizabeth the Dragon in a fiery chase.

There's more depth here than your usual movie game slop, too, with friendly leprechaun Crazy Larry selling stat increases and upgrades in exchange for your hard-earned coins. It scratches that familiar LEGO game itch of smashing objects, platforming, and exploring every nook and cranny for collectibles.

All of it looks and sounds the part, with a faithful recreation of DreamWorks' fantastical art direction and an appropriately over-the-top score.

Where Shrek 2 Remastered really comes together is its brand-new co-op multiplayer. Played locally or online, the latter requiring an Epic Games Account, you can team up with three other players. It's a neat inclusion for a game that's over two decades old.

There's also a basic but enjoyable Photo Mode, perfect for capturing some amusing shots of the gang.

Performance on base PS5 is generally stable, although frame drops occasionally rear their ugly heads during crowded skirmishes or when loading new areas. Paired with an occasionally awkward camera, these blemishes don't hurt the experience too much.

Shrek 2 Remastered doesn't quite polish away all the rough edges of its original release, but there's so much charm here that it's difficult to hold them against it.

With a varied adventure, plenty of laughs, and the welcome addition of online co-op, this is a lovely reminder of when movie tie-in games could be just as entertaining as the films that inspired them.
