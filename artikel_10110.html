Trump says he won't allow US companies to build datacenters abroad

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

President threatens 'tremendous tax' on overseas builds, but analysts question how restrictions would work

US President Donald Trump has threatened a "tremendous tax" on American companies building datacenters overseas, arguing that the infrastructure should stay at home to preserve the country's AI lead over China.

Speaking at a White House press conference after returning from the Asia-Pacific Economic Co-operation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen, Trump portrayed AI leadership as a contest America could not afford to lose.

Trump went on to say that "we're not going to allow our companies to build datacenters outside of our country," adding that "they allowed that to happen with chips and everything else and it was very destructive to our country."

The threat appeared to catch even his own administration unbriefed. "I haven't even said that to my people, but I'm telling them now," Trump said.

The Official White House Rapid Response account posted a video clip on X.

We asked the White House if the president intends to follow through, and we will let you know if we get an answer.

Trump said: "We're not going to allow companies to build datacenters in other countries. If they do that, they can, but we're going to put a tremendous tax on them. We want them to do it here."

The threat comes amid concerns that Chinese entities were able to use Nvidia's cutting-edge GPUs for training AI models by accessing cloud services hosted in third countries. The US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) issued new rules to try and crack down on this practice last week.

"We're not allowing datacenters to go into other countries, as China will take all of them, and we're not allowing that to happen," Trump repeated. "We're going to put tremendous penalties on companies that try and do it. They want to build that one in Finland, and we're not allowing that to happen," he added.

Google is expanding its facilities in Finland, including one recently in the news because the developer had reportedly illegally felled 250,000 trees to make way for it.

Such a policy seems unlikely to be allowed by Congress, where both houses will be under the control of the Democrats when the new term begins in January.

Amazon, Google, and Microsoft already operate global datacenter networks to serve customers in different regions. Restrictions on further overseas construction would complicate those businesses.

Omdia chief analyst Roy Illsley expressed doubt that this is a serious threat.

"There will be ways around that if he does impose some executive order, and the ability to build datacenters is not just an American skill, many countries can build them, it is the equipment in them that is mostly US," he told The Register.

"So I do not take this seriously as it would not work, there is no such thing as a US datacenter, they are made up of thousands of components from all over the globe. He could stop US capital from being invested in those countries, but that could have trade consequences."

Mike Kharchenko of European hosting firm HostZealot said there are good reasons why the cloud giants operate datacenters outside the US, such as latency and regulatory compliance.

"A buyer in Frankfurt won't wait for a page to load from Virginia. Some countries require certain data to stay inside their borders, and big European clients write it into contracts anyway," he stated.

Trump claimed that communities in America that host datacenters are "making so much money that they don't know what to do with it."

Those claimed benefits come alongside substantial public subsidies. Nonprofit organization Good Jobs First reported earlier this year that some states are losing billions in revenue because of the tax abatement schemes offered to companies to entice them to set up shop there. ®
