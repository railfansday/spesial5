Before &amp; After: This Couple Planned to Flip a 62-Year-Old Fixer-Upper, Then Made It Their Home Instead

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Designer Claire Thompson and her husband tackled the house together room by room.

Designer Claire Thompson has a theory about renovating a house alongside your significant other: If your relationship can survive that, it can probably survive anything. In 2021, she and her now-husband, Will, purchased a nearly 5,000-square-foot home in Jackson, Mississippi’s Eastover neighborhood, with plans to renovate the property and flip it for a profit. By 2022, they were engaged in the unfinished kitchen. Four years later, they’re still there, now with a baby daughter and a house that looks very little like the one they originally intended to sell.

Built in 1964, the five-bedroom property hadn’t been meaningfully upgraded in decades. The rooms were compartmentalized, the finishes were dated, and the quirky floor plan presented a few head-scratching challenges, like a second-floor dining room entirely removed from the kitchen below. But Thompson, who launched her design business in 2020, saw something worth holding onto. “I quickly fell in love with the bones of the home, the lot the home is positioned on, and of course the neighborhood and the street itself,” she recalls.

The decision to stay rather than sell came with a change in approach. Rather than gutting the house and putting in finishes they knew would grab buyers in a post-COVID market, the couple worked within the home’s existing footprint, opening up walls to encourage better circulation while preserving the individuality of each room. The kitchen, once closed off at either end, now connects to a light-filled breakfast nook and an inviting keeping room, where damaged parquet floors and dated ceiling beams gave way to white oak underfoot and softly textured plaster overhead.

Nearby, a former study became an inky, book-lined library, complete with an expanded bar. Even the upstairs dining room survived, despite its logistical inconveniences. “We decided to keep it because of the way it flows when we entertain and host showers or parties,” Thompson says. “I think a home that tells a story, like a nod to the past or an interesting quirk, only makes the home more intriguing.”

The renovation unfolded after hours, with Will building cabinetry and tackling electrical work late into the night. Some spaces were even renovated twice: first, with resale in mind, then with the more personal considerations of permanence. The home became a testing ground for Thompson’s design practice, but it also serves as something of a family scrapbook. “We grew together as individuals, as a couple, as business owners, and as creatives,” she says. “The home feels like us.”

The kitchen layout didn’t change too much, but Thompson was able to completely upgrade its feel and functionality by opening up both ends—one to the breakfast nook, one to the keeping room—and adding a center island.

“I really did not want upper cabinets in the kitchen, so we were very thoughtful in the functionality of the cabinetry,” she adds. “My husband loves to remind me that he built me 42 drawers for the kitchen. We have ample storage, while still making it aesthetically the way I envisioned.”

Now open to the kitchen, the breakfast nook acts as a natural overflow spot where the family eats most of their meals, casual dinners included. Removing the ceiling beams allowed Thompson to capture the feel of taller ceilings; while she wanted to keep the brick floors, opening up the walls meant she had to opt for new white oak for continuity.

“We call this the keeping room since we opened it up to the kitchen,” explains Thompson. “This is the room we really live in day to day; it’s a laid-back and comfortable space.”

The pair started by plastering just the fireplace, but then decided to extend the treatment throughout the rest of the room—it’s since become a signature finish for Thompson’s firm. “We now use this technique in almost every project,” she adds.

Previously a study, the library was born of equal parts design instincts and necessity. “My husband has so many books that we decided to wrap the room with bookshelves,” explains Thompson, who also reworked a hallway to add a bar and powder bathroom. The whole space is drenched in a Roman clay finish (Black Star by Portola Paints), creating an enveloping retreat that has become a go-to for family movie nights.

Also known as the family’s “Christmas tree room,” the formal living room is a soothing symphony of neutral hues and nubby textures. “The coffee table is one of my favorite purchases of all time,” Thompson adds. “I bought it to sell to a client but decided I couldn’t part with it! I also love to have my grandmother’s antique stool in here—it always makes me think of her and brings me joy.”

Perfectly balanced between sweet and sophisticated, 12-week-old daughter Margot’s bedroom was an opportunity for Thompson to lean into all things childhood magic. “She may never leave the bassinet in our primary bedroom, but she does have a cute room waiting for her if that ever happens!” she adds.

Connected to the nursery through a Jack-and-Jill bathroom, this guest space could eventually become a bedroom for a second child. For now, though, it’s a comfortable spot for visiting guests, and a favorite hideout of Thompson’s “first baby,” pup Lemmon.

Claire Thompson is the owner of Claire Thompson Design, a full-service firm specializing in designing, renovating, and furnishing beautiful, timeless homes. She works closely with clients to ensure their unique personalities and desires are perfectly captured by the design and that all their wishes and needs are met.
