Designers Say This ’70s-Inspired Design Trend Is Making a Glamorous Comeback

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Get ready to see this glossy finish everywhere again.

We all know design is reflective. Whether it’s in our personal style, architectural splendor, or cultural ties, great design mirrors the world around us. Now, it’s time for it to mirror itself.

Once dismissed as a tacky, ’70s-era trend (not unlike wood paneling), mirrored surfaces are making a comeback. But don’t worry—today’s mirror craze moves past the floor-to-ceiling panels of the past, opting instead for accents that add light, depth, and dimension.

“I love mirrored surfaces in interiors because they can be so transformative & work in all styles. They can add glitz and glamour, especially when you add beveled details,” says Bridget Tiek of TIEK BYDAY. “I love to see mirrors as a backbar, behind shelves, on doors, ceilings and on walls. Basically everywhere!”

Bolder than traditional ready-to-hang mirrors, mirrored surfaces are often hard to place tastefully. Here’s how designers have brought the striking detail into interiors.

Queer Eye for the Straight Guy star Carson Kressley’s Murray Hill apartment is full of personality, and his sunken living room has several glittering surfaces. The designer embraces mirrored shine as a bold accent, as seen in this disco-ball-tiled end table.

Designer Alexander D. Wilson brought a mirrored backsplash into this Upper East Side apartment to elevate the elegant yet fun palette while creating the illusion of a larger space. NYC apartments often feel like a shoebox. With this classic design trick, they don’t have to look like it.

Interior designer Sarah Vaile brought Manhattan to the Windy City in this punchy coastal home. After coating the walls in Farrow & Ball’s Borrowed Light for a classic Nancy Meyers-esque aesthetic, she framed a nearly ceiling-high mirror in crisp white molding, transforming the panel into a bold decorative piece that amplifies space and cosmopolitan chic.

Bold enough to make a fierce statement in even the smallest piece, interior designer Amy Courtney brought industrial flair to her New York City home with a silvery domed lamp to complement the bold hues of her living room’s palette.

While the glassy, expansive nature of mirrored panels certainly has its charms, if you’re hoping to bring both glossy and textured flair to your design, mirrored tiles are the perfect solution. Designer Shannon Ggem used mirrored tiles in this moody bar design to bring more light to the room without sacrificing the deep-hued palette.

While most rely on millwork to bring style to the walls, designer Michelle Prentice opted for mirror panels on the closet doors of this South Carolina bedroom. Not only do they add depth to the design, but they bring a boutique feel while adding to the visual palette and playing off the centered vanity mirror to unify the wall treatments.

As stainless steel fades from an industry standard to a passing fad, designers are getting creative when it comes to finding chic alternatives. In this monochrome green butler’s pantry, designer Bridget Tiek brought in a glassy paneled door to elevate the appliance design, taking the invisible kitchen trend to new heights.
