Jane Fonda on Documentary ‘Gaslit,’ Trump, and What the Democrats’ Midterm Victory Means for Climate Change

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

In “Gaslit,” a new documentary about climate change activists, Jane Fonda stands outside one of Formosa Petrochemical’s Texas facilities and looks through a thermal camera. The Oscar-winning actress is brought to tears by what she sees: a tremendous expulsion of methane gas into the air.

“Methane is 80 percent more destructive to the climate, in terms of global warming, than carbon dioxide,” Fonda explained during a recent Zoom interview. “It’s the most destructive chemical that we’re using, and it’s extremely dangerous for people. And yet here it is billowing into the air, and nobody even knows it.”

For years, Fonda has been sounding the alarm about the looming environmental catastrophe that will take place if the world doesn’t wean itself off fossil fuels. She launched a series of climate change protests dubbed “Fire Drill Fridays” in 2019 — a reference to activist Greta Thunberg’s charge that people should “act as if your house is on fire” — and has been arrested multiple times for civil disobedience. Fonda is embracing the cause with the same passion that has led her to speak out on everything from the Vietnam War to feminism to LGBTQ+ rights. But she worries her efforts may not be enough. “Our emission of global warming chemicals has gone way up since Donald Trump has become president,” Fonda said. “And of course the concern is always: Have we passed too many tipping points or a tipping point? Many climate scientists say if we act fast now, it won’t be too late. But it’s very hard to act fast when Trump is president.”

The political winds are changing. The midterms were a blue wave election that saw Democrats take back the House and the Senate in commanding fashion. They not only performed well in liberal bastions, but also captured the governor’s mansion in Texas and elected James Talarico to a U.S. Senate seat that has been in Republican hands for decades. Much of “Gaslit” focuses on the environmental damage wrought by the oil fields in West Texas’ Permian Basin. Will the change in leadership make a difference?

“It won’t hurt,” Fonda said. “I don’t know how strong Talarico is on these issues. We can’t do too much until our members are seated, and we actually have the majority in the House in January.”

“Renewables are ready,” she added. “They’re cheap. They’re safe. They’re job-intensive. It’s the perfect solution. It’s right there. It makes all the sense in the world. The only thing we lack is political will, and hopefully, with this swath of new people that have been elected to the House and the Senate, we will have the political will when we take power to turn this around.”

Fonda believes average citizens concerned about climate change can’t afford to be passive.

“One of the most important things that people can do is to demand that their elected officials pass legislation that makes these polluters pay for what they’re doing,” Fonda said. “Right now, it’s consumers, it’s us taxpayers that have to pay for the damage. They should pay, and we have to stop the $21 billion annual subsidies that we pay them. They make trillions of dollars of profit. If we cancel those subsidies, it would spell the beginning of the end for fossil fuels.”

Fonda is blunt in her warnings that the chaos and damage that global warming has caused will only accelerate unless the world switches to renewable sources of energy. Her assessment is sober, even terrifying, but one thing that gives her hope is the work that the activists highlighted in “Gaslit” are doing. “Gaslit” was financed and produced by the environmental group Greenpeace USA, and it focuses on people in the Gulf Coast and Texas who are trying to change things across the political spectrum. They range from a Republican cattle rancher, who has seen what the oil and gas business has done to his family’s land, to cancer survivors and community organizers who are hoping to change hearts and minds about fossil fuels.

“What is so moving to me about the film is we’re showing these people who do not give up; they will not,” Fonda said. “All of these activists are so brave and so incredible. They inspire me.”

“Gaslit” was directed by Katie Camosy. It is now available to rent or buy on Amazon Prime and Documentary+. As of November 19, the film can be streamed on the Greenpeace USA YouTube channel, with additional platforms rolling out.
