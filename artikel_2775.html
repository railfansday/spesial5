The Sixers’ performance in crunch-time games is Good, Actually

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

At first, the Sixers appeared to be headed toward a blowout loss against the Orlando Magic on Wednesday. They were without all three of Tyrese Maxey, Jaylen Brown and Dean Wade (along with Dominick Barlow and Adem Bona), and they fell into a 27-12 hole less than eight minutes into the game. Instead, the Sixers proceeded to outscore the Magic by 20 points over the remaining 40 minutes, sneaking out of Orlando with a 121-116 win.

That was their 11th clutch win of the season, which the NBA defines as a game that gets within a five-point margin with no more than five minutes remaining in regulation. The Sixers are now 11-0 in such games this season, which is by far the best record in the league. In fact, that record in clutch games puts them in rarefied air, as Mike Lynch of Sports Reference shared after Wednesday's win.

Granted, that clearly unsustainable crunch-time record is also fuel for skepticism about how good the Sixers actually are. It's safe to say that their net rating of plus-46.1 in those situations probably won't hold up for an entire season, particularly given that their overall net rating is only plus-2.7.

None of this is breaking news or hard-hitting journalism. It's stating the obvious. The Sixers are running unsustainably hot in clutch situations and will eventually lose one of these games. However, anyone who's using that record to downplay what they've done thus far this season is missing two crucial points.

The new-look Sixers are still developing chemistry with one another. They're figuring out how best to optimize their schemes both offensively and defensively. That very much remains a work in progress over a full 48-minute game, but the fact that they've been able to lock in at the end of games bodes well. That speaks to an attention to detail that Sixers teams in the past have lacked.

Granted, it helps when you have LeBron James orchestrating your late-game offense. But that's also the point. The "who takes the last shot?" questions from the offseason haven't proved to be a concern at all yet. The answer, unsurprisingly, is that the Sixers don't have a designated last-shot taker. All five guys in their starting lineups are legitimate offensive threats, so they've been adept at hunting mismatches and attacking their opponents' weak spots. That is playoff basketball in a nutshell.

Would it be ideal if the Sixers started blowing teams out so they weren't so reliant on crunch time? Of course. Buying their starters extra rest throughout the grind of the 82-game season would be a godsend. However, there's also value in getting this much crunch-time experience in the regular season, particularly for a team that's working to incorporate so many new pieces on the fly.

"You've gotta go through shit," LeBron said in the season premiere of his Mind the Game podcast this offseason. "… Those are some of the moments that we have to be able to go through some real, live battle moments, in the fight, in order to know really who we are and what we want to become. We've gotta peel the onion. We have to."

Being in these close-and-late situations will help the Sixers develop muscle memory that they can tap into later in the season and in the playoffs. They can see which plays worked and didn't work in those gotta-have-it moments. The best teams use the regular season as a lab to experiment so they have as many answers to the test as possible before the playoffs begin.

There's also a psychological component to their crunch-time performance that could pay dividends down the line. The more close wins they rack up, the more belief they'll have in one another no matter the circumstances. They'll begin to believe that no lead is insurmountable and that they can overcome any obstacle thrown in their way.

In last year's playoffs, the New York Knicks overcame double-digit deficits in every Finals game. They were 6-2 in playoff games where they went down by 10 or more points, which was the best playoff record for any team in the 30 years of play-by-play data, according to NBA.com's John Schuhmann. That didn't just suddenly begin in the playoffs, though.

"What you have to respect about the Knicks, you have to put them away every single possession," Miami Heat head coach Erik Spoelstra told reporters after the Knicks overcame a double-digit deficit in the first half of a game last December. "You can't leave anything to effing chance with that team ever. That's what they do. What they have is a will to win, all right."

LeBron and the Sixers have spent all season discussing the need for them to develop championship habits. Their early-season level of crunch-time performance might not be sustainable, but the way that they're playing in those moments matters just as much. Valuing every possession on offense and locking in defensively are non-negotiables for them to accomplish their goal of winning a championship this year.

The added bonus is that by getting off to such a hot start, they've reduced the temperature under Nick Nurse's seat for the time being. Had they stumbled out of the gate like most early-LeBron teams tend to do, Stephen A. Smith and Kendrick Perkins would likely be all over ESPN's airwaves calling for his head.

The Sixers are by no means a finished product. They'd be the first ones to admit that. But learning to win in crunch time despite that bodes well for their long-term outlook, even if it means their actual record is misaligned with their expected record for the time being. (Don't worry, Cleveland, you can still be the reigning NBA Analytics Champions.)
