Tyler Shough Given Solution for Sophomore Slump in 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The New Orleans Saints (3-7) are looking for answers as an otherwise good team continues to find itself on the losing end of several close games. Once again, the Saints fell within one score of their opponent in a tough loss to the Chicago Bears, 21-13.

New Orleans (307) had four less yards than its opponent Chicago (311). It also had just one less first down and the same number of turnovers (2). The difference was the Bears got the football in the end zone three times while the Saints only reached the end zone once.

That problem points many toward the quarterback. With that, many are wondering what is going wrong with Saints starter Tyler Shough this season.

Athlon Sports' Neal Coolong diagnosed part of Shough's problem. In his diagnosis, there might be an inherent solution. Here's some of what Coolong shared on Shough's perceived regression.

"New Orleans Saints fans spent last winter believing they had found their quarterback in the second round. Tyler Shough went 5-4 as a rookie starter, and the Saints committed to him as their 2026 starter. Ten games into Year 2, with the Saints at 3-7 and slotted at No. 8 in the 2027 NFL draft order, that answer feels less settled.

The case for Shough is still there. He has 16 touchdown passes against eight interceptions and leads the team in rushing, according to ESPN's team stats. He has also been sacked 29 times, and his 71.6 PFF grade ranks 20th among 35 qualified quarterbacks.

The concern is how he gets there. PFF charges him with 10 turnover-worthy plays against 16 big-time throws, and 18.5 percent of his pressures have turned into sacks, 23rd in the league. Fans have spent the month asking whether Shough is regressing in Year 2, and his reluctance to push the ball downfield is a big part of why."

So, Shough isn't pushing the ball downfield or getting it into the end zone. And really, there are only two ways to score. You can sustain long drives or get an explosive play.

For the second week in a row, the Saints had a receiver go for at least nine receptions and less than 12.0 yards per reception. It's not great. It does point to where the Saints can improve.

Shough is having a trouble punching it into the end zone when the Saints reach the red zone. So, the cure might be that New Orleans schemes up explosive plays to avoid having to be perfect driving the length of the field. We'll see if they can make that adjustment.
