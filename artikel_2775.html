5 Things I Think I Think About the Miami Dolphins After Week 11

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Stop me if you've heard this one before: Despite a herculean defensive effort, the Miami Dolphins lost another game due to mental mistakes and what is statistically the worst offense in all of football in points scored. I love the effort and the budding stars on an underappreciated defense, but man, this offense is hard to watch. Here are five things I think I think about the Dolphins' loss in Buffalo.

So sure, allowing 26 points and more than 400 total yards doesn't sound especially impressive. But you do a little digging, and Miami's defense turned in another quality performance.

First, the Dolphins intercepted Josh Allen twice, sacked him three times and limited the future Hall of Famer to a passer rating under 70. That's a solid defensive performance. Buffalo's rushing attack was held under 100 yards on 27 carries (3.5 yards per carry). They also forced the Bills to settle for field goals rather than touchdowns on multiple red-zone trips. And this is all without Zack Sieler, Jordan Phillips and Zeek Biggers.

And then there's this: Since Oct. 4, Buffalo has scored more than 30 points against every team except two:

The Minnesota Vikings, whose defense this season is ridiculously good

In fact, the Dolphins have given up 30+ points only twice all year, and that was against two of the NFL's elite offenses in the 49ers and Bengals.

Sure, you could argue that the Dolphins' lackluster offense is allowing teams to ease up on the gas, and I wouldn't disagree with you. Still, for a defense missing key contributors and facing one of the league's top offenses, that's an accomplishment worth recognizing. If Miami had received even modest offensive support, this game could have looked very different.

We all know Kyle McCord shouldn't be starting NFL games at this point in his young career. McCord completed just 12 of 33 passes on Sunday for 102 yards, plus an interception. Several throws sailed high, others were late, and the offense never established any rhythm through the air.

That said, the Dolphins finished with just 12 receptions on 33 targets. Twelve! Caleb Douglas caught only two of his 10 targets while dropping multiple catchable passes, including a potential red-zone scoring opportunity. (The game was probably out of reach by that point, but his drop against the Lions essentially ended any hopes of a comeback then too.) It's worth mentioning there's a solution for this:

By comparison, the Bills caught 28 of their 44 targets and consistently converted opportunities when the ball arrived in catchable locations.

Miami's passing offense wasn't sabotaged by one player – it was a collective failure.

After an explosive couple of games upon replacing De'Von Achane in the backfield, Gordon's production lately has been a little more … pedestrian. 20 carries for 76 yards against the Colts. 16 for 55 against the Lions.

What often gets overlooked is the environment he's operating in.

Opposing defenses have spent weeks loading the box, daring Miami's struggling passing attack to beat them through the air. According to Kyle Crabbs of Locked On Dolphins, Gordon has consistently faced one of the league's highest rates of eight-man boxes since the bye week. Yet despite that reality, Gordon turned 11 carries into 69 yards against Buffalo, averaging 6.3 yards per attempt and ripping off a 24-yard run.

Maybe not a dominant performance, but it was another reminder that Gordon can still be productive despite facing unfavorable circumstances.

Do you remember early in the season, when Austin Jackson was getting lit up like a Christmas tree? At the time, we said, "Sure, but he's facing Pro Bowlers Maxx Crosby and Nick Bosa, let's wait and see how he does against less accomplished defensive linemen."

Against Buffalo edge rusher Greg Rousseau, who currently has [checks notes] zero Pro Bowl nods, Jackson had a PFF grade of 34.6. (For context, a grade of 60 is considered average, a 50 is below average and a 40 is bad.) That's not only the lowest of all the offensive linemen, or even anyone on the offense – it's the lowest of anyone on the team on Sunday not named "Herring." Rousseau finished with two sacks and constantly disrupted the pocket.

Jackson hasn't been this bad all season, but even at his best, he hasn't been "good."

I've been reluctant to move Proctor out of left guard, mostly because he's been so good where he is and I still have flashbacks of what poor guard play looks like from Jonah last season. But man. We need to do something.

When a team is 1-9, it's easy to start looking ahead at next season's draft and dreaming of Jeremiah Smith rocking the aqua and orange. If you want Smith, you'll probably need the No. 1 spot overall.

Currently, the Dolphins are third in the draft order, tied by record with the Titans and Chargers but losing out on strength of schedule. (Per the NFL, the team that played the schedule with the lowest winning percentage will be awarded the higher pick — pretty much, losing to bad teams is worse than losing to good teams, and the Dolphins have faced a lot of good teams.)Can the Dolphins sneak ahead of those other two teams? Maybe:

Five of the Titans' losses have been within a touchdown, and they still have another game against the Colts coming up. With a home game against the imploding Steelers and a matchup against the Raiders still coming up, I think they can finish with 3-4 wins.

The Chargers will be tougher. I think they can beat the Dolphins Christmas weekend. Can the Chargers beat the Drake Maye-less Patriots at home this weekend? That's the key – the rest of their schedule looks like a bunch of Ls to me.

If we can get the Titans and Patriots to 3 wins, then we can afford to beat the Jets this weekend and still land the No. 1 pick in the draft. Sweeping the Jets in a 2-15 season would be rewarding in its own way.

That's my Christmas wish. Let's make it happen!

In the meantime, happy Leon Lett Day everyone!
