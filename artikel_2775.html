The 49ers are proving doubters wrong about their Kaelon Black draft selection

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The San Francisco 49ers made a couple of bold swings in the 2026 NFL Draft, taking wide receiver De'Zhaun Stribling and running back Kaelon Black well above consensus this offseason.

While the Stribling pick caught a lot of initial flak, the Black pick drew the ire of national media as well, as he was the third running back taken in the draft, with Kyle Shanahan coming out and even saying that the Indiana product was the No. 2 back on their board.

The 49ers have had their fair share of misses in the draft at running back under Shanahan and John Lynch. Their first pick at the position back in 2017 was Joe Williams, who evidently didn't pan out. From there, they've taken Trey Sermon, Elijah Mitchell, Ty Davis-Price, Isaac Guerendo, and Jordan James before taking Black this offseason.

Williams, Sermon, Davis-Price, and Guerendo all failed to pan out and were third or fourth-round picks, which added a stain to the regime's draft history at the position. But Black has been an immediate success, surpassing my expectations from the draft, and the 49ers have shown a clear trust in the rookie from Day 1.

Black has gone for 356 yards and two touchdowns on 79 carries, providing a good complement to Christian McCaffrey as a hard-nosed, rugged runner. His 4.5 yards per carry mark ranks 12th in the NFL, while he's averaged nearly 10 carries per game.

Black has really come along late. He went for 63 yards and a touchdown on 5.3 yards per carry against Atlanta (although he did fumble in that game). He followed that with 70 yards on 11 carries last weekend against Las Vegas. And he just had 53 yards and a touchdown on 4.8 yards per carry against Dallas this weekend.

San Francisco came into the year saying it wanted to keep Christian McCaffrey fresh throughout the season. It's followed through with that sentiment, as McCaffrey is averaging 12.8 carries per game so far. A year ago, he averaged 18.6 carries per game through nine games. And that's not including the high receiving volume he's had in both seasons. Last season, the 49ers star paced the NFL in touches by over 40. This year, he's 17th, having nearly 90 touches less than the NFL's leader Jahmyr Gibbs.

The freshness has also led to a far more efficient backfield. The 49ers are fourth in EPA per rush this season and fourth in rushing success rate. A part of that is the offensive line play and a part of that is the ability to rotate backs and have that trust throughout games.

Speaking on the backfield split, head coach Kyle Shanahan pointed out how valuable McCaffrey has been as a mentor to Black this season, which has led to a successful pairing.

"I think Christian's the perfect mentor just because even if he doesn't say anything, just how Christian works every second, Christian's the most detailed, focused, obsessive player I've ever been around," Shanahan said on Monday. "Just watch how he blocks when [WR] Deebo's [Samuel Sr.] running back and he's playing receiver and just the technique and the effort he goes through on every single play. Just the way Christian plays football is the best example or mentor to anybody who's on our team or anybody who wants to watch how a football player goes about his business.

"I also think Christian really respects Kaelon. I remember Christian telling me right when he got here how much of a pro that guy seemed like and seemed like he was made of the right stuff. It's because K.B., similar to Christian, he works at everything. He's very hard on himself. He's very tough and approaches each day to get better. As talents and as people, I think they pair together very well."

The two styles are quite different. McCaffrey is the elusive back who obviously flourishes as a receiver, while Black has not been afraid to exert his physicality, which came out several times against Dallas.

"Oh, it was awesome," Shanahan said of Black's physicality. "The one I got to see live was running over the safety, and the safety brought it too. I thought that was a good play by the safety, but K.B. brought it more. Then to finish it on the corner was such an impressive run. I didn't get to see his chips on the D-Lineman live.

"I saw Quinnen Williams go out in the game and they told me that was because K.B. hit him and I was trying to figure out how he did because it was on a bootleg. Then he did it another time on another player going through the B-gap, so just the physicality he looks for and then the way his body's wired that when you have that mentality and you're wired that way, obviously when he hits them it feels a little bit different."

The 49ers have been willing to lean on McCaffrey when they need to, giving him 15+ carries four times this season. But he hasn't exceeded the 20-carry mark, which has allowed him to maintain a force as a receiver, where he leads all running backs in receiving yards. We saw that play out on Sunday, as McCaffrey had 13 carries to Black's 11, but added four catches for 161 yards and two touchdowns in the air.

San Francisco's offense has continued to find new heights, and a big part of that has been Black's readiness and ability to complement McCaffrey in the backfield. Thus far, they've proved the doubters wrong with a bold draft pick who has made a huge impact as a rookie so far.
