The AI Trade Just Cracked. Here’s the Price at Which I’d Start Buying Nvidia.

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Nvidia stock is plummeting amid a flurry of scandals involving the company's co-founder and former CEO, Jensen Huang.

When OpenAI commercially launched ChatGPT in November 2022, few investors anticipated how dramatically artificial intelligence (AI) would reshape the stock market. Perhaps no company benefited more from this than Nvidia (NVDA -4.34%).

Between late 2022 and year-end 2025, Nvidia's stock price increased roughly tenfold -- transforming the semiconductor designer into one of the world's most valuable companies. The rally reflected insatiable demand for its graphics processing units (GPUs), which became critical infrastructure for training and deploying generative AI models.

However, 2026 has presented an entirely different story. After notching a record high of roughly $243 per share in October, Nvidia has experienced a seismic sell-off. On Nov. 12, Nvidia stock briefly fell below $150 following the resignation of CEO Jensen Huang.

The question now is whether Nvidia's scandals signal the beginning of a broader collapse in AI stocks. While I think investors should prepare for additional volatility, I also see the sell-off as an attractive long-term buying opportunity.

Nvidia's evolution from a gaming GPU manufacturer into an AI infrastructure leader is historic. The company's chips became the preferred hardware for training generative AI models, while its CUDA software platform established a powerful competitive advantage by keeping developers locked into its ecosystem.

As demand accelerated, Nvidia introduced successive generations of computing architectures, including Hopper, Blackwell, and Vera Rubin. This rapid pace of innovation helped drive continued spending commitments from hyperscalers like Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta Platforms, and Oracle.

Nvidia wasn't the only beneficiary of rising demand for AI chips. Broadcom and Advanced Micro Devices have also attracted investors seeking additional exposure to AI semiconductor stocks. Furthermore, cloud computing providers have benefited from the demand for AI infrastructure and services, while enterprise software companies like Palantir Technologies have become well positioned to capitalize on sovereign AI adoption.

Beyond the technology industry, utilities, energy producers, and electrical equipment manufacturers also attract attention as data centers require increasingly large amounts of power. In other words, Nvidia ignited an investment theme extending across multiple industries. That interconnectedness is precisely why the recent sell-off matters.

Over the last few days, scandals involving Huang and his personal relationships have intensified. On Nov. 12, Nvidia announced that Huang resigned as CEO and that CFO Colette Kress would assume the chief executive role in the interim.

The development undoubtedly introduces uncertainty about the company's strategy, customer relationships, and future execution. However, Nvidia was already facing pressure.

Investors have been questioning whether hyperscalers can generate adequate returns on their AI infrastructure investments. Meanwhile, the midterm elections also introduced uncertainty surrounding potential regulation of data center construction and electricity costs.

These concerns could have implications beyond Nvidia, considering the "Magnificent Seven" account for nearly one-third of the S&P 500's (^GSPC -0.35%) total market capitalization. If investors begin to reassess AI-related growth assumptions across big tech, the resulting selling pressure could drag major indexes lower.

Despite the current narrative surrounding Nvidia, its valuation has become interesting. As of Nov. 12, the company's forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio hovered around 11 -- a steep discount to its historical valuation.

Nvidia is substantially larger and more profitable today than it was during earlier phases of the AI revolution. Huang's departure poses a legitimate succession risk, while decelerating hyperscaler spending could erode future earnings.

However, I would become particularly interested in Nvidia around $155 to $160 per share. Based on Wall Street's fiscal 2028 earnings per share (EPS) estimate of $15.91, this price range implies a valuation of 9.7-10.1 times earnings.

Ultimately, smart investors can distinguish between deteriorating business fundamentals and changing market sentiment. While the AI trade will likely experience short-term turbulence, Nvidia's long-term investment thesis remains compelling for now. If Nvidia continues compounding its earnings growth, today's uncertainty could wind up looking like an attractive opportunity to buy the dip for patient investors.
