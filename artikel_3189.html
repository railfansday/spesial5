Taiwan widens its short-selling ban after the Taiex’s worst week since 1991

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The island’s NT$500bn stabilisation fund says it has been buying shares since 6 November as AI stocks slump.

Taiwan’s financial regulator has banned all short selling of local stocks below the previous day’s closing price. The Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) announced the move on Saturday, Bloomberg reported. It takes effect on 23 November.

Short selling is a bet that a share price will fall. The ban covers both margin short sales and sales of borrowed shares. Securities and futures firms that short stocks to hedge their business are exempt.

The FSC also raised the minimum margin for short sales to 150% from 130%. In addition, it stopped taking applications from brokers to list new put warrants, which also pay out when prices fall.

It is the third round of short-selling curbs this month. On 9 November, the FSC cut the cap on intraday short sales of borrowed shares from 30% to 3% of average daily volume, Crowdfund Insider reported.

A second round on 19 November banned shorting below the previous close for stocks that fell at least 3.5% the day before, according to Focus Taiwan.

Neither round stopped the slide. The Taiex fell 6.2% on Friday and lost almost 18% over the week, its worst week since 1991, according to Bloomberg. Doubts about whether AI spending will pay off have hit tech shares across Asia.

Meanwhile, Taiwan’s NT$500bn ($15bn) National Financial Stabilization Fund said it has been buying shares since 6 November. The fund said worries about tighter US regulation of AI, returns on big capital spending and margin-financing risks had triggered the sell-off.

Taiwan’s finance ministry said the economy remains strong. It grew 14.4% in the third quarter, and October export orders rose 78% from a year earlier, Bloomberg reported.
