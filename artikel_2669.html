Week 11 Chicago Bears power rankings roundup

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Chicago Bears are back in action following the bye week. It was great for the Bears to pause and have a chance to get healthy, but now is where the stakes are really going to go up.

The schedule is full of playoff-hopeful teams, and that includes four more games against the NFC North gauntlet.

Much is being made of Chicago's Super Bowl chances after their 7-2 start. Now they actually have to go out and prove it.

Let's see where the Bears rank heading into their final eight games.

Second-Half Outlook: Survive a brutal schedule down the stretch and hope that Jeffery Simmons continues to be a difference-maker

Once dubbed a prime regression candidate heading into the regular season, the Bears have survived without Caleb Williams for three games and somehow continue to play much better defense than the numbers suggest. Williams' Year 3 jump has been notable, even with a four-interception game in Week 9. The talent on offense is evident, and their ceiling feels safe with Ben Johnson calling plays. How far they go this season will come down to the defense, especially the secondary. We'll see if not making another move at the deadline comes back to bite them.

Bears (No. 5; 7-2): They can't afford to overlook the Saints, four days before a showdown with the Lions.

The Bears didn't do anything wrong on their bye week. They didn't "move down." The Bengals just moved up after one of the most impressive games of the season.

Lingering for the Bears is their home defeat to the Vikings in Week 2. They lost 9-3, and based on how the Vikings looked with Kirk Cousins in Week 10, that could be a problem. The Bears play at Minnesota in Week 18 and that's looking like an early candidate to be flexed to the final Sunday night slot.

Chicago is 0-2 in the division (Minnesota is 3-1) and that could also be bad for tiebreakers. The Lions suffered a massive injury to Aidan Hutchinson on Sunday and the Packers lost ground with a bad loss, so the NFC North could evolve into a two-team race. And the Bears might need to win in Minneapolis in Week 18 to take the division.

6. Chicago Bears (4) Having spent last week on a bye, the Bears didn't do anything wrong — they just got caught up in a Power Rankings shakeup, with a couple of impressive teams leap-frogging them. Last time out, Chicago earned a narrow win over Tampa Bay that exposed some weaknesses but also felt like an anomaly in the larger picture. I still have plenty of confidence in this team, but it has to navigate the incredibly competitive NFC North to fulfill its potential. The Bears need to come out of the break on the right foot, facing a three-week stretch that includes dates with Detroit and Jacksonville.

3. Chicago Bears (3): An offense that runs the ball more than any other in the league is about to get RB Kyle Monangai back − which is akin to making a trade deadline acquisition for free.

Non-QB MVP at midseason: RB D'Andre Swift

The Bears have maintained a top-five rushing offense (142 yards per game) despite a constant wave of injuries in the backfield. A big reason for that is the load Swift has carried in Kyle Monangai's five-game absence (toe/thumb). Swift has logged the third-most offensive snaps of any Bears skill player (63%), including 22 rushes against the Patriots and 27 at Seattle a week later. He ran in three second-half TDs against the Seahawks, has already reached a career high of 10 TDs and is five away from setting the franchise's single-season record. Despite dealing with injuries of his own, Swift has not missed a game. — Courtney Cronin
