Texas football film study: How Arch Manning's legs saved Longhorns at LSU

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Arch Manning's right arm dug Texas football into a hole in Saturday's comeback win at LSU. His legs kept the Longhorns alive in the game's most important moments.

The senior quarterback is college football's premier wild stallion. Brilliant one moment, flummoxing the next. He remains consistent in one regard, however, a trait that came to life in the final minutes of Texas' 22-21 win in Death Valley. Manning has generated 20 first downs for Texas (6-2, 4-2 SEC) as a runner across eight games this season. He delivered a season-most six on Saturday, including two with Texas' College Football Playoff hopes potentially on the line.

How the hell did the Texas Longhorns win that? The hard way.

Each of Manning's two first-down runs came in notably different fashions. Each represented a different element of the 22-year-old's best skill. How did Manning save Texas' season with his legs? Let's take a look at the film.

Both Manning's teammates and head coach Steve Sarkisian extoll the toughness of their QB. How's this run for tough?

Texas faced a 3rd-and-3 from the LSU 39-yard line with 45 seconds remaining. Manning took a snap in the shotgun formation, then felt immediate pressure from both LSU edge rusher Jordan Ross and defensive tackle Deuce Geralds. Manning didn't hesistate. It was time to hit the gas.

Arch Manning knew he had to bail from the pocket as LSU rushers swarmed him on a third down in the final minute of Texas' 22-21 victory. (YouTube)

Texas' QB didn't see clear daylight as he neared the line of scrimmage. He zagged to the right to avoid Geralds, then dove past linebacker Davhon Keys, his third shaken defender in a sea of 300-pound bodies. Manning fell to the turf with the football narrowly over the LSU 36-yard line. First down, Texas, who lived to see another day.

Arch Manning dove just far enough to record a first down in the last minute of Texas' game-winning drive at LSU. (YouTube)

Sarkisian received plenty of praise this weekend for his final play-call at LSU, in which running back Raleek Brown shifted from the right side of the formation to the far left side of the field, where he caught a game-winning four-yard touchdown pass from Manning. The formation trick was impressive, as was the wrinkle Sarkisian unveiled two plays prior.

Texas faced a 3rd-and-5 at the LSU 11-yard line with just nine seconds remaining. Sarkisian placed three wide receivers to Manning's left, and he placed wide receiver Ryan Wingo and tight end Emaree Winston to Manning's right. Sarkisian didn't give his quarterback a chance to put the ball in the air (and risk what could have been a fourth interception on the night). Instead, Manning received the snap from the shotgun, then immediately veered right as Wingo and Winston turned into blockers.

Texas tight end Emaree Watson made a key block on Arch Manning's final rush attempt of the night in the Longhorns' win at LSU. (YouTube)

Watson executed a key block on Princewill Umanmielen, LSU's best edge rusher. Wingo sealed off another potential tackler near the LSU five-yard line, and right guard Brandon Baker created a sizable running lane as a bulldozer in front of Manning. Texas' signal caller cut inside after his initial move right, and he dove upfield before falling to the grass at the four-yard line.

The designed run resulted in another Texas first down with six seconds remaining, setting up Manning's touchdown toss to Brown. Texas' final pass play Saturday stole the headlines. But the deployment of Manning seconds earlier deserves similar shine, in which the Sarkisian reinforced his reputation as perhaps ecollege football's best play-caller.
