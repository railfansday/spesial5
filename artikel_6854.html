The 5 Best Aluminum Carry-ons You Can Buy

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Every product was carefully curated by an Esquire editor. We may earn a commission from these links. Here’s how we test products and why you should trust us.

Tumi and Rimowa lead the pack, but the competition is getting close.

If you’re serious about travel, an aluminum carry-on is worth the investment. A well-made one can outlast soft-sided bags made from nylon, polyester, or canvas, as well as hard-sided suitcases made from polycarbonate—just a high-strength plastic. And it can withstand pretty much any weather: While water can seep through a traditional zipper, most aluminum-frame suitcases use metal latches and a compressed gasket to help seal out the elements, so your belongings stay safe and sound.

A full aluminum-shell carry-on typically uses high-grade aluminum-magnesium alloy sheets, reinforced corners, and those mechanical latches that now usually have TSA locks on them. Your stuff has a bodyguard. The catch? That bodyguard is a touch heavier than its polycarbonate counterparts. Full-aluminum carry-ons can weigh ten pounds or more before you’ve packed a single pair of underwear, and their shells have little give. That can be an issue if you’re taking an out-of-the-way flight and end up somewhere with carry-on weight limits.

But what likely catches your attention when you see an aluminum suitcase in the airport is the look. It’s got the retro-futurist swagger of the golden age of aviation with a modern luxury twist (and, yes, a higher price tag). There’s a reason Rimowa’s shiny luggage has become somewhat of a status symbol. Yours will probably pick up some nicks and dings along the way, but think of them as proof that it’s been places—and that you have stories to tell from all your travels.

Ready to invest in an aluminum carry-on for your next adventure? Make it one of our favorites below.

Tumi luggage comes with a hefty price tag (we told you these would be expensive!) but it truly will last for a very long time. When it comes to material, it doesn’t get more durable than this aircraft-grade aluminum, from which the exterior of this suitcase is made. Even the three-stage telescoping handle is made of aluminum.

But what’s even better about this carry-on is that, if you do end up needing more room (and the hard outer shell won’t budge because it’s just that damn sturdy), you can expand it and add two more inches of depth—yes, that means you probably won’t be able to use it as a carry-on after expanding it, but it’s a good option for when you want a smaller checked bag or like having the ability to pack more on your way back.

We can’t talk about aluminum carry-ons without mentioning the most famous of them all: Rimowa. Yes, it’s expensive, and yes, it will probably get a few dents over time. But Rimowa’s aluminum luggage is the best of the best in terms of style, durability, and recognition. Take it from Esquire’s senior director of commerce and partnerships, Krista Jones:

“When it comes to investment pieces, it all boils down to one argument. Why buy the mid-range level when the top-level is better quality? If you want a suitcase that will last you a lifetime—and this will—why spend $500 once on something you have to replace in five years? You might as well spend [$1,550] on something you don’t have to replace again. Rimowa offers that with a lifetime guarantee and will repair your bag too.”

When it comes to style, Away takes the cake for me. I’ve always loved the simplicity of the brand’s signature look with clean lines and smooth, rounded corners, and that look translates beautifully onto the aluminum version of Away’s classic carry-on. The shell is made from premium anodized aluminum, and the handles have gorgeous leather detailing—and the included leather luggage tag is a nice touch. This carry-on is also proof that aluminum suitcases don’t always have to come in silver. Yes, the silver colorway is a classic, but if you want something different, I’m obsessed with this deep metallic blue.

Earlier this year, Carl Friedrik joined the conversation and launched an aluminum luggage line of its own. In keeping with the brand’s Scandinavian minimalist roots, the carry-on has a simple aluminum exterior with leather detailing—but inside, it has a lot more going on. The interior boasts a zipped garment compartment on one side and an open section with a compression pad and straps on the other, as well as two zippered pockets for all the small things you want to keep organized.

Want a true aluminum carry-on without the $1,000 price tag? Nobl makes one for under $400, if you can believe it. It doesn’t have the glitz and glam of the Rimowa, but it works just as well and genuinely holds up. Plus, it comes with a free Nobl Air, a trackable chip that goes in your carry-on so you know where it is at all times—just in case you have to gate check it at the last minute.
