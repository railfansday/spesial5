Holiday staples still surging ahead of Thanksgiving dinner. Economist shares how to save on the spread

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Richard Volpe, professor of agricultural economics at California Polytechnic State University, explains how higher prices for food, energy, packaging and transportation could make holiday meals harder on household budgets.

The turkey may be a holiday bargain, but rising prices for meat, dairy and fresh foods could make the rest of the Thanksgiving spread cost much more this year.

Richard Volpe, a professor of agribusiness at Cal Poly San Luis Obispo, told Fox News Digital that sustained high energy costs in the midst of the Iran war — particularly for diesel — are pushing up grocery prices just as households prepare to host holiday meals.

Surging prices of ham and eggnog, in particular, could put a damper on some holiday favorites.

Shoppers can compare prices, watch for store promotions and consider store brands to keep holiday grocery bills in check. (Kim Raff/Bloomberg/Getty Images)

"Most of the biggest increases are things like fruits, vegetables and meats," Volpe said. "Fresh and refrigerated foods feel those costs sooner than shelf-stable products."

Dairy is also especially energy and transportation intensive, putting holiday staples such as butter and eggnog under pressure.

Volpe said shoppers willing to switch brands and take advantage of store promotions can keep holiday meals more affordable.

"If you are less worried about brand or banner loyalty, and you’re able to follow promotions and look for these sort of aggressive, enticing deals, there are still ways to achieve savings and to keep those Thanksgiving and holiday dinners relatively affordable," Volpe said.

With beef and pork prices high, some households may serve turkey for other holiday meals in place of pot roast or ham. (iStock)

He also acknowledged that high beef and pork prices could contribute to some households deciding to dine on a different main dish for their holiday meals this year.

"Beef and pork are particularly expensive right now, so I would expect to see some substitutions there," he said. "I would strongly suspect that across the country, there are folks who are roasting turkeys and chickens, or even salmon or some other large sort of centerpiece to the meal that’s protein-based other than beef and pork."

AMERICANS ARE MAKING COSTLY GROCERY MISTAKE EVEN BEFORE ENTERING THE STORE, EXPERT WARNS

Swapping the menu may help, but Volpe said skipping the grocery store for a restaurant or catered meal is unlikely to save money.

"It is still cheaper to go to the grocery store and prepare food at home," Volpe said.

Volpe told Fox News Digital that whole turkeys are often discounted before Thanksgiving as grocers use low prices to draw shoppers into stores. (Liz Clayman/Bloomberg/Getty Images)

He said restaurant prices have risen nearly twice as fast as grocery prices over the past two to two and a half years.

Volpe said he still expects food prices to rise at a more typical pace this year, even as the increases come during the holiday hosting season.

"I'm not expecting food price inflation anything like what we saw in ’21 and ’22," he said. "We’re actually still very likely on track for roughly normal food price inflation on the year."
