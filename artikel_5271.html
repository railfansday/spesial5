Why Is Crypto Down Today? Total Market Cap Crashes -2.5%

Why Is Crypto Down Today? Cryptocurrency markets woke up red on Tuesday, November 24, 2026. Bitcoin slipped below the psychologically important $60,000 level, trading near $59,800–$60,500 after briefly touching levels above $64,000 the day before.

Ethereum, Solana, and most major altcoins followed lower, with the total crypto market capitalization contracting roughly 1–2% over 24 hours amid elevated liquidations and persistent selling pressure. The question on every trader’s mind is straightforward: why is crypto down today?

Crypto entered the week already wounded. Bitcoin had plunged to a multi-year low near $55,000 over the prior weekend, wiping out leveraged positions and leaving the market in extreme fear territory.

Monday brought a sharp relief rally. Nvidia’s decision to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO sent the chipmaker’s shares soaring more than 30% and lifted the Nasdaq by around 7%. Risk assets broadly benefited, and Bitcoin briefly joined the party, climbing toward $64,000.

That bounce proved short-lived. By Tuesday morning, the gains had been fully erased. Bitcoin lagged the equity rebound significantly; Nvidia recovered most of its recent losses, while crypto reclaimed only a fraction of its slide. The divergence highlighted that crypto’s problems run deeper than a single day’s equity news.

Several concrete developments converged to pressure prices. First, the Nvidia-driven rally simply faded. Stock futures turned lower on Tuesday as the initial euphoria cooled.

Nasdaq-100 futures dropped around 1%, and related AI and crypto-adjacent equities gave back part of Monday’s gains. Crypto, which had been riding the coattails of the tech surge, sold off in sympathy but more sharply.

Second, institutional flows remained deeply negative. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded approximately $570M in net outflows on Monday, the fifth consecutive session of withdrawals.

BlackRock’s IBIT alone accounted for more than $500M of that figure. November’s cumulative outflows reached roughly $4.71Bn, setting a monthly record and removing a major source of structural demand that had previously supported prices.

Third, derivatives markets amplified the move. More than $800M in crypto futures positions were liquidated over the 24-hour period, more than triple the prior day’s total. Forced selling from both long and short positions created a self-reinforcing cascade as prices fell.

Broader macro factors also weighed on sentiment. Markets continue to price in a potential Federal Reserve rate hike in December, with key inflation data (PCE) due shortly.

Higher rates typically pressure non-yielding assets such as Bitcoin. Additional reports of possible new U.S. tariff measures added another layer of risk-off caution. Lingering effects from the large Binance-related hack earlier in the period further eroded confidence in the sector.

Crypto’s decline today is not the result of a single dramatic headline. Instead, it reflects a short-term relief rally that failed to overcome persistent structural headwinds.

The Nvidia news provided temporary momentum, but without positive ETF flows or a meaningful shift in monetary expectations, that momentum evaporated quickly. Leveraged traders were caught offside, and the resulting liquidations accelerated the drop.

Sentiment remains extremely cautious. The Fear & Greed Index sits deep in extreme fear, at around 10/100, and Bitcoin has still only recovered a modest portion of its November losses.

While some long-term holders and selective buyers may view current levels as attractive, the immediate market is dominated by risk reduction and profit-taking after the failed bounce.

Crypto is down today primarily because Monday’s Nvidia-fueled rally proved unsustainable. Heavy, record-setting Bitcoin ETF outflows continued to drain demand, while a surge in futures liquidations forced additional selling.

Macro uncertainty around interest rates and risk assets provided no supporting backdrop. In short, the market tested a bounce, found insufficient real buying interest, and retreated—leaving Bitcoin back below $60,000 and the broader crypto complex lower.
