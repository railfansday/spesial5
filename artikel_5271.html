Nvidia stock is facing a serious confidence test as a widening controversy puts its leadership, export controls, and China exposure under scrutiny. The reported allegations involving CEO Jensen Huang and businesswoman Alice Huang, also referred to as Alice Wang, have added another layer of uncertainty. Meanwhile, Nvidia stock has fallen from around $243 in early October to $150 in early November, before recovering slightly to approximately $165.

The controversy reportedly prompted Nvidia’s board to launch an independent investigation after Huang disclosed an intimate relationship to employees and family. Separate reports and allegations have raised questions about Alice Huang’s possible connections to Chinese intelligence and the alleged diversion of advanced Nvidia chips.

Those claims remain allegations, not established findings.

The potential business implications are substantial. Megaspeed, described as a Singapore-based Nvidia customer, has been linked to claims that billions of dollars in chips may have been routed into China, potentially bypassing U.S. export controls.

The legal and political pressure is growing. Nvidia and Huang were reportedly named in a proposed federal securities class action alleging misleading statements about compliance checks and chip diversion.

Meanwhile, three U.S. House Committee chairmen requested a closed-door briefing with Huang scheduled for November 18, 2026. The focus is reportedly foreign espionage risks and oversight.

The market reaction has been brutal. Nvidia shares dropped approximately 38% from $243 to $150 before recovering to around $165. The stock is also below its 200-day EMA band, with the longer-term bullish trend described as broken.

A sustained recovery could put $190 and $209 in focus. But if $150 fails, $130 and even $115 become potential downside levels.

For now, the Nvidia stock crash narrative hinges on whether shares can recover while the controversy remains unresolved. A rebound isn’t guaranteed, and another breakdown could deepen the damage.
