Why York Space Systems Stock Plunged This Week

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Contract delays are motivating investors to look elsewhere in the final frontier.

By Scott Levine – Nov 22, 2026 at 3:34PM EST

10 stocks we like better than York Space Systems ›

A wave of Wall Street price-target cuts following government contract delays sent York Space Systems (YSS +1.96%) tumbling 36.9% this week, according to data provided by S&P Global Market Intelligence.

York Space Systems stock changed hands at $3.65 as of the close on Friday, Nov. 20, down 36.9% from Friday, Nov. 13's close. Across those five sessions, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) fell 5.1%, and the Nasdaq-100 was down 5.1%.

Analyst downgrades and price-target reductions hit the defense hardware provider throughout the week. Barclays cut its price target from $11 to $5, while JPMorgan lowered its target from $17 to $7. The reductions followed the company's third-quarter report, in which management revealed a $749.7 million goodwill impairment charge due to delayed government contract awards. The stock is down 92% (as of the close on Friday, Nov. 20) from its 52-week high of $44.54.

York Space Systems manufactures small satellites and ground communications equipment for military, civil, and commercial space operators. It builds platforms like the Tranche 0 satellite line and Hydra MAX communications terminals used by national security agencies. The stock trades below $5, but the company generated $84.2 million in revenue last quarter from its spacecraft and ground hardware.

Management also announced plans to acquire Australian ground robotics maker Outlook Industries to expand into unmanned terrestrial defense systems. The deal expands York's total addressable market by $3.1 billion, but investors remained focused on near-term balance-sheet pressure. Wall Street's sharp price-target cuts invert the growth story, forcing investors to weigh a major impairment and defense contract delays against new expansion plans.

York ended the quarter with $308.9 million in cash and short-term investments as of Sept. 30, 2026, against $179.8 million in total debt, including lease obligations. While the company holds a $14.9 billion pipeline of government opportunities, contract timing remains unpredictable. Investors will look to the closing of the Outlook acquisition and upcoming defense contract decisions in early 2027 to see if revenue growth recovers.

York Space Systems stock is available for a fraction of what it cost last spring when it was changing hands at north of $45.

While York Space Systems claims it has a nearly $15 billion pipeline of government opportunities, there's no certainty that it will convert this to backlog -- and ultimately concrete sales. Take this, along with the fact that the company is burning through an increasing amount of cash -- operating cash flow through the first nine months of 2026 totaled $248.4 million compared to $88.2 million during the same period in 2025 -- and it seems that those seeking a compelling space stock opportunity now would be better off looking somewhere else.
