Invesco: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

International asset flows and margin expansion show how Invesco is scaling beyond the U.S.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 27, 2026, Invesco (IVZ +0.00%) announced a $1.5 billion preferred stock repurchase to strengthen its balance sheet. Replacing high-cost preferred shares with lower-cost bank loans reduces leverage and increases net income for common shareholders.

Management reached an agreement to repurchase $1.5 billion of preferred stock (shares that pay a fixed dividend ahead of common equity), bringing total preferred redemptions to $3 billion over 18 months. Funding the buyout with a lower-cost bank loan lowers overall capital costs and permanently eliminates recurring dividend payments.

Higher fee-earning revenue alongside controlled expenses drove operating margin expansion to 40.7% in the quarter. Scaling core capabilities across global assets under management (AUM, representing total client money managed) increases operating leverage, allowing top-line growth to outpace operational costs.

Net long-term asset inflows reached $35.1 billion during the third quarter, with client demand originating in Europe, the Middle East, and Africa (EMEA) alongside Asia-Pacific markets. Geographic diversification provides resilience against regional market volatility while expanding distribution channels worldwide.

Client capital continues moving into low-fee exchange-traded funds (ETFs, investment funds traded on public markets) and asset allocation model portfolios across wealth channels. Invesco relies on global distribution networks to navigate industry fee pressure while scaling active ETF platforms.
