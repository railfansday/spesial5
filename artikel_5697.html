Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Moving healthcare AI from pilot to practice" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Whether we’re physicians, nurses, care managers, or the people who keep coverage and claims moving, most of us entered healthcare to help people. This responsibility gives us an extraordinary sense of purpose, but also makes us cautious about new technology. Yet for the first time in my career, I see healthcare leaning in, because AI is exceptionally good at the repetitive work that sits between us and the patients we serve. Many of us have already experienced promising AI pilots. The question I hear most now is how to move from small experiments to enterprise-wide deployment.We’ve built healthcare technology one portal and system of record at a time. In a large health system, people navigate at least 10 to 20 systems, each with its own login and dataset for each patient. For every hour physicians spend with patients, they spend nearly two more on EHR and desk work, plus one to two hours of “pajama time.” We're short on clinicians, and we keep asking them to do paperwork instead of practicing at the top of their license.When I grew up in India, the doctor near my home knew me personally from handwritten notes. Today I’m an EHR in a big system that holds far more data about me, yet still doesn’t know me the way that doctor did. Healthcare today is data rich but intelligence poor. AI can help us put the right data in front of the right person, in the right context, at the right time.In my conversations about AI with providers and payers, I hear genuine excitement balanced by healthy pragmatism. None of us are ready for an AI doctor giving a diagnosis but documentation, phone calls, referrals, and back-office work? I see very little resistance to getting time back.

Gemini for healthcare connects data, intelligence, and action, so healthcare professionals can spend less time as human integrators and more time caring for people. Instead of simply putting a chatbot in front of clinicians or benefits specialists, it brings AI into healthcare systems and workflows through three capabilities: connectors, skills, and agents.

Organizations are using AI to provide more personalized patient care. Highmark, for example, is delivering highly tailored medical journeys, and providing care teams with actionable, real-time clinical intelligence. “Healthcare generates enormous amounts of information and data every day, yet patients often experience care as a series of separate interactions rather than one connected journey,” said Karen Hanlon, president of Highmark Health. “Through our work with Google Cloud, we're exploring how AI can help connect information across the healthcare experience, making it easier to support the whole person, create more personalized care experiences, and equip clinicians with meaningful insights at the right time.”Gemini for healthcare is now available in preview.Every job has its purpose and chores. AI can take on chores so people can do meaningful, purposeful work, such as finding every woman aged 45 to 55 whose notes mention a family history of breast cancer but who hasn’t had a mammogram. Today, no one can do that at scale. With an agent, a care manager could request that information in plain language, review the resulting list, and launch an outreach program.The real measure of healthcare AI is whether a patient gets the right care sooner, whether a clinician gets more time with a patient, and whether a person who needs help is less likely to fall through the cracks.All those years of digitizing healthcare, we were building up to this moment.
