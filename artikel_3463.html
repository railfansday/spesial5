Tom Hanks’ ‘You’ve Got Mail’ Officially Getting a Reboot

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Tom Hanks was back in the big-budget box office race earlier this year, after a difficult run of theatrical releases. As his famous, genre-breaking cowboy Woody, Hanks returned alongside Buzz (Tim Allen), Jessie (Joan Cusack), and the rest of the gang in Toy Story 5. After a mixed response to the fourth installment, Toy Story 5 was a return to form for Pixar's most important franchise.

Collider's Taylor Gates gave the movie an 8/10 rating in her review, saying, "The impressive fifth installment not only justifies its existence but feels like a necessary response to how much the world has changed in the past three decades." This was one of several near-perfect responses to the film, which no doubt helped it on its way past the $1 billion barrier at the global box office, currently ranking as the year's third-highest-grossing film. As Hanks fans bask in the glory of a successful return to form for the actor, they will rejoice further, as it has been confirmed that one of his most endearing movies is being given the reboot treatment.

Officially, Hanks' 1998 rom-com favorite, You've Got Mail, is being rebooted at Warner Bros. Jason Orley is attached to direct, 17 years after he was Nancy Meyers' assistant whilst working on the film It's Complicated. Orley is also reportedly working on a script that is based on a previous take by Deborah Kaplan and Harry Elfont, with Neal Moritz and Tania Landau set to produce under the Original Film banner. Sadly, this is about all we know so far, with plot details being kept tightly under wraps.

Four love stories, four completely different ideas of what it means to be somebody's person. This is the one that matches how you actually fall, and what it would ask of you.

You do not fall for strangers. You fall for the person you have already been talking to for years, which is the slowest and by some distance the most durable way anybody has found to do this.

You want a partner, not an audience. Somebody who wants their own life as badly as you want yours, and who would never ask you to be smaller to make the relationship easier.

The intensity was never the problem. The secrecy is. You are drawn to a love that lives entirely in private, and to the particular ache of wanting somebody you are not supposed to want.

You do not do this cautiously. When it happens for you it is immediate and total and it rearranges everything, and you would not accept a smaller version of it.

After successfully teaming up on 1993's Sleepless in Seattle, director Nora Ephron and stars Hanks and Meg Ryan reunited for 1998's You've Got Mail to even greater box office success. Against a reported production budget of $65 million, the film returned more than $250 million worldwide, split between a domestic haul of $115 million and a further $135 million from overseas markets. The film came out during a golden period for Hanks, released in the same year as his critical darling Saving Private Ryan and just a year before the beloved Toy Story 2 and The Green Mile.
