Fifpro criticises Bayern Munich for Musiala comments

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Musiala joined Bayern in 2019 after coming through Chelsea's academy

Football players' union Fifpro says it is concerned by comments from Bayern Munich's hierarchy about Jamal Musiala's contract, adding the German club "should know better".

Musiala collapsed twice in four days during pre-season and the 23-year-old Germany international said he had been diagnosed with "easily treatable absence seizures, which are due to a neurological dysfunction".

Bayern honorary president Uli Hoeness said earlier this month Musiala may never "regain the performance level he once had".

The Bundesliga club's chief executive Jan-Christian Dreesen has also questioned, external the lucrative contract extension the forward signed in 2025, running until 2030, if Musiala fails to get back to his best form, while his predecessor Karl-Heinz Rummenigge has suggested, external the deal may need to be renegotiated.

"Bayern's honorary president, chief executive and former chief executive have all publicly questioned Musiala's contract in quick succession," said a Fifpro statement., external

"In Fifpro's view, this is a co-ordinated strategy to put pressure on Musiala and his representatives ahead of any negotiations. A club of Bayern's standing should know better."

Musiala may never return to his best - Hoeness

Two collapses in four days - what's happening to Musiala?

Musiala, who joined Bayern in 2019 after coming through Chelsea's academy, came on for the German champions after 70 minutes in a 1-1 draw with Cologne on Friday as he made his first outing since scoring in a 7-0 win against Union Berlin on 18 September.

He has made five appearances for the club this season, scoring two goals and providing one assist.

"Bayern has a duty of care to Musiala," added Fifpro. "Across professional football, uncertainty about a player's future can take a toll on their mental health, particularly when they are recovering from injury.

"Making Musiala's health and future a matter of public debate adds to that pressure. Any concerns about his contract should be raised privately with him and his representatives.

"Contracts are there to be respected. An injury or a dip in form gives a club no grounds to reopen terms it agreed to, just as a run of outstanding performances gives a player no automatic right to a pay rise."

Musiala's return to the Bayern side came in the same game in which England striker Harry Kane suffered an ankle injury and was subsequently ruled out "for the next few months".
