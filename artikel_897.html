Bruno Fernandes was devastated Michael Carrick was sacked as Manchester United manager, according to his former teammate Rasmus Hojlund.

The Napoli striker, who completed his £44m move away from Old Trafford in the summer, is convinced that his old team, who are 19th in the Premier League, will only have a temporary stay in the relegation zone.

And Hojlund, a boyhood United fan who joined them for £64m in 2023, believes they have too much quality to go down.

The Denmark international was back in Manchester to score for Napoli against City in Tuesday’s 2-2 draw in the Champions League.

And he said: “I am watching all the games I can, especially from the red side of Manchester. It is unfortunate [that United are struggling].”

United have not won in their last nine league games, dating back to August, and lost 4-0 to Liverpool on Sunday in Mauricio Pochettino’s first match in charge.

They are second from bottom but Hojlund is not worried. “It is a temporary picture, I think they will get back to the top, the Premier League is like this,” he said. “I read the other day that Bournemouth were in the relegation zone after a couple of games, now they have won the last five games and are in the top half of the table.

“They have got a lot of quality, I have seen the guys training day by day and I know the players really well and they have got a great squad, good leaders, good professionals so I am sure they will turn it around. Hopefully they will get it right with Mauricio.”

Captain Fernandes was dropped by Carrick after questioning his tactics in the Champions League defeat to Como but he and Diogo Dalot caught up with Hojlund when Denmark faced Portugal and the forward said they were upset the manager was losing his job.

“In the international break when I spoke with Bruno and Diogo, they were all obviously devastated that Michael was going but also looking forward to some change,” Hojlund added. “I am still speaking with Patrick [Dorgu, his Denmark teammate] and I just wish the guys all the best and I am sure they will turn it around this season.”

Hojlund, who scored 26 goals in 95 games in two seasons at Old Trafford, said playing for United comes with great pressure, making it harder in the bad times.

“United is a massive club and you can’t take anything away from that,” he said. “There are so many people following the club around the world and you can’t really escape from it, it is almost too high when it is good and almost too low when it is bad so it is all about trying to be in that middle and now I think I tried both the highs and the lows there. The pressure is part of the club. It comes with playing for Man United.”
