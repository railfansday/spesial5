Berrada on Carrick, Poch, defending club leadership and transfers

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Manchester United chief executive Omar Berrada has spoken exclusively to Sky Sports about the start to the season; the United CEO discusses replacing Michael Carrick with Mauricio Pochettino, the futures of Bruno Fernandes and JJ Gabriel, transfer activity and the Man City charges

In a turbulent time at Manchester United, chief executive Omar Berrada has sat down exclusively with Sky Sports about United's start to the season, the decision to change head coach and facing fan criticism about the club's leadership.

With United in the relegation zone, the club sacked Michael Carrick during the November international break and replaced him with Mauricio Pochettino, who will take charge of his first game away at arch-rivals Liverpool, live on Sky Sports, this Sunday.

The change in head coach has followed a tumultuous opening few months of the campaign, with Lisandro Martinez also taking aim at the club's technical director Jason Wilcox for not preparing the team well enough in the transfer window, while Fernandes also criticised Carrick and his coaching staff's methods.

In this exclusive sit-down interview with Sky Sports' Patrick Davison, Berrada discusses:

Berrada believes United are going in the right direction, even though the club currently sit in the Premier League relegation zone ."One hundred per cent," he says.

"I know it's difficult to say this when you're sitting 18th in the Premier League table. It's clear - it's not good enough, not acceptable, and that's why we've made the change that we've made, so that we can move forward.

"But we are laying the foundations for sustained growth to put us in a position where we can be competitive consistently.

"The foundations are there. I am convinced that we are heading in the right direction."

This is the third season in a row in which United have changed their head coach in the middle of the season.

This season strongly resembles the 2024-25 campaign that saw United decide to stick with Erik ten Hag despite the club speaking to other potential candidates - only to replace him with Ruben Amorim a few months into the new season.

As a result, the United leadership team has been questioned - even more so after United defender Martinez publicly criticised technical director Wilcox over the transfer window just gone.

Asked if it has been difficult to see the fan criticism about the hierarchy, Berrada says: "It is. I'm not going to lie. But let's be clear, we're not the protagonists. This is not about the individuals, this is about the club.

"This is about doing everything we can to make our fans happy, and that means improving the results on the pitch, and that means getting a team that is performing to its maximum potential, that the fans can feel proud of.

"Being in this position comes naturally with the criticism and the scrutiny that we're going to get. We accept it, some of it is fair, some of it does cross the line.

"I think we do live in this world of social media, where people like to generate negative stories about the club because it drives more engagement, and it drives more general noise and activity around the club. But we park that."

There are lessons to be learned as well. "If you think about the fact that we've had three permanent managers in less than three seasons, clearly we haven't achieved the stability that we want to - that's for sure," Berrada adds.

"There are different circumstances as to why it didn't work out with Erik, Ruben and now Michael. And we're always learning. We're always trying to improve and understand what has failed."

However, while Berrada recognises the club's leadership hold responsibility for the club's success on and off the pitch, that responsibility also filters down to the players and coaches.

"It is our responsibility to run the club," he says. "And, yes, coaches have a very important role to play. Players, of course, have to perform at the highest level consistently for any coach.

"They're part of a club that when you're wearing the shirt, you have an enormous amount of responsibility. And that responsibility means that you have to be giving your best every day in training, in the matches. And that's the same for executives.

"So I understand the frustration from the fans. It's absolutely normal. My commitment is that we're going to do everything we can to make sure that we put ourselves in a position where we're competing to win titles consistently."

At the centre of United fan disgruntlement is the continued running of the club by the Glazer family, who work alongside INEOS chief Sir Jim Ratcliffe at the very top of the structure. Berrada insists, however, that the dual-running of the club is working.

"It's working very well," he says. "I know there's a lot of noise and people look for narratives in the media and that's fine, that comes with being part of this club.

"But from my perspective what I see is a very dedicated and engaged ownership group - both ownership groups. There's constant dialogue, we speak sometimes on a daily basis. Between them there's a very good alignment. I think they have complementary skill sets, if you want to put it that way.

"All the decisions that have been taken, we've been all on the same page, we're all supporting each other, and the shared ambition is to do what's right for the club."

So how did United come to the decision to remove Carrick? The former United midfielder was talk of the town at the end of last season after qualifying the club for the Champions League - but it all unravelled quickly after that.

Many felt United would remove Carrick after the 3-0 home loss to Bournemouth on October 25, but the club waited until the international break to make their move.

"It was a very difficult decision, to be honest, especially given all the affection everyone at the club has for Michael," says Berrada.

"When he took over the team last season, they did very well under him. They were playing some really good football, winning important matches. We qualified for the Champions League and finished third.

"We felt at the time he'd earned the right to continue leading the team. So when we started the season, it's clearly been a lot more difficult than we expected. The results haven't been good enough. I think we've dropped off in many areas.

"We wanted to give ourselves until the November international break to really take stock of what was happening with the team, what were the areas that needed to be improved. And in the end, we took the decision to make a change of coach.

"Unfortunately, sometimes when you're in the middle of the season, that's the one decision that you can make that can provoke a reaction, that can provoke a change in the dynamic. So that's why we had to take the decision."

The signs about Carrick's replacement, Pochettino, are very positive, according to Berrada.

Not only has the Argentine brought a degree of positivity to the club's training ground, but the United chief executive believes he has the "maturity" to take the Old Trafford reins.

"He has a tremendous amount of passion and energy," Berrada says of Pochettino. "We see it already in the two days he's been with us.

"There's a buzz about the place, and he's brought that with his staff. He has a very clear philosophy, identity of how he wants to see the team play. And I think he's coming just at the right time for him and us to be able to help the team and take us forward.

"He's equally as ambitious as we are, which is: we want to win titles.

"You have to think that when you're playing for Manchester United, when the results aren't going well, the pressure and the scrutiny is enormous. And all that noise around the club and around the players can be difficult to manage.

"So I think in Mauricio we bring somebody that has just the right amount of maturity and experience at the big clubs to be able to put his arms around the players and to let them perform at their highest potential."

"I think we've made progress in improving the squad over the last seasons." It is certainly a bold statement from Berrada.

United spent £163m on new players this summer - the majority of that on midfielders Carlos Baleba, Youri Tielemans and Andrey Santos.

With the jury still out on that trio, a lot has been made on the lack of activity in defence. Barring the introduction of Ayden Heaven from the academy, the United backline consists of the same players who were at the club five years ago.

As a result, United have one of the worst defences in Europe's top five leagues in terms of goals conceded.

But Berrada has defended the club's transfer policy - saying the previous summer's window where they signed Bryan Mbeumo, Matheus Cunha, Benjamin Sesko and Senne Lammens was a successful one.

"You have to take it in the context of the last few years," he says. "The summer of 2025, I think the recruitment team was widely praised with the four players that were signed. And they did very well. They helped the team achieve Champions League qualification.

"When we look at this summer, we went in knowing what we needed to do. Of course, there have been different dynamics at play. It's probably been the summer or the transfer window where there's been the highest inflation in transfer fees.

"And we've said publicly and we've said consistently in the last few years that we need to stay disciplined. We need to take the right decisions. We need to buy the right players for the right value at the right time in terms of what the squad needs.

"And when we look at the summer transfer window of 2026, I know we're receiving a lot of criticism for it, but we're actually happy with the players that we bought.

"We think that Carlos Baleba, Youri Tielemans, Andrey Santos, and Karl Darlow - and let's not forget that we've also welcomed back Marcus Rashford, who had a very good season on loan at Barcelona - these are five additions that we think have made the squad fundamentally better.

"It's true that we lost Casemiro, who is a top player and gave a lot to the club. We also lost Manuel Ugarte to, unfortunately, an ACL injury. So our focus was very much about straightening the midfield.

"When we started the transfer window, we really only had Kobbie Mainoo as our midfielder. So we wanted to reinforce that, and we did it."

United were interested in defenders this summer, most notably Newcastle left-back Lewis Hall, but Berrada insists the club were not going to overspend on players in this inflated transfer market.

"When you look at the other areas of the pitch, and there's a lot of discussion about the back line," he says.

"But we did come into it thinking: we have depth. We have nine players that can play in the back line, in the back four positions.

"So that's why we looked primarily at investing in the midfield. But we were always looking for opportunities. I think it's been widely reported that we were looking at potentially the left-back position as a reinforcement.

"I think what we've seen in the back line is we've had injuries, it's taken Matthijs de Ligt longer than we would want it to get back. Unfortunately, Luke Shaw suffered a muscle injury that meant he was out for five or six weeks.

"So could we have signed an additional defender to straighten the depth and the quality? Yes, but we have to find the right players and you have to find the right players at the right value.

"There's no point in us spending £40m, £50m for players that we're not convinced are going to add value to the team.

"So it's always about being disciplined and finding the right opportunities and getting the right players that are going to take us forward."

United also held interest in players such as Elliot Anderson, who moved to Manchester rivals City for £116m, and Berrada was pushed on the point about discipline in the transfer market.

"When I talk about being disciplined, I talk about not overspending on players that don't necessarily add quality to the team," he says.

"Because when you sign a player that doesn't work out for Man Utd, it becomes very difficult to move that player on or to get the sale we want. And that can set us back.

"So when I talk about being disciplined, I'm not talking about necessarily not spending to get the top players. We have the ambition to win, to win the Premier League, to win the Champions League, to win the domestic cups. And for that, we need top talent.

"So when we find the right opportunities, we will spend. But what we don't want to be doing is spending just for the sake of it."

One of the main themes that could define Pochettino's time at United is the future of club captain Fernandes.

Sky Sports News has reported the Portuguese midfielder has turned down two new contract offers at the club, with his existing deal expiring at the end of this season.

There is a real risk the reigning Premier League and PFA Player of the Year could leave the club in the coming months.

"Listen, I've said before, we have a tremendous amount of respect for Bruno," Berrada says. "We're very fortunate to have him as a captain.

"What he's done for this team over the last six, seven years is absolutely incredible. Last year he won, I think, all the individual awards you can win.

"And we're very proud to have him as captain. It's true that we're having conversations about his new contract, but I prefer to keep that private. I think it's something that needs to be discussed behind the scenes between him and the club."

One player who is set to depart the club is 16-year-old talent JJ Gabriel - who announced his exit from the club this week amid an approach from Real Madrid.

United were not happy that Gabriel announced his departure without informing the club - and the entire saga has been a sour note on a club that has a long history of producing academy players.

"We did everything. Absolutely everything," says Berrada about Gabriel's situation.

"Let's not forget with JJ, he's a very young boy with a lot of media attention around him. Our primary concern is his well-being.

"Credit to the academy and people that have been here for many years, they helped him in his development. We still think this is the best place for him to continue his career and to grow.

"This club has a very rich history and tradition of developing young players and giving them the opportunity, as we're seeing this season with Shea Lacey. Obviously, Kobbie Mainoo has been doing well for us for a few years now - we're not afraid of giving young players opportunities.

"Obviously, him and his family have taken the decision to leave. We can only wish him the best.

"We want players that want to be here, that feel being at Manchester United is a privilege."

Berrada was also asked about his involvement in the Manchester City charges case.

In September, an independent commission found City guilty of breaching more than 100 of the Premier League's financial rules between 2009 and 2018. City are appealing against the ruling and deny any wrongdoing.

He was the chief football operations officer at City Football Group overseeing talent management, performance services and football intelligence between 2020 and 2024.

Prior to the role, Berrada had worked as chief operating officer at Man City from 2016.

"I'm not concerned," says Berrada when asked if there are any worries about his involvement in the situation.

"I want to be very clear about this: I wasn't involved in any wrongdoings. I wasn't involved in the proceedings. There are no allegations against me.

"In that sense, there will be no surprises."

But in another complicated situation, Berrada could lead United in claiming compensation against his former club, should their appeal fail.

"I don't want to comment on that because right now, it's an ongoing process," he added.

"When it runs its course, then we'll take a view of what is our position as Manchester United."
