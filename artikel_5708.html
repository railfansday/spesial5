Democrats win House by largest margin in 50 years. And, COP31 ends with no deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Vote counts are concluding for the 2026 midterm elections, revealing a better picture of just how much Democrats dominated. Fifty-six percent of voters chose the Democrats in House races compared to 42% for Republicans. The 14-point gap is the largest margin won by Democrats in 50 years. The party has officially won the largest majority in Congress since the early 1990s. Take a look at NPR's breakdown of the U.S. House races, by the numbers.

Artificial intelligence is causing significant swings in the stock market as investors speculate about the future of the technology boom. While AI stocks have driven markets to record highs and bolstered the economy, concerns are growing about a potential bubble. Investors anticipated that AI giant Anthropic's stock market debut last week would lift markets, but instead Wall Street experienced one of its worst weeks in recent times.

Poland has called an emergency NATO session for today after Russia fired three cruise missiles into its territory. The incident highlights the stakes for European countries in Russia's war on Ukraine, prompting nations along NATO's eastern border to seek enhanced air defense support. Poland plans to present proposals to increase surveillance capabilities and deploy more F-35s to intercept incoming missiles and drones.

Delegates at COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, couldn't agree on a plan over the weekend to address fossil fuels, the main cause of climate change. The event ran two days longer than expected, making it the longest in the history of these summits. Nearly 200 nations failed to finalize targets for electrification and financial aid for developing countries, or to address the impacts of climate change. The European Union rejected any conclusion that didn't advocate for a transition away from fossil fuels, while oil-producing countries like Saudi Arabia opposed this position. Fossil fuels contribute about 73% of global emissions.

by Maria Godoy, senior science and health editor and correspondent

Clinical services supervisor Dale Weiser poses for a portrait at the Chester County Health Department in West Chester, Pa., on Oct. 28, 2026. Daniel Vasta for NPR hide caption

People had warned me it might be tough to get Amish people to talk to me as a journalist — and probably impossible to get their permission to record their voice. Not a great omen for compelling radio storytelling.

Yet as I headed off to Lancaster County, Pa., last month to report on the biggest and deadliest measles outbreak in the U.S. in decades, I knew I wanted to hear directly from the community hit hardest.

So I turned to some good old shoe-leather reporting. My producer and I mapped out Amish-owned businesses in and around Lancaster County and spent a day driving around from shop to shop. At every stop, we introduced ourselves and explained the focus of our story. We asked Amish people (easily identifiable by their traditional dress) about their views on the measles outbreak and vaccination. While some declined to talk, others opened up about how measles had affected their families and communities, and why they had or hadn't decided to get vaccinated. To my surprise, one woman even let me record her voice.

Outside a farm supply store in a rural town, I met an Amish woman who was visibly pregnant. Measles can have devastating consequences during pregnancy. She told me her husband and two children had gotten vaccinated to protect her and her unborn child. She declined to give me her name at first, then got in a car driven by her hired non-Amish driver. As they exited the parking lot, the driver stopped to ask us what media outlet we were with. "Oh, NPR!" the driver said when we replied. Then she turned to her Amish passenger and told her, "Don't worry, you can trust them."

Part of gaining trust is about showing up, showing respect and really listening. Word gets around that you're trustworthy. Public health officials in Pennsylvania have also been using a similar approach to win the trust of Amish people as they seek to boost vaccination rates in the current outbreak.
