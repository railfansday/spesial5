Grid operators file closely watched large load interconnection reforms at FERC

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The PJM Interconnection proposed revamping its interconnection process by studying large load and generation interconnection requests in a single, one-year review.

Most major U.S. grid operators have now submitted their highly anticipated proposals for interconnecting data centers and other large loads to the grid in response to directives issued by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission.

FERC in June issued “show cause” orders saying that it appeared that regional transmission organizations and others lacked adequate rules for adding large loads to the grid. FERC asked them to propose reforms to address the agency’s concerns or to explain why their existing rules were just and reasonable.

FERC detailed five issues it wanted the grid operators to address in their large load interconnection rules: efficient transmission service application and study processes, including the consideration of alternative transmission technologies; prevention of cost shifts and transparency into transmission costs; co-location and behind-the-meter generation; new transmission services for flexible large loads; and a study process for generating facilities that serve “electrically proximate” large loads and co-located loads.

The move by FERC comes amid a surge in data center development in many U.S. regions and a corresponding pushback on that development, which includes concerns that existing utility customers will pay for some of the data center-related electric infrastructure.

Below is a summary of the proposals at FERC, which were filed on Monday, except for ISO New England’s. The Southwest Power Pool is slated to file its proposal by Friday.

The centerpiece of PJM’s plan is a Fast Energization Process, under which the grid operator would revamp its interconnection study process by studying large load and generation interconnection requests in a single, one-year process — about twice as fast as the current process, according to PJM.

In its filing on Monday, PJM said its proposed Fast Energization Process is designed to address “insufficiently fast interconnection processes for load and generation, parallel and duplicative study processes, speculative requests, limited visibility into the drivers of transmission upgrades, and an ongoing resource adequacy shortfall.”

The proposed process marks “the next evolution of PJM’s interconnection process and further enhancement to the currently effective Cycle process,” Donald Bielak, director of interconnection planning at PJM, told FERC in a separate filing.

PJM’s overall proposal also addresses co-location and behind-the-meter generation rules that were at issue in a separate show cause proceeding, according to the grid operator. PJM filed a separate proposal that requires large computational loads to be able to ride through grid disturbances.

PJM’s transmission owners filed their own proposed tariffs that establish rates, cost allocation, cost recovery mechanisms, financial protections, revenue-crediting requirements and transparency measures for transmission service they provide to co-located loads and large loads.

“The proposed reforms strengthen accountability and protect existing customers,” CAISO told FERC. “They improve transparency, operational visibility, technical standards, cost responsibility, and protection against speculative development.”

The CAISO and transmission owner tariff revisions create streamlined paths for large loads to connect to the electric system, according to the grid operator. “Standardized application information, deposits, milestones, study coordination, and service agreements will make the process more transparent and predictable,” CAISO said.

As part of its proposal, CAISO plans to offer new service options for flexible large loads that can cut their electric use when transmission capability is unavailable.

In its filing on Friday, ISO-NE and the region’s transmission owners said they fully addressed FERC’s show cause order. In response to the agency’s transparency concerns, ISO-NE proposed that it maintain on its website a list of the network upgrades identified for each large load, categorized by upgrade type, and the cost estimates for those upgrades.

To address FERC’s concerns about resource adequacy in New England, ISO-NE said it plans to require that large loads bring their own new capacity and that certain large loads will be excluded from ISO-NE’s capacity market’s installed capacity requirement. The grid operator plans to work with stakeholders to craft the details of its proposed framework, with a goal of filing a proposal at FERC later this year.

MISO said its proposal builds on its other reform plans recently filed at FERC: the Zero Injection Generator Interconnection Agreement, Interconnection Reliability Requirements for Large Loads and Large Load Addition Resource Study.

The just-filed proposal includes a Large Load Project Review process, new transmission service options and tariff revisions and pro forma cost recovery agreements designed to prevent cost shifting onto existing utility customers, MISO said. In a separate filing Monday, MISO proposed Grid Responsive Integration Transmission Service for large loads that can limit their electric use under certain price and reliability conditions.

MISO’s proposals at FERC represent “an important milestone, not an endpoint,” the grid operator said. “MISO remains committed to further enhancing its planning, operational, and market frameworks as experience is gained and system needs grow.”

Among other things, NYISO’s proposal provides new types of transmission services — Firm Withdrawal Service and Non-Firm Withdrawal Service — that reflect the unique characteristics of NYISO’s transmission model and of co-located large loads, large load with behind-the-meter generation, and flexible large loads, the grid operator said.

The proposal includes a compromise approach to resource adequacy requirements, called the State Exempt Load Project exception, which aims to reconcile the NYISO’s reliability responsibilities with the New York Public Service Commission resource adequacy authority, the grid operator said.

The PSC has launched a proceeding to address state-jurisdictional interconnection reforms that will likely influence the evolution of NYISO’s large load rules, the grid operator noted. New York transmission owners also filed a complementary large load proposal at FERC.
