A virus found in one out of ten patients in China is carried by a tick species that is spreading across the US

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Researchers in China identified a new tick-borne virus, ALTNV, linked to fever and more severe illness when combined with another virus.

Researchers in China found a newly identified tick-borne virus in 10.4 percent of 3,163 patients they tested. The virus, called Asian longhorned tick nairovirus (ALTNV), also turned up in ticks collected from 15 provinces.

Patients infected with ALTNV alone recovered. Among 38 patients also infected with the virus behind severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS), seven died.

The tick species that carried ALTNV most often, the Asian longhorned tick, has spread across parts of the United States. The Chinese study tested only patients and ticks in China, so it does not show whether the virus is present in U.S. ticks.

The work was led by researchers at the State Key Laboratory of Pathogen and Biosecurity in Beijing, with Xiao-Ai Zhang as first author. They reported it in September 2026 in a letter titled “A Zoonotic Orthonairovirus from Febrile Patients in China.”

Their starting point was a gap in diagnosis. More than 30 percent of patients clinically diagnosed with SFTS test negative for Dabie bandavirus (DBV), the virus that causes the disease.

The team screened 3,163 patients with fever and a history of tick exposure. They looked for ALTNV genetic material or IgM antibodies, which the immune system makes early in an infection.

Among patients who had tested negative for DBV, 15.1 percent tested positive for ALTNV.

ALTNV belongs to the genus Orthonairovirus in the family Nairoviridae. Its proteins share less than 80 percent of their amino acids with other species in that genus.

The researchers described 56 patients with ALTNV alone. Fatigue affected 84 percent of them, and 80 percent had gastrointestinal symptoms.

Low platelet counts, known as thrombocytopenia, appeared in 38 percent. Raised levels of the liver enzymes called aminotransferases appeared in 36 percent.

All of these patients recovered without lasting effects.

In the 38 patients carrying both ALTNV and DBV, coinfection was linked to more severe illness. Respiratory symptoms affected 61 percent of them, compared with 38 percent of patients with DBV alone.

Kidney impairment affected 84 percent of the coinfected patients, compared with 66 percent. Seven of them, or 18.4 percent, died.

The two viruses circulate in the same areas and share a tick vector. The authors call for better tests to tell these tick-borne viruses apart where SFTS is common.

The team also tested 47,428 ticks gathered across 15 Chinese provinces. ALTNV genetic material turned up in 1.29 percent of them.

The Asian longhorned tick, Haemaphysalis longicornis, carried the virus most often, at 1.75 percent.

In laboratory experiments, the species passed ALTNV to mice.

Outside Asia, this tick can reduce production in dairy cattle by 25 percent in some regions of New Zealand and Australia, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) noted in 2018.

New Jersey was the first U.S. state to report the tick, on a sheep in August 2017, according to the CDC.

A single female can reproduce without mating. As a result, hundreds or thousands of ticks can build up on one animal or in one area.

Ben Beard, Ph.D., was then deputy director of the CDC’s Division of Vector-Borne Diseases.

“The full public health and agricultural impact of this tick discovery and spread is unknown,” Beard said in 2018.

By October 2025, the U.S. Department of Agriculture had documented the tick in 21 states, with Kansas the most recent.

There, it was found on a dog in Franklin County, according to the Kansas Department of Agriculture and the Kansas Department of Health and Environment (KDHE).

The ticks have been collected from New York south to Georgia and west to Missouri. They are not competent vectors of the bacterium behind Lyme disease, the USDA’s Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS) states.

In cattle, the species can transmit Theileria orientalis Ikeda, a parasite responsible for bovine theileriosis. U.S. ticks passed it to cattle in the laboratory, and it has been detected in ticks collected in the field.

In 2025, the Connecticut Agricultural Experiment Station (CAES) reported the first evidence in the U.S. of a longhorned tick infected with Ehrlichia chaffeensis. This bacterium causes human monocytic ehrlichiosis.

Dr. Goudarz Molaei is a research scientist and medical entomologist at the CAES and an adjunct associate professor at the Yale School of Public Health.

“A rigorous program in mitigating risks should include expanded tick surveillance programs, strict measures to inspect animals, plants, and other imported materials at ports of entry, accurate and proper identification of exotic ticks and pathogens, effective eradication and prevention measures, and the ability to test exotic ticks for both native and nonnative pathogens,” Molaei said.

In 2019, an Asian longhorned tick in Virginia was found to be infected with Bourbon virus, the Kansas agencies noted.

The study was published in the journal New England Journal of Medicine.
