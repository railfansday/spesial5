Is Scotland's system limiting Ben Gannon-Doak's threat?

Two years ago, when Scotland defeated Croatia in the Nations League, that November night at Hampden was the dawn of Ben Gannon-Doak.

The then-teenager took Â£77m Manchester City man Josko Gvardiol to the cleaners and fans demanded the national team be built around the winger.

The Tartan Army's love for him has never faded - it arguably peaked when he stole the show in Scotland's World Cup opening win over Haiti - but it is fair to say they are growing frustrated, arguably on the Bournemouth man's behalf.

Under Sebastien Pocognoli, Gannon-Doak - who turned 21 on Wednesday - has been deployed at right wing-back, a role that demands defensive instincts unnatural to the pacy attacker.

It is already clear the new head coach is wedded to his preferred system, but it is one that is stifling one of Scotland's greatest attacking outlets - even though his final product still leaves a lot to be desired.

As his side prepare to visit Nations League Group B1 leaders Switzerland on Monday needing a win to have any chance of a promotion play-off place, Pocognoli has addressed both concerns - citing he is not uneasy about either - but pundits and punters alike cannot seem to shift their suspicions.

In that 1-0 victory over 10-man Croatia, it was Gannon-Doak's fearlessness in facing one of Europe's most elite and experienced teams that caught the eye.

His electric speed and directness excited a support who had long been crying out for a player with such qualities and the confidence in applying them. A natural winger, if you will.

In Pocognoli's system, though, there is no space for wingers. Something that former Rangers full-back Richard Foster says must "scunner" the wee man.

"When you're a full-back and you get to play wing-back, it's amazing - all of a sudden you are 10-20 yards up the pitch, doing more attacking and you can be a bit more aggressive," he said on BBC Scotland's Sportsound.

"When you ask a winger to do that, most of the time they are absolutely scunnered. They are thinking 'I don't want to go back there'.

"Looking at the squad [against North Macedonia], the three most exciting players we had - Findlay Curtis, Josh McPake and Gannon-Doak - are all wingers and he didn't bring two on.

"You look at the previous game [against Slovenia] and the difference McPake and Curtis made by coming on and taking players on - it gives everyone a lift.

"As a fan, you want to get excited about what you are watching. We are playing in a shape where we have the most exciting talents on the bench and they can't get on unless you adjust things. I just think he has to change the formation."

Foster is far from the only one wishing for a shift in shape - and not just for Gannon-Doak's benefit. Much has been made about the Scots' options in defence, too, and whether they match-up to the method.

However, it is arguable Gannon-Doak is the biggest casualty of them all.

"I don't think we're getting the best out of Ben," former Scotland captain Scott Brown told BBC Scotland.

"You saw him making so many last-ditch headers on Friday. He's having to add things to his game that he probably hasn't done before, like throw-ins, defending one-v-one."

No need to mention that throw-in, Scott. But that wasn't the only instance where Gannon-Doak looked a little lost. Think the 'Confused Travolta' meme.

While his position is a new worry, his precision with his final product is not.

Almost in a microcosm, Gannon-Doak's play was laid bare when be hurtled to the byeline before winning what turned out to be the match-winning penalty against the North Macedonians on Friday.

"He made a great run for the penalty, but I did think he could have got his shot away a bit earlier and maybe not taken that extra touch," Brown said.

Back in 2024, the wee live-wire was forgiven for not always finding a pal with a pass or choking at crunch time with his final ball.

However, as the years go on and the caps rack up, his shortcomings are still on show. His end product is still largely poor and patience is wearing thin.

"As long as he is high on the pitch and is creating one-v-ones, I am happy," Pocognoli reiterated post his maiden Hampden win.

"With Ben, we know that he is a young age and needs to work on specific things. On Friday night, he had great opportunities.

"We know that he needs to improve on the end product. But he created a penalty and, against Slovenia and North Macedonia away, he created a number of unbelievable one-v-ones. He did that on Friday night as well.

"We know he can be good if we dominate the game and he can get in good positions if we control the ball.

So Pocognoli is comfortable for now. Already that appears to be a running theme for the head coach - even if others are not.
