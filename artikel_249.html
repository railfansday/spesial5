This week, I’ve been reading about nuclear radiation and F1 brains and phone scammers, watching about 10 minutes of Madden before bailing, playing a lot of Madden NFL instead, learning my way around Instagram Edits on the Mac, trying the 12-3-30 workout while watching Mission: Impossible movies in half-hour increments, getting my new Apple HomeView up and running (still don’t know why I bought one…), and dreaming up stuff to do with the Flipper One.

I also have for you the game of the year (decade? century?), a couple of great new productivity tools, a Netflix take on the FTX story, and much more. But mostly, it’s GTA week. Let’s do it.

Also, programming note: Installer is off next week. It’s Thanksgiving, there’s food to eat and football to watch. Hope you have a great couple of weeks, and we’ll be so back in December.

You may not know Tom Hovey, but I can practically guarantee you’ve seen his work. That’s because, in addition to many other cool projects, Tom’s also the artist behind those illustrated graphics of everyone’s bakes on The Great British Baking Show. His unique style has been all over that show, and my TV screen, for many years.

I found Tom’s work recently on Threads, when he went massively viral after debunking the idea that all those food drawings are AI with a video to prove how he does it. He’s been working with digital tools for a decade, he said, “but never ever AI.”

I asked Tom to share his homescreen with us to see how he might use his phone for his work, and also to see if I might learn any good GBBS spoilers. (Alas, none.) Here it is, plus some info on the apps he uses and why:

The wallpaper: A sunrise photo of the Swan River, where I run most mornings in Perth Western Australia. We moved here permanently 6 months ago.

The apps: Apple Podcasts, Brick, Sonos, Strava, SoundCloud, Photos, Google, Google Maps, iNaturalist, Google Calendar, Chrome, NYT Games, Gmail, WhatsApp, Spotify, Discord.

Like many people I’m addicted to my phone, so bricking my phone when I get home from the studio has been amazing. I’m obsessed with running, so Strava and Runna are used every day for training and logging my runs but also hyping up my running pals.

Six apps for my kids’ primary school is too many apps.

I have weather apps but it’s kinda unnecessary now we live in Australia, because it’s either hot or really hot!

iNaturalist is my newest app and I love it. It’s for documenting nature — flora and fauna. As I’m in a new country, I’m enjoying discovering all the native flowers and plants I find on runs. I’ve also recently done some trial runs in Perth Hills, where I’ve run into tons of wild kangaroos and snakes. So fun! My kids also love bugs and birds, so learning all about them is cool. Also when you add your photos of nature, it geotags them and sends the details to some scientists who can understand the areas you are documenting.

One of the most underrated strange things about the tech industry is how much The Social Network changed the way people think about Mark Zuckerberg. Yes, the movie takes huge liberties, and yes, every single person involved with the movie was quick to remind us that it was fiction. But for so many people, Jesse Eisenberg’s character just became who Zuck was.

I think Andrew Garfield in Artificial might be about to do the same thing to Sam Altman. The new trailer looks fabulous, all the early buzz about the movie seems to be great, and the movie absolutely could not be more perfectly timed. It’s apparently a pretty brutal look at Altman, OpenAI, and the whole AI movement — I wonder how Garfield and his handsomeness might bend the way the world responds. I cannot wait to see this movie.
