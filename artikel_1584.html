Montella describes ‘dream’ Milan opportunity, Berlusconi and the Supercoppa: “An incredible feeling”

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Vincenzo Montella’s time with AC Milan was ‘a dream’ for him, but he failed to achieve much during his time at the helm.

Looking back on Montella’s time leading Milan is tricky. On paper, the Italian had a strong tenure at the club, achieving a 50% win rate and only losing 18 of his 64 games in charge at San Siro, not a terrible record by any means.

He even ended a five-year trophy drought with a win against Juventus in the Supercoppa Italiana and returned the Rossoneri to Europe, with a sixth-place finish in the 2016/17 season. In itself, that was a first since 2014.

This is where context matters, though. The start of the 2017/18 season was tough, as the Diavolo won six of their first 14 games and he was sacked before December. Despite that, he still views the club in an excellent light.

Speaking to La Gazzetta dello Sport’s ‘La Tripletta’ podcast, Montella went over his time with the club and how he fulfilled a dream by taking the job, as MilanPress relays.

“It was a fantastic experience there [at Milan] too, it was my dream. Then, as a professional, it’s a little different, but when I was thirteen I was a fan of that AC Milan team. My older brother took me to see Maradona’s Napoli team twice to make me a Napoli fan, and Maradona scored three goals.

“Despite this, my idol was Van Basten because I was a striker: I studied him and dreamed of becoming like him, and so I was a Milan fan. I knew everything about Milan, so for me it was an incredible feeling to join Milan.”

“He liked to talk about football; he liked to give advice even when it was a bit out of context at the time, because he always had the mentality of having Van Basten as a centre forward with Papin, a two-time Ballon d’Or winner, on the bench. He wanted me to play with two strikers, and I tried to plug the gap for months.

“I told him it was difficult; I knew all the lineups of those years. I was a Milan fan: the starter was Van Basten and the reserve was Papin, both Ballon d’Or winners. So, ‘If you buy me some players like that, rest assured that I’ll play them both.'”

“It was a year when we returned to Europe. I think it had been two or three years since Milan had been there, and it was something new at the time. We won a trophy, a small one. Just the other night I saw Galliani at a dinner; he reminded me of it and remembers it perfectly.

“I have to say that I found him to be the perfect manager at the time, because he was always on my side when it came to pressure; he gave me complete autonomy in my decisions and he helped me with everything, from the number of collaborators to new things; whatever I asked for, he was always on the coach’s side.

“And so for me he was truly an incredible manager, who in that phase, in that change, was fundamental even in achieving these small results, especially after Berlusconi’s great AC Milan.

“We deserved to win even in regulation time: we had some easy chances to win it, then it came to penalties. Gigio saved the penalty from Dybala, who kicked it really hard, but he filled the net with it.”

“I was a little worried from the beginning because there was this obsession with publicising everything, almost like a publicity stunt, so I was a little worried. In theory, there was sharing, at least that’s what it looked like, but I couldn’t back out when in reality that wasn’t always the case.

“And so sometimes there were players I hadn’t even consulted about, who would arrive and then say, ‘It was shared,’ and when you’re there, you can’t say otherwise. Certainly, when you build a team and have the budget, the first thing you do is: we had a goalkeeper, I’ll get a centre forward, we were missing a centre forward.

“In the final selection, the centre forward was the only one left, with a budget of 15-20 million. And then Kalinic… yes, I chose him because that was the final budget. So our, let’s say, second-choice striker was paid thirty-eight million, if I remember correctly, who was André Silva, and the first striker was paid half that.

“And so, in my opinion, a misunderstanding of this kind was created, with so many changes, even though at that stage we weren’t even playing badly for having fifteen new players.

“We still had Calhanoglu, who wasn’t feeling well, coming off months of suspension. The team was still being built: we weren’t in line with the Scudetto objectives that had been announced, but certainly, given our development, we were absolutely on target.

“Who would I have signed up front? At the time, we were probably negotiating for Aubameyang, if I remember correctly. Ronaldo? No, I didn’t believe it, not at all.”
