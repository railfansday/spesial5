Puka Nacua injury details reveal ‘completely torn’ MCL

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Puka Nacua may have a hard time trusting Sean McVay after his latest injury. The Los Angeles Rams' top receiver's decision to get knee surgery was not a hasty one after all, as Nacua's MCL was "completely torn" off of his tibia, making it a Grade 3 strain, according to ESPN's Adam Schefter.

Nacua is expected to be in recovery for at least the next four months, but the decision to get surgery is based on the belief that without it he would have never been the same again. With surgery, Nacua should be able to fully recover and be back to his usual self by Week 1 of 2027, according to most experts.

It's hard to believe this catastrophe could have been so easily avoided.

The Rams were ready to throw in the towel on their last offensive snap against the Washington Commanders and punt the ball away with a lead, but for some reason McVay called an end-around for Nacua to throw him into the fire at full speed in the middle of the field. The hit on Nacua caused damage to his MCL and PCL, requiring season-ending surgery.

Nacua is also dealing with turf toe. Given how poorly injury diagnoses have gone for L.A. this season — from Myles Garrett to Terrance Ferguson to Davante Adams and Nacua — can any players or fans trust a return timeline from McVay ever again?

With surgery, there's little skepticism that Nacua will be able to be a top-tier receiver by next season. That's good news for the Rams, but only if they decide to keep him. It would be ludicrous for L.A. to let Nacua become a free agent (they almost certainly won't do that) but equally foolish for the receiver to play on a franchise tag in 2027. Given the number of injuries he has suffered in an offense practically designed to injure receivers, Nacua must not play for the Rams again without a long-term contract.

L.A. should consider how bad their team might look next season without Puka Nacua. This surgery allows Nacua the chance to fully recover in time to participate in OTAs, but only if the Rams have extended him first.

An extension for Nacua will be costly, but actually take up less cap space in 2027 than a franchise tag. A lot less.

With as many free agency priorities as they have, the Rams should think about extending Nacua as soon as they can. It may also depend on the futures of Matthew Stafford and McVay. If both of those people move on in 2027 to other things in life — retirement or the booth — then the Rams may have other priorities in mind.

Nacua might be thinking of moving on too.
