WHS girls hockey brings new coach, plenty of experience into upcoming season

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Nov. 17—WORTHINGTON — The Worthington High School girls hockey team has just one senior on the roster, but what the Trojans lack in age, they make up for in experience.

Many Trojans who saw plenty of playing time last year are back on the team under first-year head coach Jason Johnson.

Johnson is a broker and co-owner of Johnson Builders & Realty. He also has experience coaching many of the players on the girls hockey team, dating back to their time in Mini Mites, Mites, 10U and 12U. Now, he gets a chance to guide them at the varsity level, hoping to keep making a positive impact.

"I want to try to make a positive impact on these girls, not only in the ice arena but in life," he said. "And I'm looking forward to the opportunity to work with them and try to develop them individually and as a team, and teach some grit and resilience and life skills along the way."

Joining Johnson on the coaching staff is assistant Vincent Louis Hasara. Johnson takes over the program after a 9-15 record last season, the most wins the Trojans had in a season since 2019. It was also just two years removed from a winless season in 2023.

Johnson thinks the team he takes over can build off some of that momentum from last year, but the Trojans are already plagued with some injuries in the preseason.

"One girl is going to be out for the season with an ACL injury," Johnson said. "One of our goalies is going to be out until probably mid December with a foot injury."

Among the healthy standout players on the team is the lone senior Chloe Joens. Joens is a defenseman, a position she says suits her well.

"I really like to be physical," she said. "I want to help my team, I want to help my goalie so I view defense as a more physical, more communicative role for me."

Along with her defenseman duties on the ice, Joens is also a leader as the only senior on the roster."I just try my best, honestly. I don't let it get to my head," Joens said. "I just view myself as an upperclassman. We have a lot of good juniors that step up into the big leadership roles, so they kind of help me out."

The Trojans' roster also includes a group of eight juniors, many of which have seen lots of playing time already in their careers.

Back as a starting goalie is Macie Mahlberg, as are centers Sydnie Van Briesen and Breeta Johnson. Johnson was named All-Big South Conference last year as a sophomore.

Wingers Tayler Nickel, Madilyn Polz, Madelyn Cummings and Chloe Erlandson could also make an impact among the junior class. Joinng Joens as a defenseman is junior Leah Nelson.

Other defensemen listed on the roster include sophomores Victoria Bush and Kiley Platt, along with freshman Audrey Johnson. Forwards include freshmen Lillian Hohenstein and Khloe Kennedy.

"I feel like they're really stepping in," Joens said of the younger players on the team. "They're really watching the older girls, learning what to expect, learning expectations they have. They're really stepping up."

Many players have seen varsity minutes before and look to see many more this year.

Right now, the Trojans are working on fundamentals as the team gets back into hockey shape before the start of the season, which is fast approaching.

"At the beginning of the year this year, we're doing two-a-days, so we've got plenty of time on the ice, a little under three hours a day," Johnson said. "We started with the basics, and that goes from the basic skills of stick handling, passing, keeping your head up. And then we've been working on our breakouts, we've been working on our forecheck, we've been working on our defensive zone."

Worthington will play its season opener Tuesday, Nov. 17 at Mankato West. After that will be games against Princeton/Becker/Big Lake and New Prague on Friday and Saturday respectively. The home opener will be Tuesday, Dec. 1 against powerhouse Luverne.

"I want to see hustle, I want to be the hardest working team on the ice, regardless of who we play, I want to see resilience or relentless play," Johnson said. "You just keep on grinding. I think that's a super important skill to have to know that you're not going to win every battle, but you're going to keep on fighting."

"We're not focused on winning, we're focused on improving," Johnson added. "We're focused on getting better every single practice and eventually every single game."
