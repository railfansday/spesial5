Pat Murphy calls Jake Bauers a “Huge Part” of Brewers after $58 million exit

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Milwaukee Brewers manager Pat Murphy admitted that Jake Bauers' departure was tough for the team, but said he was happy for the first baseman as he begins his next chapter with the Boston Red Sox.

Bauers played three years in Milwaukee, contributing to the team's run to its first World Series title. Losing him means the Brewers must replace a valuable presence both in their lineup and clubhouse. Still, Murphy made clear that his disappointment over the departure did not take away from his happiness for Bauers and the opportunity he secured in Boston.

Fox Sports 920 host Hunter Baumgardt posted a video on social media, on Wednesday, featuring Murphy discussing Bauers' contributions to Milwaukee and sharing his excitement for the first baseman's next chapter.

"Someone said to me the other day, 'Is it hard now losing Jake?' Yeah, yeah. He was a huge part of that room for the last three years, and yeah. But well, you don't seem that upset about it. I'm like, I'm excited for Jake, and we move on. It isn't all about us, you know. It is about some guys getting rewarded, and it happens at different times," commented the Brewers manager.

The Boston Red Sox officially unveiled Bauers' four-year, $58 million deal on Tuesday, welcoming him to Fenway Park. The 31-year-old secured the contract after posting the strongest season of his career, batting .266/.389/.484 with 26 home runs and 86 RBIs in the 2026 regular season. He also claimed National League Championship Series MVP honors while helping Milwaukee capture the title.

Bauers' tenure in Milwaukee began with a trade from the Yankees ahead of the 2024 season and lasted through the 2026 campaign. He chose not to accept the Brewers' qualifying offer worth $23.15 million before signing with Boston. The Red Sox intend to feature him mainly as their designated hitter, though he is also expected to see some action at first base and in the outfield.

The 31-year-old's exit affects more than Milwaukee's roster, as Murphy also loses a player who became part of the team's clubhouse fabric. The manager's comments reflected that sense of loss while acknowledging the personal milestone Bauers reached after his career-best campaign.
