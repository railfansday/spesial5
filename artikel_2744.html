CrowdStrike to buy Zscaler in $44 billion deal as AI supercharges cyberattacks

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

CrowdStrike plans to buy Zscaler for $43.9 billion in an all-stock deal that combines two significant security businesses at a time when artificial intelligence is supercharging attacks.

Shares of CrowdStrike dropped roughly 5% in early trading Monday, while Zscaler surged more than 20%.

The deal is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal year 2028. Under the agreement, Zscaler shareholders will receive 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each share.

CrowdStrike's acquisition pushes the Austin, Texas-based cyber vendor into the network security market and comes amid a massive year for cybersecurity deal-making in the wake of high-profile hacks like Hugging Face.

"With Zscaler, we're building cybersecurity's operating system: a single control plane to deploy AI, control AI, and enable any agentic application to run safely," CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz said in a statement. "We see a path to become the largest AI safety and security company the world has ever seen."

Recent outages affecting Microsoft, the Federal Aviation Administration and JPMorgan Chase have sparked anxiety as businesses accelerate software patching and upgrades to aging systems in an effort to prevent bad actors from exploiting software vulnerabilities with AI.

CrowdStrike has been a major beneficiary of skyrocketing demand for AI security, with shares soaring about 80% this year as of Monday's open. Zscaler shares are up about 5% year to date after Monday's surge. Their combined market value exceeded $265 billion as of Friday's close, behind market leader Palo Alto Networks at $282 billion.

The deal marks CrowdStrike's biggest acquisition to date and is among the largest in the sector as businesses consolidate and ramp up defenses to fend off AI-powered attacks.

Last year, Google announced plans to acquire Wiz for $32 billion. In 2024 Cisco shelled about $28 billion for Splunk. Competitor Palo Alto Networks has also spent billions on several cyber and AI businesses, including startup Applied Compute and a $25 billion deal for CyberArk, which it closed earlier this year.

Analysts view the Zscaler deal as a major AI security opportunity, allowing CrowdStrike to capture more customer security spending and close a significant gap with Palo Alto.

"The addition of ZS to CRWD's platform could bring more breadth in an era of vendor consolidation and, more importantly, network security to position the company more effectively for rising demand for AI Sovereignty," wrote analysts at JPMorgan.

CrowdStrike on Monday also reported better-than-expected fiscal third-quarter results, including earnings of 34 cents per share on $1.58 billion in revenue. That topped the 31 cents per share on $1.53 billion in revenue forecast by LSEG.

Zscaler's fiscal first-quarter earnings, also released on Monday, likewise surpassed estimates.
