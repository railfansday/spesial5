Josh Kushner and Bob Iger spotted at Lakers-Jazz game

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Josh Kushner and Bob Iger attended the Los Angeles Lakers vs. Utah Jazz game alongside Lakers governor Jeanie Buss on Monday, Nov. 23. The trio was seen sitting among the crowd at Crypto.com Arena.

It was the first game they've attended since agreeing in August to purchase the team from Mark Walter at a $12.5 billion valuation. Kushner and Iger have talked with Buss about her role with the organization throughout the sale process.

Kushner also went through the franchise's "back-of-house space" with Rob Pelinka, the Lakers president of basketball operations and general manager, according to ESPN's Dave McMenamin.

Jerry West (#44) played 14 seasons from 1960-1974 with the Lakers and was a force who never left the NBA once he got there, according to USA Today's Jeff Zillgitt. The Hall of Famer, pictured here in action against the Cincinnati Royals, was remembered for his imprint as a shooting guard and builder of teams. His impact remains visible in today’s NBA and he went on to touch generations of teams and players, including LeBron James, who talked to West about winning and losing.

The Lakers often believed that Elgin Baylor (#22) remained under appreciated during his 14-year NBA career with the franchise in Minneapolis and Los Angeles. Baylor, pictured Feb. 17, 1970, in a game against the New York Knicks, is among six Lakers luminaries to have a statue outside of Staples Center, and is also among 10 Lakers players to have their jersey retired. Baylor entered the Naismith Memorial Hall of Fame after winning an NBA championship (1972), posted an NBA Finals record 61 points in Game 5 of the 1961 NBA Finals against Boston, winning the league’s rookie of the year award (1959) and making 11 All-Star appearances.

Los Angeles Lakers center #13 Wilt Chamberlain scored 31,419 points, averaging 30.1 ppg, during 1,045 games over a 16-year Hall of Fame career. Chamberlain led the NBA in scoring seven straight years, averaged 50.4 points per game in 1962 and scored a record 100 points in a single game on March 2, 1962 against the New York Knicks in Hershey, Pennsylvania. The NBA didn’t televise the game and less than 5,000 people got to witness the iconic moment. HoopsHype ranked the top 80 players in NBA history and Chamberlain came in ranked No. 6 on the list, according to Sixers Wire.

Los Angeles Lakers center #33 Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, pictured in action during the 1981-82 season in Indianapolis, is one of the greatest basketball players in history – from his days as a prep star in New York City at Power Memorial Academy to his three NCAA championships and 88-2 record at UCLA and his six NBA championships and six MVPs with the Milwaukee Bucks and Los Angeles Lakers. He averaged at least 21 points in his first 17 seasons, averaged at least 12 rebounds in his first 10 seasons and shot at least 50% from the field.

Los Angeles Lakers guard Magic Johnson (32) in action against the Detroit Pistons during the 1988 NBA Finals at the Silverdome. Magic was the face of the Lakers and the NBA during much of the 1980s when he won five NBA titles and led the "Showtime" teams in legendary showdowns with Larry Bird and the Celtics. The Hall of Famer was a three-time NBA MVP and is regarded as one of the best point guards ever to play the game.

Los Angeles Lakers' Kobe Bryant soars to the basket against Sacramento Kings's Mike Bibby during Game 4 of the NBA Western Conference finals in Los Angeles, May 26, 2002. Kobe spent all 20 years of his legendary career with the Lakers, won five championships, and is the fourth-leading scorer in NBA history. One of the fiercest competitors to play the game, Kobe could do it all. His legend in Lakers Nation will live on forever.

Los Angeles Lakers forward James Worthy celebrates his team's series-clinching victory over the Portland Trail Blazers in Game 6 of the NBA Western Conference Finals at the Great Western Forum in Inglewood, Calif. on May 30, 1991. James Worthy was a cornerstone of the Los Angeles Lakers’ “Showtime” era, helping the franchise win three NBA championships in the 1980s. Known for his clutch performances and smooth all-around game, the Hall of Famer remains one of the most celebrated players in Lakers history.
