Eagles vs. Steelers: The good, the bad, and the ugly

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

It looks like Nick Sirianni has his job for another week. It looks like the Eagles are heading towards a threepeat as NFC East champions, and another playoff run in 2026.

Well, one of those statements may have a drop of truth to it after the Eagles snapped one streak and preserved another in a 19-16 victory over the Pittsburgh Steelers on an overcast Sunday at Lincoln Financial Field.

The Eagles snapped their seven-game losing streak, their longest since 2012, during Andy Reid's last season. The Eagles also continued their streak at home against Pittsburgh, winning for the 12th-straight time over the Steelers, who have not won in Philadelphia since October 24, 1965, (20–14 at Franklin Field), 61 years and two stadiums ago.

Maybe the addition of former Eagles' coach T.J. Paganatti as a consultant had something to do with the Eagles' offensive line looking the best it has this season. The defense was strong, holding the Steelers to 73 yards rushing, 43 yards below their season average of 116 yards a game, and sacking 42-year-old relic Aaron Rodgers five times for minus-45 yards.

Joe Milton was competent, moving the Eagles, especially in clutch situations, completing 11 of 21 passes for 141 yards.

Saquon Barkley made a brief appearance as Saquon Barkley rushing for a game-high 75 yards, the bulk of which came on a first quarter 38-yard touchdown run, while Jeremiah Trotter Jr. led the defense with a game-high 13 tackles, Marcus Epps had his best game this season with a season-high 8 tackles, and a bunch of guys who had received very little playing time making significant contributions like Byron Young's two sacks, Jon Jones' three defended passes, and Gabe Hall coming off the practice squad for a sack.

But are they suddenly cured of the slopfest they perpetrated over the first nine games—absolutely not.

If the Eagles can somehow beat Dallas, in Dallas, on Thanksgiving, with the lowly tanking Arizona Cardinals and Indianapolis Colts following them, this nightmare of a season could have life, with the Eagles sitting at 6-7 in an NFC East Division up for grabs.

Did Sunday's victory resuscitate the Eagles or briefly camouflage a bad football team for one week and in the long run hurt its draft status?

The answer may be four days from now in Dallas.

The Eagles are no longer the proud owners of the NFL's current longest losing streak, which has now been handed down I-95 to sinking Baltimore Ravens' six-game skid after starting the season 4-1.

For four days, everything will be right in Eaglesland. Sirianni is 6-0 after the bye week, Milton will no doubt stir comparisons by Eagles' fans and podcasters to Randall Cunningham, and the true believers will begin making their reservations to Los Angeles for mid-February.

There was a pile of good, some bad and smallest amount of ugly this season in the Eagles' 19-16 win over the flailing Pittsburgh Steelers.

Joe Milton's game-clinching third-down, seven-yard completion with less a minute to play to DeVonta Smith.

The Eagles' defense. They kept the Steelers to under 100 yards in the first half. Vic Fangio dialed up blitzes, stunts, and caused all kinds of problems for Aaron Rodgers. They sacked Rodgers five times for minus-45 yards and kept the Steelers out of the end zone for their first 10 drives before Max Hurleman's 20-yard touchdown reception from Aaron Rodgers with 1:21 to play.

Gabe Hall came up from the practice squad to sack Rodgers for the fifth time, taking the old river down for a nine-yard loss on the Steelers' second drive in the fourth quarter. At the time,the Eagles sacked Rodgers five times for minus-45 yards.

Milton had a knack for completing big passes in big situations. His nine-yard completion to Darius Cooper for a first down on a third-and-six on the Eagles' first drive of the fourth quarter was one of them. It was just the second first down for the Eagles in their first five second half possessions. Milton later converted another third down by hitting Cooper for a 14-yard completion on a third-and-10 at the Steelers' 31 on the Eagles' second fourth quarter drive.

Nolan Smith's seven-yard sack on the Steelers' second play of the fourth quarter, zapping Pittsburgh's hopes of tying the game on a third-and-eight at the Eagles' 35. More importantly, the Smith sack pushed the Steelers out of field goal range and forced a punt with 13:12 left to play. The problem was, the Eagles had not moved the ball since the first quarter, only gaining 30 yards since torching the Steelers' defense for 128 yards of total offense in the opening frame.

Safety Marcus Epps had his best game this season, coming up to make tackles for short gains. He stopped Germie Bernard on a second-and-10 in the third quarter, he came up and took down Rico Dowdle for a three-yard loss in the second quarter. He finished with a season-high eight tackles—all solos.

Jake Elliott hitting field goals of 41, 29, 53 and 55 yards. Elliott is 11 of 12 on field goal attempts, and 18 of 18 on extra points. Elliott is 4-for-4 on field goals attempts of 50 yards or more. Hehas quietly had a great season. It makes you wonder why Nick Sirianni kept going for it on fourth down this season on the plus side of the field with Elliott kicking so well.

Braden Mann's 70-yard punt that pinned the Steelers at their four after a three-and-out on the Eagles' second possession of the second half.

Cornerback Jon Jones making a diving deflection on an Aaron Rodgers' pass intended for Roman Wilson at the goal line on the Steelers' second drive of the second quarter. Jones later came up to break up a fourth-quarter attempt to Wilson.

Linebacker Jonathan Greenard's 12-yard sack on the Steelers' first drive of the second quarter on a third-and-19.

Defensive tackle Byron Young's nine-yard on the Steelers' first drive of the second quarter. Young had his second sack late in the third quarter, pulling down old man Rodgers for seven yards on first-and-10.

The first quarter. The Eagles scored a season-high 13 points after they had scored a total of 28 points in their first nine games in the first quarter. They had 128 total yards of offense to Pittsburgh's 35 and five first downs to the Steelers' one. The Eagles' 13-0 lead was their largest this season.

Saquon Barkey's 38-yard sideline touchdown run for a 13-0 first quarter lead. It was classic Barkley, using his considerable speed to run away from TJ Watt trying to catch him from the backside and finding a rare hole.

Milton hitting Cooper with a 29-yard rope down the sideline on a third-and-13 to the Steelers' 28, and Dallas Goedert for a 38-yard pass on a great touch pass on a third-and-18 to the Steelers' 13 on the Eagles' second drive. Unfortunately, the Eagles were only able to get a Jake Elliott field goal out of it.

The Eagles getting the Steelers off the field on Pissburgh's first three drives for a total of 23 yards and no first downs. Of the Steelers' first seven plays, the Eagles had two plays for zero and minus-1 yard, and an average of 2.3 yards a play. The Eagles held the Steelers on fourth-and-one, and had Pittsburgh facing a third-and-eight and third-and-14.

Jihaad Campbell taking down Steelers tailback Jaylen Warren for a one-yard loss on the Steelers' second drive at the Steelers' 21.

Jake Elliott nailed a 41-yard field goal with 10:01 left in the first quarter.

Jeremiah Trotter Jr. slicing through on fourth-and-inches to bring down Warren down for no gain on the Steelers first drive at the Pittsburg 40. It was the utmost disrespect by Steelers' coach Mike McCarthy, thinking he could convert a fourth-down gamble that early in the game. McCarthy practically gave the Eagles a 3-0 lead. The Steelers had far more to lose as a playoff contender than what the hapless Eagles were playing for. In each game Trotter has started, he led the Eagles in tackles, and this game was no different. He made a game-high 13 tackles and led the Eagles again with 9 solo tackles. He continues to prove he is a bonafide NFL starter. Trotter and Campbell actually teamed well together combining for 20 tackles, 14 solo and one tackle for loss.

The Eagles' second quarter. They gained just 13 yards and no first downs in the quarter, while giving up 64 yards and seven first downs.

Milton missing an open DeVonta Smith down the sideline on the Eagles' fourth drive. Milton missed Smith down the middle of the field on the first play of the Eagles' second possession (after a fumble on the second half kickoff).

The Eagles coming away with just two Jake Elliott field goals on their first two drives flagged once and sacked twice for minus-17 yards.

The third quarter. The Eagles picked up only 17 yards and one first down in the quarter. They were 0 for 3 on third-down conversions.

Rookie right tackle Markel Bell and right guard Tyler Steen being split by T.J. Watt for a four-yard loss with 4:06 left in the third quarter. It was typical about everything this season. No one knew who to block Watt, with Bell thinking he was handing Watt off to Steen and Steen thinking Bell had him.
