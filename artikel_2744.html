Falcons – Chiefs recap: Wilting away in Mercedes-Benz Stadium

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

If you were daydreaming about an improbable run, simply hoping for a miracle, or just looking for something enjoyable as the season very slowly winds down, the Falcons were determined to ensure your Sunday left you bereft. The quality of the opponent aside—the Chiefs are very good, obviously—this was the bottom for the Falcons, and the point at which team and fanbase alike need to fully accept certain hard truths about the Atlanta Falcons of 2026.

Those truths? The first and most obvious one is that this is a bad football team at the moment. Despite some promising signings and trades, the talent level is not good enough across the board to make them a playoff-caliber team even in a weak NFC South, something the Falcons appeared to accept when they traded away Jessie Bates and Ryan Neuzil. Also, while this team has more resources than just any other team in the NFL, they are going to need every dollar and draft pick just to overhaul what they have in a way that brings their talent level up to where it needs to be and exorcises all the weird ghosts that have been awkwardly floating around this team for most of the last decade. And finally, there are very few positions on this roster that are actually settled with 2027 looming, meaning this team could look very, very different than it does now by May of next year.

There is a Sunday every year in this miserable stretch of losing seasons where the terrible weight of losing lands especially heavy on my shoulders, and this is the one for 2026. More than likely, I'll come out of the bye ready to joke about the Falcons reeling off some unlikely wins and finishing this year with a losing record for the ninth straight season, and we'll all lock arms and head into a pivotal offseason daring to dream of something better. But I'll tell you with naked honesty that this Sunday got to me in a way the previous nine games did not, as I saw the this team continue a frankly brutal three game slide and make it fully clear just how limited their ceiling for this season really is. At 3-7 and driving that stake into this team's hopes with relish, the Falcons ensured I can fully give up on 2026 and just try to find the bright spots in what's to come, and I do intend to take full advantage. I hope you will, as will.

For now, though, we'll be digesting a game where Patrick Mahomes threw for five touchdowns and the Chiefs ran all over Atlanta despite not having Kenneth Walker, and the defense was still the more impressive unit. Atlanta's offense has now scored 17 or fewer points in three straight games, which begs the question of whether Tommy Rees is the right coordinator, Penix is the right quarterback, and almost every player on that side of the ball is going to be part of the next great Falcons offense outside of Bijan Robinson, Chris Lindstrom, and Drake London. That seems like an overreaction to one game—and perhaps even three games—but this is an evaluation year for a regime with the resources to make substantial changes in 2027, and an offense studded with talented players looking this crummy will give them genuine pause. The Chiefs are a good, well-coached team, but the gulf between them and a frankly hapless Falcons team should give them further pause.

The bye week will give these Falcons a chance to reset and try to make something of the rest of this year, but the fate of this team seems beyond obvious at this point. As we have so many times over the past decade, we Falcons fans are left to look ahead to better days after the last bloom has fallen off the rose. May this coming offseason finally bring those long-elusive days to us, so that we may put games like in the past where they belong.

He hasn't looked superhuman very often lately, but Bijan Robinson remains a player defenses fear and a back capable of breaking some jaw-dropping runs. His best carries in this game were like that, where through sheer will, balance, and power he managed to reel off 10-plus yards despite the at-times nonexistent threat of the Falcons passing game working against him. Someday the offense will sputter to life again and he'll look like the Bijan of old.

Jahan Dotson would ideally be your third receiver, but even with his ups and downs he's been a very credible receiving threat for this team, and has had some really good weeks. He was a better pass away from a touchdown today and finished second on the team with three receptions for 63 yards, and is on his way to a career year in an offense that can and wants to get him the ball.

Drake London deserves a medal of some kind. His career has been defined by a revolving door of play callers, quarterbacks, and surrounding personnel, and yet he continually blocks his ass off and shows out as the team's best receiver. He somehow had over 100 yards in this one despite the passing game being dormant much of the day, and London deserves better than the results this team sees in the win and loss columns weekly.

Harold Perkins had a couple of ugly reps on the first defensive drive, and that must have galvanized him because he was a man possessed after that point. Perkins was fantastic against the run, a demon as a pass rusher, and more stingy in coverage after that point, standing out over and over again no matter what he was asked to do. There's little question Perkins is a player the Falcons plan to count on in 2027 and beyond, and he showed why on Sunday.

Brandon Dorlus and Gervon Dexter remain one of the best duos on the interior in the NFL, and Dexter in particular was a man possessed with five pressures, three run stops, and multiple plays where he rampaged through the overmatched Chiefs offensive line like Godzilla through the financial district. If the Falcons can bring more consistent pressure off the edge to help out D&D, quarterbacks will be miserable.

The defense did the heavy lifting on Sunday against a good Chiefs team minus key pieces, and reminded us they're the unit that is much closer to being a finished product, even if the overall results were still rough. It's not that difficult to talk yourself into Billy Bowman Jr., Xavier Watts, Kendal Daniels, Perkins, Dorlus, Dexter, and company just getting better a year from now with better complementary pieces around them.

Jake Moody came uncomfortably close to missing an extra point, but otherwise he was excellent. Moody hit both short field goal tries and had the NFL's first successful onside kick attempt, with a major assist from Chris Blair, in his Falcons debut. Hopefully he can prove to be the team's long-term solution at kicker, and the early returns were promising, even if the kicks weren't particularly challenging.

Blair, as mentioned above, had a phenomenal play. He wasn't quite there on a potential touchdown pass from Penix, but the onsides kick recovery was flawless, as Blair very confidently grabbed it away from a sleepwalking Xavier Worthy and kept his feet inbounds to give Atlanta a last gasp chance they couldn't take full advantage of.

Yes, the offense has holes and weaknesses that need to be addressed in the offseason, but it's not so bereft of talent that it should look like it did on the first four drives. The Falcons started this game with one productive Bijan Robinson carry, one eight yard catch for Kyle Pitts, and a whole lot of incompletions, penalties, and ineptitude that led to four straight three-and-outs. That represented all four drives in the first quarter, and was evidence enough that this team selling at the deadline was an accurate read of their potential. It was, as the broadcast noted, the first time since 2000 that the Falcons started the game with four straight three-and-outs.

Michael Penix Jr. simply can't miss this consistently if this offense is going to be anything of note. On the first pass of the game he had an open Drake London and missed him, and his misfires piled up throughout the first half as Atlanta continually frittered away their drives. Matt Ryan praised Penix before the game but made it clear the Falcons were looking for more consistency from him, and consistency continues to elude the third-year quarterback, given that this was another forgettable day from a player who continues to look like he needs ideal situations to thrive. If that continues, it's unlikely he'll be the starter in September 2027.

Kyle Pitts has become something of an avatar for this offense. Both are highly regarded for their potential, capable of absolutely eye-popping weeks, and yet prone to inconsistency and dreary performances. Pitts had a solid four catches for 50 yards, but that's not outstanding production, he wasn't a huge factor after the catch, and he had one catch stripped for a Chiefs turnover. A player who seemed destined to thrive in Kevin Stefanski and Tommy Rees' offense just hasn't.

Jawaan Taylor was called out during the broadcast for giving away the play call with his stance and then had multiple false start calls. The Falcons will be in the market for a new right tackle in 2027.

Jake Matthews was hit with three separate penalties, and the legendary left tackle appears to be closing in on the end of the line. Did the Falcons want to have to replace both tackles in 2027? They'll chalk this up as an off day for Matthews, who was pretty solid outside of those penalties, but Father Time is tapping his foot and Bill Callahan seems likely to put his stamp on more than just the center position.

The Falcons defense was by far the better unit through the first half, but they still had brutal lapses that gave the Chiefs a 14-3 lead. The worst single play was on Emmett Johnson's touchdown grab in the first quarter, which saw two Falcons get a shot at bringing Johnson down and utterly failing to do so, as missed tackles and bad angles led to both Kansas City scores. It's hard to hold it against them too much given that they were on the field nearly constantly, but miscues are miscues, and Atlanta had tons of them.

The run defense is a five alarm fire at this point. Johnson is a promising backup for Kansas City, but he is no Walker, yet his production suggested he was possessed by the veteran back's spirit. Since dominating some lousy ground games early on in the year, the Falcons have been among the least inspiring defenses in football and are both struggling to get into position and struggling to tackle opposing backs for going on six weeks now.

This was a very, very tough game for Atlanta's secondary, which Mahomes picked apart with his usual precision. A.J. Terrell allowed three receptions for 86 yards and two touchdowns, C.J. Henderson gave up three more for 97 yards and a score, and Billy Bowman Jr.'s mostly strong day was undone by three grabs for 41 yards and another score. Terrell in particular has been brutal the past two weeks, and the Falcons have to figure something out in the back end before Kirk Cousins tries out his spin on a revenge tour in two weeks.

The Falcons had Tommy Rees down on the sidelines, which is usually a sign that the offense is struggling (it is) and the team is taking a hard look at their coordinator's fortunes (to be determined), and the offense put up another dud of a performance. Cooper Rush being the quarterback badly hurt the Falcons the first two weeks, but they've already turned the ball over on offense more than they did in 2025 and now have scored 17 or fewer points in five of their ten games and three straight weeks. There are so many execution errors and penalty issues that you can't lay this all at Rees' feet, but the flashes of really thrilling offense just aren't there right now, with a strangled run game, too many early passes, and go-nowhere screens damaging the effort.

It's a bad sign that this team is continually getting worse as the season rolls on. You'd expect Michael Penix Jr. to be playing better football than he was fresh off a long injured stint early in the year, but he's not. You'd think this defense would be starting to figure things out with so many young players in the lineup, but they're getting destroyed by quality offenses. This coaching staff is not going anywhere, as I've said repeatedly, but it would be extremely unideal for them to go into a pivotal 2027 with any shadows of doubt hanging over them. Right now, it's hard not to feel like this staff is failing to get more out of a team that looks incredibly sloppy and penalty-prone, at the very least.

This is a team that's not even in the same stratosphere as real contenders in the NFL. They're not even in the same breath as the teams they're nominally just behind in the NFC, frankly.

A bye week. We'll take stock of what has passed and what's to come, and then get ready for the Vikings.
