An MVP is not version 1 with fewer features

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Posted on Nov 18 • Originally published at tenlux.tech

An MVP is not "version 1 with fewer features". It is the smallest thing that answers one question: will anyone use this?

Most teams build the opposite. They start with the billing system, an admin panel, three integrations and a design system — and only then ask whether the core idea works. By that point the answer costs six months.

The test is simple: if a feature can be postponed without changing whether you learn the answer, it is not in the MVP.

They usually follow each other: agree first, then test the hypothesis, then scale.

One metric, decided before launch. Not "people liked it" but:

If you cannot name the metric before the build starts, the MVP will be judged on vibes — and vibes always say "we need more features".

Building the MVP for the wrong user: the one who pays, instead of the one who uses. The person who approves the budget is rarely the person who will press the buttons every day. Watch the second one.

A focused MVP fits in 2–6 weeks, and a landing or bot in days. The rule of thumb: if the estimate is longer, the scope is not an MVP — it is a full product with an MVP label on it.
