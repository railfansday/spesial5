Andriy Shevchenko slams Mykhailo Mudryk for ‘baseless slander’ after Ukraine doping claim

Ukrainian football federation president Andriy Shevchenko has issued a sharp rebuke to Mykhailo Mudryk after the Chelsea winger blamed the national team for his positive drugs test.

Mudryk tested positive for the banned substance meldonium in 2024, and was initially banned for four years by the Football Association. He successfully appealed against the length of his ban to the Court of Arbitration for Sport, and returned to football in July 2026.

Speaking in an interview with Ukrainian YouTube channel Guest of Gordon, Mudryk, who is on a season-long loan at Tottenham, laid the blame on “the environment in the Ukrainian national team” for his suspension.

He said: “I had never heard of a drug like meldonium. It is a drug you can only get in Eastern Europe. It could only have got into my body in Ukraine. There is no such drug in England.”

He said he had only been in Ukraine with the national team, which “points to the fact it was in a national team camp”.

Former Chelsea and AC Milan forward Shevchenko, now president of the Ukrainian Association of Football (UAF), called such claims “baseless slander” and said that “numerous investigations and audits” had been unable to identify how or when the meldonium entered Mudryk’s system.

He alleged that Mudryk’s camp had tried to convince a staff member at the UAF to take the blame for the 25-year-old’s positive test.

Shevchenko said: “His representatives repeatedly approached the UAF with a proposal to find an employee who would falsely take responsibility for the meldonium found in his doping sample.

“The UAF refused, as such a proposal contradicts the principles of our work.”

Shevchenko criticised his compatriot for “baseless slander against our people, who do their work honestly and professionally”, and challenged Mudryk to provide evidence for his claims, including that he had received little support from the federation during the doping case.

He added: “I know the people who have worked with the national team for many years. I trust them, because throughout all this time there have been no incidents in the team that could make anyone doubt their professionalism.

“If Mykhailo has evidence to back up his words, let him provide it. If no such evidence exists, I expect him to publicly retract his accusations and apologise to the people whose honesty and professionalism he has called into question.”
