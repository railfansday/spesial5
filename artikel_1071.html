Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Leeds and the Premier League are launching an investigation into racist abuse and threats sent to Jayden Bogle’s family.

Bogle escaped a red card in the win over Tottenham after injuring Mathys Tel, with the Leeds full-back receiving the threats via social media.

His club have revealed they are looking to identify those who sent messages to the 26-year-old, whose family were threatened in one of the posts seen by Telegraph Sport after the 1-0 victory.

Leeds say they are “appalled at the disgusting, racist social-media abuse Jayden Bogle suffered” and will push for punishments to be taken.

The club added in a statement: “Leeds United condemns this behaviour in the strongest possible terms. There is no place for racism in football or society.

“The club will work with the Premier League and relevant authorities to identify the individuals and push for the strongest possible action to be taken against them.

“Jayden has the full and unconditional support of the club and his team-mates, and we all stand with him at this time. Nobody should ever be subjected to racist abuse.”

Daniel Farke, the Leeds head coach, described Bogle as the “softest player” in the Premier League who would never try to hurt an opponent.

Referee John Brooks did not issue a yellow card, with VAR Tony Harrington sticking with the on-field decision.

“I’m confirming the on-field decision of no red card. There’s a lack of speed going into the challenge and he’s stepping into the tackle. Confirming a reckless challenge,” Harrington was quoted on the big screen at Elland Road.

Tottenham will make a formal complaint to Pro Ref over the decision not to send off Bogle.

“I haven’t watched the scenes back so I can’t give you a clear opinion, out of the game it looked a handball but the scene with Jayden was not too far away from my bench and it looked a very unlucky coming together,” Farke said.

“Fingers crossed for Mathys that he is back soon, but it looked like there was not much speed in this action, but Jayden Bogle is one of the most fair players in the country, he would never injure or foul a player on purpose because he is such a fair character.”

Tottenham Hotspur’s purple patch of form ended with a worrying loss. Wearing their “Spurple” kit for the first time, Roberto De Zerbi highlighted a lack of character and personality from his players and refused to blame the defeat on referee John Brooks’s decision not to dismiss Jayden Bogle for leaving one of his players injured.

Spurs’ away strip is based on a 1990s design, but this performance was more Christian Gross than Jürgen Klinsmann or David Ginola. Leeds United created the better chances and eventually broke through with 11 minutes remaining when their substitutes combined for Noah Okafor to score the winning goal.

Good morning and welcome to live coverage of the Premier League match between Leeds United and Tottenham Hotspur at Elland Road. The home side, lions at night and mice in the morning, have the unenviable record of 21 top-flight matches with a kick-off time before 3pm without a win. All that extra TV moolah comes in handy but their home ground, a bearpit under the floodlights, is neutered when its denizens are yawning instead of being fired up by social lubricant.

Daniel Farke’s team started the campaign well but are now without a win in five, drawing three matches with teams beneath them in the table – Crystal Palace, Manchester United and Sunderland – and  losing to two above them, Arsenal and Bournemouth. In fairness they played well against the champions for an hour and were deservedly in the lead until Mikel Arteta turnd to his billionaire’s bench and changed the game. At Bournemouth last week they also made a decent start, had a goal disallowed for striking Dominic Calvert-Lewin’s arm and then James Trafford chucked one in.

They never recovered, found themselves increasingly bogged down when trying to unlock a low block and were mugged on the break in added time to lose 2-0. They are a robust side overly reliant on their wing-backs to create chances for their No 9 in the absence of the injured Noah Okafor who was unavailable to train with the team this week because of a groin injury but may be fit enough for the bench.

Spurs, by contrast, are buoyant following three wins on the bounce – over Coventry, Chelsea and Palace, two with clean sheets – that has taken them from 19th to ninth, three places above Leeds. They have won their last three visits to Elland Road, 0-4 under Antonio Conte, which proved to be the last match of Marcelo Bielsa’s glorious spell at the Peacocks, 1-4 under Ryan Mason, which sealed Leeds’ relegation, and 1-2 under Tomas Frank last year by virtue of goals from Mathys Tel and Mohammed Kudus as the home side struggled against their width back in the days before their switch to a back three.

Tel also scored in Spurs’ home game, a 1-1 draw in May, and then kicked Ethan Ampadu in the head to concede the penalty from which Leeds equalised. I wonder what kind of impact he will have today. Incidentally Roberto De Zerbi on his last visit to Leeds when manager of Brighton in 2022-23 had such a strop on the touchline when the home team equalised that he was within a gnat’s whisker of being sent off.
