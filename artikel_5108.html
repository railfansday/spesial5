Bitcoin’s rate-cut hopes face a $5 diesel problem in 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Cheaper crude may not bring the fuel-price relief Bitcoin needs: damaged refineries are keeping the 2027 diesel forecast above $5 a gallon.

The US Energy Information Administration raised its forecast for next year’s retail diesel price by 12%, reducing the fuel-cost relief it previously expected. That leaves less room for falling fuel costs to cool inflation and bring the lower interest rates that could help Bitcoin, even as the agency projects cheaper crude in 2027.

The EIA’s November 10 outlook puts the 2027 US diesel average at $5.03 a gallon, up from $4.49 in its October 6 forecast. That is an upward revision to the same annual forecast, rather than a 12% increase in observed pump prices.

Diesel is still projected to fall from a forecast $5.25 average in 2026. EIA also expects Brent crude to average $86 a barrel in 2027, down from a projected $97 this year. Inputs were finalized November 5, so the outlook does not specifically account for subsequent market events.

Cheaper crude does not guarantee a matching decline in finished-fuel costs. EIA assumes petroleum-product supply constraints will continue through the end of 2027, supporting wider crack spreads: the gap between refined-product prices and crude costs.

The agency attributes elevated refining margins to damaged refineries in the Middle East and Russia, which have reduced product exports. It expects disruptions to be concentrated early next year and constraints to ease thereafter, while warning that unplanned outages and deferred maintenance could disrupt refinery operations.

US refinery crude inputs are forecast at 16.5 million barrels a day in 2027, against 16.7 million this year, despite similar utilization. Running the available refineries hard therefore does not necessarily mean processing more crude.

Persistent fuel costs can reach prices beyond direct energy components. In her November 10 speech, Federal Reserve Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman warned that higher production and transportation costs could pass into consumer prices and keep core inflation elevated. She favors looking through temporary energy inflation when possible, while watching for broader price and expectations effects.

For Bitcoin, the connection runs through monetary policy and investors’ willingness to take risk. A 2023 IMF working paper found that US monetary tightening depresses a common crypto-price factor through a risk-taking channel, similar to equities. If fuel costs prolong underlying inflation and delay monetary easing, that channel could constrain Bitcoin risk appetite.

There are reasons that risk could fade. Bowman reported recent refined-product price retracement and signs of normalizing margins, and still expected core personal consumption expenditures inflation to move closer to 2% next year. November 9 Fed staff research found stronger post-pandemic oil-to-core transmission, but called the results suggestive and model-dependent; its authors said second-round effects had not yet materialized.
