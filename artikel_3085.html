Amazon's re:Invent Conference Starts Nov. 30. Here's What the Stock Did in the Month After the Last 10.

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The cloud giant's biggest week of the year is nearly here. Has the stock usually climbed after it?

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 20, 2026 at 8:52PM EST

Amazon (AMZN -4.10%) has a big week ahead. Its cloud computing arm, Amazon Web Services (AWS), hosts its yearly re:Invent conference in Las Vegas from Monday, Nov. 30, to Friday, Dec. 4. AWS calls re:Invent its flagship annual conference, and it's where the unit launches many new products every year.

It's tempting to think the stock might get a boost when AWS shows off what it's built. To find out, I checked how Amazon shares did in the month after each of the past 10 conferences, from 2016 through 2025, and compared that with the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%).

Shares trade near $257 as I write, up about 12% this year and well ahead of the S&P 500's rise of about 5%. Amazon now stands about 9% below its Aug. 3 record close of $284.02.

Not really. For every year, I measured from the close on the trading day before the conference started to the close a month after opening day, using the same window for the S&P 500.

Amazon stock rose in just four of the 10 windows. It beat the index in four of them, too. And its median return was a drop of around 0.7%, but the S&P 500's median was a gain of about 2.1%.

The best year was 2024, when Amazon climbed around 6% in the month after the conference while the index dropped about 3%.

The worst was 2022. Other factors can easily outweigh a week of product news, and that year the stock had already lost about 44% before the conference began. It slid another 12% in the month that followed, compared with a 6% decline in the S&P 500.

Last year was closer to a wash. After the event ran from Dec. 1 to Dec. 5, 2025, Amazon stock slipped about 1% for the month, while the index was about flat.

In short, there's no consistent post-conference rally in the record. Amazon has lagged the S&P 500 more often than not after re:Invent -- in six of those 10 windows.

The week of the conference hasn't been much better. Measured from the previous close to that Friday's close, Amazon stock fell during six of those 10 conference weeks.

The 2024 event makes the strongest case that AWS's news can matter. That year, AWS rolled out its Nova family of artificial intelligence (AI) models and made servers built with its Trainium2 AI chips available to customers. Amazon shares climbed every trading day that week and gained around 9%, while the S&P 500 added about 1%.

But last year's lineup was arguably just as big. AWS made servers built with its next-generation Trainium3 chips available and unveiled its Nova 2 models, and the stock dipped about 2% in the conference week.

What does seem to move Amazon stock is its quarterly reports. Shares surged around 15% on July 31, the day after Amazon released its second-quarter results. In one day, the stock moved more than it did in any of those 10 post-conference months.

That makes sense, because AWS is where most of Amazon's profit comes from.

In the third quarter of 2026, AWS posted $17.8 billion in operating income, up 55% year over year. This was around 63% of Amazon's $28.1 billion in total operating income for the period.

Plus AWS's sales growth keeps accelerating. Its revenue rose 39% in the third quarter, faster than the second quarter's 37% and almost twice the 20% growth AWS had a year earlier.

Of course, all this takes heavy spending. Amazon still expects around $220 billion in cash capital expenditures in 2026, and that could weigh on its free cash flow for a while. And the stock isn't cheap, at about 25 times earnings based on what analysts expect for 2027. But with AWS growing almost twice as fast as it was a year ago, I think the price is fair.

So, is the month after re:Invent a good time to own Amazon stock? Going by the last 10 years, not especially. After all, four weeks is a short window, and I wouldn't use the record as a reason to rush in before the conference or to wait till after it.

I'd consider buying Amazon shares for the growth AWS keeps posting every quarter. I just wouldn't time my purchase around Nov. 30.
