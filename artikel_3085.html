Genpact: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Genpact is accepting a two-point growth drag by dropping legacy accounts, a trade-off that tests how patient long-term shareholders must be.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 26, 2026 at 1:43AM EST

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 5, 2026, Genpact (G +3.12%) announced that full-year revenue growth will reach at least 7% as its automated artificial intelligence strategy, called Agentic Operations, takes hold. For long-term investors, the corporate pivot shifts the business away from labor-heavy services toward recurring software-like revenue streams.

Structural margin gains and rapid technology adoption position the enterprise for compounding long-term returns. Three things management said on the call stand out.

The company is accelerating its transition into Agentic Operations, expanding its total addressable market (TAM) -- the total revenue opportunity available -- and locking in higher-margin recurring contracts.

By shifting client contracts away from full-time equivalent, or FTE, labor billing toward outcome-based software arrangements, gross margin expanded to 36.9%.

Genpact is deliberately dropping legacy accounts that do not align with its technology strategy, creating a short-term two-percentage-point growth drag to secure long-term profitability.

Demand for automated enterprise workflows is expanding, lifting gross margin and building long-term contract backlog. Deep process intelligence and proprietary software assets provide structural advantages over legacy outsourcing peers. Management is watching execution speed and client rotation into recurring digital agreements.

Long-term performance depends on converting contract wins into durable recurring revenue while capturing market share across major global clients.
