Spurs win three in row for first time in 616 days to pile pressure on Sage

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

For the first time in 616 days, Tottenham Hotspur have won three Premier League matches in a row. Three different managers have been sacked in that time, and the mood in north London had plunged to grim lows, but finally there is a sense that something truly exciting is stirring at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium.

This victory over Crystal Palace was not as impressive as last week’s triumph at Chelsea, but it did not need to be. After Palace striker Jean-Philippe Mateta was sent off in the 13th minute, Spurs simply had to make their numerical advantage count. They did so with relative ease, taking complete control of possession and creating endless goalscoring opportunities.

Roberto De Zerbi’s team ended the night with 34 shots to Palace’s two and with 758 completed passes to Palace’s 149. The goals ultimately came from two defenders, Archie Gray and Jan Paul van Hecke, but there was a long list of Spurs players who might have scored here.

Did this defeat mark the end for Pierre Sage, the Palace manager? The Frenchman was only appointed in June but it has been a grisly start to the campaign. Palace have the worst defence in the league, have lost six of nine games and have not produced any truly encouraging performances in the Premier League.

Even in the two games Palace have won, against Fulham and Nottingham Forest, they were lucky to do so. The likes of Sean Dyche and Thomas Frank, both of whom would be strong candidates to replace Sage if Palace do pull the trigger, will no doubt have been keeping an eye on events here.

“I am really aware of the situation,” Sage said. “I don’t like this situation. [But] you can trust in me, I will never give up. I want to be the first on the pitch on Monday. I have the rage to train more. It’s not me to decide this thing [his future]. If I have one hour, I will give my best for one hour. If I have 10 years, I will give my best for 10 years.”

The decision for Palace chairman Steve Parish, then, is whether this result represented a continuation of the team’s problems or was an aberration caused by the early red card. It would be reasonable for Sage to claim this match was out of his hands from the moment Mateta lunged at Spurs captain Micky van de Ven, but such an argument might not carry much weight with Palace sitting 18th in the table.

The challenge was foolish in the extreme by Mateta, a player who was much more successful in exiting this game than he has been in his numerous attempts to leave Palace in recent transfer windows. At first glance it did not seem the worst tackle, but the replays were damning: Mateta led with his studs and made forceful contact with Van de Ven’s ankle.

It was an inexcusably brainless act, after just 13 minutes of play, and the same could be said of Palace’s defending from a right-wing corner a few minutes later. Mohammed Kudus swirled the delivery to the back post, where Gray was unmarked. Yes, Palace were defending with 10. But no, that does not justify the inability of Sage’s players to spot Gray. Even more embarrassingly, the Tottenham player was unmarked again from another corner shortly afterwards.

It was not the first time this season that Palace’s cause has been undermined by a red card. When they lost at home to Ipswich Town in September, Axel Disasi was sent off after 34 minutes. An absence of discipline? Perhaps. Sage might argue, though, that he is not responsible for his players demonstrating a lack of basic brainpower.

All of this was welcomed heartily by the Spurs fans, who celebrated Mateta’s dismissal as though it was a goal. Recent results have restored some of the enthusiasm in this corner of north London and the mood here was buoyant, even before Gray’s opener.

“Now we are playing as a team,” De Zerbi said. “Before the international break, we were playing as many good players but not as a team. We showed our maturity. We are improving.”

The second goal was always coming and it arrived in the 73rd minute, when Van Hecke attacked a wonderful cross by Mateus Fernandes. Palace goalkeeper Dean Henderson saved the first attempt but the ball dropped onto the head of Van Hecke, now seated in the six-yard box, and he happily nodded it home.

The new signings at Spurs endured a difficult first few weeks but there were more signs here that they are adjusting to De Zerbi’s system. Sandro Tonali was exceptional in midfield and teenage talent Luca Williams-Barnett also sparkled off the bench. “Tonali was unbelievable,” De Zerbi said. “We played with two players more: Tonali was double.”

Spurs face Leeds United, Ipswich Town, Sunderland and Fulham in their next four league games. The momentum could, and perhaps should, continue to build. Their optimism here contrasted sharply with the angst of Palace, and with the troubled look that was etched on Sage’s face.

“Very pleased for the performance, very tough game. You don’t know 11 [against 11] would be different game but Crystal Palace are very tough because they defend with a low block, no space and we showed we are a mature team. Maybe we could score more goals with more nastiness, but I am very happy because we created many chances. We need more goals for sure. It’s not enough, but we are improving a lot.

“I was very nervous before the game, during the game, after the red card, it’s always better playing with one player more. But today, against this team, they played with this behaviour, all behind the line of the ball, aggressive. Very tough game.”

When asked if Spurs are looking up the table, he replied: “We don’t forget the ambitions. We have to look behind what was the situation two weeks ago. In the last two years, two times 17th in the table, many games without a win in front of our fans. We have to stay focused. They are improving, increasing the level of performance.”

Speaking to Sky Sports, the Palace defender said: “Definitely was frustrating. It was one of the first tackles of the game. And we felt like we were doing well and it is unfortunate. We go again.

“The main message was don’t concede again, stay tight as a defence and try to hit them on the counter. That is the best plan when you’re down a man.

“Unfortunately, it didn’t happen today. We know what we can do as a team, as a back five, the whole unit. There were some positives in the games, but I can’t say much when you lose. We just have to go again.

“Hopefully, the next game we win. The main message is to stay together as a team. We know what we are capable of. It is staying confident and bouncing back.”
