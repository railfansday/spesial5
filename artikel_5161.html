Why CoinShares Just Sold All Its Ethereum and Half Its Solana

CoinShares, one of Europe’s largest digital-asset managers, has sold its entire corporate treasury holding of Ethereum and half of its Solana.

It’s a move that might look like a loss of conviction but is really about something more prosaic: funding a stock buyback.

The decision, disclosed Tuesday in the firm’s third-quarter results, freed up $19.3 million, comprising $16.6 million from the Ethereum sale and $2.7 million from offloading half its Solana.

That covers nearly half of the $40 million share-repurchase program the Jersey-based firm’s board approved in October, and the company was explicit that the proceeds are being “reallocating that capital towards share repurchases.”

CEO Jean-Marie Mognetti framed it as capital discipline rather than a bearish call. “We took the same approach to preparing for the share repurchase program,” he said, noting the sales give the company “the flexibility to act when conditions allow.”

In other words, CoinShares is converting idle treasury crypto into dry powder to buy back its own stock, which it can do under an authorization allowing repurchases of up to 25% of shares outstanding over five years.

The timing is notable. The sale lands during a brutal stretch for crypto markets. Bitcoin was trading around $63,200 on Wednesday, down from six-figure levels earlier in the cycle, while Ethereum sat near $1,770 and Solana around $74, both well off their recent highs.

Spot Ethereum ETFs, meanwhile, have bled cash for days, shedding $29.1 million on Nov. 17 to extend a multi-day outflow streak that has turned Decrypt’s ETF sentiment gauge “bearish.”

Those are precisely the risk-off conditions that have battered Bitcoin treasury firms and pressured the broader sector. For a company whose stock is poised to join the Russell indices in December, deploying capital into buybacks at depressed prices is a conventional play, even if the optics of a crypto manager selling crypto invite a second look.

In terms of scale, these were small corporate treasury positions, not client assets. CoinShares’ total assets under management recovered to $7.4 billion by quarter-end, up from $5.5 billion three months earlier, and its core business of digital-asset investment products is untouched by the treasury move.

The firm actually swung to a strong quarter, posting $39.6 million in net income as net inflows accelerated to $125 million and its lending and trading desks rebounded.

So the sale isn’t CoinShares souring on Ethereum or Solana, assets it still manages billions in on behalf of clients. It’s a treasury-management decision by a newly profitable, soon-to-be-index-listed company choosing to return capital to shareholders rather than let a modest crypto position sit on its balance sheet.
