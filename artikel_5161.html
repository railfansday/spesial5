Major Crypto-Stealing Exploit in JavaScript Emerges: Zoom, CNN, HSBC Affected

Malicious JavaScript code snippets were planted on the websites of 178 organizations.

The exploit originated inside Optimizely's Web Experimentation platform, a tool that many large companies use for A/B testing, which means showing users multiple versions of a page to determine which one is more effective.

Report URI estimates that on November 16 the code sat in 188 of the 1,611 Optimizely snippets it crawled, and the real number is most likely higher.

Among the websites that could become victims of the hack are some of the largest names in the world: Zoom, the BBC, CNN, Fox News, Salesforce, GitLab, IKEA, HSBC, Equifax, Air France-KLM, Ledger and Peacock. BleepingComputer confirms that the script loads on many of them.

Essentially, the script's goal was to swap a wallet address on the page for the attacker's one, capture card numbers, take screenshots of pages and more. It was a whole stack of tools used to steal personal data, wallets and other assets.

According to Report URI, the attacker's wallets received about $7,040 within the first nine hours. That is a drop in the ocean considering the names involved, though the losses from card skimming are unknown. A card skimmer was added later that day, and a hidden iframe tried to load an iOS exploit chain on older iPhones.

Optimizely confirmed that an unauthorized party had exploited a vulnerability in Experimentation to add malicious JavaScript to certain customer projects. How the flaw worked is yet to be confirmed.

If successful, it could become one of the largest supply-chain attacks we have ever witnessed. Considering the attack came from a third party, we might see one of the largest service migrations in the world, as none of the third-party services and libraries can be trusted nowadays if used through an API.

Cryptocurrency services delegate certain tasks to third-party providers all the time, so witnessing a similar situation in our field is more than a possibility.

For product owners: stay cautious and rethink the use of services prone to exploits. For users: stay vigilant and try not to put all your eggs in one basket.
