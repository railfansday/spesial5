Alisson’s brilliance takes the breath away to show way forward for Liverpool

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Andoni Iraola spun on his heels, turned his back to the action and began to bite his nails. Managers often envisage the worst outcome when the clock ticks down, and here, in the 93rd minute, Bournemouth had a corner.

He needn’t have worried. As the set-piece was swung in, Alisson Becker moved powerfully off his line and plucked the ball from the air, a catch that essentially confirmed Liverpool would leave Dorset with three points. It was a catch, too, that put the gloss on another impeccable personal afternoon.

When Iraola took charge at Anfield, one question he had to answer – given Bournemouth had conceded 167 goals in his three years in charge – was whether he could make Liverpool defensively resilient. This dour 1-0 success, though, proves he is taking them in the right direction: he is the first Liverpool manager since Joe Fagan in 1983 to start with five unbeaten league games.

Alexander Isak might have scored the goal that leaves Bournemouth winless in the Premier League but this success owed everything to Alisson’s enduring brilliance – a comment that also applies to Virgil van Dijk – the enormous promise of Jérémy Jacquet and the grit of Ronaldo Araújo.

You go to top level football matches hoping to see something that will take your breath away and how Alisson did that in the 17th minute with a reflex save from an Evanilson backheel, the shape of his body resembling a fielder in the slips diving to prevent a boundary.

It proved to be the foundation stone from which Liverpool were able to build and inflict Bournemouth’s first home defeat since January. There are plenty of [valid] questions to ask of Liverpool’s attack at present but, defensively, it is starting to come together.

“It was a really good fight,” Alisson said, in that self-effacing way of his. “But we have to improve a lot – on the ball and sometimes off the ball as well but we improved in the second half. We adapted to the opponent and that’s what we are doing.”

Any manager with a well-drilled defence stands a chance of improving and that is what Liverpool are doing. Only four weeks ago the discussion was about how easy it was for teams to play through their middle – remember that chaotic afternoon at Newcastle to start? – but that is changing swiftly.

Since Iraola replaced Jeremie Frimpong with Araújo at right-back, three consecutive Premier League clean sheets have followed. Araújo, the Uruguayan on loan from Barcelona, revels in doing the dirty work while Jacquet, the £60m signing from Rennes, oozes quality.

“He’s [Jacquet] very young and adapted really well to the Premier League,” Iraola said. “The first games, he was physically feeling the change of rhythm and I had to sub him. This was a step forward. He looked solid until the end. He’s in a good place physically and I hope he can continue his development.”

Don’t confuse positivity about one aspect of the team as being a guide to its collective health. There was always a chance this contest would become a cagey affair and, Evanilson’s chance aside, the opening 45 minutes were as flat as you could imagine.

Iraola again played his three £100m+ signings but Bradley Barcola is still adjusting to the physicality of English football and Florian Wirtz is still finding it difficult to impose himself on a game; patience with the German will not be infinite.

He would do worse than to look at Isak, who is now on a run of four goals in four league games and is influencing games in the manner Liverpool envisaged when they broke the British transfer record to sign him last August.

Isak had an unusual afternoon. His only shot in the first half almost took out some low-flying pigeons, a cross early in the second half was sliced so badly that the weekend hackers on the nearby Queens Park golf course would have felt a flood of sympathy but then he got his third opening.

Cody Gakpo, who could have been in Manchester City blue had the cards played out differently earlier this month, wriggled down the right flank and squeezed in a cross that Isak seized upon and turned in from six yards. Liverpool never looked back.

“A striker’s job is like this,” Iraola said, who badgered Isak persistently to press Bournemouth’s defence. “He had a couple of crosses that he did not hit properly or control. He has to help us to be closer to the box, to play in the opposition’s half and have more meaningful possession. And then if the team is playing there, he will receive rewards in terms of goals.”

One, ultimately, was enough to see off Bournemouth, whose start to the campaign is something of a riddle. They shouldn’t be anywhere near the relegation places but that is the reality and Thursday’s historic Europa League win in Real Sociedad couldn’t mask the home crowd’s frustration.

“Our attitude and effort is good,” head coach Marco Rose said. “But, at this level, small things and little details decide games.”

Speaking to Sky Sports after the match, Iraola said: “I’m obviously happy with the result because I know this is a difficult place to come, very difficult place to get three points. And we could see it, especially in the beginning of the game. In the first 20 minutes we weren’t at the level [required] and it cost us. We finished the first half better and in the second half we played better, but that beginning of the game has surprised us, not surprised us [because] we knew where we were coming but it [was] difficult for us.”

Jeremy Jacquet has made the kind of quietly authoritative start to his Liverpool career that showed why there was such excitement in February within the club when they completed his deal. So much focus on so many players but Jacquet has never been anything other 7.5/8 out of 10.

Having built his reputation creating a hard-working, well-drilled team rich in potential, Iraola is responsible for infusing those same characteristics into the most expensively assembled squad in Liverpool history.

His first six games demonstrate it is no quick fix. The Basque coach is the first Liverpool appointment to work with more than £700m of recent signings while being entitled to feel he has been dealt a bad hand.

Everything Iraola has said and done so far screams that he is searching for the right blend in his starting XI while having to eradicate negative traits, with and without the ball. It was telling that the excellent performance of his youngsters against Tottenham Hotspur in midweek offered the clearest hint of Iraola’s vision than anything we have seen with all the high-priced stars on the pitch.

Good afternoon and welcome to our live coverage of the Premier League match between Liverpool and Bournemouth as Andoni Iraola returns to the Vitality Stadium for the first time since his big move up north. The Basque propelled Bournemouth to new heights, guiding the club into Europe and his successor, Marco Rose, ensured they made a winning start to life on the Continent by defeating Real Sociedad in midweek.

Previewing Iraola’s Vitality Stadium return, Craig Beazley, host and creator of the Up The Cherries In All Departments podcast, said: “We’ve now got the Andoni Iraola return … Andoni is very much a legend really, there should be a statue of Sir Eddie Howe and Sir Andoni Iraola [outside the ground] as well.”

Across three seasons, Iraola guided Bournemouth to 12th, ninth and sixth in the Premier League, winning 47 of his 127 matches in charge (37 per cent).

Liverpool are still acclimatising to his exacting style of play and their results suggest as much. They are 10th in the table having drawn three and won one, impressing in flashes but struggling to maintain consistently productive final-third patterns and pressing structures. Deploying Ronald Araújo at right-back has proved effective, though, with Liverpool conceding just two goals in their last four matches.

The Cherries are searching for their first Premier League win of the season and have retained the 4-2-3-1 shape from the Iraola days, with Rose preferring a similarly vertical attacking strategy. They continue to leverage their strengths in transitions and in wide areas as Adrien Truffert rains in crosses for any one of Bournemouth’s exuberant frontline to pounce on.

Elsewhere, Manchester City welcome Sunderland, who also secured a Europa League win during the week, to the Etihad while in-form Leeds United host Crystal Palace knowing a win would lift them into the top four.
