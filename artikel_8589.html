Some big companies pay every dollar of tuition at their partner schools, even for part-time workers starting on day one, and others pay up to a set amount each year. Before you take a job for the benefit, check which schools and degrees it covers and whether you’d owe the money back if you leave.

Chosen for that benefit, an hourly job can be a debt-free route to a degree.

The benefits come in two kinds. The most generous pay all of the tuition, though only at partner schools and usually only for programs in the company’s catalog, and a few start the day you’re hired. The rest pay up to a set amount, often $5,250 a year, and you cover anything above it. At many of these employers, part-time workers qualify too.

Start with the catalog. A full-tuition benefit pays only at its partner schools and for the programs on its list, so find the degree you want there, and note how long you’d work before you can enroll.

Then ask whether you’d owe any of the money back if you left. Walmart and Starbucks don’t require you to stay after you finish, while McDonald’s makes managers who miss its transcript deadlines repay the money.

Last, make sure the degree pays. The Bureau of Labor Statistics’ Occupational Outlook Handbook lists each job’s median pay and the education it usually takes, so look up the job you want before you pick a program. If you already owe student loans, also ask whether the employer pays toward them, a separate benefit some companies offer.

Take a part-time Walmart cashier with no college credits who earns an online bachelor’s degree from Southern New Hampshire University, a Live Better U partner. Walmart’s benefit leaves her little or nothing to pay for tuition and required books. Paying her own way, she’d owe SNHU’s online rate of $354 a credit for 120 credits, or $42,480. Say she borrowed it at 6.52%, the rate Federal Student Aid set for new undergraduate loans for 2026-27, with no interest added while she’s in school. Repaid over 10 years, that’s about $483 a month, or about $15,450 in interest.

Before you accept an offer, ask the recruiter for the benefit’s written terms and its list of partner schools, then enroll in a covered program as soon as you’re eligible.
