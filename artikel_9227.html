Christie’s acquires British auction house Dreweatts

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Dreweatts will continue to operate from its Donnington Priory salesroom in Newbury, Berkshire

Christie's announced today it has acquired the British auction house Dreweatts, a move that confirms the enduring appeal of antiques and older collectibles, particularly as profits in other parts of the auction sector remain squeezed.

Both businesses will run as before, Christie’s confirms, with no staffing changes expected as a result of the deal. Dreweatts will retain its name and continue to be run “independently” by its chief executive, Henry House, and chairman Will Richards, according to a Christie's statement. Dreweatts will continue to operate from its salesroom at Donnington Priory in Newbury, Berkshire, with no sales expected to move to London.

Dreweatts, founded in 1759, is best known for country house and estate sales, and its specialist departments covering furniture, Old Masters, ceramics, jewellery, silver, clocks and wine. It holds around 70 sales a year. In 2025, it posted a record total auction turnover of £37.56m (£47.3m with fees)—a 26% increase from its 2024 results. Both Christie’s and Dreweatts are privately owned and do not disclose their profits.

A display ahead of the sale of Patrick and Amabel Lindsay’s collection at Dreweatts

The acquisition in some ways sees Christie’s backtrack its 2017 decision to close its South Kensington venue, known as Christie's South Kensington or CSK, which specialised in antiques and other traditional collecting categories. This decision was taken as the market for postwar and contemporary art was at its peak, and as the trade chased global expansion and billionaire tastes at the expense of localism and connoisseurship.

Today, auctions of Modern and postwar trophy lots might generate more glamour and headlines than high-volume and lower-value antiques sales, but the overheads for winning these prize consignments devours profit margins. Indeed, many leading industry figures criticised Christie’s decision to close CSK as a shortsighted one that haemorrhaged valuable strongholds of specialist knowledge and alienated potential new buyers.

Christie's president Bonnie Brennan says in a statement that Dreweatts possesses “particular strength in a part of the market that is highly complementary to our own. Most importantly, our clients collect across categories and at every price point, and together we have the opportunity to provide them with an even broader and more complete level of service.”

This marks the second major acquisition of a smaller auction house by Christie’s in recent years, following the 2024 buyout of the classic car vendors Gooding & Company.

Christie’s acquires Dreweatts from the advisory firm Gurr Johns, which bought the regional auction house in 2017 for £1.25m, as The Art Newspaper reported at the time. “I believe that Christie’s is the only business with the capacity, experience and shared culture to fulfil the further potential of Dreweatts,”, Gurr Johns chief told the Financial Times.
