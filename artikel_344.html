Instagram takes on AI copycat videos once and for all

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

As AI tools have gotten better at transforming videos, social media creators have faced a growing wave of accounts copying their work. From influencers watching their videos reappear with a different face, to comedians finding their jokes delivered by fictional performers, the copies can preserve the original creator's material while sending the attention elsewhere. (Read Mashable's feature on this phenomenon here.)

Now, Instagram is introducing tools to help creators catch those copies and decide what happens next.

On Tuesday, Nov. 17, Instagram head Adam Mosseri announced that the platform is beginning to roll out "Content Protection," as a "new way to see and review copies of your own videos in your insights on Instagram," giving creators the choice whether to hide them or add a link back to the original. Separately, Instagram is testing a way to detect stolen scripts and jokes, even when someone uses AI to change the visuals.

"We will not get everything right at first, but we’ll keep improving as fast as we can. More to come," he wrote on Threads.

For now, the feature remains a limited test with a small number of creators, a Meta spokesperson told The Hollywood Reporter, with plans to expand access. Mashable has reached out for a comment. The distinction matters for creators whose work has been repackaged this way, especially comedians like Sarah Purdum, who told Mashable in October that her material had been used by an AI account.

"It seems to be that female comedians work is being stolen and repurposed for sexy AI models," she said. "I feel annoyed that my work is being taken for the purpose of drawing in men."

Purdum also said that the comedy community had been discussing similar experiences, particularly among women. Other female comedians like Olivia Carter and Julia Jasunas, also had material appear in videos featuring AI performers.

Another account, "Tiffany Sloane," (account now disabled), posted a version of a routine by comedian George Brett Olson that also copied elements of his appearance and performance. Olson shared a side-by-side comparison showing similar outfits and gestures alongside the same joke. The account's Instagram highlights also linked to an OnlyFans page.

The broader Content Protection rollout gives eligible creators a way to respond when Instagram finds visual matches of their original videos. The tool specifically "alerts eligible creators when it finds visual matches of their original videos and lets them decide what happens next, including tracking a copy, adding a credit link or hiding it," a Meta spokesperson told THR.

To enroll, eligible creators will receive an invitation to enroll in their activity feed. Once enrolled, their new original videos are protected automatically, and they can manually select eligible older videos for protection.
