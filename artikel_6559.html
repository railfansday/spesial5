The Under-The-Radar Brands the Best Dressed List Loved in 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Vogue’s 2026 best dressed list certainly knows its way around a luxury label ambassadorship: Chanel swans, Saint Laurent savants, Dior devotees, Miu Miu girls and Prada protégés. But along the way, you’ll find that some of this year’s most thrilling moments for ‘stepping out’ and ‘stunning’ (as per the celebrity style news cycle) came courtesy of designers whose names aren’t quite so familiar. For this particularly discerning crowd, an emerging designer and an IYKYK label carries just a bit more sartorial currency.

The distinction between under-the-radar and fashion-crowd famous is becoming increasingly blurred. Designers like Conner Ives and Rachel Scott’s Diotima have developed considerable celebrity followings and their fair share of viral moments, while labels like Kallmeyer are hardly insider secrets anymore. The next generation, however, is hot on their heels.

Greta Lee, Hunter Schafer, and Whitney Peak have embraced the retro-futurist designs of Zane Li’s Lii, the award-winning label crafted from his unassuming and tiny Chinatown studio. Tessa Thompson, Ayo Edebiri, and Paloma Elsesser, meanwhile, found plenty to love in Giovanna Flores’s free-form silhouettes, pattern agnosticism, and deadstock materials. Elsewhere, Elle Fanning indulged in the character play of August Barron, Jenna Ortega put a young designer’s extraordinary corsetry craftsmanship on the red carpet, and John Malkovich looked to upcycled lace. Rosalía has made something of a habit of championing emerging talent, with the likes of Yasmina Atta and Laura Andraschko finding a place in her expansive, prima ballerina meets bad-girl Mary Magdalene wardrobe.

More than a passing fascination with the next big thing, this year’s best dressed list has fostered relationships with designers in ways that speak to their genuine curiosity and enthusiasm about clothes, the people making them, and the exciting places fashion is heading. Below, the under-the-radar brands this year’s best dressed loved.

Brooklyn-based Giovanna Flores is a fast favorite among Hollywood’s most adventurous dressers for her scavenger-chic designs. Her work is often made using deadstock and recycled textiles, and utilizes unexpected fabric combinations, patchworking, and inventive draping. Paloma Elsesser dropped her polka dot bodice and zebra-hemmed G.F. fit amid one fab Instagram dump, and Tessa Thompson mixed her prints with a sumptuous Bordeaux sash. Ayo Edebiri opted for a mismatched strawberry seed and gingham number, while Greta Lee wore some canary yellow velvet. (Both Edebiri and Lee are styled by Danielle Goldberg and hold Chanel and Dior ambassador titles respectively—Flores’s spliced-and-diced one-of-ones are a must-have.)

Zane Li’s Lii is a fashion-crowd secret hiding in plain sight; this year, he netted the LVMH Karl Lagerfeld Prize for Innovation and was a CFDA/Vogue Fashion Fund winner. Known for its architectural minimalism and precise construction in enchanting color combos, the New York label has struck a major chord. Greta Lee, again, was an early champion, most recently wearing an architectural two-toned blue dress with a sexy thigh-high slit. Whitney Peak wore a twisted tank and skirt look fresh from the spring 2027 runway—a collection that unspooled the emotional threads between girlhood and womanhood, reflected in dramatically skewed tops and miniskirts in scuba materials and wide-eyed shades of teal and tomato. Hunter Schafer has incorporated the retro-futurist designs into her ongoing Blade Runner 2099 press tour. Consider Lii a modern fixture in the wardrobes of Hollywood’s experimental minimalists.

Rosalía has spent the last year either en pointe or expounding the possibilities of a popstar’s wardrobe, oscillating between the ecclesiastical and the balletic. London-based, Nigerian-born designer Yasmina Atta, a Central Saint Martins graduate whose surrealist designs draw on African cinema, folklore, and subculture, was central to one of Rosalía’s most viral fashion moments. This was when she stepped out in New York in a custom Atta creation: a plunging, sheer bronze latex bodice, visible black lace lingerie, a dramatically dropped waist, and a frothy cream tutu, all cinched with studded belts—a look partly inspired by Edgar Degas’s Little Dancer Aged Fourteen. (Rosalía had also worn a slate-gray wool minidress by the designer earlier in the year.) For a musician whose Lux era has been defined by religious iconography and the discipline of dance, Atta’s fantastical yet fervent approach to fashion is a perfect fit.

Laura Andraschko’s hedonistic, subculture-informed designs also found a fan in Rosalía. The Berlin- and Vienna-raised Central Saint Martins graduate draws on various influences: indie sleaze and nightlife, aristocrats, Sloane Rangers, and Alpine bunny glam. Rosalía has worn the designer on several occasions: at the 2026 US Open, she chose an Andraschko T-shirt, while a previous Paris outing saw her in a khaki minidress from the fall 2025 Après Ski collection, with a dramatic furry hem and velvet-trimmed, off-the-shoulder neckline. More recently, of course, she had to pull on a tutu-like skirt. It’s feminine and anarchic and exactly right for Rosalía.

Jenna Ortega and her stylist Enrique Melendez spent last year crafting a deviant glam-goth wardrobe for the Wednesday star. This year, we’ve witnessed a subtle evolution into freer experiments with color and textiles. For a New York screening of Klara and the Sun, Ortega wore a sculptural creation by young designer Elias Matso, made from steel corset boning and 6,500 beads, all inspired by the colors of painter Marc Ranger. The Polimoda graduate’s approach to corsetry is theatrical and technically audacious, with references ranging from Bob Mackie to Charles James. The custom dress reportedly took more than 300 hours to make, and marked a major red carpet moment for a designer early in his career.

“This dress was a massive test of my free will and just how much I was willing to put towards something that did not have a guaranteed return,” Matso said. “It was scary, I felt crazy at times, I lost a lot of money, and now this object exists. After many blisters, rebuilds, failed trials, international shipping nightmares, and working on it in six different cities with so many helping hands it just exists, and that is wild.” A hand-crafting corsetier to know.

Elle Fanning is all about the Effie-coded whimsy right now, and Paris-based August Barron has proven a kindred spirit in this quest. Formerly known as All-In, the label is the brainchild of Benjamin Barron and Bror August Vestbø, whose designs play with heady ideas of domesticity, nostalgia, and the conventions of femininity. Fanning made a memorable appearance in a pale yellow striped polo shirt and a voluminous, bow-adorned tulle skirt from their fall 2026 Bedtime Story collection. The design duo has a talent for taking familiar clothes and articulating something thrilling throught them. Whether referencing American pageant girls, cabaret queens, housewives, gay icons Judy or a Marilyn, August Barron provides the much-needed glitch in the glamour for those brave enough to dial in.

London-based GOYAGOMA was the talk of London Fashion Week when the label made its debut earlier this year at designer incubator Fashion East (the same one that birthed Simone Rocha, Martine Rose, and Grace Wales Bonner.) Of course, Paloma Elsesser picked up his designs fast, and previous best dressed honoree A$AP Rocky is a fan. Founded by Bahamian designer Traiceline Pratt, a Central Saint Martins graduate who previously worked with Phoebe Philo and A-Cold-Wall, the label draws on memories of growing up in the Bahamas to reimagine everyday clothing through luxe fabrications, exaggerated proportions, and determined tailoring. Elsesser’s endorsement adds to an already growing list of admirers of the designer’s assuredly off-beat approach to the capsule wardrobe—expect to see it hit Dover Street Market soon, too.

Julie Kegels takes her bow during spring 2027.

Antwerp-born Julie Kegels has established herself as one of the most interrogative young voices in European fashion, and both Paloma Elsesser and Rosalia have taken notice. A graduate of Antwerp’s Royal Academy of Fine Arts who went on to work at Alaïa, Kegels launched her namesake label in 2024, exploring the tension between conventional feminine dressing and something more surreal. Her collections manipulate familiar silhouettes through draping and distortion, taking as much inspiration from working girls as she does from furniture. Winning the 2026 LVMH Prize has only accelerated her ascent—her signature thong socks are an even speedier way into the brand, or you could hold out for her plans to invest her LVMH prize money into the development of more shoes and bags.

Miyako Bellizzi styled and walked the spring 2027 show

Founded by Stella Ishii in 2013, with Lizzie Owens now leading design, New York label 6397 has built a quietly influential universe of lived-in separates and pieces that slyly riff on what “staples” can mean. Among its fans are Paloma Elsesser, Greta Lee, and Sarah Pidgeon. The brand’s debut runway show was styled by costume designer and best dressed lister Miyako Bellizzi, who also walked in the presentation. The downtown creative community has long embraced 6397, and this is the New York woman energy you just want to bottle.

Whitney Peak wears spring 2026, styled by Karla Welch.

London-based Torishéju takes an experimental and emotive approach to pattern-cutting and construction: trained as a menswear designer, Torishéju Dumi has built a sartorial world that plays with trompe l’oeil and other-century costuming made modern, combining subversive design twists and classic craft techniques. Whitney Peak, on The Hunger Games press tour, opted for a deconstructed scarlet red leather skirt brought together by sensual draping and thigh-high cutouts, paired with a sculpted black halter top. This is the one for those who like their fashion with a little friction.

Never underestimate the power of a great pair of trousers. And as Ayo Edebiri knows, the best off-duty outfits often hinge on an immaculate basic. Her choice? Still Kelly’s tailored black trousers worn with a vintage Björk T-shirt sourced by stylist Danielle Goldberg. The brand describes itself as a sartorial “slow burn.” “Building a world, making good products, it takes time,” founder Marc Kalman previously told Vogue. “I’m cool with that.”

Louisa Jacobson has never been one for fashion’s binaries. With stylist Edward Bowleg III, she delights in the push-and-pull of buttoned-up tailoring and daring exposure, masculine codes and feminine flourishes. Catalogue NYC is exactly the sort of emerging designer label that articulates that. Founded by former Peter Do designers Pablo Correa and An Nguye alongside Vincent Ho, the New York brand approaches familiar wardrobe pieces with an experimental eye, proposing new ways of wearing tailoring and dresses. On Jacobson, those ideas find an exquisite home.

A saucy sliver of toe and a beckoning, warped heel have become immediately identifiable Jude signatures. The Paris-based footwear label was founded in 2024 by Jurgita Dileviciute and Denitsa Bumbarova, and the duo’s subtly subversive designs take familiar shoe silhouettes and give them a deliciously peculiar twist—most notably with their barely-there peep toes. Zoë Kravitz is a fan of the Date pump, while Sarah Pidgeon favors the Date mule. Tracee Ellis Ross has amassed a well-curated collection of Jude footwear, from the sinuous Fame pump to the Haze sandal. It’s little wonder the label has found favor with fashion’s freakiest feet: Jude knows that even the smallest detail can demand a double take at the dogs.
