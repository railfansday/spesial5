Panthers injury report shows more help on the way for the defense

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Carolina Panthers are coming off a key win over NFC South rival New Orleans Saints. That puts them in the drivers seat for the NFC South title, and they get a chance to add some cushion to that when they host the floundering Baltimore Ravens on Sunday. The defense looks like they'll be getting more bodies back too.

Last week, we talked about the good news of Mike Jackson and Jonathon Brooks fully practicing. It turns out they only took scout team reps and should have been listed as limited participants. Both ended up out for the game against the Saints this past Sunday. Now they are both full participants again, but this time it's for real. Mike Jackson has already said he expects to play this Sunday, which is a huge add for a defense that seems to be turning the corner with new defensive coordinator Todd Wash. He might be joined by rookie Will Lee, who was starting to show flashes before he suffered his own muscle injury. That dup would obviously be a gargantuan upgrade over what the Panthers have had on the outside the last few weeks. No offense.

Bobby Okereke is still not ready to resume practicing, which makes one thing he's unlikely to play again this Sunday, but that could change later in the week. Neither of the Panthers starting tight ends practiced, but I don't recall either leaving the game on Sunday, so they may just be getting some rest to start the week. We'll have to monitor those guys as the week goes on.

The Ravens' injury report does a disservice to their actual injury woes. Lamar Jackson has had a nightmare season in terms of injuries. He's on injured reserve, and so is backup quarterback Tyler Huntley. The Ravens acquired Jacoby Brissett in an act of desperation, but that hasn't panned out so far. They're relying on newly acquired Cam Jurgens to center the offensive line after they lost several centers to injury over the first few weeks of the season. It turns out he just might not be any good anymore.
