Maikel Garcia-Bryce Miller trade gives fantasy rebound candidates new life

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Reports emerged Tuesday that the Royals were considering trading Maikel Garcia for pitching help. The Mariners made those reports outdated quickly by meeting those demands.

Per ESPN's Jeff Passan, Seattle acquired Garcia on Wednesday along with relief pitchers James McArthur and L.P. Langevin in exchange for starting pitcher Bryce Miller, plus additional arms Logan Evans and Jose Ferrer.

The change of scenery spices up a pivotal 2027 for both headliners. Does this make either Garcia or Miller a better fantasy baseball pick for 2027 (assuming we have a season)?

After a breakout 2025 season (.286/.351/.449, 16 HR, 23 steals, 81 runs, 74 RBI) in which he received AL MVP votes, Garcia endured several stints on the sidelines.

A hand muscle strain that cost him more than a month during the summer, and he had other shorter bouts with elbow cramps, an early-season right hamstring strain, and a late-summer left hamstring strain.

That would explain his crater-like drop in stolen bases to just five in 2026. When you invest in Garcia, you want that security with stolen bases and batting-average base.

Sure, that statistic as a whole is volatile, but as Garcia's Baseball Savant/Statcast chart spells out, his combination of elite Chase/Whiff/Strikeout percentages point to one of the batters likeliest to have the fewest strikeouts in the majors.

In addition, his top-level glove work would keep him in the lineup so he could eventually fall upward toward 20 homers, just like what happened in 2025.

This may prove difficult, though. Garcia already sits near the bottom of annual lists in barrel rate, which doesn't predict heavy power growth without supremely good luck or a rapid bulk-up from his 6-foot-1, 195-pound frame.

Moving to T-Mobile Park, the Park Factor for right-handed hitters overall, for doubles, and for triples sits toward the bottom of the 29-park rankings, if not the bottom; homer ratings remained almost the same.

Much of Garcia's ability to regain his fantasy juice sits on how quickly he'll return to elite steal totals. Heading into his age-27 season, Garcia carries 2,174 plate appearances, more than what's typically needed before a significant breakthrough happens.

Plus, nestled in the middle of the Mariners order likely will serve him better than helping to carry a cooked Kansas City order that wilted aside from Bobby Witt.

His rebound potential and improved supporting cast likely will justify a return to the top-100 overall picks in fantasy drafts, with a significant positional advantage of a stolen-base upside play at third base.

Miller is still trying to round back into his peak form following a 2025 marred with bone spurs and inflammation in his right (throwing) elbow that helped lead to a 5.68 ERA, a far cry from 2.94 in his breakout 2024.

Despite missing time with an oblique strain, Miller looked like he was turning a corner in the first half last season; he held a 2.18 ERA at the All-Star break.

He took an ugly turn in the second half, though, dragging himself to a 6.05 ERA and a temporary move to the bullpen. Perhaps endurance was a challenge following his elbow concerns the year before.

Swinging Strike%: 10.6 to career-best 11.6

Perhaps a contributor to his first-half success was an offseason treatment that proved successful without surgery, according to MLB.com's Daniel Kramer's report from February:

"But the Mariners right-hander discovered a solution early in the offseason that allowed him to avoid undergoing a procedure that he'd long envisioned as inevitable.

The answer was a Synvisc injection, gleaned from consultations with renowned orthopedist Dr. Keith Meister and applied to improve joint lubrication, dampen inflammation -- and above all, relieve pain.

"After I got that, that's when I started feeling a lot better," Miller said. "I thought for sure I was going to have to get the spurs shaved down and removed."

Perhaps better management of the spurs will lead to more sustained results -- meaning Miller could once again deliver as an often fantasy ace throughout the season.

Jose A. Ferrer (4.18 ERA, 3 saves, 13 holds last year) should remain on watch lists for saves and holds leagues.

Logan Evans might eventually compete for a rotation spot with the Royals, but his middling stuff should limit fantasy interest to spot starts and AL-only leagues.
