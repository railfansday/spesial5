3 quick takeaways from 49ers thriller win over Cowboys: There’s no such thing as defense

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The San Francisco 49ers beat the Dallas Cowboys 46-40 on Sunday in a high-scoring shootout that went down to the final minutes of the fourth quarter.

Defense was optional in this one, as the 49ers compiled 584 total yards, while the Cowboys had 439. There were 36 points in the fourth quarter alone, as San Francisco allowed George Pickens to go for 11 catches, 145 yards, and three touchdowns.

Still, their elite offense paved the way to another victory, and the 49ers remain atop the NFC at 8-1 with a key win going into a crucial stretch to begin the second half of the season.

Here are three quick takeaways from the 49ers win over the Cowboys on Sunday.

The 49ers did not have the prettiest game by any means on Sunday, especially on defense. They had 11 total penalties as a team for 100 yards, while Brock Purdy was sacked five times, as protection was the leakiest it had been all season long, specifically on the interior.

Heading into the game, it was expected that the 49ers would try to establish themselves on the ground against the No. 31 run defense, but they really struggled to run the ball early, as Christian McCaffrey had just 13 yards on his first six carries.

The run game didn't really get going, aside from two big runs. But did it matter? Not at all, because Kyle Shanahan, Brock Purdy, and the 49ers offense were in their bag with another incredibly efficient performance.

The Cowboys have been a pass funnel all year long, and Brock Purdy enjoyed another MVP-like performance with 389 yards and three touchdowns, completing 22 of 28 passes. He was incredible, playing mistake-free football while extending a number of plays that resulted in huge explosives for the 49ers.

San Francisco averaged a whopping 9.4 yards per play. They converted 26 first downs. They were 6 of 11 on third downs. And they had a number of timely calls, such as Deebo Samuel's 70-yard house call on an end around that had Dallas completely fooled.

Additionally, I thought the 49ers ran hard on the ground throughout the game, relying on both Christian McCaffrey and Kaelon Black, who combined for 115 yards on 24 carries.

McCaffrey was unguardable as a receiver and George Kittle regularly beat safety Caleb Downs for another big day. The 49ers won the way they knew best: efficiency. That was able to overcome their penalties and negative plays en route to 46 points.

Deommodore Lenoir had a really rough game going up against George Pickens and CeeDee Lamb. Pickens did whatever he wanted to Lenoir, catching eight balls for 126 yards and two touchdowns on San Francisco's top corner. Lamb also had a late touchdown in the red zone on Lenoir.

San Francisco couldn't cover anyone. Ryan Flournoy caught six of seven passes for 71 yards. Pickens had his monster game. And Lamb came on late for seven catches, 72 yards, and a touchdown. And the damage could've been even worse if not for three Pickens drops.

It was also an interesting day, game plan-wise. Teams have beaten the Cowboys offense by blitzing Dak Prescott. The 49ers blitzed on only 18.5 percent of dropbacks on Sunday, but Prescott was 8 of 10 for 144 yards on the 10 dropbacks he was blitzed.

For reference, Prescott was blitzed 51.1 percent of the time in a Week 3 loss to Baltimore. In a Week 5 loss to Tampa Bay, it was 52.4 percent. In Week 6 against Green Bay, it was 39.3 percent. In Week 7 against Philadelphia, it was 42.9 percent. In Week 8 against Arizona, it was 39.5 percent.

Yes, Prescott had success against the blitz in a limited sample, but the 49ers' game plan on defense did not work on Sunday in their worst defensive game of the season.

The 49ers were able to effectively stop the run on Sunday, but they were not able to get enough pressure on the quarterback, tying into the last point. Prescott averaged a 2.82-second time to throw on Sunday and was sacked just once for zero yards.

San Francisco got pressure on just 12 of 51 dropbacks. Not one player had over a 10 percent pass-rush win rate, according to PFF, even though Mykel Williams had four pressures and Nick Bosa had three. The secondary was a bigger part of the issue on Sunday, but the 49ers needed to generate more pressure against Prescott, who was quite comfortable in the pocket.

Moreover, if he did get pressure, Prescott was able to scramble a couple of times late in the game to extend drives.

Now, this is an elite offense with two very talented pass catchers, and San Francisco won't regularly face an opponent with such a good offense, but the 49ers have some questions to answer as they try to figure out their pass rush with Nick Bosa and DeForest Buckner in the fold now.
