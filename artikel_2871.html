Kings revert to struggling ways vs. short-handed teams after signs of promise

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Kings revert to struggling ways vs. short-handed teams after signs of promise originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

It appeared the Sacramento Kings had turned a corner in their miserable season last weekend. Then they reverted.

They had a spirited performance in a loss to the West-leading Los Angeles Lakers last Friday, with Zach LaVine just missing a game-winning 3-pointer at the buzzer. Then they beat the struggling Boston Celtics on Saturday while limiting one of the best teams in the East to 34-percent shooting overall.

The difference in intensity from Sacramento in those two games jumped off the floor, and it looked like the Kings might have been pulling out of their early-season malaise.

Fast forward to Wednesday, and the Kings were back to looking like a team headed straight to the draft lottery's relegation zone with one of the NBA's three worst records.

The Kings lost Monday to the San Antonio Spurs while they were missing three typical starters, including former Sacramento point guard De'Aaron Fox and all-world center Victor Wembanyama.

The Kings lost by 16. Then they traveled down Interstate 80 to San Francisco and fell to the Stephen Curry-less Golden State Warriors on Wednesday, 129-110, after falling behind by 25 points in the first quarter.

Sacramento is now 2-16 at Thanksgiving. And beyond getting second-year center Maxime Raynaud back from a knee injury soon, reinforcements don't appear to be coming.

"It's tough. We've dug ourselves a super deep hole," Keegan Murray said Wednesday. "So obviously, it's tough to figure out where my head's at. We just gotta figure out how to win games.

Sacramento outscored Golden State by 13 points combined through the second and third quarters. They cut a 29-point lead to four in the third, but the Warriors cruised to the win anyway thanks largely to shooting 46 percent from 3-point range, taking advantage of the Kings' terrible perimeter defense.

"Obviously, it's tough to win in this league with our team right now; we haven't figured that out at all," Murray said. "We can't put four quarters together to put ourselves in that position, so mindset-wise, we just got to take these couple days and just figure it out."

The Kings didn't have a counterpunch to the Warriors' decision to blitz point guard Darius Acuff Jr. with double teams. The talented rookie finished with 22 points but only had four in the first half when the game got away. He finished with another inefficient shooting night, making just nine of 24 shots.

Acuff has been just about the only bright spot from an otherwise terrible Kings season to date. But as much promise as he's shown, the 20-year-old is going to learn things the hard way, like how to manage opponents double-teaming him to force Sacramento's secondary and tertiary options to beat them.

"I think more than anything, you're going to need three outlets when you're going to blitz Darius," Kings coach Doug Christie said. "… I think we were a little late on everything in that first half, even on rotations. We have to be aggressive, and that was something that we went over in practice. It's something that we looked at in film and talked to them about."

The Kings rank dead last in offensive rating at 107.1 and dead last in defensive rating at 119.6.

They have a difficult game Sunday, when they host the 14-5 Minnesota Timberwolves before completing the home back-to-back against the 5-14 New Orleans Pelicans on Monday.

Wednesday continued what seems to be a years-long trend of the Kings struggling against teams who are missing their best players. And that was the case Monday and Wednesday by losing to the Spurs and Warriors with Wembanyama and Curry sidelined.

De'Anthony Melton torched the Kings by making eight of 12 from deep, leading to 24 points, while Gui Santos added 22 off the bench. Golden State won the battle of bench scoring, 61-19.

"San Antonio and Golden State both play good basketball with their guys moving the ball, getting good looks," Murray said. "Golden State shoots more threes without Steph, and it's more of just kick off and catch and shoot. And obviously, they have guys like Melton get hot. Teams like that have won in the past that are short-handed — just know how to win games."
