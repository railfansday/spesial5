Previewing No. 4 Virginia basketball’s matchup against Loyola Maryland

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

With the Battle 4 Atlantis just around the corner, No. 4 Virginia (6-0) has one more game before facing a considerably tougher stretch of its non-conference schedule.

The 'Hoos welcome Loyola Maryland (1-4) to John Paul Jones Arena on Friday night, fresh off an 85-65 victory over the Old Dominion Monarchs. Virginia was once again led by one of the country's best players, Thijs De Ridder, on Tuesday night, logging his third game with 20 points or more and his second double-double of the short season.

While the Virginia Cavaliers have yet to lose this season, Loyola (MD) has experienced a difficult opening stretch. The Greyhounds' lone victory came against Division III Washington College.

With Marquette, Xavier, and Kentucky awaiting the Cavaliers over the next two weeks, and a date with the #3 UConn Huskies in late December, Friday's contest offers a chance to address a few weaknesses before the stakes get higher.

Here are three things to watch on Friday evening.

Loyola Maryland hasn't been particularly efficient offensively this season, but the Greyhounds certainly aren't afraid to shoot.

Over five games, Loyola has attempted almost 30 three-pointers per contest, ranking among the top 50 teams nationally in that category. That willingness to shoot was on full display against Washington College, when the Greyhounds knocked down a school-record 19 three-pointers on 42 attempts.

Junior guard Leo Ricketts has been the centerpiece of that perimeter attack. The junior college transfer leads Loyola with 17.4 points per game and is shooting 37.8% from beyond the arc.

Sophomore point guard Carter Berg-McLean has also been a key part of Loyola's offense, averaging 5.4 assists per game while creating opportunities for the Greyhounds' perimeter shooters.

For Virginia, the main goal will be preventing Loyola from getting comfortable on the perimeter. The Cavaliers have enough athleticism to disrupt passing lanes and contest outside shots, but they'll need to communicate defensively against an opponent that relies so heavily on the three-point shot. It only takes a few defensive lapses for an underdog to start believing.

Despite having an average listed height of just under 6-foot-4, the Greyhounds are grabbing about 42 rebounds per game.

Sophomore forward D'Andre Harrison has been particularly impressive, recording 18 rebounds against Morgan State last weekend. Six-foot-six forward Sebastien Emenalo is the Greyhounds' leading rebounder, averaging 8.3 rebounds per game

The Cavaliers have a considerable size advantage with Johann Grünloh, Thijs De Ridder, and the rest of their frontcourt. Against Old Dominion, Virginia outrebounded the Monarchs 41-33, with De Ridder collecting 10 rebounds and Grünloh adding another 10. Virginia has yet to be outrebounded in their first six games, and you can expect that statement to remain true after this one.

Perhaps the most encouraging development from Virginia's victory over Old Dominion was Elijah Gertrude's performance. The junior guard scored a career-high 14 points in just 15 minutes, knocking down all three of his attempts from beyond the arc and providing a spark off the bench that Virginia fans saw from Jacari White last season.

Gertrude's performance so far this season has been a breath of fresh air for those concerned about the starting five's workload and the lack of bench contribution. While his production has been more than enough to keep Virginia undefeated, developing reliable contributions from the second unit will become increasingly important as the schedule intensifies.

Personally, I would like to see more minutes from Jan Vide, Jurian Dixon, and Favor Ibe. Getting solid minutes from a backup point guard, an offensive-minded wing off the bench, and a backup center is crucial to making a deep run in the tournament.

Friday's matchup offers another chance for these guys to establish themselves if they get the minutes.
