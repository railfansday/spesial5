Warriors claim first-ever CCC volleyball tourney win

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Nov. 11—In spite of compiling a winning overall record nearly every season, the Lewis-Clark State Warriors had dealt with something of a postseason curse in volleyball since joining the Cascade Collegiate Conference at the start of this decade.

On Tuesday at the P1FCU Activity Center in Lewiston, the curse was lifted in style. LC State maintained command from start to finish as it achieved its first-ever CCC tournament match win: a 25-18, 25-17, 25-18 sweep of the visiting Walla Walla Wolves.

"We distributed the ball really well, so we had multiple players attacking efficiently," said LC State coach Katie Palmer, whose team compiled a .340 attack percentage on the day.

Next up for fifth-seeded LC State (19-8) is a showdown with fourth-seeded Southern Oregon at 5 p.m. on Thursday at College of Idaho.

Balance was the name of the game for the Warriors on Tuesday, with no individual player putting out mammoth statistics, but many making their mark. Mikale Roy led the way in kills with nine, while Addison Wilson and season kill-leader Taylor Boyce added eight apiece.

"I think we talked about it the whole season — 'There's no better time to win than now,'" said Roy, who also produced a highlight defensive play when she made a diving one-handed "pancake" save in a rally the Warriors went on to win. "I think that was our mindset coming into this, and after coming off a few losses, there was no better time to win. I think we played pretty clean tonight; I think it all comes down to how we played as a unit, and I've got to give it to my setters and my passers."

Speaking of setters, the Warriors enjoyed 17 and 20 assists, respectively, from Kendall Focht and Teagan Scott, who Palmer dubbed the "Mario and Luigi" of the team for "doing a really nice job working together, running our offense and making sure all cylinders are firing."

The trend of parity continued in the back row, where Kadence Moore made nine digs, Ella Stosich had seven and Makenzie Stout added six.

Lewiston High alum Jordynn Albright delivered the Warriors' lone solo block of the day, reflecting a Walla Walla spike back across the net so quickly that it bounced off an opponent's head. Albright would also chip in two kills and three digs.

"The community always shows up and has always shown up for me, so it's awesome to see that transfer into college," said the sophomore middle blocker, who became familiar with the Activity Center back in her high school days. "We have a really big arena here, and it's just awesome to get to play in it again."

Visible community support included a student section whose members stood throughout the match sporting red-and-blue tassles, with the school's new wolverine mascot prominent in their midst. Cardboard likenesses of two players looked on from the front row of the bleachers, while a four-woman cheer squad with white pom-poms kept energy high during timeouts.

The victory was the Warriors' third over Walla Walla (5-18) this fall, and snapped a four-match slump from the end of the regular season.

LC State and upcoming opponent SOU (21-9) have gone 1-1 against each other this year, splitting a pair of five-set slugfests; LC dropped the first meeting on Sept. 4 at the Raiders' home court in both teams' conference debut, but got revenge at the Activity Center on Oct. 10. If home court advantage has proven decisive so far, the move to a neutral site for their postseason encounter could leave things particularly up-in-the-air.

"We just have to continue being confident and having a balanced offense as well as a really productive block — what we did tonight," Palmer said of her team's approach to Thursday's match. "We're looking forward to that."
