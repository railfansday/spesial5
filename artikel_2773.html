Avalanche Mailbag 1.0: Road Struggles, Bednar’s Approach & Can Avs Actually Get Kucherov?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Given overall road struggles and playing from behind at the start of games, is the aging curve of the team showing its signs earlier than expected?

I wouldn't say it's an aging curve as much as it is a team loaded with veterans who probably don't have an issue with giving up the first goal because they know they can come back. Which, in a way, relates to the aging curve of the overall roster.

I also think it's one of the many weird things they're working through this season after an inconsistent start to the year. This has been a different experience for a lot of guys in the sense that they're not used to losing games in the manner in which they're losing as often as it's already happened. They're not used to always giving up the first goal, and they might not be used to dominating teams on some nights and getting absolutely dominated in the same way the next night.

They've turned the corner a little bit on some of these and the Montreal game was a nice start. But I'd like to see them continue down that path. I don't blame it on age or players slowing down.

Head coach Jared Bednar does not come across as the type of coach who lays into his players, taking the approach of the team being adults and professionals. What should his messaging to the team be to motivate them to show commitment and effort?

He's certainly not the type, but I get the sense that he values meetings, whether in a group or one-on-one, to remind players what they're capable of and what's expected of them. He's a great motivator, and his approach has likely changed over the years as he's developed chemistry with this group. I would imagine nowadays it's more "hey Cale, you know you can do better and be better and here's where I think you can improve," more than anything else.

That's the type of stuff that I think this group needs. Sometimes it's a nudge that, hey, there's a reason why you're highly regarded as one of the league's best superstars. Or maybe to another player, it's hey, there's a reason why we wanted you and stuck with you.

If you were Joe Sakic, what would you consider offering for Nikita Kucherov – for both trade assets and contract extension?

Like everybody else, I played the armchair GM game with Nikita Kucherov as soon as it became known that he wanted out. I will say, it's looking more and more likely that he's going to test the market on July 1. But let's have some fun with this question:

The best I could land on was some type of deal centered around Martin Necas. I still don't know how I'd structure the deal and if it would even make the Avs better, given how much more they'd have to cut from their roster just to make the numbers work. But when the Avs traded Mikko Rantanen, part of the season why they got Necas was because he was the best player available who they believed could give you nearly the same production but is also younger and makes a lot less (at the time).

That's sort of the same thought process I'd have if I were the Tampa Bay Lightning now. Should we get someone in their prime who just put up 100 points and is on pace to do it again? Or should we (for example) trade for the Montreal Canadiens' best center prospect who, in a couple of years, could end up being our 1C for a long time? But there's also no guarantee he ever gets there?

As for salary, this is the part where the whole idea falls apart. I just can't find a way for it to make sense for the Avs, given that Kucherov is probably going to make close to Cale Makar money. If Colorado sheds money and fills in around the edges, I'd suspect Kucherov would come in at $18 million. It just doesn't seem feasible.
