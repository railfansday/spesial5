Budget carrier Norse Atlantic falls 78% after warning its finances are ‘critical’ amid soaring fuel costs

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Norse Atlantic shares plummeted Wednesday after it revealed the company is in a "critical financial situation" as soaring fuel costs amid the Iran war strain budget carriers during the winter season.

The Norwegian low-cost carrier said its situation is dependent on securing financing for its short-term liquidity needs.

The company has had discussions about a potential strategic takeover as well as other options including ACMI contracts, which refer to short-term capacity leasing.

"Norse is also engaged in discussions with selected parties on short term financing, however, there can be no assurances that such discussions will result in a viable solution," it added.

The start of the U.S.-Iran war on Feb. 28 sent jet fuel prices soaring, putting pressure on airlines around the world. Prices are now up nearly 72% to $155 per barrel year-on-year as of the week ending Nov. 20, according to the International Air Transport Association's Jet Fuel Price Monitor.

Norse, which offers cheap long-haul flights between Europe, North America, Africa, and Asia, said in October that it was forced to fly with reduced capacity that month, due to "prolonged high fuel prices."

Norse Atlantic's CEO Eivind Roald told CNBC in May that he was not "overly concerned" about access to fuel and said the airline didn't have insight that the crisis would have an immediate impact.

"Fuel price rose by more than 100% and the beginning of March we had already sold most of our seats for March so it was difficult to overcompensate with higher prices for those few seats left," Roald said.

The airline had cancelled all flights to and from Los Angeles at the time due to the cost of jet fuel, impacting over 150,000 passengers. Roald also said Norse cancelled more than 700 flights in total, which impacted more than 240,000 passengers.

The company said in August that geopolitical disruptions and higher jet fuel costs hit profitability in the first half of the year. Higher fuel costs, lower aircraft use, and operational disruptions led to an $8.4 million second-quarter loss, measured before interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization, and rent.

EasyJet, another low-cost airline impacted by high fuel costs, reported Wednesday that profit before tax slumped 89% to £71 million ($92.7 million) for the year ending in September 2026. Revenue growth was unable to offset the impact of an 11% increase in fuel costs due to the Middle East conflict, the company said.

Earlier this year, Ryanair's CEO Michael O'Leary told CNBC that other European carriers would struggle if jet fuel prices continued to soar. "I think there will be failures."

"If it continues at $150 a barrel into July, August, September, then you'll see European airlines fail and that, in the medium term, would probably be good for Ryanair's business."

Meanwhile, Ryanair's chief financial officer Neil Sorahan told CNBC in May: "I think we will see some of the weaker carriers who were already struggling before the war possibly go to the wall in the winter."

Several smaller European airlines have stopped flying this winter. Slovakia's AirExplore ceased operations, Ireland's Fly4 entered provisional liquidation, and Spain's AlbaStar suspended flights while seeking creditor protection.

Spain's Plus Ultra has also suspended all flights while it restructures, though it has not confirmed a permanent closure.
