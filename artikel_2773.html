'I'm a running back': Versatile Michael Terry III emerges as ground threat for No. 5 Texas

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

During the middle of the 2025 season, the Texas football team needed someone to help it prepare for Georgia's ground game.

After weighing its options, Texas settled on Michael Terry III. Terry was listed on the roster as a wide receiver, but the then-freshman had played multiple positions in high school (he was even a punter at Alamo Heights). The 6-foot-3, 216-pound Terry was rated by both ESPN and On3 as the top athlete prospect in the 2025 recruiting cycle, so Texas decided to see if he could lead the scout team's rushing attack.

During that week, Texas coach Steve Sarkisian was approached by some of his defensive assistants with a suggestion to take a closer look at Terry's film. Sarkisian recalled being told that "this guy's a problem. He's a problem at running back." Terry later said he was just trying to have fun and beat the Longhorns' first-team defense during that scout team stint, but his effort was still remembered by his teammates a year later.

"Well, let me tell you my shoulder was hurting," Texas defensive lineman Hero Kanu recently said.

Texas Longhorns quarterback Arch Manning (16) takes photos with fans after the Longhorns' 45-3 win over the Arkansas Razorbacks at Royal-Memorial Stadium in Austin, Nov. 21, 2026. (Sara Diggins/Austin American-Statesman)

While seated next to Kanu, safety Jelani McDonald laughed and added that "my neck was hurting, my body was getting a little sore during the week. (I told him) we've got a game on Saturday now, you can't be doing all this hard running."

So Texas began to transition Terry into the offensive backfield, which was a move that didn't immediately pay dividends. As a freshman, he played only 13 offensive snaps, according to Pro Football Focus. His lone rushing attempt came in the second quarter of the Citrus Bowl.

Recently, though, Terry has had a bigger impact as No. 5 Texas (9-2, 6-2 SEC) has attempted to restock its running backs depth following a season-ending injury to Hollywood Smothers. Terry has gotten at least four offensive touches in each of UT's last five games. And during Saturday's 45-3 win over Arkansas, he capped the first drive of the second half with a 1-yard touchdown run.

"Losing Hollywood was tough on us, but the byproduct of that has been Michael Terry's continued to raise his game," Sarkisian said.

For the season, Terry has rushed 36 times for 170 yards and two touchdowns. And as he earned more playing time this month, Texas found a variety of ways to use him.

Texas Longhorns head coach Steve Sarkisian takes in the field as the team warms up ahead of the Longhorns' game against the Arkansas Razorbacks at Royal-Memorial Stadium in Austin, Nov. 21, 2026. (Sara Diggins/Austin American-Statesman)

At Missouri on Nov. 7, Terry powered his way to a first down on a third-and-3 attempt to keep a scoring drive alive. During the opening minutes of the LSU game on Nov. 14, Terry motioned out wide ahead of a 14-yard catch before he returned to the backfield on the next snap and ran for 3 yards. In the Arkansas game, Terry cut through a hole and ran untouched into the end zone where he hit the viral "Bend" dance in celebration.

With that versatility, it may be fair to ask Terry how he now views himself. Is he a running back? An out-of-position receiver? Or is he simply an athlete?

"I say I'm a running back primarily," Terry said. "I'm still an athlete. I can still catch the ball, run the ball, throw the ball a little bit, but I say primarily I'm a running back."

While Smothers and Raleek Brown have both had their moments, Texas is averaging just 127.5 rushing yards per game. That ranks 110th nationally and 13th in the SEC. The annual statistics in the UT record book date back to the 1950 season, and none of the previous 75 seasons featured a season with a rushing average of less than 135 yards per game.

But the emergence of Terry has given Texas an additional weapon as it prepares for this week's regular-season finale at Texas A&M (4-7, 1-7). At 137.9 yards per game, A&M has the SEC's fourth-worst rushing defense. The Aggies have allowed a 100-yard rusher in each of their last two games against Texas.
