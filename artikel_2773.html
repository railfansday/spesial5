Cowboys changing things up in search of consistency vs. Titans

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Both the Dallas Cowboys, and their closest division rival this season, the New York Giants, host opponents from the AFC South this weekend. If the Cowboys were even living up to half of the expectations of this season, this Week 11 schedule would look like a golden opportunity to separate from the Giants, as they face the 7-2 Jacksonville Jaguars while the Cowboys have the 1-8 Tennessee Titans.

Instead, the Cowboys already blew an opportunity to win in the same week the Giants lost against the San Francisco 49ers. Even if the Giants do lose as home underdogs to the Jaguars, the Cowboys have invited enough added drama this week with a change of defensive play-callers to make the Titans feel like another handful of an opponent.

This is especially true considering how close both other games against AFC South foes have been this year, both in the win column, but neither convincing. Getting a convincing win is something Cowboys fans simply do not have faith in their team doing at all right now, and not something the Titans have given away easily despite struggling under the new coaching of Robert Saleh and offensive coordinator Brian Daboll.

Hope for the playoffs will still feel thin even with a win Dallas is expected to get against these Titans. The Cowboys have made their bed with this roster and coaching staff and will have to withstand however many embarrassing losses can come from it, with the Titans having the potential to be the worst of 2026.

It is time for our weekly look at how the Cowboys avoid this loss and go into Thanksgiving with a chance at a winning streak again, or what could lead to the latest AT&T Stadium embarrassment after all.

Every other week for the Cowboys passing attack seems to feel like a CeeDee Lamb feature game right now, with Lamb having a big say in the win over the Colts in week nine, but it was George Pickens' best game of the season with three touchdowns in week ten agaisnt the 49ers.

The fact we are still waiting into late November for a game where both Lamb and Pickens are established in a game plan and get going at the same time is a horrible sign for play-caller Brian Schottenheimer. Beating bad AFC teams is one thing he's proven capable of though, and if now isn't the time to see both Lamb and Pickens unleashed downfield, it may never come.

The Titans defense is effectively tied with the Las Vegas Raiders for worst in the league in yards per passing attempt allowed. All three of their cornerbacks that led the team in snaps last week against the Jaguars are new to the team this season – Cor'Dale Flott, Alontae Taylor, and Joshua Williams.

Tennessee is also the fourth worst team at getting to the quarterback this season with 14 sacks, just one more than the 49ers team the Cowboys offense scored 40 points against last week. The Cowboys defense is more of an unknown than ever before coming into this game after already underperforming all season, so the adage that Dallas can win this game by having Dak Prescott simply outplay Cam Ward by a big margin is not a reliable one.

That said, another huge Prescott game against this opponent where he's able to effectively get the ball deep to both of his top receivers should be enough to get in the win column. The Cowboys have beaten Robert Saleh defenses consistently before, and will have to do so again to keep faint NFC East hopes alive this Sunday.

The football gods always seem to work in mysterious ways, in the sense they will test the very thing a team is struggling with in the blink of an eye. The Cowboys reasoning for having the veteran coach Mike Pettine take over defensive play-calling from rookie Christian Parker has more layers than an onion. One of the more sound reasons though is that the team is heavily invested financially in their front seven, where Quinnen Williams has looked more and more like a cornerstone player to build around each week in his first full season with the team.

Pettine will likely look to run the defense with a front-centric mindset, focusing on this front seven first and then the backend, where as Parker was the opposite. For a coach that wasn't here for any offseason activity at all, and only been in the building since October, his first test of this new approach comes against a challenging opponent.

With last year's first overall pick Cam Ward at quarterback, the Titans rank in the 98th percentile in their utilization of the quick passing game this season. They have yet another dual-threat, home run hitting running back in former Cowboy Tony Pollard. Ward's best trait has always been his ability to throw the ball out of a moving pocket, and the Titans are figuring out in real time how to protect him and also target the middle of the field at a high rate to open up the sophomore passer's game.

If Mike Pettine is going to make a difference for the Cowboys after they just allowed a franchise record for yards given up in a home game, to a 49ers team that exposed them in the middle of the field, with a mobile QB as well, doing so against the Titans would actually be a solid start.

With the ball coming out so quickly from Ward's hands, the Cowboys likely can't rely on the pass rush element they got against the 49ers with five sacks either. Pass rush will have to come from sound coverage in the short to intermediate part of the field, where the Titans will look to stress Shemar James, Caleb Downs, and even Rashan Gary who got up field without contain on a number of big 49ers plays last week.

If the Cowboys cannot handle these things, and also struggle to get multiple bodies to Pollard in the run game and allow him to get around the edge or push the pile, it could be yet another long afternoon for Brian Schottenheimer's team against an inferior opponent.
