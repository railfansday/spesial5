$3.1B in Stolen Binance Crypto Has Yet to Be Laundered: Elliptic

Around half of the $6.43 billion stolen from Binance last month has yet to be laundered, blockchain analytics firm Elliptic said in a report updated on Friday.

About $3.11 billion remains in addresses linked to the theft, mostly in ETH and Bitcoin, and those funds are "very likely" still held by North Korea, Elliptic said. A further $2.85 billion, or 44% of the haul, has been frozen or seized, mostly stablecoins that issuers such as Tether and Circle froze within an hour of the theft. That compares with around 3% frozen after the $1.5 billion Bybit hack in February 2025, according to Elliptic.

Binance hack update: the XRP has started moving. About $50 million has been swapped, mostly through THORChain into BTC, with about $226 million still to move.

Bitcoin converted from other stolen assets is now about 1,650 BTC ($124 million), almost twice the figure a day earlier.…

The FBI has attributed the October 29 theft to North Korean hackers it calls TraderTraitor. North Korea has denied involvement.

The laundering so far has been "highly complex and clearly automated," Elliptic said. It has used more than 20,000 wallets on 16 blockchains, at least 36 services and at least 65,000 transactions, roughly one new wallet every 30 seconds.

The 18,270 BTC stolen from Binance, worth about $1.5 billion at the time of the theft, was split into 19 addresses on the day of the hack and has not moved since. The nine largest Ethereum addresses, holding 89% of the attacker's ETH, have not moved since October 30.

Most of the laundering so far, about $220 million in TRX, has run through Tron, where a single distribution wallet sent 684.6 million TRX to hundreds of new addresses over 51 hours, including through the night in Pyongyang. Almost all of it was bridged to Ethereum.

All of the stolen Solana, about $87 million at current prices, has been swapped, bridged or mixed, and the attacker has started laundering the stolen XRP, with about $50 million converted so far and $226 million remaining. In total, about $124 million has been converted into Bitcoin, much of it through cross-chain service THORChain.

The speed has fueled speculation that AI agents are moving the funds, but Elliptic said the evidence points to conventional automation. "The laundering is clearly automated, but nothing we have seen shows an AI agent making decisions," the firm said.

Elliptic expects the attacker to swap the ETH for Bitcoin and then cash out through OTC brokers and exchanges over a period of months. After the Bybit hack, North Korea laundered all 499,000 stolen ETH within 10 days but took months to cash out, the firm noted.

Binance has continued to restore services. On Friday, the exchange said users could now verify that their balances are backed at least 1:1 using the zero-knowledge proof for its latest proof-of-reserves snapshot, and that it aims to reopen withdrawals for all tokens on restored networks by the end of next week.
