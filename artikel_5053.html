Strategy sells $176M in MSTR shares for STRC buybacks – What’s behind the shift?

Michael Saylor’s Strategy is making headlines, but there is something under the wraps that is raising eyebrows.

In its 23rd November Form 8-K filing, the Bitcoin [BTC] DAT revealed that the company is becoming more aggressive with its capital management strategy, particularly when it comes to its preferred stock, STRC.

In fact, between 16th and 22nd November, the company sold 1,925,676 shares of its Class A common stock (MSTR), generating $176.7 million in net proceeds.

But here’s a catch: instead of directing the entire amount towards Bitcoin purchases, Strategy used just $29.6 million to acquire 457 BTC while allocating the remaining proceeds towards repurchasing STRC shares.

At the same time, the company bought back 2,183,747 STRC shares for approximately $211.8 million during the same period, using $147 million from MSTR share sales and another $64.8 million from its USD cash balance. Naturally, this has raised an important question: why is Strategy selling one security to buy back another?

So, the difference lies in the roles of MSTR and STRC. MSTR, as we all know now, is Strategy’s common stock, offering investors exposure to its Bitcoin holdings and business performance. STRC, meanwhile, is a variable-rate perpetual preferred stock with a $100 stated value that pays regular dividends.

Therefore, Strategy aims to keep STRC trading around 99-100. When it trades below this range, buybacks can help support its market price while reducing outstanding shares and future dividend obligations.

This also explains the company’s expanded buyback capacity. On 23rd November, Strategy increased its Digital Credit Securities repurchase authorization from $3 billion to $4 billion, with approximately $1.12 billion remaining. At the same time, it continues to use its ATM program to raise capital by issuing MSTR shares.

Given such a change in plans, Strategy still continues to be the largest BTC holder, with a total of 851,305 BTC, acquired at an aggregate cost of $64.23 billion and an average purchase price of $75,446 per BTC.

All in all, the filing shows that Strategy is balancing Bitcoin accumulation with preferred stock buybacks rather than directing all its capital towards BTC purchases.

This comes as the MSTR stock was trading at $84.40 at press time up by 8.48% in the past 24 hours. Meanwhile, STRC was trading at $98.26 at press time up by 5.01% in the past trading day.

Lastly, AMBCrypto’s recent report also highlighted this shift, as its MSTR shares were facing mounting pressure from Bitcoin’s decline, losing over 40% in Q4 2026.

However, one thing is clear, and that is continued MSTR weakness could make fresh capital raises more difficult, threatening Strategy’s future Bitcoin accumulation plans.
