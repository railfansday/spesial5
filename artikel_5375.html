10 states face KFC closures as franchisee Tasty Chick'n files for Chapter 11

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Free daily briefing on global business news.

A KFC franchisee's Chapter 11 filing puts stores in 10 states at risk. See what a listing means in each state, from Tennessee to Indiana.

A large share of the KFC restaurants this franchisee may shut are in Tennessee. CBS 8 reports that 24 Tennessee locations are among the at-risk addresses. Those stores make up a sizable part of a closure list of more than 70 restaurants, according to Fast Company. Within the state, the risk centers on one city: most of the listed addresses are in Memphis, Tenn., and the rest are spread across other Tennessee towns. That concentration shows where one owner happened to run its restaurants. It says nothing about how much Memphis diners want fried chicken, and a customer there is living through the owner's collapse more than a local downturn.

For Tennessee, much of the loss came before the bankruptcy did. Tasty Chick'n closed 19 Tennessee restaurants before it filed, according to Newsweek figures reported by Quartz. Those closures and the 24 listed locations come from separate counts that may not match address by address. The list itself mixes stores that are already closed or winding down with ones that are only unprofitable. Many of the state's losses happened before any judge was involved, and the stores still serving customers face an open question. A Tennessee diner who finds a familiar KFC on the list may be confirming a closing already noticed rather than learning of a new one.

Some of Florida's at-risk KFC stores may have closed before the bankruptcy began. CBS 8 reports that 11 Florida addresses are tied to leases the franchisee wants to exit, but the list does not say which of those stores are already closed. The company had shut 10 Florida restaurants before the filing, according to Newsweek figures reported by Quartz. Those two counts may overlap, so some listed Florida addresses could belong to restaurants that went dark months ago. For a Florida diner, the first question may be whether the local KFC is still open at all.

It is not yet settled whether the bankruptcy will close any more Florida stores. A listing shows that the franchisee wants out of a lease. It does not confirm that a store still serving customers will shut. Tasty Chick'n plans a sale that could keep restaurants open under new owners, according to TheStreet, and that plan does not name any specific store for closing. A Florida store that is still operating could end up with a new operator rather than a locked door.

Kentucky's place in KFC's name gives its stores no protection from this franchisee's collapse. CBS 8 lists eight Kentucky addresses at risk, spread across Glasgow, Cave City, Madisonville, Mayfield, Murray, Owensboro, and Paducah, Ky., in a string of smaller regional cities rather than one metro area. Paducah has two of the listed stores, and the failure of a single owner can put more than one counter in the same town at risk. What links these towns is the company that runs their restaurants, not anything happening in Kentucky's economy.

Kentucky holds only one part of a troubled operation. The Kentucky stores are operated by Tasty Chick'n LLC, a franchisee with 84 restaurants in all, according to Allrecipes. Nothing in the filing points to a weakness in Kentucky. The state appears because this operator happens to run stores there.

Alabama appears to have five KFC restaurants at risk, but the real number of restaurants is probably smaller. CBS 8 lists five Alabama addresses among the bankrupt franchisee's at-risk stores. The restaurant in Russellville, Ala., appears on that list twice with slightly different street wording, and Alabama Public Radio reports two Russellville addresses as well, so the five entries likely cover four restaurants. Four is the most Alabama could lose from this filing, not the number of stores expected to close.

Alabama's risk is confined to a few small towns in one corner of the state. The listed restaurants are in the north Alabama towns of Boaz, Arab, Hartselle and Russellville. None of the state's larger cities has a store on the list, so most Alabamians face no change at their local KFC from this bankruptcy. The cluster shows where this one owner happened to run restaurants, not that north Alabama's economy is weaker than the rest of the state's.

Mississippi makes up only a small part of this bankruptcy. CBS 8 reports that three Mississippi locations are among the at-risk stores run by Tasty Chick'n LLC, a franchisee with 75 KFC restaurants in all, according to Restaurant Dive. Two of the listed stores are in Houston and Fulton, Miss., and a diner in either town has no local way to change what happens next. The future of those counters will be decided as part of the restructuring of the whole company, wherever its debts and contracts are sorted out.

Tasty Chick'n plans to sell its restaurants rather than simply close them, and that plan gives Mississippi's listed stores a possible way to stay open. The restaurants could be sold "either as an entire portfolio, by individual market, or in combinations of different markets to maximize buyer participation and transaction value," court documents said, as Restaurant Dive reported. Mississippi's stores could go with the whole chain, as a market of their own, or bundled with other states. Each route still needs a buyer willing to take them, and a Mississippi store that draws no buyer could still close.

Indiana has the slightest stake a state can have in this bankruptcy, a single KFC address, according to CBS 8. The franchisee behind that store, Tasty Chick'n LLC, operates in 10 states, including Indiana, according to TheStreet. Indiana is included because this company happens to run a restaurant there. No one can judge from one restaurant how Indiana diners or the state's other KFC customers are faring. The future of that address depends on the owner it shares with stores across the other states, not on the number of customers at its own counter.

The future of this Indiana store will be settled in Texas. Tasty Chick'n is based in Plano, Texas, according to TheStreet. CBS 8 reports that the company filed its case in the Houston Division of the Southern District of Texas. Decisions about a counter in Indiana will therefore come from a Texas company and a Texas bankruptcy court. The case centers on a sale that could keep restaurants open under new owners, and nothing in that plan sets a closing date for the Indiana store. Whether it keeps serving depends more on whether a buyer wants it than on its own sales.

A KFC that goes dark in a city neighborhood or a small highway town across the South and Midwest looks like a local story about one restaurant that lost its customers. Many of these closings share a single cause instead. Tasty Chick'n, the franchisee behind these stores, now earns far less on each sale than it did two years ago, posting a restaurant margin of 4% this year against 11.8% in fiscal 2023, according to Washington Times figures reported by Quartz. The company's trouble has lasted longer than a single bad year. Rolling Out reports that Tasty Chick'n's sales fell 11.4% in 2024. Its sales for 2026 are running 2.8% lower so far, according to TheStreet. A new owner would inherit a business whose sales have shrunk in more than one year. For a diner in any of these towns, the local store's fate depends more on its owner's finances than on how busy its own counter is.

Tasty Chick'n's troubles also unfolded inside a chain that was already pulling back. Rolling Out reports that over the 12 months ending in July 2026, KFC shut 312 of its U.S. restaurants. The chain's sales in the U.S. dropped 6% in the third quarter of 2026, according to TheStreet. Any stores this franchisee loses would add to a retreat that is already under way. Restaurant Dive reports that sales at KFC's established restaurants had leveled off late in 2025. The third-quarter decline suggests that recovery was fragile. When a chain is shrinking, a buyer willing to take on a struggling operator's weaker stores is harder to count on.

CBS 8 set out to identify the states where this bankrupt franchisee's KFC stores might shut down, in places from Memphis, Tenn., and Paducah, Ky., to Roanoke, Va., and Russellville, Ala. The important word is "might," because many of those stores may already be dark. Each address marks a lease the company hopes to shed, and some of those restaurants have already stopped serving or are in the process of shutting. Quartz reports that Tasty Chick'n has shut 44 restaurants since January. A good share of the loss may have come before the company filed its bankruptcy case. The useful question for each state is what a listing actually means there: a store that is already gone, one waiting on a buyer, or one that is exposed only because of who owns it.

The fate of the Tennessee stores that remain is being decided outside Tennessee. Tasty Chick'n is a Texas company, according to Fast Company. It took its case to the Southern District of Texas, Houston Division, where an out-of-state owner and an out-of-state court will settle what happens to restaurants in Memphis. That distance is the clearest sign that Tennessee's stores are at risk because of whose name is on the lease, not because of how they performed on their own.

Whatever survives in Memphis will depend on rulings in Houston, not on how many customers walk in along Elvis Presley Boulevard.

Florida's listed stores are concentrated in one part of the state. Four of the addresses are in Jacksonville, Fla., and others are nearby in Neptune Beach, Yulee, Orange Park and Ormond Beach. A buyer interested in northeast Florida would find nearly all of the state's listed stores in that one area. Nothing yet shows that any buyer wants them.

In Florida, the remaining KFC stores will stay open only if someone takes over the northeast Florida restaurants.

The franchisee's Arkansas restaurants are spread across a string of small towns rather than gathered in one city. CBS 8 reports that 10 Arkansas addresses are on the franchisee's list, in towns such as Walnut Ridge, Mena, Hope, De Queen and Forrest City, Ark. Each of those communities has its own exposed restaurant, so the risk reaches many separate places through one store apiece. A slump in a regional economy would tend to hurt stores in the same area at once. Stores scattered across unrelated towns share only the company that holds their leases. One listing could confuse readers in a neighboring state: the Nashville on the list is Nashville, Ark., not the city in Tennessee. That address counts toward Arkansas.

The franchisee decided which Arkansas stores to list based on how each restaurant performs for its own business, not on conditions in the towns around them. The list covers restaurants that are closed, winding down or unprofitable, and being on it does not confirm that a store will close. An Arkansas KFC that still serves customers is on the list because this owner has flagged its lease. The listing judges the store's value to one company's books, not how much people in Hope or De Queen want fried chicken. For a diner in Mena or Walnut Ridge, the future of the local KFC is out of local hands. It depends on what one company decides about its leases.

KFC's own turnaround effort did not keep this franchisee out of trouble. The chain launched its "Kentucky Fried Comeback" in July 2025, according to Allrecipes, and Tasty Chick'n later put its Kentucky locations among the leases it wants to exit. A franchisee whose own finances are failing gains little from a brand's comeback campaign.

For a diner in Mayfield or Glasgow, the local store's future depends on how Tasty Chick'n's case turns out, not on the state's bond with the brand.

Virginia is on this list because of who owns its restaurants, not because of anything happening in the state. CBS 8 lists seven Virginia KFC locations among the stores at risk, and all of them belong to Tasty Chick'n, a franchisee based in Plano, Texas, according to Rolling Out. The stores do not form a cluster that would point to a regional slump. They are spread across Fredericksburg, Salem, Lexington, Emporia, Franklin and Roanoke, Va., and only Roanoke has more than one. The only link among those six towns in this case is the company that runs their restaurants. A Virginia diner who finds a familiar KFC on the list can learn nothing from it about how people in that town spend, because an owner in another state made the decisions behind the listing.

The future of these Virginia stores will be decided in a Houston courtroom and through a sale, not by anyone in Roanoke or Emporia. The company's bankruptcy case is in federal court in Houston, part of the Southern District of Texas. Rolling Out reports that Tasty Chick'n intends to sell its restaurants under the court's oversight, either all together or in smaller groups. A Virginia store could change hands along with the rest of the company's locations or as part of a smaller group, and a new owner could keep it running. The company has not said which restaurants may reopen or keep operating, so no Virginia diner can treat a listing as either a reprieve or a farewell.

In Virginia, a listed store is waiting on a buyer, not on a closing date.

The fate of these stores will be decided in Texas. Tasty Chick'n LLC, the franchisee that runs them, is pursuing its Chapter 11 case in the Houston Division of the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas, according to Alabama Public Radio. The outcome depends on the owner's finances and the court's process, not on how busy the counter in Boaz or Hartselle is. A diner in Arab or Hartselle can't learn from the list whether the local KFC is still serving, because nothing on it says which stores are already closed, which are winding down and which are merely unprofitable.

For these towns, the listing raises a question that the owner's bankruptcy case will answer, and it passes no judgment on the towns themselves.

The franchisee's collapse reaches only a handful of restaurants in Illinois, and all of them are in small towns far from Chicago. CBS 8 reports that four Illinois addresses are among the leases the bankrupt franchisee wants to exit. Illinois is on this list because one company signed leases in the state, not because KFC restaurants across Illinois are failing as a group. The four stores are in Salem, Anna, Murphysboro and Robinson, Ill., and none is in Chicago or its suburbs, the state's biggest market. Residents of four separate communities each face the possible loss of a single restaurant, while diners in the Chicago area face no change from this filing. The four towns are connected by their owner. This is where one operator happened to run restaurants, and their listing reflects that operator's footprint rather than any trend in southern or eastern Illinois.

No decision made in Illinois will settle what happens to these four stores. Tasty Chick'n LLC filed for Chapter 11 on Nov. 16 in the Houston Division of the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas, and the same case covers its restaurants in nine other states. A KFC in Anna or Robinson is one entry in a multistate proceeding being handled far from Illinois. For a diner in Salem or Murphysboro, the crowd at the local counter says little about the store's future. The court's rulings and the owner's choices about which leases to keep will decide it.

In Illinois, being listed means exposure to a distant owner's restructuring, not a verdict on the towns themselves.

The franchisor, Yum Brands $YUM, is working against a rescue. In September, Yum gave Tasty Chick'n notice that it planned to end the franchise agreements, according to Restaurant Dive. Any buyer would have to win over a franchisor that is already moving to cut ties. Yum is also among the company's creditors, with more than $15 million in royalties that Tasty Chick'n put off paying.

For the stores in Houston and Fulton, the outcome turns on whether a buyer steps forward before Yum ends the agreements.

Missouri's listed KFC stores belong to a company that plans to sell its restaurants, and their future depends on how that sale turns out. CBS 8 reports that Tasty Chick'n LLC has three Missouri locations on its list, in Perryville, Malden and Kennett, Mo. A sale could keep restaurants running under new owners, according to TheStreet, and the sale plan does not name any Missouri store for closing. Residents of these towns also cannot tell whether their local KFC is still serving, winding down or simply marked as unprofitable. The list covers all three kinds of stores without saying which one applies to each Missouri address. For now, residents know who owns their stores but not what will happen to them.

The biggest threat to these three stores may come from the brand on the sign rather than from a shortage of customers. Brands has broken with a franchisee before, and that company, Encanto Restaurants, then filed for bankruptcy under Chapter 11. Yum said Encanto "can no longer operate in a way that meets our standards." Tasty Chick'n now faces the same kind of break.

No one can yet say whether the stores in Perryville, Malden and Kennett will be sold to an owner KFC is willing to keep.

Whether Indiana's one listed KFC survives depends on an owner, a court and a franchisor, and none of them is in Indiana.
