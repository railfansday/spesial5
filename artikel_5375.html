Z Squared CEO Issues Third-Quarter Letter to Shareholders Highlighting Debt-Free Balance Sheet, Energized AI Campus Expansion to 150 MW Target, and Active Commercial Tenant Discussions

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

FORT LAUDERDALE, Fla., Nov. 24, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Z Squared Inc. (Nasdaq: ZSQR) today issued a letter to shareholders from Chief Executive Officer David Halabu following the filing of its third-quarter Form 10-Q.

This quarter Z Squared completed its shift in focus from digital asset mining to AI infrastructure. It was a defining quarter for us, and I want to walk you through where we stand and where we're headed.

The past few months have been hard on our sector, and I have felt it as a fellow shareholder. But a difficult market tends to blur two very different things: the conditions around a business and the fundamentals underneath it. The conditions have shifted. Financing is more expensive, investors are more cautious, and valuations across public markets have come down. The fundamentals have not. We believe AI still needs far more computing capacity than exists today, and the power to run it is still hard to find. Those fundamentals are why Z Squared exists.

Since we went public, our plan has been to move from digital asset mining into AI infrastructure. In my August letter I described the company as partway through that shift. This quarter we completed it. We directed that the mining fleet be powered down on June 16, and we do not expect to resume mining. We still own roughly 9,800 machines and we are evaluating the best way to realize value from them.

That decision is also what drove our third-quarter net loss of $117.1 million. In our quarter-end review, the accounting question was a simple one: when would the fleet run again? With Dogecoin trading below our breakeven level, and more recently in the mid-5-cent range, near multi-year lows, we could not responsibly put a forecast price or date on it, and under the accounting rules that meant writing off the goodwill tied to the mining business. Approximately 94% of the loss consisted of non-cash impairment charges: $106.9 million on goodwill and $3.4 million on the machines. Neither charge came out of our cash, and they leave us with a balance sheet that reflects the business we are building.

Our focus now is the Union County Campus in El Dorado, Arkansas, which has access to about 8 megawatts of utility power today, on an interruptible basis under the site's existing electric service arrangements. That matters more than the number suggests. We believe demand for land with power already in place continues to outpace supply, as developers compete for sites that can support AI workloads. New grid connections can take years to secure, which makes energized sites like ours increasingly scarce. We have one, we hold an option on land next to it to grow, and we are in active commercial discussions with multiple parties for some or all of the existing 8 megawatts. We have not yet signed a lease or received a binding commitment. Capacity beyond the existing 8 megawatts, including the up to approximately 150 megawatts contemplated by our development plan, remains a development target and is subject to additional power arrangements, tenant commitments, financing, approvals and construction.

We are also finding ways to get more out of the site we already own. A2 Advisors' initial engineering review indicates that a large portion of the existing 10,000-square-foot mining facility on the campus may be suitable for retrofit, which, if confirmed, could meaningfully reduce the cost and time of our first build-out. A third natural gas pipeline has the potential to run lateral to our property , and we plan to commission a feasibility study to determine how much additional power it could support.

We finished September with $12.4 million in cash and no debt. Management believes our existing cash is sufficient to fund our existing operations and contractual commitments for at least twelve months. As the campus grows, we intend to finance each phase at the project level against a signed tenant lease, so new capacity is built for customers who have already committed.

In August I gave you items to measure us by, and we are delivering on them. Paradox closed on September 8, paid in preferred stock with no cash or debt at closing, and with additional preferred stock issuable only if development milestones are achieved. Site planning and engineering are underway with A2 Advisors, designed around requirements from several prospective tenants. On November 18 we announced a five-year option on approximately 89.56 adjacent acres, which we intend to exercise for an initial tract of up to 10 acres following a survey and site investigations, giving the campus room for a multi-phase path to larger capacity. And we are staying disciplined: major construction spending will follow a signed customer.

I also told you to judge us on how we grow in a measured fashion. We remain debt-free. In recent weeks, tighter credit has weighed on AI infrastructure developers that carry legacy debt. We believe carrying no debt gives us the flexibility to move when the right opportunity comes along, and we are watching closely.

Our priorities for the coming months are clear:

We are being deliberate about who we sign. The right tenant on the right terms, financed with discipline, is what we are accountable for and what we intend to deliver.

Our officers hold Z Squared shares alongside you, so we feel this market the same way you do. But market swings don't change the assets we own, the campus we are developing or our belief in both. Our job is to deliver on the priorities in front of us, and then position Z Squared for what comes next.

We believe we made the right move in repositioning this company early, and that repositioning is now behind us. Now comes the part I'm most excited about: putting it to work.

Thank you for your continued support. I look forward to updating you on our progress in the months ahead.

David HalabuChief Executive Officer & DirectorZ Squared Inc. (Nasdaq: ZSQR)
