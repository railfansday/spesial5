Singapore Airlines Could Have Made Business Class Look Like This. Why Did It Choose Black And Yellow?

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

For some backstory, you may wish to read my earlier coverage on the seat, namely that CEO Goh Choon Phong has now stated that Singapore Airlines is open to rethinking the design.

> Read More: Singapore Airlines CEO May Rethink New Business Class Colors, And He Should

But are we really making a big deal about nothing?

“A yellow strip. A color palette. My god, seriously? Do you all stand there and stare at the seat for hours to gain some sort of energy transference? Personally, I sit down on it, eat the food, sleep and arrive somewhere I need to be. It’s a tiny cocoon of transport. Not a hotel room. Yes, I want comfort and decent things to pass the time. But anyone who obsesses over feeling less comfortable given a yellow stripe needs to seriously assess priorities.”

I understand that sentiment and agree that the comfort and quality of the seat, bedding, food, and drinks is far more important the color of the seat. Even so, the issue is that Singapore Airlines prides itself on the small details and the current mockup looks like a sloppy and callous oversight.

That’s certainly a matter of opinion, but what strikes me is that “the internet” has had a field day creating alternate design schemes. Look at this collage of alternatives, for examples:

A post shared by Dennis Chong (@dennischongofficial)

Every one of those strikes me as preferable to the chosen design and some look downright luxe and in the rich tradition of Singapore Airlines. This one is my favorite:

People are joking about what’s next, wondering if we might soon see new flight attendant uniforms to replace the sarong kebaya?

(Ironically, these looks like Starlux uniforms, which BMW/Designworks also designed).

I’ll leave it here, but I’d still really like to know the thought process that drove this seat design. Who agreed that this was a great new design, especially when it truly looks like Scoot, your low-cost subsidiary?

I’m still betting that SIA is going to scrap the black/yellow design and come up with something new, but that’s about as theoretical at this point as the “new uniforms” above.

Is this the most important thing in life? Obviously not. Has no impact on my life.

But it’s just a complete and abject failure of maintaining a luxury brand. A case study in stupidity. And sorry folks, branding matters.

The shame of it is that what looks like a beautiful new first class design has basically been completely ignored in favor of commenting on this spectacular failure. “Here’s our new profit center – great news, we made it look cheap and bland and just like our low cost airline” is the sentiment that is being put forward. That’s unbelievable!

This wasn’t a complicated assignment. The fact that they bungled it so badly is incredible. Someone at the airline will surely be held accountable for this, whether the internet thinks that’s fair or not.

Given that BMWs are the most super-mega-fugly vehicles on the road, it’s no surprise that this design is a total flop. That said, the mystery of why SQ management signed-off on it does still remain.

What in the world is the basis of the new yellow stripe and gray design? Is it some type of “ode to Spirit Airlines”? Certainly not aligned with a premium product. Singapore Airlines was a special experience. It’s going to be difficult to watch Singapore Airlines’ Bud Light moment.
