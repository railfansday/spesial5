From shows like Not Suitable for Work and Overcompensating, the young adult comedy genre (not to be confused with YA shows) has seen several exciting new additions lately. But as Overcompensating's long-awaited Season 2 gets ready to release, FX's beloved addition to the genre, Adults, is getting more support than ever.

After premiering Season 2 of Adults on August 27, 2026, the series, which follows five young adults dealing with the ups and downs of life, love, and friendships as they live in a house in Queens, just got the green light for Season 3. While the new installment has yet to have a release date, creators Ben Kronengold and Rebecca Shaw confirmed to Variety that Season 3 will consist of 16 episodes, double the eight episodes inits first and second seasons. The extra room not only shows the studio's commitment to the series and how successful it's become, but sets the series up to become an all-around comedy classic.

The Season 3 renewal of the series was confirmed by Variety on November 11, 2026. “We started working with FX over six years ago, back when Paul Baker was but a twinkle in all of our eyes,” said Kronengold and Shaw of Jack Inannen's now viral character. “In the years since, John [Landgraf] and the rest of the team have taught us everything we know about how to lead, produce, and tell stories that make people feel like part of a community. They’ve become family, and we’re so glad to make it official — and to start yelling at them like we do our parents.”

Of course, the renewal isn't exactly a surprise. On Rotten Tomatoes, the sophomore season of the series scored a flawless 100% from critics and a still very impressive 96% from the audience. In Collider's review, Taylor Gates stated that the second season continued what made Season 1 so great, and delivered even sharper, funnier storylines. "In Season 2, the group interacts with everything from K-pop to viral man-on-the-street interviews, making it feel new and resonant," Gates wrote. "In addition to the sharp dialogue, there's an impressive amount of well-executed physical comedy, and the cast goes all in with the slapstick vibes."

All those features together meant that Season 2 showed how production honed in on the magic of Season 1 and delivered an increasingly funny series to viewers. "With better, more refined writing and performances that feel even more confident and lived-in, Adults achieves something that few shows do by improving in its sophomore season," Gates concluded.

Now that the third season of Adults is renewed and is confirmed to be longer than its previous installments, the series has almost unmatched potential. After all, one of the biggest advantages of a longer third season of Adults would be the opportunity to let its central friend group grow at a more natural pace. With 16 episodes, the comedy could embrace the kind of everyday storytelling that made older ensemble sitcoms, like Seinfeld or Friends, so enduring. The seemingly insignificant conversations, unlikely character pairings, running jokes, and low-stakes misadventures that gradually make a fictional world feel like home. More time spent inside Samir’s (Malik Elassal) house would allow viewers to become even more invested in the rhythms of the group, not just their biggest moments.

A longer season would also give the characters room to make mistakes, experience setbacks, and figure out adulthood without having to rush through those experiences. Their messy relationships, professional frustrations, and sometimes unhealthy dependence on one another are all part of what makes the show's portrayal of being young and figuring things out feel recognizable. Rather than resolving conflicts within a handful of episodes, 16 episodes could allow those tensions to build and evolve organically.

Ultimately, Adults is the kind of comedy that could benefit from spending more time with its characters. While the short seasons so far have been a hit, a longer season could hit the sweet spot, proving that sometimes an ensemble sitcom isn't always about where the story ends up, but about the small, familiar moments along the way. Giving this friend group more episodes to simply exist together could make the show even more rewarding to follow. Fans are surely in for a treat.
