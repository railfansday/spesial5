Should You Buy C3.ai Stock After Its 93% Decline? The Answer Might Surprise You.

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

C3.ai is proof that not every artificial intelligence stock is a winner.

C3.ai (AI +3.64%) went public in December 2020, and its stock quickly set a record high of $177. It has trended lower ever since, with the downside reaching a crescendo after its founder and chief executive Thomas Siebel announced he was stepping away to deal with health issues last September.

Siebel always played a central role in the sales and customer relationship management processes, so his departure led to a dramatic decline in C3.ai's revenue. Fortunately, he returned to lead the company in May, and he's now trying to right the ship.

C3.ai stock remains 93% below its all-time high, so could this be a buying opportunity as Siebel puts his turnaround plan in place? The answer might surprise you.

Developing AI software from the ground up requires a considerable amount of financial and technical resources, which the average business simply doesn't have. C3.ai developed a portfolio of turnkey applications covering a range of different industries, and they can be tailored for the specific needs of almost any customer, giving enterprises a more practical way to tap into the power of AI.

However, C3.ai is expanding its addressable market by becoming a platform provider. It launched the C3 Agentic AI Platform last year, which builds a deep understanding of every existing process, relationship, and data set within the enterprise, allowing managers to create powerful agents that can make operational decisions and even automate tasks. In essence, it serves as an intelligent operating system for the enterprise.

The C3 Agentic AI Platform integrates with leading cloud platforms like Microsoft Azure and Amazon Web Services, so it can ingest operational data from across the entire enterprise in real time.

C3.ai's decision to become a platform provider will be critical to its future success because AI will eventually be at the center of every enterprise, so catering to those deploying just one or two applications was simply the wrong strategy for the long term.

C3.ai delivered $250.3 million in revenue during its 2026 fiscal year (ended April 30), a 35% decline from the prior year. The company then delivered $52.4 million in revenue during its fiscal 2027 first quarter (ended July 31), a 25% decline from the year-ago period.

The negative results were directly attributable to Siebel's departure, but better times might be ahead now that he's leading the company again. According to management's latest guidance, C3.ai could generate up to $240 million in total revenue during fiscal 2027, which would represent a much smaller decline of 4% compared to fiscal 2026. Plus, Siebel thinks a return to sales growth could be in the cards within the next two quarters.

C3.ai has significantly cut costs across the board to right-size the business in light of its shrinking revenue. It was a necessary step, because the company lost a staggering $470.4 million at the bottom line during fiscal 2026, an increase of 63% from the prior year. It lost a further $92.8 million during the first quarter of fiscal 2027, but that was a 20% improvement from the year-ago period, so things are starting to move in the right direction.

Shortly after C3.ai went public in 2020, its price-to-sales (P/S) ratio briefly climbed to over 100, which was a ludicrous valuation. For some perspective, the technology-heavy Nasdaq-100 index has a P/S ratio of just 6.3. Investors were expecting rapid revenue growth from C3.ai that simply never came, hence the prolonged decline in its stock over the last six years.

The stock is now trading at a more reasonable P/S ratio of 7.2, which is a steep discount to its average of 14.1 since going public. However, given the company's revenue is expected to decline in fiscal 2027, its forward P/S ratio is actually slightly higher at 8.3.

In other words, C3.ai is more expensive when valued against its future potential financial results, whereas a healthy, growing company would actually look cheaper on a forward basis. Shrinking businesses tend to destroy shareholder value over time, which is exactly why investors try to avoid them.

With that said, C3.ai saw a 73% quarter-over-quarter increase in its gross bookings during the third quarter, which typically reflects the value of signed contracts for services that haven't been delivered yet. Bookings can be a good predictor of future revenue, so this supports Siebel's expectation of a return to sales growth in the next two quarters.

However, I wouldn't buy C3.ai stock right now. I would prefer to wait until the company actually does start delivering consistent revenue growth, because any further declines could leave shareholders exposed to steep losses.
