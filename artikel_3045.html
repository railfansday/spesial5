Altria Yields 5.8% and Has Raised Its Dividend 61 Times in 57 Years. Here's Exactly How Much $5,000 Invested Pays Each Year.

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Investors starting out with relatively small stakes can extract a fair amount of income from Altria.

By Todd Shriber – Nov 22, 2026 at 7:10AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Altria Group ›

One issue that new investors often grapple with is whether to buy stocks with small amounts of capital or wait until they have more substantial cash to enter the game.

Hint: Starting small is better than waiting or not starting at all. Some experts call it micro-investing, but whatever the label, prospective market participants, particularly those with the luxury of time on their side, are best served by starting with small stakes and putting the advantages of compounding and long-term horizons to work.

Just $5,000 is a great starting point for harnessing income from Altria stock. Image source: Getty Images.

With that in mind, the example of starting somewhat small with reliable dividend growth stocks, including tobacco behemoth Altria (MO +0.25%), is worth examining.

To be fair, $5,000 isn't a small amount of money, but it's not enough to retire on, either. However, it is a solid starting point for investors seeking a fair amount of income from Altria.

Here are the particulars on $5,000 worth of Altria shares. This Dividend King, or one of the companies with a dividend increase of at least 50 consecutive years, yields 5.8%, as of Thursday, Nov. 19. That's nearly 80 basis points above the yield on 10-year Treasuries. Based on the tobacco stock's closing price of $74.68 on Nov. 19, $5,000 buys 67 shares (I slightly rounded up there). Factor in the $4.44 per share annual dividend, and a $5,000 stake in Altria today generates nearly $297.50 in annual income.

That's not too shabby. Call it $300 and Altria's dividend, assuming it's not being reinvested, could cover a couple of months of phone bills or a nice dinner out. On the other hand, $300 isn't an earth-shattering amount of money, suggesting that investors should maintain perspective on Altria's income-generating capabilities.

That $300 in annual Altria payouts from a $5,000 stake is, well, today's figure. It doesn't account for dividend growth, something this consumer staples stock is a master of, as highlighted by its 57-year run of rising payouts. Put another way, investors buying Altria today get a $4.44-per-share annual dividend, but there's a solid chance that figure will be higher in 2027, 2028, and beyond.

Altria offers cash-strapped investors other reasons to get off the sidelines. Not only does the stock yield more than 10-year Treasuries, but its dividend yield is more than 5x that of the S&P 500.

Next, there's the matter of inflation. Take a leap of faith and assume/hope that the Federal Reserve's interest rate hikes are enough to drive inflation down to the central bank's stated target of 2%. Or assume the worst and say that the Personal Consumer Expenditures (PCE) Price Index remains stuck at 3.6% as it read in September.

In either case, Altria's five- and 10-year dividend growth rates of 4.3% and 6.4%, respectively, vanquish inflation, and that's a good thing.
