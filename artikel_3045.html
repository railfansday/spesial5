Wells Fargo Is Now the Largest Institutional Holder of Spot Bitcoin ETFs. If Banks Are Buying Bitcoin, Should You?

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Wells Fargo owns a ton of the iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF, but that’s not as instructive as some investors may think.

By Todd Shriber – Nov 26, 2026 at 4:30AM EST

10 stocks we like better than iShares Bitcoin Trust ›

It is Form 13F filing season, meaning it's a good time to bone up on exactly what that regulatory document is. The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) requires that investors exercising "investment discretion over $100 million or more" reveal their holdings in Form 13F within 45 days of the end of the prior quarter.

Likely due in part to some investors' reverence for Warren Buffett, among other famous professional market participants, following 13F filings is something of a sport. However, it's not always the best way to track what the smart money is doing.

Wells Fargo increased its holdings of the iShares Bitcoin Trust, but that’s not a buy signal. Image source: Getty Images.

Take the case of Wells Fargo (WFC -0.31%) and the iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF (IBIT +0.92%), the largest spot Bitcoin (BTC +3.63%) exchange-traded fund (ETF).

Third-quarter 13F filings indicate that several banks, including Wells Fargo, increased exposure to various spot Bitcoin ETFs. On the surface, that may be all some investors need to know before grabbing for the falling knife (hopefully a temporary condition) that is the world's largest cryptocurrency.

Indeed, Wells Fargo more than doubled its holdings of the iShares Bitcoin Trust to nearly 49.6 million, worth about $2.4 billion, as of the end of the third quarter. Investors need to dig deeper than that. Here's why: roughly 85% of the bank's position in the king of Bitcoin ETFs is held on behalf of brokerage clients.

Put another way, the bulk of Wells Fargo's stake in the Bitcoin ETF is attributable to folks who trade on the bank's platform or to clients of Wells Fargo Advisors – the bank's wealth management arm. That means that the bank, for the most part, is NOT directly making a directional bet on the iShares ETFs. It's merely taking custody of the fund on behalf of its customers who own it.

Wells Fargo's involvement with the Bitcoin ETF isn't comparable to, say, Berkshire Hathaway revealing a new position or adding to an existing holding. News to that effect often boosts the stock in question because Berkshire is making a clear bullish bet. That's simply not the case with Wells Fargo and this Bitcoin ETF.

Bitcoin bulls  (sometimes known in crypto lingo as HODLers) who are disappointed by the revelation that Wells Fargo's big boost to its Bitcoin ETF position means little can take heart, as there are some encouraging signs in other 13Fs. For example, Wall Street firms added $4.2 billion in Bitcoin ETFs in the third quarter, and institutional holdings of those ETFs hit a record in the July-September period.

Importantly, some of those professional investors are using Bitcoin ETFs to make directional bets. Plus, data confirm that some well-known hedge fund managers either added to or initiated positions in the iShares ETF in the third quarter.

Still, caution is warranted because some recent buyers of Bitcoin ETFs may have already ditched those positions, and the investing public won't know that until fourth-quarter 13Fs come out next year.
