Knicks 102, Timberwolves 99: “3 straight on the road, what’s up…”

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

LaMelo Ball hit one of his patented three-pointers from the left elbow early in the third quarter against the New York Knicks last night and as the crowd cheered, he proceeded to jog back on defense. Of course, the only not totally broken Ball brother couldn't let the moment pass without completely indulging himself into a thoroughly scripted "ice in the veins"-gesture that feels as original these days as a two-handed set shot. Just a moment later Josh Hart rained on the parade, running directly by Vanilla Ball's D and finishing an easy backdoor cut with a simple layup. And just like that the Knicks were up 14 again, ignoring the protocol of how cutting a deficit to 12 in a game where your team got handsomely dominated up to that point needs to be celebrated.

It feels good to root for a professional team, doesn't it? It will allow you to say things like Real Clydes Wear Plaid shared in the Postgame Thread and which I unapologetically stole for the headline of this recap. The Knicks won their third game in a row at Minnesota last night and they probably made it a lot harder than it needed to be. Their opponents certainly had something to do with it, no matter how ill-advised some individual emotions might have been. The Timberwolves can play tremendous defense, are as long as those trees in their logo and have an aggression you could certainly link to that name-giving animal of them. They got a good albeit cranky coach, a proven system, years of winning experience. Yet in the end they also got LaMelo Ball now.

We get that he is a talented, at times iconic passer; we also get that he has found his footing fairly well on the frozen tundra near the Great Lakes so far. But we really can't get convinced that you can ever really win big with a "talent" like that. His play can remind one of the game young kids play before they really get a hang of Dr. Naismith's rules. They also take two steps before every dribble, then four after they pick the ball up. They also couldn't care less about a repetitive shooting motion or dribbling the ball without palming it every time. With the kids it's okay and fun, we let them discover themselves as players and excuse it. But Lamborghini LaMelo should be held to a different standard, he is supposed to be a professional NBA player after all.

Luckily for the Knicks, they got plenty of real pros on their roster, guys who truly care about winning and treat their profession like it means something – not only to them but to others. It certainly seemed that way when they played most of the game last night with a level of calmness and concentration that has been a hallmark of theirs ever since they went on that beautiful championship run last year. But despite all their dominance over that two-month stretch in the playoffs, you can't ignore that they are a team with plenty of cracks in their armor, many of which showed themselves last night as well. It's the kind of weakness that are often the reason for the Bockers falling behind big only to have the opportunity to stage their beloved monumental comebacks later on.

Yesterday the Knicks offense completely bogged down late when Minnesota went to more switching on D (raise your hand if you've seen that before), neutralizing all the beautiful team basketball that helped the Gotham Gang run up an almost 20-point lead earlier. Not even Karl-Anthony Towns, once again mesmerizing for most of the night against a traditional five at his old stomping grounds, couldn't regain his first-half form. Maybe he rode the pine a little long, Mr. Brown? Well, you can't play him all 48, we get it. A lineup that included Jose Alvarado, Andre Drummond and Josh Hart was still an interesting choice, to say the least. For all the individual chops it's quite a load for Jalen Brunson to carry that group offensively, it almost feels like him heaving big AD up K2 or some other majestic mountain. It might get you to the summit against certain defenses but considering some defenses are as long and physical as the Wolf Pack, it can also get cold rather quickly. Especially when Brunson still hasn't found his touch inside the paint yet.

That New York could still pull it out in the end is a testament to timely plays and a gritty, opportunistic defense that at no point in the game relented to the physically imposing Wolves coming at them. When LaMelo Ball couldn't find someone open downtown, the canines' only plan was to ram their oversized combo forwards ahead. It worked here or there but never consistently. Watch out, there's an extended arm coming at you – boom, another offensive foul! It's an interesting yet understandable choice by the league to make the off arm a point of emphasis this year (sorry, SGA). Why didn't they do the same for palming the basketball or taking more steps than a dressage horse? Your guess is as good as mine.

For the moment, though, let us not worry about it – there is a win to be celebrated. For all we know though, our guys are probably watching some film right now and preparing for their next opponent (Portland on Saturday). Maybe they are also speeding race cars through some busy pedestrian zone or practice their one-legged step-back fadeaway jumper that has netted them less than 42 percent shooting for their career?

Nah, we leave that to the guy with ice in his veins.
