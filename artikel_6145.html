Trump’s National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross fired amid widespread tech outages

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

New York Post columnist Rikki Schlott, The Federalist reporter Brianna Lyman and political commentator Joe Sweeny join 'Fox & Friends' to discuss viral social media panic over recent tech outages.

National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross is out after President Donald Trump backed his firing over concerns he lacked the cybersecurity credentials for the job, Fox News has confirmed.

Kirsten A. Davies, the Pentagon’s chief information officer, will step into the position in an acting capacity.

Davies, who was Senate-confirmed, will join Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, White House technology advisor Michael Kratsios and Secretary of State Marco Rubio on the administration's national security and cyber leadership team.

An administration official separately told Fox News that Cairncross did not want to serve another two years in the role. Cairncross took office Aug. 4, 2025, after Senate confirmation, and the exact date of his departure remains unclear.

Senior administration officials welcomed Davies' appointment on X.

"Congratulations to the new National Cyber Director Kirsten Davies — look forward to working together to strengthen America’s cybersecurity," Rubio wrote.

Clayton praised her background, writing, "With nearly two decades of leadership experience in the technology space, Kirsten Davies will be a strong asset for the Trump administration in her new position with the Office of the National Cyber Director. It has been my pleasure to work closely with Kirsten on national security matters, and I look forward to now working with her to navigate an increasingly dynamic and fast-moving cyber landscape."

Secretary of War Pete Hegseth wrote, "The Department congratulates Kirsten Davies on her new role as the National Cyber Director. Throughout her tenure at DOW, she has proven herself to be a dedicated patriot and professional, and we are confident she will advance President Trump’s America First agenda across the cybersecurity landscape."

Davies thanked Trump, White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller and Hegseth in her own post on X, calling the moment a critical juncture for U.S. technology.

"Our opportunity is to dominate the cyber landscape for generations to come …but only if we modernize and drive resilience into the foundations our great nation runs on, as well as build cyber talent for our future. Under President Trump’s leadership we will have a more secure, resilient; and supercharged technological future," Davies wrote. "I am honored to continue to serve."

The leadership shakeup follows a nearly weeklong stretch of technical disruptions that hit air travel, major banks, Microsoft services, Apple, Marriott, the National Weather Service and internal systems at the National Security Agency.

A Nov. 13 Federal Aviation Administration outage triggered a nearly five-hour nationwide ground stop and thousands of flight cancellations after contractor work on air traffic control wiring overloaded the system.

Administration officials have pushed back against speculation that hackers or foreign adversaries were behind the disruptions. Rubio said Tuesday that officials found "zero evidence" tying the incidents together or to malicious actors, while Clayton said the outages appeared linked to routine software patches and system upgrades rather than a coordinated cyberattack.
