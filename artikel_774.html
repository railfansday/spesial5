Snubs & Surprises In the 2027 Grammy Nominations

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Grammy voters always have a few surprises up their sleeves.

We all knew that the Olivias (Dean and Rodrigo) were going to get a lot of love from Grammy voters this year. And sure enough, when nominations for the 69th annual Grammy Awards were announced bright and early on Monday (Nov. 16), they both did. So did such other expected names as Taylor Swift, Bruno Mars and Rosalía.

And it’s hardly a surprise that women dominated the nominations, though the degree of that dominance is impressive. For the first time in Grammy history, the six artists with the most nominations are all women. RAYE leads with 11 nods, followed by Rosalía with nine nods, and then Phoebe Bridgers, Miranda Lambert, Ella Langley and Olivia Rodrigo, with seven each.

But that doesn’t mean there were no surprises in the nominations. It’s impossible to have 100 categories without having at least a few outcomes that don’t land as expected. This year, nearly 16,000 voting members of the Recording Academy were eligible to vote. That includes more than 2,300 new voting members who joined as part of the 2026 new member class.

Final-round voting will take place from Dec. 10 to Jan. 7. The 69th annual Grammy Awards will air on Sunday, Feb. 7, at a new time – 7 p.m. ET/ 4 p.m. PT. The move was designed to let Grammy watchers on the East Coast watch the full show without having to stay up to midnight.

Here are some of the most notable snubs and surprises from the 69th annual Grammy nominations list.

Many expected Kahan to be nominated for album, record and song of the year, but he was completely overlooked in all three of those marquee categories. His only nod was for The Great Divide: The Last of the Bugs (the deluxe edition of his Billboard 200-topping album), which is nominated for best contemporary folk album. Whatever the reasons, after a few years of being one of the top music artists in America, Kahan has received a grand total of three Grammy nods. He might do well to remind himself of this truth, nicely articulated by a talented troubadour: “California’s so much more than some awards show.”

Antonoff received three nods – album of the year for Phoebe Bridgers’ Lost Weekend and song of the year and best song written for visual media for “I Knew It, I Knew You,” his collab with Taylor Swift. But he was passed over for a nod for producer of the year, non-classical for the third year in a row.

The jazz pianist, composer and bandleader died in February 2021, but he just keeps piling up Grammy nominations. He received two more this year – best jazz instrumental album for Forever Yours: The Farewell Performance (Live) and best arrangement, instrumental or a cappella for “Overjoyed (Live).” Corea has won six of his 29 Grammys since he died.

Lefty had one of the year’s biggest pop/country crossover hits with “Boston,” but was bypassed in the CMA Awards nominations and now is also omitted from the Grammy nominations. The accusations of plagiarism that tarnished her breakthrough moment likely kept voters from supporting her.

Some speculated that the talented brother and sister might both receive best new artist nominations in the same year, which would have been a Grammy Awards first. Neither did.

These three artists were in the conversation for best new artist, but few expected them to actually make it. But all three did make it. The Womack Sisters are the first sister act to be nominated since Chloe x Halle (Bailey) eight years ago. Zeimani, Zekkujcha, and Zekuumba Womack are daughters of musicians Linda and Cecil Womack, members of the R&B duo Womack & Womack, and nieces of singer Bobby Womack and granddaughters of singer Sam Cooke.

Both artists were touted as best new artist hopefuls; both fell short. Fender had a long-running Hot 100 hit with “Rein Me In,” but voters may have figured anybody could have a Hot 100 hit at this point if they teamed up with the red-hot Olivia Dean. Role Model’s failure to receive a nod may be due to voters expecting a stronger follow-up to his irresistible late 2025 viral hit “Sally, When the Wine Runs Out.”

Spiro is nominated for best new artist, as widely expected, but her elegant ballad “Die on This Hill” was passed over for both record and song of the year nods. Not a shock – the record peaked at No. 19 on the Hot 100, a bit low to register in such a competitive category – but it had echoes of Adele’s breakthrough hit “Chasing Pavements,” which was nominated in both categories in 2009.

Wicked: For Good didn’t receive a single Oscar nomination earlier this year, but Grammy voters supported the project. The album is up for best compilation soundtrack for visual media, while “No Place Like Home” is up for best song written for visual media and Cynthia Erivo and Ariana Grande’s collaboration on “For Good” is nominated for best traditional pop vocal performance. Last year, the first Wicked was nominated for best compilation soundtrack, while “Defying Gravity” won a Grammy for best pop duo/group performance.
