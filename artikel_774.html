London Broncos confirmed as Super League's 15th team for 2027 season

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Super League clubs voted to expand competition to 15 teams for the 2027 season; London Broncos now confirmed as club to take that place; the league previously voted in favour of two salary cap amendments that allow clubs more flexibility with young players and injuries

London Broncos have been confirmed as Super League's 15th team for the 2027 season.

Earlier this month, Super League announced its 14 clubs had voted in favour of a motion to expand to 15 teams for the 2027 season.

Clubs were then invited to apply, with London Broncos - who had made no secret of their desire to be in the top flight - one of those to do so having completed the treble in 2026 during a brilliant season for them in the Championship.

"We are delighted to welcome London Broncos back to Super League for 2027," said chair of Super League Peter Hutton.

"This is an exciting moment for the club and its supporters, and an opportunity to build a lasting presence for our competition in the capital.

"They clearly impressed in the Championship last year, not only winning the League Leaders' Shield but the Grand Final and 1895 Cup in a memorable treble.

"In Grant Wechsel and Darren Lockyer, they have ambitious owners who are intent on making a success of our great sport in London and the surrounding areas.

"On behalf of all Super League clubs, I would like to put on record my thanks to both Grant and Darren for the collaboration they have shown over the last 10 days to enable the RFL to come to this recommendation.

"Expansion is about more than adding another club. We want a compelling, competitive Super League that reaches new audiences while continuing to serve the supporters who are at the heart of our sport.

"With more exciting announcements expected shortly around investment, broadcast deals and new commercial partners for Super League, we look forward to working with the Broncos as they prepare for 2027, and to seeing their supporters enjoy the elite competition in London once again."

After London went back down to the Championship in 2024, they were taken over by an ownership group including NRL and Brisbane Broncos legend Darren Lockyer.

Also this month, clubs voted in favour of two amendments to the league's salary cap regulations that allow clubs more flexibility with young players and injuries.

The first allows teams to claim salary cap dispensation if a player is injured for over eight cap-relevant matches, allowing clubs to sign a replacement player for the period.

It also forbids teams from claiming further salary cap dispensation should a player who was already exempt from salary cap implications becomes re-injured later in the season, unless he missed a further eight matches which affect the salary cap.

The second allows teams to feature a young player aged 23 and under - who has not previously played in any salary capped games - in two matches without triggering an increase in a club's salary cap spending, with that figure increasing to 50 per cent should they play between three and five matches.

Should the player feature in more than five matches, the full amount of their salary will be applied to the cap.
