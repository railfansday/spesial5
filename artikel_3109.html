(INO -3.97%) transitioned to a commercial-stage company following the FDA's accelerated approval of VOCERYQ for the treatment of adults with recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP). Management stated that the company is now focused on executing its commercial launch strategy in the United States while preparing for international expansion and further regulatory filings in Europe. The company strengthened its financial position through a public equity offering that provided $80.5 million in net proceeds, extending the projected cash runway into late 2027. Leadership indicated that the approval validates the proprietary DNA medicine platform and provides a foundation for the development of its rare disease pipeline candidates.

I'll now turn it over to Mike Sumner, our Chief Medical Officer, to provide a few more details on the approval, product label and supporting clinical data. Mike?

Michael Sumner: Thank you, Jacqui. The approval of VOCERYQ is exciting on many fronts. It's Inovio's first approved product, the first FDA-approved plasmid DNA-based immunotherapy, and most importantly, an innovative and much-needed new treatment option for adults with RRP. I'd like to spend some time today providing a few highlights from the approval of VOCERYQ and its broad adult indication. VOCERYQ was evaluated in RRP-001, a multi-site clinical study designed to assess what matters most to patients, reduction in surgery. The study followed 32 adult patients who had 2 to 8 surgeries in the year prior to treatment. Patients had visible papillomas surgically removed before receiving their first dose of VOCERYQ. Patients then received 4 doses over a 9-week period.

The primary efficacy endpoint was a reduction in surgery following dosing initiation. In this trial, we counted all surgeries after the first dose against the efficacy endpoint. We also conducted study RRP-002, a retrospective assessment of treatment effect up to 3 years after initial dosing in patients who had participated in the 001 study. The full clinical data from these studies were published in 2025 in Nature Communications and in The Laryngoscope, the top journal for the specialists who treat RRP. An additional analysis of data was also recently published in The Laryngoscope, supporting the ability of VOCERYQ to generate an immune response that linked to durable clinical benefit over the second and third years following initial treatment.

These publications highlight the meaningful therapeutic benefit of VOCERYQ, with the vast majority of patients experiencing a 50% to 100% reduction in surgery in year 1, with durable responses and continued clinical improvement in year 2. The FDA's efficacy evaluation for Accelerated Approval focused specifically on the first year treatment effect for patients who required 3 or more surgeries in the 12 months prior to treatment. For those 26 patients, the median number of surgeries in the 12 months prior to the first dose was 4.5, which included the surgical debulking required immediately prior to treatment. FDA's efficacy analysis started at the end of the dosing window at week 9.

In the following 12 months, 27% of the patients achieved complete response, as defined by not requiring any surgeries to manage their disease. And as a reminder, this response was achieved without conducting any scoping or surgery during the dosing window. Patients going from needing 3 or more surgeries a year to needing 0 surgeries following treatment would not be expected as part of the natural history of the disease. In terms of safety, VOCERYQ was found in the clinical study to be well tolerated, with study participants experiencing mostly low-grade treatment-emergent adverse events such as injection site pain and fatigue, with the majority being grade 1.

This slide lists the most common adverse reactions occurring in greater than or equal to 5% of patients, as described in the prescribing information. Of note, because VOCERYQ is a drug device combination product composed of our plasmid DNA-based drug with delivery by our CELLECTRA device, this safety profile represents the combination product. For this approval, the safety data were further supported by data from prior clinical trials of VGX-3100, an HPV-16 and 18 related disease program, which included over 1,100 administrations and a placebo control. Delivery with our proprietary CELLECTRA device is well characterized and has also been shown in additional clinical studies to be well tolerated.

It is also important to point out here that reducing the need for surgery, both as part of or following treatment, is an important consideration for patients. Every surgery matters because every individual surgery comes with significant risk. Finally, I'd like to share a brief overview of our future confirmatory study. As VOCERYQ has received an Accelerated Approval, continued approval for this indication may be contingent on verification and description of clinical benefit in a confirmatory study. We expect to submit a draft protocol for this study before year-end with the goal of aligning with the FDA on a final protocol by mid-2027.

We have agreed with the agency that the study should enroll at least 50 adult and pediatric patients with RRP requiring 3 or more surgical interventions in the 12 months prior to treatment with VOCERYQ. Subject to further regulatory feedback, we expect the study to evaluate the number of surgical interventions required over at least 3 years following completion of treatment, and compare this to an appropriate external control group or scientifically justified threshold. We may also explore continued treatment in patients without a sustained complete response as part of the study. Steven Egge: Thank you, Mike. We're excited to launch VOCERYQ and are focused on ensuring eligible RRP patients can access treatment as quickly and efficiently as possible. Let's start by discussing the significant RRP market opportunity. Based on our ongoing analysis of claims data, we believe there's a prevalent population of at least 25,000 adult patients with RRP in the United States, with additional patients diagnosed each year. Most patients continue to be managed surgically, and every single surgery comes with a risk of permanent vocal cord and airway damage.

Key opinion leaders and the RRP Foundation stated in a position paper published in The Laryngoscope in January of this year that immunotherapy should be considered the first-line treatment for RRP, and that it should be started as soon as possible in the patient's disease journey to reduce the risk of permanent damage from repeated surgeries. This position paper also noted that should VOCERYQ be approved by the FDA, it would be recommended as first-line therapy. To date, only a small fraction of the prevalent population of patients have been treated with immunotherapy, indicating there is a significant market opportunity for a nonsurgical therapeutic treatment option designed to reduce the frequency of surgeries.

VOCERYQ targets the root cause of RRP to deliver compelling efficacy, reducing the need for surgery. VOCERYQ also has a favorable tolerability profile, with the potential to minimize disruption to patient lives. And for physicians, providing treatment is simple and straightforward.

The device is designed to be easy to use and has a standard refrigeration requirement for drug storage that allows for flexibility in the location of administration. These strengths are what we believe can enable VOCERYQ to become the immunotherapy of choice for both patients and physicians. Our goal is to minimize the administrative burden and make access to treatment simple for both health care providers and patients.
