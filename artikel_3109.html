2 Growth Stocks That Lagged the S&P 500 in 2026 and Could Lead in 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

If you're looking for recovery candidates, these two are a good place to start.

By Jeremy Bowman – Nov 26, 2026 at 1:11PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Axon Enterprise ›

The S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.25%) is limping into the end of the year as AI stocks have come crashing down. The broad-market index is now up just 5% year-to-date through Thanksgiving.

While stock market pullbacks are painful to endure, they also present buying opportunities for investors, and a third of S&P 500 stocks are now down for the year.

Here are two S&P 500 stocks that look poised to bounce back next year.

Axon Enterprise (AXON +7.43%), the maker of Taser electrical weapons, body cameras, and related software, has been a longtime winner on the stock market, but 2026 has been a rough year for the law enforcement technology company.

Pressure on the SaaS sector, concerns about narrowing margins, and a delay in a federal contract have all contributed to its decline, as the stock is down 40% year-to-date.

However, much of that decline seems like an overreaction or related to earlier valuation concerns that no longer exist. The stock tumbled on its third-quarter earnings report after it said $130 million in expected revenue from a Federal Dedrone contract would be delayed. The company still expects to collect that revenue; it just may come in 2027 instead of 2026.

Meanwhile, Axon continues to invest in the business and its growth, which explains the compressing margin in some of its segments. It recently invested $1.2 billion in Skydio, a leading drone technology company, as the company expands further into drone and counter-drone technology, growing its addressable market.

Axon is still growing revenue at around 30% per quarter, and its future looks bright. Investors can take advantage of the discount as it looks poised to bounce back next year.

Like Axon Enterprise, Netflix (NFLX +0.92%) is another longtime winner that has struggled over the last year, falling 37% year-to-date. The leading streamer has reported weak engagement trends, and its original content has been lackluster, sparking concerns that it's falling behind other streamers. It also continues to fall further behind YouTube in market share.

However, Netflix has a history of bouncing back from such pullbacks, and it could only take one or two hit shows to change investor sentiment and improve engagement. The company has overhauled its content department, bringing in a new Chief Content Officer, Peter Rice, who was formerly Walt Disney's content executive, a move that should help improve its scripted TV library.

At a price-to-earnings ratio of just 16, the potential reward outweighs the risk here.
