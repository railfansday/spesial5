Qualcomm vs. Western Digital: Which Tech Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Qualcomm's chip empire faces a customer concentration question, while Western Digital's storage margins look almost too good to be true.

As mobile connectivity and data storage demands intensify, Qualcomm (QCOM +3.66%) and Western Digital (WDC +9.86%) stand as pivotal players in the tech landscape. Investors must decide which business model offers better potential.

Qualcomm provides the essential chips for smartphones and connected vehicles, while Western Digital manufactures the storage hardware that anchors global data centers. These companies represent different corners of the tech ecosystem, making them an interesting comparison.

Qualcomm is a central figure among semiconductor stocks. It designs the chips that power high-end smartphones and is moving into the automotive and Internet of Things sectors. In its latest annual report, filed in late 2026, the company highlighted key customers like Apple (AAPL +2.43%) and Samsung (SSNLF +0.00%). Since these mobile handset customers account for a large share of sales, customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business.

In fiscal 2026, revenue reached nearly $43.1 billion, a slight decline of roughly 2.6% from the prior year. Despite the dip in top-line growth, the company reported robust net income of approximately $13.6 billion. This equates to a net margin of close to 31.6%, which is a measure of how much profit is kept from every dollar of sales.

As of Qualcomm's September 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was roughly 0.5. This ratio measures total debt relative to shareholders' equity, with lower ratios indicating a more conservative financial structure. The current ratio, which shows assets available to cover short-term liabilities, stands at nearly 1.9. Free cash flow was close to $8.7 billion for the year, though note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 31% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Western Digital provides essential data storage solutions using hard disk drive technology for cloud and consumer markets. According to its latest annual report, filed for the fiscal year ending in 2026, the company noted a massive reliance on cloud providers. This cloud end market accounted for nearly 89% of revenue in fiscal 2026. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business, as three individual customers each accounted for 10% or more of total revenue.

For fiscal 2026, revenue reached approximately $12.9 billion, a jump of nearly 35.7% over the previous year. The company delivered net income of close to $9.4 billion. This resulted in an exceptionally high net margin of roughly 72.9%, indicating the company is keeping a large portion of its revenue as profit.

Based on Western Digital's July 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.1. This indicates a very conservative use of debt relative to shareholder equity. The current ratio stands at close to 1.3, meaning the company has $1.30 in short-term assets for every $1.00 in short-term debt. Free cash flow for the year was nearly $3.5 billion, and the company maintains a healthy financial position for its future projects.

Qualcomm faces significant revenue concentration in a small number of mobile handset customers. If key customers like Apple transition to their own internally developed solutions, the company could lose a major revenue stream. Additionally, high sales volume in China exposes the business to geopolitical tensions and trade restrictions. Competition is also intense, requiring constant innovation to stay ahead of rivals like Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSM +3.51%) in manufacturing and other chip designers.

Western Digital is heavily dependent on a limited number of cloud customers and suppliers for critical components. Any disruption in global demand or a shift in technology standards could hurt its market position. The company is currently navigating a transition to new storage technologies like HAMR. Furthermore, macroeconomic volatility and potential product defects or warranty claims represent ongoing concerns for the hardware manufacturer as it competes with companies like Micron Technology (MU +6.25%).

Western Digital appears more attractive based on its forward P/E ratio, while Qualcomm carries a lower P/S ratio relative to its total sales.
