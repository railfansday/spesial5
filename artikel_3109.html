Lean and Mathlib certify Alpöge’s Claude-assisted Riemann hypothesis proof

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Dozens of people compiled the 17-million-line Riemann hypothesis proof without errors. The Association for Human Mathematics says no human can yet claim to understand it.

The Lean and Mathlib communities have certified that Levent Alpöge’s Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann hypothesis is correct. In a joint statement on Saturday, the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative said they had full confidence in the proof and congratulated him.

“We hereby certify that both the statement and the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis are correct,” the groups wrote.

Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, released the proof on Friday as a Lean library of 17 million lines of code. Lean is a programming language that checks every logical step. There is no paper a human could read.

Dozens of people compiled the code without errors and got the same raw proof file, the statement said. Two separate tools then confirmed that Alpöge’s theorem is exactly the Riemann hypothesis as Mathlib states it. Experts in Lean and number theory had already vetted that statement.

Several proof checkers, written in different programming languages, checked the proof itself. Two of them, con-leche and con-ron, come with a formal proof that they cannot accept a false one. That answers the worry that a flaw in Lean could let a bad proof through, the groups said.

MIT mathematician Andrew Sutherland, who ran early checks on his own computer, keeps a public verification record. All 175,187 modules compile with zero errors, it says. He ran the checks with Claude Code. A separate, sandboxed rebuild produced a byte-identical proof file. Two of the slowest kernel checks were still running at his last update on Sunday afternoon.

The proof also covers the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions, across all moduli and characters, the record says. It does not cover Dedekind zeta functions, Hecke L-functions or automorphic L-functions in general.

Not every mathematician welcomed the result. The Association for Human Mathematics said in a statement that the proof puts technical validity above accountability, clarity and understanding.

“At this time, no human being can reasonably claim to be responsible for, or even to understand, such a document,” the group wrote.

The group called for democratic oversight of AI companies. It said firms use milestones like this as publicity for the superintelligence they want to build, and urged mathematicians to keep their research free of AI models.

The Lean groups said they looked forward to work that will help explain the new ideas in the proof. Alpöge has said he wants to understand the argument, simplify it and write it up. He works with Tristan Buckmaster on Navier-Stokes, another Millennium Prize Problem.
