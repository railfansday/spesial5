Once Upon a Farm stock dropped 38% after recalling toddler snack bars

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The company is recalling all Tractor Wheels bars after three choking complaints; the FDA warned consumers not to buy them

Once Upon a Farm is recalling its Tractor Wheels soft-baked bars for toddlers over a choking hazard, and its stock fell 38% in a week.

The company announced the voluntary recall on Oct. 30, citing three consumer complaints. The bars are labeled for toddlers ages 1.5 to 2.5. The recall covers three varieties: Apple $AAPL, Sweet Potato & Spinach; Strawberry, Pumpkin & Beet; and Banana, Pumpkin & Cauliflower. The company said no other products are affected.

Once Upon a Farm stock shed $242 million in market capitalization during the decline. Tractor Wheels brought in roughly 18% of the company’s revenue so far this year, according to The Wall Street Journal.

In its recall announcement, the company said the bars can become a clump that is thick, dry and tacky when they meet saliva and are chewed. It said no injuries have been reported to date.

The FDA's advisory describes the situation differently. The agency said it has gotten multiple reports of toddlers whose airways were completely blocked after they ate the bars. It said first-aid responses to choking, such as back blows and the Heimlich maneuver, might not clear the mass. Some incidents called for emergency responders and a hospital stay for observation, the FDA said.

The recall covers all lots, and the FDA urged consumers to avoid both purchasing and serving any flavor. The agency said the bars were sold across the U.S. and in Puerto Rico, online and at stores including Costco $COST, Target $TGT and Walmart $WMT. Consumers can return the product to the place of purchase for a refund or contact [email protected], the company said.

Once Upon a Farm is working with the FDA to remove affected products as it reformulates the bars, the company said.

CEO John Foraker told The Wall Street Journal the company pulled the entire Tractor Wheels line because it was unable to pinpoint which batch was behind the problem. "Consumer safety is our number one thing and we don't mess around with it," Foraker said.

In a filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission, Once Upon a Farm said it held a conference call on Nov. 2 to update investors on the recall. The company disclosed about $106 million in cash and cash equivalents and no outstanding debt as of Sept. 30. It also had up to $95.0 million of borrowing capacity under its credit facilities, subject to borrowing base limits and covenants.

The company expects to announce full third-quarter results on Nov. 12.
