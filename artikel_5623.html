America’s Trillion-Dollar Credit Card Debt Threatens To Lock Millions Out of the Housing Market

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

With prices still alarmingly high, it comes as no surprise that Americans are increasingly turning to credit cards more than ever.

But doing so could be silently sabotaging any hope of owning a home, if you’re not careful.

Last week, the Federal Reserve Bank of New York published its latest household debt report.

Collectively, credit card balances climbed by $22 billion in the third quarter to a record $1.29 trillion. Balances are up 4.2% from a year earlier, and total household debt, including mortgages, auto loans, and student loans, expanded by $334 billion for the period to a record $19.1 trillion.

High levels of debt are problematic for anyone, but most especially for first-time homebuyers. If your ambitions for 2027 include buying a home, it’s important to get your debt in check before it’s too late.

According to TransUnion’s most recent quarterly credit industry insights report, the number of people with credit cards rose 2% to 178 million up from 174.8 million.

The ease of use means most people turn to credit cards for their everyday purchases, like groceries, gas, and utility bills—all of which have seen a steep increase in prices over the past two years.

Now, if you’re someone who pays off their credit card in full each month, you’re in the clear. If not, the average interest rate on a new credit card offer currently stands at about 24%, and debt can accumulate quickly.

And this can cause an array of issues for first-time homebuyers.

"Issues arising from high levels of consumer debt are amplified for first-time homebuyers as they typically have less savings, limited financial history, and tighter financial conditions,” John Donikian, vice president of Best Interest Financial in West Bloomfield, MI, tells Realtor.com®.

Firstly, carrying too much debt can negatively affect your credit score, a vital metric in getting a mortgage.

While there is no firm number to qualify for a mortgage, a good credit score ranges from 700 to 749, and most conventional loans typically require at least 620. Lenders may accept lower scores if you have a larger down payment or other strong financial factors.

The higher your balances are, the more your score is dragged down, which can not only affect the interest rate a lender can offer you, but also keep you from getting a mortgage with some lenders entirely.

“Your credit score doesn’t just determine if you are approved; it influences your interest rate and the monthly payment you are offered,” says Jake Vehige, president of mortgage lending at Neighbors Bank.

“Even a small difference in interest rates can add up to thousands of dollars over the life of a loan.”

Secondly, as part of the process of getting a mortgage, lenders will look at your debt-to-income ratio to see how much house you can afford.

If a lender looks at your total monthly debt payments against your gross monthly income and your DTI exceeds its caps, your application could be delayed or even denied outright.

But both of those instances can be reversed, with time and a fixed budget that allows you to pay off your debt. In other words, your credit score can rebound.

Where first-time buyers are really at a disadvantage by accumulating so much credit card debt is the drain it will put on their savings.

The money that you’ll need to rack up to pay off the 20%-plus interest on your bills could have been money saved for your down payment, closing costs, and repair funds.

Many lenders also require proof of postclosing cash reserves, which is much harder to build while paying down high-interest debt.

“Ideally, you want to keep your credit card balances at no more than 20% to 30% of your maximum credit limit," explains Jeremy Schachter, branch manager at Fairway Independent Mortgage Corp. in Phoenix.

"For example, if you have a $1,000 limit, don’t go over a balance higher than $200 to $300. Anything that exceeds that amount can lower your credit score and make it difficult to get approved for a mortgage with a good rate.”
