After GOP's brutal midterms, ‘Chuck Norris of Florida politics’ tells party to 'get up and fight'

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Republican Lt. Gov. Jay Collins, a wounded combat veteran who learned to walk on a prosthetic leg, says the GOP must regroup by reaching more voters and staying true to its conservative values.

When an insurgent's bullet shattered his arm in combat, Republican Florida Lt. Gov. Jay Collins did not wait around for an evacuation team; the Green Beret performed emergency surgery on himself to save the limb.

After the midterms that Gov. Ron DeSantis called a "near extinction-level" event for Republicans, Collins sees a national GOP lying bloodied on the field. He advises his battered and beaten party to show the same refusal to die, and get up like a warrior.

"Where we go from here is up to us as a Republican Party," Collins, dubbed the "Chuck Norris of Florida politics" by DeSantis, told Fox News Digital in an interview just days after the midterm "bloodbath."

"This can be a definitive death blow if we allow it to be, but I believe in what we stand for." he continued. "I believe in our Constitution. I believe in our founding fathers' vision. I believe that God blessed this nation. I think we have to knuckle down, learn, apply this, and we have come out with a relentless dedication and fury to focus on what matters: To lead our state, to lead our country, and be what we need to be for the American people. That is what we should choose to do."

"Hard times happen. Warriors, leaders get up and fight. That's what we have to do now."

The metaphor is not subtle. It is a road map, set forth by DeSantis' two four-year terms – the last year of which was supplemented by Collins as his No. 2.

Collins said his home state's recent history of going from a purple state to a GOP stronghold is an example the rest of the country can follow.

"Florida's been the road map," he said. "I think we've been what I call the conservative proving grounds. I think that it's time for many here in Florida to use our voice and to be out there and help our party get back on their feet. Be resilient. Yeah, man, you got popped. You got hurt. Get back up, dust it off, and let's go lead. Let's learn."

While congressional Republicans scramble to assign blame for a devastating cycle of national losses – including the loss of Texas to Democrats – Florida has positioned itself as the country’s preeminent conservative fortress, now the largest red state on the 2028 map.

"Losing sucks, winning is awesome: Let's get back to more winning," he said. "We've just got to figure out how we're going to do that, and that's achievable. I think Florida is a blueprint. I think we have a responsibility to lead."

Under DeSantis, Florida shed its long-held "purple" swing-state identity to become the nation's largest, most disciplined red bastion. Collins looked back on how he pulled in the no party affiliated voters (NPAs), the valued middle that can flip races and states, in his state Senate race before being tapped to serve under DeSantis.

"Republicans showed up, but the NPAs showed up and they overwhelmingly did not align with Republicans," Collins said, admitting that even Florida's heavy Republican registered voter edge led to slim margins in the tough midterm environment. "What does that tell us? Well, the biggest gap we had was that we didn't spend enough time communicating to our NPAs."

Collins pointed back to how he won a Tampa race in a district "that is not a conservative juggernaut in terms of Florida," running against the Tampa mayor's mother-in-law.

"This was my very first race," he recalled. "We didn't talk to a single Republican out there. We only knocked NPA's and light D's, and we won by 10. What does that tell you? In a community like Tampa, I didn't hide from my views. I told people I'm conservative, I'm a Republican."

"What I realized is if you're honest with people, you agree on more than you don't, but you have got to be willing to step in there and have that conversation," he said. "And we did that over and over and again. And we built a base of people who trust us, because I don't play games."

"As a party, we need to go back to being the party that welcomes folks in – a little bit more of a big tent – but we have to stand on our conservative values," he added.

Collins is termed out of office along with DeSantis in January, but he will evaluate his future in the state or the national party apparatus. For now, he is committing to nothing and merely saying, "Stay tuned."

Regardless of candidate or party, Collins goes back to his military background and having risked his life and literally sacrificed limb for unity.

"I wore a flag on my shoulder every single day that I served in uniform," he said. "I wore the flag on my suit, really every single day that I was out there, serving in our state or serving our nation in any way that I could. We're the American people; we are blessed."

Once the party picks its 2028 candidate, Republicans need to be all-in against the opposition, Collins said.

"We have to row the boat," he said. "Sept. 11 was a big day in our life. Sept. 12 was incredible because we saw the power and the might of what America can be. When we rowed the boat, our enemy was fearful. They were on their heels because they saw an absolute juggernaut standing righteously to defend our people and our way of life. What our enemy fears, what other nations fear is Americans coming together."

"We have to come together and realize that our nation is blessed," he continued. "It will fall if we don't come together. But it won't happen if we don't start uniting and remind ourselves that this is America, we're allies, internal in our country, and we're going to make sure that it's the greatest country moving forward. America's worth fighting for."

"We need to focus on getting through this, the aftermath of this cycle, wrap it up, transition, and make sure that we understand exactly what happened," he concluded. "You know, when we started the war on terror, and I was a young soldier, we spent two years trying to fight the last war. It wasn't very great, to be honest with you.

"Let's not fight the last war. Let's understand the operational environment. Let's under our gaps. Our weaknesses and how we can clearly address those. And then we're going to have to move with a righteous fury."
