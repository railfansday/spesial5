Ocarina of Time Remake Actually Shows Its Entire Story Before Link's Adventure Begins

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Covering the video games industry since 2017, with experience in news, articles, and reviews. Currently a writer at DualShockers and gaming magazines.

One of the best things about revisiting a game like The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time is discovering little details you might have missed the first time around. The remake has already given fans plenty of reasons to explore every corner of Hyrule again, but one particularly interesting discovery might have been hiding in plain sight from the very beginning.

When visiting Princess Zelda for the first time as Child Link, players can spot a tapestry inside one of the windows in Hyrule Castle's courtyard. And this tapestry actually depicts the entire story of the remake, including events that players are only supposed to see after completing the adventure.

Even stranger, this is the same tapestry that can be unlocked through the game's Threads of Time feature after finishing the main story. In other words, the game is showing you the full journey before Link even takes his first steps toward becoming the Hero of Time!

Link has always been known as the silent hero of Hyrule, but the remake appears to have given him a very brief spoken line.

You just need to move the camera in the right direction to catch it (thankfully, the remake's new dedicated camera controls make that little discovery much easier). Here, take a look for yourself: see the tapestry in the original Reddit post.

This discovery is particularly interesting when you consider what happened in the Nintendo 3DS version of Ocarina of Time. Back then, the same window featured a mural referencing The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword, serving both as a bit of cross-promotion and a way to connect the lore of the two games. The remake, however, has replaced that reference with something much more specific to the adventure we're playing.

When visiting Princess Zelda for the first time as Child Link, players can spot a tapestry inside one of the windows in Hyrule Castle's courtyard.

And naturally, fans haven't wasted any time coming up with explanations! One of the most popular theories suggests that the tapestry is further evidence that we're literally reliving the legend of the Hero of Time. After all, if the entire story is already depicted inside the castle, perhaps the events of Ocarina of Time are not simply unfolding for the first time. They're a story that Hyrule's people already know, one that keeps being told and experienced across generations.

Others have taken the discovery as a reason to look at the game's story from a different perspective. If the Royal Family knew what was coming, perhaps they also knew that Ganondorf would eventually be defeated by the Hero of Time. In that case, accepting the events as they were foretold might have seemed safer than trying to interfere with a fate they believed was already written.

Nintendo has expanded one of the original Zelda: Ocarina of Time's most heartbreaking side stories.

But that interpretation also raises an amusing question: why does Zelda still tell Link that her father doesn't believe Ganondorf is up to no good? I mean, he may not completely believe her, but the tapestry clearly shows the Gerudo king's eventual transformation into a monstrous beast and his attempt to kill both Link and Zelda! You'd think that would be enough to convince the king to be a little more suspicious.

Either way, we have another theory, which is probably the most creative and crazy one (so, naturally, also my personal favorite). Some players believe that the tapestry appears unusually far to the left inside the castle corridor, leaving what looks like plenty of empty wall space beside it. And if you've been following the community's ongoing hunt for clues about a potential Majora's Mask remake, you can probably guess where this is going!

Yes, some fans believe that the empty space is intentional, potentially leaving room for more tapestries depicting other legendary adventures. The next one could very well represent Majora's Mask, especially if Nintendo is planning another remake using the same approach as Ocarina of Time.

Of course, there's no confirmation that this is what Nintendo has in mind, and an empty wall is hardly definitive evidence of another Zelda remake. Still, I can't help but love the idea that this seemingly harmless decoration is actually a little tease of what's coming next!
