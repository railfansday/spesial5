Recently, researchers used AI to "decode" segments of birdsong. In the wake of this, Live Science asks its readers which animal they'd want to communicate with.

The idea of talking to animals like Dr. Dolittle might sound far-fetched, but researchers are making progress toward understanding how other species communicate. In a recent Live Science interview, University of California, Berkeley ethologist Julie Elie explained how she used artificial intelligence (AI) and behavioral experiments to decode the calls of zebra finches (Taeniopygia guttata). For instance, Elie was able to determine which calls the birds used to communicate over short distances versus longer distances.

Birds aren't the only animals scientists are hoping to understand. Researchers with Project CETI are using AI to decipher the clicks and calls of sperm whales, while a separate team (with the search for extraterrestrial intelligence, or SETI) conducted what they described as the first "conversation" between humans and a humpback whale. The whale, named Twain, responded to recorded calls played by researchers over a 20 minute period, a "conversation" scientists believe could help us figure out how to communicate with extraterrestrial life someday.

Closer to home, cats have already figured out how to use meows to get their human's attention, whether they're demanding food, affection or simply that we open the door for them (again). Whether this means they understand the human language or not is still up for debate, but it raises an interesting question: If we could understand what animals were saying, which species would you most want to talk to?
