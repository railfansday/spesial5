Could Anthropic’s blockbuster IPO help revive crypto? Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse thinks the listing could remove pressure from digital assets, but his theory needs something more convincing than timing.

Speaking to CNBC, Garlinghouse suggested investors may have sold cryptocurrency to free up capital for Anthropic’s November 19 market debut. The catch? There’s no evidence establishing that connection.

Anthropic priced 980 million Class A shares at $83 each, representing approximately $81.3 billion in total share value. The offering included shares sold by both the company and existing shareholders.

Not all that money goes to Anthropic. Existing investors sold 23.65 million shares, and the company won’t receive proceeds from those sales.

The stock began trading on Nasdaq under ticker ANT on November 19, after pricing above its marketed range of $70 to $78.

That demonstrates substantial demand for AI exposure. It doesn’t reveal whether buyers funded their purchases by selling crypto, equities or other assets.

Bitcoin dropped toward $62,000 on November 18 before recovering above $63,000 early the next day.

Garlinghouse compared the situation with the recovery following SpaceX’s June public listing. His argument is that major IPOs can temporarily absorb investment capital before liquidity returns elsewhere.

That explanation makes intuitive sense, yet it overlooks bigger market drivers.

Interest-rate expectation, institutional fund flows, derivatives positioning and broader liquidity conditions also influence crypto prices. Blaming one IPO for market weakness ignores those moving parts.

The post-listing test is whether investment demand actually improves. US spot Bitcoin ETF flows can reveal changes in institutional demand, while futures activity offers another view of market positioning.

Neither indicator proves that Anthropic investors are returning money to crypto.

Garlinghouse’s Anthropic IPO crypto rebound theory remains plausible, but unverified. Until spot demand, ETF inflows and liquidity strengthen, the listing is a possible explanation for selling pressure not a confirmed catalyst for recovery.
