According to one of the architects of the XRP Ledger, now Ripple CTO Emeritus David Schwartz, his retirement from active development almost a year ago came at the right time. "I picked a great time to retire!", said Schwartz, reacting to the latest report from OpenAI researchers, which touches on testing a new cyber model. Just one autonomous run and the AI completely changed the familiar standards of network security, OpenAI researcher Eric Wallace confirms.

The report shows how OpenAI's pipeline works. First thing to understand, it's a fully complete cycle: the system independently finds a potential bug, verifies it, writes a working exploit to confirm it and automatically releases a patch. This was tested on the Linux kernel, the core software that runs most of the planet's servers (including OpenAI's own compute clusters).

The Web3 sphere is one where open-source code is the foundation of blockchain security, so this development is a challenge to say the least. If AI finds zero-day vulnerabilities in a matter of hours, manual code auditing by humans becomes useless.

Yes, Schwartz stepped down from the post of Ripple CTO into honorary retirement last November, but he hasn't abandoned his terminal. Just recently he showed monitoring charts of his personal server hub on the XRP Ledger network. There, a node is running under colossal load: it maintains more than 550 active connections (peers), shows latency of around 200ms and processes about 110,000 incoming messages per second.

Besides, Schwartz continues personal work on the network's internal architectural problems. In a discussion with active validators, Schwartz detailed a transaction queue bug that caused the local load metric on his server to jump to 540, triggering fee spikes within the ledger.

Schwartz picked the "right time" to step back from Ripple, but he's clearly not out of the game.
