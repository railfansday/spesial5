I’ve written extensively about sunscreen since 2019, most recently in our guide to SPF facts, spray sunscreen, and how one dermatologist uses it as a dry shampoo hack. But if you’re looking for a one-and-done power list of the best sunscreens of 2026, this is it.

And sunscreen’s benefits don’t stop there. Sun damage accumulates from daily activities like driving and sitting in front of a window, says dermatologist Hadley King, MD. That means even during the fall and winter, we should still double down on sunscreen. Here’s how to buy SPF like a dermatologist and beauty editor.

The impressive ingredient list. Allow Dr. Goldenberg to explain. "Isdin's SPF contains ‘repairsomes,’ which help with reversing sun damage in skin cells," he says. That makes this a great pick for anyone with hyperpigmentation or sun spots, especially since the formula has a glowy, tinted finish.

I had a light sunburn last summer, and my dermatologist suggested I use this SPF. After I slathered on moisturizer and layered on this liquid sunscreen during the day, I noticed my skin was less red after just two days. I was floored because I hadn’t used any new products other than the Isdin Eryfotona Ageless. A year later, and I can confirm it’s still well worth using. My skin never breaks out, the lightweight formula absorbs quickly, and it’s water-resistant for active lifestyles. It’s also available in tinted and untinted options.

It absorbs in just a few minutes and mattifies my oily T-zone and leaves behind a soft, luminous finish. Not to mention, it doesn’t cause breakouts or irritate my skin when I’m dealing with seasonal changes and sensitivities.

If I had to be nitpicky, then the only thing I dislike about Isdin is the price. However, there’s a smaller 1.7 fl. oz bottle to help ease the pain. I’ll continue to repurchase this sunscreen because it works fabulously with my oily, acne-prone skin. It’s also helpful for my 50-something mom’s mature skin. I also converted our visual editor, Jason Speakman, into a fan—he noticed a decrease in facial redness over six months of daily use.

Dermatologists advise applying two finger-lengths of sunscreen—about a quarter-sized amount—to your face and neck.

Ultraviolet radiation from the sun falls into two categories: ultraviolet A (UVA) and ultraviolet B (UVB). UVA rays cause signs of aging, such as hyperpigmentation, lines, and wrinkles. Meanwhile, UVB rays can cause more serious damage, including sunburn and skin cancer. Your safest bet is to only use broad-spectrum sunscreens to help protect against UVA and UVB rays.

Next, choose either a mineral or a chemical sunscreen. Here’s more insight to help you determine between the two formulations.

They both have benefits and limitations, but ultimately, the best one is the sunscreen you’ll actually use and reapply. As for me? Well, I like both types. However, I typically use mineral sunscreen on my face, so I can head outside immediately after work instead of lingering for the approximately 15-minute waiting period associated with chemical sunscreens.

Broad-spectrum coverage, sunscreen type, and SPF coverage are the big three considerations. But another factor worth emphasizing is other non-active skincare ingredients that will help bolster your complexion. Dermatologist Elyse Love, MD, is a big fan of niacinamide to calm the skin and improve its tone and texture. Also, look for ingredients that support your skin type and goals. For example, if you want to brighten your complexion, you'll look for a sunscreen with vitamin C. If you have dry skin, seek out hydrating ingredients like hyaluronic acid.

Whether you have oily, dry, or combination skin, there's something on our list for you. Meet our team’s all-time favorite sunscreens that we’ll continue to rely on throughout 2026.
