The gambling landscape has changed dramatically over a short period of time. Prediction markets (with a minimum age of 18), sports betting (complete with micro-bets), fantasy sports betting, iCasinos, and gambling activities within video games make gambling more accessible and more convenient than ever before. Indeed, online gambling “allows continuous and immediate access to gambling from virtually anywhere” (Forrest et al., 2026).

Adolescents have likely been exposed to online gambling in more ways than their parents might expect. Therefore, it is important for parents/caregivers to broach this topic to dispel myths about gambling, present accurate information about potential risks, and offer guidance. Once the conversation has begun, it is important for parents to listen with curiosity and care. Rather than using fear tactics, parents can provide accurate information about online gambling and sports betting to help adolescents understand the potential risks. Importantly, the goal is to facilitate a dialogue with youth, rather than having a one-way conversation. Parents can draw their adolescent into the conversation by asking: Why do they believe people are drawn to online gambling and sports betting? What are some myths they have heard about gambling? In what ways are gambling operators trying to make their products as enticing as possible? What might they do if a friend discloses that they have gotten into some trouble with gambling?

Finally, it is always helpful for parents to ask adolescents about their goals, short-term and long-term (e.g., what are their professional goals? Health-related goals? Financial goals? Spiritual goals?) and to explore their values. In this way, youth can recognize the discrepancy between their goals/values and gambling behaviors, and this recognition can serve as motivation not to engage (Miller & Rollnick, 2023).

Parents may be hesitant to start this conversation with their adolescent due to the fear they won’t have all the answers or may not say all the “right things.” Yet, the goal is not for parents to use the perfect words; instead, the goal is to open the lines of communication to encourage ongoing conversations with adolescents about the risk behaviors they face today. Saying the right thing is less important than showing adolescents they care about their experiences and are available to talk and listen.
