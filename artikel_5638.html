Franklin Templeton Will Use OpenAI ChatGPT to Predict Crypto

In early November 2026, OpenAI published a detailed customer story highlighting how Franklin Templeton, the global asset manager overseeing roughly $1.8 trillion in assets, is integrating advanced AI tools into its investment research process.

The firm is not simply experimenting with chatbots for note-taking. It is systematically building AI-powered workflows to help portfolio managers compare competing research views, surface overlooked risks, and make better-informed investment decisions.

According to Katrina Dudley, Senior Vice President of Public Market Investments at Franklin Templeton, the biggest unlock has been merging continuous intelligence with human judgment.

Earlier attempts at AI involved feeding data into a database and connecting models to it, but the results fell short because the models lacked context about how the data interrelated. The breakthrough came when the firm added agents and a semantic layer, a structured model that defines what the firm’s data means and how different pieces connect.

Without shared meaning, AI keeps guessing. Franklin Templeton only got useful research agents after they built a semantic layer — what the data means and how it connects — before scaling. Define meaning first, then let the agents run. https://t.co/3Ju9nXtdW1

Franklin Templeton is utilizing OpenAI’s Codex to create a “connected intelligence layer” that integrates inputs from research, risk, compliance, and trading teams, providing portfolio managers with a comprehensive view of investment ideas. This new workflow emphasizes continuous intelligence, allowing analysts and managers to engage with data in real time.

Dudley highlights the firm’s commitment to avoiding the filter-bubble issue common in consumer AI by ensuring tools present diverse opinions and risks. Franklin Templeton adopts a structured approach to deployment, pairing technical experts with practitioners in internal hackathons.

Promising projects undergo security reviews and expert support before production. The firm encourages experimentation within clear risk boundaries, recognizing that not every initiative will succeed, but each should yield valuable insights.

Ultimately, the goal is to create a governed, reusable intelligence foundation that allows teams to focus on gathering insights while AI handles synthesis and pattern detection. This initiative is part of the firm’s broader digital-assets strategy, including proactive moves in crypto investments.

As the largest cryptocurrency by market capitalization, Bitcoin attracts a vast audience of holders and investors. Bitcoin Hyper aims to leverage this liquidity through a Layer 2 network designed to facilitate faster transactions and decentralized finance (DeFi) functionality.

Bitcoin Hyper’s architecture uses the Solana Virtual Machine (SVM), bringing Solana-style execution capabilities to a Bitcoin-centric ecosystem.

The goal is straightforward: combine Bitcoin’s substantial liquidity and established investor base with transaction speeds suitable for modern decentralized applications. Key features include near-instant finality and low transaction costs.

HYPER is also gaining attention due to its presale pricing. The project has reportedly raised over $33M, indicating strong early fundraising momentum. Additionally, it offers 35% APY staking rewards, incentivizing early participants to stake their tokens and earn more HYPER instead of leaving their allocations idle.

With the planned launch date of November 26 approaching, the presale is entering a crucial phase. Investors seeking exposure to Bitcoin’s expanding Layer 2 ecosystem are eagerly anticipating Hyper’s market debut before it gets listed on centralized exchanges.

Prior to Franklin Templeton using OpenAI tools to predict digital asset prices, its team has been optimistic about crypto’s long-term potential. Sandy Kaul, Head of Digital Assets and Innovation, predicts the market could expand from its $4T peak to $20T-$30T, driven by the rebuilding of financial markets on blockchain and the accelerated tokenization of real-world assets.

Chris Perkins, head of Franklin Crypto, emphasizes a more measured near-term outlook, noting that investors are focusing on individual tokens’ fundamentals rather than viewing the sector as a whole.

Robert Crossley, global head of industry advisory services, points out that traditional investors are increasingly looking to crypto for diversification.

Franklin Templeton is advancing its financial infrastructure transformation through blockchain and has launched products in this space. Their strategy combines cutting-edge AI with a strong focus on digital assets, reflecting their belief in the future importance of blockchain and tokenization in capital markets.

The success of these predictions relies on regulatory clarity and institutional adoption, but the firm is committed to leveraging AI and digital infrastructure as key elements of its strategy.
