The Best Foldable Phones to Give This Holiday Season

For the tech enthusiast in your life, a foldable phone could be an interesting alternative to a traditional smartphone. We've rounded up our top picks, from book-style designs to flip phones.

If you’re shopping for someone this holiday season who deserves something a little more special — and you’ve got the budget to spare — a foldable phone can be a unique and practical gift that your loved one will use long after the holidays are over.

After reviewing our fair share of foldable phones, we’ve narrowed down our top three picks for the holidays and the perfect person on your list for each one. Who knows, you might end up caving in and treating yourself to a brand-new device? (You’ve been warned.)

For more mobile gift ideas, from budget-friendly smartphones to wearable devices, be sure to check out the video below.

The iPhone Duo is far from the first foldable phone, but as Apple’s debut into the market, it’s generated plenty of buzz. The Duo’s superpower is its ability to integrate hardware and software, creating a seamless experience across both displays.

The compact, 5.4-inch cover screen is great for sending texts and emails and checking notifications. The more expansive 7.6-inch inner display is ideal for multitasking, watching movies and working. It feels like using a smaller version of an iPad Mini that you can just fold up and toss in your pocket or bag.

The Duo is a great gift for someone who’s said they want a foldable phone, but refused to get one of the long-standing Android options already on the market. At last, Apple has its own offering that lets you stick with iOS. It’s a handy pick for people who often work on their phones and don’t always want to lug around a laptop. It’s also good for anyone who watches a lot of movies and TV shows on their mobile device. Plus, as a phone-tablet hybrid, it’s just fun to use.

The iPhone Duo isn’t perfect. It doesn’t support Face ID, but Apple did recently add an option to unlock the phone using the front camera. This isn’t as secure as Face ID, so you’ll still need to use Touch ID or a passcode for more sensitive functions like using Apple Pay or accessing passwords, and after around 45 minutes of inactivity.

The Duo’s cameras also aren’t as advanced as what you’ll get with the iPhone 18 Pro models, which have larger main-camera sensors and more advanced optical zoom capabilities. There’s also no telephoto camera on the Duo. Still, most people will be happy with the Duo’s 48-megapixel wide and ultrawide rear cameras, as well as the 12-megapixel selfie camera on the cover screen. The FaceTime camera on the inner display is best suited for video calls rather than snapping quality photos.

Multitasking on the Duo is seamless, but it’s not as flexible as it is on other foldable phones. You can only have two apps open side by side, and you can’t readjust them.

Perhaps the biggest downside is the price. The iPhone Duo starts at $1,999 for 256GB of storage. That’s why it’s a good idea to keep an eye out for any deals that can help lower the price.

For the Android-lover in your life, there’s no better foldable pick than Samsung’s Galaxy Z Fold 8. Like the iPhone Duo, this phone has a passport-size design. (Technically, Samsung beat Apple to the punch, but let’s refrain from the “Who Did It First?” game for now.)

The Z Fold 8 has a 5.5-inch cover screen and a 7.6-inch inner display that’s great for watching videos, working and multitasking with up to three apps, which you can resize to your liking.

It’s also very thin, measuring 9.7mm thick when folded and 4.5mm thick when unfolded. That means it feels similar in thickness to a standard nonfolding phone, even when it’s closed. The Z Fold 8 fits squarely in a palm and easily slides into a pocket – tiny women’s pockets included.

Like the iPhone Duo, the Z Fold 8 doesn’t have a telephoto camera. But the 50-megapixel wide and ultrawide cameras on the back and the 10-megapixel selfie cameras do a solid job.

The Z Fold 8 is also priced on the higher end, starting at $1,900 for 256GB. But its compact, sleek design and versatility help to make up for that sticker shock. Again, it’s a good idea to look out for any deals, especially around the holidays.

Some people prefer flip phones for their nostalgic designs and compact size. Motorola has plenty to choose from, but the best and most affordable pick for most people is the baseline 2026 Razr.

The phone features a classic clamshell design and a spacious 3.6-inch cover screen, making it easy to check notifications, reply to texts, control music or follow navigation without opening the phone. The 6.9-inch inner display feels like using an average nonfolding phone, even if the screen is a little narrower and taller.

For most people, the Razr’s 50-megapixel wide and ultrawide cameras and 32-megapixel selfie camera on the inner display will do the trick. There’s no room for a telephoto camera, but the Razr pulls its weight for more casual photography.

The Razr’s processor isn’t as powerful as what you’ll get on the pricier Razr Plus and Razr Ultra, but as long as you’re not playing intensive games all day, you probably won’t notice the difference.

The Razr’s $800 starting price is significantly lower than the cost of book-style folding devices like the iPhone Duo and Galaxy Z Fold 8. The Razr could be a more appealing option for someone who doesn’t need a phone-tablet hybrid but just wants a phone that’s fun to use and taps into the nostalgia of the original Motorola Razr.

No matter which phone you choose, your lucky recipient will enjoy the holiday spirit long after the season has passed, all while making quite the statement.
