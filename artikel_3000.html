Workers at Grand Theft Auto VI developer Rockstar Games won’t unionize, after the vote for a formal recognition agreement failed, with only 25 percent of votes in favor of unionization. The Independent Workers Union of Great Britain (IWGB) said that Rockstar workers in the union will continue organizing even without formal recognition.

Voting ran from November 12th through 26th, covering the launch of GTA VI. If successful, the vote would have established a formal framework for collective negotiation with the studio over pay, overtime, bonuses, leave, and pensions, along with a right to consultation over future changes including AI implementation and layoffs. Without it, Rockstar won’t have an obligation to meet union representatives, though workers and union members can still lobby for change informally.

“For anyone at Rockstar who believes in a fairer workplace, a fairer sector and a fairer society - this isn’t a sign to retreat, it’s a sign to double down on what we stand for,” IWGB said in a statement sent to The Verge. “Hundreds of our coworkers voted in favour of our union at Rockstar, yet sadly we recognise we have more work to do to build understanding and win the trust of more of our colleagues. We’re ready to put in the effort to make that happen.”

When Rockstar fired 31 IWGB members last year, the union accused it of “the most blatant and ruthless act of union busting in the history of the games industry.” That case has gone to a UK employment tribunal, with a judgment still pending.

“We are extraordinarily proud of our team, the culture we’ve built together and the launch of Grand Theft Auto VI,” Rockstar head of publishing Jennifer Kolbe told Bloomberg News. “This result reflects our team’s strong support for continuing the direct relationships, collaboration and open dialogue that have helped make Rockstar Games so innovative and strong. As we look ahead, we’re excited to build on all we’ve accomplished together.”
