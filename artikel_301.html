Najib Razak, Ex-Prime Minister of Malaysia, Is Released to House Arrest

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Najib Razak, who was brought down in the multibillion-dollar 1MDB scandal, was released from prison to serve the rest of his sentence in his mansion.

Zunaira Saieed reported from Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, and Richard C. Paddock from Bangkok.

Malaysia’s disgraced former prime minister, Najib Razak, whose role in the colossal theft of public funds earned him the nickname “Man of Steal,” was released from prison on Tuesday. He was let out under a royal pardon that allows him to serve the remainder of his six-year sentence at the mansion he calls home.

Mr. Najib, 73, who has spent the past four years in prison on corruption charges, was delivered on Tuesday night to his home in one of the most exclusive neighborhoods of the capital, Kuala Lumpur. The entire 49,000-square-foot property was officially declared a federal prison on Friday in preparation for his homecoming.

Voters ousted Mr. Najib, the son of one prime minister and the nephew of another, in 2018. He was later convicted of graft, after $4.5 billion disappeared from 1Malaysia Development Berhad, the state investment fund that he founded and oversaw. The scandal brought down his government, but subsequent royal pardons and his move to house arrest have raised accusations of special treatment for elites.

“It is an absolute mockery of justice,” Suresh Kumar, a property manager in Kuala Lumpur. “The law works for the rich and powerful and not for the poor.”

Mr. Najib arrived at his mansion about 9:30 p.m. in a silver Proton, a Malaysian-made SUV, accompanied by nine other cars and two motorcycles. Dozens of reporters who had gathered to witness his return were kept at bay by police officers who had blocked off the street near his house.

Mr. Najib is expected to remain under house arrest until August 2028. His legal problems, however, continue. He is due to begin serving a 15-year sentence on separate corruption charges related to the scandal when his current term ends. The High Court of Malaysia also ordered him to pay a staggering $2.8 billion fine. He is appealing that conviction.
