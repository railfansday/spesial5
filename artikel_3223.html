Several of the headphones on sale are relatively new, including Nothing's Headphone (1) Pro and Bose's second-generation QuietComfort Headphones, both of which launched this fall. Better yet, most of the deals below have dropped to their lowest prices yet on Amazon.

There's a good mix to choose from, too, whether you're looking for premium noise-canceling headphones for flights and commutes, a pair with enough battery life to get you through a week of listening, or something inexpensive for everyday use, with prices starting under $50. The Nothing Headphone (a) is down to a record-low $109 from $199, making it a solid option for everyday listening, especially for Android users who can take advantage of LDAC for higher-quality wireless audio. For more on it, check out this PCMag review.

Spending another $60 gets you the Nothing Headphone (1), now $169 from $299, which is also a record low. It offers a more premium listening experience with active noise cancellation, automatic playback pausing when you take the headphones off, and the option to listen through either USB-C or a 3.5mm cable, making it a well-rounded choice for everyday use. For more on it, check out this PCMag review.

Nothing's newest and most expensive over-ears, the Headphone (1) Pro, are already $80 off at $319, only a couple of months after launching at $399. This is the pair to get for sound quality, since Nothing moved to a three-driver setup in each earcup, and the result is a fuller, more detailed sound than either the Headphone (1) or Headphone (a). For more on it, check out this PCMag review.

Sony's flagship WH-1000XM6 headphones are down to $378 from $459.99, their lowest price yet. Noise cancellation is still the main reason to buy them, and they remain among the best options for blocking out engine rumble on a flight, traffic during a commute, or chatter in a busy office. For more on these over-ears, you can check out our in-depth review.

Sony's WH-CH730N brings noise cancellation down to a much more approachable price, and at $98, down from $179.99, it's the lowest it's been on Amazon. This 2026 update to Sony's budget noise-canceling line now folds up for travel, which the previous model couldn't do, and battery life has jumped to up to 50 hours with noise cancellation on.

Sony's WH-CH530 is the most affordable pair in this roundup at $38, down from $69.99—that's also the lowest it's been since its release this fall. These are simple on-ear headphones without noise cancellation, but they sound better than you'd expect at this price, and the battery lasts up to 55 hours, so a full week of commuting or classes shouldn't be a problem.

The second-generation Bose QuietComfort Headphones are down to $199 from $359, their lowest price yet. That's a substantial discount for a pair that delivers on two of Bose's biggest strengths: comfort and noise cancellation. You can read PCMag's review for a closer look at how they perform.

Bose's flagship QuietComfort Ultra headphones (2nd gen) are down to $339 from $449, saving you $110. Their noise cancellation is among the best you'll find in consumer headphones, making them particularly appealing for frequent flyers or anyone who spends a lot of time in noisy environments. For more on their features and performance, you can check out PCMag's review.

The 2026 JBL Tune 680NC are down to $49.95 from $109.95, their lowest price to date. That's a pretty good deal for anyone looking for affordable noise-canceling headphones, especially since you get adaptive noise cancellation that adjusts to your surroundings and up to 50 hours of battery life with it enabled.

For the same price, JBL's Tune 730BT trades noise cancellation for a larger over-ear fit and even longer battery life, rated at up to 76 hours. At $49.95, down from $89.95, it's at its lowest price on Amazon, and a sensible choice for anyone who mostly listens at home, at a desk, or on quieter commutes where noise cancellation isn't essential.

The JBL Live 780NC are down to $129.95 from $249.95, making them considerably more appealing than at their original price. Battery life is a major advantage here, with JBL estimating up to 50 hours with noise cancellation enabled and 80 hours without, so frequent charging shouldn't be a concern. They also support LDAC for higher-quality Bluetooth audio with compatible devices. For more on their features and performance, you can check out PCMag's review.

Apple’s AirPods Max 2 are down to $449 from $549, saving you $100 on the second-generation over-ears that Apple released earlier this year. While the design hasn’t changed much from the original, the upgrade to Apple’s H2 chip brings improved noise cancellation, while its sound remains among the best you'll find in wireless headphones. You can read PCMag's review for a closer look at how they perform.

Soundcore's Q20i headphones are down to $42.69 from $69.99, making them one of the more affordable options for anyone looking for noise-canceling over-ears from a recognizable brand. They're particularly worth considering for students, occasional travelers, or anyone who wants a budget-friendly backup pair.

The Beats Solo 4 are down to $129.95 from $199.95, saving you $70. While these lightweight on-ear headphones don't have active noise cancellation, they offer plenty of flexibility for everyday listening, whether you're using an iPhone or an Android phone, with quick pairing on both platforms, notes this PCMag review.
