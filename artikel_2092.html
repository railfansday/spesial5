Arsenal's complacency and a lack of ambition allowed Newcastle a way back and the Gunners were fortunate to escape with a point after paying the price for not killing the game, writes CRAIG HOPE

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Published: 15:17 EST, 21 November 2026 | Updated: 16:15 EST, 21 November 2026

Do not invite the hostility, Arsenal were warned. Do not prod the bear. Do not start Bruno Guimaraes. The champions, with a belligerence of their own, ignored all of that and put the grizzly to sleep in a typically smothering first half. Their mistake was in thinking it was dead.

And so, awakened by a half-time rocket from head coach Matthias Jaissle - including three, inspirational changes - Newcastle somehow led by the 62nd minute.

By this point, the two teams were unrecognisable from those who had played out a one-sided opening period, after which Arsenal were 1-0 in front. Mikel Arteta would not have settled for a draw then.

‘We were exceptional in the first half,’ he later said. ‘We have won matches playing much worse than we did here. The intensity, the aggression – we should have been 3-0 or 4-0 up. But right from the start of the second half, we weren’t at the races. We lost duels, gave the ball away and gave them belief.’

Arteta had Noni Madueke to thank for rescuing the point. The winger was his best player throughout and, had he earlier shown the same incision that produced his 78th-minute equaliser, Arsenal would have won. They would have won by half-time, in fact.

So dominant were they, maybe it was complacency. Or maybe there is a lesson here in showing more bloodthirst when on top.

Arteta, you feel, gets more of a kick from suffocating opponents. But during a pulsating second half in which St James’ Park throbbed to the beat of their brilliant team, the hosts grabbed the pillowcase.

Noni Madueke saved Arsenal's blushes with an equaliser against Newcastle in a tough second half on Saturday

‘The second half was pure belief, pure conviction, pure trust in the team-mate,’ said Jaissle, under increased scrutiny amid the presence of chairman Yasir Al-Rumayyan.

‘There were so many ball-wins with a vertical approach and causing them problems. This is the Newcastle DNA.’

Former Newcastle captain Guimaraes - booed after each of his 36, ineffectual touches - was hooked in the 70th minute, and Arsenal were better for their own changes.

The league leaders can absorb this result, of course. Of greater concern for Arteta is an injury to David Raya that requires an MRI scan.

The goalkeeper left the pitch at the same time as Guimaraes, not because of performance, but clutching his shoulder, the upshot of a robust challenge from William Osula. That home forcefulness typified their second half, if not their first.

If Newcastle were to beat Arsenal, so suggested the crumbs of pre-match optimism, they would have to start fast.

At least, that was the blueprint of old on red-hot nights such as this. Newcastle started slow. Arsenal weren’t much quicker, but they didn’t have to be. They moved through the gears as the half unfolded and, with it, they began to move through Newcastle.

Arteta likes this sort of game, when it feels like minimum expenditure for maximum return. In short, it was easy. A training-ground drill. Attack versus defence.

Come half-time, Newcastle had not touched the ball in Arsenal’s penalty area. They simply weren’t allowed to.

Saying that, even with permission they would have found a way to pass it straight out of play or shank a cross into the Leazes End.

Newcastle’s first half really was that short on inspiration. There wasn’t too much perspiration, either. And so the home fans booed on the whistle, for a fifth half on the spin at St James’.

Arsenal led by one, and for them that should have been a source of regret.

The goal came when Lewis Hall, despite screams from team-mate Dan Burn to ‘head it, head it’, decided to control, clumsily, on his chest, allowing Kai Havertz to steal. He freed Eberechi Eze and the first-time finish was routine.

The punishment of Hall’s error, however, was ruthless. Six days after bettering Lamine Yamal, England left back Hall was struggling to contain Madueke. In fact, he and each of his comrades could not get close to any of those in red.

So, it was as if two sides had swapped shirts after the break. From nowhere, Newcastle attacked. That may sound basic, but this was seismic. The exclusion zone that had been Arsenal’s penalty box was no longer off limits.

Suddenly, they were able to run, tackle and, yes, shoot. From passive to aggressive, they drew level just four minutes after the restart.

Joe Willock hurried Odegaard into a mistake, William Osula pounced on the loose ball and, when Harvey Barnes was set free, he was not going to miss.

The turnaround was complete when defender Malick Thiaw forced over the goal-line from a corner which Arsenal had failed to defend - Dan Burn’s free header told you that someone, somewhere was not doing their job.

'We needed players with more energy and physicality,’ said Jaissle, explaining his half-time changes.

‘The tactics did not change, it was purely the front footed behaviour. Throw away the doubts, then you saw what is possible. We had Arsenal shaking. I hope we can carry on this feeling.’

Newcastle looked the most likely winners until Madueke burst inside two home jerseys and fired through Lukas Hornicek from 18 yards. Still, the hosts came back for more.

Arsenal had arrived intent on silencing St James’. In the end, they were grateful for the sound of the full-time whistle.
