Saquon Barkley healthier after bye, hoping to salvage his season

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Saquon Barkley healthier after bye, hoping to salvage his season originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

Before the bye week, Saquon Barkley was battling through hamstring and back injuries for a few games.

As the Eagles get ready for the second half of their season, Barkley is definitely feeling healthier. But it doesn't sound like he's 100 percent, nor will he be.

"I wish the bye week was like a magic pill and everything just vanishes," Barkley said. "But that's only in la-la land, I guess. But it definitely did help."

Barkley, 29, has had a disappointing season. Injuries have limited him, but he has just 69 rushing attempts for 296 yards and 3 touchdowns. It's obviously not all his fault, of course. Barkley is playing behind a struggling offensive line in a dysfunctional offense.

But he also doesn't think he is personally playing to his capabilities.

"I think the biggest thing you could do in a bye week is go back and watch yourself on film," Barkley said. "Watch other football, watch other games and see how well other backs are doing. And just be honest with yourself. If I'm being honest with myself, the last couple weeks, for whatever reason, I don't like the way I was playing and what I was putting out on the field.

"The way I go about changing that is the only way I know how to, just put my head down and go to work, just continue to be consistent. But it's got to look different. It's got to look different than what it was in '24 and '25 because every season is going to be different challenges. Felt really good this week to be able to have a full week, go out there and practice and put the work in. And hopefully I see the reward on Sunday."

The last two games for Barkley haven't gone well. Against the Commanders, he had 13 carries for 30 yards and against the Giants, he had 13 carries for 22 yards. His yards-per-carry average dropped from 5.6 to 4.3 on the season after those two losses.

So Barkley and his teammates happily welcomed a bye week on the heels of a seven-game losing streak.

"I think it was helpful for everyone," Barkley said. "From staff to everyone that works in this building to players. Sometimes when a season is not going the way you wanted it to go and you're facing a lot of adversity, that week off is really important. More so for just the mind also. Obviously, the body. It's good for the body. But just the mind and getting your mind somewhere else a little bit. And then regroup and come back ready to work."

As the Eagles look toward the last eight games of the season, they are in the basement of the NFC East with a 2-7 record. They are not technically dead just yet, but it would take a mini miracle for them to pull themselves out of this.

But that's easy to say on the outside. For the guys in the locker room, they have to keep fighting even after they eventually get eliminated. That starts with Sunday against a Steelers team that ranks third in the NFL in rushing defense.

"One game at a time," Barkley said. "I said it already, I'm going to say it again. We put ourselves in this situation. Only way we're going to get out of it is by digging ourselves out. I still believe we have the men and women in this locker room and in this building to get it done. … Technically, we're still in the division and obviously has that crossed my mind? Of course. But can't even take it there.

"Just have to be focused on one game at at time and staying present and taking advantage of the opportunities and this opportunity we have coming up against a really good team who's trying to get back on track and definitely didn't like the way that they played on national television."
