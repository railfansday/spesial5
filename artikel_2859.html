The Washington Nationals will have a competition for the backup catcher spot

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

One piece of news from about a week ago that slid under the radar was that the Nats are going to have an open competition for the backup catcher job. That was something that Spencer Nusbaum reported coming out of the GM meetings. Dylan Crews and CJ Abrams trade rumors took up more oxygen, but this is an interesting development to me.

Before this, I had assumed that the Nats would roll with Keibert Ruiz and Harry Ford as the catching tandem. Ford was not particularly impressive, but he got on base at a solid clip and has plenty of pedigree. He was also a big trade target for Paul Toboni last offseason. I figured that Ford would get another shot.

When push comes to shove, I think Ford will end up with the backup catcher job, but it is clear that it will not be handed to him. Having a competition is a good idea in my opinion. It forces Ford and the other catchers to be at the top of their game entering Spring Training.

This also sends a clear message to Ford that last season was not good enough. While plenty of Nats hitters broke out in 2026, Ford took a step back. After hitting .283 with an .868 OPS in Triple-A last year, Ford only hit .223 with a .705 OPS in his second stint at the level. Obviously, it is not a good sign to regress while repeating a level.

His power took a major step back in 2026, to the point where I wonder how healthy he was. There were rumblings about a shoulder injury, but he never missed any serious time. While his numbers in AAA were unimpressive, it is worth pointing out they got better each month. That, along with his pedigree is why he got a shot in the second half.

In the big leagues, Ford was not an absolute disaster, but he was not great either. His .190 batting average was terrible, but he made up for that with a solid .314 on base percentage. Ford's ability to draw walks and work the count is his best attribute. He also showed some real flashes, with a big home run in Sacramento giving fans a strong first impression.

However, the final product was lackluster. A .664 OPS with slightly below average defense is just not good enough. Even still, I think he is the favorite to win the backup job. That is due to him not having much competition. The two primary candidates to take his job are Drew Millas and Maxwell Romero Jr., both of whom have some serious flaws.

Millas was the backup in the first half of the season, but was not very good. While he put up strong framing metrics, he was an absolute zero with the bat and his blocking was suspect. Even for a backup catcher, a .539 OPS is not good enough.

He has also suffered from lingering finger injuries that have ended his season early each of the past two years. That lack of durability is becoming a concern for Millas. While he is your traditional defense first backup catcher, Ford has a lot more upside.

The last option is a bit of a wild card, and that is Maxwell Romero, who had a breakout year in the minors last season. Before last year, Romero just seemed like an irrelevant career minor leaguer. However, Paul Toboni and the new hitting department took his game to a new level.

In 96 games between AA and AAA, the 25 year old posted an .879 OPS and hit 23 homers. Romero is your classic three true outcome hitter. He has big time power and a good eye, but he strikes out a ton. Romero hits the ball very hard at great angles, but it is tough to live with a 35% K rate.

The Nats clearly have some skepticism because they did not protect him from the Rule 5 Draft. There is a strong chance the draft does not happen due to the lockout, but unlike some other Nats prospects, Romero was left exposed. However, I think there is a path to him being a solid backup catcher who can run into homers and play competent defense behind the plate.

In my opinion, Romero is the most compelling candidate to displace Ford. However, the fact he was not protected tells me he is the third guy in this race. Eventually, Ford will win the job in my opinion, but I think it is good to push him after a rough year. The Nats need more from Ford and this competition could be the perfect wake up call for him.
