Man City sign Enzo Fernandez for £125m to end transfer saga of the summer

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Manchester City have equalled the British record transfer on deadline day after agreeing to pay £125m to sign midfielder Enzo Fernández from Chelsea.

The deal brings to an end the biggest saga of the summer and means that Enzo Maresca, the new City head coach, has landed his number one target.

City left it until the last day of the transfer window to strike an agreement after Chelsea decided that Fernández’s exit was in the best interests of the club. But a late move to replace the Argentine with Monaco midfielder Lamie Camara collapsed to the anger of the Premier League club, leaving Xabi Alonso one player light among his midfield options.

Having refused to meet Chelsea’s self-imposed deadline to sign Fernández for £120m earlier in the summer, City had to equal the £125m Liverpool paid Newcastle United for Alexander Isak 12 months earlier.

Fernández had made it clear that he wanted to leave Chelsea some time ago and was left out of Alonso’s squad for the victories last week over Luton Town, in the Carabao Cup, and Brighton.

Having initially wanted to join Real Madrid, Fernández set his heart on linking back up with Maresca at City. The pair share a close relationship and had kept in touch after Maresca left Chelsea at the start of the year.

This is the second time Fernández has become the most expensive player in British transfer history, having previously set the record when he joined Chelsea for £107m from Benfica in the 2023 winter window.

During his time at Chelsea, 25-year-old Fernández won the Club World Cup and the Europa Conference League but he was also at the centre of controversy.

In January 2025, Fernández posted a video of himself and some of his Argentina international team-mates singing an offensive song about France players of African origin after winning the Copa America. He apologised to his Chelsea team-mates and made a donation to an anti-discrimination charity, but was still made the team’s vice-captain.

Last season was Fernández’s best goalscoring campaign, as he netted 15 goals in all competitions for Chelsea but the club finished 10th in the Premier League table. In April, he was suspended for two games by the club for suggesting he would like to live in Madrid – which was perceived as a come and get me plea to Real Madrid.

He angered England fans with his celebration after scoring against Thomas Tuchel’s team in Argentina’s World Cup semi-final victory over England, before being sent-off in the final defeat by Spain.

Chelsea fans booed Fernández in pre-season, although the club’s travelling supporters sang his name when he was introduced as a substitute in the victory over Fulham at the start of this season.

Fernández thanked his team-mates and the Chelsea staff in an emotional farewell post on Instagram. He added: “I want you to know that these have not been easy days for me. Writing this is not easy either. The truth is, I haven’t been in a good place.

“Three years ago, Cobham and Stamford Bridge became home for me and my family. I grew attached to the people, to the shirt, and to the badge of this incredible club.

“Believe me when I say that, from the moment I arrived, I became obsessed with winning and with bringing this club every trophy that its history deserves. We suffered, we cried, I got angry many times, but we also celebrated titles and unforgettable victories. Together, through the good times and the difficult ones.

“I live football the same way I live my life: with passion and with all my heart. That is why I want you to know that I understand you. I truly understand you, from the bottom of my heart.

“But life is about making decisions, and right now I find myself having to make one of those difficult decisions. I’ve said it before, but I want to say it again: I have grown incredibly fond of this club. Chelsea will always have a place in my thoughts and, above all, in my heart.

“But I also understand how football works. I know how passionate you are, because I am the same. And if I were in your position, I would probably be thinking the same way.”

The sale of Fernández means that Chelsea have set a new British transfer record of their own, having secured more than £500m in sales and loan fees during the summer transfer window.

Chelsea set about spending some of the money made from the Fernández sale by agreeing a last-minute deal for Monaco midfielder Lamine Camara.

Monaco had rejected a bid of £38.5m from Chelsea on deadline day, but an improved offer of £47m was accepted late on Tuesday night, only to be called off by Monaco on the stroke of the 11pm deadline.

Chelsea had been able to complete a medical during negotiations and Camara agreed to a six-year contract with the option of a further year to bolster Alonso’s midfield options, but he will now not join the club.

Chelsea had also seen at least one offer for Bournemouth’s Alex Scott rejected this summer, while the club showed an interest in Roma’s Manu Kone and explored whether or not a move for Arsenal’s Martin Zubimendi would be possible earlier this month.
