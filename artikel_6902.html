The Best LED Mirrors for Your Bathroom

It might sound dumb, but a smart option can improve your routine.

What is an LED mirror, and do you need one? These mirrors use an illuminated strip of light that you control to give more measured, even lighting than your bathroom overhead can. Some have a front light strip, some have backlighting, others have both. The best are equipped with antifog tech to make sure your mirror stays clear and functional during your entire post-shower routine. You’ll appreciate that when you’re shaving, and your wife won’t be able to go back to applying makeup without it. Because they use LEDs, they don’t use a significant amount of energy, and—so far—they are still low-tech enough to be largely free of AI.

So if they are so great, why doesn’t everyone have one of these fancy mirrors? The truth is it’s still hard to find a good one. The interest is there, but the market is currently flooded with $100 mirrors on Amazon that will work for a handful of months and then crap out on you. And the real good ones—the ones that merge tech and design—are expensive. These four run the gamut from affordable and convenient to luxe, but they all have one thing in common: You won’t be sending them back.

Everyone starts somewhere. And honestly the $200 Amazon option isn’t a terrible place to begin—it’s got over 3,000 reviews that mostly agree with me. Really this is everything that comes standard in a smart LED mirror: touch-controlled light settings, a defogging mode, and multiple power options (with a wall-plug option for apartments and more temporary dwellings).

The only real problem I can see is the design. The thick LED strip takes up a considerable amount of mirror real estate. Other, more thoughtful designs forgo this issue with thinner strips or backlighting. To be fair, you can turn that light off and just run with the backlight, but the chunky design of the mirror’s frame still shows.

If you want to just browse LED mirrors, Visual Comfort has a wide range of designer lighted mirrors. This one is not quite as striking, clearly, as the $2,000 options, but for $400 this is a trustworthy smart mirror that will have just what you want. What I especially like about the Mera is that you can separately adjust the brightness and color temperature.

For something really high-quality on all fronts, you’re going to have to pay top dollar. This is part of Visual Comfort’s Signature Collection, by designer Thomas O’Brien. What that really says is, from a design and craftsmanship angle, this is going to be a great mirror first—one that will last. Unfortunately, that does mean some of the tech comes secondary and not always in a way you might want.
