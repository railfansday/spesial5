Here’s what happened in crypto today

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Need to know what happened in crypto today? Here is the latest news on daily trends and events impacting Bitcoin price, blockchain, DeFi, Web3 and crypto regulation.

Today in crypto, Bitcoin briefly fell to the equivalent of $17K on Kraken’s Canadian-dollar market, but the exchange ruled out a breach and said all executed trades would stand. Hong Kong has published a bill to expand crypto licensing requirements to custody, advisory, dealing and management services under the SFC’s oversight, while Bitcoin fell below $60,000 for the first time since July amid a broader selloff in US stocks.

Crypto exchange Kraken said it would honor trades executed during a sharp selloff across its Canadian-dollar markets that briefly pushed Bitcoin to the equivalent of about $17,000 on Saturday.

In a statement, Kraken said its Canadian-dollar trading pairs experienced heavy selling pressure starting around 20:00 UTC on Friday, causing prices to fall significantly below those on other markets.

The exchange said every order was processed correctly and its security teams found no evidence of a breach or compromised accounts. “Executed trades stand,” Kraken said.

Kraken’s BTC/CAD pair fell to $24,250 Canadian during the 15-minute interval beginning at 08:00 UTC on Saturday, according to exchange trading data.

On Friday, the Bank of Canada’s reference rate showed C$1.4325 per US dollar, putting Bitcoin’s low on Kraken’s Canadian-dollar market at about $16,900. This was about 71% below Bitcoin’s price of around $57,650 on Kraken’s BTC/USD market during the same five-minute interval.

Blockstream CEO Adam Back suggested that a market maker on Kraken’s USD/CAD spot market may have exhausted its funds, causing prices to diverge from broader foreign exchange rates.

Back said arbitrage subsequently spread the pricing distortion to other Canadian-dollar trading pairs. Kraken has not confirmed Back’s proposed explanation.

Cointelegraph reached out to Kraken for comments but did not receive a response before publication.

Hong Kong has published a bill that would bring crypto custodians, advisers and other virtual asset service providers under mandatory licensing or registration, extending oversight beyond trading platforms.

On Friday, the government officially published its Virtual Asset Services Legislation (Amendment) Bill 2026, proposing to regulate four activities, including virtual asset dealing, advisory, management and custody. Firms carrying out those activities would need to obtain a license from or register with the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC).

The proposed amendments to the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance would subject providers to fit-and-proper checks and other regulatory requirements. Banks and stored-value facility licensees supervised by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority would also have to register with the SFC to offer virtual asset services.

SFC chief executive Julia Leung said the proposal would strengthen investor protections and provide clearer paths for responsible innovation. The bill is set for its first reading in the Legislative Council on Dec. 2.

In October, Hong Kong said it would introduce legislation covering those four activities before the end of 2026, following consultations on the proposed framework.

The expanded regime has raised implementation concerns. In January, the Hong Kong Securities & Futures Professionals Association warned that introducing licenses without transitional arrangements could force existing businesses to suspend operations while their applications are reviewed. The association called for a six- to 12-month grace period.

Bitcoin fell below $60,000 on Friday for the first time since July, extending its recent losses amid a broader selloff in technology stocks and other risk assets.

Bitcoin (BTC) had fallen as low as $58,811 before recovering slightly to trade around $59,503, down 5.6% over the past 24 hours as of 16:52 UTC on Friday, according to CoinGecko data. The cryptocurrency has fallen 13.8% over the past week and nearly 30% over the past month.

The decline came amid a broader selloff in US equities. The Nasdaq Composite closed down 5.28% at 22,617.73, while the S&P 500 fell 3.69% to 6,910.77 on Friday, according to Yahoo Finance.

Shares of Anthropic (ANT), which made its Nasdaq debut on Thursday, closed down 8.50% at $88.91 amid concerns over valuations and spending in the artificial intelligence sector.
