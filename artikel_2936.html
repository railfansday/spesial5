San Antonio vs. Utah, Final Score: Harper leads Spurs’ final push past Jazz, 110-102

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Ascending star Dylan Harper scored 16 of his team's last 24 points to help San Antonio (12-2) claim its 12th straight victory and third straight this season over the Jazz. With the game still very much in doubt, Victor Wembanyama hit a fadeaway jumper and blocked a Keyonte George floater, and Keldon Johnson saved the ensuing carom from falling out of bounds and chased down an offensive rebound at the other end and scored a putback to put the Spurs up four in the penultimate seconds.

In a game with an exasperating amount of runs that were often one after another, Utah staged a 19-7 run spanning hte third and fourth quarters to erase the Spurs' biggest lead of the night (9) and get into the driver's seat until the visitors made that final 24-13 push.

Harper (31 points, 9 assists, and 4 assists) single-handedly willed San Antonio to victory with audacious and self-assured shotmaking throughout those critical fourth quarter minutes. Wembanyama (23 points, 9 rebounds, 5 assists, and 4 blocks) carried the offenisve production throughout the first three quarters. Stephon Castle (20 points, 9 assists, and 4 steals) and reserve Jordan McLaughlin (10 points) assured Fox wouldn't be missed TOO much. Johnson (11 points and 10 rebounds) was a menacing presence for the Spurs in the second half and especially in the waning moments.

Mo Bamba (26 points [6-for-6 from three], 5 rebounds, and 4 assists) performed admirably again in the absence of the star-studded Jazz starting bigs. Backup Brice Sensabaugh (14 points and 7 rebounds) and rookie Darryn Peterson (17 points, 5 rebounds, and 4 assists) led a group of contributors that supplied plentiful support for Bamba and made up for an off-night for Keyonte George (13 points, 8 assists, and 4 rebounds).

The teams mirrored each other over the opening minutes – with Wembanyama and Bamba taking center stage before Rutgers' Harper and Bailey exchanged buckets. While the bigs' turned away several shots at or near the rim, the Jazz swingmen made a flurry of threes to get themselves out front. Despite those outside bursts, the Spurs stayed neck-and-neck with their counterparts. While the hosts shot an astounding 7-for-10 fron behind the line, they still only went to the second up two, after McLaughlin kickstarted a closing 8-0 run to end the first quarter.

Bamba's third and fourth threes (without a miss) created some space between his team and San Antonio. While the teams continued to shoot well from the field, Utah's proficiency from distance made up the bulk of their advantage. After opposing 9-0 runs, the Jazz took advantage of San Antonio's errant shooting to leap back into the lead. Utah's perimeter men seemed to make quite a few shots late in the clock. Wembanyama rescued a handful of possessions with his shooting and playmaking and the Spurs went to halftime slightly up 58-55.

Coming out of the break, Castle figured in one each of the Spurs' first four scores – two field goals, two freebies, and one assist. Rookie Peterson dazzled with his ability to score from different spots on the floor. Bamba and Bradley resembled Utah legend Thurl Bailey by knocking down shot after shot. A 10-0 run by San Antonio got them to 79-70. Bamba's fifth straight three without a miss tied things right back at 79. San Antonio nearly went the rest of the quarter without scoring again and the teams were deadlocked at 81 going to the final 12 minutes.

Bamba and Wembanyama (say their names ten times in a row) matched opening pairs of buckets from the tip. The former Rutgers players – Bailey and Harper – traded field goals after that. However, a trio of Jazz triples put them up six. Over several possessions, Wembanyama had some nice two-man action with Vassell and Jordan McLaughlin to mixed results. After dribbling the ball for nearly 20 seconds, Castle willed home a floater. Sensabaugh, like he seems to always do against San Antonio, knocked down a pair of jumpers in the paint to keep the Jazz ahead. Tony Bradley joined a sextet of Jazz players to knock down threes. McLaughlin sandwiched field goals around a Wembanyama three, and despite the Jazz shooting 57% from the field (64% from three), the Spurs ended the quarter down just 31-33.

Bamba's third straight triple put Utah back up five to start the second. Wembanyana's catch-and-shoot three knotted things briefly at 38. After weathering nine straight from the Jazz, San Antonio answered right back with nine of their own. The Spurs failed to convert several chances to tie the game or re-take the lead. Wembanyama took his time on the right block and tossed up a bankshot-and-1 over Bamba that would have made Tim Duncan proud. Johnson's transition three put San Antonio up one. While the Jazz executed well over the bulk of the first half minutes, an Area 51 transition lob slam made it 58-55 Spurs at the break.

At the start of the third, Castle followed two quick baskets with a couple of free throws and assisted Harper's and-1 to briefly get the San Antonio lead to ten. Undeterred, Peterson capped a 10-0 Utah avalanche with a corner three and a floater to tie things at 67. The Spurs, with Johnson's prominent contributions, generated their own 10-0 run while Wembanyama sat to go up 79-70. San Antonio stayed stuck at 79 and was nearly shut out the rest of the quarter aside from some Castle free throws, and the Jazz pulled even at 81 heading to the fourth.
