Visa Paid About $335 per Share to Buy Back $20.5 Billion of Its Stock Last Year. Would You Pay $400?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Visa has been buying back its own stock, but you may want to check the stock's valuation before following the company's lead.

By Reuben Gregg Brewer – Nov 25, 2026 at 11:08AM EST

It is often viewed as a good sign when companies buy back their own stock. So it is nice to see that Visa (V +0.25%) bought back $4 billion in shares in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 and $24.4 billion for the full fiscal year. However, note that the $4 billion is relatively small, suggesting the company wasn't as excited about its own stock in the final quarter of the year as it was earlier in the year. You'll probably be even less excited about it now that the shares are trading around $400. Here's why.

For the full fiscal year, Visa paid an average of roughly $335 per share for the stock it repurchased. In the fourth quarter, that price was $369. That helps explain why it bought less of the stock. In fact, the largest repurchase occurred in the second quarter of fiscal 2026, when the financial giant spent $7.9 billion at an average purchase price of roughly $320 per share. Very clearly, Visa bought less when the stock price was higher.

With the stock currently trading around $400 per share, the company's spending is likely to be even lower. And there's a good reason for that when you look at traditional valuation metrics. At the current share price, the stock's price-to-sales ratio is 18.4x, just a touch lower than its five-year average of 19.4x. The price-to-earnings ratio is roughly 32x, compared to a five-year average of 31x. And the price-to-book ratio is 21x versus a five-year average of nearly 15x. Given those valuation metrics, it is hard to suggest that the stock is cheap.

Visa may keep buying stock, since it is generating strong cash flows. Stock buybacks help support earnings by reducing the number of shares outstanding. And they allow the company to return value to shareholders without committing to maintaining a larger dividend. As an investor buying the stock for your own portfolio, however, those aren't the types of issues you are considering. You need to ensure that you pay an attractive price for a business you want to hold for the long term. As noted, after a rally, Visa's price no longer looks attractively cheap.

Visa is a well-run company with long-term business appeal. It probably wouldn't be a huge mistake to buy it today, assuming you intend to hold it for the long term. However, the current price, which is well above the level at which Visa was making its most aggressive repurchases, doesn't look like a value opportunity. At best, you are paying full fare to own Visa right now, if not a premium.
