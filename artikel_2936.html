Winners and Losers from the Chiefs’ loss in Buffalo

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Your Kansas City Chiefs gave their fans some Thanksgiving indigestion on Thursday as they found a way to snatch defeat from the jaws of victory.

It was quite the antithesis of the last couple of weeks for the Chiefs, as their mistakes and sloppiness showed up in a cold, rainy mess of a holiday Prime Time road game.

Here are a few who stood out on Sunday as the Chiefs cooled off in a key matchup.

Running back Emmett Johnson: He didn't have any issues securing the football on his 21 touches, racking up 96 total yards and the Chiefs' only touchdown. Emmett Johnson just needed more opportunities.

Quarterback Patrick Mahomes and Tight end Travis Kelce: When the offense started slow, the GOAT-to-GOAT connection came through. Down 7-0, Mahomes targeted Kelce four times on a drive that ended on the Buffalo 8 yard line. These guys have seen bad weather on the road against great teams before, and it didn't seem to bother them as much as it did others.

Cornerback Nohl Williams and Safety Alohi Gilman: Given poor field position all evening, the Chiefs defense desperately needed someone to make a play. Williams and Gilman came up with red zone interceptions that took points off the board for Buffalo and kept Kansas City in the game. Both were potentially huge momentum shifts— one in each half— both squandered by the offense.

Linebacker Nick Bolton: The defense needed a steady hand, and the Mizzou alum delivered—fourteen total tackles (2 for a loss), and a sack on the afternoon for Bolton

Kicker Harrison Butker: This game was decided by field goals. One team hit two long kicks in the rain. Harrison Butker missed his last one; Tyler Bass hit his— Game over. Nobody in the Chiefs orbit was surprised, but it finally officially cost them a win.

Punt returner Josh Downs: The special teams theme continues in the losers column, and it has to do with the decision-making AND the execution of the Chiefs returner.

Punter Matt Araiza: It was a miserable night for Araiza. He was put in difficult situations all evening, and struggled to catch or kick cleanly. When the game was on the line, and time was running out, his kick out of the endzone was tipped and didn't get past field goal range. And that was the ballgame.

Special teams coordinator Dave Toub: Why is this entire unit such a mess? Can it be fixed by the current staff?

NFL officiating and the definition of a catch— Rashee caught it.

Note: Applying the labels "winners" or "losers" is not meant to be a judgment on the talent or character of any of these individuals. It's just a simple way to grade their job performance in a single game. No disrespect is intended.
