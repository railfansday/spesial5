Stolen API Keys at a Gift Card Supplier: $70,000 in Cards Cashed Out, Up to $2.5M in Sales Lost. Is Your Store Next?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Posted on Nov 23 • Originally published at security.hackerme.ai

Originally published at security.hackerme.ai.

If your online store, app or loyalty program sells or gives out digital gift cards, it probably talks to a supplier through an API key. In October, attackers broke into one of those suppliers. They got hold of API keys belonging to some of its business customers. Meanwhile, someone cashed out about $70,000 in gift cards. Here is what happened, who should care, and what to check this week.

GlobeTopper is a business-to-business supplier of digital gift cards and top-ups. Retailers, fintech apps and rewards platforms connect to it through one API. On its own website, GlobeTopper says it offers more than 4,000 brands across 65 countries. BleepingComputer, citing that website, lists IKEA, Adidas, Spotify, Target and Roblox among them. To be clear, nobody hacked those brands. Hackers hit their supplier.

GlobeTopper's majority owner, iQSTEL, described the incident in a Form 8-K filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission on 20 November 2026:

The filing says the attacker "obtained API credentials and integration keys of certain GlobeTopper business customers." GlobeTopper revoked and rotated all of them. It also issued new credentials with extra access limits. So far, it says, it has not found any unauthorized use of those keys.

Separately, someone redeemed digital gift cards with a face value of about $70,000 without authorization. The company says it cannot block or recover those cards, so it expects to absorb the loss. It also warns that more losses could follow if anyone uses exposed redemption links later.

Management's preliminary high-end estimate of revenue not generated during the outage is about $2.5 million. However, some of those sales may simply come back later. Also, the company is still checking whether anyone reached other customer, personal, payment or provider data.

This fits a pattern we keep seeing: a big brand name on the box, and a smaller supplier that holds the keys. We saw it with Docker Hub images that leaked live keys, and with the Apify breach and API token rotation.

Stolen API keys and gift cards are both easy to turn into cash. For example, criminals sell working keys and access to accounts on underground forums, often bundled with instructions on what the key can do. Gift cards, in particular, are a favorite way to move money out. Thieves redeem codes fast, resell them at a discount on gray-market sites, or spend them on goods they then resell. Once someone spends a card, the money is usually gone. That is why GlobeTopper says it cannot claw back its $70,000.
