Hotelbeds is accepting a lower take rate to gain market share, while investing in new sources of revenue.

In August, Skift examined why Hotelbeds was earning less from every dollar of travel it sold. Three months later, its annual results show that the pressure has intensified — and its financial filings reveal how much the company is investing in other ways to make money.

Since 2024, parent company HBX Group's investments and estimated acquisition consideration across five businesses total €102 million ($112 million), according to a Skift analysis of its accounts. About €65 million ($71 million) of that depends on how the acquired businesses perform, so the final bill could be higher or lower. Nearly a decade after using acquisitions to consolidate global hotel wholesaling, HBX is buying its way into holiday packaging, experiences, luxury travel and hotel software.

HBX Group, the parent of bedbank Hotelbeds, is selling more travel while keeping less of it. Annual results released November 25 show transaction value up 14% at constant currency to €9.16 billion ($10 billion), while revenue fell 2% to €697 million ($762 million) and adjusted EBITDA fell 4% to €403 million ($441 million). The take rate — revenue as a share of transaction value — dropped from 8.8% in fiscal 2025 to 7.6% in fiscal 2026, and to roughly 7.1% in the September quarter, against the roughly 9% assumed at its February 2025 IPO. Shares fell 21% on results day and sit about 46% below the IPO price. Growth is coming increasingly from OTAs, HBX is sourcing more inventory through third-party intermediaries rather than directly from hotels, and its ten largest customers now account for 35% of transaction value, up from 29%. In response, HBX has committed an estimated €102 million ($112 million) since 2024 across five businesses — PerfectStay in holiday packaging, Bridgify in experiences and AI, The Luxurist in luxury distribution, and Civitfun and ThinkIN in hotel software and direct sales — with about €65 million ($71 million) of that contingent on performance. Early returns are mixed: HBX recorded a €23 million impairment on its original PerfectStay stake, and both PerfectStay and Bridgify would have reduced group profit had they been consolidated for the full year. Carlos Muñoz takes over as CEO on December 1, with full fiscal 2027 guidance deferred until February.
