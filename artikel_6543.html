Chipotle’s Thanksgiving Eve BOGO Returns For One Night Only—Here's How To Get It

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Your Wednesday night plans could use a little extra guac.

Thanksgiving is practically an Olympic event for eating, but there’s nothing wrong with a little warm-up the night before. Chipotle is bringing back its Thanksgiving Eve BOGO deal, giving you a perfectly good excuse to grab a burrito with an old friend before the turkey, stuffing, and inevitable food coma take over.

The deal runs Wednesday, November 25, from 5 to 9 p.m. local time at participating U.S. restaurants, with the usual buy one entrée, get one free format we all know and love.

But, as expected, there’s some fine print worth reading before you get in line. You’ll need to be a Chipotle Rewards member, order at the restaurant, and scan your Rewards card at checkout. The free entrée must cost the same as or less than the one you're buying, and you can claim up to five free entrées per check. Kids’ meals don't qualify, and ordering ahead through the app, online, or for delivery won't work.

The timing has changed a bit from last year, too, when it ran from 4 p.m. all the way until closing. This time, you've got a four-hour window, so make sure that one friend you have who’s perpetually late gets the memo.

“The response last year showed us how much our guests embraced the occasion,” Stephanie Perdue, Chipotle's senior vice president of brand marketing, said in a statement. According to the chain, the promotion ended up being its biggest sales-driving campaign of 2025, so a return trip was probably inevitable.

And if you’re stuffed and don’t feel like leaving the couch the next day, there’s a little something for the post-Thanksgiving crowd as well. From Friday, November 27, through Monday, November 30, Chipotle is waiving its delivery fee on eligible orders placed through its app or website with code CYBER26. You’ll need to spend at least $10 in the U.S. or $12 in Canada, and delivery menu prices may be higher, with other fees still applying.

If nothing else, it’s a convenient excuse to take a break from the annual challenge of finding increasingly creative ways to eat leftover turkey.
