The Best Coffee-Table Books to Gift Your Beautiful, Discerning Friends

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Every product on this page was chosen by a Harper's BAZAAR editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

These books will please everyone on your gift list this year

Every year holidays come around, and a slight panic sets in at the thought of finding gifts that the people you love will get excited about—and that will make them feel seen. The real trick is to keep a list throughout the year of everything you see that reminds you of this person or that person. But because sometimes you don’t write things down because you’re “sure you’ll remember,” and because even if you do, we need all the help we can get in discovering cool things, we’ve put together a list of our favorite coffee-table books to give (and to receive, if we’re being honest) this year.

From fashion monographs to punk rock album covers to a guide to medieval wellness, we promise that we’ve found something that will thrill even your most cynical friend.

Richard Avedon was undoubtedly one of the greatest photographers of the 20th century and this collection gathers his work for Harper’s Bazaar from 1944 to 1965. As the world experienced generation-defining shifts, Avedon was zeroed in on his style, creating indelible images that would go on to change fashion. Bonus? It pairs perfectly with Ron Howard’s documentary on the photographer, also titled simply Avedon.

This massive pink tome, assembled by Coppola herself from her own collection of photographs, scripts, collages, and all manner of feminine detritus, offers a revealing and inspirational look at the director’s process and world-building.

With wit and humor, medievalist Larisa Grollemond sheds light on the way humans lived—and thrived(?)—during the middle ages. Is it true they never bathed? Did they have practice birth control? Were witches in fact, doctors? This books holds the answers.

Raymond Pettibon may now be a certified blue-chip artist, but he began his career in the 1970s drawing flyers and record covers for punk bands in Southen California like Minutemen and Black Flag. (He also designed their iconic four-bar logo, which has now been meme-fied.) This book collects his musical body of work, which also includes the Foo Fighters and Lana del Rey—that’s his work on the cover of Norman Fucking Rockwell.

The elusive Martin Margiela may very well be the designer that has most influenced contemporary fashion. Nowhere is that more evident than in this book that looks at the time when the Belgian designer was at the helm of Hermés. The enduring appeal of the collections he created from 1997 to 2002 would go on to set the tone for the whole quiet luxury moment we’ve been living under—except better.

Jeff Goodwin was the makeup artist for David Lynch’s iconic film Blue Velvet. His personal trove of Polaroid photos capture impromptu behind-the-scenes moments and reveal how he refined the makeup looks; they’re accompanied by his own memories of the filming experience.

How many people’s fashion obsessions began as they endlessly dressed and undressed their dolls—be they Barbie, Bratz, or even American Girl dolls? This tome looks at the symbiotic relationship between dolls and real-life grown-up fashion, zeroing in on the works of designers like Comme des Garçons’ Rei Kawakubo and Viktor & Rolf’s Viktor Horsting and Rolf Snoeren, among others.

This is a book designed to inspire. For “Kitchen Piece,” Ono suggests one “hangs a canvas on a wall” and “throws all the leftovers you have” on it, with the caveat that “you may prepare special food for the piece.” After spending time with Ono’s instructions, one realizes there is art to be found literally everywhere.

This is the only book about Carolyn Bessette Kennedy that one may ever need. Sunita Kumar Nair talks to CBK’s friends and, through a carefully curated edit of images, breaks down her style. She also reveals the true essence of the person behind all those infinitely rebloggable images.

Past Continuous is the name of an exhibit at the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York that brings together the world of Lee Krasner and her husband Jackson Pollock and looks at their career arcs in tandem. It begins with a selection of their paintings before the pair met, and traces their influences, taking a closer look at how their presences wove in and out of each other’s work.

This tome celebrates America’s 250th anniversary through over 200 pages filled with iconic images that have defined the country throughout its history, from images of cowboys wearing their worn-in Levi’s and immigrants landing at Ellis Island to Martin Luther King and Elvis Presley.

Diane Arbus’s unflinching photographs captured humanity in all its beautiful and exacting rawness. Documents tells the history of her short-lived career through essays, criticism, and other ephemera from 1967 through the present day; it’s a comprehensive guide to understanding her work.

Glen Luchford photographed a few of Prada’s most iconic ad campaigns of the 1990s. This book brings them all together along with alternate shots and test shots, and is an essential for both Prada aficionados and lovers of the decade.

Created in 1909 by Arthur E. Waite and Pamela Colman Smith in London, the appropriately titled Waite Smith tarot deck is the most popular deck in the world. This book looks at its history, uncovering the details and meaning of each individual card—and it comes with its own set of cards.

This year, the Fashion Museum Antwerp is celebrating its 40th anniversary with an exhibition dedicated to the “Antwerp Six,” the name the fashion media gave to a group of designers—Dries van Noten, Ann Demeulemeeter, Marina Yee, Dirk Bikkembergs, Walter Van Beirendonck, and Dirk Van Seene—that arrived in London in the mid ’80s fresh after their graduation from the Royal Academy of Fine Art. For those who can’t make it to Belgium to see the show, the book may prove to be the next best thing, chock full of photographs, illustrations, newspaper clippings, and essays. They may not have all become household names, but their influence can still be felt on the runways today.

Brianna Capozzi’s unique view of womanhood has made her one of the most sought-after photographers in fashion. Her latest monograph features a mix of magazine shots of famous singers and actors with images of impromptu shoots staged with her best friends. The result is a visual shot of adrenaline and a reminder that girls—well, women—just wanna have fun!
