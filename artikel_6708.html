23 Thanksgiving Bread Recipes: Rolls, Biscuits, and More

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Pies get most of the Thanksgiving baking glory. But these Thanksgiving bread recipes make a strong case for saving some oven space for buttery dinner rolls, flaky biscuits, and midnight-sandwich-ready loaves. Think golden sweet potato buns soft enough to nap on, buttermilk cornbread to eat with butter or bake into dressing, and tender sweet loaves to nibble on Thanksgiving morning. Several recipes have make-ahead options too.

There’s no need to overhaul a classic. Adapted from The Fannie Farmer Baking Book, these Parker House rolls have buttery folds, golden tops, and a finish of flaky salt. Pair them with our classic Thanksgiving menu.

These pillowy rolls get their warm color and gentle sweetness from mashed sweet potato. Bake and freeze them up to a month ahead, then reheat and brush with melted butter before serving.

Butter tucked inside each roll melts as it bakes, crisping the bottom. “If you can imagine a warm dinner roll meeting a flaky croissant, you’re getting closer,” says Hana Asbrink.

Pillowy rolls swirled with sweet-salty pork floss, mayo, scallions, and furikake. Bake and freeze them up to three months ahead, then warm them through while everyone finds a seat.

A tangzhong—a cooked flour-and-milk paste—helps these sour cream and chive rolls stay exceptionally soft. Tear one off to mop up gravy, then save a few for tomorrow’s turkey sandwiches.

Garlic bread, meet buttery dinner roll. Fresh and dried dill work their way into these pillowy buns, which get a generous brushing of garlic butter after baking. Save one for a gravy swipe.

So many layers, you’ll feel like a geologist unearthing them. Claire Saffitz’s biscuits get their height from a cut-and-stack technique. Another reason to love them: You can freeze the unbaked biscuits up to a month ahead.

All the herby goodness of Thanksgiving stuffing, packed into a biscuit: sage, rosemary, fennel seed, and scallions. Save a few for breakfast the next morning, preferably under a generous ladle of leftover gravy.

No rolling pin, no biscuit cutter, no problem. Canned creamed corn keeps these drop biscuits tender, while Calabrian chile honey butter brings a little heat.

For the guest who always takes the biggest piece of garlic bread: flaky buttermilk biscuits loaded with Parmesan and parsley. Freeze them unbaked, then bake straight from frozen when the oven frees up.

Biscuits or cornbread? This cheesy mash-up means you don’t have to choose. Sharp cheddar and fresh sage work their way into flaky, buttery layers that feel right at home on the Thanksgiving table.

Looking to start a fight? Declare a cornbread recipe “best.” This version strikes a balance: a tender crumb, buttermilk tang, and a touch of sweetness. Eat it with butter—or turn it into cornbread dressing.

Cheddar, caramelized corn, and hot honey butter take this cornbread well beyond the usual. Nervous about the flip? “Don’t be afraid of flipping the bread—I promise you can do it,” says former BA food editor Rachel Gurjar.

Pumpkin purée gives this fall-ish rendition of our no-knead focaccia recipe its golden color and gentle sweetness. Finish with rosemary and flaky salt, then tear off a piece for dipping in olive oil. You can bake it a day ahead.

Leave the oven to the turkey. These flaky, skillet-cooked parathas have brown butter and sage tucked between their layers. Parcook them a day ahead, then fry until golden just before dinner.

A garlic knot with centerpiece ambitions. This braided loaf wraps buttery dough around garlic, Parmesan, and parsley, then gets another garlicky brush after baking. Start the dough a day ahead.

Leave the stand mixer where it is. This focaccia asks for a little patience, not much elbow grease, to produce its airy crumb and golden crust. An overnight rise gets you started; garlic butter finishes the job.

Focaccia’s leaf-shaped French cousin deserves a turn in the Thanksgiving bread basket. Green olives, rosemary, and lemon zest bring plenty of flavor. And yes, the dough comes together in one bowl.

In this crusty yeast bread recipe, apple juice replaces water. The apple flavor is subtle, but the appeal of a warm slice with butter is not.

Oats and chia seeds give this gluten-free loaf a dense, chewy crumb. No kneading, shaping, or scoring; just be sure to use the gluten-free oats and oat flour specified in the recipe.

Give Thanksgiving morning the sticky-bun treatment. This pull-apart bread packs tender apples between cinnamon-sugar-coated bites, with tangy cider caramel coating the whole thing. Put on a pot of coffee and let everyone help themselves.

Chocolate or vanilla? Rick Martinez’s conchas give you both, with a two-tone sugar topping over buttery Mexican sweet buns. Another reason to love them: You can bake them up to three days ahead.

Start the holiday with a thick slice of ginger-spiked pumpkin bread and a generous schmear of salted maple butter. You can bake the loaf up to four days ahead, leaving Thanksgiving morning for coffee and slicing—and, uh, all that other prep.
