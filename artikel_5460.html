Thanksgiving Nor’easter Postpones Macy’s Parade and Targets Over 250 Million: The Latest on Power Outages, Homes at Risk, and More

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

A nor'easter and even a "bomb cyclone" are predicted to slam the Northeast on Thanksgiving this year, putting millions in a path of destruction not seen on the holiday for decades—and causing a time honored tradition to be postponed for the first time in its history.

Heavy rain, high winds, and coastal impacts to the East Coast are said to begin on Wednesday and carry through Thanksgiving, according to NOAA's Weather Prediction Center.

The biggest impact will be seen starting in the Southeast, before moving its way to the Northeast, Great Lakes, and Ohio Valley.

While flooding is a major consideration, meteorologists are more concerned about the powerful winds accompanying the storm, which could result in tree damage, power outages, and other disasters for homeowners to contend with on the big day.

The high winds ultimately forced the hands of New York City officials, the NYPD and Macy's, who late on Wednesday announced that the parade would be pushed off until the following Saturday Nov. 28, due to the weather.

The storm will ramp up in the Southeast on Wednesday, according to The Weather Channel, with rain, wind, and coastal flooding causing hazardous conditions for travelers heading home for the holiday.

Because temperatures have dropped as well, snow is also likely in parts of Missouri and Illinois beginning later in the day, continuing into the night, into Thanksgiving.

By Thanksgiving Thursday, the storm will have made its way north into the mid-Atlantic and Ohio Valley, before steadily moving into New England by afternoon or evening.

While wind is top of mind given the milestone parade, significant coastal flooding along the East Coast is poised to cause beach erosion, flooded coastal streets, and potential structural damage.

Moderate to major flooding is possible from Georgia to Long Island, alongside at least minor flooding in New England, with any heavy rain during high tide worsening the inundation, says Weather.com.

Peak water levels will hit Georgia and South Carolina on Wednesday morning, before shifting north to North Carolina, Virginia, Delaware, the Jersey Shore, and Long Island on Thanksgiving morning, and reaching coastal New England by Thursday and Friday midday.

Conditions could be especially severe in the mid-Atlantic, with widespread road flooding expected across Jersey Shore communities such as Atlantic City and Ocean City, water levels in Cape May and coastal Delaware potentially hitting their highest mark since January 2016, and Ocean City, MD, approaching its 1998 all-time record.

As the storm threatened to ground the balloons and drench the anticipated thousands of spectators, local outlets reported early Wednesday that Mayor Mamdani, the police commissioner and Macy's executives will announce the postponement of the Thanksgiving parade late on Wednesday.

The parade was scheduled to begin at 8:30 a.m. EST on Thursday, on Manhattan’s Upper West Side, and the National Weather Service had already issued a high wind warning before the call was made to move the parade.

The last reports predict that wind gusts will already exceed limits before the parade is even set to start.

"There is a significant risk that gusty winds ahead of the nor’easter cause the parade balloons to at least be lower than usual, and it’s possible they are not able to fly at all," AccuWeather Vice President of Forecast Operations Dan DePodwin said ahead of the announcement .

"This scenario is most likely should the storm move a bit more quickly up the coast. If it’s slower, the wind may intensify after the parade is over."

Sustained winds must remain below 23 mph and gusts have to be lower than 34 mph for the balloons to fly.

The last balloon grounding was in 1971, but the parade has never been canceled due to weather in its 100-year history.

Power outages and tree damage are possible, according to the Weather Prediction Center, as wind gusts of 50-plus mph are forecasted along the coast with 40-plus mph gusts inland into the Interstate-95 corridor through at least Friday.

As of Monday evening, the National Weather Service warned that “tree damage and power outages will be possible” from the storm on Thanksgiving Day.

The forecasts put the strongest gusts along the mid-Atlantic coast and inland corridors on Thursday. Along the New Jersey shore and Delaware beaches, gusts will reach 50 to 60 mph—with 40 to 50 mph inland—prompting NWS Mount Holly to issue a high wind watch for the coastal strip from late Wednesday night through late Thursday night.

Farther south, Southeast Virginia and Northeast North Carolina face 2 to 3 inches of rain alongside east to northeast gusts of 40 to 50 mph inland and potentially 50 to 60 mph on the coast, where NWS Wakefield noted a high wind watch may be needed. Meanwhile, Washington, DC, Baltimore, and Northern Virginia can expect 1 to 3 inches of rain and Thanksgiving Day gusts of 35 to 50 mph, which NWS Sterling warned could be “strong to perhaps damaging.”

The threat shifts northward later in the day, with Southern New England forecast to see gusts of 40 to 55 mph and up to 60 mph on Cape Cod and the islands. According to NWS Boston, the strongest winds in that region will arrive on Thursday night. In Rhode Island, forecasters specifically note the potential for isolated power outages late Thursday night and overnight, with coastal areas facing the highest risk.

Meanwhile, California has its own winter weather whirlwind to contend with, including massive waves battering the coastline.

According to the NWS, record-breaking king tides, combined with elevated surf and a large Pacific swell, are expected to bring significant coastal flooding to Southern California during this Thanksgiving week, with the greatest impacts anticipated Wednesday and Thursday, potentially flooding coastal properties and causing significant beach erosion.

“A series of winter storms is impacting California, bringing heavy rain, mountain snow, and potentially hazardous travel conditions,” the California Governor’s Office of Emergency Services said on Friday.

The mayor of San Francisco warned of “high tides and rain that this city has never seen before,” in the days leading up to the holiday, asking the public to avoid all but essential travel. The National Weather Service in the Bay Area added low areas would be prone to flooding, including San Francisco International Airport, which would be an added hindrance to travel to say the least.

Meanwhile, Southern California cities such as Newport Beach, Seal Beach, and Long Beach have put up miles of sand berms, some 15 feet tall or higher, to try to keep the ocean back, according to The Washington Post.

This weather seems to be a perfect storm of the warm waters of record-breaking El Niño colliding with an extra-high “king tide,” which the area already had to contend with a few weeks ago.

"In California, tides will be about half a foot higher than the highest in October," physical oceanographer Greg Dusek told Weather.com.

"This means that even if El Niño doesn't increase water levels further, there will be significant flooding along the California coast," Dusek said.

Forecasts predict the worst of the storms could strike on Tuesday and Wednesday night into Thanksgiving Day.
