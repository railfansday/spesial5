Nvidia hired Wachtell Lipton to lead probe of Jensen Huang's affair with suspected Chinese spy

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Joon Kim, a former acting U.S. attorney, will lead the inquiry as Nvidia stock is down about 6% on Tuesday

Nvidia $NVDA retained law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz on Tuesday to lead the independent investigation its board announced Sunday following CEO Jensen Huang's affair with a woman alleged to be a Chinese spy.

Independent directors on Nvidia's board also brought on Joon H. Kim, who formerly served as acting U.S. attorney for the Southern District of New York, along with his firm, the company said in a statement.

Kim will lead the investigation, according to The Wall Street Journal. A panel of independent directors will supervise the work together with Wachtell, and Kim arrived at the firm only last month.

The board said Sunday that nothing gave it cause to think Nvidia's secrets or U.S. national security had been put at risk, according to CNBC. In the same announcement, the board said it would look into claims that Jensen Huang had been singled out by a foreign spy operation.

Jensen Huang admitted the affair on Sunday in a note he emailed to employees. "My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did," he wrote, according to CNBC.

Nvidia stock closed down 4.2% on Monday and was down about 6% on Tuesday.

President Donald Trump said Monday that Jensen Huang told him he had no idea the woman was a spy and that the FBI is reviewing the issue. "I really believe him," Trump said.

The woman is Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive whom American intelligence officials consider an operative working for China. She is not related to Jensen Huang. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun called the allegation unsubstantiated.

The Justice Department is conducting a criminal probe of Megaspeed International, a Singapore-based firm suspected of illegally funneling Nvidia AI chips to China. Rosen Law Firm has filed a shareholder class action against Nvidia and Jensen Huang in federal court in San Jose, California. The suit contends the defendants issued inaccurate or deceptive statements about the affair and the relationship between Nvidia and Megaspeed.
