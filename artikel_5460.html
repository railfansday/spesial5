Action1 Extends Microsoft Intune Capabilities Without Expanding the Attack Surface

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

At Microsoft Ignite 2026, Action1 will show how Endpoint Takeover Prevention lets enterprises extend Microsoft Intune with cross-platform patching and vulnerability management without adding takeover risk

SAN FRANCISCO, Nov. 17, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Action1, a leading provider of autonomous endpoint management (AEM) solutions, today announced it will demonstrate its platform at Microsoft Ignite 2026, including Endpoint Takeover Prevention (ETP), an industry-first security architecture designed to prevent threat actors from using a compromised management platform to take control of customer endpoints.

Action1 will demonstrate its industry-first Endpoint Takeover Prevention (ETP) architecture at Microsoft Ignite 2026.

Every endpoint management tool added to the environment can become another way in for threat actors. AI is making that exposure harder to ignore, shrinking the time between vulnerability discovery and exploitation. Action1's ETP architecture is designed to address that challenge: high-risk endpoint actions, including scripts, software deployments and remote sessions, require cryptographic authorization using private keys controlled exclusively by the customer. Action1 endpoint agents independently verify that authorization before carrying out a high-risk action, so compromise of the platform alone is not enough to act on customer endpoints.

As a result, organizations using Microsoft Intune can extend their environments with Action1 without expanding the attack surface that typically comes with adding another endpoint management tool. Action1 brings autonomous patching for third-party applications, real-time vulnerability assessment with risk-based prioritization, unified coverage across Windows, macOS and Linux, and continuous patch compliance visibility. Enterprises can build on their existing Microsoft investments while closing patching and vulnerability management gaps, achieving a cohesive approach to endpoint management.

"Strengthening endpoint management should not open a new path to takeover," said Mike Walters, President and Co-founder of Action1. "Enterprises with Intune environments need unified patching and visibility into vulnerabilities, but adding another privileged platform can increase risk on every endpoint. It is a tension we see across the cybersecurity industry, and ETP was designed to resolve it. With ETP, control stays where it belongs: with the customer."

A call for stronger endpoint management security

Recent vulnerabilities in N-able's N-central platform underscore the risks associated with centralized management tools and the broad access they can hold. Action1 believes endpoint management vendors should reduce risk for customers, not become a single point of failure. The company has called on endpoint management providers, including competitors, to adopt ETP principles and help establish a new security standard for the industry.
