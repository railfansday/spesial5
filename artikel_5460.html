Alex Trebek’s Former 578-Acre California Ranch and Winery Heads to Auction for $6.45 Million

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A sprawling California ranch where Alex Trebek helped run a winery is heading to auction, giving buyers a chance to purchase nearly 578 acres of the Central Coast once owned by the longtime “Jeopardy!” host.

Known as Creston Manor, the property in San Luis Obispo County near Paso Robles is listed for $6.45 million.

In a press release shared with Realtor.com®, Concierge Auctions revealed bidding is scheduled to open on Dec. 4 and conclude live on Dec. 16 at The Dominick in New York City, where the ranch will be offered as part of the company’s year-end sale.

Lindsey Harn of Christie’s International Real Estate and Mike Goldfarb of Coldwell Banker Residential Brokerage Company are working with the auction house on the sale.

Creston Manor is the second former Trebek property in the area to come up for auction through the company this year.

Earlier in 2026, Concierge Auctions sold Windfall Farms, a nearly 724-acre property in nearby Paso Robles, for $16.9 million. The auction house said it marketed that property for 39 days before the sale.

Creston Manor was established in 1900, decades before Trebek became a partner in Creston Manor Vineyards and Winery in 1987.

According to the auction house, the game show host used the property primarily for ranching and wine production.

One vintage detail from the auction announcement ties the ranch to Washington, DC. Concierge Auctions says wine from the estate was served at President George H.W. Bush’s inauguration.

The vineyard and winery are still operational, according to the sale announcement.

The estate also retains parts of its ranching history, including a preserved blacksmith shop and the original Highway 58 bridge.

Those structures sit on the same property as the barns, stables, and livestock facilities that support its current agricultural use.

The ranch covers approximately 577.65 acres, with rolling pastureland and mountain views. Livestock pens are set up to accommodate cattle, horses, sheep, and pigs, and the stables provide dedicated space for horses.

The existing agricultural facilities are a central part of the offering, particularly for buyers who want to continue ranching or producing wine on the property.

Beyond the agricultural buildings, the property has five residences and a separate private suite. Together, the living quarters span approximately 7,550 square feet and contain 14 bedrooms and 14 full bathrooms.

According to Concierge Auctions, the estate can accommodate more than 30 guests.

Harn pointed to the combination of housing, winemaking facilities, and equestrian infrastructure as a distinguishing feature of the property.

The ranch is in Creston, a community in northern San Luis Obispo County with agricultural ties of its own. Goldfarb highlighted the estate’s seclusion and its proximity to Paso Robles, where the surrounding wine region is home to more than 200 wineries.

Paso Robles also has a downtown dining scene and the Tin City tasting district. Nearby Santa Margarita Lake offers kayaking, fishing, and hiking.

Concierge Auctions is handling the sale through its division focused on wineries, vineyards, and other wine country real estate.

Creston Manor brings those uses together on a single property, with the working winery and vineyard sharing the acreage with livestock facilities and multiple homes.

Chad Roffers, the auction house’s CEO and co-founder, said the company hopes to draw international bidders to Creston Manor, just as it did when it brought Windfall Farms to auction earlier this year.

The auction house also says a closing will trigger a contribution toward building homes for families in need through its Key for Key program, a partnership with Giveback Homes.
