What a crypto screener can and can't tell you: volume delta, open interest and funding in plain words

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Most people open a crypto screener, sort by "top gainers" and stop there. The other columns usually say more. Here is what each one means, what it's good for, and where it stops being useful. I'll use our own free screener as the example because I know exactly how its numbers are built, but the columns are standard and you'll find them in most tools.

Change. Price change over the timeframe you picked. Useful mainly for sorting. On its own it tells you what already happened.

Volume. Traded value in USDT over the same window. A 3% move on tiny volume and a 3% move on ten times the usual volume are different events. The first one can be a single order in a thin book.

Volume delta. Market buys minus market sells. Every trade has a side that crossed the spread. If buyers were the ones crossing it, delta is positive. This is the column that shows who was in a hurry. Price up with positive delta means buyers pushed it. Price up with negative delta means sellers were hitting bids and price rose anyway, which usually means someone was absorbing them with limit orders.

Buy share. The same thing as a percentage: what part of the volume was aggressive buying. 50% is balance. It's easier to compare across coins than raw delta, since a $2M delta is huge for a small cap and noise for BTC.

Volatility. (High minus low) divided by open for the window. It tells you how wide your stop has to be to survive ordinary noise on that coin right now.

Open interest change. Whether futures positions were opened or closed during the move. Price up with OI up: new positions are coming in. Price up with OI down: shorts are closing, and that kind of move tends to run out when they're done.

Funding. What longs pay shorts (or the other way round) each funding period on perpetuals. Strongly positive funding means longs are crowded and paying to stay in. It's a cost if you hold, and a hint about positioning.

Long/short ratio. The share of accounts that are long versus short. It counts accounts, not money, so a few big traders on one side can outweigh thousands of small ones on the other. Read it loosely.

One column rarely means much. Two or three together start to describe a situation:

A screener shows where the activity is. It does not predict where price goes next, and I'd be careful with anyone who says it does.

We tested this on our own data. We took a year of per-minute taker buys and sells on 7 coins (375 days) and wrote down three order-flow ideas before running them, each tested long and short on two horizons, so 12 tests. Costs were set at 16 basis points. None of the 12 beat costs. Delta describes the present very well. As a standalone entry signal it didn't pay for its own fees.

So I use a screener for two things: to find which coins are actually moving on real volume right now, and to understand what kind of move it is before looking closer. The decision still needs a plan with a stop.
