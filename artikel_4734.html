The 5 Best Smart Rings, Tested by Fitness Editors

Great-looking fitness-tracking tech right on your finger.

SMART RINGS HAVE quickly become one of the most popular forms of health tracking on the market today. These minimalist wearables deliver advanced health and fitness metrics—like sleep, recovery, readiness, and even stress tracking—without the bulk of a watch or the distraction of a screen. We put the most popular models through weeks of testing and spoke with experts to find the best smart rings for every kind of user.

One thing to be clear on: Smart rings and fitness trackers are not created equal. Smart rings definitely focus more on overall wellness while fitness trackers are more for dialing in on workout metrics. While smart rings still offer workout modes and step tracking, the workout metrics are not nearly as in-depth as those you’ll find on an Apple Watch or Garmin. But these devices offer a way to monitor your daily health-related metrics in a more subtle and discreet way. Whether you’re looking to track your sleep more accurately, understand your recovery needs, or monitor other daily health-related metrics, there’s a smart ring for you on this list.

As the Fitness and Reviews editor at Men’s Health, it’s my job to test the latest and greatest fitness tech, including smart rings. I’ve been a smart ring wearer since the first Oura Ring was released, and they’ve been an important tool in providing me with health data throughout the years.

In order to build this guide of the best smart rings, I gathered the most popular model of each brand on the market and tried them out for at least two weeks. I wore them during workouts, walks, writing, sleep, and ordinary errands around the home and out and about. This guide is the result of our experience and testing, and will be updated as new products are released.

Here is our guide to the best smart rings.

The Oura Ring continues to come out on top for us, even as we continue to test new more rings over the years. As the most up-to-date version of Oura’s popular smart ring, the Oura Ring 5 brings new sensors and algorithmic improvements to an already powerful health-tracking device. This version nixed the tangible sensors and cut back on the thickness to build an even sleeker, thinner, and more comfortable model—but don’t worry, the data has only improved with each iteration.

From a health-tracking standpoint, the Oura Ring 5 gives you a holistic picture of your wellness: temperature variation, sleep analysis, blood oxygen monitoring, heart rate variability (HRV), resting heart rate, activity tracking, and daytime stress detection. If you wear it overnight, it automatically logs how long and how well you slept, how much time you spent in deep sleep, REM, and light sleep, and even gives you a readiness score each morning.

I personally love the sleep-tracking feature on my Oura Ring. It’s definitely my most-utilized function—it’s the first thing I check every morning when I wake up. And I have to say, it’s highly accurate. I wake up at least once a night, and I’ll typically look at the clock when I do. When I check my Oura sleep metrics in the morning, it shows my midnight wake-up time almost exactly every time.

In addition to its accuracy and sleek look, the Oura Ring’s app is also a standout. It’s intuitive and easy to navigate, presenting your metrics in separate tabs so you know exactly where to look. One of the only downsides? To unlock all the features, you’ll need to sign up for a $5.99/month membership. But given the insights you’ll gain into your sleep cycles, stress levels, and recovery patterns, we find it a worthwhile investment.

For those who want insights into sleep, heart rate, and recovery—without dropping $400 or signing up for a monthly membership—the RingConn Gen 2 Air offers great value. RingConn has refined its hardware with this second generation, offering a noticeably lighter and more comfortable fit than the original.

What makes RingConn different is its inclusion of sleep apnea detection, a rare find in this industry, especially without requiring a subscription. However, the Air does not offer this feature that RingConn is known for. The RingConn Gen 2 Air monitors a wide range of sleep metrics, including time spent in each sleep stage, movement during the night, and breathing irregularities. It’s an ideal ring for anyone trying to better understand how their overnight recovery impacts daily energy and long-term health, but it does not monitor sleep apnea like its sister product, the RingConn Gen 2.

I do have some qualms with the workout tracking. While those looking for in-depth fitness tracking are more likely to go with something like a Garmin or Whoop, the RingConn Gen 2 Air is especially basic when it comes to distinguishing different workouts. I wouldn’t really trust it to correctly judge what kind of workout I’m doing, but perhaps running. It can log activity and heart rate zones, but its technology for exercise auto-detection is new this year and not entirely accurate yet.

Still, for users more concerned with sleep and recovery than fitness performance, the Gen 2 Air is more than capable. Just note that sizes run slightly large, so it’s worth using their sizing kit or sizing down if you’re between sizes. The company also released an updated version, the Gen 3 Air, but honestly, it’s more expensive, heavier, and still can’t compete with the Oura Ring. So better to stick with this version in my opinion.

Samsung’s long-awaited Galaxy Ring is a newer addition to the smart ring catalog. Crafted from lightweight titanium and available in three finishes, if you already use a Samsung Galaxy phone or smartwatch the integration takes no effort.

Designed to work seamlessly with Samsung Health, the Galaxy Ring tracks heart rate, activity, and sleep highly accurately. Its biggest selling point may be its emphasis on stress and sleep metrics, which have become central to Samsung’s wellness strategy. Galaxy Ring will even give you a sleep animal that reflects your sleep habits—dog is the golden ticket there. If you get, say, a turtle (you fall asleep slowly and may have some interruptions throughout the night), Galaxy AI will bring you a proposed plan of action to get your sleep back on track, taking sleep tracking to that next level. It also includes skin temperature sensors and supports auto workout detection for some activities, like walking and running.

While it lacks compatibility with iPhones, it does everything Android users need it to. And although Samsung hasn’t introduced a subscription plan yet, there’s speculation that one may arrive in the future to access advanced features. For now, all features are free, making it one of the most robust and cost-effective options for Android users. And if you’re debating between the Oura Ring and the Samsung Galaxy Ring, we have things to say.

Amazfit is known for its budget-friendly fitness tech that often outperforms what you may expect from an Amazon brand product. The new Helio Ring continues that by offering a smart ring that boasts solid metrics, sleek design, and no subscription fee. It’s especially alluring for users already in the Amazfit ecosystem—this ring pairs seamlessly with devices like the T-Rex 3.

The Helio Ring includes the basic smart ring metrics: sleep tracking, readiness scoring, heart rate monitoring, and activity logging. It also offers a few innovative features, like built-in AI coaching, which can help you adjust your sleep habits, activity levels, and even recovery tips based on your daily data. The ring is comfortable and well-made, featuring a durable titanium exterior and a fairly slim profile.

That said, if you’re expecting Oura-level insights, you may find the data a little surface-level. It doesn’t really stack up to its competition when it comes to the app and the data. I would say it’s much more of a smart ring for beginners looking to save a few hundred dollars while getting something that is generally useful and looks good on the finger. To go a little deeper, the app is functional, though not especially polished, and some might miss the granularity offered by more premium competitors. Still, for the price—just under $200—the Helio Ring offers a step into smart rings, especially for anyone prioritizing affordability and comfort.

The first time I put on the Oura Ring 4, I figured that this was as good as it got when it comes to smart rings. Super low profile. Super light. And a charge that lasts for a week. The removal of the nodes in previous generations was the clincher.

The app and software that come with an Oura Ring come with any of the versions. I am impressed by how small and thin the Oura Ring 5 is. They’ve managed to put that impressive tech into a smaller unit, which is even more discreet. But personally, I find the ring too small, to the point that it feels a little strange and less secure on my finger.

Senior Fitness Editor Brett Williams, NASM, still prefers the slim design of the Oura Ring 5, but if you want to score almost all of the same features in a bigger ring, you might want to opt for the Ring 4. You might even score a serious discount, as their last-gen model goes on sale often.

The Ultrahuman Ring AIR was previously on this list as our Best No-Membership-Needed Smart Ring, but after a legal dispute with another smart ring maker, they could no longer sell that version in the United States.

We are currently testing a review unit of the Ultrahuman Ring Pro, the brand’s latest offering, which is currently available for pre-order, to see if it’s worth adding to this guide. Ultrahuman is promising a longer battery life, advanced insights, and a more streamlined app experience.

Design is more than just looks—it’s about wearability. Smart rings need to be lightweight enough for 24/7 wear, durable enough for workouts and showers, and comfortable enough that you forget they’re on. Most are made from lightweight metals like titanium or aluminum alloy, and many brands include a sizing kit to find your ideal size. Pay attention to internal sensor placement as well—rings with smooth, node-free interiors are more comfortable for long-term use.

Color and style also matter. While many smart rings come in simple black or silver, several brands offer gold, matte gray, or brushed finishes to suit different tastes (though those options can cost more). Just remember: These rings are not meant to be the flashiest piece of jewelry you wear—they are designed for functionality, and you will notice scratches over time, regardless of the color you choose.

The finger that you place your smart ring on also matters. While a smart ring can still work no matter what finger you place it on, most companies recommend that you place it on your forefinger on your non-dominant hand, because that is where your pulse is the strongest and is less likely to get in the way of daily tasks.

The ring’s hardware is only half the equation—the app experience is where you’ll glean all the information your ring has to offer. Some apps, like Oura and Samsung Health, offer intuitive scoring systems for recovery, readiness, and sleep, plus easy-to-read graphics and insights. Others, like Ultrahuman and Amazfit, may have a steeper learning curve because the interface isn't laid out as intuitively. However, they aren’t outrageously difficult to follow, just a little less categorized than others like Oura and Samsung.

You’ll also want to consider whether the app integrates with your other health platforms, like Apple Health, Google Fit, or Garmin Connect—and whether it provides usable insights, not just raw data. For example, you don’t just want to see a low readiness score—you want to hear from your ring why that score may be too low and how to fix it: "Your activity was through the roof yesterday, maybe take it easy today and find some time to recover." The best apps break down complex biometrics into simple takeaways you can actually use.

In all our testing with smart rings (and even fitness trackers in general), accuracy has been the hardest metric to measure. For these smart rings, we found that bigger names like Oura and Galaxy Ring had the most consistent results across sleep stages, heart rate, HRV, and temperature tracking. Which makes sense with more funding backing the brand names. We also compared their workout metrics against wrist trackers like the Apple Watch, and accuracy held up for most workouts. Ultrahuman was close behind, especially succeeding in the recovery category. RingConn and Amazfit sometimes showed small accuracy inconsistencies, especially with workout differentiation. If you look at the prices here, you’ll see a pattern: The more expensive the ring, the more accurate the data.

These rings aren't meant to give you the most accurate workout training data, though. If you're looking for a device to wear only for your workout, that will provide you with training insights, you are going to want to go for a fitness tracker designed for that situation. If you are going to wear it during your runs or lifts, you may want a wrist or torso tracker. "Nobody really needs to be wearing two fitness trackers at any time," says Men's Health Fitness Director Ebenezer Samuel, C.S.C.S. "Find the one that fits your needs and will give you the best data for your goals."

Smart rings aren’t medical devices. They offer estimates based on algorithms and sensor data, not lab-verified testing. But if you use them consistently, even numbers that aren’t quite perfect can give you valuable insight into your health patterns over time, as rings will compare your past data against your current data for a more complete picture.

As with most technology, the more you use the product, the more often you have to charge it. Typically, a fully charged smart ring lasts about four to eight days. Oura and RingConn top the list with a full week of use per charge (potentially eight days with light use). Samsung’s Galaxy Ring is also impressive with around seven days. However, less expensive rings generally run closer to four or five days of charge.

Most rings charge via a small dock or case, and a full charge takes about 60 to 90-minutes. Consider how the charging process fits into your routine—especially if you plan to wear the ring to sleep. A good time to charge is often in the shower each day (and it's also a good time to clean it). With this method, you’ll never have to leave the ring off for that extended 90 minute charge time. And frankly, you won’t even have to worry about the length of time your ring will hold a charge. I do this with my ring, and I’ve never dipped below a 70% charge (except that one time I forgot my charger on a week-long vacation, oops).

Well, that all depends on your individual priorities. Smartwatches generally offer more features: GPS, message notifications, guided workouts, music controls, and in-depth workout analytics. But they also tend to be bulkier, need more frequent charging, and cause screen distractions.

Smart rings, on the other hand, are streamlined, screenless, and usually focused on sleep, stress, recovery, and wellness in general. They’re ideal if you want to track biometrics without getting sucked into a screen every few minutes. For the data-obsessed, wearing a smart ring at night and adding a smartwatch during the day is a great combination.

That said, if you’re a serious runner, cyclist, or athlete who wants workout coaching and performance stats in real time, a smartwatch is likely a better fit. If you’re focused on recovery, sleep quality, and managing stress, a smart ring may be the choice for you.

Its lightweight titanium build and water resistance make it comfortable for 24/7 wear, and the battery reliably lasts up to a week on a single charge. The Oura Ring 4 excels in sleep tracking, heart rate variability monitoring, and daily readiness scoring, with an intuitive app that presents data in a clear, actionable format. For most users, the Ring 4 delivers a seamless, unobtrusive way to monitor overall wellness, making it a favorite among those who dislike wrist-worn trackers.
