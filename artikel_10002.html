I can't even imagine trying to keep such old equipment working, over such a long timespan. Both the software and hardware world have been completely revamped multiple times over since then. The first C++ compiler hadn't yet been released, when Voyager was launched. Nobody ever heard of "software design patterns" or "dependency injection" or "single responsibility." I've been writing software for a mere 38 years on the job, and I personally shudder when I look at some of my old code.

These guys are geniuses, nothing less. This is an achievement worth celebrating. Perfect? No. Nothing is. But the proof is, it still runs. I'm personally amazed at what they have accomplished.

There is value in the KISS philosophy of design. Do a very few things and do them in as tight and well checked block of code as possible since every byte counted, and have redundant hardware. Software like Unix used to be before systemd and GUIs. Fire up SCO in an emulator today and it's blazingly fast compared to modern desktops. Just run DOS vs Windows for that matter. The more you expect from software, the more complex it gets, and the less reliable it is.
