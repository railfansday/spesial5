I Have Serious Questions About the 2026 Grammy Nominations

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Is the Recording Academy out of its mind?

The Grammys, more than any other awards-voting body of the big EGOT four, is easily the most chaotic. Sure, we can shake our fists in the air all day about how Stanley Kubrick somehow never won an Oscar, or ask ourselves why the Tony Awards ever allowed themselves to become a place where SpongeBob SquarePants: The Broadway Musical leads with 12 nominees. But recalling Grammy events like Macklemore beating Kendrick Lamar, Esperanza Spalding claiming Best New Artist over Justin Bieber and Drake, and Beck taking home Album of the Year instead of Beyoncé, it’s a wonder just what happens between when the votes are submitted and when the winners are revealed.

This year? The Recording Academy announced on Monday, November 16, that Kanye West is a Grammy nominee once again. And there’s a single category where Bruce Springsteen, Phoebe Bridgers, and Charli XCX are lumped together, so I guess they’re considered basically the same kind of artist to voters. Plus, new ideas like “Best Asian Pop Music Performance” are moving forward despite immediately losing support from the most prominent artist in that category, and Best New Artist nominees can apparently put out their fifth studio album and still be considered. What’s going on?

So, before the Grammys decide to ignore all of our desires and complaints and just shower Taylor Swift with more little golden phonographs on February 7, 2027, let’s try to make sense of the 2026 Grammy nominees below.

The first thought that entered my mind when I saw this year’s nominees for Album of the Year was, Are we confirming who will be available to perform before announcing the nominees now? Because even if so, I think Bruno Mars would perform either way.

It’s very cool to see how women dominated the music field this year, especially because they truly did. Olivia Rodrigo, Phoebe Bridgers, and Rosalía all earning Album of the Year nominations is a no-brainer. Olivia Dean, who won Best New Artist last year, and Ella Langley, who wrote “Choosin’ Texas,” make up the rest of the list. (Did we really love that entire nineteen-track Langley album?) Plus, of course, the obligatory Taylor Swift nominations.

But were Bruno Mars and Leon Thomas—who are tied with seven nominations each after the leading women, including an Album of the Year nod for Mars—the two men with the best offerings this year?

Bruno Mars had a very good song on The Romantic with “Why You Wanna Fight?” The Luther Vandross-esque R&B ballad was everything you’d look for in a Song of the Year nominee. But instead, it looks like Bruno is going all in on “Risk It All” and “I Just Might,” the latter of which is more of a “Happy” by Pharrell Williams-type song that somehow isn’t the lead single of the next Trolls movie.

Sorry Madonna! Apologies Robyn, Tinashe, Slayyter, and every other pop artist who soundtracked our year on the dance floor with some of the best albums of 2026. Breaking into the top four major categories still looks like a no-go.

Instead, Madonna, Robyn, and Tinashe were relegated to categories such as Best Dance Pop Recording and Best Dance/Electronic Album, even despite critical acclaim. If you want a Record of the Year nod, try writing a song for Toy Story 5.

Don Toliver is up for Best New Artist. The Houston rapper, who was signed to Travis Scott’s label in 2018, released his fifth studio album, Octane, this year. He had a song on the F9 soundtrack in 2021—which isn’t even the latest Fast & Furious movie—and his 2023 album, Love Sick, featured James Blake, Future, and Justin Bieber. But here we are, in 2026, watching him compete for Best New Artist.

Equally puzzling: Remi Wolf is nominated for Best New Artist. The singer’s exceptional debut record, Juno, came out in 2021. And her best record to date, Mud, was released recently on October 9, just outside of the Grammy submission window that closed in late August. But following the logic of how Olivia Dean won Best New Artist last year even though Dean’s album is competing this year, it’s clear that the whole Best New Artist category needs some rethinking.

2026 wasn’t a banner year for rap. I can admit that. Sure, we had very good records from A$AP Rocky, Denzel Curry, Vince Staples, JPEGMAFIA, Isaiah Rashad, Genesis Owusu, and Baby Keem. But none of the musicians I listed above went recognized this year, except for Keem’s single “Best Rap Performance” nomination.

Instead, the Recording Academy nominated Wale’s Everything Is a Lot—the Washington, D.C., rapper’s best and most mature album of the last decade, but far less influential than Rocky’s or Keem’s releases. And if not for Drake’s long-running boycott, the absence of Iceman’s “Janice STFU” just looks silly. But if you want to talk ridiculous, let’s see how Rap Album of the Year nominee Kanye West plays out for the Recording Academy over the next couple of months.

Harry Styles won Album of the Year for Harry’s House in 2023. He beat out Un Verano Sin Ti by Bad Bunny, Renaissance by Beyoncé, Mr. Morale & the Big Steppers by Kendrick Lamar, and 30 by Adele. It was a major upset. And just last month, the former One Direction member wrapped a historic thirty-night residency at Madison Square Garden in New York City in support of his latest album, Kiss All the Time. Disco, Occasionally.

Now, I’m not saying Kiss All the Time is a masterwork, but it’s strange that Styles didn’t receive a single nomination for his Harry’s House follow-up. “Aperture” was very catchy! Taylor Swift can’t sneeze without the Recording Academy inventing a new category for her to compete in. But an artist who just won Album of the Year can find himself completely shut out, even if his latest record is a success?

The global K-pop megastars reunited this year following mandatory military service in South Korea for one of the genre’s most-anticipated albums of all time, ARIRANG. The record paid homage to the popular Korean folk song of the same name while also pushing the K-pop genre forward with hip-hop-heavy material. ARIRANG’s fourteen tracks also filled the top fourteen spots of Spotify’s global Top 50 chart on its first day of release on March 20. I’ll stop there, but there are a hundred more ways to say that it was one of the biggest albums of the year.

But when the Recording Academy announced a new category this year, “Best Asian Pop Music Performance,” the group made the decision not to submit for the Grammys this year. The move was widely regarded as a protest of the new category, which many interpreted as a ghettoization to keep the rise of popular Asian acts away from the general field categories. “I hope that music can be heard and loved for what it is, rather than being divided by region or language,” the group posted on social media.

On Monday, November 16, the Academy went through with the category anyway, announcing nominees including Lisa from Blackpink, the Japanese pop-metal group Babymetal, the K-pop group IVE, and the Filipino girl group Bini, among others. But with BTS joining Drake in his boycott of the Grammys, I wonder how the Recording Academy will fare without two of the world’s most popular acts entertaining its awards show.

The Great Divide, a seventeen-track rock album that turned Kahan into one of the most popular touring musicians of 2026, was considered a favorite to receive a nomination in every major category this year. Songs like “American Cars” dealt with past failures and road trip blues, while lead single “The Great Divide” tackled how fame added extra struggles to many of his already-strained relationships. And Kahan had the resume to become a major category contender this year. He had a Number One record! He did the full press tour, he performed on Saturday Night Live, and he sold out stadiums all across the country.

But it must have been an omen when Kahan sang that “California’s so much more than some awards show” on the deluxe edition’s “Orbiter,” because he received just one nomination on Monday for Best Contemporary Folk Album. It’s a shame.

Best Album Cover is back. The category returned last year and accurately awarded Tyler, the Creator’s zany Chromakopia artwork, which featured the artist wearing a black, ceramic mask. He told Complex at the time that it symbolized how the new music was “so honest, that I think I had to wear a mask of my own face to get some of that sh*t out.”

But what about this year’s nominees? Well, Mavis Staples’s Sad and Beautiful World artwork depicts the artist sitting at a table. Interpol’s This Mirror Weighs a Ton artwork is a chandelier made of security cameras, and Lily Allen’s West End Girl is a painting of the artist wearing a polka-dot jacket. Are these nominees... the best album covers of the year?

Charli XCX’s photograph of Martin Scorsese, John Cale, and Marc Jacobs was easily the much-talked-about album cover of the year, even though Music, Fashion, Film wasn’t nominated. Olivia Rodrigo’s upside-down swinging for you seem pretty sad for a girl so in love is definitely one of the more memorable covers that come to mind. Same goes for Robyn’s blurry and nude Sexistential photo, the politics behind Kneecap’s Fenian, Thundercat biting his dark leather jacket on the cover of Distracted, or even a pregnant Latto posing with a baby tiger for the Big Mama cover ahead of her cheetah-themed baby shower.

We might not have a specific shade of green to color this year and make Best Album Cover an easy choice, but if you’re going to bring the category back? At least wow us.
