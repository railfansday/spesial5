This is one of the best times of the year to find flight deals. Black Friday is almost here, followed quickly by Cyber Monday and Travel Tuesday. All the major airlines, hotels and booking platforms have big sales. Of course, TPG will have roundups of all the best deals, but there are other ways to find deals that I've been experimenting with: artificial intelligence.

I've been an AI skeptic the past few years, at least when it comes to finding and booking travel deals, but it has come a long way since TPG tried to use AI to plan an itinerary back in 2023. (Spoiler: It didn't do a great job.)

But the technology has improved significantly over the past few years, especially when it comes to confirming you are getting a good deal.

ChatGPT, Anthropic's Claude and Google Gemini can now also help you compare prices and find cheaper airports or travel dates, decipher fare rules and restrictions, and even let you know whether it's better to pay cash or use points or miles.

I still don't find AI great at finding mileage or points awards, but it can do a lot.

"AI is already a powerful tool for finding flight deals and only getting better, but it’s still got a long way to go," Thrifty Traveler executive editor Kyle Potter said. "It’s prone to mistakes and falls victim to phantom award availability."

Still, combining AI with tools like Google Flights and Points Path can make finding deals easier and potentially save you some serious money (or miles).

So it can be a useful tool, but I don’t think it’ll ever be the only tool travelers need.

Here are some ways to use AI to find deals.

Like I always tell people looking for deals, flexibility is key. Rather than asking for a specific date or flight, describe your budget, home airport, ideal travel dates and how flexible you are in your AI tool.

AI will work with tools like Google Flights to help you find a good deal. Here's a sample prompt:

"I'm looking for Black Friday flight deals departing from New York City. My budget is $500 round-trip, I'm flexible on destinations, and I can travel between January and March 2027. Suggest 10 destinations worth checking, including nearby airports and typical fare ranges. Don't invent live prices."

ChatGPT gave me a list of potential deals.

It also showed some of its top picks, including San Juan and Barcelona.

As I continued scrolling, ChatGPT eventually showed me a link to Google Flights Explore, one of my absolute favorite tools for finding flight deals. As you can see, this prompt pulls up a Google Flights search for some cheap trips from New York City-area airports to places nearby and, if you scroll the map, far away, too. I had to narrow the search a bit for just trips in January, but there were a ton of options.

You may be asking yourself why you need AI if you are going to Google Flights Explore anyway. Great point — I think the idea is that ChatGPT can give you a few ideas tailored to your specific interests and budget.

AI can also help you find savings by flying into cheaper airports or on different travel dates that will save you some cash. You might try Oakland San Francisco Bay Airport (OAK) instead of San Francisco International Airport (SFO), for example. Or, further afield, maybe flying into Brussels and taking a train instead of flying directly into Paris can save you money.

"I want to fly from New York to Paris in February 2027. What nearby departure and arrival airports should I compare? Suggest flexible travel dates and explain which alternative airports have reasonable ground transportation."

ChatGPT suggested a few alternate airports for me in Europe.

Of course, that doesn't really help without seeing the prices. But it will give you some more ideas; you can then check the live prices at Google Flights. Here's where it gets more interesting, though. When I kept pushing the AI tool to find actual deals, it gave me a list of fares to various cities, including Frankfurt and Amsterdam. And when I prodded it again with the prompt, "Anything cheaper if I fly straight to Paris?", it found a couple of better deals.

Those are great deals. Just be sure they are actually bookable. In this case, I did not find the same deal on the FrenchBee website or on Google Flights, but I did find it on Icelandair via Expedia (where Chat had directed me).

So in this case, AI was able to find me an actual deal to Paris, though I likely could have found that same deal by starting my search on Google Flights and avoiding multiple steps. Still, I'm a bit flabbergasted that AI has gotten good enough to find actual flight deals.

With Black Friday almost here, this could be among the most useful ways to use AI.

Airlines love advertising discounts of 20%, 30% or even 50%, but those promotions sometimes apply only to base fares, select dates or specific routes.

AI can help readers understand the fine print and calculate their actual savings.

"I'm considering this Black Friday airfare sale. Analyze the terms and conditions, including blackout dates, excluded routes, baggage charges, booking deadlines and whether the discount applies to the base fare or total ticket price. Tell me what I should verify before booking. Here are the terms: [paste sale details]."

For example, Cathay Pacific has a Black Friday deal on now for "round-trip savings from the U.S. to Hong Kong and destinations across Asia, Australia and New Zealand, with 15% to 25% off Economy, 15% to 30% off Premium Economy and 10% off Business."

I tried to find a round-trip fare from Seattle to Hong Kong, which quoted me $2,687 for premium economy. I then asked ChatGPT if it was a good deal using the prompts above.

ChatGPT told me that, essentially, that wasn't a great deal. When I pressed it to find me a better deal, it couldn't. However, it did find an additional coupon I could use: "promotion code US26BF115 for the additional $115 discount and potential 3,000 bonus Asia Miles."

So when you read the TPG guide to the best Black Friday and Cyber Monday, be sure to ask ChatGPT if it's really a good deal.

Remember that a 40% discount on the base fare doesn't necessarily translate into 40% off the total ticket price.

Want to know if you should use cash or points to pay for your flights? Of course, TPG has a great points and miles calculator, but AI can help, too.

Black Friday airfare sales sometimes make paying cash a better deal than redeeming points and miles.

Readers can use AI to calculate the value of their points and compare cash fares against award tickets.

"I found a Black Friday round-trip flight for $450. The same flight costs 35,000 airline miles plus $50 in taxes. Calculate the cents-per-mile value and explain the trade-offs between paying cash and redeeming miles. Assume both tickets offer comparable benefits."

In this hypothetical example, the redemption yields about 1.14 cents per mile, before accounting for any miles earned on a paid ticket.

As an illustration, I found a round-trip flight on American Airlines from New York City to Madrid in February for $670. Similar flights cost 60,000 plus $52.09 when booked with miles. I asked ChatGPT if it was a good deal. As you can see, based on TPG valuations, ChatGPT tells me to use cash, not miles.

I've found ChatGPT to be extremely useful in telling me whether to spend cash, miles or points on things like hotels and flights.

Despite my best efforts, AI was not able to find me actual deals for specific flights in most cases. AI still works best as a complement to established flight-search tools, not as a replacement.

Here's an example of a search I tried using Claude's AI. While Black Friday hadn't started yet, it had trouble finding any great deals and didn't show anything in real time.

Of course, I've mentioned Google Flights multiple times, as it's likely your best way to find those flight deals.

Another tool that TPG loves is Points Path. Download it to layer on top of Google Flights to flag good deals on points. Points Path can also send you alerts on good award deals.

For the best results, use award search tools like Seats.aero, Roame.travel or Point.me, alongside AI. You can search for business-class availability and even set alerts to be emailed when the award you are looking for opens.

I track my flights with two other new tools. PAiback has given me back $148 this year alone. I've also been experimenting with Junova. Both companies will monitor cash prices and get you refunds if the price drops, charging you a commission on the money saved.

New users at Junova get a $25 credit when signing up through the TPG referral link (so you get a $25 credit — then when you pay the 20% commission, it comes out of your $25 "bank"). We also like Autopilot.

The point of all this is that, in my experience, AI can't yet find good flight or points deals, though I will keep playing with it. My next move is to try to integrate it with one of my flight deal search companies, but we are not there just yet.

As previously mentioned, there is a lot AI still can't do perfectly. Remember that AI can hallucinate fares, routes, promotions and availability. In most cases, AI chatbots have access to live airline pricing; AI tools may present outdated prices or expired deals. AI-generated fare comparisons may also forget taxes, fees or baggage charges. Finally, even if AI finds you a great trip, the itinerary may disappear before you can book it.

And remember, points and miles deals are still very elusive even with the power of artificial intelligence.

As Potter from Thrifty Traveler told me, "In an age when airlines are getting stingier with award space — and there are more savvy travelers redeeming points than ever before — those great award deals are increasingly dynamic, hinging on new routes or airline capacity changes that can appear and vanish within not just hours but minutes."

In other words, AI won't be able to grab you those incredible deals like companies like Thrifty Traveler can.

Always confirm the final price, itinerary and fare rules before purchasing.

As someone who spends a good chunk of every day searching for flight deals, I've been pleasantly surprised by how far artificial intelligence has come. While I'm not ready to give up my favorite flight search tools, AI has earned a place in my travel-planning arsenal.

Whether you're shopping for Black Friday, Cyber Monday or Travel Tuesday deals, AI can help you think outside the box, identify cheaper destinations and alternative airports, decipher complicated sale terms, and determine whether you're better off paying cash or redeeming points and miles.

Just don't take everything AI tells you at face value. I've encountered plenty of fares that I couldn't actually book, and AI still has a long way to go when it comes to finding award availability.

For now, the sweet spot is combining AI with tried-and-true tools like Google Flights, Points Path and flight deal-alert services. And, as always, flexibility remains your greatest asset when looking for cheap flights.

AI may not be the ultimate travel deal finder just yet, but it could help you become a smarter shopper — and save you some serious cash or miles in the process.
