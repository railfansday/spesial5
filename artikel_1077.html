Michael Carrick has been sacked as Manchester United manager after overseeing the club’s worst start to the Premier League season in 40 years, with Mauricio Pochettino looking set to replace him.

A second Old Trafford comeback of the week, this time battling from two goals down twice to rescue a 4-4 draw with Aston Villa on Sunday, was not enough to save Carrick’s job and the club announced on Thursday that it had parted ways with him after just 10 games of the season and with his side 18th during the international break.

United ended a nine-game winless run when they came from two goals down to beat Roma in the Champions League last week but they have not won in eight attempts in the Premier League and have just one top-flight win this campaign.

Carrick survived crisis talks with the United hierarchy after defeats to Como and Bournemouth last month and was given time to turn results around, but speculation over a change begun the day after Sunday’s chaotic draw and The Independent understands contact was initially made with United States boss Pochettino over succeeding Carrick on Monday.

The Old Trafford hierarchy, including Jason Wilcox, Omar Berrarda and Sir Jim Ratcliffe, met in Monaco to discuss Carrick’s future and the club are on the verge of hashing out a deal with Pochettino, who is reportedly ready to accept the position.

The Manchester United Supporters’ Trust (MUST) has branded Michael Carrick’s sacking “another depressing moment” and argued the club’s problems rest primarily with its ownership, in a now-familiar plea to end the ongoing feeling of crisis at Old Trafford.

United sacked Carrick just 10 games into the Premier League season with the club in the relegation zone and having won just once in this domestic campaign.

The club’s hierarchy met minority owner Jim Ratcliffe in Monaco on Wednesday to discuss Carrick’s future and a statement on Thursday said that it had “come to the extremely difficult conclusion that a change is necessary in order to achieve our ambitions”.

Paul Scholes thinks Man United might be going in for Mauricio Pochettino a few years too late.

He said on The Good, The Bad & The Football, in partnership with Midnite: “I love Pochettino and we work with him at Soccer Aid, didn’t we, he’s a he’s a great man. I think he’s got the presence to manage a big football club like Manchester United.

“He’s got the respect to do it. He works with young players which obviously Manchester United want. My only slight concern is have that we come a little bit too late to the party.

“I think we’ve done that in the past. You look at Van Gaal, his best years were probably behind him. Mourinho again, I think his best years were probably behind him. Saying that, I’m not ruling out what he’s going to do with this Real Madrid team because this Real Madrid team look good.

“I just - when you really think about when we was talking about this two or three weeks ago after the Bournemouth game I don’t think any of us mentioned Pochettino.”

Today is only the sixth time in Premier League history two managers - Michael Carrick and Pierre Sage - have left their positions on the same day during a season.

The last time it happened actually wasn’t that long ago, with Wolves boss Gary O'Neil and Southampton manager Russell Martin both getting canned on 15 December 2024.

Pochettino is still contracted to US Soccer until the 2030 World Cup though an early departure from his deal feels increasingly likely. United have made contact with US Soccer over paying compensation to release Pochettino with rumoured figures reaching as high a $10m (£7.4m).

Should an agreement be made, Pochettino could, in theory, leave the US before the scheduled games against Haiti in order to spend time with Man United’s squad ahead of the crucial clash at Anfield.

In that case he could arrive at Carrington either the same day or a day after being formally announced as the new head coach. That would give him a maximum of nine days to prepare the team if his appointment is confirmed today (Thursday 12 November).

The more likelier option is that Pochettino leads the USMNT for their two fixtures with Haiti and reports to Carrington shortly afterwards.

In that case, he could be in post at Man United as early as Wednesday 18 November though given the time difference between the US and the UK, Pochettino would arrive late in the afternoon or early evening.

Even so, that would give him three full days, Thursday 19, Friday 20 and Saturday 21 to settle into his new environment and have time on the grass with the squad.
