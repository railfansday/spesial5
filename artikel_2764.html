Julius Randle on Nets' 'embarrassing' blowout loss to Bulls: 'One of the worst games I've ever been a part of'

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Brooklyn Nets wore red, white and blue alternate uniforms on Wednesday night, throwing it back to the franchise's ABA heyday when they welcomed the Chicago Bulls to Barclays Center. The evening featured an appearance by 10 members of the teams that won ABA championships in 1974 and 1976 — including the legendary "Dr. J," Julius Erving.

"I fully anticipated the Nets to be so fired up having us here, they would blow Chicago out," the Hall of Famer said during an appearance on the YES Network broadcast midway through the second quarter. "But I'm looking at 62-39, so … I was wrong."

The Nets scored Wednesday's first basket, with young guard Nolan Traoré getting a runout dunk after Egor Dëmin picked off a Josh Giddey pass. A minute later, Bulls guard Norman Powell knocked down a 3-pointer to put Chicago on top … and, well, that was all she wrote for Brooklyn.

The Nets never again led, or even tied, the visitors, trailing by as many as 37 points in a truly dismal performance that settled as a 137-107 blowout loss. At home. At the hands of what had been a three-win team, with the NBA's fifth-worst offense and absolute worst defense … and without exciting lottery pick Caleb Wilson.

It was a wretched outing — one that saw the Nets commit 20 turnovers leading to 27 Chicago points, and allow 15 first-half 3-pointers and 76 first-half points, the third straight game in which they conceded 70-plus before intermission. Brooklyn gave up 62 total points in the paint, and sat idly by as eight different Bulls scored in double figures.

In the midst of the depressing showing, courtside cameras caught Brooklyn forward Julius Randle lighting into his teammates on the Nets bench:

After finishing the night with 17 points, four assists and no rebounds in 27 minutes of work in the lopsided loss, Randle — who's soldiered through a 17-win season with the Lakers, the post-Anthony Davis doldrums in New Orleans, and the 21-win end of the David Fizdale era in New York — sounded a similarly dour note about the state of affairs for the 3-11 Nets in his postgame press conference.

"That was embarrassing," Randle said, according to Brian Lewis of the New York Post. "Been in the league for 13 years, and that's one of the worst games I've ever been a part of. And not because of the score … but it's how we did it. That wasn't cool. Nothing about it was cool. We've got to find a way to figure out the root of it or whatever, but that's just unacceptable. It's just a pride, an effort, the competitive spirit — all of that, it was nonexistent."

The Nets entered Wednesday intending to celebrate the high-water mark in franchise history, but wound up closer to a historic nadir. According to Erik Slater of Clutch Points, the last two losses "mark the first time they've given up 135 points in back-to-back games since April 1 [and] 2, 1969."

That's a far cry from where the Nets hoped to be after trading this summer for Randle, a three-time All-Star and two-time All-NBA selection who'd starred for the cross-town New York Knicks before helping the Minnesota Timberwolves make a pair of playoff runs, to pair with incumbent sweet-shooting forward Michael Porter Jr. in the frontcourt, in hopes of restoring a franchise that had the third-worst record in the NBA over the preceding three seasons to respectability.

But despite the presence of the 20-point-scoring veteran forwards and some exciting flashes from young guards Traoré, Dëmin and rookie Mikel Brown Jr., Brooklyn has remained mired in the muck, ranking among the league's worst teams on both ends of the court and looking like a team without much of a sense of who it wants to be, how it wants to play, and what its path to success might look like.

"Very disappointed, just starting with effort," head coach Jordi Fernández said after the game, according to C.J. Holmes of the New York Daily News. "We were not a competitive group. We were not a connected group. We were not a selfless group. I saw selfishness. I saw pointing fingers, and I saw guys that didn't always play hard."

That's not the brand of ball that Fernández has preached since arriving in Brooklyn in 2024. The team's desultory effort, though, suggests that maybe what he's preaching isn't quite getting across.

"I can try to say it in Spanish, because in English, they don't understand," Fernández quipped after the game. "So I'll keep trying and find whatever language works. But yeah, our effort was not good."

Julius Randle didn't mince words when describing the state of affairs for his 3-11 Nets. (Caleb Bowlin/Getty Images)

If the coach's message is falling on deaf ears, then it's up to the players to stem the tide and turn things around. Randle appeared to be searching for a way to do so with his impassioned entreaty on the bench; with another game coming against the Orlando Magic on Friday, he says he and his teammates have to look within for the answers they're seeking.

"I think when you win, you find ways to win. When you lose, you find excuses. Seems like a lot of excuses," Randle said, according to Collin Helwig of NetsDaily. "So, you know, for me, I've got to go back into work. I've got to go back into the film. I've got to figure out — I've got to look myself in the mirror tonight, too, like everybody else, and figure out what I can do better. Not point the finger or anything. Like, I got to figure out as a man [...] that has played this game since a kid and love it, and has given a lot to me, I got to figure out how I can help the team and be better for the team. So, you know, I put it on me."
