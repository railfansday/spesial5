Our Offense vs. Their Defense: Chicago Bears

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The New Orleans Saints take their offense and all of its issues on the road this week to play against one of the best defenses in the league in the Chicago Bears. This is an opportunity for the Saints to show signs of improvement, as the history of this current staff, the offensive pitfalls, and roster mismanagement are now telling a story of what can be expected when the Saints' offensive unit takes the field.

This team is starting to become a team that has to be something it is not. The goodwill and grace that were given to this team by so many are all but lost at this point in the 2026 campaign. This Saints offense is now what it is, and it is bad. The play designs, play-calling, and personnel usage all deserve to be questioned, so it is understandable that the Saints are 10-point road underdogs.

The Bears' defense held the Saints to only 14 points last year in what was former Saints head coach Dennis Allen's first season as the Bears' defensive coordinator. This year, he will be leading a top-10 defense in the NFL, as the Bears are currently ranked 7th in total defense, allowing only 310.9 yards per game. They are also allowing only 91.1 yards per game on the ground and 219.8 yards per game through the air, ranking 7th and 11th, respectively.

They can affect the passer in multiple ways, as the Bears are currently tied for 11th in interceptions with 7 and tied for 9th in sacks with 26. Their 13 takeaways on the season have them ranked 10th in the league. They lead the league in third-down defense, allowing opponents to convert only 22.8% of their attempts, and they allow touchdowns on only 53.8% of red-zone trips, which ranks 12th.

The Bears' defensive front is a monster. It features Montez Sweat, Austin Booker, Dayo Odeyingbo, and the recently acquired Jeffery Simmons, who is probably the best interior defensive lineman in the league right now. Simmons has 6 sacks so far this season, and he's now playing alongside a pass rush that features three different rushers with at least 3 sacks.

The linebacker group is very talented as well, especially with Devin Bush leading the way. He currently has 53 tackles and 2 sacks on the season.

The secondary is not one to play around with, either. Jaylon Johnson, Kyler Gordon, and rookie safety Dillon Thieneman are playing at very high levels right now. Gordon is playing that nickel/slot corner role that Dennis Allen loves to utilize so much, and he's playing it at an elite level. Gordon has recorded 4 sacks in 4 games, as well as 5 TFLs, so the Saints can expect pressure off the edge.

This matchup will present a very difficult challenge for this Saints offense. Morale is a little low around this team, and they just don't seem to have any answers. In this game, the Saints will have to become something they have not been in quite some time, and that is an actual functioning offense that can play complementary football.

The Saints should be more aggressive in the intermediate passing game. The Bears are allowing 8.2 yards per attempt and 13.2 yards per completion. The Saints are currently averaging 6.3 yards per attempt and 9.5 yards per completion.

The Saints will have to continue playing well on third downs because, as previously mentioned, the Bears are the best in the league at getting teams off the field on third downs.

Finally, the Saints will have to find a way to score in the first quarter. The Bears are a team that plays a time-of-possession style of football. When they get the ball, they like to keep it, so that means the Saints have to capitalize on every possession, especially in the first quarter. Force an offense that wants to play slowly out of its comfort zone.

The Bears are averaging 34:31 in time of possession, which leads the league. That plays back into the defense's ability to get off the field on third downs.

In this game, the Saints should not play into the Bears' hands offensively by playing their style of football. The issue with that, however, is that it's the only way this Saints offense knows how to play right now.

The Bears can be beaten, but the Saints will have to find their groove and be balanced on offense to do it.
