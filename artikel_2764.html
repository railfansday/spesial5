4 things we learned from the Giants’ 23-18 victory over the Jaguars

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Sunday was the day the New York Giants' Cinderella season carriage was going to turn back into the pumpkin of past season failures. That might yet happen – the road ahead is filled with difficult opponents. Today, though, the Giants for the first time established themselves as serious playoff contenders. They not only hung with the previously 7-2 Jacksonville Jaguars, they actually dominated a good part of the game. Things got dicey in the second half as the Jaguars started to move the ball and tighten up their defense, but the Giants held on for a 23-18 victory that solidified their hold on first place (on a day when the Cowboys and Eagles both won) and perhaps more importantly, suggested that they might be more than just a competent team that can squeeze out victories over the NFL's bottom-dwellers. The NFL, which gambled that the Giants were for real by flexing their game into the national 4:30 window, won their bet with a thrilling game. What did we learn from the Giants' 23-18 win over Jacksonville?

There's been evidence of this since the start of the season, when Dennard Wilson's aggressive approach proved a welcome change from the passive Shane Bowen defense in the Giants' first game against Dallas. Still, most Giants games have been against poor to middling offensive teams, and when they faced a good offense, the Rams ran roughshod over them. Today it was the Jaguars' No. 5 scoring offense in the NFL, one that had put 30 or more points up 6 times already this season. The 18 points they scored against the Giants today was their second lowest of the season, trailing only the 12 points they scored in Denver (which has one of the NFL's best defenses). You have to credit the D as a whole. Trevor Lawrence did manage to put up 301 passing yards, but he only passed for 1 TD and was intercepted twice. Furthermore, the Jags' potent running game only had 77 yards, though admittedly star running back Bhayshul Tuten left with an injury mid-game.

The main reasons that was true today were:

Carter has been having what by some metrics would be considered a breakout season, ranking high in a number of pass rush metrics such as pass rush win rate and time to pressure. What has been lacking are sacks. Today, though, Carter sacked Lawrence twice and hit or at least pressured him a handful of other times. Carter's quickness and agility getting around blocks is other-worldly, and today he was finally rewarded for the work he's been doing. Forget the stats, when J.J. Watt seems impressed, that's good enough for me.

Part of the reason Carter was effective was that he wasn't alone. Kayvon Thobodeaux, as usual, did not get any sacks, but he was getting pressure from the other side as well (in addition to having a strong game against the run and intercepting a 2-point conversion attempt), and D.J. Reader was also getting pressure inside and had a sack of his own. There would have been more, but Lawrence is a pretty mobile QB for a guy his size, and the Giants had him running around a lot trying to escape and find someone downfield. The good thing is that the Giants did it without blitzing as much as they have in some recent games.

Part of the reason Lawrence had a long time to throw today (his longest of the season) was that the Giants did a pretty good job, all things considered, against the Jaguars' potent receiving corps. Parker Washington, who has become one of the NFL's best receivers, had 103 yards today, but that was on 17 targets, of which he only caught 8. Likewise, Jakobi Meyers had 85 yards, but again, on 10 targets of which he only caught four.

Much of that was due to Paulson Adebo. The Giants' big free agent signing last year, a disappointment then and relegated to the bench most of this year after being injured in Game 1, Adebo played perhaps his best game as a Giant. He held Washington to zero catches on three targets and Meyers to one 23-yard catch in five targets. He broke up four passes and also had an interception . Adebo entered the game after Greg Newsome left with an injury in the first quarter. If he is finally returning to his form of a couple of years ago when he was a Saint, that could be huge for the Giants, who will face some potent passing offenses in the coming weeks (he writes while watching Brock Purdy carve up the Vikings defense in the first half on Sunday Night Football).

Meanwhile, Deonte Banks continues to play great ball. Banks only gave up two receptions in 10 targets for 21 yards and had his second interception of the season. To me, this was his best game of the season, even though he had the game-saving pick-6 against Arizona. It is such a remarkable development after watching him languish the past two seasons. Part of Lawrence's problem tonight was that he couldn't find open receivers. If this continues this could be the Giants' best secondary since their 2022 playoff season.

In their first nine games, the Giants had five of them in which they gave up 163, 123, 202, 175, and 105 rushing yards. Today they gave up only 77 yards to a Jacksonville team that had rushed for over 100 yards in each of its last four games. The big news pre-game was that Darius Alexander was not active. That's quite a fall from grace for the former high third round draft pick who had given the Giants a little interior pass rush but who had been a liability against the run. In his place the Giants played Bobby Jamison-Travis, who's become a regular part of the rotation, and C.J. Okoye, who had missed the past three games. It's worth watching going forward to see how IDL snaps are distributed and whether the Giants are able to hold up against formidable rushing teams that are coming up such as the 49ers and Lions.

It can be frustrating to watch this Giants offense sometimes. J.J. McCarthy looked good early on, but he went downhill as the game progressed. To Matt Nagy's credit, the Giants finally dialed up some downfield passes for McCarthy today. Unfortunately, he was pretty awful on those passes – 0 for 5 beyond 20 yards, with most of them sailing well beyond the receiver. The Giants might have put up more than 30 if he'd had better touch downfield. He was pretty good on downfield passes as a Viking, so let's hope that today was just an aberration, because adding a downfield threat to their offense could be the difference between hanging with the good teams or being steamrolled by them.

The problem is that they need for J.J. to be good, because their rushing game has been suspect. The Giants do not have good run blockers, especially on the interior offensive line. John Harbaugh and Greg Roman have been searching all season for a bellcow to help their offense run at maximum efficiency. One problem is that all their running backs fumble. Another problem is that they tend to get injured. Another problem is that none of them are elite running backs even when they are at their best. Cam Skattebo got the call again tonight with Tyrone Tracy still injured…and he fumbled again tonight at a critical juncture. With the Giants already up 20-6, with a first down at the Jaguars' 33 and 4:01 left in the third quarter, once again Skattebo was stripped, his fifth of the season. Only a tackle by of all people J.J. McCarthy kept Antonio Johnson from returning it for a TD.

That was really a turning point in the game. It went from a solid, bordering on easy, victory for the Giants to a nail-biter. Jacksonville got the momentum, and by the 3:43 mark in the fourth quarter, Cam Little's field goal had gotten the Jaguars to within 23-18. Be honest – did you think the Giants were going to lose at that point?

I did. Then something remarkable happened. After Skattebo ran for 3 yards, McCarthy completed a 19-yard pass to Malachi Fields to get the Giants to the Jacksonville 43 yard line with 3:02 left. Then Skattebo ran for 5 yards. Then he ran for another 5 yards and a first down. Then the Giants were able to just run the clock to zeros with McCarthy kneel-downs and keepers. I don't think we've seen that since the opening game against Dallas. Overall the Giants finished with 77 rushing yards, but 33 of those were by McCarthy, basically on scrambles (he lost yardage on a couple of designed runs).

The question is why the Giants running game was finally able to run out the clock, and whether they can finally start to do that earlier in the game. They are not a terrible running team – entering today they were middle of the pack in team rushing yards. But they are eighth-worst in yards per carry. And we know that they want to be a running team. Today they had 3.1 yards per carry, pretty pedestrian.

Yet in the end they managed to run out the clock. So I can't decide whether they are or are not a bad running team. Hence the strikethrough on the section title above. Let's see how the rest of the season progresses.
