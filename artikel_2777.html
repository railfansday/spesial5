4 steps Thunder need to take in order to snap three-game losing skid

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

4 steps Thunder need to take in order to snap three-game losing skid appeared first on ClutchPoints. Add ClutchPoints as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

Amid head coach Mark Daigneault and the Oklahoma City Thunder's three-game losing streak, All-Star Shai Gilgeous-Alexander remained optimistic about the future, and for good reason. Jalen Williams, Cason Wallace, and Chet Holmgren sustained long-term injuries that sent the Thunder's season into a tailspin. Oklahoma City (10-9) has fallen to eleventh in the Western Conference standings. However, the beginning wasn't so bad.

The Thunder beat the Memphis Grizzlies, the Washington Wizards, and the Cleveland Cavs twice. Short-term success for a team missing three starters, but the injuries didn't stop there. The Thunder also lost Jaylin Williams and Aday Mara to recent injuries in seven of their last nine games. With a thin frontcourt, Oklahoma City will have to reinvent itself once more.

The Thunder's razor-thin margin of error is a common theme, but if Oklahoma City is to snap its three-game losing skid, here are four steps that should secure its first win in four tries.

While Alex Caruso has been a reliable spark off the Thunder's bench lately, starting him sets an ideal tone on both ends of the floor. Caruso finished with 11 points, four blocks, two assists, two rebounds, and two steals in the Thunder's 113-106 loss to the Timberwolves.

When the Thunder's offense goes stale, Caruso's defensive stops help Oklahoma City find its rhythm. Adding Caruso to the starting lineup would also help the Thunder spread the floor. He's connected on 44.4% of his 3-point attempts this season. Opposing defenses can't leave him open.

Caruso starting should lead to higher-percentage looks for Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and Ajay Mitchell, both of whom have attacked the rim more than any other Thunder player this season. Maximizing Gilgeous-Alexander and Mitchell's production is most important while the Thunder find ways to put wins together without three starters and only one center in Isaiah Hartenstein.

Amid the Thunder's devastating injuries, Ajay Mitchell is averaging a career-high 17.2 points per game this season. He's taken on the role of the second-leading scorer alongside Thunder All-Star Shai Gilgeous-Alexander. However, opposing defenses have found ways to limit his production, as he's now in scouting reports.

Now, it's up to the Thunder head coach Mark Daigneault to put Mitchell in position to earn more frequent trips to the free-throw line. Oklahoma City is 3-0 whenever Mitchell finishes a game with six or more free-throw attempts, including a 30-point performance in a 125-114 win against the Phoenix Suns.

Mitchell's offense is critical for the Thunder's success without its core players. And while Jalen Williams and Cason Wallace won't be returning any time soon, unlocking the best version of Mitchell between now and Chet Holmgren's return puts Oklahoma City in position to win games. Holmgren sustained a right knee injury that will keep him sidelined until late December.

Thunder rookie Bennett Stirtz scored a season-high 21 points in a 123-113 loss to the Miami Heat. The confident shooter connected on nine threes in two games, following an 18-point performance with a career-high. Stirtz is taking on a role he hadn't anticipated at the start of the regular season, but he's answered the call anyways.

Earning Thunder head coach Mark Daigneault's trust, Stirtz earned his first career start in Wednesday's loss to the Timberwolves. He'll continue to receive the green light as a reliable shooter who can create defensive stops on the opposite end of the floor. Perhaps starting every now and then, depending on the opponent.

Stirtz will only improve through the reps as a rookie. And while the Thunder's three consecutive losses aren't ideal, giving the rookie guard extended runs and experience is best for the team amidst an extended list of injuries.

Thunder head coach Mark Daigneault sees growth in his team's approach despite their three-game skid. However, Oklahoma City has struggled to defend the 3-point arc. The Timberwolves connected on 17 threes in Wednesday's loss. And when the Thunder find themselves on the wrong end of a lopsided run, the threes come raining down quickly.

While the margin for error remains thin, Daigneault hopes the Thunder can find consistency in their approach night in and night out despite their shortcomings.

"The common theme is: it's a 48-minute game, and we need to execute a few more plays in a game that comes down to one or two possessions in order to be on the other side of the result," Daigneault said. "And that can happen in the second quarter. It can happen in the first quarter, and it can happen late. So, we just got to fight for those margins right now. Improve on the things that we know the game is going to come down to. There's plenty of controllable things that we can handle in these games.

"And that shows up in the first quarter, shows up in the fourth quarter; shows up throughout. We just got to continue to invest in those things. Trust that process and allow the wind to turn for us a little bit."

With two road games next up against the Milwaukee Bucks and the Brooklyn Nets this week, the Thunder will look to return to the win column with a 10-9 record on Thanksgiving.
