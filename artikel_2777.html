Observations after Sixers survive Bucks' late surge, reach 14-3

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Observations after Sixers survive Bucks' late surge, reach 14-3 originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

The Sixers survived the Bucks' late surge and reached 14-3 on the season Saturday afternoon with a 132-129 win at Xfinity Mobile Arena.

Tyler Herro nearly sent the game to overtime with a buzzer-beating three-pointer, but replays showed that he released his shot late.

Herro had a massive performance for the 1-16 Bucks, posting a career-high 50 points on 15-for-23 shooting, 11 rebounds and seven assists.

Joel Embiid had 26 points, a season-high 17 rebounds and nine assists for the Sixers.

LeBron James (21 points), Tyrese Maxey (17 points) and Anfernee Simons (18) were among the seven Sixers to score in double figures.

The Sixers were down Jaylen Brown (left hamstring strain), Dean Wade (left hamstring tightness/soreness), Adem Bona (cervical spine sprain) and Ariel Hukporti (right Achilles tendon tear).

Next up for the Sixers is a Monday night meeting with the Trail Blazers. Here are observations on their victory over the Bucks:

Maxey returned to the Sixers' starting five after missing the team's comeback win Wednesday over the Magic with right foot soreness. He was joined by VJ Edgecombe, Kentavious Caldwell-Pope, James and Embiid.

James threw down a dunk on the day's first play. Embiid then dished to a cutting Edgecombe for a layup that put the Sixers up 4-0.

The Bucks quickly found a foothold in the game.

Embiid missed his first four shots, including two that were swatted by Myles Turner. Maxey began 1 for 5 from the floor. Herro canned a three-pointer to give Milwaukee a 15-8 lead.

The Sixers entered the game with a minus-13.2 net rating in first quarters, which ranked 28th in the NBA. They faced a 30-29 deficit after Saturday's first period.

Even with Maxey available again, Labaron Philon Jr. deservingly stayed in the Sixers' rotation following an excellent outing in Orlando.

He scored two points in six first-half minutes. The 20-year-old made a rookie mistake at the tail end of the first quarter, fouling Herro on a three-point attempt.

Dominick Barlow checked in for his first action of the season with 2:51 remaining in the first quarter. He'd been sidelined for the Sixers' first 16 games because of a lower back stress reaction.

Barlow scored his first points early in the second quarter when he tipped in a Maxey miss. He then teamed up with James in crowd-pleasing style, sprinting ahead of the pack on a fast break and flushing home the 22-time All-Star's long-distance lob.

The afternoon was not devoid of rust for Barlow. Shortly after that alley-oop, he grabbed an offensive rebound, dribbled out to the perimeter and fired an open three-pointer … which he air balled short.

To Barlow's credit, he took another three early in the fourth quarter and drained it. He logged 11 minutes and was quite productive, tallying 12 points on 5-for-6 shooting and four rebounds.

"It felt great," Barlow said of his three. "Got to shoot the ball with confidence. … I've obviously got to get a game feel; I've been missing that. But I worked too hard (in the offseason) to not shoot the ball when I'm open."

Kyle Kuzma hit a three late in the second quarter to build the Bucks' lead to 59-52. After Sixers head coach Nick Nurse's timeout, the team closed the first half on a 8-0 run featuring an Embiid dunk.

The Sixers went just 3 for 16 from long range in the first half, but their low-turnover ways helped mitigate those shooting struggles.

They committed a single turnover in the first half and finished with four giveaways. That's tied for their lowest ever on record in a single game. The Sixers have the NBA's lowest turnover percentage outside of garbage time, according to Cleaning the Glass.

"No matter who you're playing against, teams try to turn you over, they try to speed you up," James said. "Us taking care of the ball, that's a lot of the reason why we won this game."

The Sixers' shooters were bound to start sinking some open looks eventually. Simons, Caldwell-Pope and Edgecombe all made threes late in the third quarter and the team's lead grew as high as 13 points.

Thanks largely to Herro's individuals exploits, the Bucks stayed competitive. AJ Green buried a corner three to cut Milwaukee's deficit to 106-103 with 8:10 to go in the game.

James sought out his shot much more following the Sixers' timeout and did so with great success. He scored four buckets inside over the next four minutes, including a big slam that put the Sixers up 121-112.

It appeared the main questions left were whether Embiid could reach a triple-double and just how many points Herro would rack up.

However, the Bucks forced the Sixers to work down the stretch. Herro got to the 50-point milestone and trimmed the Sixers' lead to two points when he converted a layup with 19.2 seconds left.

The Sixers safely navigated to the final buzzer by the slimmest of margins. Embiid made four late free throws and everyone on the Sixers' sideline was relieved to see that Herro hadn't quite released his jumper in time.

"I try not to complain too much about any win in this league," Nurse said. "The message is we've got to finish a little better."
