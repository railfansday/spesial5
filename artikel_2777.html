LSU football HC Lane Kiffin falls to his knees after another ugly Sam Leavitt interception

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

LSU Tigers head coach Lane Kiffin could barely watch anymore at Tiger Stadium. After quarterback Sam Leavitt threw his third interception of the night against the Texas Longhorns on Saturday, Kiffin dropped to his knees on the sideline, pressed his hands together and squeezed his eyes shut in sheer disbelief.

You could hardly blame Kiffin for praying. Leavitt entered the SEC showdown with nine interceptions through nine games, and his turnover troubles immediately resurfaced under the Baton Rouge lights. Texas defensive back Jelani McDonald haunted Leavitt all night, snagging two first-half picks, one at the Texas 32-yard line in the first quarter and another in the end zone from the 12-yard line in the second quarter.

Despite those red-zone blunders, No. 24 LSU carried a 14-10 halftime lead against No. 8 Texas thanks to two touchdown connections from Leavitt to wide receiver Jackson Harris. That momentum vanished on the opening drive of the third quarter. Leavitt forced another pass into the end zone, and McDonald hauled in his third interception of the game for a touchback, marking Leavitt's 12th pick of the season and sending Kiffin straight to the turf.

Fortunately for the Tigers, their defense rescued their quarterback once again. Texas quarterback Arch Manning threw two interceptions of his own to LSU safety Ty Benefield, including a costly third-quarter pick right after Leavitt's third turnover. Running back Dilin Jones capitalized by ripping off a 44-yard touchdown run to push LSU ahead 21-10 late in the third quarter. Through that point, Leavitt completed 23 of 30 passes for 224 yards, two touchdowns and three interceptions, while Manning managed just 82 passing yards and 47 rushing yards as Jones racked up 110 yards on 13 carries.

Harris led all pass catchers with eight catches for 64 yards and both scores, giving Kiffin plenty to smile about even after Leavitt's turnovers tested every ounce of his patience in Death Valley.
