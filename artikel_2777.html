Mikel Brown Jr. impresses in first start during Nets night to forget against Timberwolves

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

MINNEAPOLIS — Nolan Traore's hamstring injury is a tough break for the Nets and their second-year pro. But a golden opportunity for rookie Mikel Brown Jr.

With Traore sidelined for at least 8-to-10 weeks, Brown finally got his first NBA start Monday night in Minnesota. It may not have been a winning one for the lottery pick, but it was an impressive one.

The Nets got drubbed 142-116 by the Timberwolves before 18,329 at Target Center. But Brown's debut in the lineup was one of the few bright spots.

Brooklyn Nets guard Mikel Brown Jr. (0) drives past Minnesota Timberwolves center Rudy Gobert (27) during a game in Minneapolis, Monday, Nov. 23, 2026. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

"I felt great," Brown told The Post. "Obviously that first wind is different from when you're coming on the bench, so I got a little gassed for a bit in the first half. But once I found my rhythm, I was flowing. But it was great to be out there.

"In the first half we played well. But defensively, we just can't give up 140 points. We let them catch their rhythm early and that's what happens when you give good teams rhythm early on in the game. They had their rhythm throughout the game. We've got to cut that water off early. Take away those wide-open shots, those wide-open dunks that they had early on in the game and we would've been fine."

Traore was diagnosed with a right hamstring strain, opening the door for Brown, who had heretofore been on a minutes limit and treated with kid gloves.

But Monday the Nets (3-13) finally took the training wheels off. Brown finished with 17 points, a career-high seven assists and five rebounds, albeit six turnovers for a Nets team that looked fluid but far too cavalier and careless.

The Nets gave away 21 turnovers for 31 points. Combined with allowing 58 percent shooting and 17-of-39 from deep.

"It's disappointing," said Julius Randle, who had 29 points in his return to Minnesota but couldn't even pinpoint the single biggest defensive issue.

Grant Nelson (16) of the Brooklyn Nets drives to the basket against the Minnesota Timberwolves at Target Center in Minneapolis on Monday, Nov. 23, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

"We've got a lot. We were better earlier in the year and now we've got some slippage in multiple areas. So we've gotta figure out where it is. But defense just starts with competitive nature first and then go from there."

Brown was a rare silver lining. He poured in all of his points in the first half, then had five of his seven dimes and all of his boards in the second.

With Brown at the helm, the Nets shot 52.6 percent and hit 18-of-36 from deep in his first game replacing the injured Traore in the starting lineup.

Brooklyn Nets guard Mikel Brown Jr. (0) dribbles against Minnesota Timberwolves guard Ayo Dosunmu (13) during a game in Minneapolis, Monday, Nov. 23, 2026. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

"It's unfortunate for the kid because he's had a great summer," coach Jordi Fernández said of Traore. "He's about the right things. He's worked really hard. He's improved his body, his game. You just saw his confidence out there and his personality and how he communicated [with] his teammates. Everything has been great. Now, it's not the end of the world. Obviously it's an injury that we feel for him; he's already started working and he'll be back at some point.

"Life goes on and we want him around as soon as he can be with the team. That's important because his energy was so good, his voice. So, 'next man' mentality."

That next man will be Brown for the foreseeable future.

Moritz Wagner of the Brooklyn Nets reacts toward a fan during a game against the Minnesota Timberwolves in Minneapolis on Monday, Nov. 23, 2026. Getty Images

Egor Dëmin not only didn't get the nod at point guard, he didn't start at all. He came off the bench for a second straight game, finishing with 10 points on 3-for-13 shooting and clear struggles.

"Yes, he can do better with this ball pressure and physicality, that's for sure, communication," Fernández said.

Jaden McDaniels had 34 for the Timberwolves (13-5).

The Nets fell behind 25-12 with 3:54 left in the first on a Jonathan Kuminga layup.

Brooklyn gave up 10 unanswered to turn a six-point deficit into a 16-point hole. They trailed 74-61 going into the break, and couldn't rally.

At 86-77, the Nets coughed up a 20-3 run that blew it open altogether.

"A lot of things we could have done better, starting with our physicality, ball pressure, contesting shots," Fernández said. "We had hands down from the beginning of the game. That's happened already too many times."
