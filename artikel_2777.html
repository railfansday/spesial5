Palo Alto inks $4.5 billion deal for AI startup Applied Compute, shares pop 7% on earnings

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Palo Alto Networks shares jumped more than 7% after the cybersecurity firm beat Wall Street's fiscal first-quarter estimates and inked a deal for artificial intelligence startup Applied Compute.

Here's how the company did versus LSEG estimates:

Revenue jumped 39% from $2.47 billion in the year-ago quarter.

Fears of AI-powered cyberattacks, heightened by incidents like the Hugging Face breach, are driving demand for cybersecurity vendors like Palo Alto Networks. Amid this backdrop, the company's shares have jumped 85% this year.

"As AI reshapes the threat landscape, we must continually evolve to protect our customers and enable them to adopt AI securely," CEO Nikesh Arora said in a release.

Palo Alto said annual recurring revenue for its next-generation security business jumped 66% from a year ago to $9.73 billion. Arora said the NGS ARR was one of the highest beats in the company's history.

The cybersecurity company bumped up guidance for fiscal year 2027 as businesses spend more on security in the age of AI.

Palo Alto expects adjusted earnings per share in the range of $4.26 to $4.30 and revenue in the range of $14.52 billion to $14.57 billion. The forecasts topped the earnings of $4.18 per share and $14.16 billion in revenue expected by analysts.

Net income totaled $482 million, or 57 cents per share, up from $334 million, or 47 cents per share, a year ago.

Palo Alto Networks has been steadily acquiring companies to capitalize on demand for cyber tools in the age of AI.

Arora said the company's $4.5 billion all-stock deal for Applied Compute will help businesses secure open-weight models as adoption grows. The startup helps businesses run and customize their own AI models and marks a major investment for Palo Alto beyond cybersecurity.

"If it works, I think this will dwarf the ability that we have in cybersecurity," Arora said on an earnings call. "This could be bigger than what we do."

Outages from Microsoft, banks and air traffic control have heightened concerns around cyberattacks over the last week. U.S. officials have so far ruled out any malicious activity, but experts say the rapid acceleration in patching is straining dated systems.

As businesses discover more vulnerabilities, Arora said the company expects tens of thousands of virtual patches by the end of the fiscal year. "Whatever they do today is going to take three to six months, maybe nine months, depending on how complex and how tired their environment is," Arora said.
