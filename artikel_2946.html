Steve Kerr, coaching staff tasked with burden of lifting impaired Warriors

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Steve Kerr, coaching staff tasked with burden of lifting impaired Warriors originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

SAN FRANCISCO – After sinking from inspirational to abominable following one cataclysmic instant, the Warriors returned home this week hoping to find their way back to mediocrity.

That, after all, was the reasonable expectation in the absence of Stephen Curry, whose oblique strain sustained 11 days ago left the Warriors, then 8-3, without their physical nerve center and emotional support sniper.

If Curry's teammates could win 50 percent of their games without him, that would defy history; the Warriors have lost 63 percent of their games when he is sidelined. They were 7-2 this season with him before the injury – splitting two games on his mandated rest nights – and they are 0-4 without him.

These were not ordinary losses but public spankings.

Until Curry returns, and there is no timeline, the burden of being competitive will get heavier on the heads of coach Steve Kerr and his staff, along with the primary players on the roster.

Can the Warriors get greater impact from veterans like Draymond Green, De'Anthony Melton and reserves Al Horford and Georges Niang? Can youngsters Yaxel Lendeborg and Brandin Podziemski, who rank behind only Curry in offensive production, recover from a forgettable road trip?

"The game is obviously harder for everybody without Steph, but especially the guys who have to take on the ballhandling and scoring role," Kerr said Saturday after practice. "I don't love that conversation, particularly for those two guys. That with Steph out, they have to do this, they have to do that. We have to do this, or we have to do that.

"If our execution isn't good, we can't expect them to make something out of nothing. If we can execute better . . . and that's what today's practice was about, and that's what the last couple of days have been about for our staff. Understanding why we're not executing better and giving our guys something to hang their hats on offensively and that's our job. That's what we've been working on."

Can't it be difficult, particularly for young players with expectations, to avoid the pitfall of believing they must fill some of the void created by a missing superstar?

"It's challenging," Podziemski conceded. "It's human nature. It's ego. It's pride. You want to showcase what you can do. That's for everybody."

Podziemski struggled to find his footing, and Lendeborg spent the entire road trip out of sorts, as if the inner "dawg" that was so visible through the first 11 games had gone astray. Aside from a 20-point second half in a 21-point loss to the sorry New Orleans Pelicans, he rarely was a presence. He clearly was affected by his inability to excel in an elevated role.

"That's why I'm (Yaxel's) coach, and that's why I talked to him today," Kerr said. "He's a rookie and he's three weeks (four weeks) into this thing. He's learning and he's growing. And it's my job to help him."

Lendeborg's intricate path to the NBA required considerable mental fortitude, which suggests he'll probably restart his engine and get back to hooping, maybe as soon as Sunday against the impressive Boston Celtics.

But navigating such circumstances while chasing potential can be tricky.

"When your best player is out, I think there's a natural instinct to look inward and say what do I need to do more of, for everybody one through 15," Podziemski said. "But when you think about it that way, it harms the team more than helps because you're putting yourself before the team. If you just think about what the team needs, no matter if Steph is in or out, whatever you're good at just bring that to the table."

Losing five consecutive games following an eight-game win streak has the entire operation trying to fix what's broken. There's no suitable replacement for what Curry brings, so it's about limiting mistakes – and there have been plenty on offense and defense – and the Warriors reminding opponents they came to play.

Not for a few possessions or even a full quarter. For all 48 minutes.

"There's no magic pill to it," Kerr said. "It's everything. It's a comprehensive game. If our defense improves, our offense will improve. But it has to start on the defensive end. We can't sit here and say we need to replace Steph's scoring, or we need to throw the ball to so-and-so. It's defense, defense, defense. Grit, competitiveness, fighting.

"Our offense will come from that. We will make some tweaks, no question, but it is it's not about the tweaks. It's about the competitive fire."

That's the surest way for the Warriors to be competitive with Curry, and it's the only way they can compete without him. Sure, they opened the season generally projected to hover around .500. Mediocrity until Jimmy Butler and Moses Moody return when the calendar flips to 2027.

Golden State's eight-game win streak raised the bar, as the Warriors looked like a playoff-caliber team. The ensuing five-game losing streak lowered the bar, as they were like one of the worst teams in the league.

As much as Curry means to this team, it has enough to climb out of the mudhole that formed with five consecutive losses an average of 23.2 points. And, yes, the rise must start with defense.
