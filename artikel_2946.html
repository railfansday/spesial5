3 Reasons I Might Buy More Nvidia in December

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The stock is down nearly 30% over the past month, even as its fundamentals have only improved.

By Rick Munarriz – Nov 27, 2026 at 7:38PM EST

I was unfashionably late to Nvidia (NVDA +0.80%) as an investor. These days, I'm unfortunately underwater. I've admired the business for decades, but it's only been eight months since I initiated a stake in the AI bellwether.

With a cost basis in the high $170s, I was doing pretty well through the first few months. The past month has been understandably rough. However, as I size up any portfolio moves I may want to make before heading into 2027, adding to my Nvidia position is on my short list. Let's go over some of the reasons I would be comfortable with buying more Nvidia in December.

I may as well start by addressing the situation with CEO Jensen Huang. Nvidia stock was already under selling pressure before reports surfaced on not just one -- but two -- extramarital affairs. Failing the moral compass test comes with a reputational hit, but it's not necessarily a career killer. Presidents, coaches, and celebrities of all genders who get caught cheating don't automatically lose their high-profile positions.

Both of Huang's adulterous accusations were problematic. Sleeping with an alleged Chinese spy posed a potential national security risk. Having a fling with a direct subordinate is reportedly against Nvidia's own code of ethics. You can't blame Nvidia directors for not simply sweeping it under the boardroom rug.

Letting Huang go wasn't a surprise. Even founder CEOs aren't above a company's code of ethics. Huang hoping for a second chance and the board initially sticking to its druthers were the next obvious chess moves. Things get thorny here because of the political intervention. The White House boasting about pressuring Nvidia's board to take him back isn't going to pass a lot of sniff tests for overreach, but take a step back.

Is the bigger risk here that Nvidia's board is now exposed as toothless, or what a hungry Huang could have done if he started a rival company? Nvidia directors will be fine in the aftermath. This isn't the kind of card a dismissed CEO can play twice, especially now with more eyes on him in social circles. The biggest positive of this very negative situation is that Nvidia and its moat are back at full strength.

A blowout report following a series of downticks is often the elixir that can turn a laggard into a leader. It didn't happen this time. Nvidia exceeded expectations. It boosted guidance for the next fiscal year. It wasn't enough to snap the bearish momentum.

Nvidia and its interim CEO signaled that its prospects were getting brighter. The market still reached for the dimmer.

I can very well be wrong, but looking back, this could be one of those obvious buying opportunities. A market that punishes improving fundamentals for a business that investors were already rotating out of doesn't typically stay down for long.

Nvidia's revenue more than doubled in the quarter it reported 10 days ago. It's now modeling a 91% jump for the current quarter. It boosted its revenue growth outlook for the new fiscal year starting in February from 70% to 75%.

You would expect to pay a healthy premium for this kind of growth. Instead, you can grab a piece of Nvidia for just 16 times trailing earnings and less than 9 times next year's profit target.

Let's reposition the cameras for a different shot of the same picture. Nvidia stock is down over the past week, even after Monday's historic but short-lived bounce. The shares have plummeted nearly 30% over the past month.

In the past month, we've seen Nvidia's fiscal 2028 per-share profit target rise from $15.91 to $16.61. It's now expected to grow revenue and earnings by more than 70% next year. Last week it delivered its biggest earnings beat in more than a year.

A lot can go wrong. The fast but now vulnerable wheels of the AI trade can get wobbly. Nvidia's record margins can contract. The floor might seem a lot lower than the ceiling may seem higher, but can you honestly feel that way given today's valuation? I believe Nvidia will beat the market from here, making it a rising name on my list of potential stocks to buy in December.
