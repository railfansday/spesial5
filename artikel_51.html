Microsoft scrambles to fix global outage affecting Teams and Outlook

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Tech firm says there has been ‘encouraging’ progress in recovery in Europe and there is no indication problem was caused by cyber-attack

Microsoft outage hits UK hospitals, GPs, schools and police

Tell us: have you been affected by the outage?

Microsoft is scrambling to fix an outage of some of its systems that has caused huge disruption to users, organisations and businesses across the globe.

In its latest update, the tech company said it was making progress in its attempts to restore services after a global outage affected millions of users from Thursday evening and into Friday.

The issue appears patchy, with some but not all users reporting problems with services including Microsoft Teams and Outlook email.

In an update shortly after midday GMT, Microsoft said: “We’re seeing encouraging recovery progress in Europe, with service availability and email delivery performance improving across a growing portion of affected Microsoft 365 infrastructure.

“Japan remains comparatively more stable, while North America is also improving; however, some users may still experience intermittent issues as recovery actions continue.”

More than 430 million people use Microsoft 365 apps, and more than 90% of Fortune 500 companies use Copilot, Microsoft’s AI chatbot, according to the company.

Microsoft said there were no indications that the outage had been caused by a cyber-attack.

Earlier, the company said it was seeing only “marginal success” from its efforts to bring its systems fully back online. It had said that a portion of its infrastructure was “experiencing elevated load” – which was “likely contributing to the connectivity errors”.

The company determined a number of servers were “unexpectedly unavailable” because of a problem that caused them to fail during automated checks.

It earlier warned that services that had restarted could become unresponsive again once they received traffic.

“Once mitigated, we will provide full details of the root cause, defence-in-depth repair items, and more within a post-incident report,” Microsoft said.

The outage website Down Detector showed an increase in Microsoft 365 problems at about 6pm GMT on Thursday, with nearly 130,000 reports of problems in 74 countries affecting access to Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams as well as enterprise services such as SharePoint and OneDrive. Copilot was also affected.

Microsoft has suggested the outage may have been caused by problems with the settings applied to servers responsible for keeping Microsoft 365 up and running.

Microsoft’s executive vice-president of Copilot, Charles Lamanna, posted on X that the company would share more information when it became available. “We regret that today’s outages caused disruptions for our customers, and we are working to address the issue,” he said.

The Microsoft outage also hit connectors used by ⁠OpenAI’s and Anthropic’s AI ⁠services.

OpenAI said it was investigating a problem with Microsoft connectors in which new connections may be broken and tools were not working as ⁠expected. Anthropic said it had identified an issue with an upstream provider ​affecting its Microsoft 365 ​connector.

The UK government said it was “working closely” with Microsoft to assess any impact on government services, adding that there were “robust contingency plans in place to minimise disruption”.

The outage has affected businesses and organisations across the world that use Microsoft products.

With the NHS email system disrupted, pharmacies were reporting issues communicating with some GP surgeries about prescriptions, leading to “significant” waiting times for medicines.

Several UK police forces urged people not to try to report crimes online and to phone them in instead.

Some schools in the UK notified parents that services such as email and Teams were down, and that reporting attendance or absence issues had to be done via phone. National Rail, which provides information across the UK network, warned that people may not be able to access tickets that have been emailed to them.

The Indian airlines IndiGo and Akasa Air said their email support services were affected, and Taiwanese security firms, life insurers and investment trust companies said fund management services had been disrupted.

In financial markets some portfolio managers have been unable to check emails, making it hard to access on some trading platforms.

In Japan, the shipping company Sagawa Express said it was unable to accept online reservations for some of its services at airports and train stations while Taisho, a leading pharmaceutical company, said it was having to handle orders by phone.

Microsoft last month reported an ​outage of its Azure cloud platform that affected a ​suite of productivity software ‌and a range ​of industries worldwide.

Mark Gregory, an associate professor in the School of Engineering at RMIT University in Melbourne, said the outage appeared to be affecting the network layer in Microsoft’s systems, and was potentially similar to the configuration issue that brought down Optus’s network in 2023.

He said that when some of the servers had configuration issues, it meant customers were forced to share a smaller pool of servers to access the cloud services.

“If you lose part of that pool, then all of the users are trying to access the remaining servers, and that overloads them. And then they go down. And so you end up with a cascading problem. Then it takes time to find the underlying problem, in this case, a misconfiguration in the network layer.”

Gregory said businesses reliant on cloud services such as Microsoft 365 should have backup options.

Associated Press and Reuters contributed to this report
