NFL didn't know Puka Nacua had surgery when agreeing to suspension

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The sudden decision of the NFL to suspend receiver Puka Nacua three games raises plenty of relevant questions. On Friday, a technically irrelevant question was answered.

After Rams coach Sean McVay disclosed to reporters that Nacua had surgery to repair a torn MCL suffered during a Week 9 win over the Commanders, Judy Battista of NFL Network reported this: "The NFL did not know Puka Nacua had had knee surgery on Tuesday when it suspended him yesterday."

Surely, that's accurate; Judy is a solid reporter. But that extra fact doesn't matter to the NFL's ultimate handling of Nacua.

The report creates the impression that, during the negotiations that resulted in the three-game suspension under the Personal Conduct Policy, Nacua and/or his representatives withheld material information. That, at some level, the league had been duped by the lack of knowledge that Nacua had undergone surgery that will sideline him for "a couple months."

First, the suspension (as the league affirmatively explained on Thursday) is separate from the injury. The NFL listed multiple examples of players who served suspensions while injured.

Second, whether Nacua (who had already missed one of the four minimum games he's required to miss while on IR), was already going to serve the entirety of the suspension while on injured reserve with or without surgery.

Third, the surgery and the extended absence actually avoids the possibility that Nacua would have returned to action exactly one game after finishing his suspension, resulting in no net games missed due to the suspension.

The mere existence of the report represents an implicit acknowledgement by the league that the negotiated suspension, aimed at allowing Nacua to double-dip when it comes to his stint on injured reserve and his suspension, underscores the broader question of whether players should be allowed to serve a suspension under the Personal Conduct Policy when otherwise unable to play. That's a separate issue for the NFL to revisit, if it chooses, through a potential revision to the terms of the policy.

As explained on Friday, the league could have refused the deal. It could have insisted on pushing the issue through the full process, with Judge Sue L. Robinson conducting a hearing and the outcome potentially being appealed (by Nacua and/or the league) to the Commissioner. But that would have entailed a non-zero-percent chance that Nacua wouldn't have been suspended at all.

For Nacua, the goal was to get the suspension served while injured. The fact that he had surgery before the agreement was reached gave him more of a buffer than the NFL knew about, expanding the window during which the suspension could be fully served before the end of the 2026 season without Nacua missing time due to the suspension. Which would still allow Nacua to have this issue behind him before the 2027 offseason, when he signs a new contract with the Rams, has the franchise tag applied, or becomes a free agent.

Of course, Nacua now needs to tread lightly. The league has made it clear that "any further violation of the Personal Conduct Policy, including failing to satisfy the conditions of his diversion program, will subject him to additional and more substantial discipline, including banishment from the League." To the extent that the league isn't thrilled by the perception/reality that it allowed Nacua to serve his suspension at a time when he otherwise could not play, the league could be ready to pounce on any opportunity to impose a longer suspension that would apply when Nacua is healthy again.
