Rapid Recap: Hawks 113, Bucks 106

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Milwaukee Bucks led by as much as 22 at the start of the second quarter, blew the lead by halftime, and never retook the lead in Sunday's 113-106 loss to the Atlanta Hawks. The Bucks threatened to take the lead late in the fourth quarter, largely thanks to a career-high night from Kasparas Jakucionis, who scored 25 points (21 of which came in the second half). However, the Bucks struggled with turnovers and unnecessary fouling, which slowed their momentum and allowed the Hawks to hold on. Gary Trent Jr. scored 18 points, while Kyle Kuzma and Brayden Burries had 14 and 13, respectively. CJ McCollum and Nickeil Alexander-Walker each accounted for 20 points, while Onyeka Okongwu had 16 and Jalen Johnson added 14.

The Bucks started the game with the upper hand. Kyle Kuzma poured in the first three, splashing a bomb as the shot clock expired, and the Bucks kept the hot shooting going. Myles Turner added one, and Kuzma made two more triples within the first six minutes, prompting a quick timeout from Hawks head coach Quin Snyder. Milwaukee forced frequent Atlanta turnovers, kept them cold from the perimeter, and shot well on offense. Jaime Jaquez Jr. even scored a fading corner three to push the Bucks' lead to 13 points, and after Kel'el Ware swatted a Corey Kispert layup attempt off the glass, Gary Trent Jr. fired a transition bomb to give the Bucks a first-quarter lead of 16! Trent's fast-break layup with just over 36 seconds left gave Milwaukee a 37-18 lead by the end of the first quarter—their best quarter of the season by a country mile.

Milwaukee pressed its advantage to open the second quarter, with Trent and Jaquez connecting on early triples, but Atlanta slowly began to break out of its shooting slump. The Hawks embarked on an 11-3 run, led by an Alexander-Walker layup that trimmed Milwaukee's lead to just 13. RayJ Dennis found Onyeka Okongwu open in the paint with a slicing pass, and Okongwu dunked on Myles Turner to quiet the raucous Fiserv Forum. Jalen Johnson powered in a fast-break dunk, cutting the Bucks' lead to single digits, and the home team suddenly found itself on the back foot. Milwaukee's hot first-quarter shooting had failed them; they shot just 3/15 from three in the second quarter as the Hawks upped their defensive intensity. The Bucks' intensity, by contrast, seemed to wane as their lead shrank, giving up several offensive rebounds and turning the ball over on silly errors; Milwaukee totalled eight second-quarter turnovers. A pair of McCollum free throws tied the game with a little over a minute remaining, and a fadeaway from Alexander-Walker after lazy rebounding efforts gave them the lead; up 55-53 at half.

Atlanta extended its lead after the half. Dyson Daniels connected on a pull-up jumper, Alexander-Walker scored from the midrange, and NAW cashed a three, pushing Atlanta's edge to eight with nine minutes remaining in the third. The Bucks looked like they'd been punched in the mouth as Taylor Jenkins called the first timeout. However, the Hawks struggled to pull away; despite leading by ten points midway through the third, Milwaukee's defense tightened to cool Atlanta's hot shooting and continued to cut the lead. Milwaukee's shooting improved, especially from Jakucionis, who poured in 10 points in the third quarter. Daniels got a floater to go at the end of the third quarter, leaving Atlanta up 87-80 entering the final frame.

With Milwaukee's second win of the season on the line, they didn't let up. The Bucks stayed competitive, opening the final frame with a four-point play by Trent (yes, Gary Trent Jr.). The Bucks feasted on the Hawks' lack of ball security, cutting the deficit to as little as one, and they sought contact on both ends of the court, a welcome sign for a young, developing team. Jakucionis and Turner each had huge and-ones, the latter trimming Atlanta's lead to just two points with 6:32 left. However, Milwaukee got into foul trouble late, and Burries and Jakucionis missed crucial jumpers that kept the game close. Milwaukee, especially the frontcourt, was too handsy on defense and kept sending sharpshooters like McCollum and Johnson to the line. Jakucionis kept the Bucks in the game, though, spearheading fast-break opportunities and hitting some big jumpers down the line. His three-pointer with 1:57 remaining trimmed Atlanta's lead to three, up 109-106, and they remained in the game when Okongwu fumbled an easy fast-break layup. Jakucionis saw his heat-check three-pointer go in and out, and McCollum went down the other end and iced a step-back midrange jumper to give the Hawks a two-possession lead with just 17 seconds left, which they wouldn't relinquish.

The Hawks had 15 fourth-quarter free throws to the Bucks' nine. The Bucks, and especially Myles Turner, were undisciplined down the stretch when it came to foul discipline. Those needless fouls derailed Milwaukee's comeback bid and gave an otherwise struggling Hawks team a free source of points.

It's time to give Kasparas Jakucionis a starting spot. The second-year guard fired from all cylinders in the second half, and his career-high 25 points came with great efficiency (9/16 from the field and 4/10 from beyond the arc). The Bucks' starting guards also struggled on Sunday; Tyler Herro had just five points on 13 shots, and Burries was pretty mediocre. Jakucionis could be an enticing option for Jenkins to turn to in response to the backcourt's inconsistency.
