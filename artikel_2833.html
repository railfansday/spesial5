Netflix’s ‘The Altruists’ sparks FTX portrayal debate among crypto bigwigs from CZ to Star Xu

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Netflix's "The Altruists," released Nov. 19, has sparked backlash from prominent cryptocurrency industry figures, including Binance co-founder Changpeng Zhao, over its portrayal of the collapse of FTX.

Critics have questioned the accuracy of the series' portrayal of the FTX collapse, particularly how it assigns responsibility of the scandal. Several crypto industry figures argue that it softens the culpability of Sam Bankman-Fried and Caroline Ellison while casting Zhao — widely known as CZ — as an outsized villain.

Zhao criticized the series in a post on X, calling it "insane" and questioning whether the show was intended to influence public perception. He declined to comment to CNBC beyond his social-media posts.

Zhao pleaded guilty in 2023 to failing to maintain an effective anti-money laundering program at Binance, in violation of the U.S. Bank Secrecy Act, in a case separate from the FTX fraud. He was sentenced to four months in prison in 2024 and pardoned by President Donald Trump in October 2025.

FTX filed for bankruptcy in November 2022 after a liquidity crisis exposed deep financial problems at the exchange. Investigations later found that billions of dollars in customer funds had been diverted to Alameda Research.

Shortly before FTX's collapse, Zhao announced that Binance would liquidate its remaining holdings of FTT, FTX's native token, intensifying pressure on the exchange.

Bankman-Fried was sentenced to 25 years in prison in March 2024 after being convicted of fraud and conspiracy charges. Ellison pleaded guilty to fraud and conspiracy, and later cooperated with prosecutors, according to the Justice Department.

Crypto commentator Kaleo wrote on X that the series portrayed Bankman-Fried and Ellison as having been pressured and groomed by Zhao into committing their alleged crimes, while casting Zhao as the figure who orchestrated the scandal.

"Whoever wrote this script is shameless," Kaleo added. He identifies himself as a co-founder of NFT project LedgArt.

Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin said in a reply on X that the show exaggerated Zhao's accent to the point of raising questions about whether the portrayal was racist.

Meanwhile, Tigran Gambaryan, a former Binance executive and U.S. federal agent, commented on LinkedIn that the series downplayed Bankman-Fried's responsibility while portraying Zhao as a "cartoonish villain," undermining the showrunners' credibility.

Binance co-founder Yi He criticized the series for portraying its characters as "one-dimensional" and "stereotyped," while suggesting that it shifted blame away from those responsible for FTX's collapse.

She compared the show unfavorably with Chinese short-form drama platform Hongguo, saying Netflix "might as well be canceled."

But the reactions were not uniformly supportive of Zhao.

In a post on X, OKX founder Star Xu said that rather than dismissing criticism as coordinated attacks by competitors, Zhao should respond with facts and evidence.

Xu was responding to a post questioning whether accounts defending Zhao were part of a bot network.

The eight-episode drama stars Anthony Boyle as FTX founder Sam Bankman-Fried, Julia Garner as former Alameda Research CEO Caroline Ellison and Terry Chen as Zhao.

Netflix did not respond to a request for comment.

In an interview published by Netflix before the series' release, Graham Moore, the show's creator and co-showrunner, said he encouraged the actors to develop fully three-dimensional characters rather than simply imitate the real-life figures they portrayed.

Speaking to Business Insider, Moore said, "I can never prove what was going on in the hearts or minds of Sam's friends when they made those decisions."

Moore said he drew on extensive research into the real-life figures' backgrounds and public statements to imagine what may have driven the fractures between them.

"The story we're telling is not a financial story, and it's not a crypto story. It's a deeply personal story," he added.

While criticism of "The Altruists" has so far unfolded largely on social media, Netflix has previously faced defamation lawsuits over dramatizations of real-life events, including "Inventing Anna" and "Baby Reindeer."

Rachel DeLoache Williams, a former Vanity Fair photo editor, sued Netflix in 2022 over her portrayal in "Inventing Anna," a series about convicted fraudster Anna Sorokin.

Williams alleged that the show used her real name and personal details while falsely depicting her as greedy, dishonest and disloyal, including portraying her as exploiting her friendship with Sorokin for financial gain.

The lawsuit was dismissed in February 2026 after the parties reached a settlement. Netflix and Williams' attorney reportedly confirmed the settlement in separate statements, saying, "Williams and Netflix have resolved the lawsuit."

In 2024, Fiona Harvey, who said she inspired the character Martha in "Baby Reindeer," sued Netflix for at least $170 million. Harvey alleged that the series falsely portrayed her as a convicted stalker who had served time in prison and sexually assaulted the show's creator, Richard Gadd.

The case is reportedly scheduled to go to trial in May 2027.

"We intend to defend this matter vigorously and to stand by Richard Gadd's right to tell his story," Netflix said in a statement reported by the Associated Press. She also challenged the show's claim to be based on a true story and alleged that viewers were able to identify her as the character depicted.
