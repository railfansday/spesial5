Cris Collinsworth wants free chicken nuggets for NBC’s new ‘Here’s a Guy’ segment sponsored by Burger King

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Although today's players might not know that NBC's Cris Collinsworth was a wide receiver and not a quarterback before becoming the voice of Sunday Night Football, he'll forever be remembered as a legendary broadcaster.

The three-time Pro Bowler has been NBC's lead NFL analyst since 2009, and in that time he's developed "here's a guy" as his signature catchphrase. The phrase is so iconic that in September, content creator "Bobby Peacock" published a recreation of the iconic 2010s dance hit "Levels" by Avicii in which he used the broadcaster saying, "here's a guy," to make every instrument for the song.

Now, Collinsworth and NBC are leaning into his catchphrase, debuting a new "Here's A Guy" sponsored segment on Sunday's SNF broadcast of the Pittsburgh Steelers-Cincinnati Bengals game.

"Proof positive that our sales team will sell anything," Collinsworth said in the third quarter. "Here's a guy, brought to you by Burger King and chicken nuggets and everything.

"Here was Amarius Mims back in high school. That's an unfair fight," Collinsworth continued over footage of the Bengals offensive tackle in high school. "Now he's in there against T.J. Watt, and that is more than a fair fight. And it has been for the 2nd straight game now, a win for Amarius Mims, and he has just been so impressive."

Collinsworth's partner in the booth, Mike Tirico, was amused by the segment, noting how fans often say "here's a guy!" to Collinsworth when they see him in public.

"And now we've sold it as part of the show!" Tirico said, chuckling.

"Brilliant. Did you get a Whopper?" Collinsworth asked. "I didn't get a Whopper. I'll take anything. I work cheap. I'll just tell you that right now. You throw out a Whopper, some fries, some of those new chicken nuggets I've been hearing about. You know, they're a little crispier, and they've got whole chicken in there. I'll take it all!"

After Sunday's segment, don't be surprised if ESPN follows suit and his next NBA on ESPN call features Mike Breen's iconic "Bang!" call as its own segment sponsored by McDonald's or Wendy's, highlighting the biggest three-pointers of the season.
