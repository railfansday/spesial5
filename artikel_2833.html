Bears and Lions Go Blow for Blow, but Bears take the Turkey Home!

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Ford Field on Thanksgiving delivered the NFC North heavyweight fight the records promised—two 8-2 teams with first place on the line—and Detroit left the first half with the clearer edge – mostly due to costly mistakes and some bad ball luck. The Lions controlled the early minutes, absorbed Chicago's response, then took advantage of a late Bears mistake to build a nine-point lead heading into the break. And then that beautiful, beautiful second half!

Detroit took the ball first and moved it efficiently. Gibbs found early running room, and St. Brown made a one-handed catch that put the Lions inside the 10. The Bears stiffened near the goal line, forcing Campbell to take the points on Jake Bates' 24-yard field goal. Lions 3-0.

Chicago answered with a promising drive that reached the red zone before Derrick Moore punched the ball out of Colston Loveland's hands. Detroit recovered. One possession later, Loveland got rolled up while blocking and was carted off with a knee injury. He's done for the day. Already without Cole Kmet, the Bears are down to Sam Roush and Stephen Carlson at tight end. Detroit used the field position to keep the pressure on and finished the quarter driving deep into Chicago territory.

Gooch Hot Take: The Loveland sequence was a double gut-punch—fumble killed a drive, and his injury removes one of Caleb's most reliable targets. That's the kind of early self-inflicted damage that usually travels in a rivalry game on the road. Detroit didn't have to be perfect, though Goff was close to it for most of the half. They just needed Chicago to hand them extra opportunities, and the Bears provided them.

Detroit finished the long drive with Gibbs' 1-yard touchdown run. Bates converted. Lions 10-0. Chicago responded with Salvon Ahmed's 46-yard run that flipped the field, but the Lions' defense held them to a Cairo Santos 40-yard field goal. 10-3.

Williams and the offense found another gear on the next possession. A 33-yard catch-and-run by D'Andre Swift set up short-yardage work, and Kyle Monangai punched it in from a yard out. Santos' extra point tied it at 10-10 with just over four minutes left. Detroit got the ball back, moved it some with St. Brown, then punted. Chicago had a chance to take the lead in the two-minute drill, but Williams was sacked deep in his own territory. On the ensuing pla,y he was strip-sacked; the Lions recovered, but an illegal batting penalty on the Bears gave Detroit the choice of the ball at the 10 or the safety. Campbell took the points and the ball. 12-10 Lions.

Detroit then drove again. After overcoming a couple of false starts, Goff hit Jameson Williams on a deep corner route into the red zone. With time winding down, Goff found Sam LaPorta in the end zone for the touchdown. 19-10 Lions at the half.

Gooch Hot Take: The safety was the turning point that didn't need to happen. Chicago had just tied the game and had the ball with a chance to go into the break even or ahead. Instead, a deep sack, a strip, and a penalty turned a competitive half into a nine-point deficit. The LaPorta score was the exclamation point—Detroit took the free points, got the ball back, and finished the drive. That's the difference between a team that manages late situations and one that creates them.

Lions 19, Bears 10 is a bigger hole than the overall play suggests, but the path to get there was real. Detroit controlled the first 20 minutes with better field position, a physical run game from Gibbs, and the benefit of Chicago's early mistakes. The Bears showed they can answer—explosive plays from Ahmed and Swift, a red-zone finish from Monangai—but they did it while playing short-handed at tight end and after spotting the Lions free possessions and a safety. Williams has been clean on interceptions so far, which matters after the four-pick night against Tampa, but the margin for error is thinner without Loveland and after the late-half penalty.

The second half would turn on whether Chicago could sustain drives without their tight end, who was becoming the go-to target, and whether the Bears' front could keep Gibbs and the Detroit run game from grinding out another long possession. Detroit has the better track record of closing these kinds of games at home. Chicago has the Caleb. At the half the Lions have the clearer path, but the team that stops handing the other one extra chances will decide whether this stays a one-score game or turns into a Thanksgiving statement.

Chicago came out of the break needing a response and got one. Kyle Monangai's second 1-yard touchdown of the afternoon cut the lead to 19-17. Detroit answered with Jahmyr Gibbs' second short-yardage score of the day to push it back to 26-17. The Bears kept coming. D'Andre Swift broke a 30-yard run for the score that made it 26-24 with just over three minutes left in the quarter. Two possessions, two touchdowns, and the game was back to a one-score affair after looking like it might get away.

Gooch Hot Take: The third quarter was the moment the Bears stopped playing like a team that had spotted Detroit free points and started playing like a team that still believed the scoreboard was temporary. Swift's 30-yarder was the kind of explosive run that changes the energy in a building that had been tilted Detroit's way since the safety.

Detroit briefly regained control. Jake Bates' 40-yard field goal made it 29-24. Then Gibbs ripped off a 70-yard touchdown run—his third score of the day—though the extra point was missed, leaving it 35-24. That looked like the dagger. It wasn't. Caleb Williams hit Rome Odunze for a 41-yard touchdown, then ran in the two-point conversion himself to pull within three at 35-32. With the game on the line, Williams drove the Bears down the field one last time and found Odunze again, this time from six yards out, with three seconds left. The score made it 39-35. No extra point was needed.

Gooch Hot Take: The 70-yard Gibbs run felt like the play that should have ended it. Detroit had the lead, the building, and the momentum. Chicago answered with two straight touchdown drives, including a two-point conversion that kept them alive and a final drive that finished the job. That's the difference between a good team that can get ahead and a team that can close when the margin is thin and the clock is running. And an aside – the officiating in this game has been atrocious.

This was the kind of Thanksgiving game that reminds you why these two teams sit at the top of the NFC North. Detroit did almost everything right in the first half—controlled the line of scrimmage with Gibbs, capitalized on Chicago's early mistakes, and took the free points on the safety. The Lions left the field at 19-10 with every reason to believe they had seized control of the division race.

The second half exposed the difference between leading and finishing. Chicago was short-handed at tight end after Loveland's injury and still found ways to generate explosive plays through Swift and Monangai on the ground and Odunze through the air. Williams, who had been careful with the ball after the four-interception night against Tampa, stayed clean and delivered when the game required it. The two-point conversion and the final six-yarder to Odunze were the plays that turned a potential loss into a statement win.

Detroit will look back at the missed extra point after the long Gibbs run and the inability to stop the final drive. Chicago will look back at the safety and the early fumble and realize how close this was to going the other way. In a division where both teams entered the day at 8-2, the Bears now hold the head-to-head edge and the clearer path. The Lions remain dangerous, especially at home, but they left three points and a chance at the division on the table.
