Dupray Bloom Review: A HEPA Air Purifier Worth Putting on Display

I’ve tested a lot of air purifiers, and most look like appliances I’d rather tuck out of sight in a corner. The Dupray Bloom Air Purifier is different. It combines a HEPA air purifier with a removable planter, turning something purely functional into a decorative accent. I planted a small fiddle-leaf fig in mine, and I love how it looks in the room. But I also wanted to know whether the Bloom’s unusual design would interfere with its primary job of filtering the air.

Absolutely, and that’s what makes the Bloom so appealing. The top features a removable planting pot with a root tray that helps keep roots above excess water. I chose a small fiddle-leaf fig, and once it was planted, the Bloom looked more like a decorative planter than an air-cleaning appliance.

There is one thing I’d caution buyers about: The planter doesn’t have drainage holes. The root tray provides some separation from standing water, but it isn’t a substitute for proper watering. I’d recommend using an olla, a small porous automatic watering vessel that slowly releases moisture into the soil. It’s a good option for keeping the plant watered without repeatedly pouring water into a pot that can’t drain.

I also appreciated that the planter lifts completely out. That means I can move the plant for maintenance or repotting without having to move the entire purifier. And when it’s time to replace the round HEPA filter, I can remove the planter, turn the unit over, and access the filter from the bottom.

Behind its decorative design, the Bloom is a serious air purifier. It uses an H13 HEPA filter with activated carbon to capture airborne particles, including dust, pollen, and pet dander, while helping reduce odors. Dupray rates it as capable of capturing 99.97 percent of particles as small as 0.1 micron.

One feature I particularly liked was the stretchy fabric prefilter. It slips snugly over the purifier’s base, creating a smooth, modern appearance while catching larger airborne particles before they reach the HEPA filter. It’s washable, too, and Dupray offers replacement prefilters in different colors.

The Bloom also has an AutoDetect mode that monitors air quality and adjusts the fan speed. There are three manual fan settings, along with a sleep mode for quieter operation. Dupray rates its lowest noise level at just 24 decibels, making it suitable for bedrooms and other living spaces where a loud fan would be distracting.

It’s remarkably simple. The removable planter makes the HEPA filter accessible, and the fabric prefilter stretches on and off without clips or complicated fasteners.

Another smart touch is the universal power adapter. It comes with interchangeable plug configurations for the U.S., U.K., and Europe. That’s not something I typically see included with an air purifier.

My only complaint—and it’s minor—is the digital control display. The symbols for Auto, Sleep, and the fan-speed bars looked slightly blurry to me. I could still identify them and operate the purifier, but a sharper display would have been easier to read. Or I might need a new prescription for glasses.

At $249.99, the Bloom isn’t the cheapest way to get HEPA filtration, but its design sets it apart from the usual boxy models. It’s particularly appealing for living rooms, bedrooms, and home offices where an ordinary air purifier might detract from the decor.

Dupray advertises coverage up to 1,517 square feet, but that’s based on just one air change per hour. For more frequent air cleaning, it’s rated to cover 316 square feet at 4.8 air changes per hour. I’d use that smaller figure when choosing where to place it.

The Bloom also offers an optional wood tabletop model for anyone who prefers an accent table instead of a planter.

I love having a living plant incorporated into an appliance I’d otherwise try to hide. The Bloom is one of the few air purifiers I’ve tested that I’d happily put on display.
