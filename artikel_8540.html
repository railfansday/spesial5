Compare Savings Accounts by the Rate You’ll Keep – It’s in the Fine Print

Before you open a high-yield savings account, compare the rate your balance will still earn six months from now, not the one advertised. Read the bank’s fine print for a boost that ends, a balance tier or cap, monthly conditions, fees, and limits on getting your money out.

When one bank’s “up to” rate tops another’s plain rate, the fine print explains the gap. The higher number is the bank’s best case, and your balance may earn it for only a few months, or not at all.

Take two sample accounts, each holding $10,000 for a year, with the interest left in. Each rate is an APY, the annual percentage yield, which includes the interest your interest earns. One pays 4.35% for six months and 4.00% after, and earns about $417 in the first year. The other pays a plain 4.40% and earns $440.

In a rate table, 4.35% and 4.40% look like a tie. After the boost, the plain account’s 4.40% earns about $40 a year more on $10,000 than the other’s 4.00%, and more on a bigger balance.

Federal Truth in Savings rules require a bank’s online ad that quotes an APY to state any minimum balance needed to earn it. Read these five terms on the rate page and in the account’s disclosures before you compare high-yield savings accounts.

Last, search for the bank on the FDIC’s BankFind, or for a credit union on the NCUA’s Credit Union Locator, to confirm your deposits are federally insured. Money in an app that isn’t a bank is insured only through its partner bank, so search for that bank instead.
