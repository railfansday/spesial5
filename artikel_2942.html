e.l.f. Beauty vs. On: Which Stock Is the Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

One of these brands is growing sales at roughly 40% while the other is trading at a discount to the market.

By John Ballard – Updated Nov 18, 2026 at 9:04PM EST

10 stocks we like better than e.l.f. Beauty ›

Investors often look for disruptive growth stories when evaluating the consumer space. Today we compare e.l.f. Beauty (ELF +2.30%) and On (ONON +1.82%) to determine which stock offers a more compelling opportunity for long-term growth.

e.l.f. Beauty has built a loyal following by offering vegan and cruelty-free cosmetics at accessible prices. Meanwhile, On is a Swiss athletic brand gaining significant market share from legacy footwear giants. Both companies are successfully leveraging digital engagement and strategic retail partnerships to outpace their respective industries.

e.l.f. Beauty operates as a multi-brand beauty company, offering cosmetics and skincare through brands like e.l.f. SKIN and Naturium. It primarily serves value-conscious consumers through major retail stores including Target, Walmart, and Amazon.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached $1.9 billion, representing growth of approximately 40% compared to the prior period. Net income was roughly $87 million over the trailing four quarters.

As of its Sept. 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio is 2.6x, which measures a company's ability to cover short-term liabilities with short-term assets. The debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.8x, a metric that compares total debt to shareholder equity to assess financial leverage.

Trailing-12-month free cash flow was $276 million. Note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 41% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation, since SBC is a non-cash expense added back to cash flow.

On is a Swiss premium sportswear brand that designs and sells footwear, apparel, and accessories globally. The company focuses on technical innovation and a premium brand image to attract runners and lifestyle consumers across the Americas, Europe, and Asia. It utilizes a mix of direct-to-consumer sales and selective wholesale partnerships to maintain high brand equity and control its pricing strategy.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached $4 billion, an increase of approximately 11% year over year. Net income for the period was nearly $527 million, reflecting a healthy net profit margin of 13%. The company has demonstrated a strong ability to scale its premium products while maintaining healthy bottom-line results.

As of its Sept. 2026 balance sheet, On carries a debt-to-equity ratio of roughly 0.4x. Its current ratio is approximately 3.1x, indicating a solid buffer of liquid assets to meet its upcoming financial obligations. Trailing free cash flow reached $478 million, providing the company with ample capital to reinvest in its global retail expansion.

e.l.f. Beauty must navigate a highly competitive landscape filled with both massive multinational corporations and nimble independent brands. The company relies heavily on a limited number of major retail partners, meaning the loss of shelf space at Target or Walmart could materially impact its results. Furthermore, its dependence on third-party manufacturers in China exposes the business to potential supply chain disruptions, shifts in international trade policy, or new tariffs. The integration of recent acquisitions also presents a risk if expected synergies fail to materialize.

On Holding faces intense competition from established global footwear players with significantly larger marketing budgets and distribution networks. While the brand is currently in high demand, it must continually innovate to keep pace with evolving fashion trends and athletic performance requirements. Managing inventory levels across diverse global regions is another constant challenge that could impact profitability if demand shifts unexpectedly. Additionally, as a premium brand, its sales remain sensitive to broader economic cycles that might limit discretionary spending on luxury sportswear.

On appears more attractive from a valuation perspective, as it trades at a lower multiple of both its future earnings estimates and its sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

These brands are both performing at a high level in a choppy consumer spending environment. I would buy e.l.f. Beauty, mainly because it is seeing accelerating growth and improving margins. I like On, too, which trades at a lower P/E multiple, but e.l.f. is growing revenue significantly faster through a challenging stretch for consumer brands. I think e.l.f. is building a powerful "house of brands" portfolio that is resonating at home and abroad.

E.l.f. posted a 51% year-over-year increase in net sales last quarter, marking the 31st straight quarter of 20% or better growth. Rhode seems to be getting more popular with each new release, while the company's Naturium brand is one of the fastest-growing in the skincare space.

E.l.f. has become the top cosmetics brand on Amazon, and management says it's also the most searched brand on the platform. People are seeking out the brand, which means the company doesn't have to spend as much on marketing to acquire new customers. This supports profitable expansion.

On is an attractive buy right now, too, as it continues to post double-digit growth while the stock trades at a modest forward P/E of about 18 -- a slight discount to the S&P 500's 19x forward P/E. But I prefer investing in reasonably priced growth stocks of companies that are growing revenue at higher rates. At the rate e.l.f. continues to grow with ample room to expand internationally, I believe the stock has a favorable chance of outperforming in 2027.
