Nvidia Stock Is Still Down 38% From Its Peak Despite Jensen Huang's Return. History Suggests a $10,000 Investment Today Will Be Worth This Much by Year-End 2027.

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Nvidia's accelerating earnings growth and historically cheap valuation could help investors double their money by the end of 2027.

By Adam Spatacco – Nov 27, 2026 at 7:51PM EST

On Nov. 23, Nvidia (NVDA +0.80%) announced that co-founder Jensen Huang had been reinstated as the company's chief executive officer. Although Nvidia stock initially soared following the announcement, shares have subsequently surrendered some of those gains and remain approximately 38% below their all-time high from October.

Interestingly, Nvidia's recent sell-off comes at a time when the company's financial outlook is becoming even more encouraging. Between accelerating revenue and earnings growth, an enormous share repurchase program, and a historically inexpensive valuation, Nvidia looks positioned for an incredible comeback.

Let's explore how much a $10,000 investment in Nvidia stock today could be worth by the end of 2027.

During the third quarter of fiscal 2027 (ended Oct. 25), Nvidia generated $115.8 billion in revenue, representing 103% growth year over year. Meanwhile, adjusted earnings per share (EPS) increased by 111% to $2.68. Moreover, management raised its fiscal 2028 revenue guidance from 70% to 75%.

Assuming Nvidia meets its fourth-quarter revenue outlook of $130 billion, the company should finish fiscal 2027 with roughly $424 billion in sales. This implies revenue approaching $742 billion next year.

While Nvidia continues to benefit from robust demand for its Blackwell chip architecture, the commercialization of its Vera Rubin architecture has become the company's next major growth catalyst. Nvidia's latest computing platform incorporates graphics processing units (GPUs), Vera central processing units (CPUs), and advanced networking technologies.

This combination broadens Nvidia's opportunities to monetize different layers of the artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure value chain. As hyperscalers continue investing in data center expansion, Nvidia's comprehensive product portfolio provides the company with multiple avenues to sustain its growth.

In September, Nvidia's board of directors increased the company's share repurchase authorization by $150 billion. As of the end of October, Nvidia had $227.3 billion remaining under its buyback program. Management intends to execute the buybacks by the end of fiscal 2028.

Interestingly, Nvidia's recent share-price depreciation could make these repurchases more accretive to shareholders. Even if net income remained flat, reducing the outstanding share count should increase Nvidia's EPS considerably.

Over the last five years, Nvidia has traded at an average forward price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple of approximately 32. Following its recent sell-off, Nvidia is trading at roughly 8.9 times estimated earnings over the next 12 months.

Given Nvidia's enormous size, I don't expect the company to reclaim its historical average valuation profile. However, even modest multiple expansion could generate multibagger returns.

By the end of next year, investors will be valuing Nvidia based on its fiscal 2029 earnings potential. Currently, Wall Street expects Nvidia to generate normalized EPS of approximately $21.65 in fiscal 2029. Applying a forward P/E multiple of 15 to this earnings estimate implies a stock price of roughly $325.

Using Nvidia's recent share price of $149, a $325 price target implies 118% upside. This would transform a $10,000 investment into roughly $21,800 in just one year.

Given Nvidia's accelerating earnings potential and historically compressed valuation, the company's recent sell-off could represent a lucrative opportunity to buy the dip for patient investors.
