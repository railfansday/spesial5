Prediction: These 3 Consumer Stocks Will Beat the S&P 500 Over the Next 10 Years

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

I think Ferrari, Hilton, and Amer Sports have the brands, the growth runways, and the discipline to outrun the S&P 500 over the next decade.

By Micah Zimmerman – Nov 26, 2026 at 5:30PM EST

Beating the S&P 500 sounds simple until you remember how good the index already is, since it has compounded at about 9.9% a year on a total return basis going back to 1928. So a stock that beats the index over 10 years needs more than a good quarter, it needs a business that can keep growing over the long term.

When I look for that kind of staying power in the consumer world, I aim for three things: a brand that people pay up for, a long expansion runway, and a management team that protects both. Here are three consumer stocks that I think long-term investors should consider for the decade ahead.

Ferrari (RACE +2.96%) deliberately builds fewer cars than people want, and that scarcity is the heart of its economic moat, which is a durable advantage that keeps rivals from stealing its customers.

CEO Benedetto Vigna said in the third-quarter release that deliveries "reflect our clients' ever-deepening desire to make each Ferrari uniquely their own." Those personal touches add profit to every car without adding volume.

Demand isn't the problem. Vigna said on the call, "We continue to experience a healthy demand across all geographies, with an order book that enters 2028."

The risks are a premium valuation, at roughly 36 times earnings, and the shift to electric models like the Luce, which has to win over buyers who love engines.

Hilton (HLT +0.52%) mostly doesn't own its hotels; it collects fees by managing and licensing its brands to owners through a franchise model, which is an arrangement where a business pays to use another company's brand and systems. That lets it grow without spending heavily on buildings.

CEO Chris Nassetta said in the third-quarter release, "We achieved our highest quarter ever for organic openings." He also said on the call, "Globally, Hilton is still less than 6% of supply providing a long runway for continued growth."

Travel is cyclical, though. CFO Kevin Jacobs said, "RevPAR in China declined 5.4% in the quarter," referring to revenue per available room, which is a key hotel measure of how well rooms are filled and priced. Middle East conflict is weighing on that region, too, and the stock trades near 49 times earnings.

Amer Sports (AS +2.56%) owns Arc'teryx jackets, Salomon shoes, and Wilson tennis gear, and every segment grew by double digits in its latest quarter. CEO James Zheng said, "I am very confident in the future outlook for our company"

The U.S. opportunity is what excites me most. Arc'teryx CEO Stuart Haselden noted that the brand has about as many stores in the U.S. as in Canada, which he said "makes it very apparent the potential that we have to expand in the U.S." Management also said Europe's store traffic is a challenge, and higher freight costs are nibbling at margins.

None of these companies will beat the market every year, but I think long-term investors should consider owning all three and giving their growth stories the full decade they deserve. Great brands tend to compound quietly.

What ties these three together is pricing power, which is the ability to raise prices without losing customers, and I think that matters more over a decade than any single quarter's results.

Ferrari's buyers wait years for their cars, Hilton's owners keep signing up for its brands, and Arc'teryx shoppers happily pay premium prices for gear that lasts.

There will be rough stretches, whether it's a travel slowdown, a weak luxury market, or a cold winter for outdoor sales, but investors who hold through those dips give each business time to prove its case. In the end, patience is the edge most investors give up too easily.
