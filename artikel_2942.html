IonQ Stock Plunged 48% in Six Months. Is It a Bargain Buy?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A sharp share price decline doesn't always equal a bargain.

By Chris Neiger – Nov 18, 2026 at 9:47AM EST

Shares of IonQ (IONQ -2.35%) are down about 48% over the past six months as investors have headed for the exits amid worries the company is spending too much on acquisitions and raising too much capital at the expense of existing shareholder value.

IonQ has always been a speculative bet on an unproven market — quantum computing — but some investors may wonder whether the recent share price pullback makes IonQ stock a bargain right now.

Unfortunately, even with the recent drop, its shares still aren't in the bargain bin. Here's why investors are betting on IonQ, what the company has going for it, and why IonQ stock still isn't a good deal.

The biggest reason for IonQ's recent share price drop was the October announcement that the company would acquire Elisity, a zero-trust cybersecurity company. IonQ said it would make the $1.2 billion acquisition in an all-stock deal, diluting existing shareholders.

IonQ announced just a few days later that it was holding a $2 billion equity offering, cementing investors' concerns about share dilution. By the end of the month, IonQ's stock was down 31%.

This decline came months after IonQ's shares tumbled over the summer as investors fled riskier tech stocks. The AI trade came under pressure during that time as investors questioned the hundreds of billions of dollars tech companies are investing in data centers, and some of the negative sentiment spilled over into quantum computing stocks.

The result was a near-meltdown of IonQ's stock, pushing it down 48% since June.

It's not all bad news for IonQ. The company has a unique edge in the quantum computing space with its trapped-ion technology helping to reduce error rates (a major problem for quantum computers), and its revenue is climbing.

Sales jumped 336% to nearly $174 million in Q3 2026, and management recently raised its full-year guidance to $510 million, at the midpoint. The company also said it has an impressive backlog of $1.2 billion.

IonQ also has $3.7 billion in cash and cash equivalents, so there's no real concern about the company's ability to keep the lights on for the next few years.

There are two simple reasons the stock isn't a bargain: The company remains highly unprofitable, and its shares still look expensive despite the recent drop.

IonQ's operating losses were $433 million in Q3, and its trailing free cash flow was a negative $689 million. It's worth noting that while the company reported a GAAP (generally accepted accounting principles) net profit of $564 million in Q3 2026, management said this was due to an $896 million non-cash accounting gain from warrant revaluations rather than operational profitability.

IonQ CFO Inder Singh said on the latest earnings call that the company is thinking about profitability, but it hasn't publicly estimated when that will occur. "Now that we are moving forward toward potentially being the first quantum company to deliver $1 billion of revenue in the future, it is also time to begin to think about charting out our path to both revenue growth and profitability," Singh said.

What's more, IonQ stock is still expensive. Even after its sell-off, IonQ's shares have a price-to-sales (P/S) ratio of 29.6, which is far higher than the technology sector's average P/S ratio of 6.5.

That's nowhere near bargain territory for a stock, and given that IonQ won't be profitable anytime soon -- not to mention that it's betting on a very speculative quantum computing market -- investors should proceed with caution before buying this one. If you do pick up some shares right now, it's probably a good idea to add a little to your portfolio over time instead of going headfirst into a large position.
