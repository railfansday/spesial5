In Alison Roman's World, Curation is King

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

With only weeks before opening, Alison Roman’s Brooklyn brick-and-mortar is still unfinished. “Today they’re doing up the walls and putting in the floors,” Roman says. In short, it does not yet look like a dreamy European-inspired grocer. There are still a mountain of calls to be made, meetings to be had, speed bumps to overcome.

The chaos doesn’t seem to be getting to the cookbook author, chef, and aspiring empire-builder. “It’s been so fun,” Roman, a Bon Appétit alum, says of the process. Perhaps that shouldn’t be a surprise; this breezy effortlessness is at the bedrock of the Alison Roman universe. You know the kind of nonchalant magic I’m talking about: chilling wine bottles in a bathtub of ice, buckets of take-out fried chicken at dinner parties—I mean, this woman was one of the foremost proponents of brothy beans.

What Roman has built has grown beyond recipes and bean propaganda. The ever-creeping edges of her lifestyle brand now encompass a jarred tomato sauce brand, once cheekily titled A Very Good Sauce, now renamed Roman’s. There’s also the new podcast, Having People Over, where she hosts guests for a meal and a yap, plus the speaking tour she’s calling “Food, Life and Other Strong Opinions.” There are the shoes (sleek mules and chunky clogs) she made in collaboration with New York–based footwear brand Margaux. And now First Bloom, the grocery store–slash–wine bar–slash–café that—as of mid-November—is on track to open in December.

The Brooklyn shop is an extension of the original Catskill’s location of the same name. Roman says this new outpost is “a totally different proposition of what we are offering people.” There’s more budget to work with, there’s more staff, there’s more space, and there’s more food.

Whereas the upstate location’s function is closer to utility—meat and produce from local farms are a major feature—in Brooklyn (where specialty grocers abound) Roman is focusing on something more akin to world-building. But do not call First Bloom a shoppy shop, those whimsically designed boutiques that seem to pop up everywhere selling the same $45 squiggly candlesticks and squeeze-bottle olive oils everyone seems to have. “I think ‘shoppy shop’ is derogatory,” Roman says. “In a world where you can buy anything from anywhere, sometimes it’s nice to be told what to do.”

At Brooklyn’s First Bloom, the shelves will be stocked with products that Roman herself deems “hyper-defensible.” There’s a reason they’re stocked, and a reason for how they’re priced. They’re best in class or a personal favorite of Roman’s. “Perspective and curation is a really special thing to be able to convey,” she says.

The vision for First Bloom is set, but with the opening just a month out, Roman doesn’t have all the details nailed down. “We are going to have a rotisserie chicken,” she says. “I don’t know how much it’s going to cost yet,” she follows up quickly, before I can ask. There will also be a couple salads and a couple sandwiches on offer—plus another secret off-menu sandwich for the neighborhood regulars and the real Roman-heads who know to ask for it. “I’m very if-you-know-you know,’” she says. But Brooklyn’s First Bloom isn’t just for popping into—you’ll be able to lounge with a glass of wine or relax with a snack and a coffee.

Alison Roman’s world is organic, craft-centered, and easy but considered. To step into it is an enticing foil to the AI-fueled tasteslop tilt-a-whirl on which we find ourselves. It’s a vision of a slow, steady, wabi-sabi life that many of us only aspire to. A life where one can even somehow enjoy, say, a monthslong commercial renovation project in one of America’s most competitive real estate markets. That’s the draw of First Bloom. The hard-to-find tinned fish, yes, but also the ease of living in Alison’s world if only for an hour.

And if not, well, it seems like there’ll be a pretty good rotisserie chicken, at least.
