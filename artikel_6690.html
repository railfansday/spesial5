Keke Palmer Wore Rare Bob Mackie—and Other Vintage Gems—at the 2026 CFDA Awards

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

As the host of last night’s 2026 CFDA Awards in New York City, all eyes were on Keke Palmer to deliver us a major fashion moment. The annual awards ceremony, after all, is all about celebrating American style and design. “Fashion is such a big part of how I tell stories, so getting to celebrate the people behind it was exciting,” Palmer tells Vogue today, post-CFDAs. “I love how a look can connect different eras, and bring a memory or a feeling into the present. To be in a room celebrating that creativity, while bringing my own personality and humor to the evening, felt like a beautiful fit.”

Of course, Palmer did not disappoint on that front. The star went archival for the night—as she is wont to do—bringing four vintage gems to the red carpet. “I wanted my looks to be unique, and celebrate some incredible talent from the past,” says Palmer of her fashion direction. “Everything with me is somehow a throwback or referential. That’s part of the kind of artist I am: Fashion becomes another way to tell that pop culture story.”

Working with stylist Molly Dickson, the duo wanted each of the four looks to have its own moment throughout the night. Palmer first hit the red carpet in a one-of-a-kind (and incredibly rare) Bob Mackie piece from the 1970s. The dress has a particularly noteworthy history: The strappy, shimmery-gold gown was originally custom-made for actor and singer Raquel Welch, which she wore to perform “I’m a Woman” alongside Cher on The Cher Show back in 1975. (You’ve all seen the clip.) “Wearing Bob Mackie was one of the first ideas I mentioned to Molly,” says Palmer. “His work holds so much entertainment history, and I loved being able to bring my own energy to a piece with that story. It’s classic, and it felt like the right way to step into the night.”

Turns out, the iconic piece of pop culture was sourced from the professional vintage dealer Timeless Vixen—a celebrity favorite that Sabrina Carpenter and Kylie Jenner also use for their extremely rare red carpet pulls. “When Keke’s styling team reached out looking for something special by Bob Mackie, this piece immediately came to mind,” says owner Lauren Lepire. “I’ve always adored his work, and with his recent passing, it’s especially meaningful to see Keke bring such an iconic design back into the spotlight.”

From there, Palmer then changed into her three other vintage frocks of the night. There was the black skin-revealing Marc Bouwer dress once worn by Toni Braxton; a striped, one-shouldered 1980s Halston number; And a slinky black 1970s Stephen Burrows to end with a bang. “For me, it was a story through the ages, celebrating these designers and the women who brought their clothes to life,” says Palmer. “We also told a black and gold color story, ending with a look that brought the two together. That sense of integration feels very connected to where I am artistically—bringing different references and parts of myself into one expression.”

While playing dress up was undoubtedly a highlight of the night for Palmer, her favorite moments included some unexpected run-ins with fellow stars. “You’re doing a show, but you also get to connect with people whose work you admire and enjoy being in the room together,” says Palmer. “I loved seeing Demi Moore backstage in between moments, seeing Law Roach, and catching up with Prabal Gurung. Those little moments in between everything are some of my favorites. Saw my girl Brandy, too!”

After delivering her statement fashions at the CFDAs, Palmer says she ended the night with one thing: “Bed! I’m a homebody.” Up next? She will be filming season two of The ’Burbs, and working on new music, too. (She just released her last video, “Keke Frankenstein,” this Halloween weekend.) “I’m building the world around my album, and having so much fun with ‘Keke Frankenstein,’” says Palmer. “I love when the music, visuals, fashion, and characters can all speak to one another.”

In other words: Palmer stays booked and busy. “Between motherhood, music, acting, and building Practice by Palmer, there’s always something happening,” says Palmer. “This fall, I’ve been making room for experiences that feed me as a person. The ‘play’ in me still has a way of becoming productive. #Virgo!”
