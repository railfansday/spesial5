AI could open wallets where ERP upgrade pitches can't, says PwC

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Consultancy argues a 'clean core' makes agents easier to deploy – but Kingfisher is taking a different route

Global consultancy PwC says IT departments struggling to secure funding for ERP upgrades should make the expected benefits of AI part of their pitch.

The Oracle and SAP mega-partner told El Reg that a "clean core" – modern enterprise applications with fewer customizations embedded in the underlying system – makes deploying AI agents easier. Selling that prospect, rather than incremental improvements to existing processes, could help IT leaders justify a migration.

Oracle and SAP are both trying to persuade customers to move from established ERP systems to newer platforms. For SAP, that means S/4HANA, preferably in the cloud. For Oracle, it means Fusion Cloud Applications. Moving from ECC or E-Business Suite, respectively, can involve root-and-branch changes to technology and business processes, rather than a straightforward software upgrade.

Thousands of organizations still rely on these aging, often heavily customized systems. IT leaders struggle to justify a costly, time-consuming migration when the reward appears to be doing the same meat-and-veg business processes slightly better. For example, a survey of SAP users last year found that 95 percent said building a positive case for migration required substantial effort or was genuinely challenging.

Speaking to The Register, Jennifer Colapietro, PwC digital core modernization platform leader, said: "You're seeing it a lot more as part of the rationale for the business case for why they should upgrade, answering the question: 'Why now? What should we prioritize?' Certainly, AI and getting the value of AI, a key business case justification for that.

"Folks have budgets for ERP, but now [funding for transformation is] being split because they also have budgets for AI. But there's synergies across them. If you look at moving to a clean digital core ERP, that will help them to realize the AI value faster... be able to turn on AI and be able to start to automate a lot of the processes and implement the agents. That helps to build the business case."

SAP recently introduced Joule 2.0 while Oracle has announced Fusion Claw. With both of these new technologies, the vendors are promising customers they can build AI agents to automate business processes and perform some work that was previously done by humans. Colapietro argued that moving to more modern platforms makes deploying AI agents easier and more effective than adding AI capabilities around legacy systems.

"It is more challenging to apply AI to the older systems. We focus on the importance of a clean digital core as the foundation for getting the value and effectiveness out of AI and scaling AI responsibly. I think that's given," she said.

An ERP replacement isn't the only route to AI, however. European home improvement retailer Kingfisher – which owns the B&Q brand in the UK – eschewed SAP's overtures to upgrade its ECC system, despite encroaching deadlines. Mainstream maintenance for supported ECC 6.0 versions ends in December 2027, with optional extended maintenance through 2030 at a premium of two percentage points on the existing maintenance base.

Kingfisher opted instead for third-party support from Rimini Street, building an AI strategy around its existing ERP system using a combination of technology from Databricks and Google.

AI might help sell an ERP migration to the board. It doesn't make replacing the ERP system a prerequisite. ®
