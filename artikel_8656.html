Before you take out a personal loan for a medical bill, ask the provider for an itemized bill, financial assistance, and an interest-free payment plan. Borrow only what’s left, since an unpaid medical bill stays off your credit reports until it’s a year past due, while a loan payment 30 days late can go on them.

Say financial assistance cuts a $9,000 emergency room bill to $5,500 and the hospital spreads it over 12 interest-free months. That’s about $458 a month, with no loan needed.

Federal tax law requires every nonprofit hospital to have a written financial assistance policy that sets who gets free or discounted care. Under the IRS’s billing and collection rules, you have at least 240 days from the hospital’s first bill after your discharge to apply, even once a collector has the account. If you qualify, the hospital must refund what you overpaid and do everything it reasonably can to reverse the collection, such as clearing it from your credit report.

Medical debt also gets more time before it can hurt your credit. The three credit bureaus never report a medical collection under $500, keep a larger one off until it’s a year past due, and delete it once it’s paid, according to the National Consumer Law Center. A loan payment 30 days late, by contrast, can go on your reports and stay there seven years. That’s why the loan comes last.
