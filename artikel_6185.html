While rivals battle the majors for the same travelers, Breeze has turned profitable by creating its own demand. Now it wants a cut of what those travelers spend on the ground.

Smaller airlines get into trouble when they try to compete with the majors, Breeze Airways founder and CEO David Neeleman said Thursday at the Skift Aviation Forum.

Breeze has tried to stay out of their way, and Neeleman said the airline has posted four consecutive profitable quarters, with a fifth expected. The airline is privately held and does not publicly report full financial results.

Spirit, which shut down in May, shows the risk. As the carrier moved to bigger planes, it was pushed into the same markets as the Big Four. The majors then moved to bigger planes too, adding seats that cost them “less than half” what Spirit paid per seat, he said.

Breeze Airways founder and CEO David Neeleman used the Skift Aviation Forum to argue that smaller U.S. carriers lose when they fly head-to-head with the Big Four, and that Breeze has avoided that fight by flying routes the majors skip. He said Breeze has posted four consecutive profitable quarters with a fifth expected, though the airline is privately held and does not publicly report full financial results. Neeleman pointed to Spirit, which shut down in May, as the cautionary case: as Spirit moved to bigger planes it ended up in the same markets as the majors, who added seats at a cost he said was "less than half" what Spirit paid per seat. Breeze instead manages to trip costs rather than CASM, sells a heavily premium cabin — about 41% of seats, soon 45% — and was the sole operator on nearly 90% of its routes last year, according to Skift Research. Neeleman said AI has let Breeze slow hiring rather than cut staff, with three or four people in yield management overseeing 300 daily flights. He also wants Breeze to earn a cut of the economic activity its routes create in small markets, citing second-home buying in places like Vero Beach, Florida, and describing a Priceline-style commission model with first steps expected next year.
