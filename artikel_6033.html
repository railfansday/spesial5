TSA Demanded United Airlines Suspend A Pilot Over Terror Support, Union Accused Of Resisting, Report Says

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

TSA reportedly told United Airlines to suspend a pilot accused of supporting a terrorist group, after the pilots union resisted action according to a federal government source cited by TV news reporter Mike Wagenheim. The new report says the intervention happened last week. The pilot hasn’t been named. Neither has the terror group he allegedly supported. Mike Wagenheim’s November 24 update on the reported TSA intervention Wagenheim’s earlier report cited two sources saying United knew of up to six additional crew members who’d expressed support for terrorist groups. United’s pilots union spokesman declined to discuss individual cases. He said the Air Line Pilots Association has a professional standards committee and legal team that assess accusations against its ethics code and United’s workplace guidelines. The Pilot Israel Barred Was Already On Leave In October, Israel prohibited flights with Sina Amjadian serving as crew from arriving in the country. Israeli officials confirmed the restriction after receiving allegations that he’d expressed support for Iran’s Revolutionary Guards and Hezbollah. But United said he wasn’t currently flying, having been on leave since 2024. He flew Boeing 737s and wasn’t qualified on long haul aircraft. Apparently there was material showing Iranian leadership imagery and mourning civilians killed in strikes. There are claims of Hezbollah support and that there was a video of him kissing the regime’s emblem. United Pilot Celebrated October 7 Rapes And Murders As ‘Bravery’ I first wrote about Ibrahim Mossallam in November 2023, after his Facebook description of the October 7 attack as “resistance by a brave people.” United removed him from service with pay but he was reported back flying. On October 6, 2026, after the flydubai pilot sought to use his plane as a missile to attack Israel, United told me: We have withheld this pilot from service pending an ongoing review. There were also reports that he returned after just a day, and that two colleagues had given up first class seats to monitor him in the cockpit. United subsequently denied both accounts. A reported claimed the union blocked his firing. TSA Can Order A Pilot Out Of The Cockpit It was striking that the reported order came from TSA. It has authority that extends well beyond passenger checkpoints. Under federal law, a TSA determination that a pilot poses, or is suspected of posing, a terrorism or passenger safety threat requires the Federal Aviation Administration to take action against the pilot’s certificate. TSA can request that the action take effect immediately. It can also issue mandatory security directives to airlines. However, it isn’t clear from the report what legal mechanism is claimed to have been used and doesn’t report an FAA certificate action. It says the pilot’s “TSA credential” was revoked and describes that as Known Crewmember access. TSA can require an airline to remove someone’s access, but losing that privilege doesn’t mean losing a pilot’s license. Flydubai Shows Why The Concern Is Real I don’t need to know a pilot’s politics. But I wouldn’t want someone who celebrates the killing of civilians at the controls of my flight. That calls their judgment into question even without evidence they’ve planned an attack themselves. The September 30 flydubai cockpit attack makes the concern concrete. The co-pilot of that flight planned a suicide attack on the Tel Aviv airport, attacking the captain with an axe to take over the aircraft. He had satisfied the licensing, good conduct and security requirements for the job, and those checks had failed to uncover his extremist intentions.
