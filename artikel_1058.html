Complacent Arsenal hang on in Prague as Gyokeres revival continues

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

With a stylish assist and a gorgeous goal, Viktor Gyokeres produced the sort of match-winning performance that suggests the Swedish striker is making significant progress at last. It has not always been straightforward for him at Arsenal but this was unquestionably one of his better showings, and it continued an encouraging upturn in his form.

Gyokeres has been the second-choice striker for most of this season, with Mikel Arteta, the Arsenal manager, usually preferring the more subtle Kai Havertz in attack. There are signs, though, that Gyokeres is growing in belief. Following this 2-1 victory over Slavia Prague, which was much more narrow and nervy than it should have been, he has now scored two goals and registered three assists in his last five appearances.

Even more promising than those statistics was his all-round play in Prague. In the first half especially, Gyokeres held onto long passes, combined with his team-mates and tested the opposition with his runs in behind. For Arsenal’s first goal, he found Noni Madueke with a brilliant pass. For their second, he curled a fabulous strike into the top corner.

Those two moments in the first half eventually ensured another European victory for Arteta’s side, but the manager was far from impressed with the second-half showing of his players. So smooth at first, Arsenal then became remarkably open as the game wore on. Slavia deserve credit for that, but there is no doubt that Arteta’s side became complacent. They were perhaps a little fortunate not to be punished for it.

Wiktor Nowak’s 90th-minute goal was hard-earned by the Czech side, who had found a way to rattle the Arsenal defence with long balls and direct runs. It was not enough, though, to steal the limelight from Madueke, who is developing nicely in the absence of the injured Bukayo Saka, and from Gyokeres.

It was a remarkable coincidence that this game fell on November 4, the exact date as the same fixture last season. On that occasion Arsenal were comfortable 3-0 winners, with Mikel Merino scoring twice, and there were plenty of reasons for them to feel confident upon their return here.

Among those reasons was Slavia’s poor form in Europe’s premier competition. The Czech side came into the match on a run of 22 games without victory in the Champions League, and in the knowledge that one more game without a win would equal the European Cup record.

To make matters worse for the home side, they recently banished their star striker from the club due to a series of disciplinary failings. Tomas Chory, Slavia’s top scorer for the past two seasons, has been chucked out of the team after showing his middle finger to the referee in a recent game. This sort of sanction would have earned Arteta’s nod of approval, no doubt, but it probably did not increase Slavia’s chances of hurting the Premier League champions.

Overall, then, this was always unlikely to be the most challenging contest of Arsenal’s European campaign. Arteta being Arteta, though, he still picked many of his favourites. There was no rest for Declan Rice, Ezri Konsa or Gabriel Magalhaes, who all started in the Prague chill.

As was to be expected, Arsenal were dominant from the start. Each member of the front three looked dangerous, with Madueke, Gyokeres and Eberechi Eze all winning their individual battles with their opponents.

The opening goal arrived after just nine minutes, when Gyokeres hooked an excellent pass into Madueke’s path. The Arsenal winger had already established his dominance over Oskar Kubiak, his opposite number, and he reached fifth gear before the Polish defender had even reached second. Madueke then slid his finish beneath the body of Nazar Domchak, Slavia’s teenage goalkeeper.
