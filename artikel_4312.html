They say that lightning never strikes the same place twice, let alone thrice, but McLaren is channeling Zeus with its newest W1 flagship. As a descendant of the hallowed McLaren F1 and a direct successor to the brand's last 903-hp P1 halo car, the W1 assumes a throne previously occupied by hypercar royalty. And if you couldn’t guess by its wind-sculpted, carbon-fiber-laden exterior, the W1 has the spec sheet to back up its noble position.

Wielding a 9200-rpm twin-turbo V-8 paired with an axial-flux electric motor, the W1 delivers 1258 horsepower to the pavement via its rear wheels, making it the most powerful McLaren ever. Those ponies don’t have to contend with much mass, as a carbon-fiber tub and body help keep the W1's dry weight to a feathery 3084 pounds. Active aerodynamics, including a rear wing that extends rearward up to 11.8 inches, add massive downforce and enable maximum attack at the racetrack. The W1 is a worthy heir to its predecessors, the latest in a highly accomplished bloodline of supersonic, roadgoing weaponry.

The W1 uses a plug-in hybrid powertrain comprised of McLaren's MHP-8 turbocharged V-8 engine working in unison with a radial-flux electric motor and an 8-speed transmission; it sends power to the rear wheels. The V-8 alone is good for 916 horsepower, and the electric motor 342 horsepower. Combined, that's 1258 ponies, the highest output of any McLaren.

The electric element includes a motorsport-derived E-module: a radial flux motor and integrated Motor Control Unit weighing just 44 pounds, similar to the type used in IndyCar racing. (McLaren participates in that series as well as F1.) The electric motor is mounted to the transmission and can spin up to 24,000 rpm. The E-module is powered by a battery of about 1.4 kWh, enclosed in a structural carbon-fiber box located low in the chassis to optimize the center of gravity. The battery maintains a minimum level of charge to crank the engine on vehicle start-up and to power the electric reversing function. McLaren quotes an electric-only driving range of about two miles and a charging time of 22 minutes to bring the battery to 80 percent.

According to McLaren, the W1 can hit 60 mph in just 2.7 seconds. More remarkably, accelerating from 0-124 mph requires just 5.8 seconds. Drivers with the will to stay in the throttle will find 186 mph comes up in a hair under 12.7 seconds. Top speed is electronically limited to 217 mph. When we get a W1 to test, we'll verify those numbers and update this story.

First Row: Supercar interiors have never been paragons of spaciousness, but McLaren insists the W1's driver-focused cockpit "provides the most intelligent use of space of any McLaren yet." That's quite a claim, particularly considering the cabin uses fixed seats, as their design contributes to the structural rigidity of the carbon fiber monocoque.

The pedals, steering wheel, instrument binnacle, and attendant controls move to help the driver find a comfortable position. Interior space is enhanced by concave sections on the inside door panels.

Cargo Capacity: The W1's primary storage area is located behind its seats, but there's only six cubic feet of cargo space back there. Best pack light.

A high-resolution 8.0-inch touchscreen running the McLaren Infotainment System (MIS) is located between the driver and the passenger. Apple CarPlay with USB-C connectivity is included, as is a USB-A port for device charging.

The McLaren W1 includes a comprehensive warranty and maintenance schedule. Similar to McLarens equipped with the V-6 hybrid, the V-8-hybrid-equipped W1 follows a 12-month service interval schedule that reflects McLaren series production supercars rather than schedules of its track-focused cars.
