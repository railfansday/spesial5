Marcus & Millichap (MMI) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Rate volatility is weighing on deal flow, but a looming maturity wave could reshape activity.

Marcus & Millichap, Inc. (MMI -3.17%) reported third quarter financial results that showed revenue growth across its core brokerage and financing segments, supported by increased activity in the middle market and larger transaction categories. Management stated that interest rate volatility remains a primary challenge to transaction velocity, as sharp fluctuations in treasury yields during the quarter triggered deal repricing and wider bid-ask spreads. The company indicated that it is prioritizing a series of long-term strategic initiatives, including the build-out of a retail leasing division and the implementation of AI-enabled enterprise systems to drive operational leverage. Additionally, the company noted that a significant volume of loan maturities scheduled for 2027 and 2028 is expected to act as a catalyst for future transaction activity as owners and lenders address assets that cannot be refinanced at current interest rate levels.

Total revenue increased 7.5% to $208 million in the third quarter, bringing revenue year-to-date to $583 million, up 14% from a year ago. Brokerage revenue grew 7%, led by our middle market and larger transaction segments, which grew 17% and 20%, respectively. Private Client revenue rose 4%, reflecting the more pronounced impact of higher interest rates at lower price points.

It should be noted that the 10-year treasury yield moved by 80 basis points between late June and late September, which caused a fair degree of repricing. The year-over-year comparison for our Private Client segment was also particularly challenging given the 17% gain we registered in the third quarter of 2025. Despite the recent round of interest rate volatility, financing availability and lenders' appetite to compete and put capital to work remain favorable, albeit with elevated caution on underwriting and borrower qualification. I'm also pleased to highlight the company's improved profitability. Adjusted EBITDA increased 20% in the third quarter and reached $23 million year-to-date compared to a $400,000 loss during the first 9 months of 2025.

Our team remains laser-focused on improving profitability while continuing to invest in critical infrastructure, tools, and talent. This includes ongoing technology investments, some upfront costs associated with opportunistic talent acquisition, and outside resources engaged during the quarter to help us evaluate potential strategic acquisitions. As I've mentioned on prior calls, MMI's near-term profitability continues to reflect the expensing of investments in top talent retention and acquisition over the past 5 years. Market conditions have hampered revenue production for many of the leading sales and financing professionals we have invested in. Therefore, our strategy to achieve operating leverage is to control costs while maintaining essential investments, maximizing revenue growth per producer, and advancing key strategic initiatives.

The biggest challenge in delivering more robust and consistent revenue growth is interest rate volatility, a major headwind to our sales force's productivity. Over the past 4 years, the 10-year treasury yield has moved by at least 50 basis points in either direction, 16x. Each significant rate movement affects valuations, bid-ask spreads, marketing time lines, lender underwriting, and whether deals can even pencil up. Keeping transactions on track requires more time, leaving even the most experienced producers with less capacity to develop new business. This has been very frustrating for our entire team. This prolonged market volatility also makes it harder for new producers to reach acceptable production levels.

As I've shared on previous calls, this is the underlying driver of our elevated new agent turnover, making quarterly tracking of net hires more volatile. We're also shifting new talent recruiting to rely more heavily on alternative channels, including our very successful internship and fellowship programs. These initiatives have developed higher-quality new producers who are gaining momentum, but will take time to translate into more measurable net additions to our sales force. Despite these near-term challenges, I want to point out several trends that remain very encouraging. Significant repricing of real estate has already occurred with further adjustments underway as the market adjusts to interest rates remaining elevated longer and much more so than previously expected.

Sellers' motivation to transact is increasing as the likelihood of interest rates returning to prior levels becomes a distant hope. More sellers are listing properties at realistic prices and showing greater willingness to transact as buyers seek more of a return premium. However, price adjustments vary widely by property type, market and asset quality, which is where Marcus & Millichap sales and financing specialists truly stand out through local expertise, deep relationships, and real-time market intelligence. Situational distress from weak operations, lower-than-expected rent growth over the past several years and the $1.2 trillion of loan maturities expected in 2027 and 2028 should result in higher transaction activity.

Lenders are better positioned to address underperforming assets and maturing debt that cannot be refinanced at current interest rate levels without the injection of significant fresh equity. Over the next 2 years, multifamily and retail loan maturities will account for nearly 50% of the total volume, positioning MMI ideally to provide solutions for affected owners and lenders in our leading business segments. Looking ahead, our long-term strategy for scaling the business, maximizing revenue growth, and achieving operating leverage centers on 6 key initiatives that are timely for me to refresh given our current business planning process underway for 2027. Our first priority is to further expand our market-leading Private Client platform across geographies and property types.

Despite our market dominance in this segment, as I've shared with you before, the vast and fragmented nature of the microcap and middle market commercial real estate market offers substantial room for growth. More effective hiring, training, deployment, and producer development will play a pivotal role in capturing this opportunity under the guidance of our local market leaders and divisional Chief Revenue Officers. We are very well positioned to leverage this talent for further growth. The second expansion strategy is to further build out our IPA platform, which is designed to bring advisory and brokerage capabilities to major accounts and institutional investors.

Adding IPA teams in multifamily, retail office, and industrial complements our Private Client business and has proven to be a scalable growth opportunity. Third, we're building on the progress we've made in expanding our finance division, MMCC. Collaboration between our financing specialists and sales teams has proven to be highly effective and, again, points to the opportunity for scaling. We're actively expanding our financing division through experienced hires and targeting boutique mortgage banking firms for acquisitions. We're also exploring complementary ways to broaden our financing capabilities. Fourth, we're expanding our auction division, which we launched organically 3 years ago.

Since then, our auction specialists have successfully integrated the service into our core brokerage offering. Collaboration across our auction, investment sales, and finance teams has proven effective and, again, scalable. In recent months, we added auction specialists in strategic regions to enable more internal collaboration with our sales force and generate incremental new business. For our fifth strategy, we're scaling our leasing capabilities in key markets. The integration of leasing and investment sales offers a strong advantage in our Private Client and Middle Market segments, where MMI can bring significant platform level advantages to owners and investors we have relationships with.

Retail leasing is our priority given our existing footprint and the opportunities for collaboration between leasing specialists and our retail investment sales force. We expect to grow leasing through a buy-and-build approach, enabling us to acquire boutique firms and groups as well as recruiting experienced teams in select markets. During the third quarter, 2 seasoned leasing executives joined senior management to help develop the strategy, systems, and support infrastructure for this initiative. Given the required planning and ramp-up time necessary to offer best-in-class support, we do not expect a meaningful revenue contribution from this initiative in 2027. However, we believe the long-term strategy will be extremely accretive to MMI's growth plan.

Last but certainly not least, over the next 12 to 18 months, we plan to upgrade a number of enterprise systems and implement a series of AI-enabled tools. Our strategy blends a series of proprietary tools, enhancements to legacy proprietary technology as well as the leveraging of commercial systems, all integrated into the MMI ecosystem. MMI is well positioned to leverage its market-leading brand and footprint to enter new revenue channels and offer more services to our core clients. The company's growth initiatives will take time to produce meaningful, sustainable incremental revenue growth, and will moderately increase costs over the next 18 to 24 months. However, we are confident that each initiative's close alignment with our core business will drive efficiencies, cross-selling opportunities, repeat business, and greater operating leverage over the next several years. This is very different than what we were witnessing directly from the field being sort of collectively observed than a year ago and especially 2 years ago, where the appetite for an actual execution of a transaction or much more decisive action upon loan maturity or significantly underperforming properties hardly ever included a discussion of an immediate decision or action. The loan modification, buying more time, expecting rates to come in, dominated the conversations in the last couple of years. That shift is the reason we believe that going into '27 and '28, there will be a lot more actual transactions that clear the market. They're going to have to be priced in order to clear the market.

And that's another area where our team is reporting a lot more realism, both by lenders of this category of potential sales and the current owners that are having some distress. So that was a little bit of a wake-up call for the market in a way that pushed more decisions to want to engage with us more actively on listings that we believe will come to market in the first half of 2027. The other more near-term challenge, Blaine, has a direct implication of what we've seen several times since this elongated downturn began, and that is the direct impact of higher interest rates, especially when they're unexpected on active listings and properties that have been negotiated, they're about to go under contract or go hard with nonrefundable deposits. Hessam Nadji: Yes. It's -- our biggest challenge really is more around the sellability of the current inventory that's on the market. As that hope has faded, more and more of our client base that we've been in active dialogue in is now bringing inventory to market.

And another positive trend is that, that initial price -- list price is more realistic. So we have near record inventory going into 2027. We're very confident that, that inventory within a 10% or 15% price range is sellable, but that's where the rub is in that the bid-ask keeps moving because of the volatility in interest rates. So the second part of your question was around delays versus deals that have fundamentally died.

We're seeing very few deals that have fundamentally died. There is a lot of motivation to sell. There's plenty of capital on the sidelines to want to transact. It's a question of bid-ask and it's a question of kind of regrouping and putting deals back under contract once we rediscover alignment between the buyer, the seller, and the lender. But we're seeing a lot more demand on the buy side for the risk premium that real estate has typically required above the 10-year yield -- 10-year treasury yield that kind of went away for a while, where investors were willing not to demand that premium over the 10-year treasury. That premium is absolutely back in the market. It's not any more than the long-term averages. It's typically 200 basis points, if you look at historical data. Hessam Nadji: The volatility is the absolute toughest headwind for producer productivity. As I mentioned, the tenure has now moved in either direction by 50 bps or more 16x in the last 4 years. Each one of those periods requires repricing of both inventory and deals under contract. It's taken us significantly longer to market an average listing. It's taken us significantly longer to oversee the various stages of contingency removal, due diligence, and closing process. And that leaves less time for developing new business, even for our most senior producers.

So this volatility is more of a challenge for our business than just the overall higher interest rate environment, frankly, because if higher interest rates had occurred and had stabilized at whatever rate the market felt was sustainable, the industry would recalibrate after some time and we would be able to kind of move on into a new cycle. The disruptive nature of this truly elongated downturn is very different than any cycle that I've been in really since the early '90s with the RTC, which took 4, 5 years through process, and that was much more of a macro breakdown of commercial real estate due to massive overbuilding and overleveraging.

This isn't the same kind of drivers, but it's creating an environment where an end to a down cycle has been very difficult to reach and to define because last year was the effect of tariffs. This year, it was the effect of the Iran war and higher inflation and then various reasons that the volatility has continued. Our approach, Mitch, has been to stay very close to our clients throughout this entire period of uncertainty starting in fall of 2022.

The number of times we've had to go back to the same client and revalue the real estate and do broker opinion of value 2 to 3 times a year and advise them on whether it was the right time to bring the property to market, what it would likely trade for is at a record high, especially when those conversations have not been translating into closed transactions unlike what we normally see. So we still believe in the client relationship. We still believe in investing, in being there as many times as it takes to guide the client on what to do with an asset, both at a portfolio level and at an asset level.

We are investing heavily on being in touch with our clients, both institutional, by the way, and private client is no different.
