USDT leads borrowing on four of six DeFi protocols examined by Europe’s banking watchdog

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The EBA urges the EU to assess restrictions on crypto firms facilitating loans involving USDT and other unauthorized stablecoins.

Tether’s USDT led stablecoin borrowing on Aave, Fluid and Spark in a July 30 snapshot published by the European Banking Authority (EBA) on Nov. 25.

The EBA renewed its call for the European Commission to assess rules for firms that give customers access to decentralized lending, including possible restrictions on loans involving stablecoins that lack the required EU authorization.

In its comparison of six lending protocols, the regulator grouped USDT and its USDT0 bridged version when describing demand on Aave, Fluid and Spark. The EBA also found that borrowers on JustLend overwhelmingly favored USDT and pointed to the token’s dominance on the Tron blockchain.

USDC accounted for a larger share of active loans than USDT on Compound and Morpho.

The EBA measured each stablecoin against all active loans on the same protocol. Separate estimates suggest EU participation in decentralized finance remains limited, and self-hosted wallets and reporting gaps make it difficult to identify where users are based.

The EU’s Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA) requires authorization for covered stablecoins to be offered to the public or admitted to trading in the EU, and USDT lacks that authorization.

The EBA’s summary of MiCA’s scope says fully decentralized services operating without an intermediary fall outside the rules. The report says some EU consumers may hold stablecoins like USDT in wallets they control themselves.

Some crypto firms give customers access to lending protocols through their own interfaces, while others offer products tied to decentralized lending.

The EBA describes arrangements that let customers put their stablecoins to work without opening a self-hosted wallet. In those cases, customers depend on the platform that handles the investment.

The EBA raised the issue in September as part of the Commission’s MiCA review, warning the Commission to weigh the costs and benefits of additional requirements for intermediaries. The options include checking whether products suit customers, requiring more disclosures, and limiting leverage.

The Commission could also assess restrictions on firms that facilitate borrowing and lending in stablecoins such as USDT, which require authorization but lack it.

The November report reiterates a recommendation for the MiCA review. The EBA plans to continue monitoring decentralized finance in 2027, including how consumers access stablecoins that lack MiCA authorization. That work could inform any further advice to the Commission.
