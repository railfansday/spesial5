Walt Launches Standalone Crypto App After Years Inside Telegram

The wallet platform that grew up inside Telegram is trying to become a destination in its own right. Walt, previously branded Wallet in Telegram, launched a standalone Android app on November 10. The company says an iOS version will follow. For the first time, customers can sign up without using Telegram, with Google and Apple among the supported account routes.

TL;DR: Walt has launched a separate Android app while keeping its existing Telegram integration. The service advertises access to more than 200 cryptocurrencies, tokenized US stocks and ETFs, perpetual futures and yield products, subject to regional availability. iOS is planned, not already live as part of today’s announcement.

The new app largely bundles products Walt had already developed inside Telegram. That means the headline is not the invention of a new trading contract or token. The commercial difference is that Walt no longer needs a user to start in a messaging app before becoming a customer.

According to the company’s product announcement, the app offers more than 200 cryptocurrencies, over 100 tokenized US stocks and exchange-traded funds, and perpetual contracts linked to crypto and some traditional asset classes. Walt also advertises yield features and AI-assisted research and trading tools. Availability and restrictions differ by jurisdiction and by individual product.

The company’s rebranding guidance explains that Walt and Telegram’s separate Money wallet are different services. That matters for users who remember an older ‘Wallet’ label and could easily assume the products now share an operator or custody arrangement.

Walt says existing users can sign in through Telegram and access the same funds through the standalone app. New users can register without Telegram. This shared-account design may reduce the inconvenience of switching channels, but users should still confirm the account recovery, security and withdrawal rules of the actual wallet service they use.

There is also an important distinction between owning a tokenized representation of a US stock and holding the original share through a conventional brokerage account. Product terms determine shareholder rights, corporate actions and redemption. NewsBTC’s coverage of transfer agents in tokenized securities describes why those legal and operational details are central to a credible market.

NewsBTC has also examined Visa’s institutional stablecoin settlement expansion. That infrastructure serves a different audience, but it underlines how wallet apps and payment networks are both trying to make digital balances more usable outside speculative trading.

The new app’s perpetual futures are another product that deserves clear risk labeling. They provide leveraged or derivative exposure rather than ownership of the underlying asset, and can carry liquidation and funding risks even when presented in a convenient mobile interface.

Walt’s distribution advantage has been access to Telegram users, but downloads, funded accounts and repeated use are different measures. The company says it has a large registered audience without publishing a full current breakdown of active users and trade volumes in this announcement. Those are the numbers that would help measure the standalone app’s impact.

The next milestones are an iOS release, geographic availability, fees and the adoption of products users cannot obtain as conveniently in the Telegram interface. Walt has now delivered the separate app. Whether customers will make it their primary home for digital assets remains an open commercial question.
