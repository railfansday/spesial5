Bitcoin has fallen for six straight weeks, its longest losing streak since February, and slipped below $62,000 over the weekend. Mark Yusko, chief executive of Morgan Creek Capital Management, said the slide is being driven by forced selling in heavily leveraged stock markets. He does not see it as a breakdown in the crypto cycle.

Speaking in an interview, Yusko said Bitcoin’s broader cycle is intact as long as prices stay above the February low, which he put in the $58,000 range.

Still in “Spring,” Despite the Drop Below $75,000

Yusko had earlier said Bitcoin had moved out of its “winter” phase and into “spring.” He stood by that view despite the break below $75,000.

He described spring as an unsettled season rather than a steady climb, with sharp swings in both directions and plenty of noise. In his view, the recent volatility fits that pattern.

The test, according to Yusko, is the February low. A move below the $58,000 area would point to a more distributive phase, in which holders are selling into the market rather than adding to positions.

Bitcoin and Nvidia Caught in the Same Shift

Yusko framed the sell-off in terms of accumulation and distribution, the cycle in which investors move between being net buyers and net sellers of an asset.

When leveraged positions go wrong, investors often cannot sell the losing stock to cover their debts. Yusko used SanDisk as an example. An investor who bought it on margin and watched it drop 50% has to raise cash by selling other holdings, such as bonds, gold or Bitcoin.

That is why easy-to-sell assets fall in a deleveraging phase, even when nothing has changed in their own fundamentals. “We are in a bad liquidation cycle right now,” Yusko said.

Yusko’s outlook comes down to a single level. As long as Bitcoin holds above the February low near $58,000, he sees the current weakness as part of a choppy recovery phase.

A break below that range would point to a deeper distribution phase and weaken the case that the cycle has turned. Bitcoin’s direction in the coming weeks is likely to depend on how quickly the selling in leveraged equity markets eases.
