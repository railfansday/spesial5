Figment to Support Staking Through BNY’s Digital Asset Custody Platform

BNY’s digital asset custody business is adding another route into staking through a collaboration with Figment. The development addresses a practical institutional problem: earning protocol rewards while keeping assets within an established custody and reporting relationship.

In its November 12 announcement, Figment said its validators would support BNY’s staking offering for eligible clients. The release names asset managers, ETF issuers, corporations and wealth managers among the potential users, without providing a launch date or a universal list of eligible accounts.

The arrangement combines BNY’s custody and servicing framework with Figment’s infrastructure for participating in blockchain validation. That allows the two providers to supply different parts of one client workflow.

Holding an asset safely and using it in a protocol are related responsibilities, but they require different operations. Custody focuses on control, safekeeping and administration. Validator infrastructure performs the continuing network duties that can generate staking rewards.

For a large investment firm, integration can reduce the number of separate processes its staff must coordinate. Reconciliation, permissions and reporting still need to work across the whole arrangement, however. A convenient interface does not make those responsibilities disappear; it gives the providers a defined place to organize them.

BNY had already outlined a staking collaboration with Galaxy in an August 4 announcement. That release described an integrated institutional service and said the offering and related infrastructure enhancements were subject to regulatory review.

The Figment announcement therefore should not be framed as BNY discovering staking for the first time. It is another development in the infrastructure supporting a previously stated direction.

NewsBTC’s coverage of BNY’s European custody expansion under MiCA provides a second part of that history. Geographic permissions, supported assets and individual client eligibility remain specific to the relevant service and entity.

Ethereum’s staking documentation explains that validators receive rewards for performing network duties. Downtime can lead to missed rewards and penalties, while certain forms of misconduct can cause slashing. Using a provider adds operational and counterparty considerations to that protocol exposure.

The economic distinction is straightforward. A holder can accumulate more units of an asset while the value of the position falls in dollars. Staking income and market-price performance are different components of the final result.

NewsBTC examined that distinction in its report on Sygnum’s ETH-denominated investment fund. That fund includes separate trading strategies, so its target should not be imported into the BNY service as though it were a quoted staking rate.

The potential benefit is an easier operational route for institutions that already hold supported digital assets with a custodian. That could matter to firms whose investment policy allows staking but whose internal processes make managing validators directly impractical.

Actual adoption would need to be judged through live availability, assets staked, service performance and the terms clients receive. Fees, exit arrangements, reporting quality and responsibility for operational losses would all affect the proposition, even if the underlying protocol reward were identical.

The announcement does not disclose those commercial details or a guaranteed level of participation. It establishes the collaboration and the intended access model. Delivering a service that institutions can use consistently, within their own mandates and liquidity needs, is the next practical test.
