Barnsley facing January nightmare as two departures predicted

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Barnsley are staring down the barrel of a first fourth-tier campaign since the 1978/79 season, and things may be made even worse in January, with EFL content creator and Reds fan Callum Fowler predicting that both Reyes Cleary and Patrick Kelly could be sold.

Daniel Stendel's side were unable to capitalise on some momentum and 12 goals in a couple of cup wins in the week leading up to their trip to fellow bottom-four side Wigan Athletic on Saturday.

A 2-1 defeat at the Brick Community Stadium saw the Tics climb out of relegation danger, leaving the Tykes winless in nine league outings, picking up just two points in that time. They're only off the bottom thanks to a superior goal difference compared to Bromley, who have the same record as them after 13 games.

It's looking pretty bleak at Oakwell right now, and the prospect of losing their best players in January is looking even more likely at this moment.

Despite Barnsley's dire situation, there's no doubt that they currently employ two of the brighter young stars in the division. Both Cleary and Kelly have a ceiling that surpasses the third tier, and perhaps could see them playing top-flight football in their careers.

Cleary burst onto the scene last season with 20 goal involvements in 30 league starts, and while he hasn't been as influential so far this year, his quality is still there, as seen with the cross he delivered for the Reds' equaliser against Wigan on Saturday.

Arguably even more concerning is the potential loss of Kelly, though. Only Derensili Sanches Fernandes and Oliver Norwood have created more open-play chances than the Northern Irish international in League One this season, and he's been the main source of everything good at the club.

Cleary's star power from last season is almost leaving Kelly to operate without the eyes on him, but speaking on the Pyramid podcast, Barnsley fan Callum Fowler and co-host Tom Garratt discussed the possibility of losing them both.

"I just don't see how it turns," Garratt said. "I'll be amazed if you [Barnsley] keep Cleary and Kelly," to which Fowler responded, "We won't; they'll both go, 100%. Cleary will at least; he'll go on New Year's Day.

"Can you imagine that team without Cleary and Kelly? It's basically that team at Wigan with no Cleary, so you don't get the goal, because no one can put that ball in."

If there's one thing that the current regime at Barnsley under chairman Neerav Parekh can attest to doing well, it's ensuring that the club squeezes as much as they can out of buyers when they approach the Tykes for their players.

The likes of Liam Kitching, Mads Andersen, Michal Helik and Carlton Morris have all sold for seven-figure fees since the club came down to League One in 2022, and while undisclosed, it's likely that the late winter sale of Davis Keillo-Dunn to Wrexham eclipsed that, too. Some suspected above £2 million.

If anything, though, that ability to sell well just stokes the frustration surrounding the current hierarchy, as not a lot of that money, if any of it, is reinvested into the playing squad. Barnsley continue to take chances on young players, and it's led to this steady decline down the League One table.

Fowler doesn't think that, if Cleary and/or Kelly are sold this January, they will be replaced adequately, and then a Barnsley squad which is already in trouble will be even worse.

"It's heart over head at times, because I'm thinking, 'surely they'll chuck some money at this in January,' but they never have, not once," he continued.

"They've put the tickets as they have for the Wednesday game [£35 for adults], almost like one last cash grab; they'll sell Cleary and Kelly and bring in nothing."

It's close to universally agreed that Daniel Stendel has Barnsley coached well — at least, better than their league position would suggest — but the general naivety of the squad and the youthful nature often leads them to fade away in games.

The Tykes have led in seven of their 13 games so far this season, but have won just two of those. They've conceded 11 times in the final half hour of games, and four times in the final 10 minutes.

The majority of the Barnsley fanbase are still backing Stendel and know that it isn't his, nor the players', fault that they're in this position. A protest is being planned online ahead of their game against Sheffield Wednesday on Sky Sports this Friday, and time will tell what impact that could have.

Plainly put, though, if Barnsley don't strengthen in January, they may be set for the drop. If they lose two of the better creative players in the league, there may be no way back at all.
