Kawhi Leonard is back on the (practice) court: will his return be enough?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The Raptors have released another brief statement outlining Kawhi Leonard's return to playing NBA basketball, and while there are positive signs emerging, little can be confirmed about when the forward will be back on the court.

"He has begun individual on-court workouts following treatment to address left knee swelling," stated the press release issued earlier today, November 24th. Leonard has not seen the court for more than one month, last having suited up in game two for the Raptors on October 23rd in a win for Toronto against the Washington Wizards.

Without Kawhi, the Raptors have been in free-fall, punctuated by a six-game losing streak earlier in November. Some of these were close, against good teams; the Raptors would develop a lead, then give it away in the second half, breaking the hearts of Toronto fans who tuned in to watch. With his clutch genes and scoring prowess, Leonard's presence on the team would have been sure to change the outcome of some of these games, but the lack of his presence has revealed both flaws, and upsides with this roster.

The Raptors roster, as currently constructed, is not that different from what it was last year. Regardless of having Leonard or Brandon Ingram at small-forward, these two players were signed for the same job: take offensive responsibilities off of Scottie Barnes' shoulders so that he can focus on playmaking and defence.

All together, a sound idea for roster construction. But the state of the starters around the pairs in both years? Less than ideal. The Raptors are currently ranked 24th in three-pointers made, and are shooting 33.8% from deep this season. A big reason for this is because this year, 3 of 5 Raptors starers are not considered good three point shooters, those being Scottie Barnes, Jakob Poeltl, and RJ Barrett, while the two perceived marksmen, Immanuel Quickley, and Kawhi Leonard, have both been unreliable.

While the Raptors, ironically, have quite a few good three point shooting players or prospects coming off their bench, the Raps' starting lineup, the players who they have committed the most money to, are either non-shooters, in a shooting slump, or injured. While a lack of clutch play and poor defence is also certainly to blame for the Raptors' losses, being able to buy a 3 certainly would help the Raptors, especially in the fourth quarter of games.

It's not 2006 anymore, and the Raptors need to get with the times. Giving Ja'Kobe Walter more minutes is a step in the right direction, but with his certain relegation to the bench when Kawhi Leonard returns, the Raptors' rotations need to consistently sport players who can make long range shots.

As a whole, the Raptors have added Leonard to a problematic roster which in many respects, he was meant to, and seemed potentially able to fix. But, succeeding with Leonard always comes contingent with being able to survive the regular periods of absence he takes as a result of health issues. And the Raptors have shown themselves largely unable to do so.

One of the lone bright spots in the Raptors' season thus far has been the ascension of Scottie Barnes to hitherto unforeseen levels. Barnes has been thrust into a primary offensive creation role and has taken to it unexpectedly well. As of November 24th, Barnes is averaging almost 25 points per game and a career high in steals. He's doing it playing an antiquated style of basketball, too, barely making any threes, and living off of midrange buckets.

Barnes has never had a higher profile, nor has he ever been more fun to watch on the offensive side of the ball.

Allen Graves, the Santa Clara rookie drafted 19th overall, has done a lot to increase his volume from three, and had his first signature game, with a 20 point, 10 rebound performance to help the Raptors put away the Detroit Pistons. While it's unlikely for that same level of play to extend the rest of the season, Graves has shown that he's capable of doing what he was drafted to do, and could be the next developmental success story for the Raptors.

Getting Leonard back to pair with Barnes, Graves, and Collin Murray-Boyles gives the Raptors a disgusting forward rotation to lean on heavily, and Leonard's offensive abilities will likely be able to drag the Raptors back into a positive win/loss ratio. But, ideally, Leonard should not be dragging dead weight behind him. The Raptors, as an organization, need to take account of the state of their team without Kawhi Leonard. He won't always be available to bail out this roster, and when he isn't, it is irresponsible coaching and management to allow the roster to go on to lose games in the manner that they are currently losing them in.

Leonard is not being paid to help rookies ascend and find their place in the NBA. This is team headlined by professional, veteran players, and Leonard or no-Leonard, they should look like it on the basketball court.

The Raptors will give more information on Kawhi's health in a week. Stay frosty.
