Russia accuses NATO member Estonia of hostile actions ‘on the verge of unleashing open war’

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Russia's Foreign Ministry has lashed out at Estonia's government, accusing the NATO member of carrying out a range of "hostile actions" and putting the Baltic state "on the verge of unleashing open war" with Moscow.

The outburst underscores rising tensions between Russia and countries on NATO's eastern flank and comes after Estonia announced it will close its road border crossings with Russia from Dec. 1.

Russia's ministry said Monday it had lodged a formal protest to Estonia's chargé d'affaires, Marek Uhtegi, over Tallinn's decision to close its border crossings and accused the European Union member of imposing a naval blockade against Moscow.

"The Estonian diplomat was again informed that such provocative and extremely dangerous actions are on the verge of unleashing open war with Russia," the ministry said in a statement, describing measures taken by Estonian lawmakers as "Russophobic."

The ministry also accused Estonia of "tacitly" providing its airspace for Ukrainian drone flights and sharply criticized Tallinn's ban on the transit of Russian and Belarusian grain through Estonian territory, referring to a policy that was introduced at the start of October.

Estonia, which shares a 294-kilometer (183-mile) border with Russia, swiftly denied ever allowing its territory to be used for military operations by another country.

It also responded to Russia's allegations by saying the Kremlin appears to be seeking to divert attention from its own efforts to undermine European security.

"Russian missiles have reached Polish territory, Danish security services have identified an assassination plot in Denmark orchestrated by Russian intelligence services, merchant vessels in the Black Sea are under constant threat from Russian attacks, and in Estonia, an arson attack targeting a defence industry company, planned on the orders of Russian military intelligence, was foiled," Estonian Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna said Monday.

"All this is happening while Russia continues its war of aggression against Ukraine," he added.

The rhetoric marks a significant escalation in tensions between Estonia and Russia. Tallinn, a staunch Ukraine ally, has frequently raised the alarm over sabotage attacks against NATO countries that have been supporting Kyiv since Russia launched the full-scale invasion of its neighbor in February 2022.

Earlier this month, Estonia said it would close road border crossings with Russia from Dec. 1 as it blames Moscow for an attempted "sabotage" of one of its defense companies, an allegation the Kremlin denies.

Tallinn has said the border closure will help to reduce Moscow's ability to conduct hostile activities against it and mitigate risks related to sanctions circumvention.

Ukraine has stepped up its attacks on Russian critical targets in recent weeks, damaging Russian President Vladimir Putin's prized Crimea bridge, striking a Russian cargo ship in the Baltic Sea and destroying one of Russia's newest ships at a Libyan port.

The suspected Ukrainian attack on a Russian military-linked cargo ship in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday prompted Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo to warn that the Ukraine war is "spilling over" into its immediate vicinity.

Finland, one of NATO's newest members, has also closed its land border crossings with Russia.

Ukraine's latest attacks on some of Russia's most protected targets have demonstrated Kyiv's capabilities to hurt Moscow even without ample U.S. military support, according to Mario Bikarski, senior Europe and Central Asia analyst for Verisk Maplecroft, a political risk consultancy.

"Ukraine will continue to look for ways to degrade not only Russia's economy, but also its geostrategic interests in the wider region, including the Mediterranean and the Middle East," Bikarski said.

The prospect of an imminent peace deal between Russia and Ukraine appears to be slim, Bikarski said, although "the latest surge in diplomatic activity and mounting losses for both sides signals an appetite from all sides to reach an agreement."
