Devin Carter speaks for first time since preseason illness

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

DENVER — Devin Carter was just beginning to hit a groove. The Celtics guard, traded from the Kings to the Hawks over the summer and waived days before training camp, had a stellar preseason debut in Cleveland.

On a training camp deal, Carter finished with 17 points, 5 rebounds, 3 assists, and 2 steals. But just a few days later, he got sick and was diagnosed with mononucleosis, an illness he deems was the worst he's ever had.

"I definitely was upset at the timing that it happened because, like you said, I had a good game in Cleveland, and I was looking forward to playing the rest of preseason and showing what I worked on all summer," Carter said at Celtics shootaround on Wednesday morning. "And then everything gets stripped quick."

After nearly a month sidelined, the former lottery pick is back on the court. He lost 10 pounds while sick, and he still finds himself a bit more fatigued than usual. The whole experience was completely foreign to him.

"I never had COVID. I never had nothing," he said. "So this is the first thing I've really ever been sick with."

In the last few weeks, though, Carter has turned a corner. He made his G League debut earlier this month and logged 12 minutes on Sunday in his Celtics regular-season debut against the Dallas Mavericks.

"The G League has actually been very helpful so far — getting my wind back and getting reps in that I missed out on for four weeks," he said. "So it's been very helpful."

Still, while he's cleared and back out there, he's still not 100% himself.

"I still feel fatigued, more so, energy's low," Carter said. "You play two minutes, everything just shuts down, and then just building that back up. Obviously, the way I play, it's kind of tough to not be tired. So on top of the way I play, dealing with the sickness is just tough. But I'm not in a rush. I definitely feel myself back in shape and playing a lot better."

Carter said he's kept a positive perspective throughout the illness.

"It's been a blessing, regardless," he said. "Everybody gotta go through something to get where they want to go… definitely coming back into my body."

He's grateful the organization has supported him through the unexpected setback that came just weeks after he joined the squad. Celtics head of player development Craig Luschenat, for example, has been sending him clips from his G League film, and he's received game edits throughout his time sidelined.

"They've been very supportive," he said. "Front office, coaches, players, everybody been supportive of me. Everybody's helped me out. Anything I need — Advil, water, everybody's been super helpful."

As he works to get back into his usual shape, he's focused on carving out a role in the NBA after he was cut just two years into his career.

"Just picking up full court, being more of a tight defender, taking care of the ball, trying to force turnovers, playmaking, whatever the team needs in the moment, guarding the best player," he said. "I think I do a good job of just doing the little things that help a team win, and I mean that's ultimately what every team in the NBA needs — somebody who can do the little things… You have the superstars and those people who put the ball in the basket. You have to have the surrounding cast."

For now, he's grateful that he found Boston as a landing spot, a place that's deeply impressed him since he first arrived.

"Everybody wants to show up," Carter said. "Everybody wants to be around people. Everybody wants to get better. And chemistry on the team is at an all-time high. I've never been a part of a team with chemistry like this, so we do stuff off the court, and it's just great to be around."
