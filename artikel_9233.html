Italy Fines Meta $887 Million for Accessing Users’ Photos Stored in Their Phones

Italy’s Data Protection Authority has fined Meta €805 million ($887 million) for accessing photos and videos stored in Android users’ phone galleries through Facebook without obtaining specific consent or providing adequate information about how the content would be used.

An investigation found that the feature was enabled by default and that Facebook sought access to users’ photo galleries through a single permission covering several app functions with different purposes. The regulator says the information provided to users did not clearly explain that granting access would allow Facebook to generate suggestions for posts using images and videos stored on their devices.

Meta has been given 60 days to disable the feature by default and introduce a separate consent process for its use. The authority says the decision affects approximately 20 million Android users in Italy.

The penalty was imposed on Meta Platforms Ireland Limited, which disputes Italy’s jurisdiction over the case. The regulator says it considered the large number of people affected, the fact that the feature remained active for some users, and Meta’s “lack of co-operation” during the investigation. The fine represents approximately 0.47% of Meta’s global turnover.

Euronews reports that the investigation began in July 2025 following a complaint from the Bolzano Consumer Protection Centre and was supported by a technical analysis conducted by Italian authorities. The proceedings did not examine whether users’ content had been used to train AI models, an allegation Meta has denied.

Meta says it will appeal the decision, disputing the regulator’s assessment of the facts, its interpretation of the law, and the size of the penalty.

“We strongly disagree with the [regulator’s] assessment of the facts, its application of the law and the disproportionate level of the fine,” a company spokesperson tells the BBC.

“This is a straightforward feature which helps people post photos and videos to Facebook. People must give permission on their device before it can operate and they can change this permission at any time via their settings.”

Last year, PetaPixel reported that Meta was testing an AI-powered feature that processes users’ photos on their devices.
