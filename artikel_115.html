Bahrain said it had intercepted “several aerial attacks” and destroyed the drones before they reached their targets, and officials did not immediately disclose any damage or injuries

However the tiny Gulf kingdom, which hosts a major US naval command, was forced to send out public warnings and sound missile warning sirens ahead of the incoming strikes

Iran said its attack was justified retaliation for recent US aggression. The US has been carrying out low-level strikes on Iranian naval sites along the country’s south coast and also on Qeshm Island, in the Strait of Hormuz, to support oil vessels traversing the dangerous waterway

Iran says ships traversing the chokepoint without permission are hitting sea mines and sinking, and are legitimate targets to be fired upon.

On a different front, in civil war-torn Yemen, the Iran-backed Houthis are putting pressure on the government’s control around Aden, the interim capital.

The Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces say they’ve been forced to withdraw from their Bab el-Mandeb counter-offensive to focus on securing Aden.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards have claimed the sinking of an oil supertanker which hit a mine in the Strait of Hormuz. There’s no independent verification of this yet.

The US military had claimed recently that it had cleared all mines from the main routes through the waterway.

But the IRGC said Sunday that two ships had hit its mines while travelling through the strait, “resulting in massive explosions and fire”.

It said the super tanker had been carrying more than two million barrels of oil when it sunk in the Gulf. Iran has made several announcements in recent weeks about ships hitting mines.

Tehran has heavily restricted shipping traffic through the strait since the start of the war, demanding vessels seek permission and travel via designated pathways. It has fired on ships trying to circumvent Iranian detection.

The US has been supporting ships going through the strait. In the past 24 hours, Centcomm posted on X a picture of F-16 fighter jets and said that more than 1.7 billions of crude oil “have been shipped through the Strait of Hormuz by Gulf partners with U.S. protection.”

Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said at a press conference in Tehran on Sunday that Iran would continue to respond to any military aggression by the US or other states.

He was asked whether the strike on Tehran, the second this weekend, showed a change in Iranian direction as negotiations with the US have stalled.

Mr. Baghaei said that Iran was “not waiting for anyone” and was focused on its security.

The US has launched strikes on Iranian naval bases in the Strait of Hormuz in recent weeks, as well as along Iran’s southern coast. This has been seen as efforts to try and degrade Iran’s striking capability as well as offering cover for commerical ships still venturing through and out of Hormuz.

President Trump has refrained from major military escalation, describing those sporadic strikes as minor. However, American media have reported that both the US and Israel have discussed plans for a fuller resumption of strikes.

However the base was struck heavily during Iran’s retaliatory strikes at the start of the war, and it’s unclear whether it’s fully recovered.

American media have reported major damage to its headquarters, barracks and radar facilities.

The US has formal bases in Kuwait, Bahrain, and Qatar, and access arrangements to military facilities in the United Arab Emirates, Oman, and Saudi Arabia.

Below is the full statement from Bahrain’s foreign ministry on the Iranian drone strikes today, which were intercepted and shot down. This has been published in Bahrain’s various state-controlled media outlets.

double quotation markThe Ministry of Foreign Affairs expressed the Kingdom of Bahrain’s strongest condemnation of the continued Iranian aggression against its territory, including the attack launched today, Sunday, November 15, 2026, by drones that were intercepted and destroyed by air defense systems in the Kingdom’s airspace.

This comes two days after a similar attack at dawn on Friday, November 13, 2026, by two drones that were also intercepted. The Iranian Revolutionary Guard explicitly claimed responsibility for these attacks. The Ministry praised the efficiency, vigilance, and capability of the Bahrain Defense Force in confronting today’s attack and its complete readiness to deter any aggression. The Ministry considered these attacks a resumption of Iran’s heinous aggression against the Kingdom and countries in the region, disregarding the provisions of international law and the principles of the UN Charter.

The Kingdom views them as a blatant attack on its sovereignty and territorial integrity, a flagrant violation of Security Council Resolution 2817, which condemned Iranian attacks on the Kingdom of Bahrain and neighboring countries and demanded their immediate cessation, a targeting of innocent citizens and residents, a sabotage of de-escalation efforts, and a serious threat to the region’s security and stability. The Ministry affirmed the Kingdom of Bahrain’s categorical rejection of the flimsy pretexts and baseless claims put forward by the Iranian Revolutionary Guard, emphasizing that no pretext justifies aggression against the sovereignty and territorial integrity of states.

It stressed that freedom of navigation and passage, by sea and air, in straits used for international navigation is a right guaranteed by the rules of international law and is not subject to the will or conditions of any state.

The Kingdom also rejects the transformation of its territory into an arena for settling scores and holds Iran fully responsible for these attacks and their consequences. The Ministry also affirmed the Kingdom of Bahrain’s inherent right, both individually and collectively, to self-defense in accordance with Article (51) of the Charter, and to take all necessary measures to safeguard its sovereignty and the security of its citizens and residents, in close coordination with its brothers in the Gulf Cooperation Council, based on the Joint Defense Agreement, which considers an attack on any member state an attack on all, as well as with its allies and partners. The Ministry noted that the Kingdom of Bahrain, as a member of the UN Security Council, calls upon the Council to condemn these repeated attacks and demands that it assume its responsibilities and take decisive measures to ensure Iran’s full compliance with Resolution 2817, and to halt its attacks, threats, and support for proxy groups in the region. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs added that the Kingdom of Bahrain affirms that its security is a red line, that its sovereignty is non-negotiable, and that it will remain, as it has always been, a proponent of good neighborliness, respect for the sovereignty of states, and non-interference in their affairs, and is committed to the security and stability of the Arabian Gulf.
