Artificial intelligence has sped early-stage drug development work at some of the world’s largest pharmaceutical companies this year, cutting by as much as half the time it takes to produce new molecules and move them into the clinic, Reuters explains. The top scientists at Bristol Myers Squibb, Novartis, Eli Lilly, Sanofi, Novo Nordisk and AstraZeneca all say they have moved beyond less glamorous tasks for AI like preparing regulatory filings. They are now using the technology for ‌designing molecules, sorting out the most promising ones, and identifying safety risks.

A few of the largest pharmaceutical companies have told a Congressional committee that they do not plan to start new clinical trial sites in the Xinjiang region of China or at hospitals affiliated with the Chinese military, STAT writes. The disclosure was made by the House Select Committee on China, which last June opened investigations into five companies — Merck, Eli Lilly, AbbVie, Pfizer, and Bristol Myers Squibb — over concerns about intellectual property exposure, military entanglements, and ethical standards at trial sites in sensitive regions of the country.
