CDC Researchers Sound the Alarm on Congenital Measles

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

— Report documents at least one likely case of pediatric meningoencephalitis

U.S. measles outbreaks since 2025 have triggered more than 100 maternal measles infections during pregnancy, as well as cases of congenital measles in infants, and at least one likely case of pediatric meningoencephalitis, according to an analysis from CDC researchers.

From the start of 2025 through Sept. 28, 2026, a total of 13 infants developed congenital measles. Six of those infants developed measles symptoms, including fever, rash, conjunctivitis, coryza, or cough, and one developed symptoms indicating meningoencephalitis, reported Thomas Filardo, MD, of the CDC's National Center for Immunization and Respiratory Diseases in Atlanta, and colleagues from the agency.

"Large measles outbreaks in undervaccinated communities during 2025-2026 have resulted in infections among pregnant women and in congenital measles, showing the impact of measles on maternal and neonatal health," Filardo and colleagues wrote in NEJM Evidence. "Healthcare systems and clinicians should be prepared to provide care to pregnant women and their infants with measles, particularly in communities with a high proportion of unvaccinated people."

Over the study period, the CDC received reports of 112 confirmed cases of measles in pregnant women from 16 U.S. jurisdictions. Nearly all of the women (98%) either hadn't received measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR) vaccination before pregnancy or had an unknown vaccination history. Of the two women who did receive the MMR vaccine, one had a single documented dose pre-pregnancy, and the other had two doses before becoming pregnant. Eleven of the women were hospitalized for measles complicated by pneumonia.

"This is a brand new phenomenon," said Michael Osterholm, PhD, MPH, who directs the University of Minnesota's Center for Infectious Disease Research and Policy.

"What's happening is we have so many women of childbearing age who were not vaccinated in the last 25 years, nor have they ever had exposure to measles because for the last 25 years we have beaten it back," Osterholm said. "Now, this group is highly susceptible to measles."

According to a Washington Post report, HHS officials last month blocked CDC scientists from issuing a nationwide alert to doctors through the agency's Health Alert Network about the growing threat of measles in pregnancy.

The new report arrives as the U.S. loses its measles elimination status and wrestles with a record number of cases. Nationwide, 5,412 cases of measles have been confirmed as of Nov. 12, according to the CDC, the highest tally in decades. Nearly all of those cases -- 97% -- have been in people who weren't vaccinated against measles.

"We're now seeing levels of measles that we haven't seen in 30 years," said Adam Ratner, MD, a member of the American Academy of Pediatrics' infectious diseases committee. "When you have measles under control," congenital measles doesn't happen, he said.

Measles in pregnancy is linked to serious outcomes both for mothers and babies. Infected mothers are more likely to be hospitalized, develop pneumonia, or to die. Infants born to infected mothers were more likely to be preterm, have low birth weight, and be admitted to neonatal intensive care units (NICU). Those who develop congenital measles are at greater risk of serious conditions such as encephalitis, subacute sclerosing panencephalitis, and death.

Because MMR is a live-attenuated vaccine, it's not recommended during pregnancy, Filardo and colleagues noted. "Therefore, protection in this population relies on both vaccination of women before pregnancy and maintaining high community MMR coverage," they explained. Unvaccinated pregnant women exposed to measles should be given intravenous immunoglobulin as postexposure prophylaxis. Infants exposed to measles in utero should receive immunoglobulin at birth.

Among the 112 U.S. pregnant women with measles infections in the 2025-2026 period, 23% were hospitalized for non-labor-related causes.

Twenty-one had pregnancy outcomes reported with 14 days of the start of a maternal measles rash. Among those 21 pregnancies, 19 were live births, and two pregnancies were lost. Nearly half of those 19 live births (8) were preterm, and eight infants were admitted to a NICU.

Most of the maternal measles cases (96%) were linked to large outbreaks of at least 50 measles cases.

The Associated Press contributed to this story.
