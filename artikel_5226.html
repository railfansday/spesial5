Bitcoin ETFs Lose $299.8M as Five-Session Outflow Streak Reaches $1.44B

TL;DR: US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $299.8 million in net outflows on November 11, the latest complete session in the data checked November 12. Five consecutive negative sessions now total approximately $1.44 billion. Individual funds are still moving in different directions.

Bitcoin’s US fund market has supplied another weak demand reading. The latest withdrawals extend a run in which new money entering some products has been insufficient to offset redemptions elsewhere.

The useful signal is the persistence of the imbalance. It deserves attention without turning one set of fund flows into a claim about every Bitcoin investor, or treating it as a reliable forecast of the next market move.

The Farside Investors table records a $269.7 million outflow from BlackRock’s IBIT and $39.1 million from Grayscale’s GBTC on November 11. Morgan Stanley’s MSBT received $9 million, leaving a $299.8 million net withdrawal across the tracked products.

Adding the table’s November 5, 6, 9, 10 and 11 net figures gives $1,437.9 million in withdrawals. That is a five-session total, with the intervening weekend excluded. November 12 entries were incomplete when checked and are not part of this calculation.

NewsBTC previously covered the November 9 ETF outflow. The new development is that two additional sessions have extended the negative run, rather than a revision that makes the earlier report a separate loss.

The positive MSBT reading prevents the session from being described accurately as a withdrawal from every product. Fund selection can reflect fees, distribution relationships, portfolio changes or investor preference; a daily table cannot establish which explanation drove a particular order.

Morgan Stanley’s launch announcement describes MSBT as a product seeking to track Bitcoin and sets out a 0.14% sponsor fee at launch. That product structure provides context for its place in the market. It does not prove that lower fees caused the latest inflow.

Nor does one fund’s gain cancel the broader imbalance. Investors can distinguish competitive shifts between providers from the total amount entering or leaving the group.

An ETF share can change hands on an exchange without the fund adding or removing underlying assets. Creation and redemption activity is the mechanism that changes the fund’s share count and asset base.

The IBIT prospectus available through iShares explains that authorized participants create and redeem baskets using cash or Bitcoin. That is why large exchange trading volume alone should not be mistaken for equally large new investment in the trust.

There is a related distinction between outflows and assets under management. A fund’s asset value can fall because Bitcoin declines, because investors redeem, or through both effects. Combining those changes without separating them can exaggerate the amount of investor withdrawal.

For traders, a continuing outflow streak may indicate that this particular source of demand remains under pressure. It still leaves open what is happening in direct spot markets, derivatives or corporate treasury accounts.

NewsBTC’s report on October inflation and the rate outlook provides another part of the market backdrop. The ETF figures alone do not establish that inflation, rates or any single headline caused the redemptions.

A sustained return to inflows would change the evidence. One positive session would be a narrower improvement. For now, the conclusion supported by the completed data is persistent net withdrawal from the tracked US spot funds, with an important exception at the individual-provider level.
