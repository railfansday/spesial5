SOL Eyes New Bullish Era as Solana Ditches ‘Beta’ Label After Six Years and Embraces Protocol Version 1.0

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Solana has officially turned a major page in its history, dropping the long-standing ‘beta’ tag after more than six years and adopting protocol version 1.0. The milestone marks a significant step in the network’s evolution as it moves beyond its experimental phase, with ongoing efforts to improve transaction speed, finality, and overall performance. The development could also strengthen Solana’s appeal to developers and institutional players, raising fresh questions about whether SOL is poised for a new bullish chapter.

Solana has officially retired its six-year-old “mainnet beta” designation, with the network’s foundation saying the rebranding highlights the significant strides made since the blockchain first launched in March 2020.

“Solana 1.0 recognizes the progress on network performance, resilience, and developer experience,” the Solana foundation wrote in a Monday statement. “That progress continues with this simplification of nomenclature.”

The foundation emphasized that Solana has operated as a fully live network since its launch, arguing that retaining the beta designation had become outdated. It noted that the label, traditionally associated with technology still in its early testing stages, no longer accurately represented a blockchain already processing transactions and hosting active applications.

According to the Solana Foundation, the reclassification requires no action from users. Existing SOL holdings, account balances, and transaction histories will remain unchanged despite the protocol’s transition to version 1.0.

The foundation also advised developers and validator operators to stick to the specific instructions accompanying software releases, stressing that the new protocol designation does not mean all network upgrades or improvements become active automatically.

Solana’s move to protocol version 1.0 comes as the network continues pursuing major infrastructure improvements, with Alpenglow and Firedancer among its most closely watched developments. Both initiatives aim to strengthen Solana’s performance, reliability, and ability to support growing on-chain activity.

The first major milestone is Alpenglow, Solana’s new consensus protocol, which went live on mainnet on October 21. The upgrade targets transaction finality of approximately 150 milliseconds, a significant improvement from the previous 12.8-second timeframe. Transaction finality refers to the moment a blockchain permanently confirms a transaction, meaning it is considered settled and cannot be reversed under normal network conditions. Faster finality could make Solana more attractive for payments, decentralized finance, and institutional applications that require near-instant transaction settlement.

Meanwhile, Firedancer brings another important improvement through an independently developed validator client built by Jump Crypto. It provides an alternative to Solana’s existing Agave client, reducing the network’s dependence on a single software implementation. This client diversity could strengthen network resilience by limiting the risk that a bug affecting one implementation disrupts the entire blockchain.

The foundation added that the rebranding establishes a unified system for tracking Solana’s updates, allowing developers, validators, and infrastructure providers to identify protocol changes consistently, regardless of the software client they use.
