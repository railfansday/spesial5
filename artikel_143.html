Trump defends commerce secretary Lutnick for allegedly slow-walking Nvidia chip export restrictions – live

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

President backs Lutnick after treasury secretary Scott Bessent blamed commerce department for delays in blocking advance AI chip exports

Donald Trump also defended his commerce secretary Howard Lutnick today, after he came under fire for allegedly slow-walking export restrictions on Nvidia chips to China. This comes after the company’s CEO, Jensen Huang, was ousted from his role following a revelation that he had a close relationship with Alice Huang (no relation), allegedly a Chinese operative.

Lutnick is now set to testify before Congress about delays by the commerce department in adding Singapore-based Megaspeed, which is associated with Alice Huang and has bought billions of dollars of Nvidia chips in recent years, to Washington’s blacklist of companies that the US considers a risk to national security despite reported concerns within the administration.

Today, Trump justified any delayed action by Lutnick, adding that he’s done a “very good” job. “If you didn’t delay it, if you did do something, you could have a blow up with China. We’re having a very good relationship with China right now,” Trump said.

Lutnick is currently traveling with the president and other senior officials and cabinet members to Manila.

Meanwhile, Scott Bessent, Trump’s treasury secretary, said in an intervie with CNBC that he has “for months” been in favor of putting Megaspeed on the blacklist – but Lutnick’s commerce department “has the pen”.

“You will have to ask them about their internal process,” he added on Friday.

Today, Trump said he understood both officials’ perspectives.

“One wants to be friendly with China and one wants to have a blowup with China. They both are acceptable but they are different views,” the president told reporters.

A federal appeals court heard arguments today in Donald Trump’s hush money case, as the president tries to see the case shifted to federal court where he could have it dismissed on grounds of immunity.

The hearing concerned a May 2024 ruling that found Trump guilty on 34 felony counts of falsifying business records to obscure a $130,000 payment to adult film star Stormy Daniels.

Earlier this month, Trump appealed the ruling in state court. But in an effort to see the charges dismissed his legal team also took the case to federal court. Today’s hearing was before a three-judge panel of the 2nd US Circuit Court of Appeals.

It’s unclear how likely the president is to be successful. Trump asked the 2nd Circuit to step in after a federal judge in August ruled against his bid to see the case transferred to federal court for a third time.

California has sued OpenAI over a July cybersecurity incident where the artificial intelligence system hacked into the company Hugging Face.

In the lawsuit, filed in San Francisco Superior Court today, California attorney general Rob Bonta alleges that OpenAI misled and endandgered the public by knowingly developing advanced AI models without necessary safeguards, and called the AI company a “public nuisance” under state law. The lawsuit asks a judge to order the company to change its business practices.

An OpenAI spokesperson told the Associated Press that the lawsuit appears to single out their company and added, “Since the Hugging Face incident, we’ve undertaken an extensive and transparent effort to understand and address misaligned model activity.”

The Pentagon has failed a financial audit for the ninth year in a row, according to an inspector general report released today. The news comes as the Trump administration is seeking a record $1.5tn for defense spending.

The Defense Department was first audited in 2018 and given 10 years to pass an annual audit. The Pentagon’s vast bureaucracy utilizes 149 financial management systems, many of which are outdated and include technology built on 50-year-old computer code.

Despite failing the audit, defense department CFO Jules Hurst told ⁠Reuters a majority of its units passed for the first time. According to Hurst, 14 of the Pentagon’s 25 stand-alone reporting entities passed audit in the 2026 fiscal year, up from ⁠11 the prior year.

“It’s for things like improving airports and basically expanding amenities, which in a time where we’ve got massive national debt, I’m not sure expanding amenities at airports is a critical priority. But safety most definitely is a critical priority.”

Cruz noted that in July 2025, Congress approved $12.5bn to be spent over five years to upgrade air traffic control and hire more controllers.

On Friday, transportation secretary Sean Duffy first asked Congress to approve $30.1bn in upgrades, before increasing the figure to $50 bnlater that day.
