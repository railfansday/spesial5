Millwall face partial stadium closure after discriminatory chants

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Millwall, who are 16th in the Championship, have average league gates of 19,418 at The Den this season

Millwall have been ordered to play a future Championship match in front of a partially-closed stadium as punishment for fans' discriminatory chants.

The club has also been fined Â£70,000 by an independent regulatory commission, and been told another offence this season will lead to a further partial stadium closure.

The sanctions were imposed for ableist and homophobic chants, heard at a game against Charlton Athletic in January.

"These chants are discriminatory, unacceptable, and will not be tolerated at The Den or anywhere Millwall play," Millwall's managing director Mark Fairbrother said in an open letter to supporters., external

Anti-discriminatory body Kick It Out said it welcomed "greater accountability for discriminatory mass chanting".

Fairbrother said Millwall condemned the chants "without reservation".

"They do not represent this club, and the vast majority of supporters who follow Millwall home and away with passion and pride," he added.

"Incidents like this hurt all of us, but none more so than people who are directly discriminated against by ableist, homophobic or other unacceptable chanting, and we apologise to anybody that was affected, offended or impacted by the chants."

Blocks 19, 20, 47 and 48 of the Dockers Stand - the two-tiered east stand at The Den - will have to be closed for the Championship fixture against Sheffield United on 8 December.

It is the first time a club has been given this level of sanction for discriminatory chanting, with the commission saying "the club's previous disciplinary record was a significant aggravating feature".

The commission pointed to chants relating to race in a 2019 fixture against Everton, religion in a 2023 fixture away at Wigan Athletic, and sexual orientation and disability in a 2025 visit to Crystal Palace, all of which resulted in fines for the club.

"History has shown that combinations of action plans and fines have been wholly ineffective to prevent the club - through its supporters - committing breaches" of Football Association (FA) rules around discriminatory behaviour, the commission added.

The FA had charged Millwall in March for the chanting against Charlton.

The club admitted the charge, and in its submissions suggested the chants were limited to 80 to 100 individuals in the north-eastern corner of the Dockers Stand, in the blocks that will be closed as part of the punishment.

However, based on other evidence, the commission concluded the actual figure involved in the chants was "likely to have been in the hundreds, quite possibly more".

It also found that the club did not make any announcement over the stadium PA system ordering for the discriminatory chanting to stop, nor did it deploy any additional stewards or police to the areas from where the chanting was originating.

"I won't dress this up. This is a serious outcome," Fairbrother said.

"This significant sanction was given because, although this is the first charge relating to a match at The Den for many years, the commission took into account a number of incidents of discriminatory chanting involving our supporters over the past few seasons at away games.

"This is the cumulative cost of that pattern. Most importantly, the current and any future sanctions mean the next incident won't just cost the club money. It will cost supporters their seats.

"Innocent fans will be shut out of their own stadium because of someone else's behaviour, and with future sanctions likely to be tougher, that threat is very real."

In a statement, Kick It Out said it had received a record number of mass chanting reports across football last season, including racist, sexist and misogynistic, homophobic, ableist and faith-based abuse.

"Education has a key role here, both for fans, so that they know the impact and consequences of discriminatory chanting, and clubs, so they can proactively help to reduce its prevalence," the statement added.

"We stand ready to support football in helping eradicate this problem."
