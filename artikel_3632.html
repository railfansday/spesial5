Rob Lowe Claps Back at Criticism of His Choice to Work With Ben Shapiro

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Rob Lowe is standing by his decision to work with Ben Shapiro — despite attracting some backlash for his call.

Speaking on the “Second Thought” podcast this week, Lowe, 62, defended starring in Confession, a psychological historical drama written by Shapiro, who is known for his right-wing political views.

Lowe insisted that he took on the project because he was more interested in the script than the politics surrounding its writer.

“It’s a really, really, really interesting assessment of not only where our world is, but where Hollywood is as well,” Lowe said on the podcast. “So, I read the script. It was written by Ben Shapiro. I called Ben and said, ‘This is fantastic.’”

Lowe said Shapiro initially asked whether he knew a leading man in his 50s who wouldn’t be intimidated by the idea of working with him. Lowe was quick to volunteer himself.

“And I go, ‘Yeah, me,’” Lowe recalled telling Shapiro, adding that he was comfortable taking on the role.

Lowe continued, “And he goes, ‘You’re good with it?’ I said, ‘I’ve worked with the most left-wing writers in the world. Why wouldn’t I also work with the most right-wing writers in the world?’”

Confession, which is directed by Václav Marhoul, follows the interrogation of a prisoner in a Stalin-era Russian jail. The film also stars Nick Nolte, James Franco and Emile Hirsch.

The film premiered at the Warsaw Film Festival in October, then received a one-night-only theatrical release on November 11. Confession then dropped on The Daily Wire’s streaming platform the same day.

During his podcast appearance, Lowe also expressed frustration that the movie failed to secure a wider festival run.

“And so, we make the movie, and the movie is what it is. It’s a movie,” he said. “Look, it is challenging. It is a tough watch, but I see the movies that are celebrated out there. I see the movies that are picked — we got turned down by every single film festival, summarily frog marched out of any conversation.”

Lowe went on to suggest that political considerations were behind the film’s rejection, saying, “I see what they’re picking up, and it’s all political, right?”

He added, “The thought would be we’d play in a film festival, we would get some buzz — never happened. And then you know, Netflix would pick it up or this one would pick. Crickets. Absolute crickets.”

After the film struggled to find a home, Lowe explained that he was happy that Daily Wire eventually became Confession’s distributor.

“I’m like, ‘Hell yeah!’” Lowe said. “I want people to see this movie. And good for them for putting it out.”
