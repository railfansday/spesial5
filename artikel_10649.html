Former Call of Duty producer says older games were “more fun” with less SBMM

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Former Call of Duty producer Mark Rubin has criticized skill-based matchmaking, saying it makes casual matches feel repetitive and leaves players with little sense of how much they’ve improved.

Rubin spent a decade at Infinity Ward, where he was a producer on Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare and the original Modern Warfare 2, before serving as executive producer on Modern Warfare 3 and Ghosts. He then had the same role on Ubisoft’s XDefiant.

That shooter famously removed skill-based matchmaking, or SBMM, to a minimum, meaning ability wasn’t considered when creating lobbies. This is something that’s been a long-running complaint for Call of Duty fans, and it’s carried on into Modern Warfare 4.

Now, Rubin has doubled down on his criticism of the feature.

In a post on X, he argued that constantly facing opponents of a similar ability makes matches feel repetitive, while removing the need to improve.

“SBMM means you keep playing the same game against the same people (ie people who play the same way and the skill as you even if they are different players),” he explained. “So the games lack diversity especially when the constant challenge keeps players playing the meta instead of diversifying their loadouts.

“You also never feel yourself get better because the games are always the same difficulty. As you get better so does everyone in your lobbies.”

He went on to acknowledge that SBMM is ideal for Ranked Play, but argued that public matches should be more varied.

“Generally there will be some people better than you and some worse,” he added. “And for me that is what made older CoDs more fun because SBMM was minimal.”

In a follow-up post, Rubin explained how he’d make tougher matchups feel more rewarding for less skilled players.

“Something I wanted to do is reward players differently based on skill. If you’re lower skilled and you kill a better player you should get rewarded for that.”

This comes as Infinity Ward faces backlash over the SBMM matchmaking in Modern Warfare 4, but there’s currently no word on whether it will be addressed in a future update.
