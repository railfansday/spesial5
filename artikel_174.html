Trump’s justice department warns Elizabeth Warren over Democrats’ plans for future investigations – live

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Letter to likely chair of Senate Banking Committee is latest warning to Democrats about their potential plans after the party’s midterms success

Hello and welcome to the US politics live blog.

Senator Elizabeth Warren, the likely incoming chair of the Senate Banking Committee, was warned by the US Department of ⁠Justice of potential “significant legal consequences” if she fails ⁠to preserve records related to ⁠investigations into ​president Donald Trump.

It is the latest warning sent by the justice department to congressional Democrats about their potential plans to investigate the ⁠administration or Trump associates, Reuters reported.

Trump last week ordered the department to look into Democratic lawmakers for “threatening” people, and US attorney-general Todd Blanche subsequently indicated that ⁠his agency was prepared to battle congressional Democrats.

In a letter to Warren, US attorney Aaron ​Reitz in Houston told the Massachusetts Democrat ‌she appeared “eager to wield your ‌future gavel like a tomahawk, hunting for the scalps of president Trump’s private associates”.

Reitz told ‌her to preserve materials “relevant to your intent to weaponize congressional investigations and deprive citizens of their core constitutional rights”.

Notices to preserve records can presage investigations. Warren’s office did not immediately respond to a request for comment. Reitz shared the letter on social media and Trump reposted it to his Truth Social account several hours later.

Warren and other Democrats have signaled ‌that when their party takes control of Congress in January, they plan to investigate large corporations, White House chief of staff Stephen Miller and Elon ​Musk, the world’s richest person, among others.

Donald Trump is preparing to depart for Asia on Monday, turning his focus to economic deals and historic three-way nuclear talks with Russia and China as his administration adjusts to a new political reality in Washington. Trump’s departure comes after the often-omnipresent president had remained largely out of public sight for the past several days.

Democratic US senator Chris Van Hollen on Sunday escalated his bid to lead his party’s incoming Senate majority rather than Chuck Schumer, asserting that he has a path to victory amid a dedicated campaign that includes a series of television appearances. Van Hollen, a Maryland progressive, appeared on Sunday on CNN, ABC and Fox News as he continues to make his case for why his peers in the Senate should reject Schumer, who has led the Democratic caucus for nearly a decade.

US air travel disruptions continued for a third day with more than 10,000 flights affected on Sunday, but transportation secretary Sean Duffy predicted a return to normal by Monday even as airlines scrambled to get back on schedule following a systemwide ground stop.

After recently becoming the first Mississippi Democrat elected to the US Senate in more than four decades, Scott Colom pledged to try to ensure Trump “follows through on the commitments” he made while successfully running for a second presidency in 2024. Colom, while being interviewed by CNN host Dana Bash on Sunday, alluded to various campaign promises made by Trump – including lowering consumer prices, halting the seemingly endless wars waged under other presidential administrations, and fully releasing files related to the prosecution of Jeffrey Epstein, the late accused sex trafficker whose social circle encompassed the US and global elite.

One quick note on the US Senate race in Alaska. According to the state’s election laws, if no candidate manages to cross the 50% threshold, that triggers a ranked-choice run-off where the candidate with fewest votes is eliminated but their vote goes to their second choice (which they mark on their ballot). This happens in rounds until the candidate with the most votes wins.

Donald Trump will begin his day in Washington, before traveling to Manila, Philippines for the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (Asean) summit.

Trump’s first order or business will be his first meeting with the “Quad” alliance since he returned to the White House last year. This group includes Indian prime minister Narendra Modi, Australian prime minister Anthony Albanese, and Sanae Takaichi, the Japanese prime minister.

Then, later this week, Trump will take part in the first trilateral summit between China’s Xi Jinping and Russian president Vladimir Putin.

Democrat Mary Peltola is leading Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan in the nail-biting race for US Senate in Alaska.

With 97% of the ballots counted, Peltola – a former congresswoman – has 49.9% of the vote and only needs more than 50% to win.

At present, Sullivan only has 46% of the vote. This would be the Democrats’ ninth flip of the midterms cycle and the first time the party has won a US Senate since 2009.

On Sunday Trump continued to blast Ana Reyes, the federal judge appointed by Joe Biden, who has temporarily blocked construction on the historic Washington public golf course that the president wants to turn it ⁠into a championship-level course.

“Why would a judge want to delay taking a terrible and very unsafe golf course, one that is mocked all over D.C.(and far beyond!), instead of quickly turning it into a great and admired one?” Trump wrote on Truth Social, noting that his administration plans to appeal the ruling. “Time is of the essence, or golf seasons, jobs, prestige, and much money, will be lost!”

US air travel was slowly returning to normal on Monday after three days of widespread disruption following an extraordinary no-takeoff order issued by US aviation authorities.

The ground stop issued on Friday afternoon was only the second time such a directive had been issued nationwide in the busy US airspace since planes were grounded following the 9/11 attacks and was blamed by the Trump administration on a faulty upgrade to communications equipment.

On Monday morning, more than 250 US-related flights – those traveling within, into or out of the United States – scheduled for the same day were cancelled, according to industry tracker FlightAware.

That marked a dramatic shift from the more than 2,300 flights cancelled on Sunday.

There were delays to more than 700 US-related flights scheduled for Monday, compared to more than 10,000 the previous day, AFP reported

A progressive and onetime presidential candidate, senator Elizabeth Warren is a vocal Wall Street critic and has repeatedly condemned the Trump administration and its policies.

She is the top Democrat on the Senate Banking Committee, and is widely expected to become chair when Democrats ⁠take over Congress, Reuters reported.

The US Department of ⁠Justice on Sunday warned her of potential “significant legal consequences” if she fails ⁠to preserve records related to ⁠investigations into Trump.

Reitz’s letter to Warren cited her statements on investigating corporate leaders. “The ​context sends a strong ​smoke signal: you intend to target private ​individuals close to president Trump,” he wrote.

Other Democratic lawmakers who have ​received similar letters include ‌senators Richard Blumenthal ​and Elissa Slotkin ​as well as representative Robert Garcia, who is in line to lead the House Oversight Committee.

Political experts and Trump’s opponents have raised concerns about efforts by the Republican president and his administration to target their own critics and political opponents.

Donald Trump is embarking on his first overseas trip since his Republican party’s thumping midterm repudiation by US voters, in what may herald a renewed focus on foreign policy for the remainder of his presidency.

The US president will meet his Chinese and Russian counterparts, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin, for their first trilateral summit, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (Apec) forum in the Chinese city of Shenzhen. Before that, he will land in Manila in the Philippines on Tuesday for the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (Asean) summit.

At the Asean summit, Trump is expected to sign a trade agreement with Thailand, push deals with other countries and lobby fellow leaders to favour the US over China on artificial intelligence.

He will also take part in the first leaders’ meeting of the Quad partnership – consisting of the US, Japan, India and Australia – since he returned to the White House in January 2025.

White House officials said the president would be accompanied to Apec by Elon Musk, the Tesla and SpaceX entrepreneur who spent almost $500m (£380m) unsuccessfully supporting Republican candidates in this month’s midterms. The reason for Musk’s presence has not been explained.

The China leg of the trip will take place amid the continuing fallout from the Nvidia spying scandal, which prompted the company’s chief executive, Jensen Huang, to resign amid revelations he had had an affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy.

Trump is under pressure to raise the matter with Xi when they hold a bilateral meeting on the sidelines of Apec.

The US president has said he does not plan to bring up the subject; administration officials insist he will not do so and that the meeting will be dedicated to implementing a trade agreement between the two superpowers.

Democratic US senator Chris Van Hollen on Sunday escalated his bid to lead his party’s incoming Senate majority rather than Chuck Schumer, asserting that he has a path to victory amid a dedicated campaign that includes a series of television appearances.

Van Hollen, a Maryland progressive, appeared on Sunday on CNN, ABC and Fox News as he continues to make his case for why his peers in the Senate should reject Schumer, who has led the Democratic caucus for nearly a decade.

On CNN’s State of the Union, he reaffirmed that he believes he has a path to victory, citing support and a widespread openness to change within the Senate Democratic caucus.

“This really is a response to the call of the voters, who clearly rejected Donald Trump and his awful policies, but they haven’t fully hired the Democrats,” Van Hollen said, alluding to how the US president’s Republican party lost control of Congress during the 3 November midterm elections.

“They’ve given us a chance, and I think we need to earn their trust. And I think that begins by not going back to business as usual.”

Van Hollen criticized reports that Schumer’s team may move the Senate caucus leadership election date earlier than 1 December. He contended that the caucus should have a debate about the group’s direction as the Democrats assume control of Congress in January for the second half of Trump’s second presidency.
