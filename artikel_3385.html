CBS’s Forgotten ‘NCIS’ Action Series Replacement Doesn’t Have a Single Bad Episode 17 Years Later

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

NCIS is one of the biggest television franchises in CBS history. The network still airs the flagship series, which recently reached its milestone 500th episode, along with the spin-offs NCIS: Origins and NCIS: New York. These spin-offs add healthy variety to the NCIS franchise: the former focuses on the early days of Mark Harmon's Gibbs, while the latter and NCIS: Los Angeles center on the adventures of NCIS agent Sam Hanna (LL Cool J). Some argue that NCIS set the stage for military thrillers like Reacher and The Terminal List, but another series followed in this franchise's footsteps in the early 2000s.

That series is The Unit, created by famed playwright David Mamet. True to its name, The Unit focuses on a group of soldiers operating within the titular "Unit" — or "Delta Force," as it's most commonly called. In addition to having Mamet and Shawn Ryan (The Night Agent) as producers, The Unit features a top-tier ensemble cast, including Dennis Haysbert as field leader Jonas, Max Martini as his second-in-command Mack, and Robert Patrick as commanding officer Thomas Ryan. It also featured enough action and stakes to rival, or even exceed, what NCIS brought to the table.

The genesis of The Unit lies in a meeting that David Mamet had with former soldier Eric Haney, who previously served in Delta Force. Haney wrote about his experiences in the novel Inside Delta Force: The Story of America's Elite Counterterrorist Unit, and said that part of the reason why CBS eventually greenlit a series based on his book was that then-president Les Moonves loved the blend of military action and personal drama that shaped the characters' lives. Haney also chose a television format because he didn't like how war movies tend to handle the act of war.

“One of my purposes for writing the book is I was so disgusted with the normal Hollywood portrayal. It was cartoonish, one-dimensional. It’s the Arnold Schwarzenegger, Sylvester Stallone film portrayals of this Neanderthal-like person who speaks in monosyllables and slaughters the whole world and there’s no repercussions, there’s no cost involved.”

The Unit wasn't afraid to show the darker side of the missions Jonas and his crew underwent. Not only do these soldiers have to undertake rescue missions and assassinations, but they also have to keep the details of said missions secret, since no one can know about their operations. This takes a toll on the soldiers—especially in their married lives, since their spouses are often left in the dark. In fact, part of the series' drama lies in the dynamic between Jonas and his wife Molly (Regina Taylor); he deeply loves her, but his commitment to the military often drives a wedge between the duo. There's a reason why the series was initially dubbed "Desperate Housewives meets G.I Joe."

While The Unit lasted four seasons, it was nearly canceled in its prime because of the 2007 Writers' Guild strike. That led to Season 3 being cut in half, and Season 4 dealing with scheduling issues since it was competing for attention with football season. Rising costs also meant that CBS wouldn't be able to produce a fifth season, which would have seen a new generation of soldiers signing onto the Unit. Ironically, part of the reason The Unit gained such a strong following is that it often aired after NCIS. Both series shared a military background and a strong ensemble cast, making them a major draw in CBS's stable of procedurals.

Despite the issues surrounding its back half, The Unit ended on a strong note, with its titular group of soldiers stopping a terrorist plot and one of their members getting married. While NCIS remains the strongest franchise in CBS's lineup, The Unit proved you couldhit all the same notes while delivering its own unique mix of action and drama.
