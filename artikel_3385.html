Sharon Horgan had a string of British hits (including a couple we'll talk about here), before scoring stated successes with Divorce, Shining Vale, and Bad Sisters. She's quickly rising in the ranks of TV super-producers, the acerbically funny HBO Max rom-com Youth (in which she also stars) being just the latest.

This earlier series created by and starring Sharon Horgan is a family sitcom that feels far more believable than most, seeing Irish primary school teacher Sharon (Horgan) hooking up with American ad exec Rob (Rob Delaney) over the course of a week, only to discover that she's pregnant once he returns home. They don't really have feelings for each other, but decide to give it a go at being a couple, eventually falling into marriage just before the birth of their child. There are lots of jokes and plenty of acerbic dialogue, but this isn't Married... with Children. Sharon and Rob can be a bit nasty to each other and to their sloppy friend group, but there's also something rather sweet in the show's conviction that having someone to be a mess with can be one of life's great joys. Stream Catastrophe on Prime Video.

A little more of a high-concept here, but nevertheless an effective show about reimagining yourself in middle age. Younger follows Liza Miller (Sutton Foster), a recently divorced woman in her 40s who finds that age is a barrier to reentering the publishing industry she left years earlier. After a compliment convinces her that she could pass for a younger woman (poor thing), she manages to convince the right people that she's just 26 in order to land an entry-level job. Seven seasons of misadventures ensue, but much of the show revolves around the twisty-turny relationship between Liza and Josh (Nico Tortorella), a tattoo artist in his twenties. Stream Younger on Netflix.

Bridget Everett stars as Sam Miller, who struggles to find her new direction after moving back to her hometown to care for her dying sister. (Don't worry: There's plenty of comedy in the drama, and it's not as heavy as it sounds.) She's solidly middle-aged and starting over, kinda—making new friends in a familiar environment where she has to confront the past and the future alike. Luckily, her love of singing—and a community of goofy oddballs—are there to help. It's another story of a person of a certain age, trying to rebuild their life following an upheaval. Stream Somebody Somewhere on HBO Max.

When all else fails: Start a punk band. This drama from Sally Wainwright (Gentleman Jack) sees five women start a punk band as a lark, just a fun way to kill some time by entering a local talent context. The women realize very quickly, though, that they're very, very pissed off—about the workplace for women, menopause, a diminished dating pool, caring for aging parents and grown children—all of it tied up in the ways in which we treat women, and particularly middle-aged women. The band is a way to reclaim their voices, which doesn't erase or negate any of their problems nor drama (and there's plenty here), but gives them a platform with which to make some noise about it. Stream Riot Women on Britbox.

More of a traditional sitcom, not that there's anything wrong with that, this one builds a lot of goodwill with the performance of Leanne Morgan, its lead and co-creator (alongside Susan McMartin and Chuck Lorre). She plays the show's lead: a Knoxville, Tennessee wife and mom, who finds herself suddenly single in her 50s after her husband of 33 years leaves her. It's a genial, old-school hang-out show with a phenomenal veteran supporting cast that includes Kristen Johnston, Ryan Stiles, and Tim Daly. Stream Leanne on Netflix.

Michele Buteau, who also co-created the series) stars here as a fashion-forward stylist who'd devoted herself to the wrong guy for way too long. As the series opens, Mavis Beaumont is ready for a fresh start, putting herself and her career first—which does not preclude falling into a number of romantic entanglements. As she says more than once, she's going to take on the fashion world with a "body-positive attitude, cute v-neck, and some lip-gloss." Stream Survival of the Thickest on Netflix.

Certainly the goofiest take here on the idea of starting over in mid-life, this is very much a traditional three-camera sitcom, albeit one with a stacked cast of TV legends. Writer Melanie (Valerie Bertinelli), beautician Joy (Jane Leeves), and soap diva Victoria (Wendie Malick) wind up spending a wild night in Cleveland after a plane to Paris is diverted, discovering in the process that, while they're seen as over-the-hill back in Los Angeles, they're all Ohio 10s—though the sassy caretaker of their house, Elka (Betty White), might not agree. Stream Hot in Cleveland on Paramount+.

She'a s hair younger than Alex, but Fleabag (she's only ever referred to as "Fleabag") is trying to rebuild her life after a bad breakup, with extremely mixed results. This very dark comedy-drama follows a free-spirited, deeply angry single young woman living in London and sharing her romantic ups and downs via confessional asides to us, the audience. She falls, rather reluctantly, for "The Priest" (Andrew Scott)—while she's a confirmed atheist, and he's obviously not, the show doesn't turn aside from the general messiness of the situation. Phoebe Waller-Bridge won separate Emmys as the star, creator, and writer of the series. Stream Fleabag on Prime Video.

Maisel was one of Prime’s first truly buzzy original series, a comedy-drama from Amy Sherman-Palladino (Gilmore Girls) about the title’s Midge Maisel (Rachel Brosnahan), a long-time New York housewife of the late 1950s who discovers a talent for stand-up comedy. Inspired by the real-life careers of comedians like Totie Fields and Joan Rivers, the show is warm and welcoming, with great performances and dialogue; it also achieves something rare in being a show about comedy that’s actually funny. Stream The Marvelous Mrs. Maisel on Prime Video.

The big life change in this earlier Sharon Horgan hit is a little different: Julia Johnstone's mother had been a big help in taking care of Julia's children, but she's not going to do it anymore, leaving this working mom (Anna Maxwell Martin) to take the full brunt of childcare for the first time (her husband in the picture, but not much use). She joins up with other working moms involved with a local support group, including shallow Amanda (Lucy Punch), star of her own ongoing spin-off. Stream Motherland on Prime Video and Britbox.
