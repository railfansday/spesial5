The fifth season of Skate will be its last as developer Full Circle looks to try something new.

When you purchase through links on our site, we may earn an affiliate commission. Here’s how it works.

A little over a year after EA launched Skate into early access—where it remains—developer Full Circle is removing the seasonal structure that has underpinned the game so far, saying that doing so will give the team "the freedom to launch features earlier in their lifecycle."

Skate got off to a bit of a rough start when it went live in September 2025, and while it's turned around the initially iffy response from players, it hasn't gone entirely smoothly since then. An initial promise that no new maps would be locked behind paywalls was chucked out the window in February, and Full Circle laid off employees that same month, saying that cutting headcounts means it can "better support Skate's long-term future and focus the team on the things that matter most to you."

The end of Skate's seasonal structure is less overtly not good than those moves, but it is a sign that things aren't going quite as Full Circle had envisioned, and the message that it's still "committed to keep driving Skate forward" has a certain 'despite everything' tone that doesn't strike me as the sort of thing you say when when all cylinders are firing.

"The game is still a work in progress, and we're using this valuable time to experiment, iterate, and figure out what works best for the long-term health of the game—especially when it comes to social features and progression," the studio wrote. "Ultimately, we want to build Skate in a way that lets us roll out fresh content much faster, even if it's not totally finished yet."

Season 5 will end on December 1, after which Full Circle will move to "regular, agile content updates." The end of seasons also means the end of the season pass; the studio is "rethinking how that kind of progression system works." Here's the bullet-point rundown of what's changing:

"Moving away from rigid season blocks gives the development team the freedom to launch features earlier in their lifecycle," the studio wrote. "This means we can deploy work-in-progress mechanics and gather your direct feedback to continuously shape and build upon San Vansterdam. We’ve started this with in-game testing of features such as Spot Battles, Block Parties, and Prox Chat before rolling it out officially, and we’re going to continue with this process to ensure we get YOUR feedback on what works/what doesn’t."
