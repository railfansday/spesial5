It’s a stark contrast to how GTA lives in the popular imagination of most people, which is as a chaos sim full of drugs, sex, and violent crime. GTA 6 has those things, but they are far less domineering parts of the experience this time around. It’s also completely divorced from how some people often experience modern video games, which is heavily augmented by a second-screen experience full of achievement guides, TikTok memes, and social media rage-bait.

People should play GTA 6 however they want, but I’d encourage them to take at least a few hours to experience the game in the dark with headphones on and as little knowledge as possible of what awaits them and how the whole thing works, their phones and laptops safely tucked away in a different room. Former Kotaku editor Kirk Hamilton was famous for telling people to turn off parts of the onscreen UI to make the experience more immersive and make it feel less like a chore list. Every big new open-world game that came out there would be a post about turning off the minimap.

I’ve never been able to play games that way. I’m too fidgety and squirrely for that kind of thing. But I always try to spend at least a couple hours in that headspace, treating what’s in front of me more as something worth experiencing for its own sake than just as a maze to speedrun through as efficiently as possible in exchange for in-game rewards, entertaining setpieces, and satisfying emotional climaxes. That’s an increasingly alien way of approaching anything in our algorithmically optimized world, but GTA 6 offers as good a reason as any to try it.
