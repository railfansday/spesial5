Amazon responds after delivery drone smashes into garage “like a sick bird”

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Amazon has responded after one of its delivery drones crashed into a Michigan homeowner’s garage, prompting an investigation by the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA).

The incident occurred on November 20 in Huntington Woods, Michigan, after a couple placed an order for shampoo and received a notification that their package would arrive by drone within seven minutes.

Amazon has been expanding its Prime Air delivery service across the US, aiming to reach nearly 500 cities and towns by the end of 2026. However, several crashes involving its drones have attracted the attention of federal regulators.

Speaking to WXYZ 7 News Detroit, Jim Brennan explained that the drone successfully dropped the package into his backyard before striking a fiber-optic cable and losing one of its propellers.

The aircraft then veered sideways before becoming trapped between his neighbor’s garage roof and nearby power lines.

“Like a sick bird, it tried to go up, it tried to get out of here,” Brennan said.

Logan Coomes, whose garage was struck, said an Amazon Prime Air team arrived within 10 minutes of the crash and worked with local utility workers to remove the drone by early evening.

The company reportedly offered to help repair the damaged roof and gave Coomes a $100 Amazon gift card.

In a statement shared with CNN, an Amazon spokesperson said its Prime Air drones conduct nearly 6,000 flights per day and have delivered “hundreds of thousands of packages this year.”

Amazon also said the incident marked the first crash of its kind involving its drone delivery operations near Detroit.

The FAA published a preliminary incident notification on November 23, confirming that it was investigating the crash.

“UAS struck a fiber cable, a residential garage, and became entangled in power lines during takeoff,” the agency said.

The National Transportation Safety Board is also gathering information but has not yet decided whether to launch a separate investigation.

Amazon’s battery-powered MK30 delivery drones are equipped with technology designed to detect and avoid obstacles and can carry packages weighing up to five pounds.

The Michigan crash follows several other incidents involving Prime Air. In October 2025, two Amazon delivery drones crashed into a construction crane in Arizona, while another struck an internet cable in Texas the following month.

The FAA has also been investigating a separate crash involving an apartment building in Richardson, Texas, in February 2026.
