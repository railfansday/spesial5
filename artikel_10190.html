Review: The Alexa Tablet 12 Pro is an iPad alternative I actually want to use

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

I knew Amazon’s new flagship tablet was different when I found myself actually reaching for it.

Of course, you might be saying, that’s the job of a reviewer: to use the device throughout your daily life and let people know the good, the bad, and the ugly. But with the Alexa Tablet 12 Pro, I wasn’t just reaching for it when I thought a tablet might do the job. Over the past two weeks since receiving my review unit (the base nanomatte model), I’ve been pleasantly surprised by just how much I’ve preferred it over other devices. Hell, I’ve been reaching for Amazon’s slate over my Pixel 11 Pro XL, my MacBook Air, and even the pocket-friendly Lenovo Legion tablet I reviewed earlier this month, and considering this company’s history with tablets, I’m as shocked as anyone.

The original Kindle Fire launched back when I was in high school, and despite 15 years of product name changes, frequent model refreshes, and an attempt to slowly move up-market, even Amazon’s bottom-of-the-barrel pricing couldn’t make up for missing apps and terrible performance. But the Fire lineup is dead, replaced by a new lineup of more expensive “Alexa Tablets” that, nevertheless, remain a relative bargain in our ever-worsening gadget economy. This model, though, is a true iPad competitor. The Alexa Tablet 12 Pro attempts to correct every mistake Amazon has made with its tablet series over the past fifteen years, and somehow, for as low as $350 on sale, it basically pulls it all off.

Let’s get some of the most important changes out of the way immediately before we get to the real star of the show. Most of Amazon’s specs sheet here won’t impress you, but context really means everything. A Mediatek Dimensity 8400 chipset paired with (in my test model) 8GB of RAM and 128GB of UFS 3.1 storage sounds like any run-of-the-mill midrange Android tablet. That’s a massive leap ahead of anything powering previous Fire Tablet models, though, and the option to move to a 256GB model with UFS 4.0 and 16GB of RAM does exist if you really feel like you need some extra power. This is also a completely unsubsidized tablet; regardless of which model you pick, you won’t have to pay $20 to remove lockscreen ads, something that remains an issue on the cheaper Alexa Tablet 8 and 11 models.

Honestly, both the design and the overall feel of the hardware exceed my expectations; this is closer to Apple or Samsung’s premium tablet offerings than it is the Fire Tablets of yesteryear. It features an aluminum chassis with rounded edges and a thin, flat frame that — let’s be honest — looks right out of Apple’s typical iPad playbook. The power button doubles as a fingerprint sensor, while an indented groove below the volume buttons on the right side for its optional stylus. As someone who thinks this slate is better off for consumption than creation, it’s the one part of the design I question, though it also didn’t really bother me.

Without a doubt, the star of the show is this display. Amazon sent me the nanomatte version, available as a $50 upgrade over the $500 MSRP base model (the $650 256GB variant only comes in nanomatte). If you’re thinking of buying this tablet, the nanomatte model is without a doubt the one to spring for. It’s one of the nicer matte finishes I’ve seen on a device like this to date, and is absolutely the main reason I won’t stop reaching for this tablet. With a couple of tweaks — the saturation levels are a little too punchy out of the box for my liking, but Amazon’s “natural” color profile is a two-tap fix — this is as good as any non-OLED screen I can think of in recent memory. In fact, where Lenovo’s LCD panel had me wishing for next year’s rumored upgrade, I really never felt like OLED needed to be on Amazon’s flagship slate to make it worth my while.

No matter what I loaded up — The Odyssey, various episodes of TV or YouTube videos, the unofficial “Dusklight” port of Twilight Princess for Android — I just kept wanting to stare at this thing. I now totally understand why people spring for the nanomatte iPad Pro, and while I’m not trying to say this LCD holds a candle to one of the best OLED screens on the market right now, it’s nice to get this sort of finish on a tablet that doesn’t start at a whopping $1,800. (The iPad Pro itself starts at $1,200, but nanomatte is only available on 1TB or 2TB models, which I didn’t know until I sat down to write this review and is absolutely one of the dumbest things I’ve ever seen a company of Apple’s size do. I digress.)

Amazon’s quad-stereo speakers sound excellent for a device of this size. They get surprisingly loud without much distortion, perfect for YouTube videos or streaming random movies, or even casual music listening. I’d still opt for headphones or external speakers for something you really care about (once again, The Odyssey being the perfect example), but that’s not exactly rare for a tablet. The fingerprint sensor is fast and mostly accurate, and the flashless 13MP rear-facing camera is… here, should you desperately need one. A more useful 8MP front-facing camera runs along the right side of the device, and there’s a single USB-C port at the bottom of the slate. No expandable storage options, something I’m genuinely disappointed about considering this slate tops out at just 256GB.

Of course, we have to address the Amazon-sized elephant in the room: the software. Unlike the “Fire OS” fork of Android that has resulted in years of Christmas morning Google searches to find out how to get YouTube on the tablet you just unwrapped, the Alexa Tablet is just… running Android. That includes some obvious quality-of-life improvements, like being able to transfer apps and other data directly from your phone or an older tablet, but yes, it also means the Play Store is here, along with practically every Android app you could hope for. Gmail, Chrome, Docs, Photos — everything’s here, and the ability to no longer have to hope that a good-enough replacement exists on the Amazon Appstore is such a relief. Hell, Amazon’s own app marketplace isn’t even pre-installed.

Now, with all of that said, there are a couple of weird software quirks you should know going in. First, you’ll likely find a handful of applications unlisted from the Play Store as “unsupported” apps. This includes Amazon’s own software — the pre-loaded Amazon app is, bizarrely, just a web wrapper for the typical browser experience — and a smattering of random third-party applications, including the launcher Lawnchair, which I installed via the APK hosted on its website. That’s the second quirk: the default launcher is, as you might expect, themed around both Amazon and Alexa, complete with two unremovable widgets. It’s far from the worst home screen experience I’ve ever had, but a third-party launcher from the Play Store (or, again, from somewhere on the web) solves that problem. Save for the rare appearance of Amazon’s own dock and Alexa search bar when swiping back home, the third-party launcher experience here is great.

One more quick software positive: Amazon says it’ll support this tablet, along with the cheaper Alexa Tablet 11, through seven years of OS upgrades and security updates. We’ll have to see how frequent those updates arrive, but still — this experience feels built to last in a way Fire Tablets explicitly were not.

The Alexa Tablet 12 Pro’s specs might not sound particularly impressive on paper, but I’ve had absolutely no performance complaints. Granted, I haven’t exactly pushed the envelope here — that Zelda shout-out is the most I’ve demanded graphically — but web pages loaded fast without crashes or reloads, apps stayed alive in the background, and moving around the OS generally felt snappy. If this bar sounds low, you probably never used a legacy Fire Tablet. Battery life, meanwhile, has been excellent, with a single charge typically getting me through a few days of mixed use. Charging speeds could be better, though, with a full charge taking nearly two hours to power the slate back up. That’s not enough reason to skip out on Amazon’s tablet, obviously, but it is one of those advantages that Samsung’s (much) more expensive Galaxy Tab S-series holds over it.

It’s possible that my excitement over Amazon’s Alexa Tablet 12 Pro comes from a place of lowered expectations. I’m sure that’s true to some extent; again, 15 years of mostly-miserable experiences, up to and including the last time the company swung for a more premium slate, will do that to you. But considering Google’s disinterest in developing its own tablets and Samsung’s increasingly-ludicrous pricing for its higher-end options, I think this Alexa Tablet delivers a great alternative. Its specs are more than enough for the average Android user, and the things that I think people really care about on tablets — its display and its speakers — are among the best you can buy right now. Unless you really want to move into a four-figure price range for an OLED-equipped iPad or Galaxy Tab, I don’t really see a reason to shop around. And believe me, I’m still shocked.

That’s especially true during Black Friday, which — despite this tablet launching just weeks prior — is deeply discounted for the holiday season. You can grab the base model Alexa Tablet 12 Pro for just $350, but you should spring for the nanomatte model at $400. That’s $150 off a price I already think isn’t terrible for the money, and frankly, an even stronger recommend if and when you can score it on sale.
