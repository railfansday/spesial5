Ether ETFs Approach an Unwanted Record After Seven Weeks of Outflows

US spot Ether exchange-traded funds are edging toward an uncomfortable milestone. The category has now recorded seven consecutive weeks of net withdrawals, placing it one additional negative week away from matching the longest losing streak identified in the current dataset. That is an especially awkward backdrop for Ethereum supporters who see exchange-traded products as a bridge between the network and traditional investment accounts.

The withdrawal trend does not tell us that institutions have abandoned Ethereum, nor does it measure how much activity is occurring on the blockchain. It does indicate that ETF demand has been persistently soft during a period of broader market stress.

The Block's review of SoSoValue figures put net withdrawals from US spot Ether ETFs at about $398.9 million for the week ending November 13. That marked a seventh consecutive weekly outflow, with cumulative withdrawals of roughly $2.42 billion since late September. Another negative week would tie the eight-week losing streaks the category has previously experienced.

A single week's figure can be influenced by a handful of large transactions, but seven weeks is long enough to warrant closer examination. NewsBTC's report on the November 9 Ether ETF outflow captured one daily episode; the accumulated weekly data reveals how the pressure has persisted beyond that session.

Owning an Ether ETF share and using Ethereum are fundamentally different activities. A fund investor primarily seeks financial exposure to ETH's price, subject to the product's particular structure and fees. A blockchain user might be paying transaction charges, supplying liquidity, issuing a token or building an application. The two can influence one another over time, but neither provides a complete picture of the other.

For this reason, it would be a mistake to use fund outflows as evidence that smart contracts are no longer being used. A better test of network health also considers transactions, fees, applications, security and adoption. Those indicators require interpretation too, particularly as activity shifts across Ethereum's scaling ecosystem.

The Ether figures arrived in a week when spot Bitcoin ETFs also lost more than $1 billion, and other crypto fund categories largely experienced withdrawals. That broad pattern suggests investors may be reducing digital-asset exposure generally rather than rotating decisively from ETH into BTC. It is a different story from one in which capital leaves one token product but pours into another.

There is still an important distinction between price losses and fund flows. ETH's falling market value can reduce ETF assets without any redemption, while net withdrawals reflect investor activity through the fund structure. Keeping those measures separate prevents the discussion from exaggerating the selling actually revealed by the data.

The network's DeFi ecosystem continues to evolve, including proposals over how protocols capture fees and share economic value. Aave's recent reserve-factor discussion is one example of the operational debates taking place independently of fund investor sentiment.

For the next trading week, investors will watch whether Ether funds can interrupt the run of negative flows and whether the change is broad or concentrated in one vehicle. A positive week would not erase the preceding losses, but it would be a measurable sign that some buyers are reconsidering. Until then, the ETF channel remains an obvious pressure point for Ethereum's market narrative.
