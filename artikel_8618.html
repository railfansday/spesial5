Set up your home filing system with a folder for each length of time you have to keep papers, from bills you can toss next month to records you keep for life. Sorting by how long a paper stays, not who sent it, means filing takes seconds and you always know what’s safe to shred.

The IRS says most tax records can go three years after you file, and a few after six or seven years. Records tied to property you own stay until three years after you file the return for the year you sell it.

Sell a house after 15 years for a profit bigger than what the home sale exclusion lets you keep tax-free, and receipts from a remodel in year two cut your tax bill. They add to your basis, what you paid for the home plus improvements, which comes off the sale price before the profit is taxed. Shredding everything older than three years would have destroyed them a decade before the sale.

Set up a file box or drawer with these folders.

Drop new papers in an inbox tray, and file or shred them once a week.

Scan what you can to save space, but hold on to originals of anything a copy can’t replace, like a car title and the permanent folder’s records. Social Security, for one, won’t accept a photocopy of the birth certificate you use to claim retirement benefits.

The week you file each spring, shred every past-years envelope whose time is up.
