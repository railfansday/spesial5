Gmail Users Vent Anger As Google Retires Gmailify and Disrupts Years of Email Habits

Gmail users who have spent years managing multiple email addresses from one inbox are pushing back as Google prepares to retire Gmailify and end automatic mail collection on the web.

I just learned of the changes when I removed an email account while troubleshooting Gmail and couldn’t add it back. I used to handle all my email in one place. Now I’m juggling three separate email clients, and keeping on top of my inboxes has become a nightmare.

Google says both features will be fully removed in January 2027. The company is already restricting new setups, leaving some users unable to add an account before the final cutoff.

The frustration is plain in Google’s own Gmail Help Community, where one user called the decision a “huge mistake” and described relying on Gmail to manage personal, work, school and nonprofit email in one browser window.

On Reddit, one user said they had used Gmail to collect messages from two accounts for almost 20 years before having to change their setup. Another wrote that the move “completely fucks up my email workflow.”

Google introduced Gmailify in February 2016, giving people with Yahoo Mail and Hotmail or Outlook.com addresses access to Gmail’s spam protection and inbox categories while keeping their existing addresses.

A separate setting, “Check mail from other accounts,” let Gmail pull messages from external mailboxes using a protocol known as POP.

Those messages arrived in the main Gmail inbox, where users could search and organise them alongside their Gmail messages.

Google is also retiring the ability to use “Send as” with email addresses hosted outside Google. For someone who used Gmail to receive and reply to messages from a business address, that removes both halves of the same workflow.

Google says the features require a disproportionate amount of maintenance. It recommends automatic forwarding for people who still want incoming messages from another provider to appear in their Gmail inbox. Messages already imported will remain there.

To reply from an outside address, users can open that provider’s website, set up a desktop mail client or add the account directly to the Gmail mobile app. Each option changes where users handle mail they previously dealt with in a single Gmail browser tab.

That’s the disruption users are describing in the forums. After years of opening Gmail to read an email, find an old conversation and reply from the right address, some will now have to do that work across two places.
