5 Black Friday Truths About Gen Z Shoppers and the Mall

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Welcome to Black Friday—the start of the most critical five days of the year for retail brands.

According to Circana, 43% of Americans will do most of their holiday shopping between today and Monday, and nearly a quarter of them will do most of it today, full stop.

This is also the day that brands are focused on Gen Z, because 94% of them plan to shop this week, according to Deloitte, and they’ll spend an average of $752—more than any other age cohort.

It’s a given that many consumers aged 14-29 will spend that money online. But a lot of shopping is also going to happen at the place Gen X made famous: the mall.

You’ll find plenty of zoomers in malls today, in fact, but they’re not all spending the way you think, and some aren’t there to shop at all. ADWEEK waded through a stack of recent market studies and tapped the shoulders of a few experts to plumb Gen Z’s current relationship with the mall and what it means for Black Friday 2026.

Gen X might have been the original mall rats in the 1980s, but today’s 18-29 age group has picked up the mantle.

According to Coresight research released in April, 48% of Gen Z consumers say they’ve hit the mall in the last two weeks. A recent Ipsos survey found that shoppers aged 18-34 were twice as likely as those 55 and up to shop at the mall. And according to a Stagwell study released this year, Gen Z not only spends more time at the mall on a per-visit basis, they also spend $296 per visit on average, the most of any age group.

Gen Z mall affinity seems strongest with women, too. A 2026 study of female zoomers’ shopping activity conducted by snack maker Sunnie and Westfield Rise found that 86% of them praised the mall for giving them “energy they can’t get digitally.”

The reasons why Gen Z likes the mall have as much to do with what the mall is as what it’s not—a small screen. Mall shopping represents a rebound effect in retail form, a response to hours of isolated scrolling.

Coresight’s work states it plainly. “Gen Z has grown up with frictionless digital commerce as a baseline expectation,” researchers wrote, “yet precisely because of this ubiquity, they increasingly seek the tangible, social and experiential dimensions of physical retail.”

It’s worth noting that experts are also starting to see the same dynamic unfolding with Gen Alpha (aged 13 and under), whose spending power and influence is already approaching $100 billion, per Charles Schwab.

“A trip to the mall with friends can be an important first taste of autonomy,” Helenor Gilmour, head of marketing research firm Beano Brain, told ADWEEK. “That shared experience is a powerful part of the appeal of shopping in stores. It is why we expect younger shoppers to embrace shopping events like Black Friday as a reason to visit malls together.”

Brieane Olson, CEO of PacSun, is well positioned to observe the evolving shopping habits of Gen Z. The brand sells its apparel online, in freestanding stores, and at mall locations. It’s easy to assume that a generation that grew up with ecommerce would stick to what they know; or they might defect because they’re tired of it. But the truth lay in between.

“We’re continuing to see just how important physical retail is to young consumers, and there’s a real opportunity for malls heading into Black Friday and the holiday season,” Olson told ADWEEK. “Young people aren’t choosing between digital and physical experiences. They want both, and they expect brands to create meaningful connections across each.”

With over 520 stores across 4.2 million square feet and sales north of $650 million a year, Bloomington, Minnesota’s Mall of America (MOA) is the biggest mall in the continental U.S. According to its 2026 Holiday Shopping Survey, 92% of Gen Z respondents expressed an intent to show up there today, with over half saying they planned to spend once they arrive.

Just as significantly, MOA has charted a symbiotic relationship between the online culture Gen Z consumes and their behavior when they visit.

“Social media may introduce them to a brand, trend or community, but there’s still an appeal to experiencing those interests firsthand,” said a MOA spokesperson. “We’re seeing that with our leasing strategy. Brands such as Edikted and Pop Mart, along with concepts like Bandai Namco and Kpop Nara, reflect how interests that develop online are translating into physical retail experiences.”

“Gen Z may discover what’s next online,” MOA chief business development and marketing officer Jill Renslow added, “but they still want to experience it together in the real world.”

From a brand perspective, it’s not enough that Gen Z merely visits the mall—retailers still need to woo them once they get there.

According to the International Council of Shopping Centers, 60% of Gen Z mall visitors say they go “just to socialize or meet friends.” Sunnie and Westfield North’s work found that 72% of female zoomers would still visit the mall even if it meant they couldn’t buy anything.

But there’s still a marketing opportunity here. Coresight’s study determined that social-media content creation is a big reason for mall visits, calling them “aesthetically curated spaces [that] serve as backdrops for organic and shareable moments.”

The brands that benefit most, said the study, are those that boast “visually engaging environments with Instagrammable decor.”

Robert Klara is the senior editor of brands at Adweek, specializing in the evolution and impact of brands.
