1960s grand touring car design shapes acte deux’s paris apartment renovation

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

inspired by automotive interiors and streamlined bodywork, the 65-square-meter renovation combines lacquered surfaces, polished metal and warm timber to rethink domestic space.

Acte Deux, founded by Johanna Lapray and Hugo L’ahélec, renovates a 65-square-meter apartment in the Paris’s 18th arrondissement, drawing on the streamlined forms and richly appointed interiors of 1960s grand touring cars. Named Gran Turismo, the project takes its reference from an era of automotive design associated with sculptural bodywork, polished details and comfortable interiors, translating these qualities into a domestic setting.

Located on the 10th floor of a 1960s concrete residential building, the apartment originally had standard ceiling heights and relatively constrained volumes. Rather than working against these limitations, the designers reorganize the interior around its expansive views of the French capital and the rooftops surrounding Sacré-Cœur.

Paris-based design studio Acte Deux reorganizes the living spaces into a continuous horizontal sequence. Living and dining areas extend along the main facade, where a large bay window and five additional windows frame the city panorama. The open layout makes the most of the views across Paris, bringing the surrounding city into the living spaces. A palette of natural whites, creams, browns and oak establishes a relatively restrained foundation. Against it, saturated lacquered surfaces in garnet, orange and blue introduce the more expressive language of the Gran Turismo reference. Warm, understated materials provide continuity, while glossy finishes bring movement and visual punctuation to the space.

One of the clearest references appears at floor level. An orange lacquered ribbon runs around the oak parquet, framing the floor like the pinstripe detailing found along the bodywork of vintage touring cars. The parquet itself recalls the original mosaic flooring, retaining a connection to the existing character of the apartment.

Across the living room wall, two sheets of polished chrome-like metal extend horizontally, recalling the appearance of an automotive radiator grille. In the dining area, a glossy blue lacquered table adds another concentrated burst of color, vintage chairs and a painting by Hervé Priou bring contrasting textures and references into the composition.

In the kitchen, Acte Deux pairs a matte lacquered kitchen with a Travertino Rosso worktop and a curved beige cabinet with glass portholes separates the kitchen from the adjoining spaces. Its rounded profile softens the transition between rooms, introducing a sculptural element that echoes the aerodynamic forms behind the project’s automotive inspiration.

The bathroom develops this language through a combination of stained and varnished wood, bronzed wall lacquer and black-and-white checkerboard porcelain stoneware tiles. The pattern evokes racing flags, extending the Gran Turismo narrative into a more intimate part of the apartment.

An orange corridor connects the main living area to the bedroom, creating a colorful passage between the public and private zones of the apartment. A concealed door opens the perspective toward the opposite end of the interior, revealing the cross-ventilated configuration of the house. Beyond a dressing area screened by wool curtains with green edging, the bedroom features thick carpeting, warm materials and a desk facing the park.

Across the renovation, Acte Deux uses references from automotive design as a framework for organizing a domestic environment. The project moves between the precision of industrial detailing and the familiarity of residential materials, combining polished metal, lacquer, timber and stone into a sequence of distinct but connected spaces.
