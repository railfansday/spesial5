A Minimalist, a Maximalist, and 30 Years of Friendship

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Two person shows are taking over the art world, with galleries doubling as matchmakers, mostly pairing dead artists with market darlings. But of course, nothing beats conversation between two old friends.

At Gladstone in New York this fall, that’s just what you’ll get, with a new show from British artists Sarah Lucas and Cerith Wyn Evans titled “Je Pense.”

You two have been friends since the ’90s. How’d you meet?

Cerith Wyn Evans: I never can answer questions like that because I never remember. We were probably both drunk somewhere.

CWE: I do remember hearing of you. I was close with a painter at the time, Angus Cook, and I’d ask him things like “What’s Gary Hume like?,” all this kind of stuff. Later I quipped, “We should really be talking about his girlfriend, a much better artist. She’s called Sarah Lucas.”

Angus said he had met you once: He knocked on the door and you answered—allegedly!—and he said “Hi, are you Gary?” and you replied “NO.” He told me “Oh my God, She’s incredible!”

SL: I remember meeting Angus at a party Matthew Marks gave for Gary, maybe that’s where I met you. A lot of things were happening at that time, around 1990; something was burgeoning. I met a lot of people then that became friends for life.

You can’t make new old friends! When I saw that you two were showing together, I admit my first thought was: a minimalist and a maximalist walk into a bar.

CWE: Who’s who? [laughs] I’m certainly the maximalist.

Ha. All that’s to say, at first glance, I thought of you two as having quite different sensibilities, but they really do converge beautifully in the gallery. I know you’ve wanted to show together for quite a while.

SL: We’ve kicked the notion around for a long time, but it was more of a mythic show, something we might do some day—it wasn’t about whether we ever did it or not.

CWE: I think if you really really care how another person thinks, if you don’t take them for granted, you imagine that you dialogue… but also the distance between our work allows a certain level of respect. Nancy Spector invited us, and really, the show’s a kind of hologram that Nancy has somehow… well, she’s the Wizard of Oz.

So the differences help. Okay, I have to ask about the burnt-out car in the gallery, obviously.

CWE: For me, that’s the fulcrum of the entire exhibition because it’s so brutal. It’s like a black hole in the middle of the entire thing, and a vortex that everything sucks everything else in, or spits it all out, or something. It’s phenomenal, extraordinary and also this quotidian tragedy, a very pure readymade. Sarah bought it at an auction, already burnt. I guess weird death perverts buy these things.

SL: I have no idea who would buy these things… apart from us.

Did you make the work in tandem and plan it out, or just bring things together at the end? I love how Sarah’s sculpture makes Cerith’s tower feel very phallic.

CWE: No, we’ve just always been very erotic, very horny. See, this is the maximalist in me coming out.

SL: Besides, things are always different when you get them somewhere anyway.

Have you guys been like doing studio visits and giving each other feedback over the years?

CWE: No no no. It’s organic. It’s natural. It’s spiritual. We’re not really that organized.

SL: That’s probably why we like each other. We get on because we don’t have that kind of mindset that would make us, I don’t know, strategic.

I’m just thinking about a lot of younger artists who are working hard to sustain creative community. I don’t know if you have thoughts or advice for them.

SL: No one takes advice, do they? Though I will say, younger artists should be in more group shows. When you’re young, you’re so eager to have your moment in the spotlight.

Has your friendship ever felt competitive?

SL: We just aren’t really strategic like that. I’m not a jealous person by nature. I’m just in my own world, making work.

CWE: I think it’s sad when people compare themselves and are concerned with who’s more famous or younger or richer.

Do you talk about art together then, or other stuff?

CWE: I don’t think I talk about anything else. But it depends what how you by mean “Art.” I’m obsessed by creativity more than much art with a price tag.
