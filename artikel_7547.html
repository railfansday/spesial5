Sheinbaum confirms US intelligence, drones helped locate accused drug kingpin

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Mexican government said the capture was carried out in accordance with Mexican laws as a matter of public security despite heavy U.S. pressure to arrest the accused trafficker.

Mexican Secretary of National Defense General Ricardo Trevilla Trejo said on Monday that a week before Meza Flores’ capture, the Mexican Secretariat of National Defense obtained intelligence that led to authorizing the use of U.S. drones to locate Meza Flores in Sinaloa.

Trevilla Trejo said these authorizations comply with Mexican National Security laws.

The general stated that four military personnel and 16 organized criminal members were killed in the operation that occurred in three phases on Friday night into the morning hours of Saturday.

U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche called the arrest a “combined interagency effort” that included the Homeland Security Task Force that supported this “Mexican operation to remove a leader of one of the most dangerous drug cartels in Mexico” in a Saturday Department of Justice statement about his capture.

An expert on the Sinaloa conflict highlighted U.S. involvement in the operation and questioned what could come next.

“The Mexican government has once again proven itself able to conduct high-risk, time-sensitive operations, using intelligence shared with U.S. authorities,” said David Mora, senior Mexico analyst of the International Crisis Group, in a statement shared with Courthouse News. “Chapo Isidro’s organization had a virtual hegemony in northern Sinaloa, with a strong hold over fentanyl production and trafficking. And this central role in the synthetic drug trade made him a top priority for the White House; he was among the FBI’s most wanted fugitives.

“The government seems to have been more prepared for the immediate retaliation by Chapo Isidro’s combatants than it was during past operations, such as the killing of ‘El Mencho’. But the most important question is what happens next,” said Mora. “Similar beheading operations have created power vacuums and thus more violence as internal cartel fighting spikes. Sinaloa already has major territorial disputes which could intensify. We’ll learn in the weeks ahead if the Mexican authorities have plans in place to deal with this.”
