Prediction: Ivory Coast vs Gambia

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Ivory Coast and Gambia will meet at Stade de la Paix in Bouake on Thursday, November 12, as the two early leaders in Group C continue their qualification campaigns for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations.

Both teams have collected six points from their opening two matches. Ivory Coast has done so without conceding, while Gambia arrives after scoring six goals against Somalia and Ghana.

Verdict: Over 2.5 goals Best odds: 16/25 Bookmaker: 1xBet

Ivory Coast has made a convincing start to qualifying, beating Ghana and Somalia by the same 2-0 scoreline. Those results have put the Elephants in a strong position before a pair of matches against their closest challenger.

Gambia has already won away to Ghana and has enough attacking quality to cause problems if the home side commits too many players forward.

Still, Ivory Coast has several ways to stretch the visiting defense. Amad Diallo, Nicolas Pepe and Yan Diomande give Renard options out wide, where beating the first defender could force Gambia’s central players out of position.

That should be an important part of the home side’s approach. Moving the ball quickly between the wings would make it harder for Gambia to protect the middle and give Ivory Coast opportunities to deliver into the penalty area.

The Elephants won the most recent meeting 1-0 in March 2025. Their opening qualifying results also suggest they can manage a game without allowing much at the other end.

However, Gambia’s attacking form gives this meeting a different feel. Ivory Coast may need to keep looking for goals even after taking the lead.

Renard has confirmed that Amad Diallo is fit following the injury concern that forced him off during his club’s match on October 31.

Odilon Kossounou and Guela Doue are absent from the squad. Evan Ndicka, Ousmane Diomande and Emmanuel Agbadou remain available in central defense, while Oumar Solet is among the new additions to the group.

Franck Kessie and Ibrahim Sangare offer experience in midfield, with Malick Yalcouye another option.

Further forward, Renard can choose from Amad, Pepe, Yan Diomande, Bazoumana Toure and Martial Godo, alongside central attacking options Elye Wahi and Bonny.

There is enough variety to change the attack during the match, particularly if Gambia manages to close the spaces around its penalty area.

Gambia’s opening results deserve attention. The Scorpions began with a 2-1 victory over Somalia before producing a 4-2 comeback win away to Ghana.

That second performance showed how dangerous this team can be when a match opens up. Gambia trailed 2-0 in Accra before Mohamed Badamosi and Omar Colley brought it level before halftime. Ebrima Colley then scored twice after the break to complete the turnaround.

Recovering from that position should give Johnathan McKinstry’s players confidence heading into another difficult away assignment. They have already shown that falling behind does not have to end their chances.

The defensive side of those performances is worth considering, though. Gambia conceded against Somalia and allowed Ghana to establish a two-goal lead. Similar lapses in Bouake could prove expensive.

McKinstry will probably want his midfield to stay close to the defense while leaving enough support for counterattacks. Sitting too deep for long periods would invite repeated pressure from Ivory Coast’s wide players.

There should be opportunities to break when the hosts push forward. Whether Gambia can use those moments effectively may determine how competitive this match becomes.

Gambia has been boosted by the return of Musa Barrow and Yankuba Minteh, who missed the opening two qualifiers through injury.

Their availability gives McKinstry additional attacking options alongside Ebrima Colley and Badamosi, both of whom contributed to the successful start.

However, Jesper Ceesay and Suleiman Camara have subsequently withdrawn because of injuries. Saidou Khan and Salieu Drammeh have been called up as their replacements.

Alieu Fadera is also unavailable with a thigh injury. Omar Colley offers experience in defense, while Ebou Adams and Abubakarr Barry are among the midfield options.

The returning attackers should help Gambia carry a threat beyond its own half. McKinstry will need that outlet if his team spends long stretches defending.

Ivory Coast should create enough chances to put Gambia’s defense under sustained pressure. The visitors have conceded in both qualifiers, and recovering from another poor start would be difficult against this opposition.

Gambia also has reasons to believe it can contribute to the scoring. Six goals in two qualifiers, followed by the return of Barrow and Minteh, give the away side a credible attacking threat.

Another controlled 2-0 home win is the main concern for this selection. Even so, the attacking options available and Gambia’s defensive vulnerabilities make over 2.5 goals the preferred bet.
