Kellen Moore uses even less play action after Drew Brees suggested it

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Who knew Kellen Moore could be petty? The New Orleans Saints head coach was lightly criticized by Pro Football Hall of Fame quarterback Drew Brees during a game broadcast earlier this season. Brees suggested Moore try utilizing more play action to diversify his offense and give Tyler Shough a chance against an increasingly aggressive pass rush. Instead, Moore responded by using even less play action in the weeks since, despite offering a chilly "Love it" when asked if the concept was something he even liked keeping in his playbook.

Sure, Moore isn't deliberately cutting down on the rate of play action passes he's calling each week because people (like Brees) believe he should be using it more often, just to spite them. The problem is worse than that. Moore has slipped into bad habits as a predictable play caller. He's admitting after each loss that he needs to do a better job to fix his team's problems, but he doesn't have those answers in front of him. He's just leaning on what's comfortable, doing the same thing, expecting different results, and losing more games week by week.

Nola.com's Matthew Paras found that in four games since Brees questioned why Moore wasn't using play action to slow down the blitz-heavy Minnesota Vikings defense, Moore's usage has fallen from 18.8% in the first five weeks to just 14.9%. He already ranked among the most predictable offenses in football before this. Now Moore is telegraphing his intentions to the opposing defense and fans watching from home. No wonder the Saints have scored just five touchdowns in their last three games.

Decisions like this will get Moore fired. Failing to adjust a system that isn't working is going to lose enough games that even the notoriously slow-to-act New Orleans Saints front office has to eventually make a change. There's still time for Moore to figure this out and make things easier for his players -- giving Tyler Shough more throws using play action, rollouts, and screen passes can force opposing defenses to spread out and slow down. But Moore hasn't even figured out he can lean on heavy personnel to make life easier for his banged-up offensive line, giving help to the backups starting outside and opening up room to run out of the backfield. The way things stand right now, it's easy to feel discouraged. Let's hope that trend changes soon.
