What India need to do to qualify for the WTC Final: Qualification scenarios explained

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

In his latest column for The Cricket News, Indian cricket expert Jamie Alter gives his take on India's chances of making the WTC Final in 2027, and what they need to do to get there.

Shubman Gill's Test team came back from Sri Lanka with a 1-0 series win.

The big question hanging over the next few months is simple: what does Team India need to do to qualify for the WTC Final?

India currently sit fourth on the WTC points table with a points percentage (PCT) of 50.00. To reach the final, they will likely need a PCT of somewhere between 64 and 68 per cent — which translates to winning at least five of their remaining six Test matches.

Because of their 2-0 home loss to South Africa last year, India's margin for error is now razor-thin.

Their remaining schedule is a mix of home and away assignments. In November, they face New Zealand for the final Test of the two-match series. Then, in the new year, Australia land for a five- match Border-Gavaskar series.

A clean sweep in Sri Lanka had been essential, and with a win at Galle in the first Test, India finally seemed to be back on track. Heading into the match, Indian batters were a lost cause against the spinners and Sri Lanka tried their best to use that to their advantage.

But Devdutt Padikkal's 167 in the first innings followed by a quickfire 44 made sure India had put enough runs on the board to be safe. After that it was upto India's own spinners who did what was expected of them.

Manav Suthar cracked through the Sri Lankan batting order to take 10 wickets across both innings in his second Test ever. For those who grew up watching the mighty generation of Sachin Tendulkar, Rahul Dravid, VVS Laxman and Virender Sehwag exit the Test scene, this game in Sri Lanka was a highlight reel.

Padikkal in particular was simply masterful taking the Sri Lankan attack of Prabath Jayasuriya and Ashita Fernando head-on. His inclusion in the squad due to Sai Sudarshan being injured was another plot point that worked in India's favour.

But the job isn't done yet. The second Test ended in a draw and then the first Test against New Zealand also did not produce any result and now India have a mountain to climb.

Against New Zealand — who famously wrecked India's chances of reaching the previous WTC Final by sweeping them 3-0 — India need to win at least one Test simply to avoid another series defeat.

India have not won a Test in New Zealand since 2009 — when current head coach Gautam Gambhir was in his prime as a player.

Then, in 2027, when the Border-Gavaskar Trophy begins against Australia, India must win at least two or three Tests across the five-match series and manage draws elsewhere.

Gill's team can secure a WTC Final spot outright by winning both of these series. But every game they lose increases their dependency on Australia, South Africa and New Zealand dropping points elsewhere.

Extremely tough, yes. But not impossible.
