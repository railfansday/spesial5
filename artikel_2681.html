Programs will be a hot seller at NorthWood

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

NAPPANEE — The best selling item at NorthWood boys basketball games for the 2026-27 season won't be popcorn or hot dogs or even an ice cold coke.

Veteran head coach Aaron Wolfe has exactly one player returning with much varsity experience as the Panthers try to improve on last season's 14-12 season, which included a 3-10 run and a stretch of winning nine-of-10 before a disappointing loss to Columbia City in the sectional finals.

"This will be a new challenge with a new group of players,'' Wolfe said. "Last year's team was a older and they new what was expected of them. This year, we'll have to slow things down and establish some habits and execute them.

"This year will definitely be about how much we improve throughout the season. We may not know who we are until February.''

The lone player with extended playing time is senior Coin Edwards, who is expected to run the point for the Panthers.

"Edwards played a major role for us last season and he will again this year,'' Wolfe said. "He is a reliable playmaker a steady defender and rebounder and a good leader.''

Edwards is also the only senior on the NorthWood roster.

Ethan Sears, a 6-foot junior, has dressed for varsity games the last two years and has played sparingly. That will change this year.

"Ethan has always been a good basketball player, but he's gotten taller and physically stronger this years and that does bode well for him,'' Wolfe said. "I believe his natural skills will show up this year. He has a good feel for the game and is a solid 3-point shooter.''

Another key to the Panthers this season could be 6-5 sophomore Wadson Gongwer, who also saw a little varsity time last season.

"Watson is improving daily and should anchor the interior defense for us,'' Wolfe said. "He's already a good rebouner and he has the ability to finish around the rim and be a good defender. His size and length make him a tough matchup for people.''

Among the other players looking for playing time for the young Panthers are 6-4 Malyk Duncan and 6-2 Ben Yoder, who are both juniors.

"Malyk is really solid inside,'' Wolfe said. "He does a nice job or rebounding and finishing around the rim, while also defensing the post players.

"Yoder is long and moves really well and has the ability to guard multiple positions. He's a slasher in the transition game and has the ability to make shots as well.''

Sophomore Kolt McCloughen came to NorthWood from Concord, where he saw some playing time at shooting guard late in the 2025-26 season.

"Kolt is a very good shooter and does an exceptional job of helping space the floor for us,'' Wolfe said. "He's a good passer, a reliable defender and has a high-level basketball IQ.''

Wolfe said that freshman EJ Riddle has show flashes in practice that could lead to some playing time at a guard spot for the varsity.

Sophomores Reid Payne and Gage Lechlitner, along with freshman Jakyah Qaiyim are also in the mix for playing time with the Panthers.

Since winning the Class 3A state title in the spring of 2023, the Panthers have lost in the sectional in each of the past three years. First to Fairfield and the last two seasons to Columbia City.

"I think Columbia City will be the favorite until someone knocks them off,'' Wolfe said. "But hey, it's November and everyone thinks they're good right now.''

Tuesday's game with Elkhart Christian starts Wolfe's 20th year at NorthWood. He has a glittering record of 329-136, including the 2023 state championship.
