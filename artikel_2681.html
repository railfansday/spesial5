Stratton wasn't drafted until the 16th round in 2017. He was a fastball pitcher with a solid slider as his secondary pitch. He's yet to put it together at the big-league level. Stratton spent the majority of his career with the Pittsburgh Pirates before being traded to the Atlanta Braves in 2025. He's mostly been a depth piece, only pitching 72 innings in the last four seasons.

Because of that, it's hard to grade Stratton's 2026 with the Padres. He didn't spend any time with the big-league club, but pitched for the Triple-A El Paso Chihuahuas down the stretch. Stratton threw 18 2/3 innings for the minor-league affiliate to fantastic results. The right-hander posted a 1.93 ERA, 1.29 WHIP and .235 opponent batting average.

That's a smaller sample size, but the results are solid. Stratton has been phenomenal in the minor leagues over the last two seasons. He's posted sub-3.00 ERAs in each of the last two years. Across 88 1/3 innings, Stratton owns a 2.85 ERA and 85 strikeouts. The righty has never been a strikeout pitcher, but he's able to find creative ways to get outs.

With that in mind, it seems likely that the Padres hold onto Stratton as bullpen depth for the dog days of summer. The Friars have one of the deepest bullpens in the game, but they always seem to be adding more arms in order to bolster their depth. Stratton could very well earn himself a call up later in the year if he pitches well enough in Triple-A.
