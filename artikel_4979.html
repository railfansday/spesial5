Coinbase Targets In-House Prediction Markets by First Half of 2027: Report

Coinbase is planning to launch its own prediction market contracts in the U.S. in the first half of 2027, as it looks to take more control of one of its fastest-growing businesses.

The exchange plans to offer the in-house event contracts to both retail and institutional customers, Bloomberg reported on Thursday, citing a person familiar with the matter.

A Coinbase spokesperson told Bloomberg the company had "nothing new to announce today," and pointed to plans it set out in October to expand its prediction markets and improve their economics by building its own clearing and other infrastructure. The spokesperson did not address the timing of an in-house launch.

Coinbase rolled out prediction markets across the U.S. through a partnership with Kalshi, after unveiling the product alongside stock trading last year. Offering its own contracts would give Coinbase more say over which markets it lists, and let it keep more of the revenue from trades.

The exchange has been laying the groundwork for its pivot. In December 2025, it agreed to buy prediction markets startup The Clearing Company, and in September 2026 it received approval from the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) for its own clearinghouse. Coinbase already runs a regulated derivatives exchange.

Prediction markets have become a bright spot for Coinbase as crypto trading slows. The business passed $200 million in annualized revenue in the third quarter, with daily traders up more than 60% from the previous quarter, and revenue growing 82% a quarter on average since launch, the company reported in October.

"Prediction markets are adding another revenue stream and are less correlated to crypto volatility," CFO Alesia Haas said at the time. On an analyst call the same day, Haas said Coinbase's new clearinghouse meant it "can launch our own contracts, and we can also then earn better economics," while continuing to offer contracts through partners such as Kalshi. Asked when, she said: "We have not shared a timeline."

The products have faced legal challenges from states that argue some event contracts amount to unlicensed gambling. Coinbase sued three states over their attempts to regulate prediction markets, and New York sued Coinbase and Gemini over their offerings in April.

Last week, a Louisiana judge temporarily barred more than 30 prediction market providers, including Coinbase's derivatives arm, from offering event contracts in the state, after Attorney General Liz Murrill sued them on November 12, Forbes reported. The CFTC has sued Louisiana in federal court to block the order, and a hearing on a longer injunction is set for November 30.
