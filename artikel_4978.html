South Korea Unveils Crypto Bill Capping Exchange Ownership at 20%

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

South Korea’s Financial Services Commission on Tuesday unveiled a government bill that would cap a major shareholder’s stake in a crypto exchange at 20%, open the door to domestic coin offerings and let bank-led groups issue won stablecoins.

The draft, titled the Act on the Innovation of the Digital Asset Industry and Protection of Users, was presented at a Virtual Asset Committee meeting chaired by FSC Chairman Lee Eog-weon. It would replace Korea’s two-track system, built on an anti-money-laundering law and a 2024 user protection act, with a single framework covering the industry, markets and users.

Lee said the law would raise trust and transparency in Korea’s digital asset market and help draw new money into the industry. He said the government would do everything it can to see the bill fully debated in the National Assembly and enacted quickly.

Under the bill, exchanges would become “digital asset platform operators,” a designation reserved for firms that hold separate licenses for dealing, brokerage and custody. The FSC modeled the ownership limit on the rules for alternative trading systems in the stock market.

Major shareholders could own up to 20%, or up to 34% with FSC approval under a presidential decree. Shares above the limit would lose their voting rights. Existing exchanges would get three years after designation to comply, and small and midsize exchanges could get up to three more.

The cap could reach some of Korea’s best-known exchanges. Mirae Asset Consulting owns 97.15% of Digital X, the exchange formerly known as Korbit, The Korea Herald reported. Naver Financial’s planned share-swap takeover of Dunamu, the operator of Upbit, would make the exchange a wholly owned subsidiary.

The FSC declined to comment on specific companies, the paper said.

“Ownership restrictions are necessary to strengthen exchanges’ public accountability and neutrality,” Yoo Young-jun, director general of the FSC’s Digital Financial Policy Bureau, said at a briefing, citing more than 10 million exchange accounts in Korea.

The bill would also move trade execution, listing reviews and market surveillance from self-regulation to formal rules. Exchanges would need outside directors to make up a majority of their boards, plus audit committees and compliance officers.

Domestic initial coin offerings would be allowed as long as issuers file a white paper with the FSC before marketing tokens to the public. The release pointed to a platform company that sidestepped Korea’s ICO ban by setting up a foundation abroad, then sold its token through local exchanges. Exchanges would have to file and publish a report on each token they list.

Stablecoins, called digital payment tokens in the bill, could only be issued by licensed firms with at least 5 billion won in capital. At first, the FSC plans to allow issuance only by consortiums in which banks hold a majority stake, though fintech and tech companies could be the largest single shareholder.

Issuers would have to hold reserves of at least 100% of tokens outstanding at banks or similar institutions, and they could not pay holders interest. Tokens with more than 1 trillion won outstanding could be designated as significant, and the Bank of Korea could request joint inspections of their issuers.

Licensed firms would carry no-fault liability to users for hacks and system outages. The FSC could impose punitive fines of up to 10% of revenue when a security lapse causes an incident. The release cited a November 2025 theft from one exchange’s wallet and an October 2026 hack at another, without naming either.

Insider-trading bans would extend to former employees for a year after they leave. The FSC could also freeze accounts when there is substantial suspicion of unfair trading. Regulators have already shown a willingness to punish exchanges, having fined Bithumb $24.6 million and suspended its new-user services for six months over anti-money-laundering violations in March.

Last week, the FSC was reported to be preparing guidelines that would let about 3,500 listed companies and professional-investor firms buy bitcoin. The new bill now heads to the National Assembly.
