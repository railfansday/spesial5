8 Best Hotel Mattress Picks for a Five-Star Staycation (2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The hotel mattress probably isn't on your vacation itinerary, but it can make or break the trip. A lumpy, unsupportive bed can derail even the most carefully planned getaway. A really good one, though, might become an unexpected highlight—the kind that has you looking forward to bedtime and quietly checking the mattress label before checkout.

Recreating that experience at home doesn't require another plane ticket. Some of our favorite properties, including design-forward waterfront retreats and charming bed-and-breakfasts, put just as much thought into their mattresses as they do their interiors. Many turn to brands like Saatva, Ostermoor, and Casper to outfit their guest rooms; others, like the W Hotels, have signature mattresses you can purchase directly. We've rounded up our favorites, such as options with plush pillow tops, supportive coils, and pressure-relieving foam, to help you bring home the best part of your stay. The room service, unfortunately, is still up to you.

For consistency, all the prices reflect queen-size mattresses.

A stay at the Ritz-Carlton is typically reflected by its refined, elegant bedrooms. The property’s eponymous bed invites the same feeling. At 14 inches, it’s a higher-profile mattress that can help make the bed the centerpiece of the room. One of the standout qualities is its coil system, as it includes 900-plus individual springs for great edge support to help keep the sides from sagging as well as promote motion isolation—a necessary factor for couples who don’t want to sense each other’s every move.

Serta is one of the most well-known mattress manufacturers for high-end hotels, such as the Waldorf-Astoria. While many luxury properties feature custom beds from the namesake brand, it doesn’t mean you can’t shop at popular retailers such as Amazon. You’d never guess it, but this premium bed is priced under $500 (making it the most affordable on our list). While Serta offers memory foam and hybrid versions of its beds, this pillow-top innerspring mattress has been a favorite in our shopping writer Julia Harrison’s space, and she says it lives up to its “Five-Star” name.

It’s filled with three zones of coil support that keep her free of back and neck problems, and the quilted cover gives her something soft to dream about each night. “I’m really not one for pomp and frills in my mattress—I’m a firm believer that a good mattress has very little going on besides a somewhat plush top to keep you from feeling any sort of spring mechanism going on inside,” she says in her mattress review. “I instantly slept well on this mattress I think because it’s quite simply: a great mattress.” Bonus: It’s a mattress-in-a-box, so it can fit easily through narrow doors and hallways once it’s delivered, and inflate to its full size in less than a day’s time after unboxing, according to Harrison. Just in case you’re on the fence, it comes with a 100-day sleep trial so you can really see if it’s right for you before committing.

The Six Bells Countryside Inn opened its doors in the bucolic Hudson Valley in 2025, and it’s already earned two Michelin Keys. In each of the 11 carefully and whimsically designed bedrooms at this retreat, you’ll find handcrafted mattresses and bedding from New York-born brand Ostermoor. Dating back to 1853, Ostermoor’s mattresses have a storied history. The beds have innovated to foam and pocketed coil inner layers and designer covers in recent years, and we can prove they’re worth the splurge.

“From the moment I plopped down on the mattress, I was amazed by how comfortable and supportive it was,” shares contributor Zoë Sessums. “It strikes a perfect balance between spring and cushion—just the right amount of give without ever feeling too soft. I consistently wake up feeling rested and supported.” Firm without feeling rigid, this mattress looks as good as it feels, too. “The tufted top, the blue and white ticking pattern, the hand-stitched details—it feels like a piece of luxury furniture, not just a place to sleep,” Sessums adds, later writing, “I would recommend this to someone who values design and craftsmanship as much as comfort.”

Saatva has found its way into charming bed-and-breakfasts and boutique retreats across the country, including Pasadena's historic Bissell House (an 1887 Victorian filled with antique furnishings) and the Cornell Inn in the Berkshires (known for its colorful, individually designed guest rooms). You can bring that same inviting comfort home with the Saatva Classic, which comes with a plush three-inch Euro pillow top, a responsive dual-coil system, and targeted lumbar support. “It’s firm (which I prefer), regulates temperature well, and is extremely durable,” our tester Erin Kaplan, vice president of communications at Conde Nast, says. “I alternate between sleeping on my side and sleeping on my stomach, and this mattress is great for both positions.”

Choose from three firmness levels—plush soft, luxury firm, or firm—, eight different sizes, and two mattress heights to find your ideal fit. Feeling extra? Saatva also offers two adjustable bases that elevate your head and feet at the touch of a button, including a premium Plus model for an even more indulgent breakfast in bed. A 365-night trial gives you plenty of time to sleep on your decision before committing.

At 1 Hotel Brooklyn Bridge, even the beds have a view. Sweeping views of the Manhattan skyline set the scene, with reclaimed wood floors, rich leather headboards, and locally crafted details like crate-inspired closets and Fresnel glass light fixtures bringing warmth and character to the rooms. With custom Keetsa mattresses in every room, you might find yourself hitting snooze despite the spectacular view.

Back at home, the Keetsa Pillow Plus offers its own take on hotel-worthy comfort. It's something of a Goldilocks mattress: not too soft, not too firm, but just right. A three-inch pillow top combines body-contouring memory foam with responsive comfort foam to help cushion pressure points, and a soft, moisture-wicking cover designed to keep overheating at bay. Individually wrapped coils provide cushioning and support while minimizing motion transfer, so even if your bedmate tosses and turns, you can stay blissfully unaware.

After all, the best hosts know a good night's sleep is part of the hospitality.

Hot sleepers, here’s a mattress for you. Which Kind of Mattress Do Five-Star Hotels Use?

Most luxury hotels use hybrid mattresses in their suites to strike the right balance of softness and support, thanks to pocketed coils and layers of foam. These are also typically medium-firm mattresses since a plush mattress can be uncomfortable for some sleep positions like stomach sleepers. Ultra-firm beds are often too stiff for side sleeping and may not align with guests' expectations who are looking for soft mattresses to sink into on their vacations.

If you want to refresh your mattress without the hefty price tag of buying a new one, a mattress topper or featherbed could be a solid transitional pick. The Ritz-Carlton and W Hotels promote one made with white duck feathers, Mandarin Oriental’s exclusive topper comprises white goose feathers, Waldorf Astoria beds feature a down-alternative fill, and Marriott’s selection is polyester.

There’s no one-size-fits-all here, so it really comes down to your preferred loftiness and weight. Duck and goose feathers are heavier than others fills, though duck is slightly less fluffy and won’t trap heat as well. Down-alternatives, like polyester, are more allergen-friendly and “a good option for those who prefer to be vegan in their home,” Danielle Trotter, the former textiles technical designer at West Elm, previously shared.

What Is the Thickness of a Hotel Mattress?

Hotel mattresses usually average 12 inches in thickness. This makes it easier to hop in and out of bed, especially when using a box spring that can add extra height. Plus, it prevents the need for deep pocket sheets.

How Can I Recreate the Hotel Bed Experience at Home?

We’ve explored the entire topic on how to make your bedroom feel like a hotel, and aside from finding a new bed with a medium-firm feel, we’ve learned that percale sheets and down pillows are pretty much a must to complete the setup. When it comes to the former, hotel sheets are usually made with organic cotton percale because the fabric looks and feels crisp, giving that refined aesthetic a boutique stay is known for. Usually a 200 thread count is a good rule of thumb to look out for since this means there’s plenty of airflow between the threads for a breathable feel. Hotel pillows are usually filled with down feathers since they’re so lofty and soft, but a down alternative is just as good in case you’re an allergy-prone sleeper (just look for materials like hypoallergenic gel fibers since they’re known for being squishy without falling flat, just like a feather-filled pillow).

What Is the Difference Between Memory Foam and Latex?

Traditional memory foam has a body-contouring effect that allows your body to sink into your mattress, reducing motion transfer (a must for bedmates!). Meanwhile, latex foam offers more responsiveness (or bounce) and overall support. As Neslihan Ozdemir, founder of Girl on the Mattress, previously told AD, latex also holds up well over time and is temperature-regulating. Latex foam mattresses (and some memory foam options) are certified organic, making them eco-friendly foam picks, too.

Known as the most common mattress type, innerspring mattresses feature a bed of coils. There’s quite a range to these mattresses: Basic versions have a grid of steel coils, while premier options have a pillow top and pocketed coil technology, meaning each coil is individually wrapped to help with motion isolation. Ozdemir added that innerspring is best for people who sleep on the edge of the bed or are heavier in weight. If you prefer a more plush mattress, but still want the support of those coils, consider a hybrid mattress, which is layered with soft foam.
