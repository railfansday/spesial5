10 Ocarina of Time Remake Changes That Break the Hearts of Nostalgic Fans

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Covering the video games industry since 2017, with experience in news, articles, and reviews. Currently a writer at DualShockers and gaming magazines.

The remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time has brought one of the most beloved and iconic adventures in gaming history back to life with a revamped visual presentation, plenty of gameplay improvements, and a surprising number of small changes that make exploring Hyrule feel fresh all over again. From quality-of-life improvements to the inventory and equipment systems to subtle environmental tweaks, there's literally a lot to appreciate about Nintendo's new take on this timeless classic.

However, as someone who grew up playing the original (and has an admittedly unhealthy amount of nostalgia running through my veins), I couldn't help but notice that some of the little things I loved are no longer around – or they are, but in a very different way.

Thomas Mahler believes Nintendo missed an opportunity to improve the level design in the Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake.

Don't get me wrong, I absolutely adore this remake and described it as a lovely modern remake with an old soul, but that doesn't mean I'm not allowed to miss a few things from my childhood, right? So, please allow me to put my nostalgic glasses on for a moment and revisit ten changes that have left a tiny hole in my heart.

One of my favorite things about the original Ocarina of Time was starting a new save file and giving Link the most ridiculous name I could think of. It was such a simple feature, but it opened the door to some quite hilarious moments, especially when characters started addressing the legendary hero using whatever silly name I'd come up with.

One of my personal favorites was naming him “Bro.” Suddenly, every conversation in Hyrule sounded like everyone was part of the most laid-back adventure imaginable.

Unfortunately, the remake has decided to put an end to our creative freedom by locking the protagonist's name to Link. I can understand the reasoning, especially considering Nintendo's efforts to make the franchise more accessible to new generations and expand its presence across other media (since the live-action movie is coming hot). Still, I'll always miss my little adventures with Bro. You will be missed, buddy.

One of the coolest easter eggs in the original Ocarina of Time was hidden in the courtyard where you first meet Princess Zelda. As Child Link, you must sneak past the castle guards to reach the princess without getting caught. Once you've had your little chat with her, however, there's nothing stopping you from exploring the area and discovering a few fun secrets.

Among them is a window you can shoot with your Slingshot, prompting a particularly grumpy guard to throw a bomb in your direction (how rude!). But that's not all. If you look through the other windows, you'll find three familiar faces from the Super Mario universe: Mario, Luigi, and Bowser, all sporting their Super Mario 64 appearances.

I was honestly expecting Nintendo to bring this little crossover back using the characters' more modern designs, perhaps even their Super Mario Odyssey models. Instead, the entire reference has been removed from the remake. It's a shame, considering how charming these little Nintendo Easter eggs can be. At least the guard is still there to throw a bomb at your face. Some traditions must be preserved, I suppose.

If there's one thing every Ocarina of Time veteran knows, it's that Rupees are important, and there's no shame in taking advantage of a good farming opportunity. One of the earliest places where you could stock up on some extra cash was a room inside Hyrule Castle's Market, conveniently filled with breakable pots.

As Child Link, you could simply walk inside, smash everything in sight, collect the Rupees, leave, and come back to find the pots replenished. Rinse and repeat until your pockets were sufficiently full.

Unfortunately, the remake has decided that we shouldn't be allowed to get rich quite so easily. While you can still find some pots containing Rupees in the room, they no longer respawn simply by leaving the castle and coming back inside. Instead, you'll have to wait quite a while and spend some time doing other activities before they return. I guess we'll just have to go back to cutting grass like the rest of Hyrule's hardworking citizens.

I know what you're thinking – why on earth would anyone miss Kaepora Gaebora being annoying? But hear me out, I promise there's a reason behind my questionable opinion!

I also ain't a fan of the original owl. In fact, I probably spent more time than I'd like to admit accidentally answering “No” when he asked whether I wanted to hear his explanation again. And if you've played the original, you know exactly how frustrating that could be. However, there was something strangely entertaining about collectively hating this overly talkative bird. Kaepora became one of those characters that practically every Ocarina of Time player had a story about, and that shared frustration was part of the experience.

Now, the remake has given him a much cuter appearance, and he's also significantly less annoying, behaving much more like the wise and mysterious owl he's supposed to be. I mean, where’s the fun in that? I can't help missing the days when simply seeing him fly toward me filled me with dread.

Ocarina of Time has plenty of memorable exploit tricks, but one of my personal favorites involved getting a Heart Piece much earlier than Nintendo originally intended.

In the original game, you could perform a particularly well-timed backflip from the top of Death Mountain, near the entrance to Goron City, and land on the roof of Dodongo's Cavern. This allowed you to grab a Heart Piece that was normally intended for Adult Link, long before you were supposed to have access to it. It was just one of those silly little tricks that made exploring Hyrule feel even more rewarding.

Well, those days are over. The remake has adjusted the backflip's height, making it considerably less effective, and even tweaked the position of Dodongo's Cavern entrance to prevent players from pulling off the same trick (not that I tried to do it or anything).

From suspicious house designs to familiar songs, fans think they've found clues.

There's an unwritten rule among players: the moment you unlock the Hookshot, you must immediately equip it and start shooting at everything in sight. And one of your first destinations will probably be the rooftops of Kakariko Village.

This was really fun because it offered more freedom than simply using it to reach the obvious targets. You could experiment with different surfaces, discover unexpected ways to navigate the environment, and generally mess around with an incredibly useful piece of equipment. Apparently, however, that wasn't quite what Nintendo had in mind. The remake has restricted the Hookshot's functionality, meaning it can now only latch onto specific designated targets, such as stone markers and certain treasure chests.

I understand that this might have been done to prevent unintended shortcuts or make progression more controlled, but I can't help feeling that something has been lost in the process. One of the most enjoyable aspects of Ocarina of Time was experimenting with your equipment and discovering creative ways to use it. So, please, Nintendo, give us our old Hookshot back!

One of my most vivid childhood memories of Ocarina of Time involves something that probably sounds completely ridiculous to anyone who didn't grow up playing the original: stepping into Hyrule Field for the very first time and desperately running toward Hyrule Castle like a lunatic.

That happened because, if night fell before we reached the castle gates, they would close, leaving Link stranded outside until morning – and Hyrule Field at night was not exactly the safest place to be.

And you’ve guessed: the remake has removed this particular inconvenience, meaning you no longer have to worry about being locked out of Hyrule Castle after sunset. My adult self appreciates not having to deal with that little problem, but the child in me misses those frantic nighttime sprints.

There's something wonderfully weird about the little laboratory at Lake Hylia. Inside, you'll find a rather eccentric scientist who spends most of his time complaining about his back pain while standing next to a water tank.

He'll even offer you a reward if you manage to dive deep enough to touch the bottom of the pool. Naturally, being the helpful hero that you are, you'll want to give it a try. But once you reach the bottom, you'll discover something rather unexpected: a shark inside a cage.

What made this little discovery even funnier in the original game was the shark itself! Rather than featuring a detailed 3D model, it was represented by a hilariously low-resolution, static image. It looked so out of place that it became one of those bizarre little details you simply couldn't forget. While the remake has kept the shark in the aquarium, unfortunately, it has also given it a proper visual upgrade. Sure, it looks considerably better now, but I miss the ridiculous charm of that pixelated little creature.

If there's one thing Ocarina of Time does exceptionally well, it's turning a seemingly innocent adventure into a horror game without any warning! One moment you're exploring a dungeon and collecting treasures, and the next you're desperately running for your life from something that looks like it escaped from your worst nightmare.

Few enemies capture that feeling better than the Wallmaster, a giant disembodied hand that suddenly drops from the ceiling, grabs Link, and sends him all the way back to the beginning of the area or dungeon.

And what made this enemy so terrifying in the original was the anticipation. You couldn't simply look up to check whether it was coming, so you had to rely on an increasingly unsettling sound effect and the shadow that gradually appeared above Link. The remake preserves most of these elements, but introduces one rather important difference: a spotlight that illuminates the area whenever it’s about to appear. Combined with its shadow, this makes the enemy practically announce its arrival in the most dramatic way possible.

I appreciate the warning, but I honestly miss the uncertainty that made these encounters so frightening in the original. Thankfully, the Dead Hand is absolutely nightmare-inducing in the remake, so I'll let this one slide.

Remember the Water Temple? If you played the original Ocarina of Time back in the day, there's a good chance that simply reading those two words made you feel a little uncomfortable.

For years, this dungeon had a reputation for being one of the most frustrating experiences in the entire game. Between constantly changing the water level and switching between the Iron Boots and other equipment, it was enough to test even the most patient players.

There was also a particularly nasty Small Key hidden underneath the floor of the central pillar, which could only be accessed after raising the water level. It was incredibly easy to overlook, and if you ever found yourself seemingly stuck with no idea how to progress, there's a good chance this little key was the culprit!

Despite keeping most of the original dungeon's layout intact, the remake has made things considerably easier by removing this easily missed Small Key altogether. This means players no longer have to spend ages retracing their steps and wondering whether they've somehow managed to softlock their game.

Of course, the Water Temple is still a memorable dungeon, and I'm certainly not complaining about a more streamlined experience. But I do find it funny that an entire generation of players had to suffer through those confusing corridors just so newcomers could have a slightly more pleasant time! You're welcome, younger Zelda fans. We fought in the trenches so you wouldn't have to.
