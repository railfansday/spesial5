10 Action-Adventure Games That Are Unexpected Masterpieces

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Ronald is a video game journalist with more than 10 years of experience in the industry, working on several of the most important Spanish websites.

The action-adventure genre is one of the broadest in the entire video game industry, encompassing countless experiences ranging from stealth and hack-and-slash to horror and beat 'em ups.

Thanks to this broad definition, countless masterpieces fall under its umbrella, including many that, unfortunately, haven't achieved the popularity they deserve due to fierce competition.

Even within the realm of major blockbusters, not all underrated titles are obscure indie games—there are games that have fallen just short of mainstream success, though I'm here to help rectify that.

So, in order to shed light on some lesser-known campaigns that haven't yet cemented their undeniable excellence in the collective consciousness, I invite you to explore this list of ten action-adventure games that are unexpected masterpieces.

As the least recognized work from Tango Gameworks, a studio that has certainly gifted us with numerous magnificent journeys, Ghostwire: Tokyo stands out among the games with the greatest disparity between its quality and its fame.

Boasting one of the finest atmospheres crafted this decade, wandering the streets of Shibuya, soaring across rooftops, and taking down the most terrifying yokai imaginable is, by far, one of my highlights of the decade.

Whether in stealth or direct magical combat, tackling one of its turbulent side quests or hunting for collectibles, the game strikes an outstanding balance between making each mechanic satisfying while still maintaining a constant sense of terror in its setting.

As far as first-person games go, Ghostwire: Tokyo is part of the most immersive, rewarding, and distinctive I've played this generation, which is saying something considering the titans with which it shares category.

As the years have gone by, games that adhere to the Metroidvania philosophy have become one of my greatest obsessions, which inevitably led me to fall in love with Pipistrello and the Cursed Yoyo.

Its ability to capture the nostalgia of the Game Boy Advance era while boasting the refined design and execution of modern products demonstrates its creators' immeasurable talent, who also imbued it with an impressive thematic personality.

The critiques of various strategic production industries resonate with our current times, while in the process, the game leverages the yoyo as a central gameplay element to make traversal, combat, exploration, and customization an absolute delight that's also pretty unique.

Pipistrello and the Cursed Yoyo is among the finest indie titles I've enjoyed in the last couple of years, and anything other than recognizing it as brilliant is to underestimate its potential.

As someone who didn't experience the franchise's heyday, Shinobi: Art of Vengeance not only caught me completely off guard, but I can think of very few resurrections that have felt so powerful.

The absolute masterwork of action and platforming that this title is results in frankly mind-blowing sequences, flowing seamlessly between both dimensions to deliver a thrilling adventure that doesn't let you breathe for a second.

As you unlock combos, powers, and movement abilities, you encounter an ever-increasing series of challenges that push the game's mechanics to their limits, which are meticulously refined to ensure you have complete control over every pixel.

Few titles in 2025 felt as satisfying to complete 100%, so Shinobi: Art of Vengeance stands as a masterfully unexpected example that, when you don't know what to do with your old IPs, outsourcing to an indie studio to work on them is the best possible idea.

The Legend of Zelda's heirs number in the hundreds of thousands, but that didn't stop Death's Door from emerging as one of its most intelligent and charismatic great-great-grandchildren.

The game perfectly understands the enjoyment that lies in discovery and exploration through iteration, inviting us on a diverse adventure with plenty of colorful biomes, enemies, characters, and circumstances that evoke all kinds of emotions.

Curiosity, fear, happiness, sadness, worry... The title silently manages a multitude of emotions both thematically and in its gameplay, limiting progression to allow for exploration of its expansive world at a prodigious pace.

When discussing isometric titles that combine action, platforming, and puzzles in the best possible way, Death's Door is at the description's forefront, though it's the ease and finesse with which it achieves this that's truly unexpected.

Castlevania is a series that went through some difficult years after the turn of the century, and it wasn't until Castlevania: Lords of Shadow arrived that the IP unexpectedly found some relief amidst so many setbacks, astonishingly becoming the best-selling game in the entire IP.

MercurySteam crafted one of the most beautiful and satisfying hack-and-slash games in the genre's history, finally adapting the saga's formula perfectly to 3D without losing an ounce of its core identity.

The whip feels better than ever, as do the movement mechanics, the puzzles, and the progression systems, all enhanced in the best possible way thanks to a magnificently crafted story and characters that keep you on the edge of your seat until the very last second.

Castlevania: Lords of Shadow completely forgoes being conceptually innovative, but the sacrifice, given it guaranteed us an extremely excellent title in all its facets, was totally worth it even when nobody was betting on such a resounding success.

In the years when God of War slowed down, and Devil May Cry practically disappeared, a third icon emerged from absolutely nowhere to keep the hack-and-slash genre alive (with Bayonetta's permission): Darksiders.

Drawing inspiration from both Santa Monica Studio's work and The Legend of Zelda, Vigil Games crafted a splendidly elaborate tale and setting within Judeo-Christian mythology, one of the least explored in the video game industry.

Giant bosses based on satisfying gimmicks, varied and refreshing puzzles that make the most of War's arsenal and movement capabilities, phenomenal art design for both environments and enemies... It's the complete package.

That the company's first title would be such a presentation of knowledge in the genre's fundamentals was unforeseen even for the team itself, but much more for the hundreds of thousands who, like me, stumbled upon Darksiders by chance and have been hooked ever since.

Returning to the topic of franchises forced to reinvent themselves to adapt to modern times, not even the most optimistic projections for the Tomb Raider reboot could have predicted the absolute brilliance it would become.

Going back to Lara Croft's origins resulted in one of the quintessential action-adventure games of the entire generation, achieving an astonishing symphony of survival mechanics, realistic yet compelling gunplay, and simply fantastic settings and stories.

Each set piece surpasses the last, and the spectacular scenery contrasts perfectly with the brutal and tense story the legendary archaeologist faces, all while possessing the intriguing premise of not yet being the intrepid adventurer we all know, but rather a young, still-innocent graduate.

From concept to execution, Tomb Raider is a tapestry of sound decisions that, among other things, reminded Uncharted fans that Nathan Drake only managed to run because Lara Croft walked first.

I know that nowadays we're used to indie masterpieces being released every couple of months, but back in the day, before that was the norm, Hyper Light Drifter arrived like a hurricane of grandeur.

Taking Dark Souls' harshness, non-linearity, and disorientation, Heart Machine assembled an adventure that is both cryptic and captivating, where the gameplay and narrative direction are entirely in the player's hands.

Where to go and why are questions that only the user can answer through exploration, embarking on an intense and introspective journey where bosses and secrets abound, motivating us to explore every last corner of its silent world.

Along with gorgeous pixel art, a remarkable sound design, and a simple yet perfect gameplay loop, Hyper Light Drifter built a castle with just a few blocks, a feat reserved only for the greatest.

The reviews for Asura's Wrath were so scathing that it took me a decade to finally deign to play it, and the sheer shock of finishing it in tears when the credits rolled was beyond words.

Yes, every single one of the comments regarding its poor gameplay was, is, and will be correct, since it's a beat 'em up where the best parts of the gameplay are the on-rails shooting and quick-time events, and that doesn't exactly reflect well on a title of this genre.

Regardless, when you have such an incredible plot, abundant with charismatic characters, memorable scenes, robust mythology, and eccentrically immeasurable cinematics, I couldn't care less that every encounter boils down to mashing the attack button a hundred times a second.

The intercosmic scale that Asura's Wrath handles in its presentation and spectacle is monumental, unique, and emotional enough to overlook its mediocre mechanics, being the only case in the entire genre where it's a masterpiece even without being fun in the traditional sense.

Bucking the trend in the genre, whose disdain for stealth became evident in the last decade, Arkane Studios made a tremendous statement in 2012 with Dishonored's release.

Coming from another underrated gem, Dark Messiah of Might and Magic, the studio embraced the lingering force of immersive sims and materialized it into a project that excels as both a stealth game and an action-adventure game.

Boasting some of the best level design in video game history, along with a story that evolves based on the player's actions rather than dialogue choices, Dishonored is one of the century's defining power fantasies.

Between Dunwall's perfect setting, the outstanding array of gadgets and powers that allow for creative and expansive use of stealth and violence, and the majestic musical pieces that accompany us, it's clear that the love for stealth games will never die as long as this IP remains available.

Years go by, and I still wonder if the genre has enough strength to produce a title that surpasses Dishonored, but for now, it remains on the throne without any concern about being replaced.
