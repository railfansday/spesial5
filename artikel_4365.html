10 Pokémon Characters Who Deserved Much More Attention

While Pokémon is best known for the Pokémon themselves, every game brings an eclectic cast of characters we interact with as Pokémon trainers. Whether it’s quirky Gym Leaders, angsty rivals, or just odd folks you meet along the way, your journey is punctuated by these interesting experiences.

This list covers some of the best things players find when taking their time in the Pokémon series.

However, sometimes a character comes along that really makes you pay attention. Maybe it’s the way they look, what they said, or the Pokémon they used, but they stick in your memory somehow. The Pokémon series is full of these characters. More often than not, they appear and disappear as fast as they were introduced, leaving you a little confused about why they didn’t play a bigger role.

Here are a few examples of characters I wish had a bigger role, or were fleshed out a bit more over the course of all nine Generations to date.

Anyone who played the original Diamond and Pearl back in the day will remember Cheryl. She’s the helpful trainer who accompanies you through Eterna Forest, right before the second gym. You might think she doesn’t have much of a role, since all she does is heal your Pokémon with her Chansey and then wander off, yet she seems to recur throughout the franchise.

Cheryl reappears later in the Battleground, a place generally reserved for powerful trainers, with a team of Pokémon with crazy-high HP stats, and also in the Battle Tower. She also appears in Pokémon Generations, getting lost in The Old Chateau, and seems to have a relative in Hisui called Sabi.

It’s a shame we never got to see a bit more from Cheryl in the story, or even in something like Pokémon Contests!

Mohn has always been a pretty interesting character in Pokémon Sun and Moon. You’ll originally meet him when you’re introduced to the Poke Pelago; however, you might notice he looks a little familiar.

Long story short, Mohn was originally a researcher at the Aether Foundation and Lusamine's husband. However, when we meet him, he’s unfortunately lost his memory. It’s rumoured that when researching the Ultra Wormholes, he fell into one and developed amnesia, but this topic isn’t really explored much.

I would have loved a bit more backstory about Mohn, his relationship with his family and just what happened to him when travelling through Ultra Space. However, we may never know.

Marley, like Cheryl, is a Generation 4 character we meet while exploring a certain area of the game. This area is an offshoot of Victory Road and can only be explored in the Post-Game.

Marley doesn’t like to talk much and seems very caring. As such, while she does have an Arcanine, it’s also equipped with Helping Hand to assist you in battle. Like Cheryl, she can also be found in the Battleground and the Battle Tower, and she specialises in high-speed Pokémon.

The thing is, Marley obviously has a connection to Shaymin, which the game doesn’t really develop much. She helps us reach Seabreak Path and can even be found there again if you bring Oak’s Letter. On top of this, in Pokémon Masters, she even has a Shaymin! Is she just an interested trainer, or is there more to it?

Malva is the Fire-type Elite Four member of the Kalos region. Most Elite Four members don’t have much to offer, aside from an interesting design. However, Malva is hiding something far more sinister beneath the surface.

From Ultra Necrozma’s stat boosts to Volo’s grueling final battle, these Pokémon encounters bend the rules to deliver memorable challenges.

See, Malva is actually part of the heinous Team Flare, which you work to dismantle throughout the game. We learn later, in Legends Z-A, that Malva was there when they captured the legendary Pokémon Yveltal and helped take it down.

This is why she seems so prickly towards you when you face her in the Pokémon League. I would have liked to see a bit more of Malva and learn more about her involvement with Team Flare. The Flare storyline always seemed a bit weak after all.

Thankfully, Malva seems to learn her lesson and helps us with Dr Xerosic in the X and Y postgame, somewhat redeeming herself.

Benga is a really odd character. He’s obviously the Grandson of the Champion, Alder; that’s pretty obvious from his radical hairstyle. However, it’s never explained how Benga got so powerful. More so than his Grandfather, the Champion, might I repeat!

Benga is the final boss of the White Treehollow in White 2 or the Black Tower in Black 2. As such, he has an absolutely ludicrous team of Latios/Latias, Garchomp/Dragonite, and a Volcarona. While the Volcarona is obviously a nod to Alder, how on earth did this little kid get his hands on a pair of legendaries?

Once you’ve defeated him, you can head back to Floccesy Town, where Benga will offer you either a Shiny Gible or a Shiny Dratini. Now he seems to have shiny Pokémon to offer you, but how did he get these? This kid is a real mystery.

Starting in Generation 5, you can find Ingo in the Battle Subway, where he serves as a main boss alongside his brother Emmet. And for a long time, that’s where he stayed. A fairly obscure, relatively uninteresting character who didn’t draw much attention.

However, you might be surprised to come across Ingo in Hisui, in Pokémon Legends: Arceus. And no, this isn’t some ancient relative like most other Sinnoh trainers; this really is Ingo, having travelled through time and lost a good chunk of his memories. But why him, and not a Sinnoh character?

Thankfully, Ingo gets a happy ending, as we learn in Legends Z-A that he eventually returned to Unova and reunited with his brother Emmet. Turns out even the most unassuming trainer can have their secrets!

And speaking of people being zapped across Space and Time, here we have Anabel. We were originally introduced to Anabel in Pokémon Emerald, where she served as a Frontier Brain, one of the most powerful and difficult trainers in the entire series.

After defeating the Elite Four, these are the Pokémon Games Where the Post-Game Feels Like a Whole New Adventure.

However, fast-forward to Pokémon Sun and Moon, and we meet Anabel again; like Ingo, she’s been mysteriously transported to Alola, this time through an Ultra Wormhole rather than by Arceus. Now she’s part of the International Police, and as part of the UB task force, she’s there to round up the rabid Ultra Beasts rampaging across the region.

We know Anabel is a strong trainer, before and after the incident, but how did she enter the Ultra Wormhole in the first place? Was she chosen? Or was it just random chance? We never really get an answer here. It would have been nice to get a little more backstory into what happened in Hoenn.

Zinnia appears only in the Generation 3 remakes, Pokémon Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire. During the adventure, you run into her masquerading as part of the evil team, but you soon learn she’s actually part of an ancient group trying to summon Rayquaza.

She plays a larger role in the Delta Episode; however, all her efforts are for nothing, as the main character ends up Mega Evolving Rayquaza and stopping the Meteor.

Zinnia has a fantastic, interesting design, and the bits and pieces we get of her backstory make it seem like she’ll play a bigger role in the story. She seems to know so much more than we do, especially about the Pokémon Multiverse, but again, we never fully explore her story. At least we still have that incredible battle theme!

If you’ve played Pokémon Colosseum or XD, you’ll have fond memories of this utterly bizarre trainer. Miror B is a funky villain and one of the four admins of Team Cipher. However, you’ll quickly notice that while he might be on the bad side, his real passion is actually dancing!

And not only does he like to bust a move, but his Pokémon do too! And, I have to admit, he’s got one of the best battle songs in the entire Pokémon series! The cool thing about Miror B is that, once defeated, he actually leaves Team Cipher in order to set up his own group, whom you meet in the sequel. He might never quite renounce his wicked ways, but for entertainment, you don’t get much better than this.

It’s certainly a shame we never got to see more of Miror B, as Game Freak seems determined to ignore Genius Sonority’s twin masterpieces.

Yep, this is one for the OGs. I remember back in Primary school, when rumours of Mew being under the truck were still floating around, there was another rumour about the dark side of the kind and generous Mr. Fuji.

The thing is, this one actually holds water, unlike the Mew story. You first find Mr. Fuji in Lavender Town, looking after orphaned Pokémon, and defending the Marowak from Team Rocket in Pokémon Tower. We later learn that Mr. Fuji and Blaine were good friends, and that they both worked on a project in the burned-down Pokémon Mansion over on Cinnabar.

Of course, the theory is that Mr. Fuji was one of the scientists who created Mewtwo through genetic experimentation on Mew. He was so traumatised by his creation that he vowed never to hurt a Pokémon again, and moved to Lavender Town to atone for his misdeeds.

This theory is reflected in the Pokémon Origins series and in Dr. Fuji in Mewtwo Strikes Back. We also know Mr. Fuji travelled to Faraway Island and left a note for the trainer before they find Mew. Was this the original Mew used to create Mewtwo?

However, I'm fairly certain we never actually got the official answer to this theory in the mainline games. For now, we’ll have to rely on theories to sate our appetite for Pokémon lore! Maybe one day we’ll know for sure?
