The two things Roberto De Zerbi demanded from Luca Williams-Barnett's first Tottenham start as 18-year-old star signs new lucrative deal, writes MATT BARLOW

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Published: 08:08 EST, 22 November 2026 | Updated: 18:17 EST, 24 November 2026

Having summoned Luca Williams-Barnett to his office to let the teenager know he would be making his first Premier League start, Roberto De Zerbi could not resist a little bit of fun.

De Zerbi cast a serious demeanour and told him he had weighed the options and figured the best course of action would be 60 minutes with the Under-21s.

Williams-Barnett did not hide his disappointment. 'Why, do you think you are ready to start for the first team?' asked the Spurs boss. The 18-year-old said he thought so.

De Zerbi cracked a smile and told him he was in the team against Ipswich but demanded two things in return.

First, that his young star would play with freedom as though 'in the park with friends' and second that he would work without the ball in the manner of the world's best players in his position, such as Lamine Yamal.

This is where the comparisons to Yamal emerged soon after Williams-Barnett lit up the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium. On Tuesday, he was rewarded for his progress with a new contract at the club.

He said: 'This is a special moment for me and my family, and one I have worked really hard to reach. But this is only the start.

Luca Williams-Barnett scored his first Premier League goal for Tottenham on his first start in the top flight

The talented academy youngster may not be lightning fast but he has pace and creates goals

'Spurs is a family to me. I've been here for so long – it's a second home, where I trust the people and coaches who have pushed me to become the player I am today.

'The club has trusted and believed in me, now I'm ready to repay that.'

Hopes are indeed high. Similarities with Yamal are evident in physique, his fluency of movement on the ball and the bursts of pace, the way they look to play. Even the hairstyle.

Just as Yamal, a left footer, likes to operate wide on the right for Barcelona and Spain, De Zerbi deployed Williams-Barnett as a right footer on the left, although most of his youth career has been spent centrally in the No 10 position.

He does not possess what you would consider in modern football to be exceptional pace, but he is quick, and his strengths are on the ball. He likes the ball. He creates. And he scores goals.

From wide on the left against Ipswich, he scored within 15 minutes, darting in at the back post to finish, and created the second for Dominic Solanke, with a 60-yard run from inside his own half and cross with his left foot.

His brilliance lit up the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, where there has not been a lot to celebrate in the last 18 months. Terrible home form was the crux of last season's relegation fears.

Here, they rose to salute a new talent, one of their own. One De Zerbi believes has the attitude to match his outrageous talent. Confident, certainly. Aware of his natural gifts and his technical skills. Perhaps bordering on arrogance when on the pitch.

De Zerbi was pleased to see Williams-Barnett, in a seat nearby on the team bus from the training ground to the stadium, watching YouTube clips of Ronaldinho, Eden Hazard and Neymar.

The Spurs boss, who has faith in the power of inspirational video footage, took this as a positive sign. The youngster was relaxed but more than this he had a passion for football and always looking to learn.

'I'm positive about his character and behaviour because I've been working with him for three or four months and I know him enough,' said De Zerbi, whose tactical instructions involved James Maddison operating closely to Williams-Barnett, a kindred spirit in a footballing sense able to talk him through the game.

When the teenager set off on the swaggering run for Solanke's goal, Maddison could be seen nearby urging him forward into the ocean of space left open by Ipswich.

The 18-year-old had rival clubs circling but Spurs have now tied him down

Born and raised in Luton, Williams-Barnett comes from a strong, grounded family - which will only help as he looks to assimilate into the senior side

Spurs have prioritised Premier League experience in the transfer market under De Zerbi to create a more mature dressing room environment. Signings such as Andy Robertson have been key to this.

The policy is not purely for the benefit of academy graduates stepping into the first-team environment, but it does help when it comes to setting the standards required in elite sport.

Anyone gifted enough to break into the Spurs squad will have been feted as a star through the youth development phase and well rewarded to resist others trying to lure them away.

Williams-Barnett was under contract until 2028 but his new deal means clubs circling must now look elsewhere.

It is easy for impressionable teens to become conceited in this situation. Williams-Barnett has had discipline and time-keeping issues but most at Spurs speak of a young man devoted to his football from a strong, loving family who are sure to keep him grounded.

He was born and raised in Luton, starting out in Luton Town's pre-academy to Under 10s, before Spurs paid a small fee for him.

His father Jonathan Barnett was a central midfielder in Step Three of non-league football, known as 'Jono' and playing for various clubs in the region including Dunstable Town and Arlesey Town and renowned for his passing, set pieces and with an eye for goal.

Although more of a dribbler, Luca grew up with football in the family, polishing his technical skills with his dad in the back garden. 'He is from a very good family and it's crucial for this type of players,' said De Zerbi.

Spurs have been here before, of course, with talented teens breaking through with N17 at their feet.

There was Mikey Moore's wonderful cameo against AZ Alkmaar after which Ange Postecoglou called for patience and commonsense, and Maddison went on TV and said it was 'like having Neymar on the left wing'.

There was Jose Mourinho taking the match ball from Son Heung-min, who had just scored the most incredible solo goal against Burnley, and handing it to Troy Parrott on his Premier League debut.

There was Mauricio Pochettino dubbing Marcus Edwards his 'mini Messi'. Pochettino soon came to wish he had not burdened Edwards with such lavish praise.

The pitfalls are well known and progress is rarely a smooth upward trajectory. It is a balancing act between maximising potential and getting carried away.

De Zerbi is quietly confident Williams-Barnett has what it takes to handle the hype.

On the new deal, thought to be for five years, the manager said: 'Luca is a player I think the world of, and I'm so happy that he has signed a new contract.

'He has benefitted from being around the first team every day, growing in stature and consistency, and has deserved his place in the matchday squad over recent weeks.

'Everyone can see how technically gifted Luca is on the pitch but I also love his calmness and decision-making under pressure.

'The best players have confidence on the pitch and humility off it. There is still plenty of room for Luca to improve, and I know he will continue to work hard every day to be the best version of himself.'
