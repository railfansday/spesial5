Where to Donate Used Furniture – 12 Places and Which Ones Pick It Up

Match each piece of used furniture to a place that will take it, since rules on size, condition, and mattresses vary. Many Salvation Army and Habitat for Humanity ReStore locations pick up sofas and dressers for free, and veterans’ groups collect smaller pieces left outside your door. Get a receipt if you’ll deduct the gift.

Americans threw out 12.1 million tons of furniture and furnishings in 2018, and about 80% of it went to landfills, according to the EPA. A usable sofa or dresser doesn’t have to end up there.

Size and condition decide where a piece can go. Charities with trucks and sales floors take big pieces like sofas, dressers, and dining sets, while veterans’ groups pick up only what one person can lift. Many places also refuse mattresses and anything torn, stained, or broken, because they have to resell what they take or hand it to a family.

Clear out the drawers first. Clothes and electronics have charities of their own.

Under IRS Publication 526, donated furniture is deductible only if it’s in good used condition or better and you itemize deductions rather than take the standard deduction.

The deduction for people who don’t itemize, new in 2026, covers cash gifts only. And starting with 2026, itemizers can deduct only the part of the year’s charitable gifts above 0.50% of adjusted gross income, which is total income after certain adjustments. On an adjusted gross income of $80,000, for example, the first $400 you give in the year isn’t deductible.

A gift of $250 or more needs a written acknowledgment from the charity that describes what you gave. Noncash deductions totaling more than $500 in a year require Form 8283, attached to your return.

When the truck comes or you drop a piece off, ask for a receipt that describes each item, and value each one at what it would sell for used, usually far less than you paid.
