6 Gear Items You Definitely Don’t Need on Your Next Thru-Hike

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Gear is fun. Who doesn’t love gear! But in the ultralight philosophy, you should actually strive to carry less of it. A minimalist backpacking setup won’t just save you money; it will keep your base weight low so you spend less time thinking about your aching knees and more time actually enjoying the trail. Remember: every ounce you don’t carry is an ounce you can brag about to all the other hikers around you.

Today we’re taking ultralight to a whole new level with 6 surprising gear items you definitely will not need as a thru-hiker.

Ed. note: We don’t know who needs to hear this, but this article is a joke. Always carry appropriate gear anytime you go hiking and be as self-sufficient as possible.

What have you eaten in the last 24 hours on your thru-hike? Let’s see: two gas-station chimichangas, several feet of meat sticks, frosting out of a container, a tortilla slathered with peanut butter and cheese slices, an entire bag of Nerds Gummy Clusters, and a giant pot of instant mashed potatoes.

Hmmm. And you actually think you’re going to need cathole supplies? You haven’t had a bowel movement since Neel Gap, sweet prince. Might as well embrace it and shave some ounces from your bathroom kit.

Just because it’s called backpacking doesn’t mean you need an actual backpack. Grandma Gatewood did the whole AT with a homemade denim duffle bag slung over one shoulder. Who needs shoulder straps when you have sheer force of will?

As an added bonus, this one-shoulder setup will motivate you to keep your base weight as light as possible.

Why bother with all these annoying small items when everyone else is already carrying them? You can always just borrow a lighter or bum filtered water and snacks off your fellow hikers. Don’t worry, they don’t mind.

In fact, repeatedly asking other hikers to lend or donate the supplies they paid money for and put in effort to carry is a great way to build a sense of fellowship. They get to feel valued in the community, and you get all the benefits of their gear and food without having to put in any effort yourself — it’s a win-win!

Whoops, I’m out of water again? No worries; Hot Sauce will hook me up. Right, Hot Sauce? Hot Sauce?

It’s often said (by me) that you have to lose yourself before you can find yourself. Take that philosophy to a whole new level by entering the backcountry alone without a map, GPS, smartphone, or any form of navigation!

A thru-hike is an excellent opportunity to dust off your primal tracking skills. Trampled vegetation, broken twigs, and random sets of footprints meandering aimlessly through the snowy woods are the only signposts you need.

Look, if you get lost, you can just ask other hikers for directions. Problem solved. If you feel awkward about asking, you can just follow them surreptitiously. They won’t mind, I’m sure. And I’m sure they’re following the same route as you. Why would they be going somewhere else?

A tent is often one of the heaviest and most expensive items in a hiker’s kit. That’s why you should definitely just not bring one. You can just sleep in that designated emergency shelter marked on the map instead. It’s the ultimate life hack.

What do you mean, not carrying a tent doesn’t count as an actual emergency? It’s raining! What do you mean rain isn’t an emergency? Yes it is. YES IT IS! LET ME IN!

Sell all your gear, give up on backpacking, and watch your base weight plummet to zero (0) ounces!

Hear me out. Walking the Appalachian Trail takes 6 months, but you could drive the entire length of the trail from Georgia to Maine in just a few days. What’s even the point of thru-hiking? Why are any of us even here? We could all be on our couches watching Netflix and eating pizza right now. Meh. This is stupid. Let’s just go home.

Going ultralight might not be easy, but in the modern thru-hiking landscape there’s really no excuse for anyone to have a base weight over 10 pounds. From embracing six months of horrific constipation to simply giving up on thru-hiking altogether, there are all kinds of clever ways to lower your base weight. All it takes is commitment and a little creativity.
