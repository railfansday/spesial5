Rams’ injuries provide silver lining of depth chart changes

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Using a punishing ground attack, the Los Angeles Rams methodically overwhelmed the Arizona Cardinals 34-6 in Sunday's Week 10 matchup. L.A. overcame another slow start, trailing the Cards well into the second quarter before the offense came alive to the tune of 436 yards. With injuries stacking up, the Rams offense was forced to improvise, both in new formations and personnel groupings.

Sets and names that Rams fans were not familiar with.

Head coach Sean McVay gave Arizona a steady diet of three tight end formations, but also mixed in two running back sets split on each side of QB Matthew Stafford's shot gun and he used a handful of jumbo blocking packages with six offensive linemen. It was bully ball and the Rams pounded out 200 yards on the ground.

The Rams wide receiver unit, 'The Island of Misfit Toys" edition, stood up quite well. Konata Mumpfield (48 reps), rookie CJ Daniels (46), Tutu Atwell (16), and the recent return from exile, Xavier Smith (9) all made plays. Running back Ronnie Rivers (17) chipped in 81 rushing yards. On the offensive line, Beaux Limmer (76) got the start at guard and after the Rams lost not one, but two centers, he moved into the pivot. Bill Murray (49) jumped in at guard, his first regular season action since Week 5 of 2024.

Is relying on a group of lesser-knowns down the stretch sustainable?

Staff feeds off emotion and excitement, the new offensive changes seemed to energize him. I didn't see him shooting the finger pistols, but he clearly was playing with emotion.

L.A. trotted out two running back formations on 17 plays and ironically, threw the football on every one of them. The Rams still ran for 200+ yards. Over this four-game win streak, the Rams have out-gained their opponents by nearly 450 yards on the ground. Corum gets the highest snap count, play time percentage, and number of touches in his career. To close out the final 12 minutes of the game, Rivers humped the rock on 15 consecutive plays.

Coming into the game with 261 total receiving yards amongst them, the Rams young corps booked 174 for Sunday's game alone. Even better was reeling in 70 percent of targets. Time and again, they found open areas, whether by scheme, created on their own, or the Cardinals ineptitude. All had catches for nice gains. Atwell's dropped pass near the goal line, may let Smith sneak by him on the depth chart.

L.A. used three tight ends on 63.5 percent of reps, 47 plays, running the football on 40 of them. They weren't just pack horses carrying the run game load, they combined to haul in six of seven passes and notch two touch downs.

Beaux Limmer, Kevin Dotson, Warren McClendon (76, 100%)

When you can pound out 46 rushing attempts, you've had a good showing by the big uglies. L.A. used Quessenberry as a sixth lineman on a handful of snaps, something fans have wanted to see. Poor McMahon, he gets a shot at his first extended action since Week 18 of the 2024 season and gets hurt. Shelton has had a solid season, it will be a big loss if he's out for extended time. It looked like he tweaked a hamstring getting out on a second quarter screen play. Limmer showed his versatility, it was his style of game, a slobberknocker run fest. Murray played fair to well, considering it was his first action since Week 5 of 2024 and cold call to boot.

Backup cornerback Damarion Williams rates a shout out, he's been a practice squad elevation in the two games since being signed. He's had a kick coverage tackle in both. Really gets after it and plays with hustle and emotion. Hard to say what the Rams will do with him once his three PS elevations are up, but if he stacks another good game after the bye, it will be a tough decision.

Dominating performance from the first line of defense. On only 36 defensive snaps, they allowed a paltry 34 yards rushing between the tackles and logged 10 tackles with 3.5 sacks. In support work, Hamilton has been getting good push in the past few games and Murchison got his hand up to knock down a short pass. With opposing teams forced into the short, quick passing attack, I'd like to see more hands up in the passing lanes.

While Garrett was quiet, Young stacked another solid outing. Stewart's time on the field has been trending steadily down and his production has run hot and cold. Bailey is still in the learning process, but if/when he can match game savvy with his athletic traits and effort, the Rams will have a keeper.

Interesting happenings here. Landman's snaps and play percentage have gone down for three straight games. It could be that he's been nicked up a lot this season, but worth watching. Most of reps for Dolac and Speights came late, after the Rams had put 31 points on the board. Dolac hasn't received a huge increase, but has double-digit reps in three of the last four games and produced well. Speights' play time is trending down ever so slightly over the past three weeks, but production has been very consistent.

If you're only giving up 108 yards passing, the secondary had a solid day. Two plays accounted for nearly half of that total. One for 21 yards into the soft area of a two-deep zone and Lake got burnt in man coverage for a 31-yard explosive. Lake hasn't been lining up much in the slot, about 15 percent of the time, and even less on wide receivers. The Cards caught him on one of those times. One thing that stood out was McCollough's snap counts and play percentage, down substantially from the past four games.

It feels as if the Rams have endured a long, arduous journey getting to 6-4, but have a grasp, however tenuous, of a wildcard seed. Now they've earned 10 days to scab up and get heads straight for the stretch drive. You cannot overplay, over state, or over hype the importance of the Thanksgiving Eve tilt with the Green Bay Packers. It is a necessary first step for a successful conclusion to the season.

There is reason for optimism. On offense, Sean McVay broke away from his standard approach. Yes, still controlled and methodical, but just enough of a tweak to freshen things up. Establishing a run game early, using an extra offensive lineman to gain a numbers advantage, lining up two running backs, and attacking the whole field, both vertical and horizontal. It's vintage McVay, just updated packaging. Defensively, L.A. has been, excepting the Buffalo Bills game, stingy versus both run and pass. Getting Aaron Donald and Myles Garrett together for 40-45 reps per game is close to a sure winning bet and the secondary likes the mixing in of man coverage with all the zone.

Although the Cards are a struggling team and L.A. would have likely won with the old ways, McVay put the Rams stretch opponents on notice, neither the offense or defense can be put into a box. Even if the offense goes back to Nacua/Adams pass-centric ways, opposing teams must account and game plan for the Rams new-look, multi-faceted offensive attack.
