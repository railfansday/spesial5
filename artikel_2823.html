Has Bo Nix quieted early-season criticisms ahead of Las Vegas Raiders-Denver Broncos Week 11 matchup?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Week 11 reignites a longstanding AFC West rivalry between the Las Vegas Raiders and Denver Broncos. To preview the game, Silver and Black Pride reached out to our frenemies at Mile High Report, and Ian St. Clair was kind enough to answer a few questions about the Broncos for Raider Nation.

Q: Bo Nix faced a lot of criticism at the beginning of the season for not pushing the ball down the field. Has that changed over the last handful of games? Is ending last season with a broken ankle impacting how he's been playing in 2026?

A: No matter what happens on the field, Nix is one of those players who will always face criticism. It's also important to add some context for why things may unfold on the field over the course of a game. Early in the season, Nix and the Broncos offense played some of the best defenses in the NFL. On top of that, Denver's running game was horrendous, and that's even putting it mildly.

Opposing defenses knew they could stop the Broncos rushing attack with minimal effort and play two-high safeties. In situations like that, it's going to be hard for a quarterback or offense to push the ball down the field. However, Nix and Denver did open it up a bit more against the Carolina Panthers. Early in the game, Nix took some deep shots that set up the offense for points. We will likely see a continuation of that this Sunday.

As for Nix's injury, there's no doubt it impacted him earlier in the season. I'd say more so mentally than physically. All the physical rehab in the world doesn't rehab the mind until you actually do it and prove to yourself that you can do it. The last few games have been more like the Nix we saw before the injury, and belief and confidence are reemerging.

Q: On a related note, Jaylen Waddle was the Broncos' biggest offseason addition but has just 30 catches for 337 yards and two touchdowns through nine games. What were the expectations for Waddle heading into the season, and what's been holding him back from meeting them?

A: The expectations were high that Waddle would bring an element to Denver's offense that would open it up. Nix and the Broncos would be able to take deep shots and use his speed in the middle of the field. It's been surprising how inconsistent that has been thus far. We've seen flashes, but not nearly enough. Obviously, what I mentioned earlier with the focus opposing defenses can put on the passing game plays into that, but it's also not utilizing him properly. Plus, the fact that Nix has been inconsistent and struggled hasn't helped. Coming out of the bye, I think we'll start to see Waddle play a bigger role in the Broncos offense.

Q: Who would you say is more important to Denver's defense and why: Nik Bonitto or Pat Surtain II?

A: My answer might change in five minutes, but I'll go with Bonitto. Mind you, I think that Surtain is the best cornerback in football. He's one of those cornerbacks who shuts down half the field. However, having a game-changing edge rusher in Bonitto sets the rest of the defense up for success, including Surtain.

What Bonitto brings and how effective he is reminds me of Von Miller, who was the Super Bowl 50 MVP. At any moment, Bonitto can make a play that changes a game. Or, in the case against the Panthers, win it. He's become one of the best edge players in the league, and he just brings so much to the Broncos' defense.

Q: Seahawks head coach/defensive play caller Mike Macdonald gave Fernando Mendoza trouble last week by throwing the book at the rookie with several exotic pressure looks. Should Mendoza expect the same from Vance Joseph this week? What can you tell us about Joseph's scheme that could give a quarterback making his second career start fits?

A: The scheme is only as good as the players who execute it, and that's where I think Mendoza will face similar challenges this Sunday. Denver is one of the best defenses in the NFL, if not the best, at pressuring the quarterback. Even if Joseph doesn't call a blitz, the Broncos front will still make life miserable for Mendoza with Bonitto, Jonathan Cooper, Zack Allen, and Co.

But then there are the times Joseph will send a cornerback or safety on a blitz out of package that Mendoza isn't expecting. But the biggest reason Mendoza will face pressure is the Raiders' currently ineffective running game. Being one-dimensional on offense is never a friend to a rookie quarterback making his second start against a great defense.

Q: Can you give us one player on each side of the ball who Raider Nation might not know much about but could make an impact in this game? A couple of the Broncos' up-and-comers, if you will.

A: On offense, let's go with rookie running back Jonah Coleman. Though I'm pretty sure Las Vegas fans know of Coleman. But he is what makes Denver's running game click. Coleman's has good vision, good speed, and runs with power. He's been fun to watch, and I would expect him to get more carries on Sunday.

On defense, there really isn't anybody who Raiders Nation hasn't heard of. So I'll go with cornerback Ja'Quan McMillian. The fourth-year undrafted defensive back has a knack for making a key play when Denver needs it, whether an interception or a sack.
