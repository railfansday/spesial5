Anthropic IPO: For hundreds of billions of dollars flowing into AI startups, a big debut is a must

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Artificial intelligence has propelled big gains in venture capital funds this year, and a flood of money — over $350 billion — into AI startups. Venture funds were up close to 18% through the first half of 2026, with 86% of U.S.-based VC investor money going into AI companies. With Anthropic set to make its public market debut this week at a reported valuation of $1.4 trillion, the market's reception to the deal and early trading in its shares may set the tone for the next leg in the private market AI boom.

It has been a period of increased volatility for tech stocks and the public market AI trade. In addition to concerns about balance sheet strength and valuation, midterm elections showed widespread public opposition to data centers, and the CEO scandal rocking Nvidia, the "central bank" of AI financing, has compounded risks in the sector.

But on the private side of tech investing, firms are closely watching how investors react to Anthropic's IPO. "I think that will end up guiding further intentions for the VC space," said Mike McLelland, managing director of investments within private markets at Cleveland, Ohio-based Clearstead. "Once you have a couple of good exits, it gives people a little more confidence in the realization story."

Anthropic is getting some deep-pocketed support for the deal. Altimeter Capital is aiming to invest $5 billion, according to its founder and CEO Brad Gerstner, who noted that was five times as much as he has ever invested in an IPO. "I think this is a cornerstone compounder for a long time," Gerstner told CNBC's "Halftime Report" on Monday. "It's where the trade is going."

Chip giants Advanced Micro Devices, Broadcom, Nvidia, Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company and Micron are all investing, as are Google and Microsoft.

The direction of the IPO market is important given that companies have been staying private longer than they did in the past and strategic exits have been relatively muted, McLelland said. A notable exception is Google's $32 billion acquisition of cloud security firm Wiz. That 2025 deal was Google's largest ever, overtaking its $12.5 billion purchase of Motorola in 2012.

VC gains have been concentrated in a small number of large funds that hold the biggest AI-linked companies. MSCI reports that the 10 largest holdings make up 22% of the value of its venture index.

That concentration comes amid improved performance.

Venture capital funds returned 17.8% in the first six months of 2026, according to Opto Investments, using proprietary and MSCI Private Capital Solutions data. Venture fund portfolio data is reported by fund general partners on a delayed basis. The six-month gains would equate to a full year return as high as 38% if the pace is maintained. It comes on the heels of a recent period of underperformance for the asset class, with a 6.19% return over five years, according to Juan Mier, lead analyst of fund strategies at PitchBook. Venture's 10-year average return is 13% annually, according to PitchBook. For the year ended March 2026, venture capital delivered a nearly 22% return, driven in large part by AI valuations, Mier said.

It is not just the largest AI companies that are venture-backed and tied to the IPO performance of the giants. Thousands of smaller companies that supply AI giants with infrastructure, component manufacturing, and data services, for example, have also seen their valuations rise quickly, said Jake Miller, co-founder of New York-based Opto Investments, which helps investment advisors design private investment programs.

Notably, AI captured $355.9 billion, or 86% of U.S. venture dollars in the first half of 2026, according to Clearstead analysis of PitchBook data. Globally, AI deal value reached $521.4 billion over the trailing twelve months. That's more than double the prior year, even as AI deal count fell 21.6% from the previous quarter.

Mier said venture capital returns are expected to moderate in the third quarter as valuations in the AI space in public markets have adjusted, and that flows to private markets eventually. "That may moderate returns for the second half of the year," he said. Anthropic's deal, reported to be on target for a $1.4 trillion valuation, is well below the $2 trillion deal value originally floated by bankers.

But amid the AI proliferation, industry participants expect VC to continue piquing investors' interest in the coming years. "It's been a great place to be, and it's going to be a great place to be given the momentum of venture companies," said Jeff Masom, head of U.S. distribution and global wealth management for private markets at Franklin Templeton, which manages several private markets investments.

That said, some investors are getting more discerning. "We see those opportunities among early-stage — Seed, Series A and Series B — companies rather than trying to get in on a later-stage company at a higher price," said Opto Investments' Miller. He's looking for opportunities to buy stakes in lower-valuation companies that could climb 10 times to 20 times from their current value through an IPO or acquisition.

Private infrastructure, meanwhile, returned 4.2% over the first six months of the year, a pace of about 8.5% a year, according to Opto Investments and MSCI data. "That is close to infrastructure's 10-year average of just under 10% a year," said Miller, but good enough to be the second-best among major private fund categories through the first half of 2026, ahead of private credit (2.6%) and buyout funds (1.4%). Private credit has been hit hard by fears about lending to software companies that could be rendered uncompetitive by AI, while private equity funds have struggled to sell portfolio companies at a profit this year, with a huge backlog of portfolio holdings.

But the AI boom has also benefited investors in infrastructure funds, given the needs for power, data centers, and fiber, areas where activity has been the strongest. "It's what's been driving most of those returns in the last 18 months or so," said Uday Karri, a member of MSCI's Private Capital Research team.

A recent MSCI report on private infrastructure showed that the index returned 11.9% over the 12 months through June 2026, compared with 7.7% for the median asset. Returns are based on MSCI's asset-level index, which measures underlying asset performance before fund fees, according to a spokesperson.

There's been tremendous growth in the past few quarters within infrastructure, and there continue to be opportunities for investors in the asset class, especially in AI-ready and liquid-cooled data centers, he said. Of course, midterm elections could slow new data center development through tighter permitting and regulation. In Texas, for example, Democratic governor-elect Gina Hinojosa campaigned on a statewide moratorium on new data center permits. Shortly after her election, the new governor stressed in an interview with CNN that the key is creating state laws that protect Texas communities, not banning data centers.

With more political pressure related to data center construction, the infrastructure sector may benefit from expected opportunities outside the AI infrastructure build-out over the next several years. "Power, grid connections, water, cooling, fiber, and permitting are likely to shape where capacity can be delivered. Shifting trade routes, geopolitical disruption, and more frequent extreme weather are supporting investment in ports, railways, intermodal terminals, cold-storage networks, and other strategic transport infrastructure," according to a report from BlackRock's Preqin division.

Legislation is also advancing that could raise project costs for the biggest data centers, but potentially have more positive impacts on broader infrastructure projects. The Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA), a sweeping bill on energy infrastructure, is "the most comprehensive permitting bill to date," according to The Bipartisan Policy Center. If passed, it is expected to increase the speed of project permitting and reduce delays.

Here too, investors need to be discerning. "For investors, the key point is that asset selection can matter more than regional allocation. Larger portfolios can spread this dispersion across many assets and are more likely to track the index. Smaller portfolios hold fewer assets, so their results may differ materially from the index in either direction," MSCI wrote.

Private credit did perform better over the trailing 12 months ending in March, 8.3%, while real estate debt and core-plus strategies returned 7.2% and 5.1%, respectively, according to data from Preqin.

"The broader takeaway is that private markets are not moving in lockstep," Pei Yin, director at Preqin Private Markets Research, said in an email. "Venture capital has shown stronger return momentum, infrastructure continues to benefit from long-term investment themes, while private credit and selected real estate strategies have offered more income-oriented sources of return."
