Final predictions for South Carolina at Clemson: Key matchups, storylines and more

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Cigars and No Cigar Today. Five-bombs and Sevens-up. Orange britches and Stormtroopers. The 123rd time.

All you have to do is look at the Arkansas game to see what happens when QB LaNorris Sellers isn't on the field. He has to play (he will), and he has to play well (to be determined).

Cornerbacks Judge Collier and Vicari Swain have major stars next to their names on Clemson's scouting charts. That means, "Go at these guys." Whether it's matched up with receivers 1-on-1 downfield or scheming ball-carriers to go at them, those two have to make plays when they arrive.

Chris Denson, QB: The national spotlight will be on starting quarterback Trent Pearman, and for good reason: A former walk-on starting against his lifelong rival is a great story. But in terms of game impact, Denson has more potential to make an explosive play under center. The sophomore quarterback-turned-receiver-turned-quarterback will be featured in packages designed for him to run the ball. Against Duke, he threw only two passes (both incomplete) but ran 16 times for 71 yards and a touchdown.

Will Heldt, DE: Clemson defensive coordinator Tom Allen said it best: "Getting to (Sellers) is one thing, the next thing is getting him on the ground." If Clemson wants to have a prayer at neutralizing the offensive threat that is Sellers from running all over Death Valley again, Heldt will be the key. The Purdue transfer is Clemson's sacks (7) and tackles for loss (14) leader in his second season with the Tigers. Heldt is likely to leave Clemson and hear his name called in the NFL draft — can he end his year with an impactful performance in the season's most important game?

The Gamecocks' offensive line has to protect Sellers and open holes for the running game, since that's the best way to keep Clemson at bay and control the game. The Tigers' defensive front stacked eight, sometimes nine in the box against Duke to limit Nate Sheppard and it worked, leaving their backfield to harass the quarterback.

Something's got to give between South Carolina's defensive backs and Clemson's wide receivers. The Gamecocks are last in Division I FBS for turnovers (6) and 122nd in interceptions (4). USC allows 229.4 yards passing per game, good for bottom-five in the SEC. Meanwhile, Clemson is 199th in passing offense and is tied with Army for last in the nation with four passing touchdowns this season (the only catch is Army has thrown 231 fewer passes). Much of that has to do with drops, something Clemson — and particularly WR1 T.J. Moore — have consistently struggled with most of the year. The Tigers are likely to get open vs. South Carolina's secondary, but there's no telling if they'll actually catch the ball.

Shane Beamer will be back for a seventh season. But there's plenty of grumbling about how he coached his sixth season, and it will only intensify with a loss in the rivalry game. Although the Gamecocks have never been consistently good through their 133-year history, fans want to see consistently good since Steve Spurrier showed them it can be done. They'll still buy tickets and be there next season, but another season entered while on the hot seat will not be conducive toward making plans for an eighth year.

Because of Pearman's heroics vs. Duke and the unwavering impact this rivalry has on the entire state, the future of Dabo Swinney and his relationship with Clemson football is a narrative that's been shelved for the time being. But make no mistake, it will return regardless of this weekend's result. Clemson has a chance — no matter how slim — to reach 9-4 a year after going 7-6. It's an improvement, sure, but look under the hood and you'll find Clemson was thoroughly outclassed by LSU and Miami while losing inexplicably against Virginia Tech and Georgia Tech.

Many point to these shortcomings as a roster construction issue, particularly in terms of whiffing on offensive line transfers and choosing not to pursue a veteran QB. Swinney, through his actions and words, has long seemed averse to heavy portal usage. Now fans and outsiders are beginning to connect the two points.

Meanwhile, Swinney and Graham Neff have spoken about Clemson's efforts to create a centralized office to determine player valuation. The Tigers, according to reports from The Athletic, spent roughly $30 million on this season's roster, and like most programs will increase that spend for 2027. Swinney has raised his voice in support for this central office while also reiterating that he and his internal staff will make the roster decisions.

With this much effort and investment going into valuation and roster building, it seems inevitable that conversations will take place between Swinney and the administration about what roster-building looks like for Clemson in the future. With signing day a week away and the portal opening in January, some of those conversations will happen quickly after the season's end.

Cloninger prediction: South Carolina 30, Clemson 14

Lot of questions about what USC will look like, and deservedly so. Can they handle Denson running at them? Can the defensive backs limit Bryant Wesco and Moore? Can Sellers complete passes against a dangerous secondary and move the ball without his top running back? Can the Gamecocks meet the moment?

Castellano prediction: South Carolina 24, Clemson 10

The storybook ending for Pearman and the Tigers is cut short as Sellers makes the 14-point difference between the two rivals with his legs alone. The Gamecocks survive despite early turnovers which send both teams to halftime tied at 10. Clemson still has a bowl game to play, but chatter about what Swinney and his program must do to build back better in 2027 will begin to swirl directly after Sellers takes his final knee on the orange paw and the clock strikes zero on Clemson's eighth power-conference home loss since 2025.
