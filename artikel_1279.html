Supreme Court makes CVC vote annulment final, but LaLiga’s December deal approval stands

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Spain's Supreme Court has refused LaLiga's appeal, making final the annulment of the August 2021 assembly vote on the CVC deal, a case won by Real Madrid and Athletic Club. It is a legal win for the two clubs, but the later December 2021 approval behind LALIGA Impulso remains in force.

Spain’s Supreme Court has thrown out LaLiga’s cassation appeal against the Madrid Provincial Court judgment on the CVC operation. The General Assembly resolution of 12 August 2021, which approved the deal, therefore stays void for good. Real Madrid and Athletic Club brought the winning case. LaLiga now carries the costs of the failed appeal.

The decision closes a five-year legal fight over that single vote. In August 2021, Barcelona, Athletic Club and Real Madrid jointly announced a challenge to the resolution. They argued it broke Royal Decree-Law 5/2015 and was passed through a highly irregular process.

Real Madrid described the annulled resolution as one of huge economic significance. In the club’s account, it was passed before clubs had enough information to judge the scope, impact and consequences of changes to the operation. The club also highlighted the court’s remark that LaLiga’s appeal tried to replace the Provincial Court’s view with LaLiga’s own self-interested view.

LaLiga reads the decision more narrowly. The annulled assembly approved the term sheet of the CVC project, and the appeal fell for lack of cassation interest. LaLiga said the court issued a procedural order rather than a judgment endorsing the Provincial Court’s reasoning, and did not examine the substance of the case.

The deal itself does not depend on the annulled vote. LaLiga’s General Assembly approved the LALIGA Impulso project later, on 10 December 2021. Under the deal, clubs received around €2bn in exchange for a share of the value of the competition’s audiovisual rights over 50 years. A new company set up by LaLiga and the fund manages that arrangement. Real Madrid, Barcelona and Athletic Club rejected it and stayed out.

Those December resolutions have survived their own court test. In June 2026, Section 18 of the Madrid Provincial Court rejected in full the appeal by Athletic Club and Real Madrid against them. It upheld the first-instance judgment of 19 February 2024 and ordered both clubs to pay the costs of that hearing. The court found that clubs still own their audiovisual rights, while LaLiga sells them collectively under the law. It also ruled that creating LaLiga Group International, owned jointly by LaLiga and CVC, did not breach the Sports Law or LaLiga’s statutes.

That leaves the practical picture largely unchanged. Iusport, a Spanish sports-law outlet, described the Supreme Court order as having no practical effect on the LaLiga–CVC deal. The ruling still matters for how LaLiga is run, because it confirms that one of its assembly votes on the operation was unlawful. The league insists it respects court decisions and will keep defending the agreements backed by most of its clubs.
