This can't go on at Celtic – or can it? O'Neill fears and the McGregor dilemma

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Welcome to The Burning Questions, our new daily debate where we take one major subject in Scottish sport and tackle the three questions everyone is talking about.

Each day, our writers will offer their verdicts on the biggest issues and arguments dominating the agenda. And Celtic are first under the spotlight, with plenty to discuss after a difficult period for the club.

Martin O’Neill’s future is one of the major talking points, while a run of just three wins in 10 games has raised fresh questions over Celtic’s form and where they go from here. Is O’Neill the right man for the job in the longer term, and just how concerning has the recent run become?

Then there’s Callum McGregor. If Besiktas win on Thursday night, is it time to give the Celtic captain a European vacation and keep him fresh for the domestic challenges ahead? Liam Bryce and Graeme Young give their answers to today’s three Burning Questions.

A defeat for Celtic against Besiktas would make it three wins in 11 games. It can't go on, can it?

Liam Bryce: It can. Sunday’s defeat to an excellent Falkirk laid bare what has been obvious about this Celtic team for a long time: any sort of coherent gameplan, with some added quality, is enough to cause them major problems.

At home, they might still be able to bully their way past lesser-resourced domestic opponents most weeks, but I wouldn't confidently back them against anyone in Europe at the moment. I'm not rushing to put my house on Besiktas. Celtic are well capable of winning this game. It's just that things have deteriorated to the point that no one would be surprised if they left Glasgow with a victory.

Graeme Young: It can't. If Celtic are pulled apart at the seams by the Turkish heavyweights, then it might be one punch too many for a side who appear dazed and confused every time they enter the field.

Celtic appear incapable of competing against teams packed with pace and power. The good results in the league appear unsustainable and now the domestic form is also going backwards. The swamped fixture calendar ensures it's a long time until January, when transfer salvation may or may not lie.

But, let's be honest, there is a structural issue within this Celtic team, both with and without the ball, which runs deeper than individual personnel.

Martin O'Neill appears to veer from bullish to crestfallen. What has to happen to make his position untenable?

Liam Bryce: An awful lot more than has happened already. I can't see the Celtic board making a change in the dugout unless there's a complete implosion domestically. They will be far more reluctant to sack Martin O'Neill, a club legend, than they were Wilfried Nancy.

The 2020/21 season feels like a decent barometer of what it might take. Celtic had effectively surrendered the league title long before action was taken on Neil Lennon, who was allowed to struggle on for several months. That appointing Nancy mid-season went so badly will likely only make an already conservative hierarchy even more hesitant.

I'm not convinced they will see much cause for concern with O'Neill's side still top of the league. I can't envisage Celtic dropping enough domestic points to take themselves out of a title race, especially against opponents who have deficiencies of their own. O'Neill is going nowhere.

Graeme Young: The fixture gods appear to be in O'Neill's favour after the Besiktas game. The next three are against Kilmarnock and St Mirren at home before a trip to Portugal for a showdown with second-tier Torreense.

It looks less like a gauntlet and more like a get-out-of-jail card after recent setbacks. However, a team on the slide often can't get out of its own way. Perhaps further setbacks are hard to swerve when every fixture now feels fraught with danger.

Is it time for Celtic to see what life after Callum McGregor looks like right now?

Liam Bryce: It would be if there was anyone to take his place. But does that player exist at the moment? I don't think so.

Joel van den Berg has barely been seen and, regardless, is still just 19. Oliver Sorensen, too, remains something of a mystery. He may possess some of the physical qualities that could help plug the midfield gaps that are causing the team so many issues, but does he have the nous to be the fulcrum of the team?

I do feel somewhat for McGregor. There's a significant body of evidence that he is a far better player than we're currently seeing. I don't really buy the narrative that his legs have gone. I'm more inclined to believe he is a victim of an incoherent system.

He has never been a midfield enforcer, or even an Andrea Pirlo type, spraying passes the length and breadth of the pitch. Quick combinations, proficiency operating in tight areas and well-timed bursts forward are his primary attributes, yet he seems reduced to a vehicle for giving the ball to Liam Scales.

But he will need replacing eventually, and Celtic's recruitment seems to have ignored this eventuality.

Graeme Young: If Celtic feel the full force of Besiktas' goalscoring exploits, then it has to be time to spare Callum McGregor for the rest of the European games.

The skipper has been the reference point in the midfield for a decade, the last five years coming with the captain's armband attached. Yes, the lack of an obvious replacement is a strong argument, but there has to be an attempt to try something different.

Any call to revamp the engine room would also benefit the skipper when it comes to league duty over the busy winter period.
