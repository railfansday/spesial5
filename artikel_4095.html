My understanding, from early reporting, @awp69, is that E-Day had sold over 400k copies, and that was being reported just days after the game released. I'm not sure who is saying 300k, but it's not me. I imagine it has sold still more sine then, and it will sell more still in the current sales.

Now, is 400k a good result on its own for a game that cost at least $300 million to make (with some estimates putting it a lot more than that)? I'd argue no, that would be pretty dismal. However, with that said, we were also led to believe that it had sold 711,000 Premium Upgrades ahead of its launch, which is not to be sniffed at, and obviously we do not know how many more consoles were sold, nor how many more Game Pass subscriptions were sold. Nor do we know how much money people have been spending on the microtransactions either. All of that does add up.

The problem though comes down to simple maths, if E-Day cost $200 million (and bear in mind that is a conservative figure because we are led to believe it cost much more than that to make), but still, if it cost $200 million to make, then the game needs to sell the equivalent of 2,850,000 full priced games in order to just break even, and it is a long way short of that in sheer sales. And of course, if the game cost the reported $300 million then it needs to sell the equivalent of 4,285,000 full priced copies. This is where the problem for the game lies as it is highly unlikely to make back its development costs, which could be very bad news for the studio.

Obviously, we know that a single exclusive is not what it is all about for Xbox; it is about creating an ecosystem that will attract people in considerable numbers, and also ensure that people already here do not want to leave. However, the problem is, there are no exclusive games are on the near horizon that will continue to attract people. It's possible that the Elder Scrolls 6 will be exclusive to Xbox, and a lot of people within these forums are pinning their hopes for Xbox on that game, but the problem there is that ES6 could be several years away.

My honest opinion is that I think that Sharma will look at E-Day's (probably) meagre sales and behind the scenes the decision to reverse course on exclusives will be made, although I do not expect her to tell us this is the case until after Helix releases as I don't think Sharma will want to hurt the sales chances of Helix by publicly admitting that it won't have exclusive games. I say this on the basis that there are no big AAA console selling games on the horizon, and that Helix is likely to release prior to the next big first-party game. I mean, there's not a lot of point in telling us right now that Xbox will no longer have exclusive first-party games when there are none in the near future anyway. She may as well wait until she can no longer avoid telling us that's the case because the game is being put up for pre-orders.

Time will tell, and I'd love to be proven wrong, but I would far rather see studios survive and our favourite games continue to be made, than pursue a self-harming exclusive strategy. Of course, all that could change if (when!) Sharma announces a change in day one first-party Game Pass releases, but I think that should have been done prior to the release of E-Day, and the next big AAA first-party game is probably years away...
