Cranberry Brie Pull-Apart Bread

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

This cranberry Brie pull-apart bread recipe is bursting with melted cheese and sweet-savory cranberry butter. It's an interactive joy to eat.

I may be a year-round cranberry fan, but it’s hard to deny just how right it feels to feast on the ruby-colored fruit during the holiday season. Fresh and frozen cranberries are perfect for cranberry sauce and not-too-sweet cranberry desserts, but I also love using dried cranberries in everything from muffins to party appetizers. And my new favorite way to use dried cranberries is in cranberry Brie pull-apart bread, where they transform a loaf of cheesy bread into a holiday masterpiece.

Cranberry Brie pull-apart bread is what happens when you make a mashup of party cheese bread and cranberry butter. A crusty, round loaf of sourdough is cut in a grid pattern and stuffed with half a pound of cubed Brie. The cheese-stuffed loaf is then coated in a dried cranberry-honey butter (bolstered by orange zest, garlic and rosemary) and baked until it’s hot, crispy and melty. It’s hearty, decadent, sweet, salty and impossible to stop eating.

Preheat the oven to 350°F. In a small bowl, beat together the softened butter, finely chopped dried cranberries, honey, grated orange zest, minced garlic and salt until they’re blended.

Cut the bread into 1-inch slices to within 1/2 inch of the bottom of the loaf. Repeat cuts in the opposite direction. Insert the cubed Brie into the cuts.

Editor’s Tip: If your sourdough is crusty, a serrated bread knife will get the job done without too much elbow grease. The best bread knives slice cleanly without tearing the fluffy interior.

Spread the cranberry butter over the top of the bread. Wrap the loaf in foil and place it on a baking sheet.

Bake the bread for 20 minutes. Unwrap it and continue to bake it until the cheese is melted, about 10 minutes longer. If desired, drizzle it with hot honey before serving it.

Wrap leftover cranberry Brie pull-apart bread tightly, or transfer it to a food storage bag and refrigerate it for up to four days. To reheat it, wrap the bread in foil and bake it at 350º until it’s warm. Unwrap it and continue to bake until the cheese is melted and the bread is crisp. If the bread got pulled apart enough that it’s not really holding its shape anymore, transfer it to a baking dish, bake it until it’s melty, and eat it like stuffing.

Cranberry Brie pull-apart bread tastes best hot and crispy right out of the oven, but you can assemble the bread a few hours ahead of time, wrap it in foil, and refrigerate it until it’s ready to bake. You can also make and refrigerate the cranberry butter up to four days in advance and soften it at room temperature before using it. If you want to bring it to a party or Friendsgiving, follow good party etiquette and check with the host about whether the oven will be available to bake it when you arrive.

If your pull-apart bread is soggy, it might need to be baked longer or at a slightly higher temperature—especially if your bread is really fresh and soft. I use kitchen timers and oven thermometers to track my bakes and ensure my oven isn’t running cooler or hotter than I’d like. Don’t be afraid to bake the bread for a little longer than the recipe suggests if it hasn’t crisped on the outside or if the cheese isn’t fully melted.

Sourdough’s structure (and slightly sharp flavor) is perfect against the richness of the Brie and cranberry butter, but if sourdough isn’t for you, try a round, crusty loaf of rustic white bread. Get a loaf that weighs about 1 pound. Whole wheat and multigrain loaves can work as well, but they tend to have a denser texture and tighter crumb that won’t pull apart as easily. If you can’t find a round loaf, an oval-shaped loaf is the next best choice, but steer clear of sandwich loaves, which can’t be cut as easily into the necessary grid pattern.

Set cranberry Brie pull-apart bread out as a party appetizer next to a crudite platter, cocktail meatballs, and a selection of festive cocktails and mocktails. You can also add it to the holiday table as part of the main course, alongside a holiday ham, roast turkey or standing rib roast.
