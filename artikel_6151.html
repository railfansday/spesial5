House China panel calls for cutting defense contracts over aerospace ties to Beijing's military

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Congressional watchdog leaders Randall Schriver and Mike Kuiken warn China could exploit American chips and AI to strengthen its military.

A new congressional report is urging the U.S. to restrict investments in China’s aerospace sector and withhold taxpayer funding from companies that build military capabilities for U.S. adversaries, warning that Western commercial partnerships transferred technology and expertise to Chinese firms with military ties.

The 65-page report, titled "China's Wingmen: How Western Government Policy Enabled China's Rise in the Skies," follows a year-long investigation by the House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party, led by Chairman John Moolenaar, R-Mich., and Ranking Member Ro Khanna, D-Calif. It examined how legal commercial partnerships transferred advanced technology and manufacturing expertise to Chinese military-linked companies.

China's People's Liberation Army (PLA) is the country's military, which the committee says has benefited from civilian aerospace technology transfers.

"American and European companies transferred technology to China that could be used against our brave men and women in uniform. With the CCP’s military-civil fusion there are no private companies in China," Moolenaar said in a statement shared with Fox News Digital.

"The PLA will exploit civilian technology and use it to advance its military ambitions and that is what happened here."

The report singled out European aerospace giant Airbus, which receives U.S. defense contracts while maintaining manufacturing partnerships in China. Although Airbus sold its stake in an AVIC affiliate in 2025, its commercial partnerships with the Chinese aerospace conglomerate continue, according to the committee.

The bipartisan panel examined at least six U.S.-China joint ventures involving American defense suppliers GE Aerospace, Honeywell Aerospace and RTX subsidiary Collins Aerospace.

Investigators found that GE transferred foundational avionics technology to a joint venture with a subsidiary of AVIC, the Chinese state-owned conglomerate that develops military aircraft, including the J-20 stealth fighter. The committee said GE withheld its original joint venture agreement despite repeated requests.

The American companies maintained that their Chinese partnerships supported civilian aircraft and complied with U.S. export controls. The report also cited previous export-control settlements involving all three companies but did not establish that the partnerships under investigation violated U.S. law.

"We appreciate the Committee’s work ensuring the U.S. retains its position as the world’s commercial aviation leader while protecting our most sensitive technologies. We have and will continue to follow relevant U.S. laws and regulations, as the report acknowledges,"a Collins Aerospace spokesperson told Fox News Digital.

"GE Aerospace takes national security, compliance, and the protection of its intellectual property seriously, and we work closely with the U.S. government – including this Committee – to ensure we continue to do so," a GE Aerospace spokesperson told Fox News Digital.

GE also said its joint venture with AVIC involves older avionics technology sold exclusively in China, not jet engines, and that the arrangement includes safeguards intended to protect sensitive information and ensure compliance with U.S. law.

Honeywell Aerospace told Fox News Digital that its commercial partnerships were fully licensed and approved by the U.S. government, adding that it cooperated transparently with the committee and continues to comply with U.S. export laws.

The committee urged the Federal Aviation Administration not to certify Chinese-manufactured aircraft, citing state subsidies and Beijing's refusal to release a final report on the 2022 China Eastern Airlines crash.

"The report’s only recommendation directed at the FAA is that they do not certify Chinese-made aircraft, which the agency has not done," an FAA spokesperson told Fox News Digital.

A representative for Airbus did not immediately respond to Fox News Digital's request for comment.
