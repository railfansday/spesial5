IHG Hotel Guest Booked Direct, Finds Hotel Chained Shut—But Was Refused A Refund

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

A guest who booked directly through IHG showed up Friday night to find the hotel’s doors chained and padlocked shut. There was an office chair outside the entrance, construction supplies filled the lobby, and other guests were waiting in the parking lot wondering where to sleep. IHG arranged a room at another hotel, however: The guest says they were charged for both properties The replacement hotel was so bad they left early. They were promised a refund for the second night, but they were charged anyway. And they were on their own to get their money back for both hotels – told they had to dispute the closed hotel’s charge, even, with their bank. They were out of pocket for $566 for just one night actually spent in a room because the Garner Hotel Fort Washington in the Philadelphia suburbs wasn’t even operating. They’d booked two nights with their partner. Older negative reviews gave them pause, but they figured the rebranding and renovations might have fixed the problems. They called twice Friday to ask about a mini fridge. Nobody answered. On arrival, they found other customers stranded, with more arriving while they called IHG. By 1 a.m., IHG had arranged a more expensive stay at the Holiday Inn Lansdale–Hatfield, about 20 minutes away. When they asked about refunding the Garner booking, the agent said another department would have to handle it. The call dropped during the transfer. The guest says the replacement room was uncomfortably hot and had noisy pipes. They called to cancel the second night and were promised it would be taken care of. They cut their trip short and drove home that night. They received a bill for both Holiday Inn nights. They say IHG then refused to refund either booking, offered escalation without a timetable, and suggested disputing the Garner charge while accepting the unused Holiday Inn night. I was charged full price for both the more expensive hotel and the nonexistent hotel. I was not “given” anything. So yes, I am expecting compensation. An older listing advertised a closure from July 6 through October 31, 2026. IHG’s Fort Washington listings page labels the Garner “Opening Soon”. Its individual hotel page has also appeared labeled “New to IHG.” Commenters checking the app reported that they could still book it for that night. One local said the unfinished condition was obvious from Route 309. I’d guess rooms were loaded for the expected reopening date and nobody stopped selling them when the hotel didn’t open. But IHG Promises To Pay For The First Night Somewhere Else… The response the guest describes is deplorable, and it’s hard to square with IHG’s published reservation guarantee. IHG says a reservation secured with a credit card qualifies for its guarantee. If it can’t be honored, the original hotel is supposed to arrange comparable accommodation and transportation, cover the first night’s room and tax, and refund any advance deposit. Another IHG page promoting direct bookings expressly says “IHG will provide you with a room” if the reservation can’t be honored “for any reason” upon arrival. It doesn’t list an exception for a hotel whose doors are chained shut. The same direct booking page touts the completeness of its hotel information with the line, “After all, we own the place.” Most IHG hotels are independently owned and operated, as the page’s footer acknowledges. This Seems To Happen A Lot? In 2022, a reader booked the Holiday Inn LaGuardia Airport through IHG for the U.S. Open. Their reservations were still in their account the day before arrival. But the property had gone into foreclosure, lost its Holiday Inn identity, and wasn’t operating as the hotel they’d booked. The guest thought it looked like a homeless shelter. They wound up paying for a Hampton Inn. I’ve also covered: Courtyard Beaumont, Texas, where a guest arrived with a confirmation to find a closed hotel, an empty parking lot and a disconnected phone. They ended up at a $300 per night Residence Inn. TownePlace Suites Murfreesboro, where a Platinum member received check-in reminders for a hotel that wasn’t finished. Marriott offered a replacement costing twice as many points. Fairfield Inn & Suites Louisville North, where a guest prepaid $1,000 through Marriott and found the property closed after its owner’s bankruptcy. And guests continued arriving without warning. Fairfield East Hartford, where Marriott’s app allowed the pre-arrival check-in process even though the hotel was closed. Marriott even writes the problem into its Ultimate Reservation Guarantee: the hotel must be “open and operational” for the benefit to apply. The guarantee excludes precisely the situation where a guest most needs one. And I covered a pregnant traveler who arrived at Queens County Inn and Suites to find a homeless shelter. That reservation came through Booking.com. Booking direct is supposed to reduce the chance of this sort of failure, but it clearly isn’t enough. Garner is built to bring existing hotels into IHG quickly and cheaply. IHG promotes flexible design standards, lower opening costs and conversions completed in a little over a month. That gets owners access to IHG’s customers and gives IHG more hotels generating fees. But checking that a property is ready seems like an essential part of what the chain is selling. With a franchise, the property owner may control the charge and the refund. IHG should be covering the guest’s problem and settling up with its franchisee afterward. Telling a stranded customer to fight it out with their bank is a choice about whose money and time should be spent. A chain’s basic promise is that I can book without investigating an unfamiliar owner, whether it complies with the brand’s standards, and that the reservation will be honored. If something goes wrong, there’s a company behind it that will make good. Checking that the hotel is open ought to be the easiest standard to enforce. If I have to research whether the hotel exists as a functioning business and rely on my bank when it doesn’t, what’s the value in booking with a chain? And then what’s the value the chain is even offering to owners, when they’re no longer delivering guests?
