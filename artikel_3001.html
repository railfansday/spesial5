3 Investing Moves All Investors Should Make Right Now if a Bear Market Is Coming

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Nobody can time the next bear market, but cash reserves, diversification, and sturdy businesses can help investors prepare for volatility.

By Rick Orford – Nov 26, 2026 at 6:19AM EST

Nobody can predict exactly when the next bear market will hit, but that doesn't mean investors should wait to prepare for it. After all, if market volatility continues to rise, having the right strategy can make a big difference.

To that end, here are three investing moves to help protect portfolios today.

Cash is often put to use during bull markets because it doesn't offer the same excitement as fast-growing stocks. But when markets become volatile, having some extra cash on the side can be a huge advantage.

For one, cash can help investors avoid selling long-term holdings if they need money during a downturn. But more importantly, it gives investors flexibility to buy quality companies at a discount and hold them over the long run.

Plus, instead of trying to time the exact bottom, investors can invest gradually. This approach can help ease the pressure of making one huge investment decision at the wrong time.

That said, investors should be careful about using leverage as a shortcut to protect their portfolios or profit from a potential market reversal. In fact, if the market moves against investors, these investments can quickly see losses accelerate.

Another way to manage risk is to spread it across different companies.

Artificial intelligence (AI) has been a powerful driver of recent market rallies. However, these rallies have been concentrated in a few of the world's biggest companies. That alone creates potential risks.

Specifically, if investor expectations around AI spending reset lower, valuations could take a bigger hit than the broad market might. Still, for investors, the takeaway isn't necessarily to abandon high-growth tech stocks. Instead, it's to avoid having an entire portfolio depend on a single name or theme.

Investors might consider spreading their exposure across less correlated sectors such as healthcare, consumer staples, and financials. After all, these businesses have different economic drivers, which can help reduce the impact of weakness in any single industry.

Additionally, it's worth looking beyond individual stocks. Broad-market exchange-traded funds (ETFs), for example, can provide exposure to companies across multiple industries, while bond funds and cash equivalents can add additional diversification. Doing so can help reduce portfolio volatility and limit the losses from any single investment.

That brings us to another important consideration: the quality of companies investors actually own.

When economic conditions worsen, companies with high debt, weak cash flow, and limited pricing power can find themselves under pressure. On the other hand, businesses with healthy balance sheets, strong competitive advantages, and recurring revenue may be better positioned.

That's why investors should review a company's fundamentals before deciding whether to invest.

Specifically, long-term investors should look for companies that generate consistent free cash flow (FCF), maintain manageable debt levels, and have business models that can survive economic downturns. For example, companies selling essential products and services are likely to be more resilient when households reduce their spending.

That doesn't mean investors should automatically favor defensive stocks over growth companies. In fact, some long-term opportunities can still come from tech, industrials, and other high-growth industries. Ultimately, investors simply need to distinguish between companies benefiting from sustainable demand and those that rely mainly on hype.

Of course, there's no guarantee that financially sound companies will avoid losses during a bear market, but focusing on the fundamentals gives investors a better starting point for finding companies worth holding through market volatility.

Nobody knows for sure when the next bear market will come, but investors can already start preparing as early as today. In short, diversifying across sectors, prioritizing financially sound businesses, and maintaining cash reserves can help build a more resilient portfolio. Ultimately, the goal isn't to avoid every market decline, but to be in a stronger position when a bear market comes.
