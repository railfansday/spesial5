Five Below vs. GameStop: Which Retailer Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Five Below's rapid store expansion goes head-to-head with GameStop's cash-rich pivot, and the valuation gap may be wider than it first appears.

Can a discount retailer's aggressive expansion strategy beat a legacy gaming brand's financial pivot? Investors comparing Five Below (FIVE -1.09%) and GameStop (GME +0.47%) face a choice between rapid physical growth and deep value.

Five Below focuses on high-volume, low-cost variety goods for younger shoppers, while GameStop relies on its loyal gaming community and immense cash reserves. Both companies navigate a shifting retail environment, but their strategies for generating shareholder value through 2027 differ significantly in execution, risk, and capital allocation.

Five Below operates as a specialty retailer focused on trend-right, extreme-value products, typically priced between one and five dollars. In its latest annual report, the company notes that it targets an economically influential demographic of kids, pre-teens, and teens, alongside value-oriented shoppers. This niche positioning within retail stocks allows the business to maintain a broad vendor base of approximately 1,000 suppliers while operating 2,022 stores.

In FY 2025, revenue reached about $4.8 billion, representing a growth rate of approximately 22.9% compared to the previous year. This substantial increase in sales helped the company generate a net income of roughly $358.6 million. The net margin for the period is close to 7.5%, showcasing the company's ability to maintain profitability while aggressively scaling its store count across the United States.

As of its January 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.9x. This metric, which compares total debt to the value of shareholder equity, suggests a balanced capital structure for the retailer. The current ratio, a measure of a company's ability to pay short-term obligations with short-term assets, is roughly 2.0x, while free cash flow for the year reached around $411.7 million.

GameStop, according to its latest annual report, operates a global network of stores and e-commerce platforms specializing in video games, collectibles, and entertainment products. The company maintains critical relationships with major gaming vendors including Nintendo (NTDOF -1.16%), Sony (SONY -0.82%), and Microsoft Corp. (MSFT -2.26%), which provide the majority of its new product inventory. Through its loyalty programs, the retailer fosters a dedicated community of members who receive exclusive rewards.

In FY 2025, revenue reached around $3.6 billion, which was a decrease of approximately 5.1% compared to the prior period. Despite the decline in top-line sales, the company reported a net income of roughly $418.4 million. This resulted in a net margin of approximately 11.5%, a significant improvement in profitability that suggests more efficient management of its global retail operations.

As of its January 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.8x. The company maintains an exceptionally high current ratio of approximately 15.3x, indicating a massive surplus of liquid assets relative to its short-term liabilities. Free cash flow for the year reached around $597.3 million, supporting the company's efforts to transform its business model and invest in new opportunities.

Five Below faces intense competition from physical and online retailers, including big box stores like Walmart Inc.(WMT +0.10%) and Target Corp. (TGT +0.57%), as well as Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN -1.74%). The company is also navigating legal and litigation risks, including federal class action lawsuits related to its inventory management and growth projections. Furthermore, its reliance on foreign manufacturing, primarily in China, exposes the business to supply chain disruptions and shifting trade policies regarding tariffs.

GameStop operates in a highly competitive environment where a rapid industry shift toward digital, download-only gaming threatens its traditional physical media sales. The company also deals with strategic risks involving its investments in Bitcoin, which can introduce significant volatility to its financial results. Additionally, its reliance on a single distribution facility in Texas and significant outstanding convertible notes could limit its future financial flexibility.

GameStop appears more attractively valued based on its Forward P/E, though Five Below trades at a more modest P/S ratio relative to its sales over the past twelve months.

GameStop has benefited greatly from its recent move to emphasize trading cards and collectibles alongside its video game business, and it is paying off. It caught many investors' eyes when it made an unsolicited bid to buy eBay Inc. (EBAY -0.68%) earlier this year, too. Though that was rejected, reports say the CEO, Ryan Cohen, is still pursuing options around eBay, including striking a partnership with the business. Cohen has shown a lot of faith in his ability to boost GameStop's business. He's been a heavy buyer of company shares of late.

In its most recent quarter, net sales were $790.2 million, compared to $972.2 million in the prior year's second quarter. The decrease primarily reflects the prior-year launch of Nintendo Switch 2, planned store closures, and the divestiture of the company's France operations.

There were big positives, though: collectibles net sales were $356.3 million for the second quarter, or 45% of net sales, compared to $227.6 million, or about 23% of net sales, in the prior year's second quarter. Its pivot appears to have momentum.

Meanwhile, Five Below is one of the hottest names in brick-and-mortar retail. The company sees impressive unit economics, with each new store it opens paying back its cost in about a year, which has management focused on growing through opening more stores. The plan is to add 150 stores annually, and Wall Street analysts say that even at that pace, management appears to have a good focus on finding quality locations.

The company has really taken to social media as a driver of foot traffic, leading to a very impressive 18% increase in same-store sales in the first half of its current fiscal year 2027. Just as impressive, the company continues to expand its gross margins even as the Trump tariffs have raised costs for much of the product it sells.

For fiscal 2027, analysts see revenue growing 20% to $5.17 billion, with net income jumping 40% or more to around $360 million.

GameStop shares have been a fun ride at times. But Five Below has the profile of a once-in-a-decade retailer that promises to continue to grow well for years to come. Even though it comes at a premium based on its P/E ratio, Five Below is the choice for long-term investors.
