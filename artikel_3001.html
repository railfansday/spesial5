SOXX and DRAM Are Down Between 35.6% and 51.9% From Their Highs. Here’s an Even Better Growth Stock ETF to Buy During the Nasdaq Correction.

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Vanguard Mega Cap Growth ETF invests in both the infrastructure and application layers of artificial intelligence (AI).

By Daniel Foelber – Nov 22, 2026 at 8:15AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Vanguard Morningstar Mega Cap Growth ETF ›

Just a few months ago, the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX -10.55%) and the Roundhill Memory ETF (DRAM -13.19%) were two of the hottest exchange-traded funds (ETFs) on the market. But both ETFs are down 35.6% and 51.9%, respectively, off their highs at the time of this writing.

The sell-off has accelerated since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO of Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%). And interim CEO Colette Kress made it crystal clear on the company's Nov. 17 earnings call that Nvidia's board has no intention of bringing him back -- leaving a creative void at the top of the world's most important chip company.

It's not just the semiconductor industry that's under pressure. The Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -5.28%) is officially in a correction -- a decline of at least 10% from its high.

As tempting as it may be to scoop up beaten-down semiconductor ETFs, the Vanguard Morningstar Mega Cap Growth ETF (MGK -5.58%) could be an even better growth ETF for long-term AI stock investors. Here's why.

Investors likely gravitate toward SOXX and DRAM for their outsize weightings in top semiconductor stocks. As of Nov. 13, SOXX had a combined 38.9% weighting in just five stocks -- Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -13.25%), Broadcom (AVGO -12.19%), Micron Technology (MU -13.89%), Nvidia, and Intel (INTC -7.97%). DRAM is far more concentrated, with a 28.1% weighting in Samsung Electronics (SSNLF +0.00%) , 27.2% in Micron, and 22% in SK Hynix (SKHY -13.11%) as of Nov. 16.

The Vanguard Morningstar Mega Cap Growth ETF is also centered around a handful of leading companies. But the key difference is that these companies span all stock market sectors, not just the semiconductor industry. As of Oct. 31, Apple (AAPL -1.90%) is the largest holding at 13.8% of the ETF, followed by Nvidia at 13.1%, Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%) at 10.3%, and Alphabet (GOOG -2.59%) (GOOGL -2.70%) at 7.8%.

Investors who are curious about the semiconductor-specific exposure in MGK can note that Nvidia, Broadcom, AMD, Applied Materials (AMAT -10.71%), Lam Research (LRCX -10.02%), Intel, KLA (KLAC -10.33%), Marvell Technology (MRVL -11.69%), Sandisk (SNDK -16.05%), Seagate Technology (STX -12.35%), and Western Digital (WDC -12.02%) make up a combined 27.1% of the fund -- giving it significant pure-play exposure to the industry.

The fund gives investors significantly more tech exposure than an S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) index fund. And it's a more concentrated bet on a handful of names than the Vanguard Growth ETF (VUG -5.32%), which is more diversified, with 135 holdings compared to 52 for MGK. But MGK isn't solely invested in big tech-focused companies. Healthcare giant Eli Lilly (LLY -0.30%) makes up 4.1% of the ETF. Visa (V -0.09%) and Mastercard (MA -0.14%) are 2.3% and 1.8%, respectively.

SOXX and DRAM invest in AI stocks purely through a semiconductor lens, whereas MGK offers a far better long-term investment that should ultimately prove less cyclical and less dependent on a single theme. For example, Apple remains just a few percentage points off its all-time high because the company doesn't depend on high capital expenditures to invest in AI over the long term. Instead, its products and services are the touchpoint between users and AI applications and tools.

Microsoft, Amazon (AMZN -4.10%), and Alphabet are heavily invested in AI infrastructure through their data center build-outs. But they also have exposure to other segments of the AI value chain through services and enterprise software application layers, and consumer electronics. Additionally, Alphabet has its own leading model in Gemini. And Amazon could become one of the largest users of edge AI through robotic warehouse automation.

Eli Lilly should enjoy significant cost-reduction benefits from AI, which could allow it to accelerate drug development. There's also a slew of software companies in MGK that hyperscalers ultimately depend on to buy cloud capacity. And as the companies behind leading frontier models go public, MGK will likely gradually accumulate shares, whereas SOXX and DRAM won't, as they aren't classified as chip stocks.

SOXX and DRAM shine when customers are lining up to buy cutting-edge logic and memory chips. And there are plenty of reasons to believe that many top chip stocks remain solid buys now. But the semiconductor industry can be cyclical. And there will likely come a time when most of the growth along the AI value chain occurs downstream of pick-and-shovel plays in companies developing useful AI-powered tools and applications.

It's also worth mentioning that SOXX and DRAM have more expensive fee structures than MGK. SOXX charges a 0.33% expense ratio, and DRAM's is even higher at 0.65%. MGK charges a dirt cheap 0.05% expense ratio, or just $0.50 for every $1,000 invested.

Add it all up, and MGK is a better long-term buy for investors seeking leading growth stocks that could benefit from AI in a variety of ways, rather than solely betting on the infrastructure layer via semiconductor stocks.
