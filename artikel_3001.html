Omada: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Omada's cost to serve fell to an all-time low, and it has seemingly found a way to profit from GLP-1 drugs whether employers pay for them or not.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 5, 2026, Omada (OMDA -20.37%) raised its full-year guidance for revenue and adjusted EBITDA, or earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization. Member retention reached a record average tenure of nearly two and a half years, which drives recurring revenue and higher customer lifetime value. Lower member care costs prove that clinical scale and software automation generate real operating leverage. Three things management said stand out for long-term investors.

Omada builds digital health tools that help members manage chronic conditions like diabetes and high blood pressure between doctor visits. Patients stick with these multicondition programs far longer than basic weight-loss tracks, creating steady recurring revenue and higher lifetime value, or total expected customer profit.

The business uses artificial intelligence to streamline routine member outreach, allowing human care teams to support more patients without sacrificing quality. As members build lasting health habits, non-GAAP cost of revenue, which excludes stock compensation and measures care delivery expenses per member, fell to an all-time low of $81.

Rising healthcare costs are prompting some employers to reconsider paying for expensive GLP-1 weight-loss medications, which suppress appetite. Omada designed its prescribing options and FlexCare program to deliver clinical support whether employers cover the underlying drug or contract through pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs), which process drug claims for health plans.

Third-quarter revenue reached $91 million with positive net income, and financial results exceeded internal projections. Widespread distribution through major pharmacy benefit managers and direct benefit integrations with health plans create durable distribution advantages as leadership manages spending discipline.

Holding $243 million in cash with no debt gives the business financial strength to expand clinical technology and enter new benefit years.
