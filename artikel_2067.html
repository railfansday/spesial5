Norwich City & Stoke City have dodged a £4m transfer bullet at Bristol City’s expense

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Norwich City and Stoke City head into the two-week international break on a high and full of optimism for the rest of the campaign, as both aim to secure a top-eight finish this season, but they may have more in common than that.

Norwich are an anomaly in the Championship this season: after 15 games, the Canaries have yet to draw a single league game, winning seven and losing eight, though four wins in their last four have silenced Philippe Clement's critics for now.

Stoke, having lost all three of their opening league games this season, have bounced back to lose just one of their last 12 league games, with no team in the second tier winning more than their nine games at this stage.

The Potters are level on points with both Birmingham City and Middlesbrough at the top of the table, with Norwich making up ground beneath them, as this two-week break will provide both clubs with the opportunity to reflect on their seasons so far.

Stoke will certainly be the happier of the two clubs at the moment, though Norwich have a lot to be excited about for the remainder of the season, having seemingly found their groove.

Both clubs looked to strengthen their ranks over the summer, and both will feel as though they have successfully done so, albeit they could not complete deals for every single one of their targets, including former Plymouth Argyle striker Lorent Tolaj.

According to VoetbalPrimeur, Norwich and Stoke were among numerous clubs interested in Swiss forward Tolaj, who enjoyed an excellent season in League One with Plymouth last time around.

Danish giants FC Copenhagen and fellow Championship club Swansea City were also credited with an interest, and whilst the 25-year-old did indeed head to the English second tier, it wouldn't be to any of those aforementioned sides.

Despite heavy interest from across the United Kingdom and the rest of Europe, it would be Bristol City that would eventually win the race for Tolaj's signature, striking a deal worth around £4 million to bring the forward to Ashton Gate.

Michael Skubala, having replaced Roy Hodgson at the helm over the summer, sought to stamp his authority on his new side with some statement additions, with Tolaj being one of them.

Joining the Swiss striker were also the likes of Sam Tickle, Sam Greenwood, Dominic Ballard, Rio Cardines, and Gibson Yah, though Tolaj was largely viewed as the pick of the bunch.

Spending time in the youth ranks of Brighton and Hove Albion before working his way up the EFL and scoring prolifically for the likes of Aldershot Town, Port Vale, and Plymouth, expectations on the striker were unsurprisingly high.

Since his move to Bristol City, Tolaj has made 12 league appearances for the Robins, scoring just twice and assisting once; at this stage, he has struggled to translate his goalscoring prowess into the Championship.

Bristol City, on the whole, have struggled to really get going under Skubala this year, winning just one of their last seven league games and heading into the two-week break off the back of a 5-0 defeat to Norwich.

Time is on the 25-year-old's side, certainly, but having struggled to really show what he is capable of in the second tier at this stage, both Norwich and Stoke may well feel as though they have dodged a bullet after this evidence.

One area where Norwich certainly haven't struggled this season is in the final third, as only West Ham United have scored more than their 32 goals in the Championship after 15 games.

Mathias Kvistgaarden and Jovon Makama are deputising superbly up top in the absence of Mohamed Toure, and if there is an area that the Canaries don't really need to improve, it's at centre-forward.

Stoke, meanwhile, have struggled to find a consistent presence up front once again this year, with each of Sam Gallagher, Lamine Cisse, George Hirst, and Robert Bozenik vying for a place in the starting XI under Mark Robins.

Though the Potters aren't exactly firing on all cylinders up top, there are few reasons to believe that Tolaj would have made much difference at all, as both clubs will surely feel no such transfer regret involving the Swiss right now.
