Wildlife photography often involves compromise. A distant subject, high ISO (the camera’s light-sensitivity setting, which adds grainy noise as it climbs), cropping, and less-than-ideal light can all leave a good photograph needing some careful refinement. A meerkat shot at ISO 3200 comes back speckled with grain. A bird’s eye lands a touch soft. A deer too far away has to be cropped, then enlarged. The trick is cleaning all of that up without the animal looking fake.

Wildlife photography doesn’t always allow photographers to use their preferred ISO setting. This meerkat image, for example, was captured with a Panasonic LUMIX DC-GH6 at 350mm, f/5.9, 1/400 sec., and ISO 3200. The settings made it possible to capture the subject in the available light, but the higher ISO also left visible noise.

Even when the subject is captured at a good resolution, slight softness can make a wildlife photo look less defined.

For this white-cheeked turaco photo, we selected 1x processing to refine the slight softness while keeping the original resolution.

The enhanced result brings out more definition in the white-cheeked turaco’s facial features and eye texture. Details around the eyes and beak appear sharper, and the image keeps its original dimensions.

Wildlife subjects are not always close enough to fill the frame. When a photo needs to be cropped or prepared for a larger display, its original resolution may not provide enough detail, especially when the subject already appears somewhat soft.

For this deer photo, we used More-Detail GAN v3 and selected 2x upscaling. This increased the image resolution from 1920 x 1440 to 3840 x 2880.

The 2x result brings out more definition in the deer’s eyes, ears, and fur, making the subject look clearer and more detailed at the larger resolution.
