Restitution Claim on $180M Van Gogh Shakes Up Sotheby's Marquee Week

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Heirs of the Jewish publisher Samuel Fischer, who once owned the painting, are requesting it be withdrawn from auction.

Heirs of a prominent Jewish publishing family have called on Sotheby's to withdraw a rare Vincent van Gogh painting highlighted in the marquee auction sales in New York City this week, claiming that the work had been sold under duress amid Nazi persecution in Europe.

The heirs allege that the family member who owned the painting, which Sotheby's valued at a record-breaking $180 million, sold it because of Nazi-imposed restrictions on what emigrant Jews were allowed to take with them at the time.

Sotheby's confirmed to Hyperallergic that the painting is still slated to go under the hammer this Wednesday, November 18, and that it was aware of the heirs' concerns and “takes provenance and restitution matters extremely seriously and has undertaken extensive research into the [painting's] ownership history.”
