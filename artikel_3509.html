10 Years Ago Today: The Harry Potter Universe Expanded With A Star-Studded Spin-Off

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

"Harry Potter" is perhaps the defining franchise of the 2000s, but all good things must come to an end. That end originally came in 2011 with "Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows – Part 2" which had the biggest opening ever up to that point in cinema history. But creator J.K. Rowling and Warner Bros. weren't content to let the Wizarding World end there. Ten years ago, they expanded the universe with a star-studded spin-off.

Set decades before Harry Potter was even born, 2016's "Fantastic Beasts and Where to Find Them," which opened 10 years ago today, brought the action to America and focused on entirely new characters within the Wizarding World. Specifically Newt Scamander, played by Oscar-winner Eddie Redmayne. Redmayne had an unexpected inspiration for his performance, namely film icon Buster Keaton. The movie itself was loosely based on an in-universe textbook mentioned in the main series, with Rowling eventually releasing a version of the book, though it didn't contain a narrative.

The resulting prequel/spin-off takes place in 1926 centered on magizoologist Newt Scamander as he arrives in New York during a worldwide tour to research and rescue magical creatures. A mysterious magical being begins causing chaos in NYC, which threatens to expose the wizarding world to the muggles of Earth.

Redmayne was far from the only big star to secure a role in the spin-off. The stacked cast also includes but is not limited to Katherine Waterston, Dan Fogler, Alison Sudol, Samantha Morton, Ezra Miller, and Colin Farrell. "Fantastic Beats" also cast Johnny Depp as Grindelwald in a surprise appearance. Arguably the most important casting decision the movie made, with the benefit of hindsight.

J.K. Rowling wrote the script for "Fantastic Beasts and Where to Find Them" personally, which wasn't the case with the other "Harry Potter" movies. David Yates, who helmed the final four "Harry Potter" movies, was recruited to tackle the spin-off as well, providing a sturdy hand.

The majority of the core cast were on the upswing. This movie was expected to be a big break for all of them. In many ways, it was. "Fantastic Beasts" was dubbed a worthy extension of the Wizarding World in many reviews, with audiences tending to agree. Even if it wasn't effusive praise, it was good enough.

The movie was released in November 2016 and did quite well for itself, pulling in $816 million at the global box office. That was more than enough to convince Warner Bros. to double down, with four "Fantastic Beasts" sequels announced before the first one even hit theaters. Everyone involved fully expected this to be the long-term future for the "Harry Potter" franchise. What's perhaps most surprising about that, especially in light of the first movie's success, is the fact that the words Harry Potter don't appear anywhere in the title. All the same, audiences found it.

The train had left the station and everyone involved had every reason to believe, on paper, that people would show up for the sequels en masse. After all, the first installment scored well enough with critics and was the eighth-biggest movie of 2016 overall. Unfortunately, the future had different plans.

Warner Bros. and director David Yates wasted no time, with the second "Fantastic Beasts" installment dated for November 2018. Much of the cast returned, with new additions entering the fold. But in no small part due to forces beyond the studio's control, "The Crimes of Grindelwald" doomed "Fantastic Beasts."

The sequel pulled in more than $650 million at the box office, but made a lot less domestically and had to rely on international audiences to bail it out. Then there's the Depp of it all. Controversy started swirling around the actor due to his public feud with his ex-wife Amber Heard, involving accusations of domestic abuse. Depp later dropped out of the "Fantastic Beasts" franchise following a court case verdict. Mads Mikkelsen took over as Grindelwald in "The Secrets of Dumbledore," but the writing was on the wall.

The third installment made hardly more than $400 million globally, failing to break even theatrically. "The Secrets of Dumbledore" proved that the magic was gone, despite the appeal of Jude Law as a younger Albus Dumbledore in a lead role. The cast continued to be star-studded all along the way but, at a certain point, appealing casts only go so far. Especially in the 2020s when audiences are more content than ever to stay at home and stream something from the couch.

Controversy and middling results with diminishing returns eventually derailed "Fantastic Beasts." Ten years ago though, the spin-off arrived with a great deal of promise and a lot of potential. That potential just never proved out.
