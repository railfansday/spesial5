If you carry the (see rates and fees) in your wallet, chances are you appreciate its premium travel perks and valuable rewards.

But even a card with a $795 annual fee can't cover every spending category or travel need. That's where a second credit card can come in handy.

I carry the Sapphire Reserve myself, but it's not the only card I reach for. Whether you're looking to maximize everyday spending, enhance your airline experience, or pick up hotel-specific perks, the right pairing can help you get more out of your wallet.

Here are seven cards worth considering alongside the Chase Sapphire Reserve.

Here's a quick look at seven options, including how much you'll pay in combined annual fees.

The Capital One Venture Rewards is a solid option for Sapphire Reserve cardholders looking to earn more on everyday purchases.

Why the pairing works: One area where the Sapphire Reserve falls short is everyday spending that doesn't qualify for a bonus category. Those purchases generally earn just 1 Ultimate Rewards point per dollar spent, while the Venture Rewards earns at least 2 Capital One miles per dollar spent on all purchases, regardless of category.

For example, you could use your Sapphire Reserve for purchases that earn bonus points, then reach for your Venture Rewards when shopping for clothing, furniture or other purchases that wouldn't earn elevated rewards with the Reserve.

For a $95 annual fee, the Venture Rewards also gives you access to Capital One's 15-plus transfer partners, opening up additional redemption opportunities beyond Chase Ultimate Rewards.

Key drawbacks: While the Venture Rewards has a relatively modest annual fee, it still brings your combined annual fees to $890.

You'll also be managing two separate rewards currencies, since Chase Ultimate Rewards points and Capital One miles can't be combined directly. While this opens up more redemption opportunities across different rewards programs, just make sure you're comfortable managing two rewards currencies before committing to both cards.

The and  are two great options for Sapphire Reserve cardholders looking to earn more Ultimate Rewards points without paying another annual fee.

Why the pairing works: Both Freedom cards offer opportunities to earn more rewards on purchases that might otherwise earn just 1 point per dollar with the Sapphire Reserve.

The Freedom Unlimited earns 1.5% cash back on purchases outside its bonus categories, making it a solid choice for everyday spending. Meanwhile, the Freedom Flex earns 5% cash back on up to $1,500 in combined purchases in rotating bonus categories each quarter you activate (then 1%).

Although both Freedom cards are marketed as cash-back cards, they technically earn Ultimate Rewards points, with each point worth 1 cent when redeemed for cash back.

For example, $1,000 in nonbonus spending would earn 1,500 points with the Freedom Unlimited, compared with 1,000 points on the Reserve.

The biggest advantage of either pairing is that you can combine the rewards earned with your Freedom card and Sapphire Reserve, allowing you to redeem them as transferable Ultimate Rewards points.

You could even take things a step further by carrying all three cards, creating a version of the Chase Trifecta. This strategy allows you to maximize rewards across different spending categories without adding another annual fee.

Key drawbacks: The Freedom Unlimited's 1.5% earning rate isn't the highest available for everyday spending, while the Freedom Flex requires you to track and activate quarterly bonus categories to maximize its rewards.

Both cards also have some overlapping bonus categories with the Reserve, so you'll want to pay attention to which card earns the most on each purchase.

Ultimately, I'd lean toward the Freedom Unlimited if you prefer a simple, consistent earning strategy, or the Freedom Flex if you're willing to put in a little extra effort to maximize rotating categories.

There's no single best card to pair with the . The right choice depends on where you spend the most and what you want from your next credit card.

Personally, I'd start by looking at where my Sapphire Reserve falls short, whether that's earning rewards on everyday purchases or offering airline-specific benefits that could make my travels easier.

After all, the goal isn't necessarily to have more credit cards. It's to make sure the cards you do carry work well together.

Before adding another card to your wallet, consider how it fits into your overall credit card strategy.

If you're applying for a Chase card, keep the issuer's unofficial 5/24 rule in mind, as well as any welcome bonus eligibility restrictions. You'll also want to weigh the combined annual fees against the additional rewards and benefits you're getting.

Finally, think about whether you'd prefer to pool more Ultimate Rewards points with another Chase card or diversify into a separate rewards currency, such as Capital One miles.

The offers plenty of value on its own, but pairing it with the right card can help you earn more rewards and enjoy benefits you might otherwise miss.

Before adding another card to your wallet, consider where your Reserve falls short, your spending habits and whether the additional rewards and perks justify any extra annual fee.
