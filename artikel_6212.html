PlanNet Sells The Dream Of A Travel Business—But 84% Of Its Representatives Earned No Commission In 2025

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

I’d never heard of PlanNet Marketing until a reader sent me its earnings numbers. I got started down a rabbit hole. They sell travel. Or, rather, they sell people on the idea of selling travel while signing up other people to sell travel. But for all of 2025, 83.96% of PlanNet representatives earned no commission or override at all. My first reaction was: this looks awfully like a pyramid scheme. It’s a multi-level marketing business selling access to a travel agency opportunity. And the more I read, the less comfortable I became with the way that opportunity is pitched to people who need money. Two companies, two ways to make money PlanNet was launched in 2015 by founder Donald Bradley. It works with InteleTravel, a host travel agency. These are separate businesses: . InteleTravel provides the travel agency operation. Advisors get booking tools, training and supplier access. They can sell actual trips and receive a share of the commission. PlanNet sells access to that opportunity. Its representatives earn money selling InteleTravel agency subscriptions and building networks of other representatives who sell them. InteleTravel reported $1.52 billion in 2025 travel sales and 140,000 independent contractors. A conventional host agency charges fees or keeps part of an advisor’s commission in exchange for infrastructure and support. But the salient feature of PlanNet is the multilevel compensation for selling agency access. You can be an InteleTravel advisor without joining the paid PlanNet representative program. However, InteleTravel’s sign-up page routes enrollment exclusively through PlanNet. Here’s the current U.S. pricing from InteleTravel’s benefits page and PlanNet’s current policies and compensation plan. Using monthly billing, doing both costs $59.90 a month, or $718.80 over twelve months, before joining fees or other business expenses. Program    Joining fee    Recurring fee InteleTravel advisor    $179.99    $39.95 monthly PlanNet representative    $19.95    $19.95 monthly; $191.52 annual option Here’s a video describing their commission structure: InteleTravel advertises paying advisors 70%–80% of supplier commissions. A hypothetical $4,000 commissionable booking at a 10% supplier commission produces $400 so an advisor receiving 70% gets $280. You can make a living with enough customers and bookings. What PlanNet Pays For PlanNet’s compensation plan pays for something different: Sell an InteleTravel agency subscription and receive $50. Personally sponsor a PlanNet representative who makes an agency sale and receive a $25 matching commission. A qualifying Gold Builder receives a $10 bonus on agency sales in their network, down to the next Gold Builder. Qualifying representatives receive $4 monthly per active agency subscription in their compensation matrix, potentially through nine levels. At least two active personal agency sales are required to qualify. Gold Builder requires nine active personal agency sales. Higher ranks and bonuses depend on building larger qualifying organizations. The plan also includes rings and a Rolex award. What triggers those payments is selling agency subscriptions. It’s not selling travel, it’s selling the ability to sell travel. Someone can keep paying for an agency, sell no travel, and still generate a monthly payment for people above them. Recruiting another paying participant turns out to be valuable to the recruiter even when the opportunity turns out to be worthless to the person recruited. And friends and relatives become prospective customers (marks) for the business opportunity. The Federal Trade Commission says having a real product isn’t enough to make a multilevel marketing business legal. It’s how the compensation and incentives work that matters. Poor earnings on their own doesn’t mean it’s a pyramid scheme. But the economics here certainly look like one to me. The Target Market (‘Victims’) In a company-published recap of a 2022 Atlanta event, a representative describes helping people “move to the other side of the poverty line.” Another invokes the opportunity to “change your bloodline’s finances.” Here’s a sample, A 2024 company profile celebrates a single mother of five who says she had been living paycheck to paycheck before finding the opportunity. It also reported celebrity Jess Wright telling people unable to afford to “invest into your future, then this is exactly why you need this travel business”. Someone’s inability to afford a fee is being turned into an argument for paying it. But being shown the celebrity’s lifestyle tells you very little about your own likely return. It’s also not clear whether people actually receive the requisite training to sell travel, understand cancellation terms, handle client money, know the products, and fix problems. You’re Guaranteed A Profit! InteleTravel offers a first-year profit guarantee. It counts both earnings and the value of travel savings and privileges for the advisor. It covers fees paid to InteleTravel not all expenses or PlanNet fees. Eligibility includes specified training, a qualifying cruise or vacation-package sale, a completed hotel stay booking each quarter, and purchasing business cards within the first 90 days. The claim must be made within 30 days after the first anniversary. PlanNet’s policies: Prohibit selling or transferring the position you buy If you don’t keep paying, you lose your downline commissions You can’t make negative remarks about the company, its compensation plan or leadership. It’s An Old Business Model This isn’t an original business model. Evolution Travel and Archer Travel is another marketing network paired with a host agency. Evolution’s compensation plan combines travel commissions with sponsor payments and network ranks. It raises the same question about whether a participant is building a customer base for travel or a network of paying agents. YourTravelBiz, or YTB: was alleged to be a pyramid scheme built around selling travel websites. A 2009 settlement required $1 million in penalties, costs and restitution. The company’s securities filing says the settlement involved no admission or court findings of wrongdoing. One executive named in that filing was Andrew Cauthen, the Andy Cauthen whom PlanNet honored on his retirement as president in January 2026. WorldVentures: a travel club version of multilevel marketing. Its U.S. disclosure from July 2018 – June 2019 reported that 85.4% of representatives earned no commission. Norway’s regulator found it to be an illegal pyramid system. I’ve long argued that a good travel agent’s expertise has real value. Someone who knows a destination, understands a complicated itinerary or takes responsibility when things go wrong can earn their commission many times over. I don’t see how I’d ever want to work with a PlanetNet agent, and that’s a problem. Top travel advisors can still do well but this is not the broad-based business it once was because of a new thing called the internet.
