Indiana Fever GM search narrows, multiple candidates come with NBA ties: Sources

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Indiana Fever's search for a new general manager is ramping up.

The franchise is expected to evaluate several finalists, including many with NBA backgrounds, and make a hire in the next few weeks, according to multiple sources familiar with the search who were granted anonymity because they are not permitted to speak publicly about the search process.

Among the list of candidates being considered are Sacramento Kings vice president of basketball operations and player personnel Phil Jabour, Boston Celtics vice president of team operations and organizational growth Allison Feaster and former Dallas Mavericks assistant general manager Matt Ricardi. Clare Duwelius, former Minnesota Lynx basketball operations manager and current Unrivaled general manager and executive vice president, was also being considered at one point in the search process.

The Fever's front office overhaul began on Oct. 23 when the franchise announced longtime president Kelly Krauskopf would step down after three decades with Pacers Sports & Entertainment. She informed leadership of her decision before the end of the 2026 season. Later that same day news broke that the Fever had fired general manager Amber Cox after two seasons.

Cox's firing followed her heavily scrutinized exit interview, during which she was dismissive of critiques of the Fever fan base. Four-time All-Star Kelsey Mitchell shared comments during the same session that directly contradicted Cox, detailing difficulties of competing for fans who she said "that may not like you." She added that the organization is "unbelievable," but said it hasn't been easy "trying to be yourself."

Fever coach Stephanie White has one year left on her contract and is expected to remain in her position, according to a source familiar with discussions who spoke on the condition of anonymity to preserve relationships. White represented the Fever at last week's WNBA competition committee meeting, which includes league leadership along with coaches and general managers from every team.

The Fever shakeup was the first step in what is expected to be one of the most critical free agency periods in the franchise's history that starts with an attempt to re-sign Mitchell who is an unrestricted free agent. The Fever also will look to extend three-time All-Star Caitlin Clark under the league's new EPIC (Exceptional Performance on Initial Contract) clause. A highly anticipated lever in the league's new collective bargaining agreement, the EPIC clause allows players signed to their rookie scale contract who have been named MVP or to an All-WNBA first or second team to renegotiate the final year of their rookie contract to sign a multi-year extension.

Four-time All-Star Aliyah Boston signed an extension under the EPIC provision in April, keeping her with the franchise through 2029. Despite boasting the top offense in the league, powered by record breaking seasons from Mitchell and Clark, the Fever were bounced by the Las Vegas Aces in the first round of the playoffs. The Fever have failed to make it past the semifinals since drafting Clark with the No. 1 overall pick in 2024.

Pacers Sports & Entertainment hired Excel Search & Advisory to lead the search for a new Fever general manager. The firm is also leading the league's search for a new commissioner. Clark is represented by the agency's sports management sector.

Riccardi, who worked in the Brooklyn Nets organization in a variety of roles from 2010 to 2022, was with the Dallas Mavericks from 2022 to 2026. He spent most of his last year there as the interim co-general manager, and had been elevated from his assistant general manager position when former president of basketball operations Nico Harrison was fired from his post. Riccardi was a finalist to replace Harrison, but the job ultimately went to Masai Ujiri.

Feaster, a Harvard graduate and three-time Ivy League Player of the Year, was drafted fifth overall by the Los Angeles Sparks in 1998 and finished her WNBA career with the Fever in 2008. (She played overseas until retiring in 2016.) She joined the Celtics as their director of player development in September 2019 and was later promoted to vice president of player development and organizational growth.

Jabour, who has been with the Sacramento Kings since October 2020, is their vice president of basketball operations and player personnel. He has spent two decades in the NBA, previously with Houston, Orlando and Philadelphia in a variety of roles.

The Fever are one of nine WNBA franchises with varying levels of shared ownership with an NBA franchise. Conversations around both leagues about their GM search have included an expectation that the Fever will operate more closely in line with their NBA counterpart. That is already being reflected in the franchise's $78 million investment into a state-of-the-art practice facility which is scheduled to open this coming spring, but also in their comprehensive team staffing.
