CFP Committee Sends Shocking Message With Latest College Football Playoff Top 25 Rankings

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

ATLANTA — The College Football Playoff selection committee delivered another revealing verdict Tuesday night, unveiling its updated Top 25 rankings following a chaotic weekend that reshaped the national championship conversation.

Georgia, Alabama, Texas and Tennessee entered the evening with significant postseason implications, while several national contenders awaited decisions that could alter their playoff positioning. With rivalry week approaching, the latest rankings provided another indication of how the committee views the country's leading programs.

The committee released its fourth rankings of the 2026 season Tuesday, Nov. 24, following several surprising results across the country.

Georgia entered the evening ranked first nationally, while undefeated Utah held the second position. However, losses by previously undefeated UCLA and several other contenders created opportunities for movement throughout the Top 25.

Rankings reflect the committee's Nov. 24 release. Previous rankings are from Nov. 17.

Georgia entered Tuesday's announcement ranked No. 1 following another convincing SEC victory.

Kirby Smart's Bulldogs improved to 10-1 with a 34-6 win over South Carolina, strengthening their position as one of the nation's leading championship contenders. Meanwhile, undefeated Utah continued to present a compelling alternative near the top of the rankings.

The committee placed Georgia at No. 1 and Utah at No. 2, establishing their latest positions entering rivalry week.

Georgia Remains at No.1 and faces a tough challenge in the iron bowl this weekend.

Perhaps the most intriguing SEC storyline involved Alabama, Texas and Tennessee, three programs entering the rankings with identical 9-2 records.

Alabama held the No. 3 position last week, while Texas ranked fifth and Tennessee ninth. However, the Volunteers' head-to-head victory over Alabama and the Longhorns' collection of quality wins created an interesting comparison.

The committee ranked Alabama No. 6, Texas No. 3 and Tennessee No. 5 on Tuesday.

The committee made a shocking decision dropping Alabama all the way down to No. 6 while Texas and Tennessee reach the top 5 entering rivalry week

With rivalry week approaching, each program has another opportunity to strengthen its postseason résumé.

Alabama will host Auburn in the Iron Bowl, while Texas travels to face Texas A&M. Tennessee will visit Vanderbilt in its regular-season finale.

UCLA entered last week's rankings fourth nationally with an undefeated record, but Saturday's loss to Michigan changed the Bruins' postseason outlook.

The result created an opening for several teams seeking a higher ranking, including Notre Dame, Miami and Texas. Meanwhile, Indiana's loss to Washington introduced additional uncertainty among the committee's leading contenders.

UCLA landed at No. 9 in Tuesday's rankings

Dropping 6 spots after suffering a huge loss to Michigan

The battle near the final playoff positions also intensified following Saturday's results.

Wake Forest and Texas Tech entered Tuesday ranked 11th and 12th, respectively, while BYU and several other contenders remained firmly in the discussion. Their positions carried additional significance as the committee continued evaluating potential conference champions and at-large candidates.

Tuesday's rankings placed Texas Tech at No. 11 and Oregon at No. 12.

The Indiana Hoosiers dropped all the way down to No.14, while Wake Forest moved up to No. 8

The rankings are not identical to the projected 12 team playoff bracket, which also accounts for automatic conference champion berths.

Consequently, teams ranked outside the top 12 can remain in contention for automatic qualification, while others must rely on at-large consideration.

The latest rankings arrive before one of the most anticipated weekends of the college football season.

Georgia will face Georgia Tech, Alabama will host Auburn and Texas will visit Texas A&M. Tennessee's matchup with Vanderbilt also carries important implications for the SEC's postseason outlook.

The committee will release its next rankings Tuesday, Dec. 1, following rivalry week.

The final 12-team College Football Playoff bracket will be announced Sunday, Dec. 6, after the conference championship games.
