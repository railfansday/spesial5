Who should Wales pick for autumn finale v Japan?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Wales head coach Steve Tandy, with one eye on the World Cup, vowed to rotate his side for Nations Championship finals weekend even before his side had faced Australia.

Hopes of an autumn clean sweep - something only achieved once in 2018 under Warren Gatland - were dashed by the Wallabies at the Principality Stadium on Saturday.

Wales dust themselves down before finishing the November campaign against familiar foes Japan on Saturday (13:10 GMT).

The Six Nations is not a time for experimenting, so it presents a rare chance to have a look at players in the countdown to next year's World Cup in Australia.

Wales spluttered to victory against Eddie Jones' side at the start of the month and will expect a more comfortable success at Twickenham's Allianz Stadium.

The fixture, a fifth meeting with the Brave Blossoms in 16 months, presents a chance for Tandy to test his squad depth.

Eleven of Wales' players started against Japan, New Zealand and Australia - Blair Murray, Louis Rees-Zammit, Eddie James, Max Llewellyn, Sam Costelow, Tomos Williams, Nicky Smith, Dewi Lake, Archie Griffin, Dafydd Jenkins and Jac Morgan.

It is fair to assume that plenty of big hitters will watch from the stands at Twickenham, after the mental and physical demands of the past fortnight.

"I can rotate the team and we'll still put in a performance. It doesn't matter who wears the shirt, we've got to perform," said Tandy.

"Whenever we put a team out, the objective is to win a Test match. That will be no different next Saturday."

The core of the first-choice team will remain in England after the Japan game and rejoin their clubs in the Prem.

Former Wales and Lions wing Alex Cuthbert urged Tandy to resist the temptation to give the frontline players a breather.

"We need to put our best team out. We really want to put a marker down against Japan," he said on the Scrum V podcast.

"It's an opportunity to really put Japan to the sword if we really want to take some momentum into the Six Nations."

The Japanese are stretched by injury and conceded 24 tries in defeats by England and Scotland.

Wales fielded the same starting line-up against Japan and Australia either side of the New Zealand victory.

Man of the match against the All Blacks James Botham went from the XV to missing out completely against the Wallabies, much to the frustration of his father Liam.

Botham's fellow Cardiff back-row forward Taine Basham and Leicester wing Gabriel Hamer-Webb suffered the same fate.

Versatile forward Taine Plumtree has proved his worth from the bench in the past two fixtures but might hope for a first Test start since Scotland in the third round of the 2026 Six Nations.

Tandy is spoilt for choice in the back-row and gave promising Exeter flanker Kane James a second cap off the bench against the Wallabies.

Wales have also developed depth at loose-head prop to the extent that strong-running Rhys Carre, Welsh Rugby Writers' Association (WRWA) player of the year after his Six Nations exploits, missed out against New Zealand and Australia.

Smith has started all three November Tests and Ospreys' Gareth Thomas has been strong off the bench.

Wales selected three uncapped players in their initial Nations Championship squad with Cardiff loose-head prop Rhys Barratt, who played against the Barbarians in the summer, joined by the Dragons pair of scrum-half Che Hope and centre Aneurin Owen.

That figure became four when in-form Scarlets wing Macs Page got a call-up due to Rio Dyer's hamstring injury.

Tandy has given first caps to nine players since taking the reins in the summer of 2025 and could hit double figures at Twickenham.

There are seven other players in the squad who are yet to feature this month.

Centre Joe Hawkins failed to get off the bench against the Wallabies, when he was also Costelow's cover at fly-half.

Hooker Liam Belcher, tight-head Keiron Assiratti, lock Seb Davies, wing Ellis Mee and wing or full-back Tom Rogers have had no minutes, while fly-half Jarrod Evans was called into the squad last week when Dan Edwards suffered an injury in training.

Reuben Morgan-Williams kicked the ball off to seal victory for Wales against New Zealand [Huw Evans Picture Agency]

Reuben Morgan-Williams scored a sniping try in the win against the Barbarians in June.

The Ospreys scrum-half will hope to wear nine on his back for the first time in a Test match on the return to Twickenham.

Wales have been reliant on world-class Tomos Williams, and his deputy Kieran Hardy faces a fitness race for the World Cup after a serious knee injury.

Morgan-Williams was called into the fray for the last quarter of an hour against New Zealand and was introduced just five minutes into the second half against the Wallabies.

"Reuben played a fair part of the game and did really well," said Tandy. "He was very accurate in what he did.

"Tomos had a tight groin and we will assess that, but we've got to find out about Reuben."

Wales are scheduled to name their team on Thursday morning.
