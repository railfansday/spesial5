Fort Defiance volleyball sweeps Amelia to reach state semifinals

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

FORT DEFIANCE — Fort Defiance is one step away from returning to the Class 2 state volleyball championship for a third year in a row.

Fort beat Amelia with relative ease 25-6, 25-14, 25-13 Saturday, Nov. 14 to advance to the state semifinals where it will play Madison County, a team Fort just beat for the regional title earlier this week.

Fort hosts Madison Nov. 17. A win there and Fort would play in Salem for the state championship Nov. 21. Fort has reached, and lost, the state championship in 2024 and 2025.

In the postseason, Fort has made quick work of Clarke, Madison and now Amelia to get to this point, sweeping all three. Fort coach Amber Pitsenbarger has just seen her team's confidence swell now that playoffs have started.

"That's been the one thing I've been trying to instill in them again, that they are all good players and we have so much depth, playing confident is what we've been really good at," she said. "Everybody lifts each other up if we make mistakes. I feel like we just have that really good team chemistry since we got in the postseason."

It was the usual suspects against Amelia. Fort's Caroline Hanger, the Region 2B player of the year, had 13 kills, 10 digs and three aces. Harper Carter and Maddie Gale both had seven kills. Gale added 24 assists and 10 digs for another all-around solid game by the freshman. Libero Norah Anderson finished with 11 digs.

Asked what was clicking for this team now, senior Livia Clinedinst said it was the hitting.

"We have hitters everywhere," she said. "We've been working so hard in practice every day to perfect our hitting, seeing the block and just being able to hit no matter what."

Hanger is the leader in kills this year with 272, but Carter, Gale and Clinedinst all have triple digits with Carter closing in on 200. And in fewer sets played, Annalee Halterman and Amelia Good both are averaging 1.5 kills a set. It's hard for defenses to focus on just one hitter.

Hanger, a junior, said she has seen a drive in the senior players as they near the end of their high school volleyball careers.

"They just want to be at the top, all of them want it so bad," she said. "I can just see it in all of them and that makes me want to work even harder for them."

Clinedinst said she realizes that every game now can be her last on a volleyball court. Senior Alexis Miller agreed, but she said this group of seniors is so motivated to finally win a championship.

"Everything is just starting to click now," Miller said. "This is a good time to peak."

Buffalo Gap's season came to an end on the road against Middlesex in the Class 1 volleyball state quarterfinals. Middlesex won 25-23, 17-25, 25-19, 25-12.

Buffalo Gap was led by Bailey Tribble's 12 kills and 13 digs and Avery Wheeler's 24 assists and 17 digs. Madison Botkin added 11 kills and 10 digs, while Brailyn Green had 12 digs.

The team's lone senior, Brynan Hildebrand, played tough defense with three digs, and "lots of hustle," her coach, Elizabeth Ashby said after the match.
