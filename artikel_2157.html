West Ham face Taty Castellanos, River Plate transfer exit threat

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

As West Ham continue to fall short of rekindling their fortunes despite Scott Parker's recent appointment, the possibility of Taty Castellanos departing the London Stadium in the coming months has now emerged in a development that will spark mixed reactions from Hammers supporters.

West Ham's return to the Championship following 14 successive seasons of solid Premier League standing is not going to plan right now, with the East Londoners appearing increasingly unlikely to seal a previously-expected top-flight return at the very first time of asking as champions.

A mixed run of form featuring consecutive home defeats at home to a pair of promotion rivals in Southampton and West Bromwich Albion ultimately ended Nuno Espirito Santo's unpopular tenure in charge, and the Portuguese boss has been swiftly succeeded by former Hammers midfielder Parker.

The ex-Bournemouth and Fulham boss, having already achieved three promotions from the second-tier with as many clubs after most recently guiding Burnley back to the promised land in 2025 before his dismissal towards the end of the previous campaign, has encountered a challenging return to the club thus far, though, taking just one point from his opening two matches.

Last Wednesday's thrilling 3-3 comeback draw at Cardiff City after overturning a three-goal defecit at half-time was followed up by a disappointing 2-1 defeat away to Blackburn Rovers over the weekend, and with the Hammers yet to enjoy the anticipated upturn under Parker, they now face the threat of losing one of their big-name performers once the January window rolls around.

Castellanos has endured a particularly difficult and underwhelming introduction to the Championship, which the Argentine forward was expected to light up after his mid-season arrival back in January preceded encouraging goalscoring form in spite of his side's ultimate failure to dodge the drop.

Six goals from 18 Premier League appearances means that the current return of just one strike across 14 second-tier fixtures - nine of which have been starts - is not what fans were anticipating, and they had presented vocal criticism towards the divisive 28-year-old for a number of weeks before Parker hauled the attacker off at the interval against the Bluebirds and then dropped his name out of the starting line-up at Ewood Park.

It has now emerged that Castellanos could leave East London as early as January, with a fresh report from Argentine-based outlet El Intransigente claiming strong long-term interest from River Plate.

As per the update, River Plate have held interest in the two-cap Argentine international's services in recent windows and he represents a "top target" in the eyes of manager Leonardo Ponzio.

Blackburn Rovers captain Todd Cantwell has been among those reacting to the club's dig towards Scott Parker's West Ham after Saturday's 2-1 victory

It's said that the Buenos Aries-based giants feel strongly about Castellano's profile and international experience, with the striker having risen to prominence outside of his native Argentina in Uruguay with Montevideo City Torque before enjoying productive spells at New York City, Girona and Lazio ahead of joining West Ham earlier this year for a reported £26 million figure on a four-and-a-half year contract.

West Ham supporters have grown increasingly critical of Castellanos and his long-term future under Parker remains unclear at this moment in time, although a fresh hurdle could prevent River Plate from sealing the deal.

River Plate have been presented with a significant obstacle to overcome if they intend on returning Castellanos to his homeland, as it's a move which wouldn't come cheaply for the 38-time Primera Division champions.

Indeed, the report has added that the salary Castellanos earns at the London Stadium is the main challenge for River Plate right now, leaving the club to weigh up alternative options such as acquiring the sought-after frontman on loan or even purchasing a percentage of his rights.

Both of those options, of course, would require the prior approval of West Ham, whose own stance on potentially parting ways with the ex-Lazio striker just a year after his arrival with a club likely unable to recoup the figure of purchase is also not yet known.

It's noted that Castellanos' lengthy contract with the Championship promotion hopefuls puts Parker's side in a comfortable negotiating decision, and hold all the cards as River Plate will not be able to wrap up any deal without their support.

Interestingly, however, the update also makes reference to previous interest elsewhere, reminding that Castellanos had reportedly wished to leave West Ham in the summer amid loan interest from Seville-based rivals Real Betis and Sevilla that never materialised.

The London Stadium faithful remain very much divided on Castellanos at the moment, with some making their minds up on the out-of-form forward and others willing to persist with further patience.

Prior claims of a January exit if the price is met at the end of the previous month saw some West Ham fans refuse to hold back on their feelings about Castellanos' future, and this one fan believes that his hapless form in the Championship would make for no great loss if he was to depart.

Another would be happy to see the Hammers striker leave at any cost, even if the club themselves surely won't take that stance.

Castellanos has left some frustrated for reasons beyond his inability to find the back of the net on a regular basis, too, and having formerly scored at a relatively-prolific rate leading the line for a side relegated from the Premier League, his work-rate and application have been called into question.

His display in the aforementioned draw at Cardiff drew particular criticism, and West Ham were a much more fluid, functional and threatening outfit once the Argentine was withdrawn for Joel Piroe at the break.

Now, with the Suriname international scoring in successive encounters, it's unclear how Castellanos will force his way back into starting contention ahead of a positional rival with a historically-goal-laden hit-rate at this level.
