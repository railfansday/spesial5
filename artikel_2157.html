Top 15 best home fans in the EFL ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Throughout the English football pyramid lies the age-old debate: where can you find the best atmosphere at a football stadium in the country?

Many will argue that it is hard to make such a case that clubs in the Premier League provide such hostilities due to various factors which have recently influenced the matchday experience of supporters within the top-flight.

As such, to go in search of the answer to said question, football fanatics may have to dig deeper into the Championship, League One and League Two.

In all three divisions you will find old-school grounds, passionate fanbases and some of the country's biggest fallen giants, who are still able to attract strong crowds despite dwindling recent fortunes.

Football League World recently put forward the 15 clubs with the best sets of away supporters, and now the roles have been reversed, and we are now looking at which of the EFL's 72 sides have the best home fans.

Football League World assesses and ranks the FIFTEEN best away fanbases in the EFL right now

Getting this list underway are Cardiff City, who, in recent times, have been able to retain a strong core of loyal supporters in South Wales despite mixed on-pitch fortunes.

Indeed, only two clubs averaged a greater home gate than the Bluebirds' 19,564 last season during their promotion-winning campaign in League One, which is a testament to such loyalties when some visiting sides will have not brought the greatest of visiting crowds from across the English border.

That cross-nation rivalry often makes for a hostile atmosphere that can certainly be an advantage to Brian Barry-Murphy's side.

Next up are Leicester City, with Foxes supporters certainly seeing their loyalties tested at present, with the Russell Martin era not exactly getting off to the smoothest start following back-to-back relegations.

The locals in this part of the East Midlands still showed up with the second-highest average attendance in last season's Championship in fairness, although there have been question marks over the atmosphere generated in LE2.

That said, when the club's day-to-day running has been eye-catching for all the wrong reasons of late, emphasised by a six-point deduction in February, it isn't hard to see why the mood isn't great.

West Bromwich Albion are known for having one of the most loyal Championship fanbases, despite the fact the Baggies are competing against several local sides for a new generation of support.

Average attendance figures at The Hawthorns have been above a 20,000 figure ever since the beginning of the 2001/02 season, meaning the majority of the 26,804-capacity stadium has been full.

However, it has, again, been debated whether the atmosphere can be improved in B71 despite such an impressive level of core support.

In 12th are Bradford City, with the West Yorkshire side consistently drawing impressive attendance figures to Valley Parade season-on-season despite the fact the club has languished in the EFL's bottom two divisions since 2004.

The 2025/26 League One season saw Bantams fans average gates of 20,444, which was the second-strongest in the third tier, and with the club's owners doing their utmost to make season tickets affordable in this part of the world, even more seats have been taken up this term.

It was announced in pre-season that at least 16,352 season tickets had been sold for 2026/27 - a club record.

Plymouth Argyle fans are some of the most respected in the country, as they turn up in their thousands irrespective of the Green Army's fortunes on the pitch, whilst also making Home Park a notoriously difficult ground for visiting teams to take points away from.

Although the distances for those in the away end to travel can often put off visiting fans, there is still a strong atmosphere generated in this part of the world more often than not, and average attendances have, in recent seasons, stayed at a level between 15,000 and 16,000 - one of the strongest in the EFL's lower reaches

Sheffield United's home crowd at Bramall Lane make it into 10th on this list, with the stadium famed for generating a vociferous atmosphere on occasions, particularly from the Kop stand.

However, compared to many other sides who ranked high based on average attendances last season, it will have been a disappointment among Blades fans that almost 6,000 of the stadium's seats were not filled.

The current situation the club finds itself in at board level has also made for an intriguing atmosphere at recent matches.

In ninth are Southampton, with St Mary's one of the larger stadiums in this season's Championship and often full to the brim with home supporters.

Those on the South Coast have also seen their loyalty tested in recent times after two relegations from the Premier League in three years, the latter of which saw them win just one home game all season.

Saints supporters also saw their club heavily criticised over the recent spygate scandal, but this has only added to the atmosphere on matchdays, particularly if those from the away end are quick to 'taunt' their rival fans for such misdemeanours.

Norwich City's home crowd at Carrow Road may not be the most hostile, but it is extremely hard to knock the fact that Norfolk's only professional club continues to attract such strong crowds despite how easy it could be for some locals to follow clubs outside the region.

The Canaries have endured extremely mixed starts in each of the past two seasons, with it even looking as though relegation to League One might have been possible at one stage.

Despite the club's fluctuating fortunes, you have to go back 22 years to find the last time average attendance figures dropped below the 20,000 mark in this part of the world.

Portsmouth's home crowd at Fratton Park is seventh on this list, with the historic ground still retaining plenty of character and a strong atmosphere to boot.

Despite being the fifth-smallest ground in the Championship this season, there have been less than 1,000 empty seats on average, and throughout John Mousinho's tenure, his side have accumulated plenty of points on home turf.

It was even claimed once by AC Milan and Brazil icon, Ronaldinho, that Pompey fans generated "the best atmosphere in world football," which is high praise indeed.

Millwall are next up on this list, with the hostile atmosphere at the Den well-documented throughout generations of football supporters.

The Lions home crowd is amongst the most raucous in English football despite the fact their stadium is the fourth-smallest by capacity in the Championship, and many were hoping to see such an atmosphere in the Premier League this term before Alex Neil's side fell short in the 2025/26 play-offs.

Again, a core of the club's supporters will turn up to SE16 regardless of the club's divisional status, with the best atmosphere undoubtedly being in the Dockers derby against West Ham United.
