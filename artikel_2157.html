West Ham Show Their Worst And Best In Extraordinary Cardiff Draw

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

West Ham’s extraordinary 3-3 draw against Cardiff City was quite possibly the most bizarre game I’ve watched this season. At half-time, with Scott Parker’s new team trailing by three goals, I genuinely wondered whether we were about to concede six or seven. We hadn’t just been beaten in that opening 45 minutes; we’d completely capitulated.

Yet somehow, the same group of players emerged for the second half and produced a performance almost unrecognisable from what had gone before. West Ham dominated Cardiff, scored three goals and could quite easily have won the game. Jarrod Bowen, who had been largely anonymous before the break, suddenly looked like the best player on the pitch by an absolute mile.

I would love to know what Scott Parker said at half-time, because whatever happened in that dressing room had an extraordinary effect. This was a manager who hadn’t even taken a training session with his new players, yet the transformation was incredible. Whether it was his words, the substitutions or simply the players finding something within themselves, I haven’t seen a turnaround quite like it for a long time.

The frustrating thing was that West Ham actually started reasonably well. There was some intensity, some urgency and a willingness to compete for second balls that had been conspicuously absent during the final weeks of Nuno Espírito Santo’s reign.

Parker’s first team selection included Soungoutou Magassa as a specialist defensive midfielder, while Divine Mukasa was given the freedom to play further forward. Both decisions made sense to me, particularly after watching Mukasa spend so much time trying to compensate for problems elsewhere in midfield under Nuno.

Morato was winning tackles, Mukasa looked comfortable on the ball and West Ham appeared to be playing with a little more purpose. Unfortunately, it didn’t last.

After 11 minutes, Joel Moylan was afforded far too much space outside the penalty area and unleashed an absolute thunderbolt into the top corner. There may have been a slight deflection off Max Kilman, but the bigger concern was the amount of room Cardiff had been allowed in central areas.

Even then, West Ham weren’t completely out of the contest. However, the confidence began draining out of the players, and what followed was a collapse that reminded me of everything we’ve been watching for far too long.

In the 37th minute, a simple long ball over the top exposed Morato’s lack of pace at left-back. He couldn’t recover, conceded a penalty and Moylan duly converted to double Cardiff’s advantage.

Two minutes later, it was 3-0. An inswinging corner was met by Moylan at the near post, with Jarrod Bowen somehow finding himself responsible for challenging him in the air. Moylan completed his hat-trick, and West Ham’s defending was absolutely dreadful.

Three goals conceded in three completely different ways: a long-range shot, a straightforward ball over the top and a corner. It was almost as though Cardiff were being invited to choose how they wanted to score next.

What concerned me most wasn’t simply the defending. It was the complete disappearance of confidence.

West Ham looked shell-shocked. Players who had started the match with some urgency suddenly appeared frightened of making mistakes. There was no cohesion, no understanding and precious little belief.

I genuinely feared the worst. Having watched the dreadful defeat against West Bromwich Albion just days earlier, this felt like another team completely falling apart.

There appears to be some serious footballing PTSD within this group after years of being crap. The moment something goes wrong, the confidence seems to disappear, and one setback quickly becomes two or three.

At half-time, I wasn’t wondering how West Ham could get back into the match. I was wondering how many more Cardiff might score.

Parker made three substitutions at the interval, introducing Ezra Mayers, Mohamadou Kanté and Joël Piroe in place of Morato, Magassa and Taty Castellanos.

They were bold changes, but they were absolutely necessary. West Ham needed more pace, more energy and a completely different approach to the game.

Mukasa scored West Ham’s first goal in a remarkable comeback

Suddenly, West Ham were pressing Cardiff, winning possession and moving the ball forward with purpose. There was an intensity to their play that simply hadn’t existed during the closing stages of the first half.

Joël Veltman began getting forward down the right, combining with Bowen and providing genuine attacking width. Kanté brought tremendous energy to midfield, carrying the ball forward and running directly at Cardiff’s defence.

Even Joël Piroe, despite a couple of poor early touches, was at least dropping deeper and attempting to become involved in the game.

West Ham were playing much more directly, regularly looking for balls behind Cardiff’s defence rather than simply passing possession around without any real threat. It wasn’t always pretty, and at times it was almost route-one football, but it was causing Cardiff enormous problems.

The home side had looked completely comfortable before half-time. Now they were struggling to cope with the speed and aggression of West Ham’s attacking play.

I don’t know precisely what Parker said to the players during the interval, but I’d have loved to have been a fly on the wall.

Of course, the tactical changes made a difference, but this was about more than tactics. The players suddenly looked as though they believed they could compete again.

The comeback began in the 57th minute with a genuinely lovely goal from Divine Mukasa.

Bowen produced a beautifully weighted chipped pass over the Cardiff defence, and Mukasa timed his run perfectly. His control with his right foot was exquisite before he delicately lifted the ball over Nathan Trott with his left.

It was a wonderful piece of football and exactly the sort of attacking quality we’ve been crying out for.

More importantly, it gave West Ham something to believe in.

The Hammers continued pushing forward, with Bowen becoming increasingly influential and Veltman offering support down the right. Cardiff were beginning to look uncomfortable, particularly when West Ham played early balls into the space behind their defensive line.

In the 65th minute, another direct ball from Bowen was cleverly flicked on by Piroe. Arne Engels timed his run beautifully and finished past Trott to make it 3-2.

From looking completely dead and buried, West Ham were suddenly within one goal of an equaliser.

Kanté produced an excellent opportunity after Mavropanos picked him out with a lovely pass. His first touch was superb, but Trott made a brilliant save to deny him.

Cardiff were retreating deeper and deeper, desperately trying to protect their lead. West Ham, meanwhile, were playing with a confidence and attacking intent that had been completely absent just half an hour earlier.

It was extraordinary to watch the transformation in our captain. In the first half, he had looked just as nervous and uncertain as everyone else. After the break, almost everything good West Ham produced seemed to involve him.

He was drifting into central areas, creating opportunities, playing clever passes and generally dictating proceedings.

In the 81st minute, West Ham finally got the equaliser their second-half performance deserved.

A corner initially appeared to have come to nothing, but Bowen kept the attack alive and swung another dangerous ball into the penalty area. Max Kilman was still forward and produced a superb half-volley to beat Trott.

I could scarcely believe what I was watching.

The remarkable thing was that West Ham weren’t satisfied with rescuing a point. They continued attacking and looked the more likely side to score a winner.

Edvin Austbø, who had replaced Manor Solomon, brought an immediate willingness to run at defenders. In the 89th minute, he unleashed a powerful shot that Trott could only parry back into his penalty area.

There was a real whiff of panic about Cardiff by this stage. Having been three goals ahead at half-time, they now looked desperate for the final whistle.

A minute later, Bowen produced another wonderful piece of jiggery-pokery before chipping the ball towards the far post. Ezra Mayers made an excellent run into the penalty area and attempted a left-footed volley that went tantalisingly close.

Even in stoppage time, West Ham were still creating opportunities.

In the 92nd minute, Bowen delivered yet another dangerous inswinging ball, this time finding Tomáš Souček. The midfielder directed his header on target, only for Trott to produce an excellent save.

It would have been an extraordinary winner, but even without a fourth goal, West Ham had completed a comeback that looked utterly impossible at half-time.

I suspect Cardiff were extremely grateful there weren’t another ten minutes left to play.
