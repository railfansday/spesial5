The Solar Report, Week 4: Phoenix shows progress while familiar roster concerns persist

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Week four is in the books for the Phoenix Suns, and as I sit here contemplating what I learned this past week, it was more of a reminder that we all need to consume a healthy dose of settle-down juice early in the season, despite some of the concerns that have presented themselves. Those concerns might be entirely valid, but there's a reason they play 82 games. Over a large enough sample size, regression to the mean can occur, individual performances can stabilize, and the true identity of a basketball team can begin to reveal itself.

It was an interesting week given all the chatter surrounding Dejounte Murray and his potential arrival in Phoenix, which feels an awful lot like Klutch Sports agent Rich Paul attempting to orchestrate a landing spot for one of his clients. The connection makes sense. Paul has a relationship with Suns owner Mat Ishbia, and Phoenix already employs plenty of Klutch clients. While national reporters with connections to Paul, such as Chris Haynes, have indicated that the Suns have interest in Murray, local reporting has suggested otherwise.

It's a dangerous game in and of itself, and one of the lessons we were reminded of this week is that being disappointed in a player's performance, as many of us have been with Jalen Green thus far, doesn't mean you ship him out of town the moment another player bats his eyes at you or his agent starts campaigning for a change of scenery. We've been through the eye-batting bullshit before with Bradley Beal. There's no reason to make another rash decision, particularly when it involves bringing in somebody whose attitude raises legitimate questions.

For all the challenges Green has encountered thus far this season, his attitude appears to remain intact. I'd much rather have that disposition in the building and exhaust the possibilities of making things work than bring in a player whose handling of his current situation raises concerns about how he might conduct himself in Phoenix when things inevitably become difficult.

Disappointment can, and perhaps should, lead to change. But change comes in many forms. It doesn't necessarily require a different jersey, a different locker room, or another transaction that fundamentally alters the composition of your basketball team. Sometimes it means a trip to the second unit, where a player can rediscover his confidence, find his rhythm, and settle into a role more conducive to his success. There are plenty of avenues worth exploring with Jalen Green before entertaining the idea of sending him elsewhere. We're four weeks into an 82-game season. Pass the settle-down juice.

Week four was supposed to be a get-right week for the Phoenix Suns, and ultimately, it was. They beat the teams they were supposed to beat and lost to the teams they were expected to lose to. But frustration persisted along the way, rooted in issues we've identified throughout the early stages of the season that are becoming increasingly difficult to ignore. We'll get into those in greater depth below.

As far as overall production pertaining to restocks is concerned, week four was Khaman Maluach's worst of the season in that category. From my perspective, if you're generating somewhere around 16 restocks per 36 minutes, you're having a fairly impressive defensive impact relative to your time on the court. That isn't what we saw from Maluach this past week. He never eclipsed 14 restocks per 36 minutes, with his best performance coming during the limited action he saw against the Los Angeles Lakers on Sunday.

Truth be told, I think Maluach's situation is intertwined with why the Suns continue to suffer so mightily on the glass. The coaching staff simply doesn't appear to trust him enough to leave him on the court for extended stretches. During a week in which Phoenix encountered some sizable opponents, Maluach averaged only 17.8 minutes per game.

I can understand why certain aspects of his game haven't earned him more playing time, most notably his tentative hands and occasionally poor positioning. But he also possesses the highest rebounding percentage on the team at 15.3%. Considering rebounding has been the Suns' most glaring statistical deficiency this season, you'd think that would warrant a little more run.

I understand that he's 20 years old. I understand that development requires patience, and I understand that this past week was his least productive in our restocks category. Now that we have a month's worth of data at our disposal, we're beginning to develop a clearer picture of how Maluach has performed this season. Inconsistent is probably the most appropriate adjective, and a weekly average of 10.6 restocks per 36 minutes doesn't necessarily inspire a great deal of confidence.

Perhaps fatigue is a factor. Perhaps it's a lack of connectivity with his teammates or the constantly evolving nature of his role within the rotation. Given the inconsistencies we've witnessed throughout the lineup, my hope is that Maluach's opportunities begin to stabilize, providing him with a greater sense of what is expected of him on a nightly basis.

Growth requires repetition, and for a team desperately searching for answers on the glass, there's certainly an incentive to invest in that growth.

Yeah, the starting five doesn't work. Of the 16 five-man lineups across the NBA that have played at least 80 minutes together this season, the Suns' starting unit ranks dead last in plus/minus, dead last in net rating, and fourth worst in rebounding percentage.

We can spend plenty of time dissecting the why, but that's the what. And the what is downright pitiful.

No rest for the weary, as the Phoenix Suns are right back at it tonight in Denver to take on a Nuggets team that defeated them 127–120 in their final game of October. Denver enters the matchup with a 7–4 record, good for the seventh seed in the Western Conference, and a victory could go a long way in helping the Suns establish some positive momentum.

Why? Because the road doesn't get any shorter from there. Phoenix travels to Sacramento on Wednesday before heading to Utah on Friday to complete its week five slate of games, a three-game road trip that will provide another opportunity for this team to find its footing.

That's it for this week, Bright Siders. Stay frosty.
