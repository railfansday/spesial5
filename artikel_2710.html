'Thankful' Xaivian Lee delivers in a major way for Gonzaga in Purdue victory | Rewind

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Nov. 3—LAS VEGAS — Gonzaga preserved one streak and ended another by holding off Purdue 78-72 in Monday's season opener at T-Mobile Arena.

The Bulldogs continue to be a tough out on the first night of the college basketball campaign, picking up their 23rd straight season-opening victory — a streak that goes back to a Nov. 14, 2003, loss to Saint Joseph's at Madison Square Garden.

In the process, Gonzaga ended a five-game skid against a Purdue program that handed Mark Few's program three double-digit losses from 2022-24 and won the first two meetings between the schools in 1999 and 2000.

"Apparently (Zach) Edey was the curse," TNT sideline reporter Allie Force said in a postgame interview with Few, referring to the 7-foot-3 two-time Naismith Trophy winner who had a prominent role on previous Gonzaga scouting reports.

Xaivian Lee may not have occupied the top spot on Purdue's scouting report, but the transfer point guard who wasn't sure if he'd be playing college basketball three months ago leads our first regular-season rewind.

Lee returned to the Hall of Fame Series one year after starting for Todd Golden and Florida against former Gonzaga assistant Tommy Lloyd and Arizona.

The Toronto native scored 14 points on 5 of 17 shooting and 3 of 11 from the 3-point line, pulling down six rebounds and dishing out five assists in Florida's 93-87 loss.

Lee presumed he wouldn't get another shot in the Hall of Fame Series — or in college basketball, for that matter — but after a NBA Summer League stint with the Cleveland Cavaliers, the point guard and others around the country began exploring the possibility of a fifth-year waiver. Lee joined a lawsuit in California, securing a preliminary injunction before committing to a Gonzaga program that was desperate for point guard depth after Mario Saint-Supery's unexpected mid-summer departure.

The former Florida and Princeton player looked like a seamless fit in exhibitions against Arkansas and Lewis-Clark State and delivered for Gonzaga in Monday's opener, scoring 18 points on 4 of 7 shooting, 3 of 4 from the 3-point line and 7 of 9 from the free -throw line. He had four rebounds, three assists and just one turnover.

"I think it's kind of poetic that our first game is the exact same as my one last year," Lee said. "It's definitely a lot nicer to come out here with a win, but the last couple days I've been really thankful to be out here with these guys and playing. I thought today we did a really good job. Obviously we can still get a lot better and I think we will, but we executed pretty well."

Monday's debut should be a positive sign, both for Lee and Gonzaga, when accounting for the fact the point guard committed to Few's program on Aug. 26 and didn't arrive in Spokane until early September, giving him roughly two months to jell with teammates and learn the playbook before opening against a talented, well-coached Purdue team.

Lee was steering the ship when GU opened up the first double-digit lead in the second half, knocking down a 3-pointer and drawing a foul on another 3-point attempt. He accounted for three of the eight free throws made by GU players inside the final two minutes to help seal the win.

"He's fast, obviously we were aware of him," Boilermakers coach Matt Painter said. "I saw him at the (NBA) combine because I went to see a couple of our guys at the Combine. I hadn't seen him play before. I know that's crazy, playing at Florida, but I didn't see him play last year so then I watched tape. His speed, we really wanted to get to him to limit that speed.

"Made three huge 3s. Just like him and (Davis) Fogle, more than anything was keep them off the free throw line. Lee was pretty impressive, 3-to -1 assist-to-turnover ratio, 4-for-7 (from the field), 3-for-4 (on 3s), but the 7-for-9 from the line, that's where we felt like they just did a better job being aggressive and being able to draw those fouls."

Gonzaga's frontcourt faced not one, but two troubling developments in the first half.

Matched up with 7-foot-4 Purdue center Daniel Jacobsen, Massamba Diop struggled to defend without fouling and the Arizona State transfer was bench-bound after picking up his second foul with four minutes left in the opening frame. Fellow frontcourt starter Braden Huff, meanwhile, dealt with his own issues. Huff missed five of his first seven looks and struggled to convert the short floaters and push shots that usually drop at a near-automatic rate.

The Spanish forward who became eligible just 10 days earlier was Gonzaga's most effective big in many ways, providing 29 quality minutes off the bench in his college debut. Almansa was GU's top bench scorer with 11 points, making 4 of 6 shots from the field and going 3 of 3 from the free-throw line while adding four rebounds.

"He's been great, he's just been a pleasant surprise," Few said. "Easygoing guy, reminds me so much of Killian Tillie the way he can initiate offense. He's got just a wonderful feel for the game, you can have him bring the ball up the floor and I think these guys would tell you, players like that are really easy to play with."

Few may grow tired of the question eventually, but for now Gonzaga's coach is still willing to opine on the ongoing 5-in-5 eligibility chaos impacting his program and others around the country.

Gonzaga had two 5-in-5 transfers on the court Monday — Lee and fellow point guard Javon Bennett — and another, wing Chauncey Wiggins, on the bench. Wiggins, a 6-foot-10 sharpshooter who spent three seasons at Clemson and one at Florida State, continues to explore eligibility options after coming up emptyhanded in two separate state lawsuits.

Few was asked Monday how he's navigated an unprecedented era of college basketball.

"Great, we're well-educated now on the legal system, on the American justice system," Few said. "We wake up every day, these guys are practicing with us and they're wonderful teammates. They're all graduate students, do a great job in class. They're very, very good players and the exact type of players we want in college basketball."

Gonzaga's coach has been outspoken on the topic, reiterating Monday the high school class of 2022 should be grandfathered into the NCAA's new 5-in-5 eligibility model.

"The NCAA screwed up on this deal," Few said. "They did. We brought it up as coaches. We told them this isn't the right time. I'm involved with the Protect College Sports Act and that thing's coming down the pipe here shortly and you've just got to be fair and let these '22s in. So if you're on the right side of it, I think you always feel good about it and I feel great about it.

"Blessed to have X (Lee) playing, Javon came in and made two huge free throws there and when we get Chauncey, look out because he's a heckuva player."
