Measles cases by state in 2026: a look at 10 states in CDC data as of Nov. 5, from Pennsylvania's 1,476 cases to Florida's 149

Pennsylvania has the highest 2026 measles count in the CDC's state-by-state data, with 1,476 cases reported as of Nov. 5, 2026. That is 806 more than South Carolina, the next state on the list. Pennsylvania's count is also more than double South Carolina's 670.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention publishes U.S. measles cases by state for 2026 on its measles data page. The listing covers about 50 states. The District of Columbia and New York City appear as separate entries. This article looks at 10 of those states, ordered by their 2026 case counts.

Together, the 10 states in this selection account for 4,240 cases. Pennsylvania alone represents more than a third of that total. The top three states — Pennsylvania, South Carolina and Utah — hold 2,682 of those cases. Utah, third with 536 cases, is the only other state in the group above 500.

Wisconsin is fourth with 419 cases. The middle of the group is tightly packed. New York state has 210 cases, Kentucky has 204 and Ohio has 202, a spread of eight cases across three states. Texas follows with 192 and Virginia with 182. Florida closes the group with 149 cases.

The selection ends at a clear break. Minnesota follows Florida with 139 cases, 10 fewer. Arizona has 110, and Indiana has 105. No state ties Florida for 10th place.

The CDC listing gives two more figures for each state alongside its 2026 count. Each slide reports those numbers as they appear in the source. In Texas, the second of those figures is 803. That is 611 higher than the state's 2026 count of 192. Texas is the only state in the selection where that middle figure exceeds the 2026 count. Florida's third figure, 11, is the largest last figure among the 10 states.

New York state and New York City are listed on separate lines in the CDC data. The New York figure in this article is the state entry only.
