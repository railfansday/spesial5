Japan spent a record $50 billion in a single day to prop up the yen

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Tokyo bought 15.4 trillion yen over three days from July 30, including a joint operation with the U.S. on July 31

Japan committed a record 7.98 trillion yen (about $50 billion) on July 30 alone to shore up its currency, according to Ministry of Finance data released Monday.

The ministry’s quarterly figures showed that Tokyo stepped into the market on three separate days. It committed 5.55 trillion yen on July 31, then a further 1.87 trillion on Aug. 3, according to Reuters. The combined 15.4 trillion yen bought across those sessions makes it the largest yen-buying episode Japan has mounted, according to The Japan Times.

The July 30 outlay beat the prior one-day high of 6.28 trillion yen from April 30, in a ministry data series that starts in 1991, according to Reuters. The three-day total also came in higher than Japan's outlays during April and May, according to The Wall Street Journal.

The three-day breakdown surprised the market, because traders had assumed the intervention was confined to two sessions, according to The Japan Times.

The U.S. took part in the July 31 operation by selling euros for yen, according to Reuters. It was the first time since 1998 that Japan and the U.S. had worked in concert to lift the yen's value, according to The Japan Times.

Japanese Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama and U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confirmed the coordinated intervention, according to The Wall Street Journal. Bessent addressed the yen's decline candidly over the summer and endorsed Tokyo's moves to push back against the currency's undervaluation.

The yen had fallen to near 164 per dollar, its weakest level in 40 years, when Japan re-entered the market on July 30, according to Reuters.

The Bank of Japan lifted its benchmark rate to 1.25% in September, and another increase is anticipated before the year closes, according to The Wall Street Journal. The central bank has pointed to the weak yen as a significant consideration in its projections for the economy and inflation.
