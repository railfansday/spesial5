Prime Video Successfully Replaces The Boys After 5 Seasons

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Turning a controversial, divisive, and extremely graphic comic series into a live-action R-rated show was a huge gamble for Prime Video, yet The Boys quickly became one of the platform's biggest hits. Not only did it vastly exceed expectations to become one of the greatest superhero masterpieces of the decade, but it also gained a huge following, making it one of the most talked-about shows throughout its seven-year journey.

Unsurprisingly, Prime Video isn't letting go of the franchise after The Boys' series finale aired earlier this year, but despite Vought Rising and The Boys: Mexico being in development, the main show's conclusion leaves the door open for a new flagship superhero show to take over the platform. Luckily, Invincible's growing success makes it the perfect replacement.

While The Boys is undoubtedly the more mainstream series, Invincible found success alongside it and is often considered the better of Prime Video's two main R-rated superhero projects. Like The Boys, it is also a comic adaptation; however, Invincible is much more faithful to the source material, and as a result, tends to be more consistent.

Still, despite building its own dedicated fanbase and earning many plaudits, Invincible has never truly escaped The Boys' shadow, until now. With Invincible season 4 ending on a high, the stage is set perfectly for season 5, taking the project to the same number of seasons as The Boys, and with no signs of it slowing down, there couldn't be a more fitting replacement.

With season 5 scheduled to premiere in February 2027, Invincible is approaching the same milestone as The Boys, but there is still so much more story to tell. Having deviated heavily from the comics and opted for a largely different story, The Boys decided to call it quits after five seasons and 40 episodes, which proved to be the right decision.

Had it dragged on any longer, the show's reputation may have tanked even further, and there's still a variety of spinoffs that give fans of the franchise a reason to stick around. In contrast, Invincible is essentially a one-to-one adaptation, and while there was an Atom Eve special episode between seasons 1 and 2, the project has largely focused on bringing Robert Kirkman's original story to TV while adding a few extras.

So far, the series has covered about half of the comics' story, meaning we could still get another four seasons before it's all said and done. There's also the opportunity to expand this universe on-screen with more specials or miniseries, potentially following the likes of Tech Jacket or Battle Beast, who also have their own comic run.

Even without these though, Invincible will comfortably outlast The Boys and should come close to doubling its season and episode count, especially since season 6 is already confirmed. Considering the project is still extremely popular and continues to get more intense, it's hard to imagine it getting canceled, meaning it is likely to see out its entire vision.

Invincible is already set to break the record as Prime Video's longest-running animated show, and if it does reach season 8 or beyond, it could become the platform's longest show ever. This would make it a historic hit that transcends The Boys, and assuming it avoids a controversial ending like its competitor, Invincible is poised to have a superior legacy as well.

While Vought Rising will bring The Boys franchise back into the limelight in 2027, Invincible will inevitably step up as Prime Video's main superhero series. Having already accumulated a lively fan base as well as an immaculate reputation, Invincible has all the tools needed to step up, and the extra marketing could do wonders for this already successful hit.

Seasons 3 and 4 produced eight episodes that earned a 9 and above rating on IMDb, which means one in every two episodes achieved this incredible score. Two of them even earned a rare 9.9, showing the quality is certainly there. The fact that the story was left in such a good place means there's no reason to expect Invincible to decline, making it a seamless replacement for The Boys.

It's not without its flaws, as production delays have hurt the show in the past, while there remain concerns about the animation. However, recent years have seen improvement in both areas, particularly the length between seasons, and the fact that Invincible is releasing annually makes it even easier to promote and build an audience.

With a live-action movie supposedly still in production and a recent video game release, the franchise continues to evolve, which only helps make the show feel bigger. Invincible's ability to switch genres, feature plenty of humor during its brutal story, and throw shade at itself all adds to what makes it so special, proving why Prime Video should continue investing heavily.

Even if it never quite matches the mainstream appeal of The Boys, Invincible remains a massive gem that the platform can utilize for years to come, and if they were to give it a bigger budget and iron out any lingering issues, the superhero series could become one of Prime Video's greatest hits of all time.
