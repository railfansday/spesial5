Guarding Stars Review: A Charming Holiday Movie That Follows All The Rules

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Guarding Stars is far from groundbreaking as far as Christmas romcoms go, but Leighton Meester and Jared Padalecki make it worthy of a watch.

Somewhat cheesy Christmas romcoms have been a holiday season staple for decades now, and unlike many other genres, part of the popularity of these films is their predictability. Generally, people aren't checking out the latest Hallmark or Netflix holiday movie looking for plot twists or significant departures from the standard fare. In fact, as much as the tropes of this genre – like a city girl making her way to a small town, finding love and the joy of Christmas, and deciding to abandon her corporate job – have become comedy fodder online, that is exactly why they work.

The bar was therefore relatively low for Netflix's latest holiday movie, Guarding Stars, which was just released on the streaming platform. Yet, two elements of this movie raised the stakes slightly. For one, Guarding Stars is an adaptation of a novel, titled The Bodyguard, and that always adds a slightly different level of expectation. Fans of the book have their own ideas about how the characters should act, look, and sound.

Additionally, the leads of Guarding Stars are both major names: Gossip Girl's Leighton Meester plays the protagonist, Hannah, and Jared Padalecki, of Gilmore Girls and Supernatural fame, plays her love interest, Jack. Ultimately, both of these aspects helped. The source material gives the movie more originality than is typical of the genre, and Padalecki and Meester are both very charming in their roles. However, this movie is far from revolutionary, and audiences will (more or less) only be getting exactly what they came for.

Unsurprisingly, given their respective backgrounds, both Padalecki and Meester nail their characters in Guarding Stars. They are easily the standouts of the movie, and honestly why this holiday entry has a solid chance to stand at least a little apart from the crowd. While Meester is funny and relatable, it's often Padalecki who steals the show. This is somewhat unexpected, not because he doesn't have the acting chops, but rather because he's never really played a lead in a romcom like this.

Guarding Stars hits all the points that romcom fans will appreciate.

Yes, when he was younger, he was Dean on Gilmore Girls, a role for which he was almost exclusively a love interest – but that show was certainly different from a Netflix Christmas movie. Hilariously, the closest approximation audiences have really seen from Padalecki is his hipster Sam Winchester persona in one episode of Supernatural. That's only really true in the beginning, when Jack comes across as a cool, slightly jerk-ish celebrity (although it's very clear that Padalecki is having fun with that character type, which makes it all the more enjoyable to watch).

Guarding Stars also hits all the points that romcom fans will appreciate, from the basic plot involving a fake dating dynamic (a tried and true romance novel scenario) to the "only one bed" romcom trope. And, to Netflix's credit, while the movie plays it more than safe by touching on those plot points, there are ways in which Guarding Stars is better than the typical streaming Christmas movie. For one, Meester and Padalecki have legitimate chemistry; for another, there are some genuinely funny, sweet moments that, at the very least, make the film fun to watch.

Nevertheless, Guarding Stars' shortcomings are numerous and obvious. The greatest offense of the film is its primary villain (although, in fairness, there is actually a story decision toward the end that ameliorates this problem to a degree), who is, at multiple moments, cartoonishly evil. The motivations the villain has are also flimsy at best, and this entire arc feels like a throwaway concept to give the movie an edge while really wanting to focus on the relationship between Jack and Hannah.

What should be the most harrowing moment of the plot is also undercut by the fact that the stunt itself looks like a clip one would see from behind-the-scenes footage before editors get their hands on it and make it look significantly more convincing in the final cut. Even so, Guarding Stars does what it set out to do. Yes, it's a typical holiday movie, and it mostly follows the story beats one would expect, but it is also fun and even cute in its goofiness. Fans of the book will no doubt like it, and those looking for a new feel-good holiday film will find it here.

Guarding Stars is now streaming on Netflix.
