Stellar Floats XLM Rewards Pool After 1,000% Payments Jump

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Home » Trendsetter News » Stellar Floats XLM Rewards Pool After 1,000% Payments Jump

Stellar is exploring an XLM rewards pool funded by network fees as monthly payment volume rises tenfold.

Stellar is considering a proposal to reward XLM holders who lock tokens and contribute utility to the network. Crypto market commentator All In Crypto highlights the idea in a video reviewing Stellar’s third-quarter webinar, asking whether rising network activity can translate into stronger demand for XLM.

The proposal would put network-generated fees into an XLM rewards pool. But Dixon, speaking during the webinar, stresses that it remains an early concept open to feedback, not an approved change or a promised payout.

“I’m not sure this is the right proposal,” she says, urging participants to read the accompanying paper and explore its interactive dashboard before commenting. Her central argument is that “utility should drive XLM.”

The webinar reports that monthly payment volume on Stellar rose from roughly $1.8 billion in January to $18.5 billion in October. October’s figure was 89% higher than September’s, following eight consecutive months of growth.

Another $7.5 billion reportedly moved during November’s first 10 days. Those figures describe money moving across the network, however, not purchases of XLM or returns accruing to token holders.

All In Crypto is openly bullish but acknowledges uncertainty over the token’s future value. That distinction matters: greater use of Stellar’s infrastructure does not, by itself, establish how much additional XLM users need to hold.

The webinar also describes a U.S. Bank pilot in which the bank issued a dollar-backed stablecoin called USBDC and used it to settle a cross-border payment between its North American and European entities. According to the presentation, minting, redemption, freezing and claw-back controls operated at the protocol level, without custom contract logic.

Stellar’s Project Throughput offers another glimpse of its ambitions. The webinar cites early benchmarks of roughly 1.2 million payments per second on one machine and about seven million across eight machines.

Those are technology-preview results, not reported production-network throughput. The project is available on Futurenet with open-source code for developers to test.

Now, the unresolved question is less whether Stellar can process more activity than how that activity benefits XLM.

The rewards proposal could create another connection between network use and token ownership, but its design, eligibility requirements and eventual adoption remain unsettled.
