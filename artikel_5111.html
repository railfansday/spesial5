US card debt hits $1.29 trillion as Bitcoin loans expose a collateral divide

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

US card debt reached $1.29 trillion. Bitcoin-backed loans advertise lower rates, but borrowers must weigh fees and the risk of an automatic collateral sale.

US credit-card balances reached $1.29 trillion in the third quarter, the New York Fed reported on Nov. 10. Bitcoin-backed loans advertise lower borrowing rates, but the comparison comes with a different risk: losing the collateral in an automatic sale.

The New York Fed's report puts card balances $22 billion higher than the previous quarter and $52 billion higher than a year earlier.

Separately, the Federal Reserve's latest measure of interest assessed on card accounts was 22.36%, while Coinbase advertises crypto-backed borrowing from 5.1%.

That gap can translate into substantially lower modeled interest costs, but accessing it requires assets. A $10,000 payout would require $25,500 in Bitcoin at a chosen 40% initial loan-to-value ratio once the processing fee is financed.

The rate may be attractive, but the collateral can be sold automatically if the loan becomes too large relative to its backing.

Consider a constant $10,000 card balance for one year, with no principal repayments and simple interest at the Fed's 22.36% measure, and the modeled interest cost is $2,236.

For Coinbase's variable-rate product, assume a payout worth $10,000 in USDC and hold the advertised 5.1% rate constant for a year. Coinbase's variable-loan processing fee is 2% on the first $250,000, with 1% charged on amounts above that tier.

The $200 fee brings the opening debt to $10,200, and simple interest on that amount is $520.20. Adding the fee produces a modeled first-year borrowing cost of $720.20.

The gross difference is about $1,516. This simplified illustration gives neither a Bitcoin borrower quote nor guaranteed savings, and it does not calculate a full APR. It excludes platform fees, compounding, rate changes, conversion costs, and liquidation effects.

Coinbase says a platform fee may apply monthly to variable-rate loans, and the lending rate also changes with supply and demand, so 5.1% cannot be treated as a promised yearlong cost. The actual offer determines how much of the apparent gap survives.

On the card side, the 22.36% figure in the Federal Reserve's Nov. 6 consumer-credit release appears in the August and third-quarter observations for commercial-bank accounts assessed interest. The corresponding figure for all accounts was 21.19%.

The Fed annualizes finance charges relative to the average daily balances on which those charges were imposed, excluding accounts without finance charges. The rate cannot simply be applied to every dollar of the New York Fed's $1.29 trillion estimate.

Coinbase says eligible, verified US customers outside New York can borrow without a credit check. Borrowing capacity depends on eligible collateral, with limits shown in the product. Removing the credit check replaces it with an asset requirement.

Through the integration, pledged Bitcoin is converted into Coinbase Wrapped Bitcoin (cbBTC) and transferred to a smart contract on Morpho, a lending protocol on Base.

Collateral held there is marked locked and nontradable, although qualifying excess collateral can be withdrawn within loan-to-value (LTV) limits. Variable-rate loans have no scheduled minimum payments or fixed due date, but interest continues accruing. Payment flexibility still requires monitoring the collateral.

LTV divides outstanding debt by the collateral's current market value, so a $10,000 debt at an illustrative 40% initial LTV needs $25,000 of Bitcoin before fees.

Including the financed $200 processing fee raises that requirement to $25,500 to preserve the same starting ratio.

If debt reaches the market's liquidation threshold relative to collateral, a sale can happen automatically. For Bitcoin-backed borrowing, Coinbase describes repayment of debt and interest with an additional 4.38% penalty. Any Bitcoin left after the required liquidation returns to the Coinbase account.

For a hypothetical Bitcoin-backed loan with an 86% liquidation threshold and constant debt, a loan beginning at 40% LTV reaches that threshold after a roughly 53.5% fall in collateral value. A separate loan beginning at 60% LTV reaches the same threshold after about a 30.2% fall.

A larger cushion requires more collateral or less borrowing. Coinbase also identifies protocol security, liquidity, and Coinbase Smart Wallet issues as risks, adding dependencies alongside the obligation to repay.

The New York Fed said credit-card transitions into early and serious delinquency improved slightly in the third quarter. Annualized flows into serious delinquency were 6.9%, versus 7.1% a year earlier.

In a separate Nov. 10 analysis, New York Fed economists said card delinquency increased most sharply after 2022 among borrowers in lower-income neighborhoods, widening the gap with higher-income areas.

For someone with sufficient eligible assets, crypto-backed borrowing may reduce interest costs after fees. For someone without them, the advertised rate does not resolve the financing problem, as borrowing capacity hinges on the assets available to pledge.

The useful comparison is the fee-inclusive offer against the amount of crypto committed and the liquidation buffer left behind. A lower borrowing rate can coexist with a new risk of losing Bitcoin through an automatic sale when the borrower intended to keep it.
