Pierre Sage fears for his Crystal Palace future after Brighton humiliation

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Pierre Sage has admitted that he fears for his future at Crystal Palace, adding that he felt “shame” after his side slumped to their joint-heaviest defeat by rivals Brighton and Hove Albion.

Sage replaced Oliver Glasner as manager in June but since then Palace have won just two league matches with him in at the helm. The south London club have taken seven points from seven Premier League games and have already conceded 17 goals, the most of any team in the league.

“I don’t know,” Sage said when asked if he will get enough time to rectify Palace’s slump. He admitted that his shame was about how Palace were overwhelmed, leaking three goals within the first nine minutes of the game before eventually losing 5-0.

“We are not at the level we expected, and I’m really sorry for that,” the 47-year-old said. “I just want to apologise to the fans because it’s not normal to show this face.”

The Frenchman said that he took full responsibility for the 5-0 defeat, and understood why hundreds of Palace fans left well before the final whistle.

“I cannot blame anybody because we didn’t do the job in a good way,” he said. “I’m responsible for this team, and when the team play as today, it’s not normal. It’s my fault. I understand them.

“Another feeling is about shame because that’s my feeling after the game and from the beginning. It’s not normal to not respect the necessary of this kind of game.”

Sage admitted that he will be assessing how Palace need to rethink their approach in the coming fixtures, beginning with Thursday’s trip to Besiktas in the Europa League.

“The first work to do is on me,” he said. “I’m responsible for what’s happened on the pitch, and I have a lot of work to do because I have to change this kind of thing.

“It’s maybe in the preparation, in the way we give them the motivation to start the game just before in the locker room in the warm-up. We have to analyse that because it’s not normal. And today it was really at the highest level we cannot expect, and that’s the moment to change the thing.”

For all the scrutiny on his back three, Sage urged his entire side to take greater defensive responsibility, highlighting how the team were too passive in pressing Brighton. Palace have struggled to regain the solidity that long characterised the side since the departures of Marc Guéhi in January and Maxence Lacroix this summer.

“The way of defending is not only on the back three; it is for every player, and there are some things to do collectively, too,” Sage said. “When we defend low we struggle a lot.”

Sage joined Palace after a fine campaign with Lens. He led the club to second place in Ligue 1, just six points off Paris St-Germain, and victory in the Coupe de France. Yet, despite Sage’s reputation when he moved to Selhurst Park, his suitability for the Palace job is increasingly being questioned.

Three years ago, Patrick Vieira lost his job after a defeat by Brighton. Now, Sage could suffer the same fate, with doubts growing that he will still be in charge for Thursday’s trip to Besiktas.

“It’s not my decision,” Sage said. “I don’t want to accept the level of the moment if we are able to do better. And today the work is really important to do, and I will do it. It’s my job, and I’d like to change this.”

With only Manchester City and Arsenal above them, Brighton and Hove Albion are entitled to dream of making history. Yet, whatever happens between now and May, one accolade is already surely their own: the goal of the season award.

Diego Gómez’s 56-yard strike will instantly become the stuff of Brighton lore. The Paraguayan’s audacity will be even more cherished for coming against Crystal Palace, who were pummelled 5-0 in the M23 derby.

All Premier League defeats are equal but some are more equal than others. Three years ago, Patrick Vieira lost his job after a defeat by Brighton. Now, Pierre Sage is at grave risk of suffering the same fate. Good afternoon and welcome to our live coverage of the M23 derby – or El Gatwicko, as the kids call it these days – as high-flying Brighton and Hove Albion host Crystal Palace at the Amex Stadium. Being frank, this is neither the fiercest nor the most storied Premier League rivalry, but few would have imagined when these sides faced off in the Third Division in the mid-1970s that this would ever become a top-flight match-up sandwiched between European games.

Brighton have been outstanding this season and took their league form into the Conference League, thrashing Lithuanian champions Kauno Zalgiris on Thursday night. Fabian Hürzeler was delighted by his fringe players’ performances on Thursday night and may have some selection questions to answer with Jack Hinshelwood and Georginio Rutter finding the net. Brighton are third in the Premier League and were the division’s heaviest scorers after the last round of matches.

Yet the Palace nut has been a tough one to crack for Hürzeler, having not yet registered a win in four attempts. When asked what he enjoyed most about the rivalry, the German said: “Nothing yet, because I didn’t win any games so far. Hopefully on Sunday this changes… I like these games because they’re derbies, more emotions, it’s a rivalry, it’s [us] against Crystal Palace.”

In his pre-match interview Hürzeler admitted to being an admirer of Pierre Sage, and while the Frenchman’s side have failed to match Brighton’s fast start, they rallied impressively to defeat rivals Nottingham Forest last weekend and fought back valiantly away at Lyon, albeit in a losing effort, in midweek. The return of Ismaïla Sarr from injury is a significant boost.

Elsewhere Bournemouth host Sunderland hoping to secure their first Premier League victory of the season. Marco Rose’s side and Tottenham are the only teams still without a win, but Bournemouth have been perfect in the Europa League and will look to transfer some of that momentum into the league.
