Youri Tielemans In Trouble at Man Utd as Pochettino Methods Emerge

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Mauricio Pochettino has arguably the biggest job in football on his hands now that he has been charged with turning around the miserable fortunes at Manchester United.

The USMNT boss will arrive at Old Trafford next week, upon completing his international duties during the current break, to find a club in turmoil and stuck in the relegation zone three months into the season.

His methods simply must have a major impact or he is going to follow the same way as a growing list of predecessors in departing the manager's role in the not too distant future.

Michael Carrick's time in charge, similar to that of Ole Gunnar Solskjaer, has shown that rescuing a calamity is a very different job at United than relaunching the club as a successful entity moving forward.

Much has been made of the cycle between managerial styles, from big-name disciplinarian, to former player and squad-friendly vibesman, to expansive European tactician, with Pochettino arguably sitting somewhere between the first and third of those tags.

His reputation for old-school adherence to physical fitness is very much more of the former and is likely to come as a shock to some of the current side who have so badly underperformed this season.

According to official Premier League statistics, United are 19th out of 20 teams in terms of total distance covered so far this season, and 18th in distance covered per 90 minutes.

While that often translates as the teams with lower amounts of possession being obliged to make most of the running, it is probably also instructive that league leaders Arsenal rank second in both statistics this term.

Gary Neville spoke to former Tottenham full-back Kyle Walker on The Overlap earlier this year and mentioned visiting Pochettino's training to find the Argentinian making his players do heavy running drills during the season.

Walker explained how far the coach would push them using the "Gacon Test", as he compared Pochettino's fitness focus to Pep Guardiola's ball-work focus at Manchester City.

There are numerous examples of big names detailing how hard they were made to work around games when playing for Pochettino, and how it doesn't compare to any other coach they'd ever had.

Harry Kane responded to questions in 2015 about ex-Southampton forward Dani Osvaldo having claimed the manager had worked him "like a dog" at St. Mary's by admitting, "He works us hard, maybe dog's a bit strong."

Then-Spurs midfielder Moussa Dembele told The Guardian the following year “I’ve played in other teams and we speak with a lot of other players and you can’t compare it... We work very, very hard and the training sessions are still tough now."

Former Tottenham full-back Kieran Trippier told journalists in 2017 that he was considered overweight when he arrived from Burnley, and said, “It’s really intense. It’s relentless really, how much stuff we do,"

While ex-Saints striker Rickie Lambert previously told BBC Radio Solent Pochettino had reacted to his request to reduce in-season running during training by instead making it even harder, saying, "I knew exactly what he was doing and he broke me!".

If that's how the ex-PSG boss worked his teams that were doing well, then it seems a foregone conclusion that he will arrive with a bang on the training pitches at Carrington for a sluggish and underperforming United squad.

One of those most in line for a rude awakening from Pochettino's arrival could be summer signing Youri Tielemans. The Belgian international is far from the worst performing in terms of distance covered within the United squad, but already faces being one of the odd men out in the midfield with the new boss tipped to favour Kobbie Mainoo and Carlos Baleba.

The 29-year-old seemed to have landed firmly on his feet upon arrival from Aston Villa in the summer, as he happily explained to Rio Ferdinand how his status as a player returning from the World Cup meant "you don't do loads of running" and just "chill".

He went on to speak about how Carrick "sees me in him" due to their similar playing styles, so to lose the former England international already will have been a blow to his prospects in itself.

If Tielemans isn't one of Pochettino's first choices to lead the recovery this term, then he is going to have to force him to change that stance with his output in training, which might be that much tougher if he is obliged to go from minimal running to extensive amounts in between match days.

There is no reason why it would be impossible for the former Leicester and Villa star to do that, but just four months after his big move to United, the Belgian faces a major readjustment that could have long-term ramifications for his career at the club.

A return to relentless energy and tactical intensity with targeted youth promotion and off-field discipline at Old Trafford.
