Tidal Becomes First Streaming Service to Ban AI Music From Its Platform

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The company claims that 94% of AI-generated tracks uploaded to the platform come from Suno.

Tidal announced Tuesday (Nov. 24) that AI-generated music will be banned entirely from the platform, beginning Dec. 9. This follows the company’s announcement in July that AI music had been demonetized and labeled, with requirements for distributors to label AI songs. Tidal says that it will remove any songs that distributors say were created solely with AI tools.

In a post on social media platform X (formerly known as Twitter), Tidal claimed that 94% of AI-badged tracks had come from Suno. The company also claimed in a blog post that as of Nov. 6, 60% of the songs that it had received daily were AI-generated, that 96% of AI-generated songs on its platform had received no streams over the last three months and that it had taken down 66,000 tracks from people it suspects are impersonating another artist. The company says that songs created with assistance from AI tools, but are not entirely AI-generated, will be allowed to remain on the platform.

“If you make music, you should be able to put it on Tidal knowing it won’t be buried under tracks no one asked for, or copied by someone pretending to be you,” the blog post read.

Tidal’s statement also said that 43% of songs delivered to the platform over the last three months have been marked as AI and labeled with a badge. Labeling has been a big part of the push to regulate AI music across platforms. This summer, a coalition of record labels got together to propose a new system for tagging AI-generated songs on streaming services and called on those platforms to adopt it. So far, none of them have adopted that exact proposal, but services like Tidal, Spotify, Deezer and Apple Music all created some form of a system on their own.

Tidal is the first streaming service to outright ban AI songs from its platform. It joins online record store service BandCamp and radio company iHeartRadio as the only firms placing a full prohibition on fully AI works. Other sites like Deezer, Spotify and Apple Music have explored solutions that fall short of a total ban. Commonly, services will try labeling AI music, regulating spam and fraud associated with it or removing it from recommendation engines, rather than resorting to a full ban.

Just last week, SoundCloud announced a new policy update to better tackle harmful side effects of AI music, like spam, impersonation and streaming fraud. The platform, however, has taken a much more flexible stance toward AI than other services. SoundCloud recently formed partnerships with AI companies Moises and ElevenLabs to allow a direct pipeline for those generated songs from those trusted partners to be shared on SoundCloud.

Importantly, both services make songs based on fully licensed training data and have large support within the music business. “Simply wishing away the advent of AI music is not a strategy,” SoundCloud CEO Eliah Seton told Billboard about the new policy update. “There’s been a very binary frame: [AI music] is either good or evil, accept it or reject it…it’s high time we changed the frame from something so binary and reductive to include the nuance that artists deserve.”
