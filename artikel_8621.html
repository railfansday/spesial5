Buy a money order where the fee is lowest, and pay with cash or a debit card. Before you leave the counter, fill in the name of whoever you’re paying, since only that person or business can cash it, and add your name, your address, and a memo. Keep the receipt until it’s cashed.

Walmart charges no more than $1 for a money order, while Chase charges $5 apiece on its Total Checking account.

The post office charges $2.65 for a money order of up to $500 and $3.75 for one of more than $500. No postal money order can be for more than $1,000. Like Walmart, the post office takes cash or a debit card, and it refuses credit cards.

That makes Walmart the cheapest of the three, unless your landlord asks for postal money orders. Say your rent is $1,200 and your landlord does. Buy one for $1,000 and one for $200, and the fees come to $6.40 ($3.75 plus $2.65), while two $600 money orders would cost $7.50. For a payment that would take several money orders, ask your bank for a cashier’s check instead.
