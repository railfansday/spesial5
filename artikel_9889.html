Royal Navy orders £142M in minehunting drones from US supplier

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Huntington Ingalls Industries lands deal despite MoD pledge to buy British wherever possible

Britain's Royal Navy is investing £142 million ($185 million) in underwater drones to tackle sea mines, but the contract has gone to an American defense supplier.

The Ministry of Defence (MoD) says that the UK's ability to protect vital sea routes will be strengthened through the procurement of these Uncrewed Underwater Vehicles (UUVs).

These will form part of the Royal Navy's move toward a "Hybrid Navy" comprising a mix of crewed, uncrewed, and autonomous platforms in order to meet future challenges.

The UUV selected for the job is the REMUS 620, built by Huntington Ingalls Industries (HII), the largest military shipbuilding company in the United States. HII has a facility at Portchester, on the south coast of England, but is still a US-owned company.

We asked the MoD how the award fits its pledge to prioritize buying British wherever possible, and what share of the contract's value will support UK industry.

The contract includes HII's Sea Launcher automated launch and recovery systems and will bring support services previously delivered overseas to the UK. HII says it will support 15 jobs, while the MoD identifies Portchester among the locations benefiting.

HII says this is the largest international order in its 25-year history of supplying uncrewed systems. The MoD has previously acquired REMUS 100, REMUS 300, and REMUS 600 vehicles for mine countermeasure operations.

The new vehicles will locate sea mines to support clearance operations and help keep shipping routes open.

By using drones to comb a body of water for explosive devices, the Royal Navy's crews can be kept a safe distance from any potential immediate threat. Traditionally, this work has been conducted by specialist crewed ships such as the Hunt class or Sandown class, but these are being retired from service in favor of uncrewed systems.

The Royal Navy also operates SWEEP, an uncrewed boat towing equipment that mimics a ship's magnetic, acoustic, and electric signatures to trigger mines from a safe distance.

The new REMUS underwater vehicles can be sent ahead of other vessels to search beneath the surface, making minehunting operations safer, faster and more flexible, according to the MoD.

"This investment strengthens our ability to protect freedom of navigation, which is critical to our security and our economy," said Defence Secretary Wes Streeting MP.

Like others in the portfolio, REMUS 620 resembles a naval torpedo. It is capable of operating at depths of 600 meters and has an endurance of up to 110 hours on a single mission. The drones will be integrated with SeeTrack and Neptune software developed by SeeByte in the UK, which is already used by the Royal Navy.

In trials announced in October 2025, a REMUS 620 demonstrated launch and recovery using a submerged test fixture replicating a Virginia-class submarine's torpedo tube. The work could enable submarines to deploy and retrieve underwater drones on future missions. ®
