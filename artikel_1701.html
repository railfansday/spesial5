Guardiola urges Portugal to ‘ensure a dignified end to Ronaldo’s career’

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Pep Guardiola has called on the Portuguese Football Federation (FPF) to "ensure a dignified end to Cristiano Ronaldo's career".

Ronaldo called time on his international career earlier this month, retiring as Portugal's all-time leading scorer and appearance-maker, netting 146 goals in 234 games.

After making his debut in 2003, Ronaldo led Portugal to their first-ever major trophy at Euro 2016 and also won the Nations League in 2019 and 2025.

His time with Portugal ended abruptly, with the 41-year-old leaving their camp midway through the September-October international break after a fallout with head coach Jorge Jesus.

He was subsequently given a 30-day suspension by the FPF, though he announced his retirement ahead of the November break.

The FPF said they would plan a farewell for Ronaldo, and Guardiola has urged them to do so.

"The Portuguese Football Federation must ensure a dignified end to Cristiano Ronaldo's career," Guardiola said on Romario TV.

"Cristiano Ronaldo shouldn't leave the Portuguese national team in this manner. They can't let him go like this.

"There are exceptional players, and Cristiano Ronaldo is one of them; therefore, this situation must be rectified.

"Whether they rectify it or not isn't my concern, but they absolutely must. That's right; what he's achieved in the world of football deserves a different ending."

Messi also retired from international football this season, after Argentina finished runners-up at the 2026 World Cup.

Guardiola believes Messi and Ronaldo both helped prolong the other's career at the highest level.

"I think the two of them helped each other a great deal; if Messi hadn't had Cristiano, or if Cristiano hadn't had Messi, their careers would likely have been shorter," Guardiola added.

"But they had each other in their rearview mirrors; watching what the other was doing made them think: 'I want to be better than him, I want to win more than him'."
