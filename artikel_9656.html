MEXC Faces US Financial Restrictions Over Alleged Sanctions Evasion

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The US Treasury says it has severed MEXC Global from the United States. The reason it gives is an alleged $16 billion in transactions connected to Iran, North Korea, and Russia.

On November 6, FinCEN issued an order against the exchange and said it was a money laundering concern. I'm working from The Block's reporting by Sarah Wynn here, and I've linked the order so you can read what it says yourself.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent didn't soften it in his statement:

"Financial institutions cannot have it both ways: Either you support the Iranian regime, or you have access to the U.S. financial system."

He went on to say that Treasury will keep going after the Iranian regime's economic lifelines and its attempts to get around US sanctions with digital assets. The department added that the action will protect the US financial system from the illicit finance threats it says MEXC Global poses.

Treasury also addressed what MEXC says about itself. The exchange says it maintains anti-money laundering controls and that it will block people in countries including Iran and North Korea from using it. Treasury's answer was that those AML controls had "severe deficiencies."

The Block describes the past year as a crackdown of sorts on crypto platforms that allegedly help Iran evade sanctions, with an ongoing war against the country in the background. MEXC is the latest name in a sequence:

The report describes the first two as Iranian exchanges. It doesn't give MEXC that label, and the $16 billion Treasury cites is tied to North Korea and Russia as well as Iran.

On Nov. 7, MEXC said in a post on X that it was continuing to operate normally and that user assets are secure. CEO Vugar Usi said deposits and withdrawals were continuing and that the platform is taking the concerns raised by FinCEN seriously.

"We have indeed seen an increase in withdrawal volumes, but everything has remained orderly. We sincerely appreciate the understanding and trust our users have shown MEXC at this critical moment."

The quotes in The Block's report don't show MEXC disputing the $16 billion figure. They say the company takes FinCEN's concerns seriously. The report was last updated on Monday, November 9.

The first is the gap between what an exchange says about itself and what a regulator says it found. MEXC says it blocks users in countries including Iran and North Korea. Treasury says the controls behind that had severe deficiencies and puts the alleged volume at $16 billion. That is a disagreement over how a stated policy holds up in practice, and the linked order is the place to look for Treasury's detail.

The second is the speed. The order came on Friday. By Saturday, the CEO was confirming higher withdrawal volumes. If you keep funds on any exchange, that's the timeline to keep in mind. I can't tell you from this report how things will play out for MEXC users, only that the CEO describes deposits and withdrawals as continuing and the situation as orderly.
