Micron Stock Just Hit Its Lowest Price Since May. I'd Buy More Now.

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

With most of this year's output already spoken for, has the memory maker's stock dropped too far?

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 21, 2026 at 3:52PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Micron Technology ›

Shares of Micron Technology (MU -13.89%) fell about 14% on Friday to close near $595. That's the lowest close for the memory chipmaker's stock since early May.

Micron closed at a record high of $1,213.56 on June 25, so the stock has lost about half its value over five months. And memory stocks dropped across the board on Friday, with Sandisk down about 16%.

But management said in late September that customers had already committed to over three-quarters of Micron's planned shipments for this fiscal year. At roughly $595, the stock costs about 3.5 times expected earnings for that year, fiscal 2027, which ends in early September 2027.

I think the fall has more to do with worries over memory prices than with how much Micron can sell.

Micron president and chief operating officer Manish Bhatia said on the company's Sept. 30 earnings call that "we have more than 75% of our shipments committed for the year," referring to fiscal 2027. He added that this "allows us to shift our conversations on allocation with our customers out to 2028."

Put another way, buyers have mostly claimed their share of Micron's output through next September, and the talks have already moved on.

The numbers back that up, helped by demand from artificial intelligence (AI) data centers. Micron's revenue in its fiscal fourth quarter, the 14 weeks ending Sept. 3, came to $54.2 billion. That was 31% higher than in the previous quarter and 379% above the year-ago quarter. Earnings per share climbed more than elevenfold year over year, to $32.87. For the first quarter of fiscal 2027, management sees revenue of about $61.5 billion, roughly 13% above fiscal Q4, and it expects every quarter of the year to beat the one before.

So growth is easing from a blistering pace. But it isn't reversing.

Committed shipments, though, don't all come with committed prices. Micron's multiyear strategic customer agreements, which it estimates will make up over 35% of its revenue through 2030, are where it sets most prices ahead of time. Even in those deals, around a quarter of the expected revenue is priced periodically based on market prices.

That probably leaves most of Micron's sales linked to where memory prices head. And prices did a lot of the work last quarter.

In fiscal Q4, prices for Micron's DRAM (the short-term memory in servers, PCs, and phones) rose a high-teens percentage from the previous quarter. DRAM made up 73% of revenue. Prices for its NAND flash storage chips, 26% of revenue, climbed around 30%.

These price gains show in Micron's gross margin under generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP). It was 44.7% a year before, reached 84.6% by fiscal Q3, then hit 86.8% in fiscal Q4. Management's forecast for this quarter is around 86%. It expects margins to inch up over the rest of fiscal 2027, though with "a more moderate rate of price increases."

There are early signs prices might ease. On Nov. 19, TrendForce predicted that NAND flash contract prices may drop 0% to 5% in the first quarter of 2027, with a broader downturn starting in the second quarter. Also, TrendForce said the average spot price for mainstream DDR4 memory chips dipped around 1% in the week ending Nov. 17, while branded DDR5 spot prices were mostly steady.

Sure, a 1% dip in spot prices isn't a memory bust. But I'd track DRAM prices closely from here. After all, Micron's profits have ridden on rising prices.

Micron has plenty of room before lower prices erase its profits. The cost of the chips Micron sold was just around 13% of its revenue in fiscal Q4. Rough math shows that if prices dropped 30% and these costs stayed flat, its gross margin would still be about 81%. That would be around where it was for all of fiscal 2026, and about double fiscal 2025's 39.8%.

Of course, prices might drop more than that. And costs won't stand still while Micron keeps building. Its capital expenditures came to about $27.4 billion in fiscal 2026, almost double the year before.

But the stock price already allows for a steep fall in profits. Even on fiscal 2026's results, when Micron earned $74.33 per share, the stock costs just around 8 times earnings. On this year's expected earnings, it's under half that.

I'd buy more here, albeit only if the position is still small even after my addition, and I'd keep Micron a small part of a diversified portfolio. Memory prices can turn fast, and the stock might drop further before they do.
