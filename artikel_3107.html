Greg Abel Just Approved a Record $9.6 Billion in Stock Buybacks for Berkshire Hathaway Last Quarter, More Than Double the Pace Set in the Second Quarter. Is Berkshire Still a Buy as Shares Sit Near All-Time Highs?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Berkshire Hathaway is using its cash hoard to buy its own stock, which suggests the board believes it's still a good investment.

By Reuben Gregg Brewer – Nov 21, 2026 at 7:15AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Berkshire Hathaway ›

Greg Abel took over as CEO of Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -0.47%)(BRKB -0.36%) at the start of 2026, when longtime CEO Warren Buffett stepped down. It was the biggest change at the company in decades. However, Buffett hand-picked Abel and helped to train him for the top spot. And yet, Abel has been surprisingly aggressive in his decision to repurchase Berkshire Hathaway's own stock, something Buffett was very careful about.

With Berkshire Hathaway's stock sitting near all-time highs, is it worth buying? Abel clearly thinks the answer is yes. If you have been thinking about buying the stock, you may want to listen to what his actions are saying.

Before digging into Berkshire Hathaway's repurchase, it is worth taking a quick look at another investment Greg Abel orchestrated during the quarter. But that story actually goes back to the first big acquisition under Abel: Taylor Morrison Home. It was an $8.5 billion purchase, with Abel specifically stating, "Over time, we expect to unify our site-built homebuilding operations into a combined platform enabling us to deliver the dream of homeownership to more Americans."

Home builders are deeply out of favor today, with home affordability already an issue even before interest rates started to rise (making mortgages more expensive). It was an opportunistic acquisition that was reminiscent of Warren Buffett's investment approach. This is why it was notable that Abel nearly doubled the company's position in publicly traded homebuilder Lennar (LEN +1.01%) during the third quarter. Abel is essentially buying into an industry he believes is mispriced by the market, extending the bet he made with Taylor Morrison Home. Again, this is really no different than what Buffett had done many times before in his long tenure as CEO.

The key point here is that Abel hasn't suddenly and dramatically changed the way Berkshire Hathaway operates. Historically, the company bought back its own shares only when it believed the stock was trading below its intrinsic value. Of course, that value is determined by the company, and it doesn't disclose the figure it arrives at. But the fact that Greg Abel is buying back stock is a clear indication that he sees value, even though the share price is near all-time highs.

The pace of the share repurchase picked up materially between the second and third quarters, too. In the second quarter, the financial giant bought $4.5 billion worth. In the third quarter, that more than doubled to $9.6 billion. Those are huge numbers, but there's an important nuance here that shows up on the balance sheet.

Far too often, companies take on debt to fund stock buybacks. Berkshire Hathaway ended the third quarter with roughly $377 billion in cash. That is a massive sum of money, so the $9.6 billion won't weaken the company's financial position. In fact, the company has more cash now than it did at the start of 2026, when it had $373 billion. Buying back Berkshire Hathaway shares is just another investment decision, and it isn't hugely material to the company's operations or financial position.

Essentially, Greg Abel's actions suggest he and the board believe Berkshire Hathaway's stock is worth buying today. These weren't bold moves, but that's not the point. The buyback is a signal, and it's pretty clear: Berkshire Hathaway stock is attractive even at current levels.

Would it be fair to suggest that Berkshire Hathaway is a screaming buy? Probably not. However, if you have been thinking about buying the stock yourself, don't let the stock's lofty price tag scare you away. After all, that fact didn't scare Abel away.
