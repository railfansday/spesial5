Pursuit is earning more from each visitor, but the real test is how much cash is left after reinvesting in its attractions and lodges.

Experiential attractions in busy destinations can grow without drawing bigger crowds. An operator can sell a better ticket, add a second experience, or give visitors a reason to stay the night. But outside theme parks and ski resorts, few public companies show what that business earns, or how much cash it takes to keep growing.

Pursuit Attractions and Hospitality is one that does. It runs the Banff Gondola, the Maligne Lake cruise to Spirit Island, and Sky Lagoon in Iceland, along with lodges in several of the same markets. Pursuit expects about $485 million in revenue in 2026. Excluding Flyover, the flying-theater business it sold in July, management expects roughly $460 million in revenue and $126 million in adjusted EBITDA.

Pursuit Attractions and Hospitality, the Denver-based, NYSE-listed operator spun out of Viad at the end of 2024, is one of the few public companies that shows what experiential attractions actually earn. It runs the Banff Gondola, the Maligne Lake cruise, Sky Lagoon in Iceland, and lodges in several of the same markets, with Canada accounting for 54% of 2025 revenue. In the first nine months of 2026, visits to comparable attractions fell 3%, which the company attributed mainly to weather in Canada, but ticket revenue per visitor rose 7.5%, lifting ticket revenue 4.2%. Lodging is close to a second engine: in the peak third quarter, attractions brought in $131 million and hospitality $112 million, with comparable properties 92.3% full at $334 a night. Pursuit expects about $460 million in revenue and $126 million in adjusted EBITDA in 2026 excluding Flyover, the flying-theater business it sold in July. The cost side is where the test sits. Pursuit plans $103 million to $114 million in capital spending in 2026, operating cash after capital spending fell to $23 million through September from $56 million a year earlier, and a separate $300 million growth plan is expected to add more than $40 million in annual adjusted EBITDA by 2030. Safety obligations are also a cost: a fatality inquiry report released in October 2026 into the 2020 Columbia Icefield rollover identified brake failure and inadequate inspection, and Pursuit said it accepted all four recommendations.
