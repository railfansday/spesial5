Sloppy Joe’s with Ranch Potatoes

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Easy, saucy, delicious Sloppy Joe’s! My kids’ favorite dinner. Super simple to make with just a few ingredients.

Published by Lindsay Ostrom on October 28, 2026

Here’s the thing – it’s humble, it’s non-fancy, but my husband and my kids are so happy when I make this for dinner.

I’ve been on a JOURNEY with these. I tried making Sloppy Joe’s several ways this summer, since my daughter kept asking for them. But with every batch I made, she always kindly let me know that they were still “not as good as the ones at school.”

Lucky for me (and all of us), I know several of the wonderful people who do the food service at their school. I was able to get some school recipe intel, which is how I ended up with this very simple “recipe”:

It’s rich, and meaty, and easy, and just so very family-friendly.

Bjork and I like ours topped with pickled jalapenos or crispy onions, and the girls, of course, like them “plain” on a squishy white bun.

I always always make these with what we call Ranch Potatoes, which are the truly the delight of my life. In our house, it’s not Sloppy Joe’s if there’s no pile of crispy, buttery Ranch Potatoes on the side. 🙂

Regarding diced onion, pepper, or garlic in the Sloppy Joe filling: this recipe has none. Everyone I cook for enjoys these the same (or even more?) with an uncluttered texture and just a few hits of garlic and onion powder instead, which makes this whole thing even faster and easier.

My youngest requested this as her birthday meal this year. Hope you like it as much as we do!

Leave it sitting undisturbed for a few minutes so that it develops a nice golden crust.

Garlic powder, onion powder, beef bouillon, ketchup, and BBQ sauce!

Add some water as needed to make the mixture loosen up and turn a bit glossy. It can just hang out until you’re ready to eat.

When you’re close to eating, pop your potatoes in the oven.

Once they’re roasty toasty, add a knob of butter and some ranch seasoning. Toss to combine. (If you don’t have ranch seasoning, just use melted butter, fresh garlic, and dill. UNREAL.)

Load it onto a soft bun – we love this with ranch potatoes and green beans!

The meat mixture is ready to go right after you add the ketchup and BBQ sauce, but I usually like to let it simmer for 30-60 minutes to really caramelize around the edges and deepen in both flavor and color. That’s why I do the potatoes after the meat!
