Well, It Sure Seems Like Mitigating Climate Change Is No Longer a Concern for Democrats

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Conservative Republicans and their oligarchical allies are getting everything they want out of a bipartisan bill that could walk back important environmental laws.

Last Thursday, and on a bipartisan basis, the Senate approved the so-called Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA), an alleged effort to “reform” the federal permitting process for things like energy projects, hard-rock mining, and, yes, data centers. At the very least, the bill lived up to one part of its clumsy title; its passage through the Senate truly was bipartisan. It passed by a 62–33 vote, and liberal Democratic senator Sheldon Whitehouse of Rhode Island was one of its strongest advocates.

It’s also a very big apple with more than a few razor blades concealed inside. Environmentalists have been giving BAAJA the side-eye for a number of reasons. Out in Nebraska, Democratic state chair Jane Fleming Kleeb suspects the bill is connected to a sudden revival of interest in restarting our old friend, the Keystone XL pipeline, the continent-spanning death funnel meant to bring the world’s dirtiest fossil fuel from Alberta ultimately to refineries around the world.

My problem with this whole thing is that this “kind of civil war” is taking place exclusively on the Left. Conservative Republicans, and their oligarchical allies, are forcing the action before the new Democratic majorities take over in January. They know exactly what they want out of the bill and what they don’t want in it. The president already has said there won’t be any wind farms under the provisions of this bill on his watch. (The birds! The whales!) The bill now is with the House of Representatives. May I suggest a discreet dragging of the feet? It is the holiday season, after all.
