1. How did right-wing presidential candidate Flávio Bolsonaro react to incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva’s runoff election victory on Sunday?

He called for his followers to protest his loss in Brasília.

He said he would request an annulment of the election.

Lula became the first Brazilian presidential candidate in history to come from behind to win the election’s second round, FP’s Catherine Osborn reports. Bolsonaro, the subject of several criminal probes, subsequently fled to Miami, where he called himself “the leader of Brazil.”

2. Student-led protests in Serbia that began in November 2024 culminated in the defeat of longtime right-wing leader Aleksandar Vucic in elections on Sunday. What incident sparked the original demonstrations?

Vucic’s decision to send military equipment to Russia

The collapse of a concrete railway station canopy

A factory spill that polluted the Danube through Belgrade

The revelation of a mass domestic surveillance network

The student protesters’ improbable victory against the Vucic regime could be instructive for their counterparts in the United States and European Union, Edward P. Joseph writes.

3. The Chinese Communist Party’s fifth plenum began on Monday. Traditionally focused on economic policy, what is the priority for this year’s gathering?

The shift in focus is a sign that Chinese President Xi Jinping is worried that his anti-corruption campaigns aren’t paying off, FP’s James Palmer writes in China Brief.

4. Early results show no clear winner from Israel’s parliamentary elections on Tuesday. But what is clear is that voter turnout was at its highest this century. About what percent of Israel’s registered voters turned out to cast a ballot on Tuesday?

Even if the opposition bloc succeeds in forming a governing coalition, there is little indication that Israel’s policies toward Palestinians will dramatically change, FP columnist Steven A. Cook writes.

5. At a Texas rally on Tuesday, U.S. President Donald Trump floated adding a 51st state. What territory or country did he suggest?

Trump’s “America First” foreign-policy agenda has been wildly unpopular with voters, as recent polling indicates, Jonathan Monten, Jordan Tama, and Josh Busby write.

6. The third Russia-Africa Summit began on Wednesday in Moscow. Russian President Vladimir Putin asserted that Moscow would champion greater African involvement where?

In the continent’s own critical minerals industry

At the World Bank and International Monetary Fund

African leaders’ attendance has dipped since its high point in 2019, partially driven by Moscow’s unfulfilled promises on the continent and the forced recruitment of Africans into Russia’s military ranks to fight in Ukraine, FP’s Nosmot Gbadamosi reports in Africa Brief.

7. Why were three French nationals sentenced to life in prison in Iraq on Thursday?

For spray-painting protest graffiti on Iraq’s parliament building

For smuggling weapons to the Popular Mobilization Forces

The arrests highlight how conflicts and divisions that may feel distant to Washington remain alive and pressing in the Middle East. Earlier this month, Hekmat Aboukhater wrote about how the Trump administration is inflaming sectarian divisions in Iraq.

8. Ireland said on Friday that it will set up a government body that, for the first time, would research and prepare for the possibility of what?

Sending troops to defend Palestinians in the West Bank

Unifying with the U.K.-run Northern Ireland

The announcement comes as Scotland and Wales also push for greater autonomy from London, which has reeled from years of dysfunction, Jamie Maxwell wrote in May.

9. Contestants gathered in Belgium on Monday to participate in a competition to see who could craft the most impressive sculpture made from what material?

Among the chocolate creations were birds, an elf, and a praying mantis, as photographed by The Associated Press.

10. An Australian court ruled on Monday against an inmate’s claim that his inability to have this product violated his right to “enjoy his culture as an Australian.” What product did the inmate sue to access?

The court found the ban on Vegemite, a yeast-based spread, was justified because of the product’s potential to impair drug-sniffing dogs’ ability to detect narcotics as well as its possible use in brewing alcohol, The Associated Press reports.
