Delta CEO: “Customers Are Not Choosing Airlines Based On Wi-Fi”

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

I’ve gotta be honest, I don’t envy the situation that Delta CEO Ed Bastian is in. Unlike most other carriers in the United States, Delta hasn’t opted for SpaceX Starlink Wi-Fi, and has instead gone with Amazon Leo.

In theory that’s fine, except Amazon Leo is years behind Starlink, and just faced a new setback, due to a rocket failure. Elon Musk has also repeatedly publicly ridiculed Bastian, regarding what Bastian reportedly said about Musk to employees.

There’s now an interesting update, as Bastian made some comments about the carrier’s Wi-Fi choice, and overall customer preferences. It sure is amazing how narratives can change, based on what’s convenient…

This week at the Fortune 500 Innovation Forum 2026, Delta’s CEO was asked about the carrier’s decision not to choose Starlink, plus the public spat with Musk. Here’s what Bastian had to say about this:

“We’ve been the leader in Wi-Fi, and in free and fast Wi-Fi, and we’ve been at it for five years. I’ve got a long history with Elon on that. We’ve looked at Starlink, we actually helped them started in aviation, we gave them our planes to use during COVID, when we weren’t flying, and they tested the system out. We just couldn’t come to agreement on a business model.”

“We’ve been the leader in free, fast Wi-Fi. We are confident there will be more than one LEO product in the world other than Starlink, and I’m confident Amazon has the capability to do a great job. Customers are not choosing airlines based on Wi-Fi. We’ve been out with a great product from Viasat, which we’ve got two new satellites in the sky. It’s not Starlink caliber but it’s pretty darn close.”

Look, before I get too deep into this, let me make several points that I think no one reasonable will disagree with (which is not to say some commenters won’t disagree!):

When Bastian formally unveiled plans for free Wi-Fi at CES 2023 in Las Vegas, he said the following:

“At work, at home and everywhere in between, connectivity is essential to daily life, and your journey on Delta should be no different. Our vision has long been to deliver an experience at 30,000 feet that feels similar to what our customers have available on the ground.”

“We didn’t just want free Wi-Fi to offer base-level service – we wanted it to be transformative for the entire onboard experience. It is imperative all customers onboard can enjoy their favorite content just as they would at home, and we’ve put this system through meticulous tests to make that possible.”

Earlier this year, when announcing plans to partner with Amazon Leo for connectivity, Bastian said that “we need to stay the leader” in inflight connectivity. Obviously that won’t be the case for a long time.

As I see it, Delta’s problem here is pretty simple. Customers absolutely notice a difference between Viasat and Starlink. The issue isn’t that people will stop flying Delta altogether. The issue is that Delta has built its entire brand on being the industry leader, and on being on the forefront of technology.

There’s no denying that this sentiment among the public changes over time, because inflight Wi-Fi is easily one of the top amenities that passengers notice nowadays. Seriously, I can’t count the number of people I’ve heard from or just overheard talking about their first Starlink experience, and how blown away they were.

I guess to look at it from another angle, there’s no airline that loves talking about how premium it is more than Delta. What makes Delta premium? If we’re going to believe that Delta is premium, it’s not based on any one single thing, but rather, it’s based on the sum of the experience. And I think anyone who thinks a major Wi-Fi disadvantage lasting for years isn’t part of what contributes to consumer sentiment is lying to themselves.

Delta CEO Ed Bastian is now downplaying the importance of inflight Wi-Fi. He’s arguing that “customers are not choosing airlines based on Wi-Fi,” which in isolation, is probably true. But he’s also arguing that Viasat is “pretty darn close” to Starlink, which is most definitely not true, as I see it.

I wouldn’t be so critical of these statements if it weren’t for Bastian spending years hyping up how it’s “imperative all customers onboard can enjoy their favorite content just as they would at home,” while also saying “we need to stay the leader.” And for that matter, Delta has of course also long hyped how premium it is — if that’s to be believed, it’s not about any one feature, but instead, it’s about the sum of the experience.
