Five years after the Glasgow Declaration, tourism has plenty of climate commitments but little shared proof of what they’ve achieved. The industry is still split on whether better measurement or faster action should come first.

COP31, the world’s annual marathon of climate talks, kicks off next week. After years of planning climate action, tourism operators told Skift that they now want to see the receipts.

The 31st United Nations Climate Change Conference will take place in Antalya, Türkiye, from November 9 to 20. Co-hosted with Australia, this fresh round of climate talks is being billed the implementation COP, after parties ultimately failed to agree on a path forward last year in Belém, Brazil.

The Brazilian COP30 presidency promised to publish plans for moving away from fossil fuels and protecting forests before the next round of talks.

The voluntary roadmaps, released Tuesday, are designed to help policymakers implement their existing climate commitments through national policies, investments and measurable results — and should indicate some of the focus of the forthcoming talks.

COP31 opens in Antalya, Türkiye, from November 9 to 20, co-hosted with Australia and billed as the "implementation COP" after parties failed to agree on a path forward at COP30 in Belém. Tourism gets a thematic slot on November 18 for the third year running, this time inside a day on human and social development, and the sustainability executives Skift spoke with said they want evidence of change rather than new pledges. Radisson Hotel Group's Sven Wiltink pointed to inconsistent measurement of emissions, water, energy, and waste, arguing that without common metrics "comparisons are meaningless and claims remain unverifiable." Intrepid's Susanne Etti said the sector needs electrification and falling emissions, not more commitments, targets, or frameworks, and that resilience, adaptation, and loss and damage funding have become more urgent after a year of extreme heat, wildfires, and flooding. Booking Holdings' Danielle D'Silva said the industry can do more on both adaptation and emissions reductions. COP31 also marks five years since the Glasgow Declaration on Climate Action in Tourism, which has drawn more than 900 signatories; revised operational guidelines will be released at the conference, with co-author Jeremy Smith saying the aim is to lower the measurement and reporting burden and bring in companies that stayed out.
