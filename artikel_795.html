Eckert: Time to 'close the chapter' on Spygate ahead of Boro reunion

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Tonda Eckert speaks to Sky Sports News reporter Mark McAdam about 'Spygate' fallout, their points deduction and reunion with Middlesbrough. Watch Southampton vs Middlesbrough live on Sky Sports Football from 7.30pm on Friday night; kick-off 8pm.

"I think we've experienced a little bit more in the last 12 months than normal."

It may be the understatement of the century, but it's not a huge surprise considering Tonda Eckert is a relatively understated man.

The storm of 'Spygate' is over, but the aftershock is still being felt. Southampton are sixth in the table, seven points behind leaders Middlesbrough ahead of their reunion on Friday night - live on Sky Sports.

Were it not for the four-point deduction, the gap would be smaller. Were it not for the entire controversy, the Saints would possibly be in the Premier League right now.

Eckert knows he broke the rules, and is thankful for the club and the support-base for backing him. Something that wouldn't have been easy after the ignominy of being kicked out of the play-offs.

"There have been some points, especially over the summer, where I think the club has made a commitment that not many clubs would have made," he admits.

"I'm very grateful, first of all, for the opportunity to step up in the first place, but also for the support over the summer. Obviously, that goes from everyone in the building to our supporters.

"This summer has been the biggest test we can possibly have in terms of the relationship between a football club and a fan base, and a manager and a fan base. I think the supporters have been great. It's been a tough period for them.

"Towards the end of last season, it was difficult to process for everyone because of the process we used. I would have wanted to address the supporters a little earlier, so it took almost two weeks before I got to speak to them.

"From there on, I think the commitment has been very clear. It started with the football club and some players who committed to the project.

"You could just sense, the closer we got to the season, that everyone wanted to leave that behind and show what we are about as a football club and how much joy we bring when we go on the pitch. We felt that throughout the summer and on the first day of training.

"To come out of that situation with new strength just shows what this football club is about. Now it's for us to look forward and make sure that we continue to do that.

"There was still some uncertainty for the first block of games, and you just need to find a way, for me and obviously for everyone else in the football club, to put that to the side. As soon as the season starts, you need to perform.

"Then, obviously, to close that chapter finally, we needed a decision from the club. I think that's helped us to close that chapter."

The uncertainty came from the FA investigation, which wasn't resolved until October. In the end he was given a fine and a suspended six-week ban.

"Obviously, it's never easy to work on a daily basis if you have some uncertainty, and that's not just for me," he says. "I think it's the same for everyone at the football club.

"But I think we found a way, even in the first block of games, to perform. There's always something going on in football, sometimes a little bit bigger and sometimes not as big, but you need to push that to the side and make sure you find a way to perform.

"Once the decision came, I think it was important for everyone that we could finally close that."

For Eckert and Southampton it is just about points on the board, now. Trying to make up the deficit they were dealt at the start of the season.

"Obviously, if you could choose, no one would choose to start the season on minus four points," he says. "That's what it is. I think we are strong enough to make our way forward in the table.

"You shouldn't underestimate us coming from behind. We've shown that we are able to chase teams down, and that's the job for this year.

"It's been important for us to get some points on the board early in the season. We are still not where we want to be towards the end, so we need to make sure we continue."

Last week, Southampton handed Eckert, still just 33, a contract extension until 2029. They clearly feel he is their man to get them back to where they want to be.

"I think we've always been very open with our ambitions," he says. "There's no point in hiding that.

"I think the conditions are there to go up. It's never easy, but it's for us now to finish the job."
