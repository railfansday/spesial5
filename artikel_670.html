Bassam Tariq Says Marvel’s Scrapped ‘Blade’ Reboot Taught Him “Life’s Too Short to Make Sh** Films”

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

"It was funny and strange to start casting and building without a proper script... That's also very dangerous because if the text comes and it doesn't add up to what you were saying, your credibility gets completely shot," the director said in a new interview.

Bassam Tariq is opening up about what he learned working on Marvel‘s scrapped Blade reboot.

In an interview with The New Arab published Friday, the Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother director was asked what his time working in the “studio system” taught him about how he wants to approach filmmaking. Tariq said it felt “dangerous” to begin casting without “a proper script,” and that he prefers to “have full control over my script.”

“It was funny and strange to start casting and building without a proper script. You’re sitting there and explaining to actors what their role will be, and they just have to believe you,” he said. “That’s also very dangerous because if the text comes and it doesn’t add up to what you were saying, your credibility gets completely shot. All I have is my integrity, and I’ve been thinking about that a lot.” Related Stories Hollywood Flashback When Kevin Feige Got Off to a Less-Than-Incredible Start at Marvel TV 'VisionQuest' Creator Terry Matalas Tells All About That Supersized Finale and Post-Credits Surprise

In 2021, it was reported that Tariq was attached to helm Marvel’s Blade film; Mahershala Ali was announced as the project’s star seven years ago at Comic-Con 2019. The actor confirmed in a July interview with GQ that he would not be leading Blade.

The Hollywood Reporter exclusively reported in September 2022 that Tariq had exited the project, with the director saying in a statement at the time, “It’s been an honor working with the wonderful folks at Marvel. We were able to put together a killer cast and crew. Eager to see where the next director takes the film.”

Tariq continued in his interview with The New Arab, “If you’re the bearer of bad news to your actors, but you’re not the one making any of these decisions — and it’s actually coming from a corporate entity — that’s not a great way to be a director. I know some people can take that and run with it, but I learned I was losing credibility with my actors, and it wasn’t a healthy way to operate as a filmmaker.”

In short, the director said his “big lesson” was that he wanted to have full control of the scripts of his films, noting “life’s too short to make shit films.” Tariq added, “If we disagree, it’s between them and me. It’s quite sad because we had such high hopes for what we wanted to build.”

Ali said in a September interview with GQ that he agrees the failed film is Kevin Feige’s “biggest failure.”

When asked why the film didn’t happen with the interviewer, the Oscar winner said, “That’s a Kevin Feige question.” He continued, “They had, as you said, a two-time Oscar-winning actor under contract and you got billions of dollars. That’s a question for them. I don’t mean anything personal to you, but I don’t know why it didn’t. To this day, I don’t know why it didn’t happen. I just know that it was — Kevin, he said it was the biggest failure of his career and I agree.”
