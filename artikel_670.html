Disney Sued for Allegedly Using Unlicensed Music in Social Media Ads: ‘Never Paid a Dollar’

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The new case is one of dozens brought against brands over music on Instagram and TikTok, but none have targeted a media giant like Disney.

The latest in a flood of lawsuits over music on social media is targeting the Walt Disney Co. — and claiming that the Hollywood giant should have known better.

In a case filed Tuesday (Nov. 24) in Detroit federal court, Zac Hiller and his LAA Music claim Disney illegally used three of the publishing company’s songs across seven social media videos even though the entertainment colossus “never asked for a license” and “never paid a dollar.”

“For a hundred years, The Walt Disney Company has taught the world one rule: if you want to use what someone else created, you ask permission and you pay,” Hiller’s attorneys write. “Disney enforces that rule against everyone else. This case is about Disney exempting itself from it.”

The Disney posts were broadcast to “audiences in the millions,” the lawsuit says, including a promotion for Hulu that garnered 481,700 likes and 17,500 shares. The lawsuit claims the posts were removed after complaints, but that taking them down is not enough: “Taking a video down after getting caught does not undo the infringement.”

The new case is the latest in an ongoing crackdown against commercial social media posts that feature copyrighted songs. Dozens of lawsuits have been filed, many by the major music companies, over such allegations over the past five years, and many more claims have been settled behind closed doors.

The problem is rooted in the fact that sites like Instagram, TikTok and Snapchat offer libraries of licensed songs that make it easy for users to add music to their posts. But those tracks are for personal use only; businesses or influencers must utilize a far more limited commercial library or get a synch license, just like any conventional advertisement on TV.

While many of the lawsuits have come from Universal Music, Sony Music or Warner Music, Tuesday’s case was filed by Hiller, an NFL agent and founder and CEO of the agency Loyalty Above All, which counts players like James Cook, J.K. Dobbins, and Shemar Stewart on its client roster.

The case claims that Disney used three songs owned by Hiller and LAA Music, including “Take it Off ‘Lights Off’ Beat”; “New Opp (beat)”; and “Gang Gang.” Three songs were allegedly used by ESPN, one by Hulu, one by FX and two more by Disney Parks.

“Disney negotiates synchronization licenses every day [and] employs people whose job is to clear music,” Hiller’s attorneys write. “Its infringement here was not the oversight of a company too large to notice. It was a business decision: use the music now, take the promotional benefit, and make the rights holder chase it later.”

Neither side immediately returned requests for comment on Tuesday.
