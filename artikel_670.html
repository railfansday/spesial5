Mahershala Ali Says It ‘Doesn’t Matter’ That He’s Won 2 Oscars Because ‘There Are Not Roles for Me’ in ‘the Vast Majority of Projects’: ‘You’re Like Everybody Else Waiting’

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Mahershala Ali got candid about the impact — or lack thereof — his two Oscar wins have had on his career in Hollywood while appearing on a recent episode of Carmelo Anthony and Kazeem “Kaz” Famuyide’s “7PM in Brooklyn” podcast.

The actor, who took home trophies for best supporting actor for both 2018’s “Greenbook” and 2016’s “Moonlight,” asked the hosts about the meaning of an Oscar, and if it really validates whether someone is “a good actor” or not. “There’s more good actors than there is good material,” he said. “It’s not even that all these people are competing for these same spots. It’s just that out of all the material made, there’s only a handful of them that check certain boxes.” Related Stories Mahershala Ali Says It ‘Doesn’t Matter’ That He’s Won 2 Oscars Because ‘There Are Not Roles for Me’ in ‘the Vast Majority of Projects’: ‘You’re Like Everybody Else Waiting’ Marvel’s ‘Blade’ Director Exited Failed Movie Because 'Life’s Too Short to Make S--- Films' and It Was 'Dangerous' Working 'Without a Proper Script'

He continued: “The vast majority of projects that get greenlit, there are not roles for me in those. You gotta think that way, you know what I mean? So, it doesn’t matter if I got 17 Oscars. If someone is not writing something for you or greenlighting something with you in mind, you’re like everybody else waiting and looking and searching.”

While the star noted that actors could produce their own projects, the timeline is usually as least a “three to five year” journey. When asked by Famuyide if an Oscar win validates Black actors, Ali responded: “It’s kind of just talk. It doesn’t translate to anything real. You don’t want to find yourself in a situation where, oh, that’s ‘the diversity project.’ You just want to do great material as a Black person. You’re not thinking of yourself as this exotic other, per se. You just are living your life.”

Most recently, Ali starred in Bassam Tariq’s “Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother,” which hit theaters on Sept. 25. Tariq was also the original director of Marvel’s failed “Blade” movie, in which Ali was set to star in the title role, which he recently discussed in an interview with The New Arab.

“It was funny and strange to start casting and building without a proper script,” Tariq told interviewer Hanna Flint about his short-lived Marvel career. “You’re sitting there and explaining to actors what their role will be, and they just have to believe you. That’s also very dangerous because if the text comes and it doesn’t add up to what you were saying, your credibility gets completely shot. All I have is my integrity, and I’ve been thinking about that a lot.”

“If you’re the bearer of bad news to your actors, but you’re not the one making any of these decisions — and it’s actually coming from a corporate entity — that’s not a great way to be a director,” he continued. “I know some people can take that and run with it, but I learned I was losing credibility with my actors, and it wasn’t a healthy way to operate as a filmmaker. Life’s too short to make shit films, so that was my big lesson: I have to have full control over my script. If we disagree, it’s between them and me. It’s quite sad because we had such high hopes for what we wanted to build.”

Speaking of the film’s inability to get made, Marvel Studios president Kevin Feige had previously said he felt “like a gigantic loser and failure,” a statement that Ali agreed with in a GQ interview in the months following. “To this day, I don’t know why it didn’t happen. I just know that it was—Kevin, he said it was the biggest failure of his career and I agree,” said Ali.
