Melania Trump Calls for ‘Peace’ When Asked About Children in Gaza and Israel at Amazon Series Premiere

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Amazon Prime Video’s “Melania: The Series” offers a peek into the often-reclusive first lady’s personal life and role inside the White House, including her efforts to provide children in foster care with educational and career opportunities.

But as the first lady seeks a more public-facing role in international peacemaking efforts, most recently taking credit for brokering a ceasefire in Ukraine, how often does she think about the children in Gaza, Israel and other parts of the world affected by conflict? When posed the question at the New York premiere of her new two-part Amazon series, the first lady told Variety: “I want peace all around the world. That’s very important to me. We should all live in harmony and peace.”

“I hope we can,” she added, before vaguely suggesting how that peace might be achieved: “To get together.”

The first lady’s call for peace comes at a pivotal moment in President Donald Trump’s presidency, one where he attempts to recast himself as a global peacemaker after a bruising midterm defeat. (In the wake of the “blue wave,” Vice President JD Vance identified “end[ing] the war” and “chang[ing] our relationship to Israel” as two of the “top four causes” he and his most trusted pollsters believe could win back Republican voters who flipped their votes.)

Efforts “to get together” were underway just hours before the Prime Video series premiere, as Presidents Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin convened for a historic trilateral summit in China. The meeting culminated in a joint declaration emphasizing peace, commitments to the United Nations and a reaffirmation that “a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought.”

While Trump celebrated the meeting as a historic breakthrough (fittingly rating his trip a “12” out of 10), some foreign policy analysts argued that the summit was more spectacle than substance and raised concerns over legitimizing Putin without securing concessions on Ukraine. The joint declaration also did not directly address the ongoing conflicts in Gaza or Iran.

The two-part docuseries is the second project released under Amazon’s $40 million licensing agreement for the Brett Ratner-directed “Melania” documentary, which debuted in January. Producer Marc Beckman oversaw the bidding war for what he calls “the biggest documentary in history.” (While the film exceeded box office expectations, its $16.6 million worldwide gross falls far short of the all-time documentary record. To put it in perspective, Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s “You Can See Everything” earned more in its second weekend playing in U.S. theaters alone than “Melania” did during its entire worldwide run.)

“I went to multiple entities, from Amazon to Netflix to Hulu to Paramount, and all of them were interested in picking up the film,” Beckman said. “I can tell you point-blank, Amazon’s team was honestly the best in Hollywood. They understood how important it was to get this story out. It’s a piece of history. It’s never been told before, and we were really impressed with the leadership at Amazon. Really great people.”

Did Jeff Bezos participate? “No, he never was involved,” Beckman claimed. “He wasn’t involved in the negotiation, and he wasn’t involved in anything else.” (Democratic lawmakers, including Sen. Elizabeth Warren, have accused Bezos’ company of “bribery in plain sight,” alleging that Amazon vastly overpaid for the rights and outbid its rivals by a wide margin to curry favor with the Trump administration. Bezos defended the purchase as “a very wise business decision.”

“Melania: The Series” is now streaming on Prime Video.
