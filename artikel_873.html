Thomas Frank ‘close to a heart attack’ in dramatic first Crystal Palace win

Thomas Frank secured his first win as Crystal Palace boss but admitted his new side nearly nearly gave him a “heart attack” in the nervy final five minutes of their 2-1 Europa League comeback against 10-man Real Sociedad.

Chelsea loanee Mamadou Sarr broke the deadlock for the visitors in the 66th minute at Selhurst Park but Frank’s first home contest turned on its head when Jon Martin received a second yellow card six minutes later.

Jean-Philippe Mateta drew the sides level from the penalty spot and Dwight McNeil poked Palace into the lead from fellow substitute Jorgen Strand Larsen’s bicycle kick, but they were denied a chance to put the game away when Yeremy Pino’s penalty was saved.

“I think first and foremost to get it over the line, and (it was) an extremely important win for various reasons,” said Frank, who took charge of Premier League basement club Palace for the first time in Saturday’s league loss to Coventry.

“One, of course, confidence, and it relates to belief against an extremely good team. I think the first half we were compact and very disciplined in many ways. We missed the high-pressure situations we had, and I think we struggled to keep the ball well enough.

“Second half I felt we were much better in the high pressure, and I felt we kept the ball much better. I actually felt we got a bit more momentum in the second half but then, out of the blue, they scored the 1-0 goal.

“But what I really like is the belief from the players, what they showed, and that we are threatening on set-pieces, we cannot underestimate that.”

Unai Marrero’s save to deny Pino from the spot kept the Spanish side in with a chance of an equaliser and it nearly came at the death from Orri Oskarsson, whose effort was pushed away by Dean Henderson – his last of six saves on Thursday night.

“We know in football one moment can change the game,” Frank continued. “The penalty and the red card changed the game, then we still needed to score.

“To win still is important and we went brave, we went with the offensive substitutions and kept two strikers on the pitch, and that paid off.

“And then I think we were all close to getting a heart attack in the last five minutes.”

Frank agreed the win could be a significant turning point for Palace’s fortunes ahead of a critical Premier League clash with Hull on Sunday.

“That’s why it’s so important to keep doing the right things, keep believing, and keep going,” he said.

Palace’s Premier League survival remains the priority but the result also propelled the Eagles into play-off position in their European campaign, four places out of the top eight after five matches.

Asked about his ambitions for the European campaign – given Palace’s league position – Frank replied: “I think we want to aim high.

“We are ambitious for this tournament, and that’s why the win was so important.”
