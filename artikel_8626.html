Deciding Between A Dream House And Millions In Anthropic Stock

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

With Anthropic going public at a $1.5 trillion valuation, a lot of people in San Francisco are about to face a decision they have never faced before. Further, they're experiencing FOMO or regret in not investing more in Anthropic earlier.

So let's run a fun thought experiment for an “everyday millionaire” in San Francisco. You get to pick one, and only one:

Option 1: A $7.5 million, 4,500 square foot, fully remodeled 5 bedroom / 5 bathroom single-family home on a large lot with views in San Francisco. Free and clear. Move in tomorrow.

Option 2: $8.5 million of Anthropic stock at whatever it prices at, with a six-month lockup.

My first goal is to set the line like a bookie would, splitting readers as close to 50/50 as I can.

My second goal is to get you thinking about what the money is actually for. Follow the chain backwards. You take the job for the equity and hope there's huge upside. You want the windfall to buy a house. And you want the house because that’s where your family lives.

Option 1 hands you the end of that chain today. No lockup, no tax bill, no waiting. That is the whole debate.

You probably noticed Option 2 is worth more on paper. That is not a thumb on the scale. It is the tax code.

A house and a share of stock are not the same currency. One is an after-tax asset that throws off shelter every single day. The other is a pre-tax asset that throws off nothing at all.

To convert Anthropic stock into a $7.5 million house in California, you need $11.92 million of stock at long-term capital gains rates. That is 20% federal, plus the 3.8% net investment income tax, plus 13.3% to California. A 37.1% haircut.

And that is the friendly version. If your shares are RSUs that vest at the IPO, they get taxed as ordinary income. Now you need $15.84 million of stock to buy the same house.

Quick note for early employees: qualified small business stock could wipe out a chunk of that federal bill. But Anthropic blew past the $50 million gross assets test years ago, so this only helps a very small number of people who got shares in 2021.

So a $7.5 million house is worth roughly $11.9 million of Anthropic stock today. I set the line at $8.5 million because the stock also gets four years to grow, and the house does not grow nearly as fast.

I have a long-standing belief that equity in a company is a type of funny money. The value is based on growth and profit assumptions years into the future. A stock provides no utility. Its value can tank overnight, like we've seen with NVIDIA and many tech stocks recently. You can look at it on a screen, but that's about it.

Only when you sell it and buy something does it become real. And the moment you sell it, the government takes a third or more.

A house is different. A house pays you every single day in the form of shelter you do not have to rent. Your goal in getting rich from stock is to buy the dream house, not the other way around.

Owning that $7.5 million home outright costs $117,750 a year, and property taxes are almost the whole story at $87,750. Utilities, gardening, and services run $15,000. Maintenance on a freshly remodeled house is about $10,000. Insurance is $5,000. So “free and clear” still costs you $9,812 a month before you buy a single grocery.

But renting the same house in San Francisco costs about $25,000 a month, a 4% gross yield. So owning it beats renting it by $182,250 a year, and that gap grows. Over the six-month lockup alone, that is $91,125 handed to a landlord while you wait for permission to sell.

Proposition 13. Your assessment is locked at your purchase price and can only rise 2% a year. If the house doubles, your tax bill does not. Meanwhile your stock's tax bill grows right alongside the gain.

The $500,000 exclusion. A married couple shields half a million of gain when they sell a primary residence. Anthropic stock gets zero exclusion.

You can borrow against it without triggering a taxable event. Try getting a line of credit against locked-up pre-IPO shares.

And there is the part that does not show up in a spreadsheet. Your kids get bedrooms. You might get a rare enclosed front yard, or the sunset ocean view at 7pm. You cannot raise a family inside a brokerage account.

Now let me argue against myself, because the bull case is not stupid.

Anthropic's 2026 annualized revenue run rate is about $80 billion, up from roughly $47 billion in May and about $9 billion at the end of 2025. The company also reported its first positive adjusted operating income, reversing a loss of about $5.6 billion in 2024.

Here is my pro forma, and I am deliberately staying inside the company's own guidance. I have the run rate at $90 billion by December 2026, $150 billion by December 2027, and $200 billion by December 2028. Growth decelerates from 67% to 33%, which is still extraordinary for a business of that size.

If those numbers land, which I think they will because companies sandbag their first guidance, the multiple does the work for you. Whatever Anthropic prices at, that number gets cut roughly in half by the end of 2027 and in half again by the end of 2028. The company simply grows into the price.

A house is not going to do that. A house is going to sit there and appreciate at optimistically 10% per annum, but probably closer to 5%, while asking you for a new roof.

Say you take the $8.5 million in Anthropic stock, ride out the lockup, and plan to buy your dream house with an enclosed front yard in four years.

The house is not waiting for you. At 5% appreciation, $7.5 million becomes $9.1 million. And while you wait, you are renting at $25,000 a month, which burns about $1.27 million over four years.

So the stock has to produce $10.4 million after tax just to get you back to even. Pre-tax, that means your $8.5 million stock has to become $16.5 million. The extra $6.1 million goes to the government.

Anthropic has to appreciate 1.9 times, from wherever it prices, within four years. Call it a double. That is an 18.1% compound annual return.

If it prices at $1.4 trillion, you need $2.7 trillion. If it prices at $1.5 trillion, you need $2.9 trillion. The dollar figure moves. The requirement does not. The house costs what it costs, rent costs what it costs, and California takes what it takes. None of that cares what Goldman and Morgan Stanley settle on tonight.

A 50% gain is not enough. Up 50% leaves you with $8 million after tax, more than a million short of the asking price alone, and $2.4 million short once you count the rent you burned getting there.

If growth keeps decelerating, from 33% in 2028 to 25% in 2029 to 20% in 2030, the run rate reaches roughly $300 billion by the time the four years are up.

So the real question is what multiple of that $300 billion the market has to pay for you to break even.

Mature software companies trade at 6 to 10 times sales. Run the math across the range and there is a clean dividing line at about $1.54 trillion.

Price below that, and break-even only requires an ordinary multiple. At $1.4 trillion you need the market to pay 9.1 times sales in 2030. At $1.5 trillion, 9.7 times. Both sit inside the normal band. The stock wins even if Anthropic gets re-rated down to a perfectly boring software company.

Price above $1.54 trillion, and break-even requires a premium multiple that still has to be there four years from now. That is a different bet entirely, because it depends on sentiment rather than execution.

For perspective, back in September, when Anthropic was floating a $2 trillion valuation, break-even required 13 times sales.

SpaceX went public in June at around $1.75 trillion. It surged past $2 trillion, then fell back. It has been a volatile stock out of the gate, and Anthropic may trade the same way.

That is the thing about a six-month lockup. You do not get to sell into the euphoria. You get to watch it, and then you get to find out what is left when everybody else's lockup expires on the same day as yours.

The other half of this trade is that San Francisco real estate is in a melt-up, driven by the exact wealth we are discussing.

Between January and late June 2026, 136 single-family homes closed at or above $5 million, more than double the 67 transactions in the same period a year earlier. In June alone, 44 homes sold for at least $1 million over asking. At the very top, even all-cash offers of $25 million and up have been outbid.

This remodeled Victorian on Castro St. sold in September for $7,650,000 against a $5,495,000 list. That is 39% over asking, $2.15 million above what the sellers hoped for, for 3,416 square feet on a 2,622 square foot lot. Smaller than the house in our scenario. This is what the money actually buys here.

A 4,500 square foot remodeled home on a large lot with views is a unicorn property. Homes with views usually don’t have large useable lots and vice versa. Maybe 5-8 trade in a given year. If you wait four years to buy it with your stock proceeds, you are not bidding against today's buyers. You are bidding against every other newly liquid Anthropic and OpenAI employee who made the same plan you did.

Instead of the $7.5 million house being worth $9.1 million in four years, it could be worth well north of $10 million.

That is the trap. Everybody's exit strategy is the same few houses.

Some of you are already typing that the answer is to take the stock and move somewhere a mansion costs $2 million. Two problems.

The job that created the windfall is in San Francisco, so leaving means leaving the equity behind. And California taxes equity compensation based on where you worked while it vested, so moving after the fact does not get you out of the bill you are trying to avoid.

Further, would you really walk away from a job like that when there are so many more millions to make? Only if you’re a FIRE person who values time and freedom more than money.

As for the mansion itself, I toured a bunch of them in Honolulu last summer and came away thinking buying a mansion in paradise might be backwards. Then again, having a mansion in a beautiful city like San Francisco is a pretty amazing combination.

Real wealth is not the number. Real wealth is the gap between what you own and what you can actually enjoy.

You can spend the next four years watching a ticker and eating at home to preserve optionality, and end up with $10.4 million and four years of your kids' childhoods spent in a sub-optional rental you did not love. Or you can spend those four years in the dream house, providing for your family and making memories, and end up with $9.1 million or more of home equity plus four years you actually lived.

That said, I believe Anthropic could reach $3 trillion in market cap by the end of 2027 and $5 trillion by the end of 2028. Against anything in tonight's IPO price range, that is a healthy amount of upside. If I am right, the stock wins this debate within about thirteen months and it is not even a contest.

I drew the fair line myself, and I am still leaning toward the house. Watching names like Google and Nvidia drop 30% to 40% in a few months is a reminder of how fickle stocks are. And not having to fight a bidding war over a unicorn property would save my wife and me a lot of heartbreak.

At 49 with two young kids, time is too precious to put my life on hold for money that won't be used. I might end up with far more money holding Anthropic stock. But by the time I sell and pay the capital gains, I doubt I can lock down the dream house at a reasonable price.

Because if I have $20 million in Anthropic stock in four years, so do thousands of other employees and investors. Bidding against one buyer is hard enough. Bidding against dozens for the same unicorn property is impossible to win. And what good is money you cannot convert into the thing you wanted it for?

So I take the bird in hand. I have watched enough people optimize their way through a decade and arrive at the finish line wealthy and vaguely disappointed. Money is a means to an end. And one of those ends is living in the nicest house you can comfortably afford.
