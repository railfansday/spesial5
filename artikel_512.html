Hands-on: Xbox launches lightweight Nano Controller, and its just right for me

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Xbox has launched its new Nano Controller, designed for easy portability and smaller hands. Even so, gamers with larger mitts may still find this lightweight controller worth picking up.

Announced last month, the Xbox Nano Controller offers the functionality of a traditional Xbox Wireless Controller in a much smaller package. Measuring 133mm x 66.5mm x 36.6mm, it's a little more than half the height and notably slimmer than its older sibling, as well as roughly a third the weight at 101g.

I got to try the new Xbox controller ahead of its launch, and I've had a good experience so far.

The Nano Controller's small rectangular form factor doesn't feel cramped, as those familiar with the Wireless Controller might expect. While the Nano doesn't have its sibling's large ergonomic handles, its smaller substitutes and remarkably lightweight build still make it easy to hold.

Forgoing larger handles also helps keep the Nano's design small enough to comfortably fit in a jacket pocket or handbag, making it more likely to be used with portable devices. Like the Xbox Wireless Controller, the Nano Controller can connect to Xbox consoles, Windows PCs, tablets, compatible smart TVs, and iOS or Android devices.

In addition to this increased portability, the Nano Controller's reduced size and lightweight build specifically targets people with smaller hands, making it easier for children and petite adults to use. The Nano's design lets players' fingers travel a shorter distance when switching between buttons, while its lightweight design should help them play longer without getting fatigued.

According to a 2000 study by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) (via Wayback Machine), the average hand length of a 40-year-old American man is about 7.6 inches (19.3cm), measured from the tip of the middle finger to just below the crease of the wrist. The average breadth is 3.5 inches (8.9 cm), measured from the exterior edge of the pinky to the exterior edge of the index finger. In comparison, the same measurements for an average 40-year-old Japanese woman are 6.8 inches (17.2 cm) and 3.1 inches (7.8 cm).

My own hand measurements are roughly 6.7 inches (17cm) and 2.6 inches (6.5cm), so slightly smaller than the average Japanese woman. Apparently, this puts my hand size on par with that of a large-handed American 11-year-old, according to a study done by the U.S. Department of Health, Education, and Welfare in the 60s. As such, I'm pleased to report that someone with similarly sized hands should have little trouble holding and operating the Xbox Nano Controller, which fits comfortably in my hands and feels delightfully lightweight.

Yet despite it being targeted toward smaller hands, large-handed individuals and average-sized adults may enjoy the Nano as well. My dad's hand measurements are close to those of the average American man, but he preferred the Xbox Nano Controller over the Wireless Controller on first use, citing its more closely spaced buttons and, in particular, its significantly lighter weight.

The Nano Controller's diminutive size is by far its most prominent selling point. Still, it also has another notable advantage over the Xbox Wireless Controller: an internal rechargeable battery.

The Xbox Wireless Controller uses two AA batteries, which can power it for up to 40 hours. If you'd prefer the ease of plugging it in to recharge, you'll have to buy an Xbox Rechargeable Battery for $24.99, which provides up to 30 hours of playtime per charge.

In contrast, the Nano Controller has an internal battery that can be recharged via a USB-C port on its top (though a cable doesn't come included). Microsoft hasn't stated exactly how long the Nano Controller's battery lasts. Still, even if its battery life is shorter than the Wireless Controller's, the ease with which the Nano Controller can be charged should help make up for it.

The Nano Controller launched today, and it's more affordable than the full-sized Wireless Controller, setting players back $49.99 as opposed to $64.99. Factoring in the Wireless Controller's $24.99 Rechargeable Battery accessory, that's around $40 less for similar functionality.

If you already have a standard Xbox Wireless Controller and are happy with it, there's no need to rush out and pick up a Nano right away. However, if you have small kids or small hands, like to game on the go, or would just like a more lightweight option, then the Xbox Nano Controller seems like a useful alternative to have in your pocket.

The Xbox Nano Controller is available now at the Xbox online store for $49.99, or via Amazon for $49.
