On-chain metrics from reputated dashboards like CryptoQuant, CoinGlass, and even TradingView reveal a clear divergence in Bitcoin holder behavior during the November 2026 market correction.

Following a failed late-September rally to $87,153, Bitcoin dropped to the $65,800–$66,083 support zone. While short-term traders and spot ETFs are actively liquidating positions, long-term investors are absorbing the downturn with minimal panic selling.

Current Bitcoin holder behavior shows structural resilience among experienced investors despite immediate spot price weakness.

Tracking Bitcoin holder behavior requires examining total supply held by wallets inactive for over 155 days.

According to CoinGlass data as of November 11, 2026, Long-Term Holder Supply holds steady at 16.24 million BTC.

Rather than distributing into the late-September bounce to $87,153, older cohorts retained their coins, allowing mid-2026 purchases to mature into the long-term bracket.

The Long-Term Holder Spent Output Profit Ratio (LTH-SOPR) currently reads 0.90. Readings below 1.0 confirm that the small fraction of older coins moving on-chain are doing so at a slight loss.

Simultaneously, Supply-Adjusted Coin Days Destroyed (CDD) sits at a subdued 0.94, well below the August 2026 spike above 3.0. Older coins remain largely dormant, proving that conviction holders are not driving the November sell-off.

Recent market entrants display reactive Bitcoin holder behavior during price drawdowns. The Short-Term Holder SOPR (STH-SOPR) dropped from above 1.02 in October to 0.96 in mid-November.

When STH-SOPR fails to hold 1.0 as support, it signals that traders who bought between 1 hour and 155 days ago are exiting at a loss, adding direct downward momentum to spot markets.

The broader market’s Net Unrealized Profit/Loss (NUPL) has compressed to 0.21, placing the network in the lower hope/fear zone.

Historically, NUPL readings near 0.21 indicate that most excess speculative profit has been wiped out. Because aggregate unrealized gains are low, long-term investors have little financial incentive to distribute coins at $65,836.

Exchange flow data confirms that spot selling pressure is stabilizing rather than accelerating. Total Exchange Inflow dropped to 8.1K BTC, while Total Exchange Outflow sits slightly higher at 9.3K BTC.

Net outflows during a price drop indicate that buyers are withdrawing coins to cold storage rather than depositing them onto trading platforms for liquidation.

Analyzing Bitcoin holder behavior alongside institutional flows highlights the primary cause of the November crash.

The 30-day ETF Demand Growth and Apparent Demand metrics have plunged deep into negative territory, reaching roughly -200K BTC in combined apparent demand contraction.

Without institutional ETF inflows to absorb routine selling, even modest retail capitulation pushed price down through moving average supports toward the $65,670–$66,083 horizontal zone.

Note on How to Read This Table: On-chain metrics often reflect two different timeframes at once. The Bearish column shows what is hurting Bitcoin’s price right now (active selling and weak demand), while the Bullish column shows why those exact conditions historically help form a long-term price bottom.

So to keep it simple, the current Bitcoin holder behavior reflects long-term accumulation paired with short-term institutional and retail distribution. Long-term holders are not distributing at $65,800; they hold 16.24 million BTC and keep older coins dormant.

Instead, the drop from $87,153 stems from negative 30-day ETF demand growth and short-term holders selling at a loss. If Bitcoin holds the $65,670–$66,083 support level while exchange outflows continue exceeding inflows, current conditions align with structural bottom formation rather than cycle-top distribution.
