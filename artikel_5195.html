Bitcoin crashes near $62,000 over weekend as crypto liquidations topped $1.8 billion

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

After seven sessions of US spot ETF outflows, Strive CEO Matt Cole compares Bitcoin’s downturn with the SpaceX IPO selloff and its later rebound.

Bitcoin plunged to a four-month low near $62,000 on Sunday, Nov. 15 as heavy selling in dollar-denominated markets intensified a broader cryptocurrency downturn.

Bitcoin fell to about $62,000 on Nov. 15, its weakest level since July, before rebounding. Binance Research said the decline extended BTC's losses to 15.6% over 11 days, which is its sharpest comparable selloff since June.

The selling pressure has pushed Bitcoin into a price region that on-chain analytics firm Glassnode had identified as particularly vulnerable.

Glassnode reported that Bitcoin had entered the upper boundary of its largest concentration of potential liquidations between $60,000 and $63,000, a zone it had warned about for weeks.

During the weekend selloff, CoinGlass recorded nearly $2 billion in liquidations across the crypto market over 24 hours.

Bitcoin's latest downturn has exposed a striking disparity in selling pressure between dollar-denominated trading venues and Binance, suggesting risk aversion is particularly pronounced in USD markets.

Between Nov. 11 and Nov. 14, aggressive selling exceeded buying by 7,438 BTC on a major dollar-based spot exchange, compared with 2,707 BTC on Binance, according to Binance Research.

The reported selling imbalance on the unnamed USD venue was about 2.75 times Binance’s.

The pressure has also coincided with substantial withdrawals from US-listed investment products. US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded nearly $2 billion in net outflows across seven consecutive sessions from Nov. 5 through Nov. 13, according to Farside Investors.

Binance Research linked the simultaneous pressures to mounting uncertainty over interest rates, dollar liquidity and the sustainability of the artificial intelligence investment boom.

The concerns have coincided with upheaval at Nvidia, where Jensen Huang abruptly resigned as chief executive on Nov. 12, leaving longtime finance chief Colette Kress as interim CEO as investors scrutinize the sustainability of AI infrastructure spending ahead of Anthropic's blockbuster IPO.

However, Strive CEO Matt Cole believes Bitcoin's current weakness resembles the conditions surrounding SpaceX's record-breaking public offering earlier this year.

Cole observed that Bitcoin declined 19% over the 50 days before SpaceX's June 12 listing, closely resembling the 18% decline ahead of Anthropic's planned IPO when he made the comparison on Nov. 13.

Anthropic’s proposed offering could total about $64 billion at the top of its indicated price range, including shares sold by existing holders.

Cole noted that Bitcoin continued declining after SpaceX's listing, reaching its lowest point 18 days later before staging a sharp recovery. By the 100th day after the offering, the cryptocurrency had gained 28% from its IPO-day closing price and 39% from its subsequent low.

His chart sketches Bitcoin near $90,000 by late February 2027 if the post-SpaceX price pattern repeats after Anthropic’s listing.

The comparison offers a competing interpretation of the current downturn, although the similarities do not establish that either public offering caused Bitcoin's decline. The earlier episode also suggests that additional losses could precede any sustained recovery.

The next test comes on Nov. 16 when US spot Bitcoin ETFs reopen after seven consecutive sessions of withdrawals, followed by Nvidia's quarterly earnings on Nov. 17.

Investors will scrutinize the chipmaker's outlook for AI infrastructure spending and financing commitments as uncertainty persists around the sector's investment cycle.

An easing of aggressive spot selling and ETF withdrawals could strengthen the case for a recovery, while continued pressure would leave Bitcoin exposed to another decline toward $60,000 before Anthropic's market debut.
