Arm Holdings beat revenue expectations thanks to data center demand

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Revenue rose 27% to $1.44 billion, a record for a second quarter, as license revenue climbed 50% and royalty revenue rose 8%

Arm Holdings reported fiscal second-quarter revenue of $1.44 billion on Wednesday, up 27% year over year and a record for a second quarter. License and other revenue totaled $773 million, a 50% increase, and royalty revenue came in at $670 million, up 8%.

Non-GAAP earnings were $0.52 a share, compared with $0.39 in the year-ago period. Wall Street had forecast sales of $1.38 billion and per-share earnings of 47 cents, according to Bloomberg.

Royalty growth came from higher royalty rates per chip, such as Armv9 architecture and Arm CSS, and from more Arm-based chips deployed in data centers, the company said. Memory pricing's effect on the smartphone market partly offset those gains. License revenue growth was driven by demand for Arm technology, the timing and size of multiple high-value license agreements, and contributions from backlog, the company said.

Spending by data center operators and the vendors that supply them on licenses and chip designs from Arm has grown, making up for weakness in the smartphone business.

GAAP net income was $130 million, or $0.12 a share, compared with $0.22 a share in the year-ago period. Non-GAAP operating margin rose to 44.2% from 41.1%, and annualized contract value increased 12% to $1.79 billion.

Arm expects fiscal third-quarter revenue of about $1.54 billion, the company said. Analysts had averaged a forecast of $1.53 billion.

CEO Rene Haas said the company has more confidence in reaching its revenue target for its new data center chip, according to Bloomberg. Arm expects revenue from the chip to reach as much as $2 billion across fiscal 2027 and 2028.

"We could probably do more if we had more supply," Haas said. "We're signaling strong confidence in achieving that number."

The chip is the AGI CPU, Arm's first in-house central processing unit built for data centers. Arm said in May it saw a path to $2 billion in demand for the chip, twice what it had projected at the chip's March unveiling. The open question has been whether Arm can secure enough manufacturing capacity, as chipmakers compete for limited production capacity during the AI spending boom.

Arm stock barely moved in after-hours trading following the results. It had risen over 75% so far in the year, measured at Wednesday's close.
