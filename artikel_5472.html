Nvidia-backed Agility Robotics becomes first U.S. humanoid robot maker to go public

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The company raised about $465 million through a merger with Churchill Capital Corp XI and related financings, including a Foxconn-led investment

Samuel Boivin / NurPhoto via Getty Images

Agility Robotics began trading on Nasdaq $NDAQ on Thursday as the first U.S. publicly listed pure-play humanoid robotics company with active commercial deployments, the company said. Its common stock and warrants are trading under the symbols "AGLT" and "AGLTW" following the completion of its business combination with Churchill Capital Corp XI.

The business combination and related financings raised about $465 million in gross capital before transaction expenses, the company said. The total includes proceeds from a private investment in public equity led by Foxconn and $100 million of equity funding received before closing. The Foxconn-led investment was roughly $201 million, according to Bloomberg. Nvidia $NVDA, Amazon $AMZN and SoftBank Vision Fund 2 are also backers.

Agility said it will use the proceeds to fulfill customer orders, expand commercial deployments, scale production of its Digit robot and advance its robotics and physical AI platform.

The company said Digit 5, its next-generation humanoid robot, has attracted committed multi-year orders worth more than $300 million. The orders are subject to contractual milestones, product features and specifications. Digit 5 was unveiled in September. Early customer access is expected to begin in the first half of 2027, with general availability targeted for the end of 2027.

Digit operates at GXO, Schaeffler and Toyota $TM Motor Manufacturing Canada, the company said. Agility is expanding its partnership with GXO to deploy Digit at two more U.S. logistics facilities in early 2027. At GXO's Georgia site, Digit has moved more than 100,000 totes. AGCO has signed an agreement for a Digit pilot expected to begin in 2027, and Agility has more than 30 prospective customers in its pipeline.

"Becoming a public company gives us access to more resources to help accelerate that progress, bring intelligent robots to more workplaces and help build a future where people and robots accomplish more together," Agility CEO Peggy Johnson said in a statement.

Digit 5 is designed to work in closer proximity to people, the company said. Earlier Digit models could only work while fenced off by protective barriers, according to Bloomberg. Humanoids with legs are harder to anticipate than wheeled machines, which makes them more hazardous to be around.

Johnson told Bloomberg the listing adds pressure “because we are the first out of the gate and will be the company that others are looking to, not only for those metrics but also safety.”

Agility's listing follows that of a Chinese rival. Unitree Robotics went public on Shanghai's STAR Market in August, when its stock closed 460% above its IPO price in its first session. Unitree competes with Tesla $TSLA and Hyundai Motor Group-owned Boston Dynamics.
