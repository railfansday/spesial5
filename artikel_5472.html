U.S. is requiring licenses to reexport AI chips to five countries

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Commerce Department's interim final rule targets remote access to advanced AI chips through data centers in third countries

The U.S. Commerce Department released an interim final rule on Sunday that tackles how advanced AI chips and servers can be reached over the internet through facilities hosted in other nations.

The department's Bureau of Industry and Security said the rule is meant to stop companies from sidestepping current limits on sensitive U.S. technology by relying on remotely hosted computing setups. It said the rule aligns protections on remote access with current controls on the physical shipment of that technology.

The Treasury Department and the National Security Council wrote the rule and directed its release, the bureau said.

Companies will have to obtain a license before re-exporting advanced AI chips to Brazil, Singapore, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand, according to Bloomberg. The move follows tighter scrutiny of chip sales overseas in the wake of alleged spying connected to Nvidia $NVDA.

The bureau did not say in its announcement when the rule takes effect.

Some Chinese AI companies had contacted overseas data-center operators about reserving Nvidia chip capacity amid concern that Washington would restrict cloud-based access to advanced processors. U.S. export controls keep Nvidia's top-end AI processors out of China, but Chinese firms can still lease them in data centers abroad.

U.S. investigators have examined whether Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud company, helped Chinese clients obtain export-controlled Nvidia chips. Megaspeed has rejected claims that it illegally sent chips to China.

Jensen Huang, who stepped down as Nvidia CEO last week, has acknowledged a relationship with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive suspected of spying for China. The two are unrelated, and China has denied the allegation.

Separately, the House approved the Remote Access Security Act in January on a 369-22 vote. The bill would give the Commerce Department authority to oversee how restricted technology is accessed through the cloud. The Senate had not acted on it as of Nov. 12.
