What Scotland need to secure Nations League promotion play-off spot

Scotland’s hopes of winning promotion to the top tier of the Nations League are still alive but they will need to pull off a remarkable result when they take on Switzerland tonight.

Sebastien Pocognoli’s side were given a boost following their 2-1 victory over North Macedonia on Friday which ensured they have the chance to overtake Slovenia in the Group B1 table and finish inside the top two.

The top two spots in the group will automatically be promoted into the A League for the next edition of the competition though a third place finish will keep Scotland in the B League, the competition’s second tier.

Scotland’s fate is out of their hands so what do Pocognoli’s team need in order to secure promotion this evening?

The first and most crucial aspect of Scotland’s promotion charge is that they must collect three points when they travel to Switzerland tonight. The Scots head to Sitterstadion in St Gallen with a clear objective but the task will be difficult against a Swiss side who have already secured top spot in the group.

Switzerland top the table with an unassailable 13 points while Slovenia sit second on eight and Scotland are third on seven.

Earlier in the group stages, Scotland secured a goalless draw with Slovenia before losing a crucial tie 2-1 at Hampden Park back in early October. That means Slovenia have a superior head-to-head record against Scotland.

Because, although a draw against Switzerland would be enough to move level on eight points with Slovenia (should they also lose to North Macedonia), Slovenia’s superior record would win them promotion over Scotland.

All that means Scotland must win this evening and move to 10 points in the table to be in with a chance of getting promoted.

But history is stacked against them. Scotland have met Switzerland five times and have only triumphed once. That victory came in the first meeting between the two nations back in 1996 thanks to an Ally McCoist winner and the Swiss have won two and drawn two of the following four encounters.

Despite that record optimists may say the Scots are due a win and Switzerland may decide to rest key players with the group already won.

What else must happen if Scotland are to be promoted?

Well, Slovenia have to drop points against North Macedonia. Should Slovenia win their final group game they will finish on 11 points and Scotland will be unable to catch them regardless of the result against the Swiss.

That means a draw between Slovenia and North Macedonia or a North Macedonian victory opens up the route for Scotland to leap ahead of clinch a promotion place.

However, that permutation feels extremely unlikely as North Macedonia haven’t earned a single point from their five matches so far. They’ve been beaten twice by both Switzerland and Scotland and lost 2-0 to Slovenia in their previous contest. Not only have the North Macedonia’s not earned a point yet but they have only scored one goal - in their most recent defeat to the Scots - and that will not trouble a competent and determined Slovenia side.

Yet, should the unlikely scenario occur and North Macedonia take points away from Slovenia at the same time Scotland defeat Switzerland, the Scots will finish second in the group and will be promoted to the top tier of the Nations League

A second place finish would also have a knock-on effect for Euro 2028 as, despite being a co-host, the Tartan Army would be place in an easier qualifying group with a pot two seeding before the draw.
