Game Preview: San Antonio Spurs vs. Sacramento Kings

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Spurs bounced back quickly after having their 12-game win streak snapped by the Pistons, beating the Clippers and Pelicans at home. The opponents weren't exactly world-beaters, but taking care of business, especially while dealing with Dylan Harper's absence, is exactly what they needed to do. And the same task will be at hand on Monday.

Sacramento has the second-worst record in the league. The Spurs already made easy work of them this season. On paper, it should be an easy win. San Antonio, unfortunately, has struggled at times with playing down to opponents, and Victor Wembanyama, who has been dominant lately, is listed as questionable. Wemby had 50 points and 18 rebounds in under 32 minutes of playing time against the Pelicans, an insane performance but not too surprising for The Alien these days, as he has ascended to a new level of superstardom. De'Aaron Fox, who has been great this season despite not posting eye-popping numbers, could also be out. If those two join Harper on the sidelines, things could be tougher than expected on Monday.

The Kings might be bottom-dwellers, but they are coming off their second win of the season, a narrow and surprising victory over the Celtics. Granted, it took a horrendous shooting performance from a "live by the three, die by the three" Boston team missing Jayson Tatum, but good times have been rare in Sactown, so both the fans and the players will take it. Whether they'll get momentum from the win and whether that would even matter against a vastly more talented opponent is unclear, but, once again, the Spurs should take the game seriously, especially if Wembanyama is not available. Even if there are a couple of major absences, San Antonio can beat the Kings, but they'll need to take the game seriously.

Spurs injuries: Victor Wembanyama (back) – Questionable, De'Aaron Fox (shin) – Questionable, Jordan McLaughlin (shoulder) – Questionable, Dylan Harper (Hip) – Out, Tobias Harris (calf) – Out, Jayden Quaintance (knee) – Out.

Kings injuries: Ben Simmons (knee) – Out, Maxime Raynaud (knee) – Out.

The Celtics rested players against the Kings and lost the game. Again, their shooting is what doomed them, and it was only a one-point loss, but in the NBA, anyone can beat anyone else if a few things break right. If the Spurs go for the load management approach in Sacramento, they will need whoever suits up to step up.

There's no way to replace Wembanyama, and Luke Kornet has struggled this season. It's possible that with increased minutes Kornet will find some rhythm on offense, but the Kings are a really good defensive rebounding team, so putbacks shouldn't be easy to come by. Someone will have to feed Kornet and rookie Tarris Reed near the basket on the pick-and-roll if San Antonio hopes to get production from their interior players.

Fox would be the perfect man for the job, but he's also listed as questionable. So is Jordan McLaughlin. If the two former Kings don't suit up, Stephon Castle will have to do the heavy lifting as the lead shot creator. He could get some help from David Jones Garcia, while rookie Ja'Kobe Gillespie, who has been getting great numbers in the G League, should also get some run if the Silver and Black need perimeter scoring.

The core players should be fine, but Mitch Johnson will need some guys who have not been getting much run to have good games to make sure the Spurs secure the win.

The Kings have three veterans in the starting lineup that should produce at a high level, at least on offense. Domantas Sabonis, Zach LaVine, and De'Andre Hunter might not be the type of core a team should build around, but they are proven commodities, guys that could take advantage of a potentially shorthanded Spurs team. But the X-Factor for Sacramento could be their young guns.

Darius Acuff Jr. could win Rookie of the Year. Despite struggling with scoring efficiency, he has looked like a future offensive engine, someone who can score at all levels and pass well. Anyone who averages 25 points and seven assists is someone the defense has to try to limit. The other young-ish piece is Keegan Murray. The 26-year-old forward missed the first encounter with the Spurs but has been rounding into shape recently, with his game-winner against the Celtics showing that he can do the little things while also coming up big for his team when needed.

The Kings are not the deepest team in the league. They rely heavily on their starters. A few of them will have good games, but the Spurs should know how to at least keep the older guys in check. They'll need to do the same with the younger players, especially if injuries and load management take away their talent advantage.
