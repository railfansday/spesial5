Winter is the ideal time to make progress on your financial goals.

When it’s cold outside, it’s the ideal time to stay home, organize your finances, set some new money goals and make progress toward them.

That’s the promise of the viral winter money trend known as “The Great Lock In” that’s circulating on social media.

“You’re probably not doing too much,” says Robert Baird, wealth manager at Cornerstone Financial Services in Southfield, Michigan. “It gets dark early. So those can be great months to lock into saving and not spend in excess.”

One important component of the Great Lock In is developing new routines that will last well into 2027, says Jordan Mangaliman, founder and fiduciary wealth advisor at GoldLine Wealth Management in Fullerton, California.

“We want a long-term habit versus a Band-Aid fix,” he says. “In today’s economy, money goes in and out so blindingly fast.”

Ready to join the movement? Here’s how to get started.

What’s your money moonshot? Maybe it’s paying off all your credit card debt or purchasing a home. Or spending less than you earn. Defining it is the first step toward achieving it.

“Spend a lot of time identifying what that goal is,” Baird says. “Everyone’s in a different position, so comparing yourself to what you see online might not be applicable.”

Even while setting ambitious money goals, it’s worth grounding them in reality, Mangaliman says.

If you want to save a larger portion of your income, he suggests trying the new saving pattern for a couple of months to see how it feels rather than saying you’ll do it forever and then getting frustrated.

“It’s a marathon, not a sprint,” Mangaliman adds.

Once you’ve outlined those big goals for your Great Lock In, create a budget to help you achieve them, Baird says.

He suggests combing through your previous credit or debit card spending to see where your money is going. Then you can decide what you want to change or shift. Using a tool like the 50/30/20 budget calculator can also help. This approach allots 50% of take-home pay toward needs, 30% toward wants and 20% toward savings and debt payments beyond the minimum.

Baird suggests including saving as a line item in your budget so you don’t forget to do it. “Building that cost into your budget will put you in a better position later,” he says.

Any Great Lock In should include growing your savings, because that makes achieving other goals easier, Baird says.

“Maybe there’s something you could cut in the short term to build up that savings,” he says. Good targets include subscription services, food delivery, entertainment and travel costs.

Saving more is especially challenging in today’s environment, when everyday expenses like gas are higher than they were a year ago.

Baird says that makes winter a good time to try to reduce costs. There can be fewer temptations to go out and spend money when the days are shorter and the temperatures are colder.

Mangaliman recommends setting aside three to six months’ worth of expenses, although even a smaller amount is helpful. “Ultimately it’s about what you can afford,” he says. “It’s the foundation of your financial house.”

Along with building savings, paying down high-interest debt is another pillar of the Great Lock In.

With credit card interest rates higher than they were a few years ago, carrying credit card debt has gotten more expensive. “I would consider applying some savings to paying down debt, especially right now when your debt is costing you more than you can earn in savings,” Baird says. (You still want to reserve an emergency fund to cover unexpected expenses.)

“High-interest debt is one of the biggest thieves of wealth,” Mangaliman says. And one way to avoid turning to debt again in the future is to build up that emergency fund so it’s there for you if you need it.

Even when you are committed to a Great Lock In, some hiccups along the way are inevitable. Success depends on continuing anyway.

“It’s like a diet,” Baird says. “Just because you ate ice cream on a Sunday doesn’t mean on Monday you can’t go right back to being disciplined again.”

“Be kind to yourself and do everything in moderation,” Mangaliman suggests. Just wanting to improve your finances is already a great first step, he says.

In fact, he embraces the Great Lock In mentality himself. “There’s an old saying: It’s not how you start, but how you finish,” he says. “And you want to finish the year on a strong note so you carry those habits into 2027 and beyond.”
