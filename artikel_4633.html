Preparing Kids for Their AI Future

Posted November 25, 2026 | Reviewed by Davia Sills

As you’ve most certainly seen on the news, artificial intelligence (AI) is here, and it’s in the process of changing our society. Depending on your point of view, AI seems to be either a blessing or a curse. Maybe both. While AI may help doctors cure diseases that have plagued us for centuries, it may also be an existential threat to our survival on the planet. The bottom line is, AI is here, and whether it’s the ultimate triumph of technology or the worst creation ever invented is yet to be seen.

While all of the differing reports on AI reverberate through the news cycle, it’s important for parents to know how AI can affect their children. For example, AI has aided in suicides by telling teens effective ways to harm themselves. Obviously, this is an extreme and major mistake. Hopefully, steps are being taken to stop that type of information from being dispensed in the future.

While these types of extreme examples get headlines and are acutely dangerous, there are other dangers that AI brings to children and their education that are not as widely publicized. For example, students can now use AI to do their homework or write their term papers. In the space of a few minutes, students can feed their assignment into AI and voila, out pops a well-written, factually sourced paper. If the student does a little tweaking, it may not be recognizable as AI, and the teacher may accept it as the student’s own work.

While cheating in school is not new, it’s never been this easy to game the system. While this can certainly be appealing to many students, the drawbacks of using AI to cheat carry real risks for their future. Beyond the information they are not learning within the paper they were supposed to write, they are also shirking responsibility. This can harm students by giving them a false sense of security in the present but damaging their ability to deliver what’s necessary in the future.

In any career, meeting deadlines is important. One of the ways in which we all learn to complete tasks within specific time frames is through school projects. If students don’t learn this, it can hold them back in whatever career they pursue. This is just one example of how AI’s seeming help can have long-lasting consequences.

AI is also going to make many jobs unnecessary. A student who doesn’t develop critical thinking skills, as well as specific areas of expertise, becomes an adult who is ripe for being replaced by AI. It seems that people who are able to think creatively, understand how to accomplish tasks efficiently, and learn to follow through on commitments are more likely to maintain careers.

That’s why, as parents, it’s important that we talk with our children about the value of thinking for themselves. If they surrender all of their choices and individuality to AI, they’re likely to become little more than worker drones in perhaps the very near future. The more skilled and resourceful they are, along with building a work ethic that gives them self-esteem and self-confidence, the better prepared they are for the future.

The question becomes, what can we do to help? By sharing with our children the importance of learning and taking responsibility for their own futures, they can be better prepared to deal with whatever changes happen. By building up their self-image and confidence, they can see more possibilities for themselves and be more ready to face the challenges that arise in their lives.

It does not seem to be in the best interest of today’s youth to surrender to the ease of AI. By doing so, they may not be learning vital life lessons, which can undermine their futures. Our children need to know they still have a place in the world and can prepare themselves to thrive no matter what changes may occur.
