14 Best Work Jackets for Men 2026, According to GQ Editors

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

For better or worse, the best work jackets for men are no longer associated with garages, factory floors, and loading docks. Instead, as the likes of Paul Mescal and Austin Butler (who told GQ he wore his for a year straight) continue to fly their flag, a great work jacket has become every guy's fastest route to semi-rugged heartthrob style.

Honestly, they make looking good almost too easy. First, they inject your fits with casual, weekend tough-guy energy. (This, to be clear, is a plus.) Second, they flatter you in all the right places, framing your shoulders and neatening up your waistline whether you’re pairing them with jeans or the kinds of wide, puddling trousers we've long been yapping about.

Better still, you don’t have to spend a fortune. The classics from Dickies and Carhartt are as dependable as ever, and even the independent designer options are within reach of your budget—this is a cotton-forward option, after all. Here are 14 of our favorites, all of which will only look better after a few years (and a few scuffs) of real wear.

The Carhartt Detroit work jacket is the classic for a reason. It’s been around for more than 70 years—not quite as old as blue jeans or cowboy boots, but easily in the upper echelon of American fashion’s great exports. So much so that today, luxury brands are riffing on it, even selling pre-distressed, washed-down versions at triple the price. (And yes, this is also the work jacket that Austin Butler told us he wears.)

Cut to the waist, and built from thick, stiff canvas with three perfectly placed front pockets, the Detroit is an Eisenhower-style silhouette (during WWII, the then-Army General overhauled U.S. military dress and introduced the cropped, boxy jacket to American forces). Its insulated blanket lining and corduroy collar also make it one of the few true year-round, toasty work jackets out there. Simply put, this is a real piece of clothing for real life. (Though if it's not fancy enough for you, there’s always the street-ready, slimmer, unlined Carhartt WIP version.)

Just like Dickies' iconic 874 work pants, the brand's insulated Eisenhower jacket (there he is again) is a hard-wearing, warm, wallet-friendly piece of gear that fits incredibly well. Nearly as old as the Detroit Jacket above, it doesn’t always get the same respect. But maybe that's just because it flies a little more under the radar, with its logo patch tucked discreetly near the hem. Decades in, it remains a low-key, low-cost icon—less shouty than its Carhartt cousin, but every bit as authentic.

Wallace & Barnes, J.Crew's workwear line, is back with tough fabrics, heritage details, and a big hit of #menswear-era nostalgia—and this wool-lined work jacket shows exactly why folks missed it so much. It has all of the heartthrob-coded details you probably had in mind when you decided to look for a jacket like this: Corduroy collar, zippered chest pocket, slightly cropped fit. But it isn't just good-looking. It also has a wool-blend blanket lining, for superior warmth to help you enjoy it even if the depths of winter. And that, if cost-per-wear logic still applies, makes it more than worth its slightly higher price point.

Welcome to the world of truly warm fabrics, and actually glove-friendly pockets. Filson’s been mastering cold-weather gear since your great-great-grandfather was in short pants, and this work jacket continues that legacy. Based on the brand’s original 1914 Cruiser, it’s cut from 24-ounce Mackinaw wool — a tightly woven twill that truly blocks wind, sheds rain, and stays warm even when wet. Without being mean, it’s built to outlast even you. Pricey? Sure. But it’s the kind of thing your kids will remember you by (and fight for) someday.

Buck Mason’s archive is full to bursting with vintage pieces from decades past, which explains why its collections are filled with absolute bangers these days. Here the brand has taken a mid-century work jacket and remade it, tweaking the fit to make it a touch more relaxed, and using a soft Italian doeskin cotton fabric that’s more plush than anything a worksite would have issued back in the day. There's even more to love in the details: Shoulder pleats for easy movement, real horn buttons on the chest pockets, and a dressy point collar.

For Lo-heads, Polo Ralph Lauren's hard-wearing zipper jackets are the stuff of legend. And while you might have grown up with an inherited cotton example from your dad, in the last decade of so, Polo has been pushing its ‘Bi-Swing’ polyester and/or nylon takes instead. That's fine, but some of us aren't so willing to trade in the soft, rugged comfort of a full-cotton jacket. That's where the rarer Bayport Poplin jacket comes in. A work jacket with no delusions, it’s not stealing the working man’s valor — it knows exactly what it is. It’s Ralph Lauren, and it knows you’re working hard at hardly working. But it's also strong enough to handle whatever activities you throw at it, leisure or otherwise.

Sure, Copenhagen's Mfpen has its silhouettes dialed, but don't overlook its fabrications. Here, it’s a dense wool-blend with a multi-tonal yarn construction. Textured, substantive, perfect.

While most waxed cotton jackets lean more country estate than work site, Tom Beckbe's high-quality range include a few closer, more modern fits. Perfect for those looking for a work jacket that's also waterproof.

A designer-adjacent example for the fashion crowd, this piece from South Korea's FrizmWORKS doesn't just look good, it feels good too, thanks to that soft leather collar that's just buttery once it's flipped up.

Stan Ray is part of what you might call the big three of American workwear, alongside Dickies and Carhartt). The Texas-born brand’s aptly named Roughneck Jacket delivers exactly what you’d expect. It’s tough, practical, and built for wear. Ribbed cuffs keep the cold out, while the washed-out hue delivers a perfectly broken-in look from day one.

This jacket from New York's Corridor—made from a hefty cotton-blend twill—is a more colorful take on the work jacket, and bound to give any fall fit a lift.

Just as the Polo jacket above is an RL icon, so is this faithfully-recreated nautical deck jacket from Ralph's RRL line. It's restocked every fall, it sells out every fall.

A key feature of all work jackets is their shorter length, but for those of us who want to make sure ours hits right at the belt, look no further than Abercrombie’s entry.

A leading Parisian purveyor of vintage garms, BRUT has recently moved into making its own stuff, all rooted in the archival vintage styles they obsess over. The results are, how you say, on point.

These days, work jackets seem to have an undeniable stranglehold on the imagination of guys who spend all day staring at their keyboards. (There’s a political element to this, of course. Whether or not you act in solidarity with your fellow laborers under capitalism, a work jacket allows you to look the part of the everyman.) But also, they’re just a great jacket for busy lives! There’s a generally accommodating cut that doesn’t restrict your movement, plenty of pockets, and hardy materials that can easily handle a snag, or sudden shower.

Style is subjective, we know—that’s the fun of it. But we’re serious about helping our audience get dressed. Whether it’s the best white sneakers, the flyest affordable suits, or the need-to-know menswear drops of the week, GQ Recommends’ perspective is built on years of hands-on experience, an insider awareness of what’s in and what’s next, and a mission to find the best version of everything out there, at every price point.

Our staffers aren’t able to try on every single piece of clothing you read about on GQ.com (fashion moves fast these days), but we have an intimate knowledge of each brand’s strengths and know the hallmarks of quality clothing—from materials and sourcing, to craftsmanship, to sustainability efforts that aren’t just greenwashing. GQ Recommends heavily emphasizes our own editorial experience with those brands, how they make their clothes, and how those clothes have been reviewed by customers. Bottom line: GQ wouldn’t tell you to wear it if we wouldn’t.

We make every effort to cast as wide of a net as possible, with an eye on identifying the best options across three key categories: quality, fit, and price.

To kick off the process, we enlist the GQ Recommends braintrust to vote on our contenders. Some of the folks involved have worked in retail, slinging clothes to the masses; others have toiled for small-batch menswear labels; all spend way too much time thinking about what hangs in their closets.

We lean on that collective experience to guide our search, culling a mix of household names, indie favorites, and the artisanal imprints on the bleeding-edge of the genre. Then we narrow down the assortment to the picks that scored the highest across quality, fit, and price.

Across the majority of our buying guides, our team boasts firsthand experience with the bulk of our selects, but a handful are totally new to us. So after several months of intense debate, we tally the votes, collate the anecdotal evidence, and emerge with a list of what we believe to be the absolute best of the category right now, from the tried-and-true stalwarts to the modern disruptors, the affordable beaters to the wildly expensive (but wildly worth-it) designer riffs.

Whatever your preferences, whatever your style, there's bound to be a superlative version on this list for you. (Read more about GQ's testing process here.)
