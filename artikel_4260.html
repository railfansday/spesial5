10 Best Boss Fights in Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Covering the video games industry since 2017, with experience in news, articles, and reviews. Currently a writer at DualShockers and gaming magazines.

Alongside its completely revamped visuals and modernized gameplay designed around the Joy-Cons, one of the things I was most excited about when I finally got my hands on The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake was facing its bosses all over again.

I've played the original countless times, and there are so many incredible boss fights throughout this adventure that the mere thought of experiencing them again with all these changes was almost too exciting to handle. Thankfully, despite my ridiculously high expectations, it managed to exceed them. Congratulations, Nintendo, all my childhood nightmares have officially been upgraded!

Some of the original's most charming quirks from Zelda: Ocarina of Time didn't make the journey.

Jokes aside, the remake does an incredible job of staying faithful to the original bosses while giving them enough visual and gameplay improvements to make these encounters feel fresh. And honestly, the excitement I felt when entering these arenas was just as strong as it was the first time around. And picking a favorite Ocarina of Time boss is still as difficult as picking just one Zelda game to call your favorite: there are simply too many amazing ones to choose from!

So, I decided to put together a ranking of the ten best boss fights in the new Ocarina of Time, including a few mini-bosses to make things even more interesting. Let's see which encounters managed to steal the spotlight. Naturally, there are lots of spoilers here if you haven't completed the remake or the original, so beware!

Fighting Gohma is actually pretty easy, I must say, but there's no denying that she makes a fantastic first impression as Ocarina of Time's opening boss. This is basically the first real test of your skills, giving you a taste of what's waiting for you throughout the rest of the adventure – and there's something incredibly satisfying about finally defeating your first major enemy.

Besides, her introduction is absolutely fantastic! You enter what appears to be an empty room, already anticipating the boss encounter because you know something is waiting for you. But there's nothing there. So, naturally, you look up... and surprise! Gohma is crawling across the ceiling, ready to ruin your day.

Her imposing size makes the encounter even more memorable, especially when you realize she's trying to give birth to a bunch of smaller spiders just to make your life miserable. Lovely! The remake takes all these elements and makes them even more impressive with its updated visuals, giving Gohma a much more intimidating presence. Not bad for our first big fight.

What's not to love about fighting a giant blob of goo? I'll admit that I never found Morpha particularly exciting in the original game, but the remake completely changed my mind.

One of my favorite things about this encounter is how quickly it goes from zero to one hundred. After surviving the Water Temple, you're probably expecting some terrifying aquatic monster, perhaps a giant shark or a creature covered in tentacles. Instead, you walk into the boss arena and find... a blob.

However, things get considerably more interesting once the battle begins. Morpha uses its gelatinous body to create enormous tentacles that can grab Link and take a surprisingly painful chunk out of his health. And believe me, they're considerably faster this time around, so you'll want to keep moving.

Falling into the water is also something you should avoid at all costs, since the rest of Morpha's body is lurking beneath the surface. It's a surprisingly stressful encounter, but also fun, especially because you have to use the Hookshot to pull Morpha's inner nucleus out of its tentacles and attack it. It's the only boss that requires you to use what is the coolest equipment in the game!

My first encounter with the Iron Knuckle in the remake was just as terrifying as I remembered it being in the original. This heavily armored enemy hits like a truck, and every successful attack can take a ridiculous amount of Link's health away. Even worse, its powerful strikes can send you flying across the arena, making it incredibly difficult to regain control of the situation.

A big part of what makes this encounter so intimidating is that you're facing the Iron Knuckle as Child Link. You're considerably weaker than Adult Link, and you don't have access to the powerful equipment and abilities you've grown accustomed to using throughout your adventure. So suddenly, you're reminded that you're still a pretty vulnerable little kid, and this walking tank is more than happy to prove it!

The remake's improved visuals make the encounter even more impressive, giving the Iron Knuckle an intimidating presence that makes every swing of its enormous axe feel dangerous. It's a little like eating your grandmother's homemade food after worrying that something might have gone wrong with the recipe. Somehow, it turns out even better than you remembered.

King Dodongo is an enormous creature that spends most of the fight rolling around its circular arena, trying to crush Link before eventually opening its mouth to unleash a fiery attack. What I love most about this boss is how clever its main weakness is. Since the creature seems so eager to swallow Link, let’s give it something else to eat, of course!

So all you need to do is avoid its rolling attacks and throw a Bomb into its mouth whenever it opens it, and watching this enormous beast swallow an explosive and immediately regret its life choices never gets old.

It's also a fantastic early boss because it makes you realize just how much bigger and more dangerous Hyrule is compared to the relatively cozy Kokiri Forest. Nothing says “your adventure is officially getting more challenging” quite like fighting a creature thirty times your size over a pool of lava.

Dead Hand has always been one of the most nightmare-inducing enemies in the entire Zelda franchise, and apparently, Nintendo knows exactly how much we fear this thing. Because if there's one boss that the remake refuses to make less terrifying, it's this horrifying creature!

Just like with the Iron Knuckle, your first encounter with Dead Hand happens while playing as Child Link. Veteran players already know what's coming as soon as they enter the room and spot those creepy hands emerging from the ground. And you can feel the tension building before it actually appears. Once it finally reveals itself, things get considerably worse.

Its updated design makes its face even more disturbing, and watching it attempt to bite Link with its enormous mouth is enough to make you question whether you accidentally launched a survival horror game instead of Ocarina of Time. I honestly don't know how Nintendo managed to make this thing even creepier, but I certainly won't be looking under my bed tonight.

Nintendo has expanded one of the original Zelda: Ocarina of Time's most heartbreaking side stories.

Fighting an invisible enemy is already challenging enough. Fighting an invisible enemy while standing on top of a giant bongo drum that it's constantly playing? Now that's a completely different story.

Bongo Bongo is easily one of the most creative boss encounters in Ocarina of Time, and its updated appearance takes the entire experience to another level. The idea of fighting this enormous, headless creature while trying to keep your balance on a drum is already wonderfully bizarre, but the atmosphere surrounding the encounter makes it even better.

The remake also makes some of its attacks noticeably faster, meaning you'll have even less time to react while trying to keep up with the rhythm of the fight. And since Bongo Bongo is literally drumming throughout the entire encounter, you can bet that things get pretty intense! It's such an unusual boss concept, and I love how the remake manages to preserve its original identity while making the entire battle feel even more chaotic.

Facing Dark Link is one of the most memorable moments in the original Ocarina of Time, and the remake manages to make this encounter just as special. As you progress through the Water Temple, you'll find yourself fighting Dark Link (accompanied by a rather creepy-looking Dark Navi, by the way), which ends up being an unforgettable battle.

The symbolism behind this encounter is already fantastic, since what better way to challenge the Hero of Time than by forcing him to face a darker version of himself? But the remake takes things even further by giving Dark Link a new move that might just be the coolest animation I've seen in the entire game.

Whenever you try to attack him with the tip of the Master Sword, he can actually dodge your strike by jumping onto the blade and standing on it – yes, he literally uses your own sword as a platform to avoid being hit, and it's ridiculously stylish! I also swear the controller gives you a little vibration when he pulls off this move, which makes the moment even more satisfying. Or maybe I'm imagining things because I'm still trying to process how cool it looks. Either way, Dark Link remains one of the best highlights of this remake.

I'll admit that Phantom Ganon has always been my favorite boss in the original Ocarina of Time, and a reason for that is the incredible atmosphere surrounding the Forest Temple. By the time you reach this dungeon, you've already seen what Ganondorf has done to Hyrule during his seven-year reign of terror, and there's a constant sense of dread surrounding his presence.

So, when you finally reach the boss room and see what appears to be Ganondorf himself, it's a pretty intimidating moment! Even if is a phantom version of him. What I love about it is that it uses a painting mechanic, where Phantom Ganon hides inside the painting and you must pay attention to spot him before he strikes you! Visually, it is a truly striking moment, and when he gets off the painting to face you off is amazing.

Also, the remake makes this encounter even more memorable by extending Ganondorf's dialogue after the battle. Once the boss is defeated, the real villain appears and calls him a useless puppet, warning that he knows Link’s intentions and won’t let him accomplish them so easily.

Twinrova just hits differently in the remake. The combination of their updated designs, new animations, and fully voiced cutscenes makes this battle feel considerably more special than it already was in the original. Gameplay-wise, the fight remains faithful to its original mechanics. You'll need to use the Mirror Shield to absorb the correct elemental attacks from Koume and Kotake before redirecting that energy toward the opposite witch. It's a clever mechanic that forces you to pay attention to both enemies while carefully timing your movements.

However, what really makes this encounter shine is how expressive the two witches have become! Their personalities come through much more clearly now, especially when you're managing to land a successful attack. Watching them react to your moves, get increasingly frustrated, and argue with each other is genuinely hilarious.

Things get considerably more serious once they fuse into Twinrova, turning the encounter into an even more intense battle involving both fire and ice. But the fun doesn't stop when the fight ends. The final interactions between the two witches are fantastic, and their newly voiced dialogue gives the entire sequence so much personality. Honestly, I could watch these two argue for hours.

There was no way I could finish this list without giving the final boss encounter the top spot. Facing Ganondorf and, eventually, Ganon is the culmination of everything you've experienced throughout Ocarina of Time, and the remake manages to make this already iconic confrontation even more impressive.

His updated animations, additional moves, and much more expressive character model make every moment feel considerably more dramatic. You can really feel that you're facing the main villain who has been threatening Hyrule throughout your entire adventure, rather than just another boss waiting at the end of a dungeon.

And then, just when you think the battle is over, Ganondorf transforms into Ganon, giving you one final confrontation against an enormous beast that looks terrifying in the remake's updated visuals. It's the perfect way to bring the adventure to its conclusion and also to deliver the best-looking sequences of the entire game. Now, if you'll excuse me, I think I need to replay the entire game again.
