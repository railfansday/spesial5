Bitcoin ETFs face record monthly withdrawals – What keeps institutions in?

Bitcoin [BTC] ETFs faced their worst month since launch, recording approximately $4.94 billion in Net Outflows during November so far. With several days still left, the month had already overtaken June’s $4.51 billion withdrawal.

Yet institutional ownership data offered a different perspective. Billions were leaving the funds, while reported institutional exposure remained substantial.

Just for a quick recap, the year started weak. January recorded $1.61 billion in Net Outflows, followed by $206.52 million in February.

A brief recovery followed. March attracted $1.32 billion in Net Inflows, while April brought another $1.97 billion. May, however, recorded $2.43 billion in Net Outflows. June followed with $4.51 billion, making it the year’s second-worst month.

July’s $172.43 million in Net Inflows offered a near-flat pause before a stronger rebound. August attracted $3.52 billion, followed by $2.65 billion in September. That recovery unravelled in October, when $515.03 million left the funds. November’s $4.94 billion withdrawal then pushed the year towards another low.

In fact, Glassnode also pointed out that Bitcoin ETF trading volume has fallen below its median since launch.

Hence, the persistent selling pressure has raised concerns about institutional confidence in Bitcoin. This is because a closer look at institutional ownership data suggests that Wall Street is not abandoning Bitcoin ETFs just yet.

In fact, institutional participation has continued to expand despite the deteriorating market sentiment, raising an important question: What is keeping institutions invested in Bitcoin ETFs when investors are pulling billions out of them?

According to Bloomberg ETF analyst James Seyffart, 2,223 institutional filers collectively reported approximately $29.77 billion in Bitcoin ETF exposure, equivalent to 356,072 BTC.

Investment advisors accounted for the largest share, with 1,863 filers holding approximately 160,195 BTC worth $13.39 billion. Brokerage firms followed with 59,255 BTC, while hedge funds held approximately 58,088 BTC. Banks and trusts also have considerable exposure, holding 28,937 BTC and 19,723 BTC, respectively.

More importantly, the data shows that institutional ownership increased by 56,493 BTC over the one-year period. Well, according to Alex Thorn from Galaxy Research, institutions have also added approximately 49,229 BTC-equivalent during the three-month period.

This suggests that the recent outflow streak has not translated into a complete institutional retreat. In this, Wells Fargo has emerged as a major institutional holder, with its aggregated filings showing approximately 30,549 BTC in exposure.

Jane Street, Goldman Sachs, Millennium Management, and Morgan Stanley were also featured among the notable holders. However, the current selling pressure cannot be overlooked.

Ecoinometrics noted that November’s ETF flow trajectory was closely following its bearish simulation. Therefore, if the trend continues, its model suggests Bitcoin could potentially revisit the low-$50,000 range.
