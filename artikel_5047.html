Skin changes are part of life—thanks to sun exposure, the natural dip in collagen and elastin once you hit your late 20s, or shifts in weight (hi, pregnancy!). So if your skin doesn’t quite have the same snap-back it once did, that’s completely normal. The good news? You don’t need pricey lasers or an intense 10-step routine to see a difference. The right body-firming lotion can help skin look plumper, smoother, and more resilient.

Firming body lotions can offer smoother, tighter, and less dimpled skin. While nothing can compare to the results you may get from an in-office procedure—topicals aren’t going to work miracles, after all—you might be surprised at just how far hydration can improve the appearance of crepey skin. “The physical transformation of hydration can be profound on our skin,” says dermatologist Nava Greenfield, MD. “The skin will actually appear plumper when a lotion used to ‘firm’ is applied. Adding nutrients to the skin on a consistent basis can also help in creating a healthy, firm appearance.”

Like other categories of skincare products, some firming formulas will always be better than others. To help streamline your shopping experience, I asked dermatologists for their expertise on how to shop for body lotions, along with product recommendations.

For the past few months, beauty contributor Danielle Blundell and I have been researching and testing the best firming lotions. After consulting six dermatologists for their shopping guidance, we asked for their no-fail recommendations as a starting point. We got our hands on some of these picks and shared our testing feedback to help you determine which option works for your unique beauty needs. And to ensure there was a variety of firming body lotions across all budgets, we spent hundreds of hours vetting products for their ingredient list, formulation, absorbency, long- and short-term hydration and firming benefits, and pricing. Of the dozens we tested, these were the best ones we’re willing to auto-refill.

You’ve probably been using body lotion since middle school, and firming formulas look and feel roughly the same. They’re applied the same way, too; just massage a dollop into the skin, let it dry for a bit, and you’re good to go on with your day. A few key features, though, are what can separate a good option from a great one, and this is what you’ll want to consider when shopping for the best firming body lotions.

For dermatologist Dr. Rachel Nazarian, MD, certain ingredients are a must if you’re looking to tone and improve the skin’s texture.

Ultimately, whatever lotion, oil, serum, or cream you choose comes down to your skin’s needs. On the most basic level, sometimes it’s just a matter of texture preference—maybe you like the lightness of an oil or serum and want to go that route, or the richness of a cream is what you gravitate toward in the colder months. But some skin types really do fare better with certain formats over others, so you’ll want to keep that in mind.

Serums, gels, and lotions are lightweight and generally better suited for women under 40 with normal or acne-prone skin. Because they’re not heavy, they’re also fantastic if you have sensitive skin all over. You can follow it up with a cream as spot treatment in areas like your kneecaps and elbows that require more moisturization, says Dr. Sartor.

Creams are thick and deeply hydrating, making them helpful for dry and mature skin. You can also use them if you have sensitive skin. However, there is a trade-off. Hyaluronic acid penetrates the skin more easily in lighter serums and gels, so you might need to choose between a gentler product and a more effective moisturizer.

Though not as important as ingredients or format, size can matter when it comes to choosing a firming body lotion, mainly because it can factor into a product’s price and usage. If you want to make a smaller, more expensive lotion go further, consider applying it to target areas—think the butt, thighs, tummy, or upper arms. Consistency is key, though, so a product that you can use more frequently could be better than something you’d have to use sparingly.

It’s important to manage your expectations for firming lotions—they’re not the cure-all for sagging skin. Severe laxity and deep folds aren’t going to disappear overnight, but your skin will perk up and even plump a bit temporarily upon application.

“You can expect to see your skin slowly showing fewer fine lines and less sag,” says Dr. Greenfield. “A firming cream also has the potential to introduce nutrients that allow your skin to feel smoother and more supple.”

Proper timing is key for absorption and even application, so do yourself a favor and apply right after you’ve just bathed so it sinks in easily, especially if you’re only using this kind of product once a day.

“It is best to apply body lotion when your skin is still damp from a shower but not wet,” says Dr. Greenfield. “Towel dried is optimal. After exfoliating, it is crucial to apply a moisturizer. Best results will ultimately be with consistent and liberal use.”

“Creams are not capable of the same type of tightening that lasers and surgical options can offer,” says Dr. Nazarian. “Despite this, with consistent, continued use, you can certainly see improvement in laxity over time.” But again, commitment to regular use will impact results because everyone’s skin is fighting an uphill battle to a certain extent, at least when it comes to the loss of skin firmness and volume. For the best results, you have to commit to using one regularly to improve the quality of skin laxity, says Dr. Nazarian.

The short answer is yes—but with the caveat that it’s possible to have some kind of a reaction to any body lotion or cream. “Most of these body firming lotions have very little risk,” says Dr. Nazarian. “They are often directed to be used daily, some even twice a day. Major risks include irritation from one of the ingredients or a potential allergy to one of the ingredients.”

Just steer clear of anything with retinol in it, if you’re nursing or pregnant, and discontinue use of a product you have a negative reaction to. “Occasionally, people may experience drying of their skin, especially if the ingredients have the ability to decrease oil production, such as with retinol,” says Dr. Nazarian. “Monitor for those changes and decrease use if they are noted.”
