Bitcoin flashes a signal seen at four previous market bottoms as $62,000 comes under pressure

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Glassnode saw losses at levels associated with past bottoms, while CryptoQuant’s 30-day total remains below the losses recorded at February and June lows.

The Bitcoin rebound from just above $62,000 was weaker the second time. The bounces came on Nov. 15 and 18, and the second session closed closer to its low. By then, a CryptoQuant measure showed the steepest losses since mid-August on older coins that moved. The next question is whether spot buyers can absorb that pressure for more than a day.

The Nov. 15 and Nov. 18 lows were $62,006 and $62,063 on the daily candles. Bitcoin finished the first day at $65,351 and the second at $63,068.

In a Nov. 19 analysis for CryptoQuant, Woominkyu put 30-day net realized losses at $8 billion, well short of two earlier 2026 readings. Glassnode’s Nov. 17 report found losses at levels it had seen around past market bottoms when measured against a year’s worth of realized profits.

Both gauges start from an estimate of what investors gained or lost when coins moved. Glassnode compares realized losses with profits taken over the previous year. On that relative measure, the weekend selloff reached readings Glassnode associated with lows in 2018, 2020, 2022 and early 2023.

Glassnode’s ratio reached levels seen near past lows. CryptoQuant’s 30-day net loss was smaller than in February or June.

A steep wave of losses can stand out against past profits even when the 30-day net dollar loss is smaller than in earlier selloffs.

The Glassnode article specifies the profit lookback. It leaves the exact loss window unstated. Both measures describe past coin movements.

CryptoQuant’s series tallies profits and subtracts losses over a rolling 30-day period. On that measure, net losses hit $30 billion on Feb. 20 and $18 billion on June 30. Even with a smaller monthly reading, losses were concentrated in the latest selloff. The four days from Nov. 15 to Nov. 18 produced $5 billion in net losses, more than half the 30-day figure.

A burst of losses can look extreme against a year’s profits while the month’s net dollar loss stays smaller than it was in a previous selloff. Earlier gains still in the 30-day window can offset part of the recent losses. Neither gauge sets a threshold Bitcoin has to reach before the price can stabilize.

Woominkyu’s Nov. 19 CryptoQuant Quicktake lists net losses of $30 billion on Feb. 20, $18.4 billion on June 30, and $8.4 billion in its latest reading.

That pressure reached coins that had been idle for more than 155 days. CryptoQuant’s long-term-holder spent output profit ratio fell to 0.73 on Nov. 18, its lowest since mid-August, according to Woominkyu’s analysis. The reading implies roughly 73 cents in value when those outputs moved for each dollar of value when they were created. The series measures only older outputs that moved, including transfers that can occur without a trade on an exchange.

The spot market gave a more tentative signal. Glassnode saw Bitcoin briefly trade at a premium on Coinbase around the Nov. 15 low before slipping back to a discount. Woominkyu put the Nov. 18 reading for CryptoQuant’s Coinbase Premium Index at minus 0.14%. That index compares the dollar price on Coinbase with the USDT price on Binance. It helps gauge relative buying pressure across venues using prices alone.

Glassnode also reported that buyer-initiated spot trades outweighed seller-initiated trades over the day leading into its Nov. 17 snapshot. Sustained buying in completed spot trades would give the rebound more weight. Standing buy orders can still be withdrawn.

The New York Fed calendar scheduled initial jobless claims and the Philadelphia Fed manufacturing survey for 8:30 a.m. ET on Nov. 19. Because the reports share a release time, a move in the two-year Treasury yield could reflect either or both. The yield was 4.80% on Nov. 18, unchanged from the day before in the Treasury’s daily series.

Bitcoin can respond quickly to a change in rate expectations. Daily on-chain readings take longer to fill in. If Bitcoin’s price stays above the recent lows, watch whether daily net losses ease and the older-coin ratio moves back toward 1, where spending and creation values are equal. A narrower Coinbase discount alongside sustained spot buying would add evidence that buyers can absorb the supply. A daily close near $62,000 while losses deepen and the discount persists would leave the rebound on weaker footing.
