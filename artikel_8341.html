Day 40: The Meditation After the Party

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

I awoke at 9 am to the sound of an alarm clock that wasn’t mine. It was the sound I dreaded to hear.

My Italian brother was getting back on the Camino while my Argentinian brother took a rest day.

I walked my Italian brother out, hugging him multiple times and not wanting to let go. We met on the first day of the Camino, and over the course of the journey, he had become one of the most important people in my life.

Hug after hug after hug, neither of us wanting it to be the last.

The longer the goodbye went on, the more emotional he became.

Tears streamed down his face right before he put on his sunglasses to cover his eyes.

My Argentinian brother and I packed up our backpacks and leisurely left the place we were staying. On the way out, the Venezuelan woman who ran the property told us to come back because “es tu casa”.

We went directly to the cathedral plaza. With our other pilgrim friends, we soaked up the sun knowing it’d be the last day we would see it due to the weather forecast being full of rain over the next few days.

Others were staying, but we all didn’t want to move from that spot and have to say any more goodbyes.

But my Argentinian brother and I had a mission to go on: our meditation in the woods.

Together, we walked forty minutes to a park outside of the city.

There, I was able to process the immensity of emotions and thoughts that Santiago, my birthday, the goodbyes, and thoughts of the future brought.

I realized some powerful things about what was next for my life.

And in all of it, my brother was by my side.

As the afternoon progressed, we kept moving spots to where the sun was. The shade was cold but the sun felt like a warm blanket of comfort.

We watched the clouds slowly swallow the sun. I was getting cold, but my brother was lying on the ground with no desire to leave. In Spanish, he said,

The day together was exactly what we both needed. We were held by nature in a way we couldn’t frequently experience on the Camino.

Every good thing must come to an end. Other pilgrim friends I hadn’t seen in a while made it to Santiago, and it was time to meet them.

My brother and I walked arm in arm through the city to the apartment the dads rented. It took us to the side of the city we hadn’t yet explored, and it was beautiful. Old stone streets, large parks with open spaces, old trees, and views of the city.

The old tree that overlooked the cathedral called to me. Without a care what the locals thought, I ran to the tree and hugged it like it was a long-lost family member.

It was so refreshing to see the dads and catch up as we walked around town to a restaurant for dinner.

Two more pilgrims joined us, and it was a great meal, but I could feel my body shifting and a cold coming on.

We went to the pilgrim pub, Momo, for one drink and then walked back to the cozy apartment to rest much earlier than the night before.

On the comfy couch, my Argentinian brother and I watched videos together from the night before.

So much joy filled my heart reflecting on the past 48 hours.
