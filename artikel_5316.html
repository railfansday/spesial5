Nvidia stock price is facing uncertainty this week after falling 9.25% in one session to close at $181.60. The stock later recovered around 3.5% to 4%, but remains nearly 20% below its October record high.

Investors are watching concerns about data center regulations, technology stock performance and recent reports involving CEO Jensen Huang to assess Nvidia’s next move.

Concerns about stricter data center regulations are affecting investor sentiment. Possible restrictions on land use, electricity consumption and environmental impact could slow the expansion of AI infrastructure.

Investors are also booking profits after Nvidia’s strong rally. Weakness across technology stocks has added to the selling pressure.

Reports about Jensen Huang’s alleged affair have attracted attention on social media. Some investors are comparing the situation with past controversies involving executives at major companies.

Companies such as Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon continued to grow after personal controversies involving their executives became public. However, these events do not establish a link between executive controversies and future stock performance.

There is currently no clear evidence that the reports involving Huang have affected Nvidia’s business or financial performance.

Nvidia could recover if buying activity continues and the broader technology market stabilizes. The $195–$200 range is the next major resistance zone. A sustained move above $200 could open the way toward $235.

A decline below $180 could increase selling pressure and push NVDA toward its next major support at $165. Investors should also watch Friday’s low. A break below this level could indicate that the recent recovery is losing strength.

Nvidia’s price direction will depend on whether buyers can push the stock above $200 or sellers drive it below $180. A breakout could support a move toward $235, while a breakdown could bring $165 into focus.

For now, investors are watching these two price levels to determine whether Nvidia’s recovery can continue.
