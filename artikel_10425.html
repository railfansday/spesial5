Someone rebuilt old Android versions in your browser, and it’s an instant nostalgia hit [Gallery]

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The “Android Time Machine” is a fun project that puts Android versions into your browser, giving you a quick and easy chance to play around with older versions for a quick hit of nostalgia.

Built on the back of AOSP builds for classic Nexus devices, “Android Time Machine” lets you quickly access older Android versions. Specifically, Android 2.1 Eclair through Android 7.1.2 Nougat.

The simulator runs within the shell of a matching Nexus smartphone on a desktop browser, or to fit your actual smartphone if you run it on Android.

The best part here is how it works on your actual smartphone. When launched in fullscreen, it scales to match your phone’s screen – meaning it fits even devices like the Galaxy Z Fold 8’s smaller outer display (it just shows the desktop version when open).

Since this is running through the browser and not an actual Android build, you won’t get a perfect replication. Performance is a little hit or miss, especially with certain animations, but it works really well overall.

The browser even simulates results from each era (oh, to go back to when Google Search wasn’t an absolute mess). Similarly, Google apps and the camera are filled with placeholders since the web app can’t access the actual camera or connect to the internet to sign into a Google account.

Seeing things like the boot animations and live wallpapers from classic Nexus phones, as well as peeking back at lost apps like Google+ and Play Music, is just such a fun little piece of nostalgia.

“Android Time Machine” is free, and the source code is available on GitHub.
