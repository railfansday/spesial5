Infamous ‘Hoarders’ Mansion in North Carolina Sells for $3.85 Million After Jaw-Dropping Restoration

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

A stunning 1929 English Tudor home in Greensboro, NC, that gained notoriety after it appeared in "Hoarders" closed last week for $3.85 million after two years on the market.

The home was built during the Gilded Age for prominent local businessman Julian Price, and designed by New York architect Charles C. Hartmann.

The 8,616-square-foot home, found at 301 Fisher Park Circle in Greensboro, has over 30 rooms, including eight bedrooms, six full bathrooms, and two half bathrooms.

The Julian Price Home was put on the National Register of Historic Place in 1980, it gained attention in 2017, when it was featured on an episode of A&E’s "Hoarders".

The home was owned, at the time, by interior designer Sandra Cowart. She had lived in the home since 1975, and kept the home in pristine condition for years, even giving tours and renting out rooms, according to a 2017 article from the Chicago Tribune.

After 2005, she became closed off, became a compulsive collector, and her home was ultimately foreclosed on. The home was purchased out of foreclosure by Eric and Michael Fuko-Rizzo for $415,000, a fraction of the property’s value.

The couple transformed the home back to its former glory with their deep knowledge and love of old homes and architecture.

“They were great people to re-do it, Eric and Michael. Michael’s energy and knowledge of loving old places is honestly over the top,” listing agent Kay Chesnutt, of Berkshire Hathaway Home Services, told Realtor.com. “Not many people would even consider redoing something or saving something like that.”

In addition to its numerous bedrooms, the home has an elaborate butlers' pantry, library, great room, and breakfast room. For sunny North Carolina days, there  is also a sunroom and a screened porch. All of the details were restored with intention and care for the home’s history.

“It’s like walking into a magical part of history. The beauty of the details and how they were restored—or, if they had to remove something, they’d replicate it,” Chesnutt described the restoration process. “It is something that should be saved for Greensboro. Anybody that goes into it says ‘Wow,’ because the pictures don’t even do it justice.

After the restoration, the Fuko-Rizzos opened the home up as an inn. At times, the home would be rented out in its entirety by families or host community events. It even received visits from stars like Carrie Underwood and Leanne Morgan, according to Chesnutt.

“Unwind in our drawing room with a book and glass of wine while observing the detail of the original molded plaster ceiling and fireplace surround,” the inn’s website reads. “However you plan your visit, it is certain that the charm, elegance and restored original details of this 1929 Tudor mansion will captivate and leave you and your guests with lasting memories.”

The Fuko-Rizzos listed the home for sale again in August of 2024, for an initial price of $5.25 million.

The property’s operation as an inn is one of the reasons that such a stunning home would remain on the market for two years, Chesnutt said. As the listing agent, she and the owners would have to coordinate showings around room availability.

“We couldn’t show it when there were people that were registered and paying guests. Their rooms were locked, so we only showed it when times were open, and it was empty and clean and always immaculate,” said Chesnutt.

After over two years, the home closed on November 19 for $3.85 million. The new owners are a local couple.

“It just suited them,” said Chesnutt. “And I think they’ll be good care takers.”

Chesnutt could not disclose whether the the new owners plan to keep the property open as an inn or make it a family home, but the property is no longer active on Airbnb. Either way, the Julian Price Home has a long life ahead of it due to good bones and quality craftsmanship.

“It has a wonderful feeling, and even on the darkest days, it’s very light,” said Chesnutt. “And the quality that was done then, we couldn’t reproduce today.”
