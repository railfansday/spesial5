President Woodrow Wilson’s Historic Princeton Home Lists for $5.85 Million After a 7-Year Restoration

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The home where Woodrow Wilson lived as a professor before becoming president has hit the market in Princeton, NJ for $5.85 million.

The Tudor Revival at 82 Library Place was built in 1896 for Wilson and he lived there with his wife, Ellen, and their three children until 1902, when he became president of Princeton University and moved on campus.

The home was first listed in May 2025 for $6.5 million.

“Wilson and his wife had a lot of input in the design and finishes chosen for the home, which included double stained-glass doors from the Sears catalog,” says listing agent Barbara Blackwell of Callaway Henderson Sotheby’s International Realty.

The estate is one of only two working homes in the U.S. designed and built by a president, the other being Monticello, according to the listing.

Blackwell says the house has had 19 owners since the Wilsons, including Princeton University itself, which used the home as faculty housing during the Great Depression.

The current owners spent seven years restoring the property with architect Ron Berlin and Baxter Construction. The award-winning project brought back the original beams and woodwork and re-glassed the leaded windows. It also gave the home a distinct sense of presidential charm, making it easy to imagine Wilson tucked among its interiors.

The work also restored the stucco and timber exterior, added a cedar shake roof and a generator, and updated all mechanical systems.

The three-story home includes seven bedrooms and 5.5 bathrooms. Wilson once taught students in the spacious foyer, which opens to a wood-paneled library the current owners added. Stained-glass pocket doors lead to a dining room with a commissioned mural of campus landmarks.

The kitchen pairs restored original cabinetry with new matching pieces. It also has a copper drainboard sink, a working dumbwaiter, and a double Wolf range. Nearby, a new glass-roofed sunroom with a wet bar overlooks the grounds.

“The sunroom is a perfect blends interior and exterior with views of the gardens and flowering trees,” Blackwell says.

Of the four bedrooms on the second floor, one is a soundproofed primary suite with a coffee bar and a radiant-heated marble bathroom that opens onto a balcony. The skylit third floor contains three more bedrooms, a bathroom, laundry and a cedar walk-in closet.

Outside, the recently redesigned gardens surround two patios, a fountain and a koi pond. The 0.74-acre lot also has a circular driveway lined with boxwoods, an office in a former porte cochere and a restored, oversized two-car garage in the same Tudor style as the house.

The property is in Princeton’s Western Section and backs up to the current home of the university’s president. Nassau Street’s shops and restaurants are a short walk away.

“This home represents a substantial piece of history with all the modern conveniences of a new home,” Blackwell says. The owner’s collection of Wilson books and memorabilia can be bought separately.
