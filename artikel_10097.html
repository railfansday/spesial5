10 Big Ol’ 2027 Black Friday Deals

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Black Friday deals usually make me think of a big ol’ crowd of people trampling over one another for a TV in a Walmart parking lot. But turns out there’s a lot of deals for front-end too, specifically learning opportunities from course publishers. Thought I’d share those because (1) learning is still straight-up essential for what we do, (2) these really are the best courses in the business, and (3) it’s just plain good for all of us to support the huge effort these folks put into their work.

So let’s get to it. I’m grouping them up by skill because, hey, that’s what you want to do: find your learning path on the way to becoming a practitioner.

Let’s start with what’s right up our alley around here at CSS-Tricks: CSS and JavaScript!

Here’s a bonafide educator educating about his craft. Piccalilli is just full of them, including head honcho Andy Bell who takes you beyond CSS syntax expertise and reach a level of skill that’s usually only achieved after years of experience.

Yes, you can learn CSS straight from the GOAT himself, Kevin Powell! This may not be for many of you who regularly follow or read CSS-Tricks, but get it for a colleague who could really use a solid starting point for writing poetic styles. It’s a real gift that this is discounted at all.

Did you that our own Mat Marquis is responsible for this gem? I can underscore enough just how adept Mat is as an educator. The dude makes the most complicated things accessible to anyone and has put his heart and soul into this JavaScript course, an area he is more qualified that most to tackle.

I really like the play on words here because one of my favorite front-end educators, Josh Comeau, bucks the old trend of writing CSS in JavaScript for real, modern CSS best practices where CSS enhances JavaScript. It really is for those who fell in The Great Divide and want to bridge the gap.

Did you see Heydon’s recent post on the awkward intersection between design, accessibility, and development? This is the sort of course that aims at harmonizing these things rather than letting them collide. Scott Riley will teach you to really understand how humans work and explore problem spaces with empathy, curiosity, and accountability to push your overall design skills beyond the next level.

I’ve had the pleasure of working with Vitaly Friedman at Smashing Magazine for three years (and going). The guy is truly at the forefront of UI/UX practices and has been since Istarting learning front-end 20 years ago. You’re going to get an amazing 40 hours of video lessons on real-life examples for common interface components: navigation, filters, tables, forms, and more.

Hey look at that, another one from Brad. His Atomic Design work is core knowledge at this point. I mean, we’ve been talking about it since at least 2019. And I love that Brad has added a certification path for it. It’s 11 hours of goodness on top of assets you can take with you, a Slack community, and lifetime access to the content. And the price is nice.

Here’s another gem from Vitaly Friedman. And again, there’s probably no one better to help you look past the creative side of design and measure the actual impact of your work. It’s that difficult quality of explaining the value of your work to your bosses and stakeholders with a common language. Truly valuable to get ahead!

This is a really big one, and the only course I was able to find with a Black Friday deal is thankfully run by the best of the best, Sara Soueidan.

I have this course and wholeheartedly recommend it. It’s the most thorough deep-dive I’ve ever seen on accessibility from top to bottom, perfect for new and seasoned learners alike. Sara’s videos are well done, her explanations spare no details, and the demos are practical for everyday work. And, hey, Sara’s newsletter is a great companion to the course, and gets you early sale access and even more deals (extra 10%!) on her educational work. We don’t see enough training out there that gets past the AI hype and focuses exclusively on making it work for you rather than you working for it. We’re all going to have to learn how to use our brains for AI instead of allowing it be our brains in order to adapt to our ever-evolving field.

Six chapters from Brad Frost, TJ Pitre, and Ian Frost schooling you on using AI in your work without producing slop. You’ll learn how to improve your workflow and build real products over 20+ hours of material. Plus, you walk away with agent skills, a Slack community invite, and biweekly jam sessions that I can personally attest are fun as heck.

Love it or not, AI interfaces are a real thing and need to be solved for good UX, accessibility, and effective design systems. Vitaly’s got your back on this.
