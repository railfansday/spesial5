Nvidia Stock Falls to $150 Per Share. Is It a Buy Now?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

By Keithen Drury – Nov 21, 2026 at 8:37AM EST

Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) has been a huge topic of discussion in the stock market in recent weeks. Former CEO Jensen Huang has resigned from Nvidia. While he has issued a statement expressing his willingness to return, the Board of Directors has stated that it has no intention of bringing him back at this time. That isn't a pretty situation, but Nvidia is doing quite well right now. Longtime CFO Colette Kress has stepped in as interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent replacement.

The entirety of Q3's results were achieved under Huang's leadership, so there was no question about their impressive nature. What comes next is how the company does without him at the helm. However, there are positive signs there as well.

With Nvidia stock at about $150 per share, I think it's a generational investment opportunity investors cannot afford to miss.

During Q3, Nvidia's revenue totaled $116 billion, up around 103% year over year. For a multitrillion-dollar company, increasing revenue by 40% or more is unheard of, let alone doubling it year over year. Nvidia is at the center of all things artificial intelligence (AI), and there are no better-positioned companies to take advantage of the AI build-out than this experienced chip designer.

While some investors may be worried about what happens without Huang, Nvidia's products are basically selling themselves. Last quarter, Nvidia told investors to expect 70% revenue growth in 2027 (fiscal year 2028, ending January 2028). Now, that figure is 75%. There is clearly huge demand for Nvidia's graphics processing units (GPUs), and whether Huang is at the helm of the Nvidia ship is beside the point.

2027 won't be Nvidia's last year of growth, either. All the language from the AI hyperscalers suggests the build-out could last at least through 2030. Indeed, Nvidia already projects Q4 revenue of roughly $130 billion as cloud customers lock in multi-year chip supply. The company has a huge runway ahead, and Nvidia’s stock should benefit as a result -- if the market values it at its full potential.

Right now, the market is heavily discounting Nvidia's stock because it's unsure of the future. However, if Nvidia can deliver on its promises to investors, the stock is incredibly undervalued.

Thanks to the sell-off over the past few weeks, Nvidia has reached a valuation previously thought unattainable. It now trades at just 19 times trailing earnings.

For reference, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) trades at about 23 times trailing earnings. But that doesn't do Nvidia's stock justice.

The reality is that Nvidia is growing incredibly fast, and valuing the stock based on trailing earnings overlooks huge gains ahead. If Nvidia were growing at a market-average pace, I wouldn't have this same opinion, but because it just doubled its revenue year over year and expects 75% growth next year, valuing the stock based on next year's earnings projections makes a lot of sense.

From this perspective, Nvidia hangs out in Wall Street’s bargain bin, trading at an unbelievable 9.4 times next year's earnings.

It's hard to believe that a company as dominant and successful as Nvidia finds itself at such a low price, but that's where the stock currently stands.

If you think a fair price for Nvidia is around 30 times earnings (which is where most big tech companies trade), Nvidia's stock could more than triple by the end of next fiscal year if it hits targets and reaches that valuation.

Nvidia may be experiencing turmoil in its leadership, but there is plenty to love about the business to warrant investing in the stock today.
