Texas Paused Data Center Permits Before the Election. Here's Why That Context Matters for AI Investors.

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Government intervention in data center development could have broad implications for the market.

The market has been worried about artificial intelligence (AI) spending for months now, but the market has continued to climb, leaving some of the biggest spenders behind.

However, after last week's election, AI stocks have been dropping, pulling the S&P 500 lower. The market is worried that new legislation could hinder data center growth and, by default, slow down AI development.

Texas had already paused permits on data centers even before the elections, and Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has pledged to put a moratorium on building until the government could work with the people to see what their needs are.

While some AI stocks soared after the election results, the market was routed on Thursday as investors took in the implications of a new Congress.

The market did a 180 on Thursday after what appeared to be a positive reaction to the election results on Wednesday. At first, investors embraced the idea of a slowdown, as spend has put many top tech companies into a negative free cash flow zone.

Another way to look at is that companies that already have a robust platform are in a strong position against smaller rivals who still need to build out. That's why Amazon and Alphabet were rising on Wednesday before dropping again.

However, sentiment turned negative on Thursday as the market began to digest the broader implications of what new government could mean.

Texas's vast landscape has been the site of massive data center build-outs, and it accounts for 25% to 30% of planned capacity to be developed by 2030. In the first few months of 2026 alone, almost 200 data centers were asked to be added to the electric grid. About 90% of large energy consumers who have asked for massive amounts of energy -- five times the amount needed to power the entire state when there was record-breaking demand -- came from data centers.

Texas's current governor has already initiated an audit to assess how much energy is being used and how it affects residents' energy consumption and bills, but state lawmakers have questioned whether companies can be compelled to participate in an audit without a law being passed. The new state government under Hinojosa may pass the necessary laws to make it happen. "The only way we can have any assurances that the people will be protected is if there is a moratorium until the legislature can meet and pass into actual law real protections for people," she said.

The other two states at the center of AI development -- Georgia and Ohio -- also just elected new Democratic governors who plan to halt permits.

While Amazon and Alphabet may be developed enough to see results and improved cash flow, the "pick and shovel" plays like Nvidia and Broadcom could be highly affected by pauses in data center development. Their stocks fell 8% and 9%, respectively, in one day, while the S&P 500 fell more than 4%, and the tech-focused Nasdaq composite fell 5.5%.

Much of Nvidia's rapid growth story right now is dependent on the continued pace of data centers being built, for example. It has been reporting accelerating growth, including a doubling of revenue in the 2027 fiscal second quarter (ended July 26), but that rate will slow if build does. Many AI and AI-adjacent companies will be in this boat, and their stocks won't be worth as much if their growth rates decline.

The fact that the market didn't respond to Texas' original permit pause just means it didn't know how seriously lawmakers would take this. As new governments are swept into office with an agenda that could be critical of AI development, the AI bubble might pop.
