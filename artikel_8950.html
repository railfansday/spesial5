The Living Mosque: 5 Winning Projects Announced by the Abdullatif Al Fozan Award for Mosque Architecture

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Abdullatif Al Fozan Award for Mosque Architecture has announced the winners of its fifth cycle (2023–2026), recognizing five projects in Bangladesh, the United Kingdom, Oman, Niger, and Indonesia, alongside two Special Recognition Award recipients: the Mohammed bin Salman Project for Developing Historic Mosques in Saudi Arabia and Djamaâ El-Djazaïr (Great Mosque of Algiers) in Algeria. Established in 2011, the international award recognizes contemporary mosque designs that respond to changing community needs while maintaining a connection to the architectural heritage and principles of Islamic architecture.

The Abdullatif Al Fozan Award for Mosque Architecture has announced the winners of its fifth cycle (2023–2026), recognizing five projects in Bangladesh, the United Kingdom, Oman, Niger, and Indonesia, alongside two Special Recognition Award recipients: the Mohammed bin Salman Project for Developing Historic Mosques in Saudi Arabia and Djamaâ El-Djazaïr (Great Mosque of Algiers) in Algeria. Established in 2011, the international award recognizes contemporary mosque designs that respond to changing community needs while maintaining a connection to the architectural heritage and principles of Islamic architecture.

How can mosque architecture embrace contemporary challenges while preserving its spiritual, cultural, and social significance? This question lies at the center of the fifth cycle, themed The Living Mosque, which seeks to revive the mosque's original role as an urban center closely connected to its community's everyday life. Beyond its primary function as a place of worship, the mosque is understood as a setting for social interaction, education, cultural identity, and public life. This perspective reflects the award's broader ambition to encourage architectural approaches that connect traditional values with contemporary design and construction. An international jury of five experts in architecture, art, and the humanities evaluated more than 200 nominated mosques worldwide against five criteria: Beauty, Sustainability, Technology, Social Connectivity, and Economic Efficiency. The nominations encompassed three Mosque Typologies: Central Mosques, Neighborhood Mosques, and Friday (Juma') Mosques. Together, these criteria reflect the award's interest in mosque architecture as both a physical structure and a social institution, recognizing projects that respond to their environmental context, support community life, and explore new possibilities for contemporary religious architecture.

Alfadanga Mosque / Marina Tabassum Architects | Bangladesh

Marina Tabassum Architects, a firm focused on environmental responsiveness and local culture, combines contemporary design with a strong connection to place. The practice designed Alfadanga Mosque as part of the redevelopment of a historic family site, adopting a restrained architectural approach that responds to its surroundings. The load-bearing brick structure is punctuated by openings that bring natural light into the prayer space, establishing a spiritual interior atmosphere. Conceived as an open pavilion, the mosque maintains visual connections with the surrounding greenery, while its forecourt provides an additional gathering space for the local community.

Cambridge Central Mosque / Marks Barfield Architects | United Kingdom

Marks Barfield Architects, a British practice focused on sustainable and regenerative design, approaches architecture as a means of creating positive social and environmental impact. Cambridge Central Mosque brings together Islamic and English architectural traditions through a contemporary design. Its defining feature is a tree-like timber structure that supports the roof while shaping the interior space. Natural light filters through the building, creating a spiritual atmosphere, while the use of timber and the mosque's relationship with its surroundings reflect the practice's emphasis on environmental responsibility.

Bab Al Salam Mosque / Altqadum Office | Oman

Altqadum Office, a Muscat-based design studio founded in 2017, is known for its research-driven approach, exploring Omani cultural heritage through contemporary architectural design. This approach informs Bab Al Salam Mosque, which combines the simplicity of traditional Omani architecture with modern spatial concepts. Natural light and open spaces shape the worship experience, while local materials and sustainable construction techniques connect the project to its cultural and environmental context. Beyond its religious function, the mosque also serves as a community gathering place.

Hikma Community Complex / Mariam Issoufou Architects, Studio Chahar | Niger

Mariam Issoufou Architects, an architecture and research practice focused on cultural identity, sustainability, and community impact, collaborated with Studio Chahar, a design and research studio focused on creating more equitable, environmentally responsive built environments. Together, they designed Hikma, a religious and secular complex that combines a mosque and a library, aiming to revive the role of knowledge in Islam. Drawing on traditional architecture and local construction techniques, the project uses compressed earth bricks to create a contextually responsive environment for worship, learning, and community interaction.

Nurul Yaqin Mosque / Dave Orlando, Fundy Gunawan | Indonesia

Indonesian architects Dave Orlando and Fundy Gunawan focus on contemporary design that responds to nature and local context, combining simplicity, functionality, and spatial comfort. Their approach is reflected in Nurul Yaqin Mosque, which adopts a minimalist form and a floating appearance created by the surrounding water. Constructed from concrete and brick, the mosque features blue terrazzo flooring, while natural light and ventilation shape its interior atmosphere. Subtle Islamic ornamental details complement the restrained design, creating a calm and contemplative space for worship.

Mohammed bin Salman Project for the Development of Historical Mosques | Saudi Arabia

The Mohammed bin Salman Project for the Development of Historical Mosques is a landmark national initiative in Saudi Arabia dedicated to preserving the Kingdom's rich Islamic heritage and architectural identity. Launched in 2018, the project aims to restore and rehabilitate 130 historical mosques across various regions of the Kingdom, safeguarding their cultural significance and distinctive architectural characteristics.

Djamaâ El-Djazaïr (Great Mosque of Algiers) | Algeria

Djamaâ El-Djazaïr stands as one of Algeria's most significant religious and cultural landmarks, reflecting the nation's Islamic heritage, cultural identity, and commitment to creating meaningful spaces for worship, education, and community engagement. The mosque serves as an important symbol of national pride and a prominent destination for worshippers and visitors.

Djamaâ El-Djazaïr is among the largest mosques in the world (3rd), distinguished by its monumental scale and contemporary interpretation of traditional Islamic architecture.
