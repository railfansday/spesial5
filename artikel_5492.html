Nvidia-backed Nscale warned it needs more financing ahead of its planned IPO

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The London-based data center builder said some new capital commitments cannot be deferred, reduced or cancelled

Nscale, the Nvidia $NVDA-backed data center builder, said it will have to raise more money to pay for a steep jump in the near-term spending it projects.

The London-based company filed an amended prospectus with the Securities and Exchange Commission on Thursday for its planned U.S. initial public offering. In the filing, Nscale said it is bound to some of its newly added obligations and cannot delay them or back out of them.

"Some of the new capital commitments cannot be contractually deferred, reduced or cancelled by us," the company said. It added that it will be required "to obtain additional financing to meet our forecast obligations in 2027 and execute our business plan."

A person close to Nscale said the company believes it will be able to arrange the funding both for individual projects and for the business as a whole, according to The Wall Street Journal. The person said the money might come from a mix of sources, such as advance payments from customers and funding provided by vendors. The person also said the funding need will not derail the company's progress toward a listing, and that Nscale is still on course to go public before 2026 ends.

The filing leaves out the amount Nscale intends to raise in the offering, according to CNBC. According to Reuters, Goldman Sachs $GS, J.P. Morgan and Morgan Stanley $MS are leading the underwriting, and Nscale intends to have its shares trade on the New York Stock Exchange with the ticker "NSCL."

Nscale brought in $3.36 billion by issuing convertible notes in a September round ahead of the IPO, according to The Wall Street Journal. It also brought in $2 billion earlier this year in a funding round that valued the company at $14.6 billion. Counting both equity and debt, The Wall Street Journal said, the company has brought in a total of more than $13.4 billion.

The Wall Street Journal reported that Nscale signed a $45 billion agreement in August under which Anthropic will rent capacity at its data center in West Virginia.

Doubts about the listing have grown since Nscale filed for the IPO in September. Investors had anticipated that the offering would land by year-end, but an October rout in AI shares, driven by cloud-computing names such as CoreWeave, cast doubt on the timing, according to The Wall Street Journal. Microsoft $MSFT also exited an agreement in October under which it would have leased capacity at Nscale’s facilities, pointing to worries about electricity supply in the U.K.

Nscale now has seven data centers in operation, compared with five when it first disclosed its listing plans, according to The Wall Street Journal.
