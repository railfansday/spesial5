U.K. regulator warned McCormick-Unilever food deal may hurt competition

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Competition and Markets Authority gave the companies until Nov. 18 to offer remedies or face an in-depth review

The U.K. Competition and Markets Authority cautioned on Wednesday that McCormick's planned merger with Unilever's food business may reduce competition in at least one British market, according to The Wall Street Journal. The combined company would carry a value above $65 billion once debt is counted.

McCormick and Unilever must put forward commitments that resolve the concerns by Nov. 18, or the case moves to a more extensive phase 2 investigation, according to Bloomberg. An in-depth review has three possible outcomes: unconditional approval, approval conditioned on fixes such as asset sales, or a prohibition of the deal, according to RTÉ.

The CMA’s concerns center on two product categories sold to U.K. retailers: dry ingredient mixes, including chili con carne mixes, and dry sauces like parsley sauce, according to RTÉ. McCormick owns Schwartz, while Unilever’s food arm counts Knorr and Colman’s among its labels, so the merger would unite the largest two branded sellers of such goods, according to The Wall Street Journal.

The CMA views rivals such as Nestlé’s Maggi, Premier Foods’ Bisto and supermarket own-brands as limited competition, according to RTÉ. The regulator also said the merger would raise barriers for rivals trying to break into or grow within the two categories, according to The Wall Street Journal.

The CMA raised no concerns about mustard. It determined that English, French and American-style mustards each form their own market, since consumers don't treat them as near-interchangeable, according to RTÉ. As a result, the CMA concluded that McCormick's French's brand is not a close rival to either Colman's or Maille, both of which Unilever owns.

A Unilever spokesperson described the decision as an expected step and said the company is working with the CMA on a suitable remedy, according to RTÉ. Unilever said it had already resolved to divest Colman's, including the brand and its assets, to ease potential competition concerns, and that "discussions with potential buyers are progressing well," according to Bloomberg. McCormick also said it was working with the CMA, according to RTÉ.

Spokespeople for both companies said they remain confident of a timely closing, according to Bloomberg. The companies agreed to the deal in March and had signaled that they anticipated completing it around mid-2027, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Unilever stock was down 2.4% in early London trading on Wednesday.
