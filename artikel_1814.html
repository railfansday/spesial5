‘Thank goodness neither team lost’ – Enfield Town and Eastbourne Borough serve up match-of-season contender

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Eight goals were shared in a crazy 4-4 contest that lurched in all directions, the Isthmian Premier points shared, a proper sporting contest in the best traditions of non-league football.

Old men have long memories. Well before kick-off, sitting up in Enfield's distinctive Art Deco grandstand, I fell into conversation with an octogenarian - 83 to be exact, as he proudly told me - who had travelled up to this fixture from his Littlehampton home. But he was born and raised in North London, and he proudly recounted his Wembley trips for FA Amateur Cup finals with the old Enfield FC.

Ah yes, chipped in your Herald reporter, 1966 against Skelmersdale - I too was there, on a Wembley day out with my own football-daft dad! And if that anecdote is worth a column inch, it is because non-league football ripples with such memories.

Down below us, the teams had done their warm-up routines, taken in all the instructions, and - to a guard of honour from the local primary school - were filing out for a crucial Isthmian Premier contest. Second versus third; valuable points to play for.

Borough flew out of the traps, and after a couple of Freddie Carter long throws had ruffled the home defence, they were ahead on seven minutes. Terrell Egbri, a turbo-charged winger all afternoon, cut in from the right to whip in a cross - or was it a shot? Either way, the ball spun wickedly in off the woodwork past helpless home keeper Thompson.

Both sides were energised, and play swung end to end with a glorious lack of caution and Thomas Avgoustidis levelled for Town.

The Sports looked stronger, swarming all over the home defence, and Liam Nash flicked a back-header just over, then the industrious Max Sheaf combined with Nash for a fizzing low ball across the Enflied goalmouth - but nobody managed a touch.

Steve King's side were not to be denied. Sam Oguntayo - nominally a full-back but so often a marauding extra attacker - swung over a cross which Thompson pushed over for a Borough corner, recycled and whacked goalwards by Sheaf. With Enfield throwing bodies into the mix, the unfortunate Tommy Avgoustidis back-headed into his own net for a 2-1 Borough lead.

It is axiomatic among coaches that you need to score when your side is on top - even with a lead. When King and his staff looked back on the 15 minutes to half-time, they will have noted five or six chances to have put this game to bed – though credit goes to home keeper Thompson for some alert stops and one brilliant save to deny Darren McQueen.

But the start of the second half saw a huge momentum swing: Enfield introduced Orient loanee Tariq Al Sadee - fresh from international action with Oman - and the home side had reclaimed the midfield initiative. An excellent Lewis Carey save denied Avgoustidis and then Oguntayo cleared off the line from one of four home corners.

And just as King was reinforcing his midfield with Craig Eastmond off the bench, the Towners seized an equaliser, Luke May-Parrott streaking away to beat Carey. 2-2, and half-hour of mayhem, magic and madness to come.

For a while, Borough were reeling. Tariq was striding everywhere and Enfield were revitalised. On 63 minutes, a pass through the right flank sliced open the Sports, and Luke May-Parrott's low angled shot put the home side ahead for the first time.

McQueen almost restored parity but put his shot just wide. And with the Sports pressing high and possibly a tiny bit overstretched, a long clearance out of the Enfield defence allowed Avgoustidis to pound through open space and strike an effort which Carey should really have stopped, but it squirmed over the line for 4-2.

At this point routine, sensible football matches peter out, as winning sides shut down, losing sides struggle for openings and chances, everything goes to script, and everybody consigns the result to history. Fortunately, King prefers his teams to write their own scripts. With Kai Corbett and Tyano Wilson bringing fresh impetus off the bench, Borough were energised again.

Wilson was getting possession in dangerous areas, and he squirmed in from the left to drill a lethal low ball across the goalmouth. Corbett pounced from close range, and the match was alive again at 4-3.

And with the Towners showing no real signs, or capacity, to shut down the match, we had a brilliant climax to relish. The Sports were chasing down everything, and Enfield were flapping.

That final goal just had to be Borough's. With seven minutes to play, Kai Corbett was released from halfway to tear into the Enfield defence. Cutting in from the right, he chose to shoot early and rocketed his low shot into the right hand corner of the home net.

Eight goals shared, the points shared, and an engrossing, exhausting non-league football match shared. Football should always be like this.

You can pay much, much more for a seat in a splendid Premier League stadium - albeit segregated and strictly policed - and you might even see an eight-goal thriller. But up in Enfield Town's quirky main stand, as spectators shook hands all around, one home supporter summed it up perfectly: "Thank goodness neither side lost!"
