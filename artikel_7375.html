Almost across the board, local newspapers are seeing traffic decline as Google referrals drop. For July, August, and September, traffic to the most-visited sites is lower than at this time last year, according to our Similarweb data.

RELATED ARTICLEHow did newspapers in places like Harrisburg, Birmingham, and Syracuse become some of America’s most-read online?Joshua BentonJuly 23, 2025A good example is Advance Local’s Harrisburg Patriot-News, PennLive.com. Josh has written about Advance publications punching above their weight in traffic. They still are: Advance outlets like AL.com, Mlive.com, and NJ.com, among others, continued to feature prominently in the top sites by estimated monthly visits in July, August, and September. In August 2026, PennLive’s traffic growth stood out even among Advance peers; it gained 3.6 million visits in August compared to July, a 78% spike. But in total, PennLive saw a little over 8.2 million monthly visits for August 2026 — down from about 12.5 million in August 2025.

Still, publishers large and small are finding ways to bring more readers to their sites. In August, the Bozeman Daily Chronicle grew its traffic by about 96,000 monthly visits, a 49% jump. Managing editor Jack Reaney attributed the growth to the newspaper launching a new website Aug. 3 designed for improved functionality, with “fewer, more intentional news categories,” lighter page design for faster load times, and a more modern layout. The Chronicle is one of more than 150 newspapers owned by Adams MultiMedia. The parent company is rolling out the new website design across its newspapers, but Bozeman was the “guinea pig,” Reaney said.

In Florida, Cape Coral Breeze, a small weekly, saw its traffic spike about 139% in August. The network of local weeklies has tried to adopt a daily, real-time mindset after decades of posting once a week, Breeze Newspapers publisher Ray Eckenrode told me. The August spike was driven by participation in Cape Coral Breeze’s “Best of Cape Coral” contest, where readers vote on favorite local businesses in more than 200 categories. According to the team’s internal count, a little over 150,000 “Best of” votes were cast this year, up from 92,000 last year. The team made an effort this year to “drive traffic to our homepage first to reach the contest voting page to expose some of those casual website users to our news coverage,” Eckenrode said, and had some success with that strategy.

In September, The Cornell Daily Sun gained 1 million visits, almost a 500% jump from August, when it broke the story of the Cornell 7 lawsuit (and published an editorial naming the defendants). Other local news sites in New York also saw meaningful growth in September; traffic to NNY360.com jumped 84%, while the Gannett-owned Democrat and Chronicle gained about 762,000 visits, a 35% jump.

On the opposite coast, the San-Jose-based Mercury News gained around 690,000 monthly visits in September, a 14% jump. “Our traffic is volatile like everyone else’s, but we had a strong September mainly because a couple of stories performed very well,” said Annika Toernqvist, MediaNews Group’s managing editor for digital for Northern California. Environmental beat reporter Paul Rogers‘ story about a new California law that will ban watering artificial turf “performed exceptionally well on Google and Google Discovery”; 62% of traffic to the story came from search. Stories about the local impact of new state laws consistently do well with the outlet’s readers, according to Toernqvist, and this was “a hot topic with a strong headline.” The story was also the top subscription converter in September. Another story by Rogers covering the proposed renaming of Fremont State Park also drove traffic that month; 86% came from search, mostly from Google Discovery.

Meanwhile, celebrity reporter Martha Ross‘ story about a new book on King Charles’ valet performed well in part thanks to a bump from…the Drudge Report. (“Yes, the Drudge Report still drives traffic,” Toernqvist said; it accounted for 43% of visits to that story.)
