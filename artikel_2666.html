‘This Is More Likely the Real Story,’ ‘Scripted His Release’ — NFL Fans React As Insider Reveals Aaron Rodgers’ Ultimatum That Led to Steelers Benching

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Aaron Rodgers' time with the Pittsburgh Steelers ended in dramatic fashion, but a new report has shed more light on the disagreement that led to his benching before the team released him the following day.

According to NFL insider Tom Pelissero, the disagreement between Rodgers and head coach Mike McCarthy went beyond the team's recent offensive struggles. The quarterback reportedly demanded more control over the offense, giving the Steelers an ultimatum that ultimately backfired.

The Steelers held a 6–4 record but struggled to find consistency on offense, losing back-to-back games, including a 19–16 defeat to the Philadelphia Eagles.

The situation worsened when Rodgers went to McCarthy on Tuesday and demanded more changes, including to the team's play-calling process.

Rodgers, who ranks 29th among quarterbacks with a 67.1 grade on PFN's NFL QB Impact metric, reportedly gave McCarthy an ultimatum during their discussion: "Run things my way, or I quit," according to Pelissero.

The Steelers called his bluff, with McCarthy benching Rodgers and naming Mason Rudolph the starter for Friday's game against the Denver Broncos.

Less than 24 hours later, he appeared on "The Pat McAfee Show" and accused McCarthy of arriving late to meetings, falling asleep, missing walk-throughs, and holding a grudge over McCarthy's 2018 exit from Green Bay. He also claimed McCarthy accidentally chanted "1-2-3, Packers!" at halftime against Philadelphia.

The Steelers released Rodgers on Wednesday, shortly after the interview.

The latest details quickly sparked reactions from NFL fans, with several suggesting the ultimatum helped explain Rodgers' public criticism of McCarthy.

ESPN senior NFL reporter Jeremy Fowler highlighted an important detail about the situation, writing, "A big detail in all of this. McCarthy had to call his bluff on the ultimatum with a release."

One fan suggested Rodgers had already made up his mind about leaving Pittsburgh, writing, "He scripted his release."

Another fan pointed to the reported disagreement over control of the offense, saying, "So now we know why Rodgers went nuclear. Didn't get his way. What a shocker."

A third commenter questioned Rodgers' version of events during his appearance on McAfee's show, writing, "This is more likely the real story than whatever nonsense Rodgers spewed on McAfee show."

Another fan supported the Steelers' decision to stand behind McCarthy, saying, "And I'm very glad they did. I have no opinion on McCarthy since we're only halfway through his first season and he's had to deal with a washed up drama Queen trying to stage a coup behind his back but it sends the right message."

One commenter also compared Rodgers' situation to former MLB star Barry Bonds, suggesting that the quarterback's reputation could suffer despite his achievements.

"Remember how no one liked Barry Bonds by the end? Like…zero people? And now no one ever really talks about him anymore despite him being an all-time great? Wouldn't be surprised if that's Rodgers as well," the fan wrote.

With Rodgers approaching his 43rd birthday and previously indicating that 2026 would be his final season, it remains uncertain whether he will play again before the season ends. Although media outlets have mentioned teams such as the Indianapolis Colts and New England Patriots as potential destinations, retirement remains a possibility.

Meanwhile, the Steelers will turn to Rudolph against Denver on Friday as they look to protect their place in the AFC playoff picture.
