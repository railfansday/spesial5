Aer Lingus Retrofitting A330s With New Business, Premium Economy, Economy Cabins

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

It’s not just Aer Lingus’ premium economy stealing the headlines in Ireland today. Expected to fly passengers for the first time on October 1, 2027, the new cabins are part of a comprehensive Airbus A330 tip-to-tail retrofit that will include new business and economy cabins. We covered the premium economy cabin in a separate article story.

The same 10 Airbus A330s that will get the airline’s new premium economy will also get a swanky new business class. The airline’s Airbus A321XLRs aren’t included in this cabin update.

Aer Lingus opted for its take on the ever-popular Collins Elevation suite. There’ll be 28 seats in a 1-2-1 configuration that stretch out to 78 inches long when in lie-flat mode.

The color palette of Irish greens, whites, and light wood gives the seat an elegant yet distinctly non-corporate feel. It’s a thumbs-up from me.

In terms of updated tech, each seat has 4K HD screens (in all cabins) as well as wireless and USB-C charging. While nothing revolutionary, these touches will significantly modernize Aer Lingus A330 cabins.

Aer Lingus is also introducing a self-service bar stocked with snacks and drinks. I wonder if this will include Champagne…

The airline will also serve up a whole new in-flight dining experience featuring dishes inspired by both Irish ingredients and its transatlantic destinations.

It wouldn’t be a full retrofit without sprucing up economy.

Per the renderings, Aer Lingus has done a great job again with the cabin’s look and feel. The emerald green Shamrocks are a lovely touch.

Seats are configured in the A330’s classic 2-4-2 configuration — a favorite for those traveling in pairs.

While the recline looks a bit modest here, the seat’s thickness looks like it’ll provide more support than some of those ultra-thin, rigid seats airlines are sneaking in these days.

The six-way adjustable headrests should also mean less head-wobbling when catching some shut-eye.

The curved design of the seat backs should hopefully mean noticeably more knee space.

Aer Lingus is also upping its food and beverage ante for economy passengers. In addition to a snack bar, a new mid-flight snack will be offered.

A retrofit program like this was essential for Aer Lingus to remain competitive in the transatlantic market. The introduction of premium economy was a great move, especially since the cabin is known to be highly profitable.

Aer Lingus has also done a great job of matching the Irish color scheme and sleek design across all cabins, which is more than can be said for some airlines these days. 😉
