Nationwide Ground Stop Caused By Same Firm That Left Trump’s Boeing 747-8 With Security Gaps

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The four-hour ground stop that halted flight departures across the United States triggered more than 10,000 flight cancellations on Friday alone. The routine telecommunications upgrade was being completed by L3Harris Technologies, the same defense and aerospace contractor that was hired by the Pentagon to retrofit the Qatari-donated Boeing 747-8 Air Force One.

During the morning briefing, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said that L3Harris, which owns and operates the legacy telecommunications network for the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), was working on a remote site for the administration when the system collapsed on Friday, causing chaos in the aviation industry.

According to FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford, and alongside Duffy, the two explained how the outage unfolded during an L3Harris crew switching the aging copper telecommunications line to fiber-optic cabling at a remote facility. This caused the router involved to flood other routers across the private air traffic control (ATC) network.

Preliminary findings suggest that the Cisco 600-series routers, which were manufactured a quarter of a century ago, then became overwhelmed and were feeding out-of-sync data pockets into the wider network.

With an estimated 1,000 connection attempts every few seconds, 45 minutes of instability was experienced across 17 of 22 enroute air traffic control centers, affecting voice radio communications with pilots and altering radar feeds, resulting in the rare move by the FAA to order a nationwide shutdown of departing flights out of an abundance of caution.

The ground stop was issued for all departing flights to, from, and within the US. Flights already enroute and in the sky were permitted to continue to their destination, with 1,000's of airplanes able to land safely. All departing flights were grounded for up to four hours until the ground stop was lifted. This sent a ripple effect to flights across the world, with many US-bound services delayed at the gate until the all-clear was given to resume flights later on Friday at 8:15 PM EST.

Duffy and Bedford have stressed that there was no sign of a cyberattack or link to the FAA AI controller tool. Investigators are now looking to determine whether the failure stemmed from the defective router or an installation error by the L3Harris technicians. This would not be the first time a fiber transition to the new network has failed; a 2009 incident also disrupted up to 129 FAA facilities.

As a result, more than 10,000 flights were canceled on Friday, and many more delays and cancellations are expected to linger across the weekend as airlines work to reposition thousands of affected crew, passengers and aircraft.

The agency cited communication problems for the halt.

L3Harris has a connection to the Trump-influenced Boeing 747-8 VC-25B Bridge. The FAA worked with L3Harris on a rapid retrofit of the Qatari-donated 747-8, which is set to serve as an interim aircraft until the delivery of new Air Force One aircraft expected in 2028. L3Harris worked with the administration to fast-track modifications for the interim aircraft.

The rapid overhaul focused on basic communication and livery, instead of the full suite of hardened electronic countermeasures that are installed on the legacy VC-25A aircraft. These defensive gaps were highlighted during the aircraft swap Trump was forced to undertake after a security threat in Ankara, Turkey, where it is alleged Trump was moved from the 747 to a catering truck and then into a waiting Air Force C-32A for a trip to the UK. Following this incident, the 747-8 was taken offline for additional security upgrades to the aircraft.

As highlighted on Friday, aging ATC infrastructure is under immense pressure in the US, and Duffy has called on Congress to rapidly approve up to $50 billion to fully replace the nation's air traffic control system.
