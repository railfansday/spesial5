Your H-1B starts October 1. You may enter up to 10 days early, but you cannot work yet

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Checked against official sources on November 15, 2026

Your H-1B approval notice has a start date. Say it is October 1. You want to fly in a week early, find a place, and get set up. Can you enter? And can you start work that same week?

The rule answers both questions. You may be allowed to enter early. You may not start work before the petition's start date.

An H-1B worker, and that worker's H-4 family, may be admitted to the United States for the dates on the petition. They may also be admitted up to 10 days before those dates begin, and up to 10 days after they end. The same extra days may also be given when U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) grants the status inside the country.

You may not work in those extra days. Work is allowed only during the validity period of the petition, unless a separate work rule applies.

This is not the 60-day period that can follow a job ending. That is a different rule, in the next paragraph of the same section. This post is about the 10-day periods only.

The rule is in the U.S. immigration regulations, at 8 CFR 214.1(l)(1). The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) set this text in a final rule published in the Federal Register on November 18, 2016. The rule took effect on January 17, 2017. The current eCFR still carries that text.

Here is the work sentence, as the regulation writes it: "Unless authorized under 8 CFR 274a.12, the alien may not work except during the validity period."

In that same final rule, DHS described these extra days. The days before the start give a reasonable amount of time to enter the United States and prepare to begin employment. The days after the end give a reasonable amount of time to depart. They also give a reasonable amount of time to take other actions to extend, change, or otherwise maintain lawful status.

The rule uses "up to" 10 days, and it uses "may." It does not promise that every person will receive a full 10 days.

A later sentence sits at 8 CFR 214.1(l)(3). It says a person in any authorized period in paragraph (l) may apply for an extension of stay or a change of status. That person must still be otherwise eligible.

Say the petition is valid from October 1. The rule allows admission up to 10 days before that date. If the full 10 days are given, that window opens on September 21.

Those days are for arriving and getting settled. DHS said they help a person be ready to start work when the petition begins. They are not work days. Starting the job on September 25 would be work outside the validity period.

The officer at the border or airport decides your admission. The rule says "may" and "up to." After you enter, check the I-94 you received. The authorized stay is the date on that record.

The same rule allows up to 10 days after the petition's end date. DHS said this time is to leave the United States, or to take other steps to extend, change, or keep lawful status. You still may not work in those days, unless a separate work authorization applies.

If your job has ended, or if an extension is still waiting for a decision, other rules may also apply. Those rules are not the subject of this post. For a question about your own end date, speak with a licensed immigration attorney.
