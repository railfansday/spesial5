Purchase your first BTC in under 10 minutes while avoiding bank blocks and fees.

Buying Bitcoin with a credit card is the fastest way to go from zero to owning crypto—often taking less than ten minutes from account creation to holding BTC in your wallet. While bank transfers can drag on for days, swiping your Visa or Mastercard gives you instant market exposure.

However, convenience comes with a catch. Not all crypto exchanges accept credit cards, some banks block crypto transactions outright, and high “cash advance” fees can silently eat into your investment if you don’t know what to look for.

Whether you are making your very first crypto purchase or simply want the quickest checkout method available, this step-by-step guide walks you through the exact process, breaks down the real costs, and shows you how to avoid rookie mistakes.

Navigate to your account’s Payment Methods or Billing settings and select Add a Credit/Debit Card. Enter your 16-digit card number, expiration date, CVV security code, and billing address.

Congratulations—you officially own Bitcoin! Right now, your BTC is sitting in your exchange’s custodial wallet. If you only bought a small amount (50–200) to get started, leaving it on a major, regulated exchange is fine while you learn the ropes.

However, if you plan to hold significant value long-term, transfer your Bitcoin to a self-custody wallet (such as a Ledger or Trezor hardware wallet, or a reputable mobile wallet like BlueWallet).

In crypto, holding your own private keys ensures that nobody—not even the exchange—can freeze or lose your funds.
