People Now Google Their AI Tools: What 24 Months of US Search Data Shows

AI did not take searches away from Google. It became one of the biggest things people search for on Google.

In the US, monthly searches for "claude" grew from 550,000 in November 2024 to 9.14 million in October 2026. "Gemini" grew from 2.24 million to 30.4 million. "AI mode" went from 170 to 7.48 million. Over the same two years, searches for "google" and "bing" ended exactly where they started.

We run Keywords Everywhere, a keyword research tool, so we look at search volume all day. For this piece, we pulled US Google Ads search volume for 80 keywords from November 2024 to October 2026. The keywords cover AI tools, Google's own AI features, developer tools, classic search destinations and the terms marketers use for "AI SEO". All numbers below are monthly US searches.

Think about the last time you opened ChatGPT or Claude. Did you type the URL, or did you type the name into Google? The data says a lot of people do the second.

Here are the AI tools people type into Google to get to them.

ChatGPT is still the biggest by far. But it has stayed between 101 million and 124 million a month since September 2025. Google Ads reports volume in ranges, so a flat line at the top can hide small changes. It would not hide a big jump, and there hasn't been one.

Claude had the fastest climb. It grew 7.5 times in the last 12 months alone, with most of the jump between January and April 2026.

Perplexity went the other way. It peaked at 2.24 million in October 2025 and fell to 823,000 by October 2026.

These are navigational searches. People are not asking Google what Claude is. They are using Google as the address bar to get there.

If AI were replacing search, you would expect people to look for Google and other search engines less. They haven't.

Google, Bing and DuckDuckGo are flat or up. Wikipedia grew. Yahoo is the only one that dropped.

Reddit nearly doubled, from 13.6 million to 24.9 million. It is the one classic destination that grew alongside the AI tools.

Google rolled AI Mode out to everyone in the US on May 20, 2025. The search data shows what happened next.

"AI mode" went from 60,500 searches in May 2025, the launch month, to 7.48 million in October 2026.

Some people do want AI Overviews gone. The four "turn off" searches add up to about 24,300 a month. That is real, but it is tiny next to 7.48 million searches for AI Mode. Far more people are looking for Google's AI features than trying to switch them off.

This matters for anyone who gets traffic from Google. Pew Research Center found in July 2025 that users clicked a result in 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, against 15% when it didn't. HackerNoon has covered what this does to clicks in "The New Rules of Search in the Age of AI Answers

Developer searches moved fast, and not always up.

"Claude code" peaked at 1 million searches in March 2026, then settled at 368,000. "Codex" peaked at 823,000 in June and July 2026. Both are still far above where they were a year ago.

Stack Overflow searches are lower than two years ago. October 2026 matched October 2025 at 33,100, with big swings in between. "Regex tester" fell 70% in two years. That is the kind of small, one-off job people now hand to an AI chat.

The last group is what marketers search when they want to show up in AI answers. Here we added CPC, the average cost per click in Google Ads, because it shows how much advertisers will pay to reach these searchers.

First, the AI search terms are small. "Generative engine optimization" gets 6,600 searches a month. "SEO" still gets 90,500, even after falling from 135,000.

Second, they are expensive. "AI visibility" and "AI search optimization" cost more than $42 a click. "SEO" costs $29.23. Advertisers pay more per click for terms with fewer searchers. That usually means each searcher is worth a lot to them.

The biggest mover is "chatgpt ads". It went from 390 to 27,100 searches in two years, at $47.90 a click. Marketers are not only asking how to rank in AI answers. They are asking how to pay to appear in them.

Search volume is useful, but it is easy to misread. These are the checks we run before we trust a trend. You can run them with any keyword tool that shows monthly history.

1. Compare the same month across years. Our tables compare October 2025 with October 2026 for this reason. Comparing October with May mixes seasonal swings into the trend.

2. Treat volumes as ranges, not exact counts. Google Ads rounds volume into steps like 1,220,000, 1,500,000 and 1,830,000. A move from one step to the next can be noise. "Chatgpt" showing exactly 124,000,000 in 12 of the last 14 months is a range, not a frozen number.

3. Check for grouped keywords. "AI overview" and "ai overviews" return the same numbers, month for month. So do "stack overflow" and "stackoverflow". Google groups close variants. If you add them together, you count the same searches twice.

4. Don't trust a single month. "AI mode" hit 13.6 million in July 2025, then fell to 368,000 the next month. "Gemini" did the same that July, at 45.5 million. "Keyword research" jumped to 1.83 million in August 2025, then fell to 8,100 two months later. Look at the months around a spike before you build anything on it.

5. Read volume and CPC together. Volume tells you how many people search. CPC tells you what advertisers think those people are worth. The AI search terms above have low volume and high CPC. That makes them good topics for a few deep pages, not for chasing traffic.

6. Know if a search is navigational. Most of the 124 million "chatgpt" searches are people trying to open ChatGPT. You will not get that traffic with a blog post. Navigational volume tells you how big a brand is, not how many people want to read about it.

If you want a deeper method, the HackerNoon story "How To Do Keyword Research When a Third of the Answers Never Get Clicked" walks through filtering keywords by whether the click survives.

Watch your brand searches. People now reach AI tools by searching the name. Your brand search volume is one of the clearest signs of demand you can get, and any keyword tool can show it. Track it monthly.

Look for task searches. "How to turn off ai overview" grew from 2,400 to 18,100 searches a month. Searches like this ask for exact steps. A page with clear, tested steps answers them better than a summary does.

Check the trend before you write. "N8n" lost 63% of its searches in a year. "Codex" grew four times. A page built on last year's numbers can miss by a wide margin.

Price the click before you chase it. If a term costs $40 a click in Google Ads, advertisers value each visitor from it at about that much. Use that to decide which pages deserve the most work.

The data does not show people leaving Google for AI. It shows Google becoming the way people find AI. Searches for Claude, Gemini and other AI tools grew many times over in two years. Searches for Google and Bing held steady. Searches for Google's own AI Mode grew from almost nothing to 7.48 million a month.

For developers and marketers, the useful habit is simple. Look at monthly search data before you decide what to build, write or pay for. It shows what people actually type, not what a trend piece says they do.
