Hunters are allowed inside Grand Teton National Park until December 6 under a 1950 law, but only 40 permits were issued this year

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Grand Teton hunters must use non-lead ammunition, carry bear spray and turn in every elk head for chronic wasting disease testing. Visitors should wear bright colors.

In Grand Teton National Park in Wyoming, a small number of licensed hunters are allowed to take elk inside the park until December 6.

The annual elk reduction program runs from November 14 through December 6, according to the National Park Service.

It is authorized under the park’s 1950 enabling legislation and managed jointly by the Park Service and the Wyoming Game and Fish Department.

This year, only 40 permits are authorized, and hunters may take only cows and calves.

After reviewing available biological data, the Park Service and Wyoming Game and Fish determined that the program is necessary “to maintain the herd at its population objective and achieve desired elk distribution,” according to the Park Service.

The program uses Wyoming-licensed hunters with limited-quota permits to harvest elk in designated areas. The Park Service calls it “an important management tool with requirements that distinguish it from traditional elk hunting programs.”

Participants must carry a state license for Elk Hunt Area 75, a conservation stamp, an elk special management permit, a 2026 park elk reduction program permit and a hunter education card, regardless of age.

They must use non-lead ammunition and wear fluorescent orange or pink clothing. They also have to carry bear spray that is immediately accessible, in a non-expired can of at least 7.9 ounces.

Archery equipment, handguns, non-centerfire rifles and artificial elk calls are prohibited, the Park Service says.

Only Area 75 is open for the program this year. It lies mostly east of U.S. Highway 89, according to the park.

Within Area 75, the Antelope Flats portion and the Snake River bottom between Deadman’s Bar and Ditch Creek are closed throughout the season.

Area 79 is closed to the program this season “to limit harvest pressure on northern migratory and resident elk,” the park says.

To support surveillance for chronic wasting disease, known as CWD, participants must turn in the head of every elk harvested in the park at the designated drop-off location in Moose.

Park staff collect lymph nodes and send them to the Wyoming Game and Fish Wildlife Health Lab for testing, and hunters can check their results online.

Elk face other health threats too. Earth.com has reported on an emerging hoof disease that may change how elk bodies work.

The areas remain open to park visitors during the program, the Park Service says, and visitors are “strongly encouraged to wear bright colors.”

Park Service and Wyoming Game and Fish staff patrol the area to check that hunters follow the rules and to share information about bear safety.

Hunters are not the only pressure on elk in the region. In nearby Yellowstone, research suggests cougars pose an even bigger threat to elk than wolves.

The information was obtained from the National Park Service news release 2026 Elk Reduction Program begins November 14 in Grand Teton National Park (November 6, 2026) and Grand Teton National Park’s Elk Reduction Area 75 page.
