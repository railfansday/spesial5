Kimberly-Clark vs. Lululemon Athletica: Which Consumer Goods Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

One trades at a cheaper valuation, while the other boasts stronger cash generation.

One company provides everyday essentials while the other defines premium lifestyle apparel. Deciding between Kimberly-Clark (KMB +1.53%) and Lululemon Athletica (LULU -0.26%) depends on your preference for stability versus growth.

Kimberly-Clark dominates household essentials with brands like Kleenex and Huggies, offering a defensive play for conservative investors. Conversely, Lululemon Athletica leads the high-performance apparel market through an efficient direct-to-consumer model. Both firms are navigating shifting consumer habits, but they offer vastly different risk and reward profiles for investors looking ahead to 2027.

Kimberly-Clark provides essential personal care and tissue products to consumers through brands like Huggies and Kleenex. Its largest customer is Walmart (WMT -1.12%), which accounted for nearly 16% of net sales in its latest annual report filed for the period ending December 31, 2025. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business if purchasing patterns shift.

In FY 2025, revenue reached nearly $16.4 billion, representing a decline of approximately 2.1% compared to the previous year. Despite the lower top-line figure, the company reported net income of roughly $2 billion. Looking more closely at its financial picture, the company has $956 million in total cash and $6.5 billion in total debt. Operating cash flow over the 12-month trailing period is $3.3 billion.

Lululemon designs and sells high-performance athletic apparel through an omni-channel retail model that includes company-operated stores and e-commerce. It operates within the consumer discretionary stocks category, focusing on premium brand positioning. The company also utilizes wholesale channels like fitness studios and athletic organizations to reach specialized audiences.

In FY 2025, revenue reached roughly $11.1 billion, which represents growth of nearly 4.9% over the prior year. The company generated net income of approximately $1.6 billion during this period. Lululemon has $1.3 billion in total cash and $2.1 billion in total debt. Operating cash flow over the 12-month trailing period is $1.9 billion.

Kimberly-Clark faces intense competition from both branded and private-label rivals in the consumer products space. It is also sensitive to price volatility for raw materials like pulp, often sourced from large providers like Suzano (SUZ -0.63%). Additionally, the company must manage risks associated with its Kenvue acquisition, including integration challenges and higher indebtedness.

Lululemon operates in the fiercely competitive athletic apparel market, where it must constantly adapt to changing consumer preferences. The company remains vulnerable to supply chain disruptions, as its production is heavily concentrated. Furthermore, recent changes to trade policies and duty-free exemptions could impact its cost structure.

Lululemon currently trades at a lower multiple on both sales and earnings estimates compared to the more defensive Kimberly-Clark.

Right off the bat, I would pick Kimberly-Clark in this battle. One reason is that 2027 is likely to be as full of investment uncertainty as 2026. But with a more defensive stock like Kimberly-Clark, you have less to worry about in your portfolio. That's because its brands, from Band-Aid to Cottonelle, are products people will always buy, no matter what's going on in the stock market or the economy.

Also, Kimberly-Clark is a Dividend King, which requires the company to have increased its dividend payouts for 50 or more consecutive years. It's cleared that hurdle by boosting its payout for 54 consecutive years, and it's also paid a dividend for 92 consecutive years. Speaking of its dividend, it currently yields 4.6%.

The company can serve as a defensive position within a portfolio, but one thing to watch is its recent acquisition of the consumer health brand Kenvue. On paper, the two seem like they would mix well, but there's always the risk that an acquisition won't work out as smoothly as anticipated.

In comparison, Lululemon is a company working on its next chapter. Its sales are declining, and it brought in a new CEO in September. As it will take some time for changes to be implemented, the company is on less solid footing than Kimberly-Clark.
