The Phoenix Suns’ core four are getting better, with more runway to grow

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Phoenix Suns are 7-7 overall after a 1-5 start to the season. We were hoping for a much quicker start to the season than last year, but the difficulty of opponents, combined with the addition of Miles Bridges and Oso Ighodaro's injury, meant the Suns were not at their best to start the season.

The Suns are now 6-2 since Ighodaro was inserted into the starting lineup. Devin Booker is back to playing at a near All-NBA level this season. Jordan Ott is learning the right rotations. Dillon Brooks looks like he is in full villain mode on both ends of the floor after an up-and-down week in November that culminated in a 1-point performance in a loss to the Lakers. His move in the rotation to be the go-to scorer with the second unit to begin the second and fourth quarters appears to have unlocked him.

Miles Bridges is starting to find his lanes and facilitate an offense that needs to keep moving. His defense and rebounding are getting better as well.

Even Jalen Green, who finds himself in trade chatter, in the last two games, has had inspiring second halves to show what Brian Gregory's vision of this team is supposed to look like. Green's 18-point, seven-assist and seven-rebound night was a glimpse into who the Suns need him to be more consistently as one of the best athletes and downhill threats on this team. Combine that with the energy he played with against the Nuggets in that second half, where he had multiple steals, and maybe, just maybe, this backcourt pairing might work between him and Booker. The fit will never be perfect, but if Green can play this way more consistently, it will quiet a lot of the noise around him for the time being.

Regardless of the level of opponent, the Suns offense was crisp and flowing all night long against the Kings. The ball movement, spacing, and unselfishness jumped off the television screen. All four of the Suns starters had over five assists against the Kings; Ighodaro only had two. But that level of effort and commitment to making each other better on both ends of the floor started against the Nuggets in a win Monday night.

In back-to-back games, the core four have all scored in double figures, and the only occurrence of someone scoring below 15 points was Bridges against the Kings; he scored 13 points. Against the Nuggets, the core four combined for 67 points, and against the Kings they combined for 80 points.

The numbers also back it up as well. Before the Suns played the Magic on November 5, the core four had a -59 over the first seven games of the season. Since then, it is only a -2 in eight games, and the group continues to play better. The eye test also shows that they are playing better as well.

This group still has a long way to go, and it still has yet to put it together against a high-quality opponent like the Thunder or Spurs, but the good news is the next seven games on the schedule are: at Utah Jazz, at New Orleans Pelicans, at Houston Rockets, home vs Dallas Mavericks, at Utah Jazz, home vs Los Angeles Clippers, and home vs Sacramento. The Suns should realistically finish this stretch 5-2 or better.

However, it is even deeper than the record over the next seven games. The Suns need to continue to prove that how they have played the last two games is who they are. They are still building winning habits and are still working through defensive and rebounding lapses that have plagued them all season.

Does the 3-point shooting efficiency continue for Booker? Can Green play with the defensive intensity and playmaking? Can Brooks sustain the energy to be the emotional leader night in and night out? Does Miles Bridges continue to do the little things like rebounding and defending regardless of his shot attempts? And as a group, can this core four show up and put teams away without sleepwalking through quarters that give the lesser teams life?

This team still has flaws that might be too difficult to overcome, but if the Suns answer yes to most of these questions over the next two weeks, then they will be right back up into the top eight in the Western Conference when the calendar turns to December.
