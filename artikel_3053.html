Forget Nvidia Stock at $160 per Share. Buy This Semiconductor ETF Instead.

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Nvidia’s business is booming, but its headlines aren’t. Here’s a way to stay invested in AI chips without leaning on one company.

A month ago, Nvidia (NVDA -0.86%) was riding high as the dominant supplier of artificial intelligence (AI) chips. But wow, can things change fast in the stock market.

First, there was a sell-off in tech stocks, brought about by fears that spending was getting out of hand. Then the results of the midterm elections were seen to throw up roadblocks for data center approvals and construction. And finally, a scandal forced Nvidia founder Jensen Huang to resign, and investigators and Congress are probing the company's connections with a purported Chinese spy.

Now Nvidia is trading near $160, down 26% in the past month and 32% below its all-time high.

But there's an interesting disconnect here. Nvidia's business hasn't suddenly fallen apart. In fact, the company reported $96.2 billion in revenue in its fiscal 2027 second quarter, a whopping 106% increase from a year earlier. And big tech companies are expected to spend $800 billion on AI infrastructure this year, and more than $1 trillion next year.

Undoubtedly, there are plenty of opportunities in AI stocks. But if you want to limit your exposure to Nvidia and its bad headlines right now, a viable option is the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH -0.46%). It might be the smartest investment you can make right now.

The VanEck Semiconductor ETF tracks the performance of the MVIS US Listed Semiconductor 25 index, which holds the largest and most liquid companies involved in chip production and equipment businesses that are listed on U.S. exchanges. Nvidia is the largest holding, at a little more than 17%. But the ETF also has several other companies at the forefront of AI-capable semiconductors -- including Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing, Broadcom, and Advanced Micro Devices. These three companies collectively make up nearly 23% of SMH, which has 26 holdings total.

Data source: VanEck, YCharts (performance as of Nov. 13, 2026).

The most notable fact from the table above? Nvidia is the only member of SMH's top holdings that is in the red so far in 2026. And while Nvidia is down more than 12% for the year, SMH shows a 35% gain.

The ETF charges a reasonable 0.35% annual expense ratio, or $35 annually per $10,000 invested. And you get exposure to AI processors, memory, chipmaking equipment, and other semiconductor businesses.

The semiconductor industry has more going for it than one company, regardless of how dominant Nvidia has become. For investors who want to stay invested in AI without making a $160 bet on Nvidia's next chapter, SMH looks like the more attractive choice.
