Our Offense vs. Their Defense: Cincinnati Bengals

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Another week and another disappointing showcase for Kellen Moore and the offense. Not only have the New Orleans Saints suffered back-to-back losses, but it's also now been back-to-back games in which Tyler Shough has looked like a shell of himself. This week is not going to present an easy opportunity for the Saints to get right, as they will have to make those plays happen for themselves when they face a team that is considered to be one of the favorites to win it all in the Bengals.

The Saints' inept rushing offense is at least remaining consistent in its struggles. This is another game where an improved rushing attack would be a major help against one of the best passing defenses in the NFL, considering the Saints currently field the 28th-ranked rushing offense. The Bengals rank 11th in the NFL in passing defense, allowing only 216.2 yards per game. Their rushing defense is not that great, but it's better than what the Saints' offense has been able to produce. They allow 123.9 rushing yards per game, which ranks 21st overall. The Bengals' defense also ranks 21st in total defense, allowing 361 yards per game.

The advantage the Saints could have would be on third down, as the Bengals have one of the worst third-down defenses, ranking 30th in the league and allowing opponents to convert 46.5% of their opportunities.

New Orleans needs to run the ball with consistency at some point. In the games the Saints have lost, the offense has been one-dimensional. And this matchup is not easy at all. They struggled with the interior presence of Jeffery Simmons against the Bears. Well, the Bengals will be bringing Dexter Lawrence and Jonathan Allen into this game on Sunday. Given the way the interior offensive line has played for the Saints, this does not inspire much confidence heading into this matchup.

DJ Turner and Bryan Cook will occupy the secondary for the Bengals, and the priority for this elite pass coverage unit will be to slow down Chris Olave. The Saints will need to stay ahead of the chains and establish a better rhythm in their play-calling. If the Saints can stay in manageable third-down situations, they have shown the ability to convert. They currently rank seventh in the NFL in third-down conversions on offense.

The Saints need to get close to 125 rushing yards in this game. This will help keep the Bengals' explosive offense on the sidelines, open up passing opportunities for Shough downfield, and give the Saints more scoring opportunities.

This will be a difficult matchup, but this is the beauty of the NFL. On any given Sunday, any team can win, and any team can lose. The Bengals will also be playing on a short week, as they played on the road on Monday night. Will the Saints be able to take advantage? That remains to be seen.
