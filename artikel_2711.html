Rock bottom is near if Cowboys can’t get back on track vs. Titans

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

It shouldn't exactly be hard to find reasons to expect the Dallas Cowboys to play well against the 1-8 Tennessee Titans this Sunday, especially after (in the biggest vacuum of all time) a one-score loss to the NFC leading San Francisco 49ers, yet that is where expectations currently sit going into Thanksgiving week for Brian Schottenheimer's team.

The elephant in the room is that coaching is far and away the Cowboys biggest problem right now, and for fans that believe this to be the case, following the trends of opposing coaches the team has gone against this season tells an interesting story. It all started with a loss to the New York Giants to start the John Harbaugh era in New Jersey with a bang. This early separation between the Giants and Cowboys has held up as the Giants continue to lead the NFC East, and the Cowboys look more and more like pretenders to catch them by the week.

The streak of playing against other defensive-minded head coaches continued after week one, as the Cowboys drew their own former defensive coordinator Dan Quinn at home in week two. Given the way the Cowboys defense has played in their last two home games against NFC West opponents, it feels more accurate than ever before to say the Cowboys benefited greatly from the Commanders losing starting QB Jayden Daniels in this game.

Nonetheless, that week two game is in the books as a win for Schotty against a defense-first head coach. The same goes for week four, when Dak Prescott and CeeDee Lamb put their hero capes on to beat DeMeco Ryans and the Houston Texans.

In between the Commanders win and this AFC South win, the Cowboys dropped a game to another defensive coach in his first season with the team, leaving the win on the doorstep in Brazil to Jesse Minter and the Ravens. Immediately after the Texans win came another letdown against a defensive coach, losing at home to the winless Tampa Bay Buccaneers and Todd Bowles.

Another first-year head coach put the Cowboys back in the loss column in week eight when Mike LaFleur became the latest Cardinals head coach to apparently own the Cowboys, and it took returning to AFC South play to get back in the win column – again by a narrow margin – in week nine at the Colts.

Now, the next opponent for the Cowboys is Robert Saleh's Titans. Another test at home, where the Cowboys are 7-14-1 to date since 2024, and have lost their last two at home to the Titans. Another defensive head coach, but this time one with the worst possible track record against the Cowboys.

On the other side of the ball, another running back to deal with in former Cowboy Tony Pollard, who has touchdowns of 32, 40, and 36 yards in his last three games. Brian Daboll, the former Giants head coach before Harbaugh, is the one getting the ball to Pollard and calling plays for the Titans offense. He'll be doing so in AT&T Stadium for the first time since week two of last year, when the Giants led by Russell Wilson put up 37 points in an overtime loss.

Daboll is 0-7 in his last seven games coached against the Cowboys, which spans his entire tenure with the Giants. Saleh is 0-6 in his career coaching against the Cowboys, his most recent game against them also coming in Arlington. Saleh was the head coach of the other New York franchise in 2023, when the Jets visited the Cowboys also in week two, and Dallas won 30-10. Before that, Saleh was the defensive coordinator for the 49ers in the last two games the Cowboys managed to win against San Francisco, in 2017 and 2020.

Week 11 feels like a can't win scenario for the Cowboys. The coaching matchup from a pure statistical history favors the Cowboys in such a big way, that any misstep the Cowboys make this week will be maximized – something evidenced already when Christian Parker was stripped of play-calling duties.

The coaching matchup in a deeper sense would seem to give the Titans a path to victory, as they come in with loads of experience poised to run circles around a Cowboys staff that doesn't know what it's doing, and also nothing to lose.

The Giants came into week one with a similar coaching structure, an experienced head coach with a former head coach as OC calling plays for a mobile QB, and really looked like the better team for all 60 minutes against the Cowboys. It's scary to think what both former New York head coaches can do with this setup against the Cowboys for the Titans, with Cam Ward coming off his highest completion percentage of the season against a very good Jaguars defense.

When the best thing the Cowboys have going for them coming into this matchup is they can't be riding too high coming in since they didn't win last week, that is not good. The Titans also come in off a 40-23 loss to the Jaguars, but the Cowboys should be exceptionally careful not to use this as any extra reason to downplay their opponent. The Titans never trailed by more than ten and were within seven very late in the fourth quarter.

The Titans also have losses this season by four to the Eagles, five to the Giants, six to the Ravens, three to the Browns, and seven to the now red-hot Bengals (after leading 13-0). The Cowboys have lost to two of these teams and beat the Eagles without Jalen Hurts, who played when the Eagles were in Nashville in week two.

Are the Cowboys closer to the 5-3-1 Giants, and capable of getting two wins in five days to prove this and make anything else this season matter, or closer to the 1-8 Titans and ready to join them in the ranks of having no hope for the playoffs after this Sunday?

It all starts with beating the Titans at home for the first time since 2002, which ended up being Dave Campo's last year as HC for the Cowboys. If Schottenheimer is unable to do so, it will be hard not to feel like a similar fate at season's end isn't inevitable.

The Cowboys streak against the Titans even resembles a mirror image of how Schotty has led the Cowboys to mostly alternating wins and losses over two years. Since a Cowboys blowout win at the Titans in 2006, these sides have gone back and forth in each game in 2010, 2014, 2018, and most recently 2022 when the Cowboys won in week 17.
