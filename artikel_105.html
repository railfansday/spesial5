Danish MPs approve Greenland security pact with US

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Danish foreign minister says deal ‘removes all uncertainty’ over territory but warns trust in US needs rebuilding

The Danish parliament has unanimously approved a new Arctic security pact between Greenland, Denmark and the US in what it is hoped will mark a new chapter in relations, months after Donald Trump’s threats to invade the largely autonomous territory.

The deal, which was signed by the US president and the prime ministers of Greenland and Denmark, Jens-Frederik Nielsen and Mette Frederiksen, in September after months of talks, updates a 1951 defence agreement to give the US underwater access to Greenland and specific permission to construct a missile defence system.

It also recognises Greenland, a former Danish colony, as an equal partner and stipulates that if it were to become fully independent of Denmark, it would remain in Nato.

Having already been approved by the Greenlandic parliament earlier this month, all that remained for the agreement to come into effect, said the Danish foreign minister, Lars Løkke Rasmussen, was for Trump to send a notification to the US Congress.

“We have now resolved our part of the equation,” Løkke Rasmussen said after the bill was passed. “This is a positive development, as it removes all uncertainty surrounding Greenland and establishes a proper framework for future cooperation.”

But he warned that trust with the US had been damaged. “Consequently, there is a significant task of rebuilding ahead. But this will take place within the framework of an agreement, which is a good thing,” he said.

Before the deal was signed, Trump said the US would “immediately begin the process of developing a large military presence in the appropriate part of Greenland”. But Løkke Rasmussen said on Thursday that the US was yet to inform Denmark of any plans to establish new military bases.

The US ambassador to Denmark, Ken Howery, said on social media he was “thrilled that the Danish parliament today voted unanimously 109-0 to ratify the Greenland security agreement”.

In a statement, he said: “On behalf of the United States, I welcome the strong support in Denmark’s parliament for the ratification of the historic US-Denmark-Greenland defence agreement, paving the way for it to enter into force.

“The agreement is a tremendous accomplishment, one that modernises our partnership for the security challenges of the future and bolsters the security of the United States, the kingdom of Denmark, including Greenland, and all of Nato.”

He added: “Thank you to colleagues in Denmark and Greenland for the many hours spent negotiating the agreement, which makes us safer, stronger and more secure. I look forward to continuing our work to deepen our cooperation as we implement the agreement and advance other shared priorities with our friends and allies throughout the kingdom of Denmark.”

Relations between the US and Denmark and Greenland – and the Nato alliance as a whole – had come under unprecedented strain amid repeated threats by Trump to take over the Arctic territory that led to Denmark preparing for a US invasion.

Such was the perceived threat in January that Copenhagen reportedly sent over blood bags, and explosives to blow up runways, in case of US attack.
