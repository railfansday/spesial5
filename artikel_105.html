Ecuadorean Fishermen Who Survived U.S. Boat Strike Sue Trump Administration

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The first lawsuit by strike survivors targets a secretive U.S. military campaign that has killed more than 240 people.

By Genevieve Glatsky and José María León Cabrera

Genevieve Glatsky reported from Bogotá, Colombia, and José María León Cabrera reported from Quito, Ecuador.

Ecuadorean fishermen sued the United States in U.S. federal court this week, claiming American military drones bombed their vessel in the Pacific Ocean earlier this year. It is the first lawsuit by survivors of a boat strike who are seeking accountability for a Trump administration campaign that has been cloaked in secrecy.

Since September 2025, U.S. forces have carried out 72 strikes against alleged drug-trafficking boats, killing at least 244 people, a tally by The New York Times shows.

Filed Monday in Pittsburgh, the suit seeks financial damages for 17 fisherman from the port city of Manta, Ecuador, and an order halting U.S. strikes on small boats in the Caribbean and eastern Pacific.

The fishermen made up the crew of a boat that left Manta on March 2, 2026, fishing near the Galápagos Islands, according to the complaint. While returning to shore on March 17, the Ecuadorean Navy intercepted and boarded the boat for a two-hour inspection.

Officers found no contraband or criminal activity and let them proceed, according to a report by the Ecuadorean Coast Guard provided by Daniel Kovalik, a lawyer for the fishermen.

Hours later, the lawsuit claims, two armed drones from a U.S. vessel allegedly struck the boat, engulfing it in flames and severely burning two crew members, Jordy Alexander Flores Lucas and Leonel Alberto Pilay Barahona.
