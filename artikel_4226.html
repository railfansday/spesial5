Last week, the official Cyberpunk 2077 trading card game launched to the public. It came off the back of a record-breaking $28.3 million Kickstarter campaign, making it the most-funded game in the site's history. In a time when TCGs have never been more popular, it looks set to make a huge breakthrough.

In perfect synergy, some budding players have brought Cyberpunk 2077's TCG into the world of Night City, allowing you to visit actual TCG stores and compete in competitions against other players.

Open//77 is a huge fan-made online Cyberpunk 2077 world. Its range of servers allows Cyberpunk 2077 players to roleplay, race, and compete in PvP matches, completely transforming the game created by CD Projekt Red. It's mightily impressive.

The servers get regular updates and improvements that have recently included new vehicle customization, Braindances, and Xbox Launcher support, but it's the new TCG additions that are some of the most exciting, in-depth ones we've ever seen.

First spotted by Kanto Post, the Open//77 team has added the full Cyberpunk TCG in a playable format to its multiplayer servers. You can enter TCG stores, buy card packs, build decks, and sit down at tables to compete with other Cyberpunk TCG players. You can see it in action below:

The Open//77 dev log detailed the effort that went into implementing the card game in the futuristic RPG.

"Light RP now has proper duel table gameplay with deck support, playmats, challenge flow, synced table state, duel overlays, seated camera support, and the full game loop for real matches," it said. "This was not just visual polish. The rules engine now covers the actual phases of play, resources, attacks, reactions, stealing, overtime, and win or loss conditions. On top of that, every card effect in the current set has been scripted, turning the card game from a basic prototype into something much closer to a complete feature for a Cyberpunk 2077 RP server."

Its addition required creating an entire TCG layout. Cards are expanded when hovered over, keyword explanations are available in full detail, and prompts like "your move" pop up when it's your turn.

It fits perfectly into the world of Cyberpunk, with players able to buy packs of the TCG with their hard-earned Eddies, and as far as UI for a digital tabletop game goes, this is preem.

Unfortunately, there are a handful of drawbacks to the TCG's addition. You'll need the PC version of both Cyberpunk 2077 and its DLC, Phantom Liberty, and you'll need to download Open//77's online servers. It also uses generative AI, like many of the recent browser ports of retro games. If ethically, that's not a problem to you, this is going to be an absolute blast.
