10 Soulslikes That Are Unexpected Masterpieces

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Ronald is a video game journalist with more than 10 years of experience in the industry, working on several of the most important Spanish websites.

Creating a new genre is quite difficult, and while that's not exactly what FromSoftware did, it certainly gave rise to a new generation of titles aptly categorized under the Soulslike label.

The reasons why this phenomenon emerged organically and without premeditation are plentiful, as are the numerous gems that its rise to popularity among developers has gifted us.

Gradually, the niche became a popular segment among creators and players, but even so, its expansion has been such that many creations, despite their quality, have flown under the radar of most.

Therefore, in order to give due recognition to those works that oscillate between being unknown and not properly appreciated, I invite you to read this list of ten Soulslikes that are unexpected masterpieces.

As a self-proclaimed devotee of boss rushes, especially given that they're always the most interesting aspect of any Soulslike, no amount of praise is enough to highlight Rubinite's current popularity.

Despite its branching nature, it features an engaging story that perfectly complements the gameplay, which isn't the sole focus of the title; rather, both elements work together to create a truly well-rounded experience.

However, it's clear that anyone playing Rubinite will do so for the bosses (or for its gorgeous pixel art), and in this department, it performs sensationally, with varied adversaries in both aesthetics and gimmicks that truly maximize the game's satisfying mechanics.

As one of the year's best indie games, it inevitably makes the list of the most outstanding Soulslikes, especially since it pleasantly surprised me even though I'd been eagerly awaiting its release since the day it was announced.

The so-called flow state is a subtle key to the excellence of a Soulslike, as the magnificent AI LIMIT demonstrates through its simple yet direct combat that truly manages to captivate you from the first touch.

I admit I'm usually wary of titles that share its visual style due to past bad experiences, though this game reversed my expectations with its solid gameplay, both in its design and execution.

AI LIMIT's game feel is the experience's highlight, with mesmerizing encounters that, while not at the most challenging end, strike a perfect balance between being challenging and never frustrating.

If your mind, like mine, unconsciously compares it to similar products within and outside Soulslikes, I recommend you completely dispel any doubts, as few other examples of the genre can offer this level of reward.

If you remember the feeling you had the first time you played a Soulsborne game, that pleasant wonder of being captivated as you discovered an expansive world brimming with majesty, then encountering Mohrta is a gift from heaven.

Despite the obvious gameplay differences, since the game is both an FPS and an ARPG, the sense of discovery is practically the same, generating indescribable joy when you come across a new weapon, enemy, area, or NPC.

Everything in the game is so bizarre yet charming that it's difficult to play it in short bursts, because what it really makes you want to do is burn the midnight oil while enjoying its unparalleled inventiveness in terms of story, worldbuilding, and design.

If you've played any other title from its developer, Scumhead, you can more or less imagine what Mohrta has in store, though I can already tell you that it's the best video game this eccentric creator has ever produced.

There's nothing more fitting for this season than being destroyed by the same boss fifty-seven times in a row.

While I always found Mortal Shell a fascinating exercise in imagination within a dark and obtuse fantasy setting, I always considered it merely good, nothing more, but I can't say the same for Mortal Shell II.

Coming from a first title that was rough around the edges and failed to fully convince, Cold Symmetry surpassed even the most optimistic predictions with a sequel that addresses its predecessor's every single flaw.

The enemies' legibility, ease of spatial awareness, combat's feel, orientation within the main content, level design's firmness... It's comparatively much more robust, and I'd be lying if I said I expected such a significant leap in quality.

Therefore, Mortal Shell II proved me wrong, and nothing could make me happier. That it belongs in the discussion of the best Soulslikes of all time is undeniable at this point, yet it doesn't rank higher on the list because I'm sure there were devotees who knew this would be the outcome.

Bloodborne is the kind of video game that, once you finish it, leaves you with the bittersweet feeling that you'll never experience anything like it again, but Vigil: The Longest Night comes pretty close.

Although its gameplay is much more tactical and careful than its 3D counterpart, its atmosphere is undeniably analogous, especially due to the artistic and narrative similarities that place both within the realm of cosmic and body horror.

From there on, the comparisons begin to diminish, as it's more reminiscent of Salt and Sanctuary in its combat and progression systems, though its founding elements are masterfully aligned with FromSoftware's prodigious child.

Therefore, Vigil: The Longest Night achieves a feat that very few others can: making you feel like you're playing Bloodborne. This might seem like a weak argument for calling it a masterpiece, but when you truly grasp the difficulty of such an undertaking, it's not so far-fetched after all.

If you haven't played Queen's Domain yet, be sure to leave this article open in another tab while you download it, because one of the most immersive games of 2026 awaits you.

If King's Field were released today, I'm sure it would strive to resemble the work of Freshly Baked Games LLC, whom I was honored to discover through their debut title, The New Flesh, and with whom I've been inseparable ever since.

The decision to follow their lead was the right one, as keeping an eye out for the release of their retro-fantasy action RPG has given me one of the year's finest experiences, especially since I love titles inspired by Dark Souls more for their level design, interconnectivity, and progression than for their combat systems.

Some might say that Queen's Domain isn't Soulslike enough because it aligns more with the philosophy than the practicalities, but for me, that's enough, as it's one of the most highly recommended and captivating games released in the last couple of months.

In these Soulslikes, studying your enemy and playing carefully is vital.

Soulslikes tend to be very traditional in terms of what they do and how they do it, but the arrival of an outsider to the genre like One More Level brought a breath of fresh air in the form of Valor Mortis.

As the creators of Ghostrunner, a first-person platformer that excels in its spectacular movement sequences, their ARPG inherited that skill to create the most enjoyable experience in the genre in terms of movement.

The levels are gigantic in scale and verticality, allowing you to fly through the air using a grappling hook, run along walls, and swing to ledges, giving it a completely unique feel that no other Soulslike has ever achieved.

Add to that combat that balances parries and dodges like Lies of P, spectacular and grotesque (though easy) bosses, and customization options that are limited but refined, and you realize that Valor Mortis is much more than what people say about it.

Considering they had just released the least-reviewed Darksiders game to date, nobody bet a penny on Gunfire Games' success with Remnant: From the Ashes, and yet nobody remained silent when it came to praising its remarkable quality.

Making a permanently cooperative title with third-person shooter mechanics in a sporadically online and predominantly melee branch is among the boldest moves the genre has ever seen, which is already quite an achievement.

Regardless, Remnant: From the Ashes doesn't stand out simply for its innovation; it's a robust and extremely fun game whose variety of weapons, bosses, and environments allows for a deep adventure, brimming with content that's worth experiencing in its entirety.

Its breakout performance is a bit of a blow to me, as I suspect that, over the years, they'll prioritize this IP over Darksiders, but if that's the sacrifice to keep getting the best co-op games in the genre... so be it.

The First Berserker: Khazan's announcement certainly didn't leave any avid fan indifferent, as it gave the impression of being a major beast within the genre, but I don't think anyone could have anticipated its true severity.

Although its average critical reception is more mixed, I'm more inclined to agree with the unanimous opinion of the gaming community that it's a serious contender to be FromSoftware's ultimate heir.

It's difficult to describe what you have to feel to comprehend it truly, but holding the controls while fighting The First Berserker: Khazan's bosses is an untamed magic that courses through your entire body, filling you with adrenaline and excitement that are tough to match.

Were it not for the somewhat simplistic nature of its basic enemies and environments, the discussion about the title would be very different, yet you forget all of that when you enter the zone of maximum concentration thanks to its diverse, refined, and satisfying gameplay.

Every genre has its defining features, and the Soulslike is one that gets misunderstood fairly often with games like these blurring the line.

The only reason I disagree with those who believe the previous Soulslike is the best of them all is due to Lies of P's existence, a title that no one even came close to predicting, even if today it is commonly recognized under said distinction.

When we talk about unexpected masterpieces, the fact that NEOWIZ, a company originally conceived to distribute music games for the PlayStation Portable, developed the only Soulslike capable of rivaling the originals is among the entire century's most unpredictable events.

No matter how much faith you had in its release, there was no way to imagine that Lies of P would have such a vast arsenal, iconic and demandingly engaging bosses, levels crafted to perfection, a spectacular soundtrack, and even a story that keeps you engaged until the very last second.

Add to that its worldbuilding, side quests, flawless combination of parries and dodges, level of customization through the weapon assembly system, masterful enemy positioning and shortcuts... and you're left with a title that can only be described as majestic.

Lies of P raised the bar for the genre to a new level, and since 2024, it has become the ultimate benchmark despite coming from such a modest and distant background as that of its creators.

From the indie ecosystem to productions approaching AAA measures, these are ten Soulslike franchises that should come back by 2027.
