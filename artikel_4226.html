As a lifelong Pokemon Trading Card Game collector, I think the era I have the fondest memories of is Delta Species. At 13, I delivered newspapers. When I was paid at the end of each week, a buddy and I would catch a bus into our local town center and buy a tin containing packs like Holon Phantoms and ex Crystal Guardians. We'd then take these tins to the nearby Burger King and rip packs with our greasy fingers.

These tins, which we paid $20 for, are now worth around $10,000...

There was something special about pulling Pokemon that weren't their original types: an Electric-type Feraligatr, a Grass-type Snorlax, or a Fire-type Arbok all spring to mind. The stamped reverse holos were just the icing on the cake.

A few years back, my love for this era of cards grew so strong that I started a random collection challenge: get every card that wasn't its normal type with the reverse holo stamp from the Delta Species, Holon Phantoms, ex Crystal Guardians, and ex Dragon Frontiers sets. Some of the cards were expensive, but I could chip away at it bit by bit. That is now no longer a reality.

Over the last three months, the prices of many of these cards have skyrocketed to unobtainable levels, with some increasing by as much as 400 percent.

For me, the Delta Species era focuses on four sets: Delta Species, Holon Phantoms, ex Crystal Guardians, and ex Dragon Frontiers. They include Pokemon in Delta Species forms, repping a different type, alongside some stellar exs and mighty expensive Gold Stars.

I recently noticed that a few of the cards in my collection had increased in value exponentially, so I decided to dig deeper, and it's grim reading for anybody looking to buy cards from these expansions.

I started my research with the titular Delta Species expansion, first released in 2005. The set featured 114 cards, 35 of which bore the Delta Species symbol, including every Eeveelution released at the time. In the last three months alone, 12 of these cards have more than doubled in price (with many more climbing by 50, 60, or 70 percent).

Of these 12 cards, the average increase was 177 percent. If you'd bought these 12 back in early August, they'd have cost you $3,916. Today, you're looking at $10,576. That doesn't factor in the other 102 cards in the set, which have all increased.

Unsurprisingly, many of the cards that have skyrocketed feature some of the most popular Pokemon: Mewtwo, Rayquaza, Umbreon, Groudon, and Metagross, and this set alone has crushed my dream.

I'm not sure why, but Holon Phantoms is arguably the most notable of these sets, and it's also the one with the biggest price change.

Of the 111 cards in the set, once again, 12 have seen their prices double since August. A few more errant cards enter the list this time, with the likes of Mankey and Torchic both more than doubling, but some heavy hitters, including Gyarados, Raichu, and Mewtwo, make the list.

In August, these 12 cards would have set you back $3,167; today, it's $70 shy of $12,000. That's 12 cards increasing by a combined $9,000 in just three months. The data is below:

ex Crystal Guardians and Dragon Frontiers have yet to see major moves like Delta Species and Holon Phantoms, but more than a few cards are moving up in price.

None of Crystal Guardian's big hitters have seen a +100 percent shift yet, but Pokemon like Torchic, Treecko, and Charmelon have all risen over that milestone.

Eight cards have doubled in price since August, going from $219 to $529. Child's play when you think about it...

Dragon Frontiers saw only seven cards increase by double, but some of them are big ones. The Electric-type Feraligatr and Grass-type Snorlax I referenced earlier are both included in that.

These seven have risen from $1,016 to $2,965.

This can all be attributed to the scalping and investing that is ruining the hobby, and it's something I've written about on more occasions than I can remember. These cards were released when grading wasn't particularly popular, so the volume of PSA 10s in the wild is very, very low. This has meant that Delta Species cards at PSA 9/10 have shot up in price, and therefore single-card prices have risen as investors look for gradeable versions.
