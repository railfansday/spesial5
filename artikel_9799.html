How to Enforce Node.js Tenant Domain Quota During Property Mail Reconciliation

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

A property company can own its mail domain while your platform controls the DNS account used for onboarding. Resolve that ownership boundary before accepting another domain: use the tenant table for admission, then reconcile it against complete zone inventories on a schedule. A provider-wide count cannot tell you which property company has exhausted its allowance.

TL;DR: count active domains and pending reservations in your own tenant table, enforce the quota and reserve capacity in one transaction, and reconcile against the zone list to catch out-of-band additions. For a Node.js backend pointing company mail at a provider with MX records, keep quota admission separate from permission to edit DNS. Customer-owned zones need the customer's authorized publication process; platform-owned zones need an explicit tenant mapping.

The practical trade-off is onboarding speed versus explainable ownership. An unfamiliar zone should trigger review before it changes a customer's capacity calculation. It should never trigger an automatic MX rewrite.

The DNS layer has no concept of your application's tenants. One account may hold several property companies' domains alongside the platform's operational zones, while a customer-owned zone may remain outside that account entirely. Replacing the tenant count with the length of a provider response therefore changes what you are measuring. It doesn't make the quota more accurate.

Two requests that both read a count of two can both pass unless the count and reservation share transaction protection.

The ownership model also determines what reconciliation can prove. For a platform-owned zone, compare inventory within the platform's authorized account scope. For a customer-owned zone, track the domain's tenant association and the evidence available through the customer's approved access. An absence from the platform account is expected for some customer-owned zones; treating it as a missing zone would create false alarms and invite unnecessary migrations. Record the observation scope with the ownership decision rather than trying to infer either from a domain name.

Keep receiving-mail routing separate from sender authentication. MX records direct company mail to its receiving provider. DMARC alignment concerns outbound authentication, including rent reminders or OTP messages, and needs its own checks under RFC 7489. Neither a quota pass nor an MX change proves inbox delivery.

Count active domains and unfinished reservations. Keep capacity occupied while a provider operation has an unresolved outcome; otherwise a retry can compete with a fresh request for a slot that was never safely released. Use a stable application operation identifier so repeated admission attempts find the same reservation.

Start with read-only reconciliation. Review unexplained differences, confirm the customer-owned versus platform-owned classification, and record the approval needed to publish the receiving provider's exact MX values. Then enable transactional reservations, decision events, and time-bounded overrides. Connect provisioning only after the admission outcome is durable.

For acceptance, run two simultaneous admissions with one slot left: exactly one should reserve capacity. Replay its operation identifier and confirm the count stays fixed. Add an illustrative unattributed zone to the collector fixture and confirm it enters review without being assigned or deleted. Supply an incomplete snapshot and confirm it cannot authorize corrections.

Finally, stage MX publication under the recorded owner's authority and check the resulting DNS state against the selected mail provider's instructions. Keep outbound authentication checks distinct. Offboarding needs the same discipline: removing a tenant association does not authorize deletion of a customer-owned zone, and an unresolved removal must not silently free capacity for another provisioning attempt.
