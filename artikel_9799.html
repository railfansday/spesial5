Google Play In-App Review API: why the card won't show

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Posted on Nov 6 • Originally published at appranker.net

You wired up the Google Play In-App Review API, the task completes without an error, and no rating card ever appears. Usually nothing is broken: the card is quota-limited, the API never tells you what happened, and only one of Google's three test methods shows the real flow. This post gives you a working setup for Kotlin, Expo and Flutter, a rule for when to call it, and a table of the usual reasons the card stays hidden.

The flow is two calls. requestReviewFlow() fetches a ReviewInfo object from Google Play, and launchReviewFlow(activity, reviewInfo) shows a native Play card over your app where the user picks 1 to 5 stars and can add a comment. It runs on Android 5.0 (API 21) and later, including tablets and Google TV, plus ChromeOS, but only where the Google Play Store is installed.

The quota is why Google says not to put the flow behind a button. A user who has used up their quota taps it and sees nothing.

Since each user gets roughly one shot per period, your trigger logic matters more than the integration. Aim for a moment right after the user succeeded at something, while they are between tasks, once they have used the app enough to judge it.

Save review_last_asked_at at the moment you launch the flow. You will never learn whether a rating was left, so launch time is the only timestamp you can trust.

Kotlin. Add com.google.android.play:review:2.0.2 (and review-ktx with the same version for Kotlin extensions). Fetch ReviewInfo shortly before the success moment, since it expires after a while:

Errors you may log: PLAY_STORE_NOT_FOUND (-1), INVALID_REQUEST (-2) and INTERNAL_ERROR (-100). Log them, never show them to the user, and wait for the flow to finish before moving to the next screen.

Expo. npx expo install expo-store-review, then check StoreReview.hasAction() before calling StoreReview.requestReview(). Bare React Native apps can use react-native-in-app-review.

For a permanent "Rate us" menu entry on Android, link to https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=&showAllReviews=true (or the market:// form) instead of calling the API.

Most "it's broken" reports come from testing in a setup that cannot show a working card.

For the internal test track, all four must hold: the account is on the track, it is the primary account selected in the Play Store, it installed the app from Play, and it has not reviewed the app yet. After that first Play install, you can push new builds from Android Studio and keep testing.

If none fit, check that your eligibility gate ever returns true. A gate that never opens looks exactly like a broken API.
