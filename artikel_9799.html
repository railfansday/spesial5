Opportunity Discovery: A Field Guide for When You’re Stuck Waiting for a Sign

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Posted on Nov 16 • Originally published at smtm-store.pages.dev

You are not lacking ideas. You are drowning in them. You have a Notion page, a Trello board, or a notes app folder labeled "Ideas" that contains 47 entries ranging from "Uber for dog walking" to "B2B SaaS for dental supply chain logistics." None of them feel right.

The stuckness isn't confusion. It’s selection paralysis masked as discovery.

You sit down to "find an opportunity." You open Google Trends. You scroll Twitter/X for "pain points." You read Indie Hackers posts titled "How I made $10k MRR in 30 days." You feel productive. You are not. You are consuming other people’s finished movies while trying to write your own script.

You are stuck because you are playing "Entrepreneur Simulator" instead of doing the grunt work of finding a specific person with a specific bleeding neck wound you can reach and treat.

1. "Scratch Your Own Itch" (Without the Second Half) The advice is usually "build what you need." The failure mode: you build a tool for a problem you used to have, or a problem you have now but only because of your specific weird workflow. You lack the distribution channel to reach others like you. You end up with a custom script, not a product.

2. Keyword Research / SEO Gap Analysis This finds demand, not opportunity. It tells you what people search for. It does not tell you what they will pay for, what they are frustrated by, or if you can actually deliver the solution. High volume, low difficulty keywords are often low intent or dominated by aggregators you cannot beat.

3. "Talk to Customers" (Generic Edition) You book 15 calls. You ask: "What's your biggest challenge?" They say: "Time management." You build a to-do list app. They don't buy. Why? Because "talking to customers" without a hypothesis and a specific profile is just socializing. You need to talk to one specific type of person about one specific workflow where money is already changing hands or time is actively being wasted.

4. Trend Surfing (AI, Web3, No-Code, etc.) By the time a trend is a hashtag, the alpha is gone. Building on a platform trend (e.g., "GPT wrapper for X") makes you a feature, not a business. Platform risk eats your lunch. You are competing with the platform vendor and 10,000 other wrappers.

5. The "Idea Validation" Theater Building a landing page, running $50 in cold traffic, getting 3 emails, and calling it "validated." This validates curiosity, not willingness to pay. It wastes 2 weeks you could have spent finding a real lead.

Stop looking for ideas. Start auditing assets. An opportunity is just an intersection between what you uniquely control and what someone urgently needs.

Do not brainstorm. Inventory. Open a spreadsheet. Create four tabs.

Tab A: Access (Who trusts you?) List every person you can message right now who would reply within 24 hours.

Tab B: Competence (What can you build/do fast?) List specific technical or operational capabilities you can execute without learning a new framework.

Tab C: Domain Scars (Where have you bled?) List specific, painful, repetitive workflows you have personally executed or watched up close for > 6 months.

Tab D: Capital/Time Constraints (Your Real Boundaries)

Cross-reference Tabs A, B, and C. You are looking for: Access + Competence + Scar.

Pick three intersections max. Rank them by: Severity of Scar × Speed to MVP × Access Warmth.

Do not build. Do not make a landing page. Sell the workflow.
