Solana Price Prediction: NYSE Eyes SOL Validator as Network Enters New Era

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Wall Street is getting closer to Solana, and this time, it isn’t just buying tokens; it could change the direction of its price prediction. Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), the owner of the New York Stock Exchange, is considering operating a Solana validator. This arrives as Solana officially graduates from its Mainnet Beta era.

Yet SOL holders aren’t exactly celebrating as the token remains deep in correction territory after falling from its September highs at $125. With Wall Street knocking on Solana’s door, holders now face a tempting question: Is a $90 recovery finally coming, or does SOL have another painful trip toward $60?

ICE is considering operating a Solana validator, according to comments from ICE Clear Europe President Hester Serafini at Solana Breakpoint in London yesterday. Validators help secure Solana’s network and participate in transaction processing and consensus. Having one of the world’s largest financial market operators participate could strengthen Solana’s institutional credibility.

ICE is already preparing for a closer relationship with the blockchain. Its planned tokenized securities trading infrastructure can accept tokens minted on Solana. The company hasn’t confirmed a validator launch, but the possibility puts Solana firmly on Wall Street’s radar.

The announcement also follows the Depository Trust and Clearing Corporation’s November 12 plans to connect its tokenization service to Solana during the second half of 2027. The DTCC sits at the heart of US securities settlement, making the development another important institutional milestone.

Solana is also making a major change under the hood. Also last night, the Solana Foundation announced that the network had officially dropped its Mainnet Beta designation, introducing Solana 1.0.

Yesterday at Breakpoint, Solana officially left beta. Alongside 1.0, Zelle routed a $1.25M confidential bank transfer live on stage, and ICE announced new onchain lending infrastructure.Announcements:– Solana 1.0 officially marked the network’s graduation from “mainnet beta”… pic.twitter.com/kH7UONiIhF

The transition marks years of Solana’s development as the network moves toward a more mature production environment. Its Alpenglow upgrade targets 150-millisecond transaction finality, while Firedancer introduces another independently developed validator client.

In other words, Solana isn’t just chasing faster transactions anymore. It’s positioning itself as infrastructure for financial markets that cannot afford to move at internet meme speed.

Solana has upgraded, but another project is trying to bring its speed to Bitcoin without leaving Bitcoin’s liquidity behind. Bitcoin Hyper ($HYPER) is building a Bitcoin Layer 2 powered by the Solana Virtual Machine (SVM), combining high-speed execution with access to Bitcoin-based liquidity.

The project has already raised more than $33.2 million, while HYPER remains available at just $0.0136877 per token. That’s barely above one cent, with the presale approaching its scheduled November 26 closing date.

The timing is becoming increasingly difficult for prospective buyers to ignore. With fewer than 10 days remaining, the project is approaching its next major milestone as its testnet ecosystem expands.

Staking adds another incentive. Bitcoin Hyper offers a 35% APY for participants locking their tokens through its buy-and-stake option.

While Solana continues building its institutional presence, Bitcoin Hyper is pursuing a different opportunity: bringing Solana-style execution to Bitcoin’s enormous liquidity ecosystem. With more than $33 million already committed and the presale countdown approaching its final days, HYPER is positioning itself for attention ahead of its scheduled launch.

Here’s where things get interesting. Despite the institutional headlines, SOL remains considerably weaker than its September performance. The token has dropped from $125, leaving buyers scrambling to defend the $70 region.

The daily Relative Strength Index has also entered deeply oversold territory, displaying that sellers may have exhausted much of their immediate momentum. But this also creates an interesting setup for a potential relief rally.

The first challenge sits around $74–$75. Reclaiming this area could bring $80 into view, where bulls need a convincing breakout before targeting $89–$90. A successful recovery toward $90 would show a meaningful reversal from the recent lows. It would also give investors a reason to believe institutional adoption is finally influencing market sentiment.

However, the bears haven’t disappeared. Losing the $70 support region could expose SOL to another wave of selling. The next major downside target sits around $60, potentially wiping out another chunk of investor confidence.

The institutional story gives Solana a compelling long-term narrative. ICE’s potential validator participation, DTCC’s tokenization plans, and the network’s latest upgrade all strengthen its position in the race for blockchain-based financial infrastructure. But the market still needs Solana to deliver the money before a bullish price prediction.
