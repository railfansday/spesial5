Is crypto’s 2026 outlook at risk as regulatory FUD returns to Washington?

After the election, investors are looking for clues about crypto’s regulatory future.

However, one thing is clear: the road ahead remains uncertain, with volatility already creeping into the market. The impact of the recent election goes beyond the broader U.S. macro environment, extending into the regulatory landscape as well. The crypto industry has seen huge gains under Republican leadership in the U.S. with President Donald Trump’s vision of making the country the “crypto capital of the world.”

However, with Republicans holding a narrow majority in Congress, investors are becoming more cautious about what comes next. The recent challenges faced with the CLARITY Act, and the increased focus from the SEC around tokenizations, are adding to the uncertainty in the regulatory outlook.

That said, signs of a regulatory shift are already starting to emerge.

In the latest development, House Democrats are drafting a new bill regarding how crypto should be structured moving forward. The bill includes expanding the SEC’s jurisdiction and will also include provisions excluded from the CLARITY Act. This comes ahead of any potential change in who controls Congress.

From a regulatory standpoint, tighter SEC oversight could mean stricter compliance rules, increased scrutiny of crypto firms, and greater regulatory pressure on digital asset companies operating in the U.S. The key takeaway? Regulatory pressure may not stop at tighter SEC oversight.

Senator Elizabeth Warren, known to be a strong crypto critic, is expected to become the Chair of the Senate Banking Committee, potentially giving her greater influence over financial regulation and crypto policy.

Is regulatory FUD shaping up to be the next major bearish catalyst for the broader crypto market?

The recent political upheaval in the U.S. Congress has already started to affect regulation. Discussions around a new crypto market structure bill, increasing anti-crypto sentiment and potential increased scrutiny from the SEC are adding a new layer of uncertainty to an already volatile market.

Further adding to the concern, Bitcoin [BTC] is looking bleak on its yearly chart. Historically, Bitcoin has never closed consecutive years in the red. However, this could change soon. Bitcoin is currently trading around 12% lower on its 2026 yearly candle, with just two months left before the year closes. This follows a red candle in 2025, putting BTC at risk of recording its first-ever back-to-back years of annual losses.

In essence, Bitcoin’s next move may depend on more than just market sentiment.

As regulatory uncertainty increases amid potential increased attention from the SEC, Bitcoin has another factor to contend with. If selling pressure continues, BTC could be heading toward its first-ever back-to-back annual losses, breaking a decade-long historical trend.

A new crypto bill could give the SEC more control, raising concerns about stricter crypto regulations.
