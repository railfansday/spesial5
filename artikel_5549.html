CTM Research Finds One in Five Adults Say They Tried to Contact a Mental Health Provider and Never Heard Back

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

New research examines persistent barriers to behavioral health care, generational differences in AI adoption, and the importance of human connection

ODENTON, Md. and MILLERSVILLE, Md., Nov. 10, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- One in five U.S. adults who tried to contact a mental health or addiction treatment provider reported they never received a response, according to CTM's second annual behavioral health survey. The findings reveal persistent challenges in accessing care and explore how consumers' attitudes toward artificial intelligence are shaping the way they seek behavioral health support.

The findings come amid ongoing challenges in the behavioral health system and growing interest in AI as a tool for navigating care. According to the Health Resources and Services Administration, 137 million Americans live in a mental health professional shortage area, underscoring the challenges many communities face in accessing care.

CTM's research suggests that while people are exploring new options, getting connected to a person who can help remains central to the care journey. Three-quarters of respondents said they are comfortable speaking with a live person by phone about a mental health or addiction concern, up from 64% in 2025. Yet one in five respondents said they had tried to contact a mental health or addiction treatment provider and never heard back, a figure unchanged from last year.

"When someone reaches out for help, they shouldn't have to wonder whether anyone will respond," said Todd Fisher, co-founder and CEO of CTM. "In behavioral healthcare, technology should help providers connect with people more quickly and consistently, not create another hurdle. Whether reaching out by phone, text, or another channel, the goal is to help them reach trusted care that can understand their situation and guide them toward support."

The data underscores an opportunity for behavioral health providers to make the path to care more accessible by offering multiple ways to connect, improving follow-up, and using technology to support the people responsible for helping patients. As AI becomes more embedded into how patients find health information, providers must consider not only which tools they adopt, but how those tools help people move from an initial question to meaningful support.

The full report, The Changing Face of Mental Health Access, explores these findings and their implications for behavioral health providers navigating evolving consumer expectations. To access the report, visit https://tinyurl.com/3pyys555.

CTM commissioned kNOW Instant Insights to survey 1,000 U.S. adults ages 18 and older from August 31 to September 3, 2026. Year-over-year comparisons are included where survey questions and response options were consistent.
