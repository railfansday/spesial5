OPEC crude oil output is nearing pre-war levels as Saudi Arabia rebounds

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

OPEC production rose 930,000 barrels a day in October to 22.8 million, about 88% of February levels, a Bloomberg survey shows

Andrey Rudakov / Bloomberg via Getty Images

OPEC pumped 22.8 million barrels of crude a day in October, a gain of 930,000 barrels a day that leaves output at about 88% of its February level, according to a Bloomberg survey.

Saudi Arabia was responsible for the bulk of the increase, according to Bloomberg. The kingdom boosted output by 970,000 barrels a day to 9.2 million, leaving it roughly 800,000 barrels daily short of the pace it held when the year began. Saudi oil exports ran at an average of 7 million barrels daily in October, according to tanker-tracking data compiled by Bloomberg. Bloomberg attributed the gain to Saudi Arabia routing a larger number of tankers via the Strait of Hormuz and loading cargoes along its Red Sea coast, despite hostilities connected to the U.S.-Iran conflict.

Iraq added 140,000 barrels a day to reach 4.15 million, and Kuwait added 130,000 to reach 2 million, according to Bloomberg. Those gains were canceled out by lower output in Nigeria and Iran.

Iran's output dropped 120,000 barrels daily to 1.9 million, the weakest reading in Bloomberg's monthly records going back to 1989. Bloomberg said satellite imagery has not captured a single tanker at Iranian export terminals, Kharg Island among them, since late August, with the U.S. blockade of Iran's ports still in force.

Brent crude traded near $98 a barrel this week.

Saudi Aramco's CEO, Amin Nasser, said at an Abu Dhabi industry conference earlier in the week that the firm intends to "maximize production going forward in the fourth quarter," according to Bloomberg.

Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Iraq and Kuwait exported a combined 15.53 million barrels of crude a day in October, a 10% increase that put volumes in line with levels at the start of the year. Kuwait's shipments of 1.44 million barrels a day were the highest since before the war began. Iran's exports stayed at zero for a second month.

JPMorgan $JPM estimates Middle East oil exports now stand near 20.7 million barrels a day, up from 9.8 million in March, although they have yet to return to where they were before the conflict erupted in late February, according to Reuters.

OPEC and its allies are examining the volumes each member is technically capable of producing, as groundwork for 2027 output targets to be set at a ministerial meeting on Nov. 29, according to Bloomberg.
