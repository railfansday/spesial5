Cutting Back on Family Gifts – Propose a New Rule Before Anyone Shops

If your family’s gift giving has outgrown your budget, say so plainly, weeks before the first gift is bought. Then propose one new rule, such as gifts for the kids only, a price cap, or a year off from presents. A specific plan is easy to say yes to, and no one gets caught off guard.

The relative you’re dreading telling may be relieved to hear it. In Drive Research’s 2026 holiday survey, 32% of holiday shoppers said they feel pushed to spend more on gifts than their budget comfortably allows.

So raise it as a change that saves everyone time and money, not as a hardship you have to explain. A vague apology singles you out. The others keep swapping gifts, and you still get presents you feel you owe back. A specific rule for the whole family puts everyone on the same footing.

Timing matters as much as the wording. The National Retail Federation’s 2026 holiday survey found that 43% of holiday shoppers planned to start shopping before November, so propose a rule for holiday gifts by early October. After October, send the proposal before Thanksgiving weekend, since 64% of consumers in the survey said they were likely to wait until then to do most of their shopping.

For a family or friend group that swaps gifts at every birthday, propose the rule a few weeks before the next one. Say it in person or in one group message, so everyone hears the same plan at once.

Keep a fallback ready for anyone who objects. Picture a sister who texts her four siblings in early October to propose kids-only gifts and a family dinner out. Three write back relieved. The fourth still wants to swap gifts, so she suggests a $25 cap for anyone who keeps giving.

Then keep to the rule yourself, and expect a relative or two to give anyway. Thank them warmly, but don’t buy a gift back, since that restarts the exchange you just ended.
