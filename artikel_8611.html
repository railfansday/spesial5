Price Comparison Websites – Search Now, Track What Can Wait, and Check at Checkout

No single price comparison site always finds the lowest price, because each kind works differently. Use a shopping search engine when you’re ready to buy, put a price tracker on anything you can wait for, and let a browser extension check for a lower price or a coupon code at checkout.

In the second quarter of 2026, online sales made up 17.1% of all U.S. retail sales, by the Census Bureau’s count, adjusted for seasonal swings. With that much buying done online, checking prices before you pay is a habit worth building.

Each kind of tool looks for a lower price at a different moment. A shopping search engine compares many stores at once and shows today’s prices side by side. A price tracker watches one item for days or weeks and alerts you when it falls to the price you set. A browser extension works while you shop and looks for a lower price or a working coupon code before you pay.

Deal websites work the other way around. They post whatever happens to be on sale, while a comparison site starts from the item you name.

Use one of these when you’re ready to buy. Search for the exact item, with its model number if it has one, and compare prices with shipping added. If the lowest price is at a store you’d rather not buy from, ask your usual store whether it matches competitors’ prices.

Google Shopping lists offers from stores that send Google their product details, so one search lines up many sellers’ prices for the same item. It can also tell you whether today’s price is low, typical, or high compared with the past three months. To have it watch a price, sign in, tap Track price, and set a target. Google then emails you and sends an alert through its app when the price drops below that target.

The catch is that offers labeled Sponsored are ads, and a store’s payment to Google can affect where they rank. A store that doesn’t list its products there won’t appear at all. Before you buy, check the final price, shipping included, on the store’s own site.

ShopSavvy compares prices at thousands of stores, online and nearby, and its free app scans a product’s barcode in a store to show whether another seller has it for less. It also charts how each product’s price has moved and can watch an item for a lower price or a restock.

Its alerts can lag. Re-checks run on the phones and computers of whoever is watching the item, and on ShopSavvy’s own servers for some large stores, so it never promises an alert the instant a price falls. A price it hasn’t seen for over 30 days carries a note that it may have changed.

Pricepirates sets eBay listings, auctions still running among them, beside offers from thousands of online stores. Its apps for Windows, Mac, iPhone, and Android are free and show no ads. Start with it for anything you’d buy used or from an eBay seller.

An auction’s price is only its current bid, which can climb before the auction ends.

A tracker suits a purchase that can wait. Tell it the price you’d pay, and it watches the price for you.

camelcamelcamel tracks prices on Amazon and emails you when an item drops to the price you name. Its charts show each item’s price history, so you can tell whether today’s price is near its recent low. The Camelizer, its free browser extension, puts the same chart on the Amazon product page.

It covers only Amazon, so it never tells you when another store sells the item for less.

BuyVia’s free app compares prices at online stores and local chains such as Walmart, Target, and Best Buy. Set an alert on an item, and it sends a push notification or an email when the item goes on sale online or in a store near you.

Its sale alerts skip Amazon, so put a camelcamelcamel alert on anything you might buy there.

An extension runs inside your web browser and reads the store page you’re on, so it can look for a better deal without a separate search. Many shopping extensions only pay cash back or test coupon codes, while these three also check what other sellers charge.

When you look at a product on Amazon, Capital One Shopping checks other sellers and shows a lower-priced match if it finds one, and at checkout it tests promo codes on its own. It also alerts you when something you viewed or bought drops in price, and a drop soon after you buy is the moment to ask the store for a price adjustment. You don’t need a Capital One card or bank account to use it.

In return, it records the pages you view, what you buy, and the price you paid. Its rewards are gift cards, not cash.

Honey, which PayPal owns, looks for coupon codes on more than 30,000 sites and tests them at checkout with one click. On Amazon, it compares sellers with shipping costs and Prime status counted, and its Droplist alerts you when an item you saved drops in price.

Droplist watches only the store you saved the item from. Honey also records the stores and pages you visit, and what you buy there, while you use it.

Price.com compares prices on new, used, and refurbished items. Its extension works at more than 100,000 stores, where it applies coupon codes and cash back at checkout, and its website can email you when the price of an item you want falls.

The extension asks for access to your data on every website you visit, not just stores.

Start with the next item on your shopping list. Search it on Google Shopping now, and if it can wait, tap Track price and set the price you’d pay.
