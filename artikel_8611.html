Ground Ambulance Bill – The No Surprises Act Won’t Cap It, but Your State Might

The No Surprises Act protects you from surprise air ambulance bills but not ground ambulance bills. Before you pay a ground ambulance bill, check whether your state caps it, make sure your insurer processed the ride as an emergency, and ask the ambulance service for a discount or payment plan.

With private insurance, the No Surprises Act keeps an air ambulance from charging you more than an in-network one would, as long as your plan covers emergency care. What you owe a ground ambulance depends on your state’s law, which may cap the bill at what an in-network ambulance would charge you.

People in 22 states had some protection from surprise ground ambulance bills as of February 2026, the Commonwealth Fund found. But states can’t regulate self-funded employer plans, where the employer pays claims from its own funds, and 71% of workers with job-based coverage are in such a plan, according to KFF’s 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey.

Say your state limits you to what an in-network ride would cost and your plan’s in-network ambulance copay is $150. A $1,800 bill for the part your insurer didn’t pay then drops to $150.

Ask your state insurance department whether a state law covers your ride and your plan. Your HR office, or the number on your insurance card, can tell you whether the plan is self-funded.

Next, check the bill against your explanation of benefits, the statement your insurer sends showing what it paid and what you owe. If the insurer denied the claim or processed the ride as a nonemergency, ask it to process the claim again as an emergency, and appeal in writing if it won’t.

Then call the ambulance service’s billing office to negotiate the bill. Many ambulance services are part of a fire department or a government agency. A federal advisory committee reported in 2024 that some public services, including those in Baltimore and Baton Rouge, waive patients’ share of the bill for emergency rides.

Ask whether the service cuts charges for residents or for financial hardship, and for a payment plan on whatever is left. Every step here can wait until after the ride, so never let worry about a bill delay a 911 call.
