Paying Taxes by Credit Card – Worth It Only for a Bonus or 0% APR

Putting your tax bill on a credit card costs a processing fee of 1.75% to 1.85%, nearly all of what even a 2% cash back card returns. Pay the IRS by card only when the payment earns a sign-up bonus you’d otherwise miss, or a 0% intro rate lets you spread a bill you can’t pay at once.

On a $5,000 tax bill, that fee comes to $87.50 to $92.50. A 1.5% cash back card earns back only $75, and even a 2% card’s $100 leaves you just $7.50 to $12.50 ahead.

The IRS’s card payment page lists two payment processors. Pay1040 charges 1.75% on a consumer credit card and ACI Payments 1.85%, each with a $2.50 minimum. An American Express card costs 2.89% at Pay1040, so pay with one through ACI Payments. Paying through tax software as you e-file costs 2.49% or more. A sign-up bonus or months of 0% interest can beat those fees by hundreds of dollars.

Say a new card pays a $750 bonus after $6,000 in purchases, and you owe $6,000. Charge the bill and you pay $105 to $111 in fees and keep more than $600 of the bonus.

A card with a 0% intro APR, an opening stretch with no interest on purchases, lets you pay off the bill over months for nothing but the fee. An IRS payment plan charges interest and a monthly late-payment penalty until you finish, and most plans longer than 180 days carry a setup fee. Suppose you filed on time and need a year to pay $6,000. At the IRS’s late-2026 rates, a plan adds about $325 in interest and penalties, plus its setup fee, against $105 to $111 in fees on a card at 0% that long.

Pay the balance off in equal monthly amounts before the intro rate ends, since whatever’s left then is charged interest at the card’s regular APR. Skip the card in the months before you apply for a mortgage or car loan. A $6,000 balance on a $10,000 limit uses 60% of that limit, which can lower your credit score until the balance comes down.

IRS Direct Pay takes the payment from your bank account for free, and a debit card costs a flat $2.10 to $2.15. Before you charge the bill, multiply it by 1.85% and use the card only when its bonus or the IRS interest and penalties you’d avoid come to more.
