Week 13 college football betting, odds: 3 early bets to make right now, including in Oklahoma-Missouri

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

College football rivalry week is here, and some of the toughest teams to price are the ones making changes this late in the season.

Oklahoma has won three straight with Jason Witten calling plays, Minnesota just won after changing its offensive coordinator and Wisconsin got a big performance from a quarterback who had lost his starting job. There are reasons to believe those offenses are improving, but I want to be careful about how much of last Saturday carries into this week.

I'm starting with three unders, looking at the way these teams have been moving the ball and how much work it takes them to score. Here are my three early Week 13 bets.

Oklahoma has looked better since Witten took over. John Mateer has settled down, and the offense is giving its defense a chance to rest. I understand why people would expect more points here.

Against Ole Miss, all three of Oklahoma's offensive scoring drives took at least 12 plays, and Isaiah Sategna added a punt-return touchdown, which helped turn a game in which Mateer threw for 111 yards into a 24-17 win. Then against Texas A&M, the Sooners were tied entering the fourth quarter before finishing a 15-play touchdown drive that took more than eight minutes. A later interception set up a one-play touchdown to Sategna, making the final score look a lot more comfortable than the game had been.

Witten can keep Mateer in manageable situations, use the running game and keep Missouri's offense off the field. Oklahoma's defense has held Florida and Texas A&M to seven points apiece during this stretch, with Ole Miss scoring 17 between them.

Missouri will test it. QB Austin Simmons threw four touchdown passes against Kentucky, and WR Donovan Olugbode caught two of them. I'm more concerned about those receivers creating quick scores than Missouri consistently driving the length of the field. The Tigers also allowed Kentucky into the red zone seven times while giving up only 14 points, so their defense gave up more chances than the final score suggests.

I expect Oklahoma to stick with the patient approach that has worked under Witten, while making Simmons put together longer drives to score.

Wisconsin just scored 42 points at Purdue, so this is a pretty low number to come back and bet under.

RB Colton Joseph returned to the starting job and ran for 187 yards, including touchdowns from 32 and 66 yards out. Darrion Dupree broke a long touchdown run, too. Purdue couldn't handle the quarterback run game, and Wisconsin kept going back to it. Minnesota has to do a better job there.

The Gophers allowed Northwestern to rush for 196 yards, and Joseph gave Wisconsin a way to turn a missed assignment into a touchdown without throwing the ball. That's the biggest concern with this bet — but I still don't trust Minnesota to score much.

P.J. Fleck fired offensive coordinator Greg Harbaugh Jr. after the Penn State loss, and Eric Koehler took over the play-calling against Northwestern. Darius Taylor ran the ball better, and two missed field goals hurt, but the Gophers still went into halftime scoreless. Their first touchdown required a fourth-down conversion, and Anthony Smith then forced and recovered a fumble to give Minnesota a short field for its second TD. QB Drake Lindsey made a great throw late to set up the winning field goal, but even with those plays, Minnesota only finished with 20 points. It hasn't scored more than that in any of its last six games.

I can give the coaching change some credit without expecting everything to be fixed before a trip to Wisconsin. The Badgers' defense ranks No. 21 in our ratings, and neither offense is in much of a hurry between snaps. If Minnesota can make Joseph and Dupree string together first downs instead of breaking long runs, Wisconsin can move the ball while taking plenty of time off the clock.

San José State nearly forced overtime against UNLV, but the offense still had to work awfully hard to get to 22 points.

QB Luke Weaver threw 40 passes while the Spartans ran for just 56 yards. They needed a late touchdown pass to Carson Clark to get within a 2-point conversion, then couldn't complete the tying attempt. A week earlier, they scored three points at Air Force. We still rank this offense 128th, and I'm not convinced Weaver gets enough help from the running game to keep it moving against North Dakota State.

The Bison's defense certainly gave us a reason to hesitate last week. They led Northern Illinois 31-3 before allowing touchdowns on the Huskies' final three possessions. I still put more weight on a defense we rank No. 29, which held Hawai'i to 13 points and UTEP to seven in its previous two games. San José State needs to sustain drives against that group, and its last two games haven't given me much confidence it can.

QB Nathan Hayes is coming off a career-high four touchdown passes, but the Bison finished Saturday by adding defensive tackles as blockers and handing the ball to DJ Scott to run out the clock. With North Dakota State favored here, I expect it to lean on the running game if it gets ahead. I'll back its defense to keep San José State from turning this into a game Hayes has to win through the air.
