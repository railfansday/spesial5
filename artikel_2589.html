Austin Reaves talks for first time ahead of Spurs clash: ‘Felt like I broke my foot’

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Austin Reaves, who has missed the last five games because of a bruised left heel, was a full participant in Lakers practice on Wednesday but his status remains unknown for Friday's game against the Spurs.

"It felt good to play basketball again," Reaves said in his first public comments since suffering the injury Nov. 11. "Wind was a little bad, but kind of to be expected being out two weeks. But felt good. You know, we'll see how I wake up [Thursday]. We're still going in the right direction, but felt really good today."

Austin Reaves, left, and Luka Doncic talk during a game against the Utah Jazz in Los Angeles, Wednesday, Nov. 18, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

Reaves sustained the injury after landing awkwardly following a missed fast-break dunk in the third quarter of a game against the Bucks.

He said he's not quite sure what happened on the play.

"No, I had no idea," he said. "Tried to play through it, and then I drove baseline, planted on it, and was like, I can't do this. So walked to the back and was in some pretty bad pain. I didn't think I broke my foot, but it sure felt like I broke my foot."

Lakers coach JJ Redick, who suffered a similar injury in April 2016, has repeatedly described the heel bruise as a "pain tolerance issue." Reaves echoed that Wednesday.

"It's not a strain in your hamstring or something like that," Reaves said. "It's kind of just something that's going to linger around if you continue to play before you're ready. And basically, the main message was, let's get this back to normal before we put you back out there and put yourself at risk again so later on in the season you won't have to deal with a flare up or something."

The Lakers went 4-1 after Reaves suffered the injury, but they clearly missed his offensive production after falling to the Jazz on Nov. 18, 109-97, and barely squeaking past the Hornets (118-114) and Kings (106-104). Reaves is averaging 24.7 points (51.3% shooting from the field, 40.9% from beyond the arc), 3.3 rebounds and 4.7 assists.

"One of those things that just, it sucks," Reaves said. "You know, I was rolling, playing well. We were rolling as a team, wanted to continue to be out there and play games. Hate missing games. But it's one of those things, like I said, there wasn't much that we could really do. But we took care of business."

Austin Reaves #15 of the Los Angeles Lakers reacts during a game against the Utah Jazz at Crypto.com Arena on Monday, Nov. 23, 2026, in Los Angeles. NBAE via Getty Images

When asked how Reaves looked Wednesday, Redick didn't hesitate.

When reporters prodded him to see if he was being sarcastic, he doubled down, "Yeah, not joking," he added.

Austin Reaves (15) of the Los Angeles Lakers smiles during a game against the Utah Jazz at Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles on Wednesday, Nov. 18, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

The Lakers sure hope that Reaves can return Friday and that Redick is right considering the challenge they have ahead of them.

The Lakers and Spurs are tied for the best record in the NBA at 15-3 and Luka Doncic and Victor Wembanyama are currently in a two-man race for the MVP Award.

When asked if it's important to him to return against the Spurs, Reaves balked.

"I mean, I want to play," Reaves said. "…I didn't get hurt the night of Milwaukee and look at the schedule and be like, 'God, I need to be back before the San Antonio game.' If I'm back, good, we'll go to war. But I got to listen to my body and take care of myself. But it should be fun if I'm healthy and get out there."
