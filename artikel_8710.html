JJ Spaun Got His Swing Back After a Misdiagnosis Nearly Wrecked His PGA Career

PGA star John Michael “JJ” Spaun has been at home with the golf clubs since he was gifted that first plastic toy set at age 3. But while the young protégé followed in his mother’s golf-playing footsteps, and furthered them by making progress in the PGA, a mysterious health issue would begin to derail his game.

Spaun sat down with M&F to discuss his initial Type 2 diabetes diagnosis, and how finally being discovered as Type 1 brought him back to greener pastures.

“I would say, in the majority, golf was a pretty easy sport for me to pick up,” says Spaun. “But I think I battled with what time and effort I wanted to put into it as a kid. Growing up in Southern California, other sports were pulling me in. Skateboarding was a huge presence at the time. I wanted to do what the other kids were doing culturally, which was skateboarding and extreme sports.”

In his youth, Spaun played a variety of sports aside from golf, including skateboarding, bicycling, baseball, basketball, and hockey. But while he did eventually fully embrace golf, his other experiences began to pay off. “I think those sports all played a role in my development, in my mechanics,” says Spaun. “Like hand-eye coordination, footwork. My swing coach, who I have been with for around six years, is a firm believer that skateboarding really developed my footwork for my golf swing even as a child. He considers my feet to be my superpower.”

JJ Spaun has just completed his 10th season with the PGA. He turned professional in 2012 and earned his first PGA Tour Card in 2016, but the players’ game began to take a downturn in 2018. He struggled with consistency and even lost his tour card during the 2020-21 season, where he placed 174th in the FedEx Cup rankings. Since the summer of 2018, Spaun found himself almost unable to complete a round of golf.

“I was 28 at the time,” he recalls. “I remember putting on my belt and running out of holes to put the notch in, so my belt was now too big for me. I suffered with severe thirst at night, and then frequently urinating, like, three or four times every night. I started losing weight very rapidly.”

Initially chalking all this up to a demanding golf season, Spaun decided to see a doctor after weighing himself and realizing that he’d inexplicably lost another 20 pounds in just a couple of months. The athlete was diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes and hoped that the treatment would help him return to his prior form on the golf course. But that wasn’t to be.

“After three years, losing 50 pounds, going down to 155 pounds, I hadn’t weighed that since high school, so I finally got advice from an endocrinologist,” shares Spaun, who was then diagnosed with Type 1 diabetes, rather than Type 2.

Sadly, with 90% of diabetes sufferers having Type 2, medical advances for detecting Type 1 have been slower to emerge. Type 2 is often triggered by obesity, whereas Type 1 is thought to be more of a genetic issue causing the immune system to attack insulin producing cells in the pancreas. “They felt sorry for me, telling me that I was Type 1, but I said this is great, because I’ve been living miserably,” says Spaun. “My energy was at an all-time low. My golf was going down the drain. And now I had a real solution.”

Finally gaining the correct diagnosis in 2021, Spaun started insulin therapy. “In a matter of weeks, I could feel the strength coming back,” says the top golfer. “I was able to eat things without restricting my glucose range, as you do with Type 2. I started to feel better and things started coming together.”

Losing so much weight took time to overcome, however. “Your mechanics and your movements are off,” explains Spaun. “I’m glad to be handling things well. It’s definitely a challenge, but I’ve got all the support in the world to really make it as easy for me as possible.”

Incredibly, with the weight coming back and his treatment working well, Spaun had his breakthrough season in 2025, including a major victory at the 125th US Open. Spaun tells M&F that he uses a Dexcom G7 continuous glucose monitor to keep his levels in check and has recently switched from insulin injections to a pump system known as the Omnipod 5. “It’s been a game changer for me,” he shares.

In September 2026, Spaun was also able combine his love of traveling to England with his passion for golf, winning the BMW PGA Championship at Wentworth, and even donating a portion of his winnings to the Addington Fund, a British Charity that supports farming families. The golfer tells M&F that he discovered the financial hardships that many UK farms face during his holidays in the Cotswolds, where discovered the popular Prime series, Clarkson’s Farm.

“I’m the type of guy that likes to put in the work, because I’m a strong believer that you get out of this game what you put in,” says Spaun. But with the official season now over, the able athlete can look ahead to more family time, and another trip back to England.

No doubt, JJ Spaun is a highly decorated athlete with an even promising future, but living with diabetes has taught him that being present in the moment is more important than any trophy, so we conclude our interview and Spaun heads out on the school run.
