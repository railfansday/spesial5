Iris Excursion

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

I close out 6 days of backpacking while diverting onto an alternate route to change things up.

Nature’s alarm system worked well through the night, perhaps too well. I felt pretty good that bears were unlikely to hang around a part of the forest so near to roads and homes. I’d been seeing deer consistently since I started hiking in New Jersey. I was awoken by leaf-crunching more times that I can remember. Each time I listened carefully to the steps, and each time I concluded that the animal was a deer. It was certainly too loud to be a squirrel, but not intrusive enough to be something as heavy as a bear. They took their sweet time moving on as well. At one point I was fed up to the point that I decided to unzip my tent and have a look. The sound of me unzipping my tent was enough for the animal to snort loudly – the hallmark sound of a deer warning other nearby deer of something out of the ordinary.

You might think spending 12 hours in my tent would be enough that I’d be off like a shot a first light. It sure would be nice to get back to my van early. Maybe I should have bought less cozy sleeping gear. Whatever the case, I made absolutely no haste this morning, quickly convincing myself to stay out of the 38 degree morning. I made breakfast, spilling a few ounces of water in my tent in the process which prompted a formidable snort of my own. I was glad that out of all the gear I’ve shed over the months, my large camping towel was not one of them.

It was almost 9:00AM when I got everything packed up and started making tracks on the AT. I was still on track to finish hiking in the 4:00 hour. However, there were some opportunities to save a bit of time on the way back. I decided it wasn’t worth taking the spur trail up to the High Point a second time, which opened up a shortcut back to the State Park HQ that was considerably flatter. I hardly felt the climb back up the ridge, the biggest issue was the thick layer of leaves spread across the trail.

Yesterday, as I neared the High Point State Park, I noticed several trails intersecting with the AT. One of them, the Iris Trail, crossed the AT several times. I studied the trail on FarOut and found that it was roughly the same distance, but definitely flatter. The hardest parts of the AT yesterday were exactly what the Iris Trail would be avoiding. I decided this would be a worthy alternate route that would give me some new things to see.

The first thing I noticed on this trail was how much easier it was than the AT. It was wider, graded nicely, and largely devoid of rocks. Unlike the AT, bikes are allowed, though I only saw one gentleman riding a bike on the trail. The trail passes Lake Rutherford, a surprisingly significant lake hidden up along the ridge. I was enjoying my easy ride on the Iris Trail until I felt my shoes sink into the trail. I didn’t react right away, taking another step, hoping the soft spot was a one-off. It was not. My shoe plunged several inches into wet mud. What looked like more pleasant, leafy trail was a streak of muddy trail several feet wide and about 20 feet long. I jumped off the trail, feeling my right foot suddenly overcome by cold water. I surveyed the trail, no sign of mud, except where I’d just stepped, the perfect trap. The mud had come up and over my toe box. I snorted again.

The creek that was discolored yesterday was back to normal clear flow today. Weird.

A pond near some pastures below the New Jersey High Point.

I made my way around the mud and continued on the Iris Trail, crossing the AT twice more before rejoining it for good at the Mashipacong Shelter. A few miles later I was taking in the view at the pavilion on Sunrise Mountain. Taking the Iris Trail allowed me to make up some time, I was now tracking toward getting back to the van at 3:30PM. Once I got back to the Culver Fire Tower, I knew it would all be downhill. I had managed to nurse my ankle along for the last two days, I just had to keep it that way as I made my way down the leafy rocks leading back to my van at Culvers Gap. I took my time and used Sunrise Mountain Road to save a few steps. I rewarded my early arrival with some basic hygiene activities – teeth brushing, and a shave with my electric razor. I still look a bit scraggly, I haven’t had a haircut since July, but that’s what hats are for.

It was a short drive to Newton, New Jersey for my usual serving of town food. I had picked this town because it also had a cafe open late – 10:00PM, where I proceeded to write up the blog posts from yesterday and today. I finished the day off by bagging up all my food for the upcoming week, one more Sunday chore off my back. Thanks to finding a good writing spot after each of my 3 2-day backpacking stints this week I would be able to have plenty of down time tomorrow.
