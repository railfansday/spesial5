A wraparound FEHB plan is generally considered to be a plan which, after Medicare Parts A and B pay their amounts, then [FEHB] picks up the remaining costs of the deductible, copays, and coinsurances (but not for Rx--at least none I have seen).

As an example, a hospital stay is covered by Part A except for the (2026) deductible of $1736. The FEHB plan then pays the $1736. Doctors and so forth charge $400. Part B covers 80% or $320. FEHB then pays the remaining $80 coinsurance/copay. The FEHB plan would also pay the Part B deductible of $283. In addition, if the FEHB plan normally has a deductible, the FEHB plan "waives" it.

Not all FEHB plans do this. There are plans that clearly state in their brochures in Section 9--about how they work with Medicare--that they do not waive the plan deductible, copays, or coinsurance.

This is why I believe it is important to closely read the brochures. In the 5 years we have had Aetna Direct and Medicare A and B, we have paid zero out of pocket except the FEHB premium and Part B premiums and Rx coinsurance.

This is why one will see articles about looking for plans to reduce costs. I still sometimes read about people on a $900-$1000/mo premium plan when they also have Parts A and B. They could choose a plan with a premium less than half that amount. We saved approx $500 per month over the last 5 years just by changing from our prior plan to our current plan. That's $30,000 over 5 years. Now, there might be situations where a person needs a particular plan for their situation, such as Rx, overseas coverage, etc., but it can help to do the research.
