Have you heard? MatPat’s return, Tubi CEO’s next move, and Trisha Paytas’ payday.

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Each week, we handpick a selection of stories to give you a snapshot of trends, updates, business moves, and more from around the creator industry.

This week, a beloved couple has a new channel on YouTube, a vaunted exec has a new gig, and a much-memed creator is making bank (and hitting bookshelves).

Welcome back to YouTube, Matthew and Stephanie Patrick. The man known as MatPat and his wife Stephanie haven’t shared many videos since they sold their Theorist brand nearly four years ago. But these crazy kids couldn’t stay offline forever, so they’re back with PatChat, a new channel featuring the couple’s conversations about internet culture. PatChat won’t upload nearly as frequently as the Theorist channels, and that’s ok. Let the Patricks live a little bit!

Daily industry updates, straight to your inbox.

CNN panelist pushes back against Hasan Piker lawsuit. Piker argued that Scott Jennings defamed him by misconstruing statements that were clearly said in jest. In response, Jennings’ lawyers argued that Piker did make those statements, sarcastic or not — so they are looking to have the $72.5 million lawsuit thrown out. If commentators can be held liable for sarcasm, the internet might be in real trouble.

WhatCultureGaming launches new YouTube channel to pursue different types of content. After building a popular gaming hub, the WhatCulture team is trying something a little different with MegaWhat. The plan is to release original, personality-driven content that will give the WC team more creative freedom while still allowing them to feed the YouTube algorithm on their main channel.

Anjali Sud leaves Tubi to take point at SiriusXM. As CEO of Tubi, Sud led the transformation of the Fox-owned service, which is now a major creator hub. (She was also CEO of Vimeo before that.) Now, Sud is becoming CEO of SiriusXM, giving her a chance to work her magic in the realm of digital radio. Mike Bidgoli will take over for Sud as the new top exec at Tubi HQ.

Elevate Communications brings in TikTok vet to expand creator operations. Elevate is elevating Sam Valentine, who previously connected TikTok with luxury fashion brands as the app’s Head of Digital, Social, and Creator Services. He’ll spearhead digital and social partnerships at Elevate, where he will work with clients that are eager to build creator programs in sectors like sports, entertainment, and healthcare.

Meta brings in well-traveled exec to be the VP of its in-house content studio. After a long stint at NBC News, as well as stops at Goldman Sachs and BlackRock, Rich Latour has arrived at Meta. He will be put in charge of a studio that emphasizes Meta’s brand and highlights its impact across various communities and industries.

Trisha Paytas has a book out (and she makes $100,000 a week on YouTube). While promoting her memoir Crying on the Kitchen Floor, which was recently published by Hachette, Paytas told Andy Cohen that her YouTube earnings can hit six figures during a good week. Oh, and she also has a new dating show that is rolling out on Tubi. Slow down, Trish!

Hulu taps Tinx to revive The Circle (now with TikTok voting). The creator born Christina Najjar is taking the hosting role in a new version of the reality competition series that previously aired on Netflix. Tinx will have big shoes to fill replacing previous host Michelle Buteau, but with help from the voting audience, she can ensure that the Circle revival doesn’t miss a beat.

The first Clio Creator Awards will fete Dhar Mann. Actor Terry Crews will host as the notable advertising award ceremony expands to the realm of creator content. Dhar Mann, YouTube moralist and engineer of many a brand partnership, will receive an inaugural honorary award at the new ceremony.

Kind works with a Savannah Banana to sell a TikTok-exclusive foodstuff. Baseball player Jackson Olson has amassed a significant TikTok following after getting a roster spot on the best-known “Banana Ball” team. Olson is now parlaying his influence to sell a new type of Kind bar that celebrates World Kindness Day — and is only available for purchase via TikTok Shop.

GTA VI codes are flying around on Kick and Twitch ahead of launch day. All of the major platforms want creators to play GTA VI on their services once the game launches, and giving out free copies is a good way to engender that loyalty. At this point, I’m wondering if GTA VI will be able to live up to its immersurable hype, or if viewers will tire of it after it because the main topic of conversation everywhere.

Barbie and Hot Wheels are on TikTok Shop this holiday season. TikTok continues to onboard name brands as it prepares for its most lucrative holiday season shopping spree. So if someone on your list has their eyes on some Mattel products, deals on those items will be hitting TikTok Shop in no time.

Celebs are consenting to having their voices cloned for AI ads. Ogilvy and CAA have teamed up for a partnership that will make a-list talent more accessible for advertisers and agencies that rely on generative AI pipelines. George Clooney is a CAA client, but if he goes along with this, I’m going to have no choice but to start calling him George Cloney.

Shaq is good at dunking, but can he hide in plain sight? The Big Diesel has a new gig: An ABC series that will ask contestants to hide the 7’1″ goliath in plain sight. I think, to be fair, the contestants should get to start by hiding Kevin Hart before working their way up to Shaq.

Another impulse spending deterrent is taking off in China. The WeChat mini-app dubbed ‘Wallet, Please Wait’ lets users decide whether strangers will be able to hold off on big purchases. I would be more interested in app that lets me vote on whether a stranger should be able to tell a stranger how to spend their money.
