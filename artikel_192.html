Nvidia has reinstated Jensen Huang as its CEO. The company announced on Monday morning that the board “voted unanimously to reinstate Mr. Huang following a series of discussions and meetings with Mr. Huang, after which the Board was satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang’s candor and ability to lead NVIDIA.”

In its statement, the board says that it received input “from senior levels of the United States government” and determined that “Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.” Donald Trump responded to the board’s decision in a post on Truth Social, saying Nvidia “made the correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO… The United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security. The Attorney General of the United States has confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.”

The board’s statement says that its investigation into reports that Huang was the target of a Chinese spy plot will continue, with Huang’s support and cooperation, while interim CEO Colette Kress will continue as CFO, and Harvey Jones will remain as the recently appointed board chair.

“I am honored to return to NVIDIA and lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a new industrial revolution,” Huang said in a statement. “I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment. I want to thank the NVIDIA Board for its confidence and deliberation through this process.” This move comes after Jones said during an earnings call last week that the Nvidia board “does not intend” to return Huang as CEO.

Huang resigned as CEO earlier this month following reports of extramarital affairs with an alleged Chinese spy and two Nvidia employees, including an executive who reported to him. In the wake of his departure, more than 200,000 people, including Tesla CEO Elon Musk, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan, and Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick, signed a letter to Nvidia’s board, calling for his reinstatement.

Nvidia shares tumbled in Huang’s absence, while industry analysts raised concerns about an outsider replacing him at a time when Nvidia is so critical to the AI boom. Nonetheless, the board maintained as recently as last Tuesday that Huang wasn’t coming back.
