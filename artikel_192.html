US midterm elections 2026 live updates: Democrats win key Senate and House races in battle for control of Congress

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Democrats Roy Cooper, Jon Ossoff, and Chris Pappas notch key Senate wins; Florida Republican Ashley Moody and Byron Donalds prevail in Senate and governor races, respectively

With polls closed on the east coast, we’ve seen a number of races called already. Here are the top lines we’re following:

Jon Ossoff has been re-elected to the Senate in Georgia, defeating Mike Collins, his Republican challenger.

Roy Cooper, North Carolina’s former governor, was elected to represent the state in the US Senate, marking the first time in 10 years voters sent a Democrat to Congress’s upper chamber.

Chris Pappas, the four-term Democratic congressman, has won New Hampshire’s US Senate race, protecting the open seat from a Republican flip in a closely fought contest against John Sununu, the former senator.

Democratic senator Mark Warner has won re-election in the US Senate race in Virginia. He fended off a challenge from Bert Mizusawa, a Republican.

Republican Ashley Moody won the special election against progressive insurgent Angie Nixon in the US Senate race in Florida.

Democrat Debbie Wasserman Schultz won re-election in Florida’s 20th congressional district, securing a 12th term. She beat her Republican challenger, Brent Andersen.

Democrat Kathy Castor, who has served for 10 terms, won re-election in Florida’s 14th congressional district, which was re-drawn this year to boost Republicans.

Democratic congressman Jared Moskowitz also won re-election in the redrawn 25th congressional district in Florida, beating out his Republican challenger Scott Singer.

Muted – and nervous cheers – at the Democrats’ watch party here as CNN’s early returns show the party’s Senate candidate, Troy Jackson, fewer than 800 votes ahead of the Republican incumbent, Susan Collins, with about 12% of ballots counted. It could be a long and tense count.

Almost all polls have now closed in Idaho, Montana, Nevada and Utah.

One of the more under-the-radar gubernatorial races we’re watching is in Nevada, where Democratic attorney general Aaron Ford is hoping to unseat Republican governor Joe Lombardo in the Sun Belt battleground state that Trump won in 2024.

Many polling places in one Oregon county will also close at 10pm ET.

In a surprise to no one, the Adam Hamilton watch party is indeed playing tracks from the Hamilton soundtrack along with other cheery pop hits. (The DJ has also been playing anthems associated with other Democratic leaders – including Obama’s anthem, Signed, Sealed, Delivered by Stevie Wonder, and Bill Clinton’s Don’t Stop by Fleetwood Mac.)

The mood is high here, with attendees cheering everytime CNN shows positive results for Democrats anywhere around the country. Many are waving miniature American flags or holding posters that say, “It’s time for something different.”

As the polls closed in Iowa at 8pm local time, Iowa’s Democratic party chair, Rita Hart, took the stage at a watch party for the Democratic candidates to commend volunteers and campaign staff for all their work, saying all that was left was to wait on the results.

The Democrats here at the state historical society of Iowa, right by the state capitol in Des Moines, are feeling “nauseously optimistic”, Hart said. Hart knows just how close elections can be here: in 2020, she lost a congressional race in the state’s second district by just six votes. “A six-vote loss is a loss you never forget,” she said.

Iowa voters have been inundated with ads – so much so, Iowa Democrat Micki Henderson said, that her 12-year-old son got a campaign ad pop-up on his school-issued iPad. She’s found other Iowans open to hearing about candidates outside their typical party this year.

“When you’re talking about local things, like water, there’s much more middle ground,” Henderson said.

Alison Meagher, a 22-year-old who has been knocking doors for third congressional district Democratic nominee Sarah Trone Garriott each weekend for the last couple months, said she is feeling both fear and excitement while awaiting the results.

“I will say, selfishly, it is a little nice to be recognized again and be in the news,” Meagher said. “It’s been a little while since we’ve been purple. It’s exciting, it’s a rush.”

As she’s knocked doors, people have been willing to hear about Trone Garriott and other Democratic candidates, often saying they’ll do some more research when they can.

“I think it helps that we all shop at the same gas stations and all shop at the same supermarkets,” she said. “I’m coming to you as a peer.”

Democrat Rebecca Bennett has won the race for New Jersey’s seventh congressional district, flipping a Republican district that congressman Tom Kean has held on to for three terms.

Notably, Kean, was not seen in Congress, or public, for more than three months this year. He also missed 100 votes without explanation – as Trump’s war on Iran piled pressure on the US economy, and the president threatened fresh tariffs on global imports.

The excitement and suspense is through the roof at the Michigan Dems’ official election night party, with food, drinks and a DJ playing a mix of dance and pop music to a packed house, while briefly pausing the music and announcing that the polls were closed, to raucous cheers

Polls are now closed in Minnesota, but as votes were being cast earlier one local government, in the Minneapolis suburb of Rogers, had to take time out from running its election to issue a statement on Facebook debunking viral misinformation promoted by rightwing influencers.

Officials in Rogers reassured voters that a poll worker, photographed wearing a white T-shirt spattered with fake blood and the words “Problem Solved”, was not celebrating the killing of Charlie Kirk, the late Republican activist, as rightwing figures with huge followings on the social media platform X claimed.

As the MS Now correspondent Brandy Zadrozny reports, the city of Rogers said that it looked into the viral claim that the election judge’s attire was a political statement and she had assured them that it was merely a Halloween costume, worn during a holiday shift on Saturday. “The election judge in question has given the City permission to note that they are a Republican judge pursuant to the requirement to maintain party balance per Minnesota Statute,” the statement added.

The false claim that the shirt was a mocking reference to Kirk’s killing was seen millions of times online after it was spread by a host of rightwing figures with huge followings on the social media platform X, including the Pizzagate conspiracy theorist Jack Posobiec, Townhall columnist Dustin Grage and Daily Wire writer Matt Walsh.

The photograph was first posted on X by Liz Collin, a partisan reporter for the rightwing Minnesota outlet Alpha News, who made national news in January when bodycam video of the fatal shooting of Renee Good by an ICE agent was given to her.

As Jared Holt, a senior researcher at Open Measures who tracks rightwing disinformation explains, the claim that the T-shirt was intended to mock Kirk’s death was previously raised and debunked last year, In fact, it has been worn by mathematics teachers on Halloween for years, well before Kirk was killed.

Polls closed in Ohio an hour and a half ago, and long lines of people have filed into the Hilton hotel in downtown Columbus to watch results roll in at the Ohio Democrats’ election night watch party. The crowd cheered when large screens flashed the news that Democrat John Ossoff won Georgia’s Senate race and booed when they learned that Republican Ashley Moody won the Florida Senate race. The atmosphere is joyful. Every 10 minutes, the audience chants, “Amy! Amy! Amy”, in hopes that the Democratic gubernatorial candidate Amy Acton wins the Ohio race.

Heather Hogrefe, 58, told me she drove two hours from Perrysburg in north-west Ohio. “Ohio is so corrupt and it has been a one party state for too long,” said Hogrefe. “It’s time for that to end.” Hogrefe, a teacher who works with at-risk high school students, launched a fundraiser with her friends to support Acton and raised $20,000. “We made it our new year’s resolution to get her elected,” she said. Hogrefe said she rejects how Ohio Republicans have expanded the state’s school voucher program but haven’t adequately funded public schools. A victory for the Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown would mean new possibilities for disadvantaged students, Hogrefe said.

“If we can fight for Ohio and win, it means that we can do that in the rest of the country,” said Brandy Alexander, 53, a friend who drove to the event with Hogrefe. “We used to be purple, almost blue, but then Trump came to power. What happens here represents what we can do in the rest of America.”

Over glasses of red wine, the women cheered, as the DJ played hits from Stevie Wonder, John Legend, Bill Withers and Alicia Keys. “We’re feeling hopeful tonight,” Hogrefe said.

As results continue to trickle in, my colleague Ariana Baio has been tracking the success of the Senate candidates that Donald Trump backed this cycle. Three of the president’s Senate endorsements have lost so far (in New Hampshire, North Carolina and Georgia), while four have won their races (Kentucky, West Virginia, Wyoming and Florida).

The Republican National Committee urged voters to “stay in line” in a social media post featuring a video of Donald Trump this evening.

In a linked video, Trump said: “Hi Republicans, we’re doing really well. If you’re in line, stay in line.”

Keisha Lance Bottoms has won the governor’s race in Georgia, beating Republican Rick Jackson.

Her victory means that Lance Bottoms, the former mayor of Atlanta, has made history as the first Black woman ever elected governor in US history. She will also be Georgia’s first female governor.

A few hours earlier, Stacey Plaskett was elected the first Black female governor of a United States territory. Plaskett will govern the US Virgin Islands.

In a social media post, representative Ayanna Pressley congratulated Plaskett, writing: “When more Black women lead, everyone who calls our country home benefits. Congratulations, sister!”

The crowd was jubilant at newly elected senator Chris Pappas’s victory party as he took the podium, with Democrat Stefany Shaheen, who just won her race for Pappas’s representative seat, and her mother, Jeanne Shaheen, whose seat Pappas won, in the wings. He thanked his opponent John Sununu for a “very gracious” call to concede.

“Tonight’s results are a wakeup call for corporate special interests and billionaires who have been rigging the economy, for the cowards in Congress who have enabled healthcare cuts, tariffs and war, and for the incompetent president who can’t even be bothered to care how people are being hurt,” Pappas said to cheers. “Tonight, voters in New Hampshire and all across the country simply have had enough and are demanding a change in direction. I am ready to fight with everything I’ve got to deliver the change that we need.”

Though Pappas, a longtime Granite state elected official, was the establishment Democratic candidate, his victory speech echoed much of the rhetoric of the populist candidates that have seen success this cycle – from Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan to James Talarico in Texas – and the crowd ate it up. With his husband, Vann Bentley, beside him, Pappas pledged to fight for reproductive freedom and, to even louder cheers, against the creation of datacenters and ICE detention facilities (the latter got the loudest scream of the night).

“This was a hard-fought victory tonight. It was made even harder by the tens and tens of millions of dollars that poured into New Hampshire from Donald Trump, Elon Musk and corporate special interests who are just fine with the way things are going right now,” Pappas continued, addressing the gathered campaign staffers, volunteers, supporters and local Democratic officials. “They threw money, attack ads and outright lies at us every day for months. But we beat them anyway, didn’t we?”

Democrat Pia Dandiya has won the US House race for Florida’s 22nd congressional district, a re-drawn seat in Palm Beach that was gerrymandered to benefit Republicans.

Dandiya, a former high school principal, beat her Republican challenger, Casey Askar – a marine veteran.

Republican Byron Donalds, a congressman, has won the governor race in Florida, succeeding Ron DeSantis.

Donalds beat Democrat David Jolly, a former US representative, after receiving Trump’s endorsement.

Navajo voters in Arizona and New Mexico are struggling to reach polling locations after record flooding in the Four Corners region over the past two weeks.

In north-east Arizona, Valinda Shirley, the vice-president of the Navajo Nation’s Rock Point chapter, told AZ Central: “The ruts are too big to ignore. I did have voters that were washed out and couldn’t get through to their homes or couldn’t leave their homes for several days.”

Yesterday, the Apache county board of supervisors declared a state of emergency as heavy rain poured down across the region for a second weekend in a row.

In neighboring New Mexico, the secretary of state’s office issued an election advisory about the weather on Monday.

Navajo communities aren’t the only Indigenous voters affected by the El Niño-driven thunderstorms. Parts of the Fort Apache Indian Reservation were put under mandatory evacuation over the weekend and the Hopi Tribe has issued a public safety warning about the flood risk.

Flood waters have also affected southern Utah, where lieutenant governor Deidre Henderson issued a declaration of emergency election accommodations to allow some voters there to request and return their ballots electronically.
