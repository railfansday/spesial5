GM’s V2G pilot wants to pay California drivers for power from parked EVs

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

GM Energy and WeaveGrid are launching a vehicle-to-grid (V2G) pilot that will let select Southern California Edison (SCE) customers send electricity from their EV batteries back to the grid. Eligible drivers could get more than $16,000 in help with equipment and installation, and earn money when the grid needs their power.

GM says the pilot participants will be its first EV drivers in California to export power from their cars to their homes. Through the California Energy Commission, eligible customers in SCE’s Emergency Load Reduction Program can get up to $8,000 toward a GM Energy V2H system and up to $8,099 toward eligible installation costs, including connecting the system to the grid.

Chevy, GMC, and Cadillac EV drivers can use time-of-use energy management to take advantage of lower rates and may be paid for participating in eligible demand-response events. How much they save or earn depends on their rate plan, electricity use, and participation.

GM already offers managed charging through 13 utilities, and each program varies by utility.

An EV battery stores direct current (DC), and your house and the grid use alternating current (AC). GM’s current home backup system uses a wall-mounted inverter to convert the power. With AC bidirectional charging, the car’s onboard inverter does the job. Moving that work into the car could make the equipment on your garage wall simpler and cheaper to install.

Not in CA, but we use solar, time of use, and EVs to minimize our energy costs. We’re not feeding power back to the grid from our cars, but the combo of TOU, solar and EVs does allow us to reduce costs significantly. Solar covers all the expensive peak power, I have the cars start charging early enough to use any extra solar, and the rest is off peak at half cost.

GM is joining CharIN‘s North American AC V2G task force, which is working to align technical standards, equipment certification, and utility approval requirements. In Southern California, the pilot will give GM and its partners a look at how the process works for drivers, from signing up and getting the hardware installed to getting utility approval and sending power back.
