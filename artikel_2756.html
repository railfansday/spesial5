Glenn Thomas vs. Matt Patricia decided the game

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

There will be plenty of blame to go around after Ohio State's 31-28 loss at Nebraska, as the Buckeyes are now 7-4, and their playoff hopes are officially over. But for me, the matchup that decided this game wasn't between players. It was on the sidelines.

Nebraska interim offensive coordinator Glenn Thomas called a near-perfect game, and Ohio State defensive coordinator Matt Patricia, one of the highest-paid assistants in college football at a reported $3.75 million, got outcoached from start to finish. And the ending, allowing two third-down QB runs to seal the game, was inexcusable.

From the opening drives, Nebraska looked like the team with the plan. The Huskers scored on three straight first-half drives to take a 21-7 lead. They went 13 plays and 63 yards for their first touchdown. They converted on third-and-goal when Anthony Colandrea found Kwazi Gilmer for a 7-yard score. Then, after an Isaiah West fumble, they went 47 yards in nine plays, capped by a 13-yard Colandrea touchdown run. By halftime, Nebraska had 259 yards and 16 first downs.

Ohio State's defense couldn't get off the field all night. Nebraska finished 10-of-16 on third down, with 376 total yards, 176 of them on the ground, and didn't turn the ball over once. Colandrea went 19-of-28 for 200 yards and three touchdowns, and he hurt Ohio State with his legs in the biggest moments over and over again.

That's the part that bothers me most. Everyone knew going in that Colandrea was a threat to run. I wrote this week that Ohio State had to stay disciplined in their rush lanes and keep him in the pocket. It was the most obvious key of the game, and Ohio State still couldn't stop it.

Glenn Thomas kept Ohio State off balance all afternoon, mixing quarterback runs, quick throws, and play-action. Patricia never seemed to have an answer. This is the same Ohio State defense that had just held Northwestern to 53 rushing yards and recorded five sacks. And then just one week later, they look completely lost.

To Ohio State's credit, the offense fought back. The Buckeyes tied the game 21-21 with back-to-back touchdown drives. Then, after a Luke Fahey interception led to Colandrea's 26-yard touchdown pass to Quinn Clark, Ohio State answered again, with Bo Jackson's 1-yard touchdown cutting it to 31-28. All the defense had to do was get one stop.

With two minutes left, Nebraska faced third-and-2. Everyone in the building knew what was coming. Colandrea kept it and ran for five yards and a first down. Then, on the next set of downs, it happened again. Another third down, another Colandrea run, and another first down to end the game. I honestly had no words watching it live. This wasn't a trick play. It wasn't a perfectly executed deep shot. It was the most predictable call in football, against a defense that knew it was coming, and Ohio State still couldn't stop it. Twice. With the season on the line.

That falls on the defensive coordinator. Whether it was the alignment, the rush lanes, or the personnel, Ohio State didn't have a plan for the one thing Nebraska had done all day. And that's inexcusable for a coach being paid like Patricia is, or for any coach at this level, honestly.

I don't want to put an entire loss on one coach. Ohio State turned the ball over twice, and the offense struggled to sustain drives early. Nebraska deserves credit as well. They played without three of their best defensive players and still found a way to win.

But this isn't the first time this has happened. Against USC, the defense gave up a 69-yard screen on the second snap and a 70-yard catch-and-run later in the game. Against Oregon, they gave up a fake punt, multiple third-and-long conversions, and a 48-yard run through missed tackles. Now against Nebraska, they couldn't stop a quarterback run on third down when everyone knew it was coming.

That's three losses in four weeks where the defense couldn't make the key stop. When a unit that was this dominant in October falls apart like this in November, you have to ask questions about the coaching. Ohio State still has Michigan next week, and that game will matter regardless of the playoff picture. But after what happened in Lincoln, I'm not sure what this defense will look like. And if Patricia can't fix this in seven days, the questions about his future will only get louder and louder.
