Chinese chip stocks notch biggest ever one-day gains as Beijing signals broader market support

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Chinese stocks rebounded sharply on Friday after a rare intraday state media readout cheered investors as top policymakers pledged to expand the role of state-backed funds in supporting the market.

The STAR index surged 14%, led by Chinese chipmakers such as CXMT, Cambricon and SMIC. The CSI Semiconductor index of 40 chip stocks climbed by 17.6% in its biggest single-day gain on record, surpassing the prior record of nearly 17% in late 2024, according to Wind Information.

The gains were widespread across mainland and Hong Kong stock indexes. The Hang Seng index rose 2%, while the CSI 300 index climbed 4%, its biggest jump since October 2024, according to Wind data.

The rally comes as governments pour support into domestic chip industries while tightening controls on sensitive technology in the global race for artificial intelligence.

The U.S. unveiled a new rule last week to further restrict Chinese companies’ access to advanced AI chips, including through overseas data centers, adding pressure on Beijing to accelerate its push for semiconductor self-sufficiency.

Friday's rally comes after the CSI 300 on Thursday sank to its lowest level since the escalation of the U.S.-China tariff war in the spring of 2025, according to Wind data.

In recent months, investors have weighed the commercial feasibility of Chinese artificial intelligence developers and tighter U.S. restrictions on advanced chips.

Chinese Premier Li Qiang oversaw a meeting Friday morning of the State Council, the country's top governing body, which saw officials pledge to strengthen the role of "National Team" — referring to state-backed funds — to stabilize markets and ensure ample liquidity, according to a readout released by state media Xinhua News.

The explicit reference to the "national team" is a first for a State Council meeting, said Bob Chen, a Shanghai-based venture capitalist and economist.

Authorities also stressed that "capital markets are crucial to the broader economy," according to the readout, a rare comment from a government that has not always looked favorably on the financial sector.

"Stock market performance is now prioritized at the highest level for the Chinese government," which is boosting investor confidence, Chen said.

"That's very strong language from the top officials," said Zong Ke, a portfolio manager at a Shanghai-based quantitative fund.

By Ke's calculations, stock trading volumes surged rapidly after the readout was released around 1:00 p.m. local time (12:00 a.m. ET). Full-day turnover across the Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing exchanges was 52% higher than the previous session.

Policymakers also vowed to strengthen the role of "stabilization funds" like Central Huijin Investment, a state-owned investment firm that holds equity stakes on behalf of the State Council.

That message "effectively institutionalized Central Huijin's role as a state stabilization fund," said Hong Hao, managing partner of Lotus Asset Management. "And it's significantly positive news for the market."

Hao expects this market rally to prove more durable than the "fleeting bounce" triggered by Beijing's stimulus talks in the past, which wore off in days. "This time it's for real."

The readout of the Cabinet's meeting, typically released well after trading hours, was published around noon local time during trading hours, an unusual timing that's viewed by markets as a bullish signal.

The State Council also pledged better policies to steer medium- or long-term funds, such as insurance and social security funds, into equities, and encouraged listed companies to pay more dividends and step up share buybacks, while vowing a crackdown on financial fraud to protect investors.

The national team will accelerate asset purchases, Ke said, adding that he expects more supportive policies to follow, such as further loosening the institutional constraints that have limited long-term funds from investing in stocks.

Beijing's reliance on intervention to offset stock volatility has, however, raised questions about the market's ability to sustain longer-term gains.

But the market needs this kind of support right now, said Ke, as the near-term aim is to ease liquidity pressure and restore confidence so the market doesn't fall much further.

Chinese AI-related stocks also carry less downside risk than their U.S. counterparts, as the companies have spent less on AI so far and their shares have already been through a sharp correction, Ke said.

That, together with the pledged state backing, has given investors more confidence that the market may be nearing its bottom, Ke added.
