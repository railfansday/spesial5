10 things we learned from Patriots vs. Lions

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

For a second week in a row, the New England Patriots got blown out by an NFC North team on Sunday. Once again, they looked listless and disengaged for the majority of the game.

Let's get into our lessons learned from the Patriots' 35-10 loss to the Detroit Lions in Germany.

The Patriots haven't had many playmakers on offense this year, but Drake Maye has still put together a (mostly) solid year. Without him, however, it looks like one of the worst offenses in football.

Sunday's game was concrete proof of that. Tommy DeVito couldn't get the ball to open receivers, separation was on and off, and they couldn't run the ball. If Maye misses time after the bye, and it's looking increasingly likely that that is going to be the case, it's going to be tough to watch.

Speaking of Tommy DeVito, after Drake Maye went out with an injury, the Patriots were forced to turn to him. The media likes DeVito and the Patriots clearly like him too, since they gave him a contract extension this offseason. Unfortunately, he just isn't that good of a quarterback.

On Sunday, he missed open receivers left and right and on more than one occasion threw guys directly into contact. I understand that he's the backup inserted on short notice, and that the Patriots aren't asking him to be a great player because they really don't want him playing at all, but it would be nice if he was at least able to competently run the offense. Against Detroit, he wasn't.

A.J. Brown's inability to do anything positive this year has been almost laughable. He got hurt in the first game, missed a good chunk of time on injured reserve, came back, was a non-factor, and then got hurt again. He returned on Sunday, was unable to get open and had a drop as well. He finally made a nice catch-and-run to move the stick on a third down, and then promptly went to the locker room with a hamstring injury.

It's been a rough year for Brown, and the Patriots front office: they effectively bid against themselves to trade for him and still gave up a first-round pick in 2028 to do so. Unless he somehow manages a complete 180, the trade is going to go down as a massive failure, and potentially hamstring the Patriots for years to come.

Their defense was the reason the Patriots made the Super Bowl last year, and the hope was that it would take another step forward in 2026. Unfortunately, that has not happen and the unit has instead taken a step back.

It did register a couple of turnovers on Sunday, but the unit was unable to stop its opponent more or less all day long. They came out without any fire, missed tackles all over field, and put the Patriots in a hole right away.

We all were impressed with the job that Zak Kuhr had done last year, but the league has seemed to figure him out, and whatever he's doing isn't working. Yes, they have dealt with some injuries, but, for the most part, the defense has been fairly healthy. They have been out-coached, out-played, and, to be frank, it oftentimes looks like the other team simply wants it more than they do.

With this defense, the Patriots aren't going anywhere, especially if Drake Maye ends up missing time.

Christian Gonzalez is one of the best corners in the NFL, and surely one of the best players on the Patriots. The last two weeks, however, saw him have back-to-back rough games for the first time in his career.

On the game's first third down against the Lions, his struggles continued, when Amon-Ra St. Brown caught a pass in the flat, shook Gonzalez into oblivion, and then took the ball 56 yards for a touchdown. It was a bad start for Gonzalez, who later also fell victim to a pick play on another St. Brown score.

it wasn't all bad, however. He also registered an interception on a play where he ran the route for St. Brown and undercut him for the pick. It was a play that showed the player he has been, and will try to be again.

While we are on the topic of interceptions, the Patriots have finally won the turnover battle for the first time this season. They picked off Jared Goff twice, and only turned it over once.

In a game where they got embarrassed again, and with everything looking absolutely terrible in New England now, I'll take any positive notes I can get. Now, if they only were able to go a game without turning the ball over…

The NFL is filled with players who are really fast, really strong, and really elusive. None of those players, however, measure up to Gibbs. His ability to stop and start is ridiculous, and his speed is nothing short of blazing.

He leads the NFL in touches, and there is no sign of him slowing down. People had thought that TreVeyon Henderson would be the Patriots' version of Gibbs, but there is no one in this league like him, and, just like most teams that have played the Lions this year, New England had no chance of stopping him on Sunday.

In fact, he ended up out-gaining them by himself: Gibbs had 153 rushing yards and 99 receiving yards for a total of 252 yards. The Patriots offense finished the day with a grand total of 151.

The Patriots activated Gilliam off of injured reserve before the game, and they were hoping he would be a factor in the running game and on special teams. Instead, the Patriots couldn't run the ball to save their lives, Gilliam twice ran into his return man when he was trying to block, and he also committed two penalties on special teams.

Personally, I didn't love activating him off the IR, and, for Sunday at least, the Patriots didn't get any better with him on the field. With Drake Maye set to miss time, however, they might have to start relying on him and the running game.

Mike Vrabel did a fantastic job last year, there is no denying it. The culture he was trying to build was obvious, and the result was a mentally and physically tough team that reveled in the opportunity to go on the road and prove itself.

This year, however, the team has been a disaster. There have definitely been a lot of injuries, but the mental toughness has almost completely disappeared. The amount of pre-snap penalties is alarming, and the unforced errors in judgement and physical play have been bad. To go along with that, the way that he and some of the players have been speaking to the media is confusing as well.

If you're going to be a culture guy, then the culture has to matter, and it doesn't look good right now. I'm not saying that they should fire Vrabel or anything, but it bears watching. If the team doesn't start playing better, and they don't fix the fundamentals (turnovers, penalties, tackling), then there might be a reason to move on sooner rather than later.

Obviously, not every team is going to be great every year, but he has hand-picked a lot of these guys for the culture here because he coached them before. If this team has any sort of culture issue, it's squarely on Vrabel's shoulders.

(I also think that it's notable that they seemingly rushed A.J. Brown back twice instead of giving him a long rest, and he's been hurt both times. Maybe that's the medical staff, but it's ultimately the coaching staff who makes the decision to play the guys, even if they are physically cleared.)

Perhaps no team in the NFL has needed a bye week as much as the Patriots do. They played two bad games in a row, have a ton of injuries, including at quarterback, and desperately need to get their minds right and get healthy. The Patriots are going to have to come up with something significantly better than what they've brought lately, because they are getting crushed and look like a team on the verge of giving up.

A reset over the bye seems necessary, and might help them get back to a spot where they look like a competent and competitive football team.
