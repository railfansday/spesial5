Rob Manfred and the owners keep pushing for a salary cap

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

MLB owners are meeting in New York this week, and after one meeting session Wednesday, Commissioner Rob Manfred met with the media.

Well, of course he's going to say that. He works for the owners, he's their mouthpiece, that is the job of Commissioner in the 21st Century, no matter how many of us might want it to be different.

That prompted this response from MLBPA Interim Executive Director Bruce Meyer:

"There can be no clearer example of the league's lack of seriousness than their insistence that they will not negotiate over the economics outside of a radical change to the system that would dramatically reduce overall player compensation for all categories of players and strip fundamental rights from players like the ability to have truly guaranteed contracts. There should be no illusions about the owners' true motivation in pushing for this system. They want to make more money in profits and franchise values by artificially suppressing player salaries."

Meyer is, of course, correct. The last sentence is key, for anyone who still thinks the owners' desire for a cap is about competitive balance. It is exactly about profits and increased franchise values. I'm going to beat this drum as hard as I can during the lockout so that as many people as possible know that. That's the truth even with these Manfred statements, quoted by Evan Drellich in The Athletic:

"Thirty clubs are 100 percent unanimous in support of the proposals that have been made," Manfred said later during a news conference at MLB headquarters. "I'm not in a position to speculate about what happens in the event that we don't achieve our overriding objective, which is to get a cap and floor system that addresses the competitive problem that our fans perceive."

If you "perceive" that, perhaps you didn't watch this year's postseason. Then Manfred went on and took a shot at the MLBPA:

"The salary cap and floor are not the real culprit here," Manfred said. "The problem the last time, and a big part of the problem now, is that the MLBPA wants more, and its proposals undermine — further undermine — the competitive quality of our game."

Those kinds of statements aren't going to produce goodwill with the players, I would say. Beyond all that, there's more in this Drellich article that show that MLB is way out of touch:

On Friday, MLB released a five-minute video featuring a top league executive, Morgan Sword, attempting to sway fans on a cap. The clip is a piece of a broad campaign MLB is waging in an effort to win over hearts and minds.

"This is comically bad," veteran pitcher Jameson Taillon responded to MLB's video with Sword. "They're being very careful and selective of what they're saying/showing and how they're delivering it. This is going to be ugly if they're trying to sway fan/public support weeks before they even lock us out."

Now, of course Manfred's statement about "no deal without a cap and floor" is posturing. If he's 100 percent serious and owners are going down that road, we can say goodbye to the 2027 season. I don't think that's going to be ownership's position throughout negotiations. Could be wrong here — maybe this time the hard-liners will keep their hard line.

We stand today just 12 days from ownership's lockout. It doesn't seem likely that anything will happen between now and Dec. 1 that will prevent ownership from putting that lockout in place.

And then, we will wait. If you recall last time this happened, there were no serious negotiations between the sides until January, and no real movement until it got to crunch time in early March. I wouldn't expect things to be any different this time.

As always, of course, we await developments.
