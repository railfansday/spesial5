Aaron Rodgers reportedly gave Steelers an ultimatum

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The fallout from the Pittsburgh Steelers' benching of four-time NFL MVP Aaron Rodgers has been extraordinary. The franchise announced the news on Tuesday evening, and by Wednesday afternoon, Rodgers was live on The Pat McAfee Show, saying that the "ship has sailed" on any return for him to the franchise and going scorched earth on head coach Mike McCarthy.

"Mike came in late, sat down, and within 10 minutes began snoring. Woke himself up, was rattling his papers. We all looked around. Five minutes later, you hear the snoring again. And less than five minutes after that, he walks out of the room like nothing happened. Thursday, he misses an offensive meeting," Rodgers told McAfee.

"Friday, he doesn't come to the walkthrough. We only have a walkthrough really on Friday. Doesn't come to the first walkthrough, doesn't come to the protection walkthrough on Thursday. Saturday, he misses the morning meeting. Then we don't have a team meeting Saturday night for whatever reason in Philly."

Rodgers also said that McCarthy ended a locker room rant with "one, two, three, go Packers!" and subsequently lost most of the locker room. Rodgers has since been released. However, although McCarthy didn't outright confirm Rodgers' accusations during his media availability, he did admit that there was an "embarrassing moment" and that things had gotten "emotional."

Later on Wednesday, Netflix's Tom Pelissero provided his account of what went down behind the scenes.

"Now, some of what Rodgers said was true, including McCarthy's slip-up breaking down the huddle in Philadelphia. I'm told McCarthy was so embarrassed he apologized to the team the next day," Pelissero said.

"But I am also told frustration had been building among coaches and some teammates about Rodgers, who had increasingly been calling plays himself in recent weeks. Rodgers and the offense were struggling and getting further from their identity, which is running the football. So after back-to-back losses and with tension increasing, Rodgers went to McCarthy on Tuesday to demand more changes, including to the play-calling process.

"McCarthy was open to it, but the feedback he'd gotten from some coaches and some in the locker room was that's not what they wanted. Those discussions ended with an ultimatum from Rodgers. Rodgers: Run things my way, or I quit. And the Steelers called his bluff. By Tuesday afternoon, McCarthy had decided he wanted to start Mason Rudolph on Friday against the Denver Broncos."

Pelissero went on to say that Steelers general manager Omar Khan relayed the news to Rodgers over the phone, and that although Rodgers took the move personally, for McCarthy and the Steelers, it was simply the move that put the team in the best position to win.
