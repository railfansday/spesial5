Bears-Lions Thanksgiving audience will be huge — and it could have been even bigger

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Last year, both of the early Thanksgiving Day games broke the prior record for NFL regular-season audience size. This year, the all-time record of 57.2 million from the Chiefs-Cowboys game is likely safe.

With one small tweak to the schedule, it perhaps wouldn’t be.

In 2025, Packers-Lions attracted an incredible 47.7 million viewers. This year, the looming battle between the 7-2 Bears and 7-2 Lions should approach (if not match . . . if not beat) that number.

The problem is the second game. The 2-7 Eagles are in soft-tank mode. The 4-6 Cowboys have disintegrated into defensive disarray. While it will still attract a very large audience given the captive audience and the usual Thanksgiving routines, it likely won’t approach 50 million.

While the NFL has granted a permanent hammerlock to the Lions and Cowboys for the traditional Thanksgiving afternoon games, a simple tweak would have set the stage for Bears-Lions to break 50 million — and perhaps to threaten last year’s record.

Here’s the idea. Why not install flexibility to flip the start times of the afternoon games?

It’s no different than the usual Sunday-afternoon flex, like the one that happened this weekend when Steelers-Eagles moved from 4:25 p.m. ET to 1:00 p.m. ET and Jaguars-Giants slid to the higher-profile slot.

On Thanksgiving, the late-afternoon game always does better than the early one. In some years, the Lions game could be viewed as more attractive than the Cowboys game. This year would be one of those years.

It’s something to consider. Getting an annual Thanksgiving Day game doesn’t mean owning the window. And given the NFL’s current willingness, on and after Week 12, to flex from Sunday to Thursday or Sunday to Monday, why not reserve the right to acknowledge that, for the two afternoon games on Thanksgiving, the “best” one should be played in the higher-profile slot?
