Deutsche Bank shares sink as whistleblowers reveal Epstein payments, Trump tax leak, Russia bets

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Deutsche Bank shares fell sharply in early trading on Tuesday after whistleblowers leaked about 15 million files from the German lender, with media reports revealing alleged historic failures across its risk management and financial crime controls.

These include suspicious payments linked to late financier and convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein, and accidental leaks of President Donald Trump's tax statements.

The leaks contain board minutes, emails, internal investigations and other material that point to systemic failures across the Frankfurt-listed lender's business functions stretching back two decades.

Shares in Deutsche Bank were 4.7% lower by 9:20 a.m. E.T., having fallen more than 6% in early trade in Frankfurt.

Deutsche Bank processed $582 million in suspicious payments linked to Jeffrey Epstein, far more than previously known, the FT reported. Suspicious payments are not, by themselves, proof that every transaction was criminal.

The German lender also mistakenly leaked details of U.S. President Donald Trump's taxes in March 2017, during his first term in office, the FT said. The blunder — described by CEO Christian Sewing, who was then Deutsche's private banking head, as "an awful mistake" — was kept secret from the White House.

The wide-ranging investigation — which was led by a consortium of more than 200 journalists worldwide including the FT, Der Spiegel and Le Monde — also said Deutsche Bank had failed to offboard around 28,000 problematic investment bank clients who had been identified for removal by early 2018.

It also alleges that the bank concealed information around cost targets in 2021, when external monitors in New York reportedly warned that Deutsche's risk appetite was running ahead of controls.

The files also contain details of the bank's relationships with oligarchs and transactions that the investigation links to Russian money-laundering concerns. It was these ties that motivated whistleblowers to send the information to Indian journalist Josy Joseph and South African journalist Khadija Sharife.

The reports once again put Deutsche Bank's risk management practices and controls under the spotlight following years of regulatory scrutiny.

The bank — which has been raided multiple times by German officials this year — has, since 2012, paid out some 18.6 billion euros ($20.5 billion) in settlements over alleged market manipulation, money laundering, mis-selling and misrepresentation, among other things, the FT said.

In a statement, Deutsche Bank said the issues detailed in Tuesday's media reports go back many years.

"Where relevant, we reviewed these matters at the time and worked closely with regulators," the lender said.

The bank said it has undergone a "fundamental transformation" under CEO Christian Sewing, pointing to 7 billion euros of investment in its risk controls between 2019 and 2025.

"Deutsche Bank is a different bank today and remains focused on executing its strategy which has proven successful, as evidenced by record profits in 2025 as well as in recent quarters."

CNBC has reached out to BaFin, Germany's financial regulator, for comment.
