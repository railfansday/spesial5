Bethune-Cookman coach Alvin Wyatt OK after medical issue in Florida Classic

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

ORLANDO — Bethune-Cookman interim head coach Alvin Wyatt is OK and "in good spirits" after prematurely leaving the Wildcats' sideline during the Florida Classic against Florida A&M on Nov. 21, a Bethune-Cookman official told the Daytona Beach News-Journal.

Wyatt started feeling ill in the second half and spent a portion of the third quarter on the Bethune-Cookman bench. He was stretchered off the field under a blanket by medical personnel in the fourth quarter.

Assistant head coach and offensive coordinator Donte' Pimpleton was the coach made available to the media after the game in place of Wyatt.

Wyatt, affectionately known by his nickname "Shine," is the winningest head coach in Wildcats history with a 90-54 record from 1997-2009. The 78-year-old was tabbed as the interim leader on Nov. 15, the same day Raymond Woodie Jr. was fired.

"Wyatt gave them a sense of history, gave them a sense of what the Classic is all about from a guy who has been here, I don't know how many years, and coached on every level here," said Reggie Theus, the Wildcats' vice president for athletics.

While Wyatt's afternoon was unfortunately cut short due to an undisclosed medical issue, his return brought a sense of nostalgia to Camping World Stadium. Wyatt walked Bethune-Cookman out of the tunnel with his trademark style.

"He's an enthusiastic guy. He brings a lot of energy. He has a high enthusiasm," long snapper and team captain Cole Hash said. "He came in and implemented his way of thinking and the way that he wanted us to do things, and I think he's a great human being, a great person, a great coach."

Wyatt's winning ways were not replicated for Bethune-Cookman, however, as Florida A&M forced five turnovers and defeated the Wildcats 27-14.
