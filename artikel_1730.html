Standings, highs, lows, key players: How Milan Futuro, Primavera and Women have started 2026-27

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The second international break of the season is upon us, which is a good time to take stock of how things are going beyond he men’s first team.

An ownership and management should not just be judged on one area of the club, but rather multiple different departments. The youth sector needs to be developing players in the right way, while the rising prevalence of women’s football gives added impetus to make the Femminile side as competitive as possible.

That being said, can be hard enough keeping up with how the senior team are doing without thinking about the various other sides operating under the Milan umbrella. So, we have provided a summary of how Milan Futuro, Milan Primavera and Milan Women are doing so far in 2026-27.

It was all change over the summer for the Futuro as they brought in Sergio Navarro as the head coach. The idea was to create some continuity with Ruben Amorim’s more continental style of football, and the pedigree that the Spaniard has suggested it was a positive move.

While not a necessity, the objective for the campaign will no doubt have been set as competing for promotion back to the third tier, which is the level that the project started at prior to the relegation in 2024-25.

Position: It is a case of so far, so good for Navarro’s men. They sit fourth in the standings with 23 points from 11 games, obtained through seven wins, two draws and two losses. They have scored 21 goals (third-most in the league) and allowed 15.

Importantly, the Futuro have a game in hand on the sides above them. If they win it they would go level with Chievo Verona (yes, that Chievo) and Nibbiano & Valtidone who are on 26 points. The former, however, have a 10 better goal difference.

The U23s are part of the Premier League International Cup and they got past Everton 2-1 in the first game, with matches against Stoke later this month and Tottenham in November to come, both away from home.

High point: On October 18, Milan’s second team went to the current league-leaders Chievo and beat them 2-1, with Bonomi scoring a late winner. That was an early marker of what this team are capable of. A special mention must go to the Premier League Cup win at Goodison Park against Everton, too.

Low point: That aforementioned win in Verona came in the round after Milan had lost 2-1 at home to Rovato Vertovese. They are a side that have only won twice all season and currently occupy the relegation spots, so it was a big blow before a big response.

Key players: There have been multiple players that have stood out. Levin Asanji has scored four goals in the league, as has Lorenzo Ossolo. Fabio Pandolfi has been playing a deeper midfield role and looks to have quality well beyond his 18 years of age, with three goals to his name.

Silvano Vos is having a resurgence, while the likes of Emanuele Sala, Mattia Cappelletti, Alessandro Bonomi and Emanuele Borsani have all started every single game, aiding their development under the new coach. El Hadji Malick Cisse – a January arrival – is also turning a lot of heads.

For a bit more deeper reading on the tactical approach used by Milan Futuro under Navarro, we suggest reading the analysis we put out over the international break.

The 2025-26 season was a mixed one for the Primavera side, as their record of 11 wins and 11 defeats in 38 games shows perfectly. They finished 11th, nine points off the relegation play-offs but 10 points off the Scudetto play-offs, though a lot of the younger talents had been again accelerated into the Futuro side.

Giovanni Renna remained at the helm going into the new campaign, the coach that has been promoted up the ranks and thus knows a lot of the players. The hope was that continuity would yield better results, and so far it has.

Position: At the time of writing, the U20 side are sixth in the Primavera 1 league, which puts them in a final play-off spot. They have won five of their first 10 games but they have also lost four, so they remain a bit of a rollercoaster ride of inconsistency.

Looking upwards, Parma are currently top on 21 points, but the Rossoneri are within striking distance of Inter, who are in second on 19 points. However, Renna’s side are also a bad round away from being in the bottom half too.

Milan Primavera progressed in the Coppa Italia Primavera at the end of October, beating Catanzaro 2-1 thanks to a late winner from Dotta. Como are the opponents in the next round.

High point: It’s hard to look past the last two rounds in truth. The Primavera seem to have really clicked, scoring 10 goals in the last 180 minutes of action, beating Monza 5-0 away from home and then AlbinoLeffe 5-1 at home.

An honourable mention must go to the 2-0 win over Juventus in September, given they are in a similar position with also having a B-team (Juve Next Gen), so they will continue to serve as a useful point of comparison.

Low point: Back on October 10, the young Diavoli lost 2-0 against Atalanta which was their third league defeat in a row. There was talk of crisis mode as the slide down the table continued, and against an Atalanta side that are now third-bottom of the league. Thankfully, they turned it around quickly.

Key players: A bit like with the Futuro, the goals have been shared around the Milan Primavera squad. Mattia Zaramella has emerged recently as a star with four goals, as has Max Anastasio who arrived from Bologna and has the same amount.

Then, Andrea Cullotta (a centre-back), Vincent Ibrahimovic (a midfielder) and Edoardo Tartaglia (a full-back) all have two goals. Renna has called upon the same core group of players largely. Matteo Geroli, Francesco Domnitei, Cullotta, Zaramella, Tartaglia, Anastasio and Yanis Massaouadi have all appeared in every round.

Last season was not one that Milan Women will want to shout about, given that they finished sixth in the table and seven points off the play-off spots in the end. They won just nine of 22 games, placing them below even Fiorentina and Lazio, plus Juventus, city rivals Inter and eventual champions Roma.

However, like with the Primavera, consistency was the name of the game. So, Suzanne Bakker remained as the head coach, and there wasn’t as much player turnover as in some recent years.

Position: There have only been seven rounds so far in Serie A Femminile, but the Rossonere have got off to a stuttering start. They have just seven points from their opening seven games, which puts them in eighth place in the table. They have scored 11 goals but let in a joint-league-high 16.

Looking upwards, Roma lead the way with 21 points having won every game. Inter are second on 16 points – so nine more than Milan – while Juventus occupy the final play-off spot on 12. Over their shoulders, Sassuolo are in bottom (the only relegation spot), with three points.

In the Women’s Cup, Milan drew 1-1 with Como, beat Como 1907 (affiliated with the men’s team currently in Serie A, the others are a stand-alone club) 2-1, and then lost 1-0 to Juventus to end their campaign.

High point: This one is quite an easy pick, and it has to be the 2-1 victory over Juventus last month. Thea Kyvåg and Monica Renzotti overturned Lenzini’s opener, which had come with just under a quarter of an hour left on the clock, engineering a late comeback that sparked jubilant scenes.

Low point: The tone for what has been a disappointing campaign so far for Bakker’s ladies was set in round one, when they were beaten 4-1 by Como 1907. Then, losing 1-0 away at Como was another dark day, given they have only won twice.

On November 7, the 3-0 derby defeat to Inter – which included a red card for Milan – served only to highlight the big gulf between the two teams at the moment.

Key players: Christy Grimshaw remains the shining light for Milan Women, with three goals and an assist to her name. The scoring charts then see Sara Stokic on three goals, with Sara Fornes, Park Soojeong and Monica Renzotti all netting twice.

Grimshaw is one of three players to start every game so far under Bakker, with the others being defenders Angelica Soffia and Nerea Nevado. Sandra Estevez has emerged as the number one between the sticks too.
