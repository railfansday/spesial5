The is most compelling for businesses that regularly fly Alaska Airlines or Hawaiian Airlines, especially those that issue cards to employees or book flights through the Atmos™ for Business travel platform.

The refreshed card charges a flat $195 annual fee with no additional fee for employee cards. It offers quarterly Alaska Lounge passes and waives fees for eligible same-day confirmed flight changes.

Having held Alaska and Hawaiian cobranded cards over the years, I still consider Atmos Rewards points among the most valuable airline rewards you can earn, thanks to the program’s partner award pricing. Whether this card is worth the fee comes down to how often your team flies Alaska or Hawaiian and which perks you’ll actually use.

Before diving deeper, here’s a quick snapshot of what the offers:

The card’s highest earning rate is reserved for flights booked through Atmos for Business (only accessible to businesses spending at least $5,000 annually on Alaska and Hawaiian flights). Its other main draws are travel-day perks and the ability to redeem Atmos Rewards points for flights on partner airlines.

One important distinction: The refreshed card is available to new applicants as of Nov. 18, 2026. Existing cardholders retain their previous benefits and won't automatically transition to the new version.

For a $195 annual fee, the offers travel perks that can save businesses money on Alaska Airlines and Hawaiian Airlines flights.

The refreshed card adds several benefits, including lounge passes, a Global Entry/TSA PreCheck credit and new ways to earn flight discounts through spending.

I’ve generally found Alaska to offer one of the more pleasant flying experiences among U.S. airlines, with cabin crews that are consistently friendly and service that feels more personal than that of many competitors.

The card includes one Alaska Lounge pass per calendar quarter. Alaska charges $65 for a single-entry pass, so using two passes can save you $130 if you would otherwise pay for those visits.

Alaska Lounges are concentrated on the West Coast and in Alaska, with the most locations in Seattle.

Because the passes are issued quarterly, this benefit is particularly useful for business owners who travel throughout the year rather than taking just one or two trips annually.

The card waives the fee for eligible same-day confirmed flight changes (Saver fares excluded). This can be useful if a meeting wraps up early or runs long and you need to switch flights.

The refreshed card now has a $50 Instant Travel Delay Credit voucher for a meal or drink at the airport when an eligible flight is canceled or delayed by at least two hours on the day of travel.

That could come in handy during a lengthy delay, although it won't cover larger expenses like a hotel stay or replacement airfare.

Cardholders also receive up to $120 in statement credits for Global Entry or TSA PreCheck application fees every four years.

The card also offers several other airline-specific perks that can help you save money and improve your travel experience.

When you pay for an eligible Alaska Airlines or Hawaiian Airlines flight with the card, you and up to six guests on the same reservation each get a free first checked bag. On routes where the first checked bag costs $45 each way, that’s $90 in round-trip savings per person.

You’ll also receive preferred boarding on eligible flights paid for with the card, which can help you find overhead bin space.

The card also gives you 20% back on eligible inflight purchases with Alaska Airlines and Hawaiian Airlines.

If you’re working toward elite status, this card can help.

You’ll earn 1 status point for every $3 in eligible purchases, with no cap. That makes this card a useful supplement to flight activity.

Another new benefit is the ability for the primary cardholder to share redeemable Atmos Rewards points with up to 10 other Atmos Rewards members at no additional cost.

This can be particularly useful for business owners who want to combine points with family members or other travelers to book award flights.

Businesses that put significant spending on the card can unlock additional rewards at two annual spending thresholds.

While these benefits could be valuable for businesses with substantial expenses, the six-figure spending requirements make them less attainable for smaller companies.

For international business travel, it’s helpful that the card charges no foreign transaction fees.

How effectively you earn and redeem Atmos Rewards points will play a major role in the value you get from this card.

The highest earning rate applies only to flights booked through Atmos for Business, Alaska and Hawaiian’s self-managed booking platform for companies and organizations with at least $5,000 in annual travel spending.

If you have an eligible Bank of America small-business account, you’ll receive a 10% bonus on points earned, which boosts your effective return across all categories.

For domestic Alaska Airlines and Hawaiian Airlines economy flights, you’ll typically redeem between 4,500 and 17,500 points each way. That can provide solid value.

Atmos Rewards points are my favorite airline currency to collect. I redeemed 70,000 points (plus $18 in taxes and fees) for a one-way business-class ticket from Los Angeles to Madrid earlier this year. That redemption delivered outsize value compared to the $2,100 cash fare. If you’re willing to learn partner award pricing, the upside here is significant.

In my view, the only makes sense for a specific type of traveler.

If you rarely fly Alaska or Hawaiian, you may struggle to get $195 in annual value from checked bags, lounge visits and same-day flight changes. There are no premium statement credits and no standout earning multipliers outside select bonus categories.

The card earns just 1 point per dollar on most purchases. If most of your business spending goes toward expenses outside its bonus categories, you’ll likely earn more with a flat-rate business card.

Another meaningful drawback of the refreshed card is the loss of the annual $99 Companion Fare, which cardholders could previously earn after spending $6,000 in an account anniversary year. However, existing cardholders won't automatically lose this benefit. Those who opened the card before Nov. 18, 2026, can retain their existing benefits rather than switching to the refreshed version.

The is the $95 personal card in the lineup. Both cards offer a free first checked bag and preferred boarding on eligible flights.

The Ascent also lets you earn an annual $99 Companion Fare (plus taxes and fees from $23) after spending $6,000 on purchases in an account anniversary year.

The $195 Business card offers employee cards at no additional annual cost, one Alaska Lounge pass per calendar quarter and waived fees for eligible same-day confirmed flight changes.

It also earns 10 points per dollar spent on flights booked through Atmos for Business and 2 points per dollar spent on eligible dining. The Ascent earns 2 points per dollar spent in categories such as gas, local transit, cable and select streaming services.

If you mainly fly Alaska or Hawaiian for personal trips and can use the Companion Fare, I’d lean toward the Ascent. If you need employee cards or regularly book through Atmos for Business, the business card has more to offer.

I think the is worth its $195 annual fee if your team regularly flies Alaska or Hawaiian and can use the checked bag benefit, lounge passes or same-day change waiver.

Even a solo traveler who would otherwise pay for a checked bag and a lounge visit on each of two round trips could save $310, assuming $45 bag fees each way and two $65 lounge passes. If you rarely check a bag, visit a lounge or change flights, the fee is harder to justify.

For a limited time, new cardholders can earn 90,000 bonus points after spending $6,000 on purchases within the first 90 days of account opening. Those points are worth about $1,440 based on our valuations.

This is the highest points total we've seen on the card. Previous offers included a $99 Companion Fare, so the new offer may be less valuable to you if you would have used the fare on an expensive itinerary. If you can meet the spending requirement through your usual business purchases and have a use for the points, this is a great time to apply.

Some applicants report that Bank of America strongly favors customers who already have a banking relationship with the issuer, such as a checking or savings account, though this isn’t a formal requirement and approvals still largely depend on your overall credit profile.
