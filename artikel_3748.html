Here's a terrifying reminder that the cold hand of Death is on my shoulder: I'm pretty sure I first played Black Mesa back in 2012, at the tender age of 19. In the intervening 14 years, the fan-made Half-Life 1 remake has walked quite the road, including getting Valve's official blessing to sell itself—for money!—on Steam in 2015.

And they're still going, the mad lads. Just yesterday, Black Mesa got a hefty major update "featuring visual upgrades, engine optimizations, and a complete interface overhaul". It's called the Anomalous Improvements Update and it's a real humdinger.

The biggest improvement—if you are me, a man who tries to play basically everything on his Steam Deck these days—is the Steam Deck verification and much-improved controller support. The game will now recognise and let you use a gamepad right from the jump, rather than forcing you to faff around in settings, and generally seems like a rather seamless delight on Deck in the little bit of time I've mucked about with it.

Black Mesa's also been optimised "to deliver significantly better performance across the entire game". By way of evidence, the devs show a side-by-side of Gordon Freeman hanging out in Xen. Pre-update, he gets ~130 fps. Post-update, he gets ~170. Now, admittedly, both of these framerates fall into a category I like to call "absolutely fine", but it's always nice to squeeze a little more juice out of a game.

There's other goodies too, like an overhauled UI (all the better to gamepad with), a sumptuous lighting rework, and better mod support. Also, the team brags about its "Parallax Corrected Cubemaps", which might sound like high nerdery (and it is) but translates to "local, room-accurate reflections." Quoth the devs, "Parallax-corrected cubemaps bring dynamic interior depth and realistic specular reflections" to the hallways of Black Mesa (the facility) and the ichorous tunnels of Xen like you ain't never seen before.
