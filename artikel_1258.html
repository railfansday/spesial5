Mykhailo Mudryk reveals he spent 'MILLIONS' on legal bills to fight doping charge as he blames his stint with Ukraine for two-year ban in tell-all interview

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Published: 19:00 EST, 18 November 2026 | Updated: 02:46 EST, 19 November 2026

Mykhailo Mudryk has blamed his stint with the Ukrainian national team for the failed doping test which cost him two years of his career and revealed how fighting his case personally cost him millions of pounds.

The Chelsea winger, who is now on loan with Tottenham Hotspur, has opened up for the first time since he returned to football in the summer when his suspension was suddenly lifted.

It was in a two-hour interview with Ukrainian journalist Dmitry Gordon via his YouTube channel. While the title of the video mentions ‘suicidal thoughts’, Mudryk clarifies during the conversation that he did not consider committing suicide despite being in a dark place. ‘Kill myself – no, that’s not a way out,’ he said when asked that question.

But he did discuss how Chelsea broke the news of his failed test to him, whether they helped their £89million signing from Shakhtar Donetsk during his ban, his belief that the drug meldonium entered his system while he was in Ukraine, and why he is now with Tottenham. He further claimed that he has faced threats from gangsters, and admitted his dream is to someday win the Ballon d’Or.

On the Ukrainian national team and their federation, the Ukrainian Association of Football, Mudryk said: ‘I’ll be honest, it pretended to help. But in reality, there was no help. They created more problems than they solved. Especially when we needed help the most, they turned their backs on us.

'I just would like, for the future, everyone to understand that you can only rely on yourself.'

Mykhailo Mudryk has spoken publicly for the first time about the doping case that cost him two years of his career

Mudryk has criticised the Ukrainian Association of Football over their support during his case

Daily Mail Sport contacted the Ukrainian Association of Football for comment.

Continuing, Mudryk said: ‘The UAF is afraid of being left to blame in this situation, and afraid of the criticism or whatever else might come raining down. Distancing themselves from their own player, not helping, pretending to help, and in the end, first they drag the case out for months without really helping the case in the end. But at least the UAF isn’t to blame.’

Mudryk was handed an initial four-year ban after being charged by the Football Association for failing a doping test, with his last appearance prior to that coming in November 2024 in the Conference League away at Heidenheim. He scored in that 2-0 win for Chelsea. He was able to return when a seismic resolution was struck after changes to the 'scientific reporting standards'. Mudryk appeared for Chelsea during their pre-season tour before joining Tottenham on loan.

On how and why he failed his test, Mudryk said: ‘I had never heard of a drug like meldonium, never seen it, never come across it in my life. I only learned about it when they told me the news. I definitely have ideas. It’s a drug you can only get in Eastern Europe.

‘This drug could only have got into my body where I was, and that’s Ukraine. There are no such drugs in England. It supposedly stimulates your heart and helps you, as far as I understand, handle physical load better.

‘I was at home and Chelsea’s management came to see me and they said we needed to talk. Then they say you’ve returned a positive doping test and now you’re facing a ban.

‘You can’t believe it’s true. You can’t believe this is happening to you. If I’d ever taken anything anywhere, I’d have understood that this conversation might happen one day.

‘At least I’d have understood it was possible. But when you spend a huge pile of money on watching your diet, on a nutritionist, on a chef. When you spend that much money every year on all these things, that’s the last thing you expect. Because it speaks for itself: that you take things very seriously, like nutrition, everything that goes into your body, you watch it very closely.

‘Otherwise there’d be no need to spend money on it, because you could manage without it. They tell you this news, and you sit there, and you kind of listen to them but don’t hear them.

Mudryk returned to action when his ban was lifted in August, with the winger revealing Xabi Alonso told him to get game time and that he was 'counting on him'

The Ukrainian joined Tottenham on loan but has seen injury restrict his involvement to date

‘At some point you just go into a trance. It feels like you’re falling in a dream off a skyscraper. You’re flying down, you feel yourself flying down, and only in a dream do you wake up when you hit the ground, but in real life you don’t wake up, you just keep falling. It hits you very hard mentally. At that moment the tears came later.’

Mudryk revealed how ‘the figure is in the millions’ when asked about the lawyers fees which were needed to fight his case. He confirmed he paid for that himself, along with the personal trainers and massage therapists he used to maintain his fitness while suspended. However, Mudryk did add that Chelsea continued to pay his salary during his time out of football.

On joining Tottenham and being reunited with Roberto De Zerbi, who formerly managed him at Shakhtar Donetsk, Mudryk said: ‘It’s a perfectly logical decision. The club (Chelsea) has a new coach (Xabi Alonso), he sees football his own way. He sees the players he needs for that football. He came up and told me straight to my face: “You’d better go and play, get some game time, and then come back to Chelsea. I’m counting on you.”’ On De Zerbi, he added: ‘We met and he says: “Are you happy? Come on, we’ve still got a lot of work to do together.”’

Mudryk added how difficult it was after switching to England and, along with encountering issues with his former representatives who encouraged him to move to Chelsea rather than Arsenal, he said: 'They give you ultimatums, you know. That’s how it is.’
