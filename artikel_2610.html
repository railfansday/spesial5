Demetrious Johnson Criticizes UFC 334 Main Event, Explains What’s Missing Compared to PRIDE Heavyweights

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The heavyweight clash between Ciryl Gane and Josh Hokit proved to be the highlight of UFC 334. Despite entering the fight with plenty of questions surrounding his championship status, Gane had to dig deep over 25 minutes to get past the relentless Hokit and walk away with a unanimous decision victory.

Hokit entered the contest as a divisive title challenger, with many questioning whether he had done enough to earn a shot at the belt. However, both men put those narratives aside and delivered an entertaining contest that kept fans engaged until the final bell.

The judges scored the fight 48-47 across the board in Gane's favor, underlining just how competitive the matchup was. The heavyweight showdown also produced a historic 383 combined significant strikes, setting a new UFC record for the division.

The performance earned widespread praise from several notable names in the sport, including Dana White, Alex Pereira, Francis Ngannou, Paulo Costa, and Merab Dvalishvili. However, Demetrious Johnson was far from impressed.

The former UFC flyweight champion watched the fight and shared his live reactions. And it is safe to say that he was not particularly pleased with Gane's approach. Johnson repeatedly criticized the Frenchman for fighting off the back foot and focusing on neutralizing Hokit's forward pressure.

He also questioned why Gane did not use techniques such as clinching against the fence to slow down the pace and establish better control. During his livestream on YouTube, Johnson did not hold back, saying, "What are we watching right now? This is literally fucking horrible. Where is the athleticism? Where is the explosiveness? Where are the body kicks? I'd rather watch K-1 kickboxing than this s**t."

However, Johnson offered a more technical breakdown in a separate post-fight reaction video on his YouTube channel. He said, "I'm not going to lie, at the beginning I was laughing, then the second part I was kind of like, they're just kind of rocking sockin robots."

Johnson then discussed Hokit's fighting style, acknowledging the heavyweight's impressive pace while pointing out the technical limitations in his striking.

He said, "You know Josh Hokit, he does something very special. He puts on a an amazing pace, but he doesn't have a lot of technique to go with that amazing pace, meaning that he's got a great 1-2, great wrestling, and that's about it. There's no, you know, I'm out here popping a jab, popping a jab inside nine, popping a jab 1-1-2 move to angle 2-3-2-3, leg kick. It's basically 1-2, 1-2, 1-2, 1-2."

Despite the criticism, Johnson was impressed with Hokit's potential and believes the heavyweight could eventually become a world champion with some technical improvements.

He added, "If Josh Hokit takes about 6 months to a year to really sharpen his kickboxing and his clinch game, I think he could be a world champion, and he almost won that fight against Gane, and that was because of the pace that he could put on people right."

Johnson then shifted his attention to Gane's performance and what he felt was missing from the Frenchman's game plan. He compared the current heavyweight division to the PRIDE era, when fighters such as Fedor Emelianenko, Minotauro Nogueira, and Mirko Cro Cop regularly delivered explosive performances.

He said, "When I just think about, like, the old-school heavyweights, I think about, like, Fedor Emelianenko, Minotauro Nogueira, even Cro Cop; I just feel there's something missing in this heavyweight era. I feel like, but I was watching it, and I was like, I want to go back and watch K1″

"K1 Dynamite Max heavyweights, like they were just boom, just so much faster on the feet. But Ciryl Gane was backing up the whole entire time, and I felt the first couple rounds Gane just couldn't get his rhythm."

Johnson also acknowledged that Gane eventually found his footing as the fight progressed. The champion appeared to find another gear in the championship rounds, using his size and strength more effectively to wear down Hokit.

"And then I think third round, fourth round, fifth round, that's when he hit his second wind, and then that's when he started to actually pick up the pace, started to use his size, started to weigh on Josh Hokit."

Although Johnson's assessment of the main event was particularly harsh, he ultimately considered UFC 334 a solid card. More importantly, Gane's victory and Hokit's impressive showing gave the heavyweight division some much-needed momentum.
