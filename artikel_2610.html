Bucks vs. Raptors Player Grades: Herro and Turner lead the way in slide-ending victory

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

After losing 13 consecutive games, the Milwaukee Bucks finally tasted victory, defeating the Toronto Raptors 116-111 in a nail-biter. The Bucks had been building toward this—having deserved to beat the 76ers in their last game—and finally put it all together in The Six. Most encouragingly, Milwaukee got key contributions from a struggling veteran in Myles Turner, while both rookies played very well and closed the game. Hopefully this carries into the rest of the season, and the Bucks can stack more wins. Read our full summary of the game here and catch a six-minute audio recap on the Bucks+ podcast, Bucks In Six Minutes, below.

37 minutes, 32 points, 6 rebounds, 8 assists, 1 steal, 1 block, 3 turnovers, 10/16 FG, 4/5 3P, 8/8 FT, +10

What a funny game for Herro, who scored just three points on 1/5 shooting in the first half (granted, he did have four assists), and then 29 in the second half on 9/11 from the field and 8/8 from the charity stripe. Herro mentioned this in his postgame interview, but aside from some nonchalant moments dribbling the ball up the court, I didn't think he played badly in the first half; the Raptors gave him a lot of attention, and so he gave the ball up early. Then in the second half, the vet got more opportunities and found a rhythm, especially at the free-throw line. Herro's composure throughout the second half, to shepherd the young Bucks to victory, was great to watch.

22 minutes, 6 points, 3 assists, 1 block, 3 turnovers, 3/8 FG, 0/2 3P, -16

I don't know what others thought, but it almost felt like Rollins looked a little small out there against the likes of Shead and Walter. He just struggled to make much of an impact on either side of the ball.

32 minutes, 18 points, 5 rebounds, 3 assists, 0 turnovers, 8/15 FG, 2/3 3P, +4

Aside from his customary contested three with 18 on the shot clock, Kuzma was pretty damn solid. He was Scottie Barnes' primary defender, and although Barnes made a bunch of shots over him, most felt well contested. Kuzma's two-to-three-point ratio was much better in this one too; 2-4 attempts per game seems like a much better balance than 6-8. I also love that he didn't turn it over.

24 minutes, 3 points, 4 rebounds, 2 assists, 1/7 FG, 1/5 3P, -4

Again, zero impact from Jaquez. When he starts alongside Kuzma, one of them will get open threes; you can't go 1/5.

28 minutes, 27 points, 11 rebounds, 2 blocks, 4 turnovers, 6 PF, 9/12 FG, 5/6 3P, 4/5 FT, -12

What a game from Turner—best of the season by a long way. When he looks for his shot the way he did last night, the Bucks look so much more lethal, and so does he; Turner had 11 points in the first quarter. Turner made two huge plays down the stretch, and the emotion he showed after both of them was great to see. Also, a solid second audition for the Raptors' front office, just quietly, after Turner bungled the first one. I know you liked what you saw, Bobby Webster.

23 minutes, 9 points, 3 rebounds, 3 blocks, 0 turnovers, 4/6 FG, 1/2 3P, +22

Despite his modest stat line, I thought Ware was great. One triple and three lobs; I love how he's been rolling with purpose and intent lately. How he caught that lob from Ament is beyond me; ridiculous athlete. That said, the most impressive part of Ware's game to me was his defence; for all the (fair) criticism you can level at him, his timing blocking shots is easily one of his best features. Figuring it out.

29 minutes, 14 points, 10 rebounds, 4 assists, 3 steals, 4 turnovers, 4/7 FG, 1/2 3P, 5/6 FT, +20

Impact. Impact. Impact. Burries made four shots and was still easily one of the best players. His poise down the stretch on both ends was amazing. That smooth crossover to get to the cup in crunch time wasn't even his best late play; his steal/wrench on Barnes took that spot. I don't know if I've ever seen a play like that; it reminded me of a former Buck who used to make those sorts of plays late in games… The kid's a maniac in the best way possible.

25 minutes, 7 points, 4 rebounds, 1 assist, 1 block, 1 turnover, 2/5 FG, 3/4 FT, +7

Ament's immense impact in this game will not show up on the stat sheet, and that may be the most pleasing part. I've wanted to feel his impact more and more, and this game felt like his best yet at impacting winning at a high level regardless of his stats. The main part of that was how well he guarded Scottie Barnes. Ament moved with him in space and forced tough shots; he was so good at it that Jenkins closed with him because they needed the length and foot speed he provided to win the game. Also, the Raptors tried to hide their worst defender, Jamison Battle, on Ament, which meant he had to play as a screener to get Battle into the action. I liked the rookie's ability to play out of those actions; he missed Ware on a lob once out of the short roll, but that will serve him well in the long run. Finally, just filing the play where Ament came hard off the Ware screen and threw the lob over Poeltl; I'm not sure he has had a play like that this season. If the rook can add that string to his bow…

I'll get the negative out of the way first: I still don't love starting Juquez and Kuzma, and the stats back that up—starters had a -32.8 net rating in 13 minutes. Other than that, Jenkins pressed many of the right buttons; the call to close with Ament was gutsy, and it paid off in spades. I also thought his overall game management was solid. Going at Poeltl relentlessly allowed 1) the Bucks to get in the bonus early in the fourth, which allowed them to keep the scoreboard ticking over as they went on that field-goal drought, and 2) to foul Poeltl out and thus open up the rim to score with no shot blocker present. Called some well-timed timeouts too when the team was frazzled trying to nurse the lead.

Limited Minutes: Caris LeVert, Kasparas Jakucionis, AJ Green, Gary Trent Jr.

Inactive: Pete Nance, Cormac Ryan, Kam Jones, Bogoljub Markovic

The Bucks could have played much better in many areas, yet they still won. I swear, this team can compete with more teams than people think.

Postgame, Jenkins praised the group's ability to stick to it, saying that eventually, the breakthrough would happen, and it happened.

Myles Turner reached 10,000 career points last night. His reaction: "Man, there are really no words for it. It's a testament to a lot of hard work and a lot of patience… There's no question that I've been struggling a little bit to start the season, but to have a breakthrough like tonight was really big for my confidence and just a reminder of who I am in this league."

The Bucks avoid "beating" the franchise-record 15 consecutive losses. I'm sure it would have felt good for the players to get that monkey off their backs.

The Bucks had 11 first-half turnovers (and 17 overall). I couldn't find the Raptors' points off turnovers in the first half, but they beat the Bucks 15-6 in fast break points for the game. Of course, that would have been 15-10 if Milwaukee converted on either of those easy opportunities in the fourth.

Tyler Herro's averages over the last two games: 41 PPG, 8.5 RPG, and 7.5 APG shooting 64% from the field, 65% from three, and 100% from the free throw line.

The Bucks beat the Raptors 52-44 in the paint battle.

Jenkins cut his rotation to about eight guys after previously using nine or 10.

I do want to recognise how valuable those end-of-game reps will be for Ament and Burries. You can train for those situations all you want, but nothing can replicate the real thing.

In the fourth, the Bucks had a stretch where they had all three of Kuzma, Jaquez, and Ament in the game.

The Bucks try to continue their winning ways on Wednesday night against the Brooklyn Nets. Catch the game at 7:00 p.m. CST on MY24.
