Hot Take Tuesday: Do the Chicago Bears have a Garrett Bradbury problem?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

It's a glorious week. The Chicago Bears won, albeit ugly, raising their record to 8-2, and they may have the biggest Bears-Lions game coming up on Thanksgiving in the Super Bowl era.

The winner of the game will have sole possession of first place and put themselves in prime position to earn the number 2 (possibly, but doubtful, the number 1) seed entering the NFL playoffs.

The Bears are a road favorite on Thursday afternoon, but if they are going to win this game, the offense is going to have to play better than it has in the last two games. Yes, the defense should be able to keep the Lions' explosive attack mostly at bay, but the Lions are still going to almost certainly crack 20 points, so the Bears offense will have to do some scoring of their own.

The offense has looked out of sorts, and that's largely due to the running game not firing the way it should. I firmly believe that if the running game gets going, the passing game will click right along with it, and this offense will start humming like we all know it can.

But why is the running game having issues? Many of us (Tom Brady included) thought that the running game was just missing Kyle Monangai, and when he returned, so would the rushing attack.

But it didn't happen. And it didn't happen against a poor run defense.

Yes, Monangai was ineffective, and if he had more success, things could have been better, but why didn't he have success? Why is D'Andre Swift well below his yards per carry that he averaged last year?

I'm not going to pretend I'm Brian Baldinger breaking down the tape, but when I watch the OL play, I watch a combo of Darnell Wright and Jonah Jackson that looks just as effective as it did in 2025. I see Theo Benedet possibly playing the best LT that anyone has played over the last one and a half seasons. Joe Thuney is still a good player, but he definitely isn't playing as well as he did last season, and that brings us to center.

Garrett Bradbury is not playing well; he has not been playing well, and him next to Thuney is definitely the spot in the line that you can circle as the weakness in the running game. You can't run behind Wright and Jackson every single time; it simply won't work.

Last year with Drew Dalman, the center and guards were doubling, scraping, and getting to the second level at an absolutely fantastic level. The second-level blocks just aren't there as consistently as they were last year, and I think that's creating an issue for the rushing attack to be cohesive.

The blocking is not awful. If this was the blocking we were getting three or four years ago, we'd all be over the moon, but times have changed, the Bears are trying to win a Super Bowl, and there needs to be an improvement.

Don't get me wrong, there have been other things that have prevented the Bears from running the ball as well; Cole Kmet has been a significant loss. Sam Roush continues to improve, and hopefully in a few more weeks he will be closer to what Kmet was, but he isn't there yet.

So while Bradbury isn't the only problem, he is one of the problems, and he's one of the problems that can be fixed. Is it time to try Logan Jones?

Transitioning to a different center is a tricky thing to do midseason. You want consistency at the position. A short week is not the right time to switch to a rookie; the bye week would have made sense, but it also could be done with extended time between the Thanksgiving game and the following week.

I think the Bears should give Bradbury this game, and if it's still not working, I think they need to make a change at center.

Is Logan Jones ready? He certainly was an experienced college player, so one would hope so. I know Ben Johnson puts a lot on a center's shoulders, but while Bradbury might be handling the between-the-ears, he certainly isn't handling the physical.

If Jones isn't ready, is it too crazy to call Drew Dalman and see if he's still in playing shape and if he's changed his mind? Those really are the only two options, but here's what I know: if the run game operates like this as the temperatures get cold and the competition heats up, the Bears aren't going to win the Super Bowl.

Perhaps there's a way to make this run game work with Bradbury. Ben Johnson is a lot smarter than me, so if there's anyone who can find a way, it's Ben. I know he knows the run game is currently unacceptable. But when Ben Johnson says it's going to be a focal point of the bye week, Kyle Monangai returns, the Bears are playing a poor run defense, and this was the result? It's time to be a little worried.

We are far from panicking; there's absolutely no reason to do that at this point, but it's time to start trying a few different things to see if they can make this thing go, and the center position needs to be at or near the top of the list.

There's a lot on the line on Thanksgiving; if the Bears are going to have another statement win on national television, it needs to start with running the football. It's time for Ben Johnson to separate the men from the boys and make this thing go. Thursday would be the perfect day to start.
