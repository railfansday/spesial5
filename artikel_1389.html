KILMARNOCK 2 ABERDEEN 4: Dazzling Dons get a Frick pick-up but it's more misery for McCann's men

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Published: 15:33 EST, 21 November 2026 | Updated: 16:15 EST, 21 November 2026

Having been bored senseless by the football offered up under Stephen Robinson, Aberdeen supporters were – at the very least – crying out for the next appointment to provide some sort of entertainment to get excited about over the dark winter months.

In Mario Frick, they may have got their man. Whether or not the former Luzern boss will be a success at Pittodrie is anyone’s guess. But on this evidence, it’s going to be a lot of fun watching him have a go.

At times, they were scintillating here on their way to a thrilling win which moved them five clear of their hosts and provided some much-needed breathing room ahead of a tricky upcoming schedule which involves trips to Ibrox and Tynecastle, as well as two home clashes against the Dundee clubs.

Yes, it wasn’t perfect. Far from it, in fact. Defensively, they were far too open. Kilmarnock just weren’t good enough to take full advantage.

But you suspect that will be forgiven to an extent as long as they can play with this level of panache and aggression going forward.

Findlay Marshall celebrates after scoring the Dons' fourth goal at Rugby Park

Frick has arrived with the promise of bringing ‘heavy metal’ football to Pittodrie. It’s taken a little over 90 minutes to understand what he's talking about.

‘It was very chaotic,’ he admitted. ‘It's quite different to Switzerland, because there is technicality, but it’s not as physical as it was today.

‘It was difficult. We were lucky as well because Killie, especially in the second half, played very well, but I think at the end it's a deserved victory.

‘When the players were young, they didn’t think about mistakes, didn’t think about any negatives.

‘They played only to win for fun, and I told them to play as if they were young players and I think, especially first half, it was very good.

‘You don't know what's going on or what can happen in this first game, so I'm very happy we won.’

Frick shows his delight after tasting victory in his first match in charge of Aberdeen

Kilmarnock spurned a golden opportunity to take a sixth-minute lead at the end of a rapid counter-attack, with Joe Hugill finding himself all alone in the Dons box.

The striker took a heavy first touch, allowing Marius Muller to quickly narrow the angle and block the effort. It’s easy to see why Hugill has found the back of the net just twice this year.

And the hosts were made to pay for that miss within 90 seconds as Aberdeen went in front.

For the umpteenth time this season, Killie failed to clear their lines under little pressure, with Stuart Armstrong teeing up Kevin Nisbet to sweep home a lovely finish from the edge of the area. The unsighted Nico Mantl in the Killie goal didn’t even move.

Mind you, he should have done significantly better four minutes later as Nisbet notched his second of the afternoon.

The keeper’s positioning as the Dons striker stepped up to send a low free-kick in off the post was as poor as it gets.

You got the feeling that the walls would have caved in had Aberdeen grabbed a third, but a brief drop-off in intensity from Frick’s side enabled Killie to drag themselves back into the contest.

A bit of pinball outside the Dons box saw the ball fall favourably for skipper Greg Kiltie 12 yards out, who adjusted his body brilliantly to drill beyond Muller on the half-volley.

Yet with a defence as dodgy as this, manager Neil McCann can never feel overly confident.

Ante Palaversa – terrific on his first appearance of the season – was given far too much time in the centre circle to get his head up and pick out Nesta Guinness-Walker on the left.

He glided past MJ Kamson-Kamara as if he wasn’t there before dinking a ball right on to the head of Brad Lyons, who rose completely unopposed to power home against his former employers.

If Killie continue to defend like this, relegation is a certainty. Will the manager even get the chance to turn it around? Going off the abuse being hurled in his direction from the home punters, his jacket is evidently on a shoogly peg. Many fans have already made up their mind.

Nisbet's free-kick finds the net for his second as the Dons take control of the game

Michael Schjonning-Larsen’s deflected effort from 20 yards shortly after the restart looked destined for the goal before Muller instinctively leapt to his right to claw it away on the line. It was a superb stop.

Goodness knows what the keeper was thinking as he tried to pass out from the six-yard line, playing it straight to the feet of Tyreece John-Jules – only just on the park – who steadied himself before finishing.

You would have stuck your house on Nisbet putting this one to bed with five minutes remaining, however with just Mantl to beat, he pulled his left-footed effort wide.

Findlay Marshall showed him how it’s done with stoppage-time looming, running half the length of the field before drilling low into the corner to consign Killie to another sobering defeat.

McCann said: ‘I probably sound like a broken record, but we create really big, big chances, and we don't take them.

‘We're letting games pass us by. We're giving teams the opportunity to start ahead of us because of the goals we're conceding and then it's difficult to work back.’
