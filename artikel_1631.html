Arsenal 1-1 Dortmund: Familiar flaws hold Arsenal back

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A 1-1 draw against Borussia Dortmund in the Champions League on Tuesday raised some very familiar concerns and, if we’re being honest, revealed nothing we didn’t already know.

Arsenal can play world-class football and completely dominate teams for long spells, but lack the world-class attack to make the most of that domination, the sort of players you just know will create and convert big chances when the time comes. And the way the team is starting games after the break is worrying, with teams who are battered to within an inch of their lives early on suddenly able to compete.

I really feel like I could write two versions of today’s blog. One where I’m miserable, of course: the players can’t shoot, they make all the wrong choices in the decisive moment, we can’t keep wasting chances and dropping off after the break, if Andrea Berta doesn’t deliver the true heirs to Thierry Henry and Robert Pires in January, then he should be stuffed into one of the cannons outside the Armoury and fired into the cold night air.

The other version is much more glass half full: Arteta is coaching masterclasses every week, this level of dominance in games is something I could only dream of a few years ago, that’s the most shots we’ve had in a game all season, we’re top of the league, we’re creating loads without any set-piece reliance and without Bukayo Saka – the goals will come.

Some people reading this would prefer (and relate better to) one version, and others the other version, I’m sure. Let’s see where we end up!

There were, as there tends to be in these Champions League games, a fair few changes from the weekend. Kepa, Piero Hincapie, Ben White, Myles Lewis-Skelly, Christos Tzolis, and Viktor Gyokeres all came into the team.

Despite featuring half a different side, there were plenty of parallels with the Newcastle match. Arsenal were dominant from the off, with Hincapie and White taking turns to play off of Gyokeres. Dortmund had no idea how to cover them and both fullbacks were the targets of early crosses into the box.

After a couple of long-range efforts, Arsenal got their goal they deserved, with Gyokeres launching a ferocious, dipping effort beyond Gregor Kobel. The Swede can put his foot through the ball, that’s for sure, and the Dortmund backline gave him the sort of time and space to set himself that he doesn’t often get.

The pressure continued after the opener for a while, with Tzolis forcing a good save and Madueke just unable to sort his feet to tap in the rebound.

It’s fair to say the game settled down after 15/20 minutes, with Dortmund growing into it a little, but it was still Arsenal who looked like scoring the next goal. Rice shot over from almost the same patch of grass Gyokeres had scored from, and the midfielder had another chance when a beautiful one-touch move ended with him bursting through, hurtling towards the Dortmund box. Maybe he could’ve picked out Madueke in support, but he went alone and Nico Schlotterbeck just got across to block the shot.

Another huge chance was denied by the offside flag as much as the finishing – Madueke won the ball high, Odegaard squared it to Gyokeres, and the Swede fired over from near the penalty spot. Odegaard was ruled offside but I’m not convinced: had the ball hit the net, I think we’d have seen a very tight VAR check.

There was one thought on everyone’s minds at half-time: don’t do the same thing again. After Slavia Prague and Newcastle, do not do the same thing again.

Gyokeres was, by UEFA standards, lucky not to concede a penalty when a corner struck him on the arm. There was another early warning sign with Dortmund sliding the ball across the box and Maximilian Beier kicking the air. He didn’t make the same mistake with his next opportunity. Centre-back Joane Gadou (who was very impressive all evening) charged through the heart of the pitch before playing the ball to Beier, who jinked to unbalance Gabriel and slotted home.

Sometimes a goal is just a good goal, and I don’t think anyone was really at fault here. It’s a great run from Gadou, Beier tricks Gabriel to take the ball onto his weaker left side – it’s fair if Gabriel is expecting (and blocking the angle) on the attacker’s favoured foot, I know how that can happen. I think if we score that goal we’re talking about two brilliant individuals, rather than defensive errors.

My first thought? Not again. My second? OK, how do we respond? Because on Saturday, the response was poor.

This time it was a lot better. Jobe Bellingham had a rebound disallowed for offside, a moment that really brought the crowd back into the game, but in truth, that was Dortmund’s only real moment of threat. From the goal on 55 minutes to full-time, Arsenal had 15 shots to Dortmund’s two.

And that doesn’t include Serhou Guirassy playing one-twos with the post after a clever, low free-kick from Martin Odegaard late on.

Madueke had an effort blocked. Gyokeres flicked a header wide. Madueke shot wide beyond the far post after a good run. Gyokeres missed a big chance with his head from six yards out. Rice hit the near post from a tight angle after a brilliant pass from Eberechi Eze.

A couple of late corners came to nothing, and that was it.

Mikel Arteta was clearly pleased with the performance on the whole:

Well, I think I love the way the team played. There’s no question how superior, how dominant, how much of a threat we were, how connected everybody was to the game … It was great to watch. It was unbelievable that sometimes the ball hasn’t ended up in the back of the net. That’s my feeling.

to do what we’ve done out there is just something to be very, very pleased about, because there is very little more that we can demand from the team.

But we all know what that little more is:

obviously the feeling is that we should reward ourselves much more when you put that kind of performance, the amount of chances, the situation, and even shots that are not there. We are three against two, two against one, and the ball doesn’t land with the right person. There is an element of luck, there is an element of us finishing the actions better. We need to insist, because I think what the team has done and shown today is the right direction.

Often when Arsenal don’t win games, it feels like it’s because the team haven’t created enough chances. We end up focusing on that one moment, where that one player didn’t execute the way you wanted. But last night wasn’t one of those: Arsenal didn’t struggle to turn dominance into good, slick football, and that football into a healthy number of decent opportunities.

That’s incredibly frustrating, but I do think you win a lot more often than not when you do that. Maybe that’s a hard sell for some this morning, but it’s how I feel about it when we play well, especially when we’ve been winning games anyway. That’s the first time this season we’ve gone two without a win across all competitions, and we’ve won 14 of 19 games so far. Sometimes things don’t fall for you, and this one felt a lot like ‘one of those’, certainly more than Newcastle did.

A word for a couple of promising performances too, with the boss asked about Gyokeres in his post-match press conference:

What I love the most is how connected he was to the team. We used him many times in different ways. His link-up play, the way he held the ball, the way he threw it in behind and stretched the pitch. There was a lot to like in that performance and that’s the victory that we need.

His finishing was off after the brilliant opener, but at least he was in the positions: too often this season and last, Gyokeres has gone through a game barely registering a shot. He had seven last night, and if he can do that more often he’ll score a lot more. His general play was improved too, both holding the ball up and competing in behind.

And I thought Martin Odegaard was excellent too. His eight chances created from open play was the most of any player in any Champions League match so far this season.

Since we returned to the competition in 2023, we’ve had a player create more than five chances in a match just twice: Odegaard at Napoli in September (6) and Odegaard last night (9). He’s incredibly unlucky not to be leaving the game with a couple of assists.

The draw leaves us in a decent position to finish in the all-important top eight in the league phase still, although you have to squint a bit to see us landing in the also-important top two. Real Madrid are up next and that’s a reminder of the sort of teams we’re up against: finishing in the top two is a tall order at the best of times.

With each passing game, it is impossible not to wonder what could be done in January to add to the team. Unless there’s a sudden uptick out of nowhere, those thoughts aren’t disappearing. And even if there is a massive uptick, those thoughts will still be around. But 30 shots is the most we’ve had in a game all season, and the same group of players have scored more goals with fewer shots (/chances) plenty of times. Let’s hope we see some more clinical finishing on Sunday.

Guess I landed on the glass half full version.
