‘Three or four’: Roberto De Zerbi outlines Tottenham transfer plans

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Your weekly briefing on Tottenham, featuring team news and expert analysis from Sam Tabuteau

Roberto De Zerbi believes Tottenham are “three or four positions” away from completing the squad rebuild.

After securing Premier League survival on the final day of last season, Spurs moved aggressively and decisively in the summer transfer window, twice breaking their transfer record to sign midfielders Mateus Fernandes and Sandro Tonali for a combined £185m.

Centre-back Jan Paul van Hecke signed from Brighton for around £52m, while Savio arrived later in the window from Manchester City for a fee that could reach £85m if all add-ons are met.

Omar Marmoush also swapped City for Spurs, signing on loan with a conditional £60m obligation to buy at the end of the season, while Spurs further bolstered their squad with the free transfers of Marcos Senesi and Andy Robertson.

A mammoth summer of spending then came to a close on deadline day when Chelsea pair Tosin Adarabioyo and Mykhailo Mudryk arrived at Hotspur Way.

Spurs have undergone seismic changes over the last six months and finally appear to be bearing the fruits of their labour and sizeable financial outlay, with De Zerbi’s side winning four of their last five games.

The club’s position on and off the pitch is a far cry from the dire situation they found themselves in when De Zerbi took charge of his first game away to Sunderland in May, with the club mired in relegation trouble.

A 1-0 defeat that afternoon had Spurs seriously thinking about the possibility of life in the Championship. But as De Zerbi prepares to return to the Stadium of Light for the first time since that near-fateful day, he admits Spurs need just three or four more additions to complete their squad.

“As a team, as a squad, I think we are in a very good position because we, I think we made a great session of the transfer market. We changed ten, twelve players, and we brought all first targets in the window,” De Zerbi said.

“And for the future, I think we don't need in too many positions. I think we need to finish the squad. Yes, in maybe three, four positions, and then we have not to move too many players.

“We need to stay together. We need to improve the connection, the relationships. Improve the style of play, the quality of the play. To improve the mentality, but I think we are in the good way to become better and to do something special for this club.”

Having finished just one point above the relegation zone last season, Spurs have not had to contend with European football this term, allowing De Zerbi precious time on the training pitch to instil his ideas.

They are now, though, approaching a run of nine games in five weeks that will test their squad’s durability and increased depth as injuries continue to hamper them.

Despite their injury issues, De Zerbi believes his squad have an advantage heading into the festive period, with Spurs enjoying more time to rest, recover and prepare for matches in the first few months of the season.

“Yes, I think it is a big advantage,” De Zerbi continued before acknowledging that the benefit of one game per week only works in the first full season when you are trying to build principles and identity.

“For sure, the first season when you are working on the basis of the team, it’s better playing one game per week.

“But if you are from two, three, four seasons already on the bench, I think nothing changed because you have a stronger squad, you have a stronger principle of the play.

“You have not to lose too much time preparing the game or giving information, giving principles. The players in two seasons, they already know everything.

“Yes, you can prepare in the more details the game, more strategy and less principle. While now we are working more in principle and less in the strategy, but at the end it's part of the journey. It's a natural journey.”
