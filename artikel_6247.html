Qantas Launches Historic New 15-Hour Nonstop Route From Sydney: See Map Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

In an unexpected and highly intriguing network development, Qantas has announced a brand-new long-haul route. The oneworld member will begin flying from Sydney to the increasingly popular Cape Town.

It'll be historical in various ways. For example, it'll become Qantas' record third route to South Africa, while being the first time that Australia has had flights to Cape Town. It'll also be Cape Town's new longest route within the Southern Hemisphere. However, it will be far from Qantas' longest nonstop service on the Boeing 787.

On October 19, 2027, during the spring in the Southern Hemisphere, Qantas will lift off from its busiest and critical hub of Sydney Kingsford Smith Airport (SYD) to Cape Town International Airport (CPT). On a great circle basis, the brand-new route will cover 5,945 nautical miles (11,010 km) each way.

As this represents the shortest distance between two airports, real-world operations will increase that distance. There won't be any payload restriction, which means a full load of passengers and belly freight.

QF90: 5:55 PM-2:50 PM+1 (12 hours and 55 minutes)

Like so many new routes, SYD-CPT will be flown three times weekly. Given the block time and stage length of the airport pair, this means that one Boeing 787-9 will be able to operate it.

All of Qantas' 787-9s have 236 seats, which is not many. That's mainly because they're quite premium, with 42 seats in business (1-2-1), 28 in premium economy (2-3-2; 38" pitch), and 166 in economy (3-3-3; 32"). Around 18% of the capacity is in business.

Booking data shows that 87,000 round-trip passengers flew between Australia and CPT in 2025, with traffic up by nearly a fifth compared to 2024. While many South Africans live in Australia, it is unclear how many come from the CPT area. Many of the passengers would have been Australians visiting the increasingly popular city for tourism.

Unsurprisingly, SYD was the largest market to/from CPT. With around 23,000 passengers, the local traffic was not overly high, although the new nonstop flights may grow that by around 40%. The airline will also target passengers connecting to Auckland Airport (AKL), Brisbane, and Melbourne, in particular, with those places having around 42,000 passengers.

Significantly, SYD-CPT is relatively high-yielding, with the average fare across all airlines, cabins, and passengers being US$1,335 one way (excluding taxes but including a fuel surcharge). Reflecting higher premium demand, the average fare for SYD-CPT is around 12% higher than for SYD to Johannesburg's O. R. Tambo International Airport (JNB).

Interestingly, SYD-CPT flights will cover marginally less distance than SYD-JNB. As such, the fare difference is particularly notable, especially when coupled with similar operating expenses. Commenting on the new route, senior airline network planner, African specialist, and industry commentator Behramjee Ghadially said that:

"This new service will perform very well for Qantas, seeing how the Aussies love to visit new exotic places, especially where a combination of beach, wine and safari can be experienced in an upscale manner [although most safari-related activities are in eastern South Africa].

Qantas will also target tour operators selling South Africa packages from Australia and New Zealand as they can now offer an open-jaw itinerary for their clientele, such as flying into JNB and flying back from CPT using the same airline."

As the above map shows, Qantas will now fly from SYD to CPT and JNB, along with from Perth International Airport (PER) to JNB. The latter route is the only one on which the airline has head-to-head competition, as it competes directly with the troubled South African Airways. It is also the one on which Qantas deploys an Airbus twinjet.

OAG data shows that Qantas flew between PER and JNB until 2023, with the route, which is nearly entirely overwater, returning 11 months ago, in December 2025. The operating A330-200 flies AKL-PER-JNB-PER-AKL. At some point, it then operates to SYD, where the specific airframe is switched.
