The Yangwang Altior is a Mega-Luxury Electric Sedan from China Aimed Right at Rolls-Royce

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

BYD’s luxury arm has an answer to those in the super-luxury end of the market with this quad-motor EV. It even looks like a Rolls.

Last week, Yangwang broke a speed record. This week, the Chinese luxury car brand is doing something rather different: it’s revealing a Rolls-Royce-esque full-size luxury sedan called the Altior. And sure, plenty of the details are unique to this Yangwang, but it’s hard to look at this sedan and not see the famous Ghost in its proportions (it’s about two inches longer than one) and design language.

The chassis and powertrain under this car’s sheet metal is nothing like that of the Rolls it looks so much like, though. Instead of a V-12, the Altior is fitted with a quad-motor electric powertrain, with all four electric motors independent from one another. Total output isn’t available, but we suspect it will have to be somewhere in the 1000-horsepower range with that many electric motors.

Yangwang makes several neat claims as to the car’s abilities. For one, it’s apparently capable of performing a tank turn; we’re not sure how useful that will be in a luxury sedan, but the possibility exists. An active body control system reads the road ahead and adjusts the suspension to provide the most comfortable ride possible. Yangwang also claims the Altior has an “emergency flotation capability” that can “provide additional support if the vehicle accidentally enters water.” That sounds similar to Elon Musk’s claims about the Cybertruck being capable of briefly acting as a boat ... and in both cases, we wouldn’t recommend testing the claim.

Power comes from what Yangwang says is its second-generation “Blade Battery,” and the claim is 621 miles of range on China’s very optimistic CLTC cycle. Charging specs weren’t revealed, but the Blade Battery 2.0 with “Flash Charging” (which the Altior has) in the Yangwang U7 has an astounding peak charging rate of about 1500 kilowatts.

The Altior’s interior is undeniably gorgeous, though it trades much of the ornamentation of a Rolls for the tech-forward screens we’re accustomed to seeing in high-end Chinese EVs. Hetian jade is used for the air vents, while semi-aniline leather is found on the seats, and Yangwang uses Suzhou embroidery (the technique involves splitting silk threads) to create flowing cloud motifs. Real metal switches are found throughout. The floor mats are made of wool, and natural woods were selected for the trim.

A four-seat layout and so-called zero-gravity seats with massage ensure the rear occupants are mighty comfortable. The car has a 39-speaker Dynaudio sound system, while a 21.4-inch overhead screen drops down to provide entertainment for those in the back. Plus, there are two fridges, and the one in the rear even has a warming function. In case you needed further evidence of the inspiration Rolls had on this interior’s design, there are even bespoke umbrellas that are integrated in the doors. (That said, Yangwang says it got the idea from the “Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests at China’s Temple of Heaven complex,” with no mention of Rolls to be found.)

This massive sedan sure won’t see roads in the U.S., but Yangwang intends to sell it in Europe in the future, where it can go toe-to-toe with the British-made luxury sedans it borrows so much from.
