China's military is actively seeking Nvidia's top AI chips, U.S. commission says

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission says Chinese firms also reached Nvidia chips through data centers in Malaysia

China's People's Liberation Army is "actively soliciting" top-tier Nvidia $NVDA processors to power military applications driven by artificial intelligence, according to CNBC, which cited the 2026 annual report to Congress from the U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission, released on Wednesday.

"China is seeking to evade U.S. export controls to gain the most advanced semiconductors that underpin its intelligentization drive," the commission said in the report, according to CNBC.

The commission said Chinese-made chips from makers like Huawei and Cambricon still lag behind the products of Nvidia and American competitors like Advanced Micro Devices. China still has not cleared the toughest hurdles, the commission said: cutting-edge logic manufacturing, memory, chipmaking tools and Nvidia's CUDA software, and it probably won't manage that anytime soon. "The resulting gap in compute is substantial, largely due to U.S. export controls on advanced chips," it said, according to CNBC.

The commission also said Chinese companies have reached Nvidia chips remotely, through data centers located in third countries such as Malaysia.

The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule this week on remote access to advanced chips and servers. BIS said the rule is meant to keep existing restrictions from being circumvented through remote computing arrangements involving data centers in third countries. The Treasury Department and the National Security Council wrote the rule, BIS said.

The rule came after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and members of Congress faulted Commerce for failing to adequately block the diversion of Nvidia's top-end chips into China, according to CNBC.

Nvidia has also been shaken by CEO Jensen Huang’s resignation, which followed reports linking him romantically to a former Megaspeed executive suspected of spying for China, according to CNBC. U.S. investigators are looking at Megaspeed for possible diversion of Nvidia chips to China.

In October, a Chinese university closely linked to the PLA bought two Nvidia H200 chips despite being on a U.S. export blacklist. An Oct. 21 procurement record on Northwestern Polytechnical University’s website shows the purchase cost 874,700 yuan ($130,600).

In a separate case, a Chinese company obtained smuggled Nvidia chips that were put into service at a Shanghai data center connected to Megaspeed, according to documents reviewed by Bloomberg. Megaspeed has denied the allegations.
