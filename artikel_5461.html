Denmark's Leo Pharma is pushing its IPO plans past 2026 on market volatility

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

CEO Christophe Bourdon said Leo Pharma's owners see market conditions as unsuitable and declined to give a new timeline

Leo Pharma is pushing its planned initial public offering beyond 2026 because of market volatility, CEO Christophe Bourdon said in an interview, according to Bloomberg. The Danish skin-treatment drugmaker had targeted a listing this year.

Bourdon said the company is ready for an IPO but that conditions will keep it from happening in 2026, according to Bloomberg. "The timing of the IPO lies in the hands of the shareholders and both shareholders have assessed that current market conditions do not support a listing," he told Bloomberg.

The LEO Foundation holds 80% of the company, and private equity firm Nordic Capital owns the rest, according to Reuters. Bourdon told Reuters that when a listing happens hinges on how the business performs and how willing investors are to back fresh market debuts.

An IPO is still what the company intends, Bourdon said, but he would not say when it might now happen, according to Bloomberg. He told Reuters an IPO is a "natural and viable option" for the company. He said Leo Pharma is concentrating on steady expansion and on reinforcing its operations so that it can move ahead with a listing once conditions are right.

Other companies have also delayed or scaled back planned listings as equity market volatility grows, according to Reuters.

Leo Pharma on Monday reported nine-month results. Revenue rose 9% to 10.96 billion kroner ($1.65 billion), or 11% at constant exchange rates, the company said. It kept its 2026 outlook for revenue growth of 9% to 11% at constant exchange rates.

Nordic Capital put €450 million ($496 million) into Leo Pharma in 2021 for a minority stake, when the company outlined its intention to get ready for a possible listing over the following four to five years, according to Reuters. Leo Pharma enlisted seven investment banks to assist in running an IPO earlier this year, according to Bloomberg.

Reuters reported that press coverage earlier in November suggested turbulent markets might delay the IPO until 2027, and that Leo Pharma chose not to respond to that speculation when it surfaced.
