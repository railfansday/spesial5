I Found a Real Estate ‘Unicorn’: 2 Homes, 1 Mortgage, and $187K a Year

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Thinking about investing in real estate, but not sure what to buy? Welcome to "My First Deal," where real people spill about how they chose what to invest in, how they found the property, and how that deal is performing. If you have a story to share about your investor journey, email news@realtor.com.

When Steve Kwong ran the numbers on his first traditional investment property, he realized a single broken water heater would wipe out an entire year of profits.

So, the 48-year-old Orlando Realtor® walked away from the deal. Then, he teamed up with a business partner, Rob Leventhal—owner of STR pricing, a revenue management service—and pivoted to short-term rentals.

They searched for three months before buying what Kwong calls a real estate "unicorn": two homes on one lot in Pigeon Forge, TN, with one mortgage. Combined, the two homes grossed $187,000 in 2021—a banner year for most short-term rentals.

Originally, I was going to buy a long-term rental on a golf course in Apopka, FL, and would be renting to a friend. I got under contract, but decided not to close. I ran the numbers and would have made $300 a month, which is one capital expense away from not making any money in a year.

I was talking to my friend Rob, who had friends who wanted to buy STRs. This was 2019. We decided to form a partnership and buy something together. We did our research and found Realtor Avery Carl of The Short Term Shop. Everything online led us to buy in the Smoky Mountains—specifically Pigeon Forge, TN.

We relied on Avery to guide us. She advised us that we didn’t want a one- or two-bedroom property. We were looking to spend around $450,000. We were also relying on the data, which told us which parts of town would be good.

Avery and her team did video tours of other homes we were interested in. This was the only one we saw in person.

It was already performing really well. When we were interested in anything, we asked Avery to get the financials to see what the average nightly rate was and what they spent on cleaning. We liked the location. This is down the road from The Island, which is one of the biggest attractions in Pigeon Forge. It also had a private, wooded lot. What drew us to this one most of all was that we were getting two properties on the same lot for one mortgage—it’s a unicorn.

We figured we could bring in higher numbers than what the previous owners had done because we did a lot of research. We had taken a seminar with Richard Fertig and watched all of his videos about how to provide an exceptional guest experience. The industry was at a turning point back then, with people just starting to realize they could do this remotely. We were confident if we implemented all those strategies we would hit a home run.

We offered around $450,000 and settled on $462,000.

We put 20% down. We could have put down less, but we were told that if we ever had to exit in a bad market, we would be at an advantage to sell if we put more down.

From my real estate commissions, and my business partner saved income from his W-2 job (at the time, he worked for a law firm).

As soon as we listed, we got bookings. In the first three months, we made enough to pay the first year of mortgage payments. The first year, we grossed $87,000 and we thought that was good. Our best year was 2021 when we grossed $187,000. The all-in cost to run these for the year is about $45,000 to $50,000, which includes mortgage and expenses. In 2021, that meant bringing in $137,000. That said, we self-manage, so we are not paying 20% to a vacation property manager.

The $187,000 was a [COVID-19] pandemic year, and Tennessee was one of the first states to open. We knew that number wouldn’t be realistic over time. The average gross per year is closer now to $120,000.

You have to continually educate yourself with what is going on in the industry. You will be left behind if you're not up on the newest trends and technology. There is also so much competition.

We hired a VA to help answer messages. At night, we use a little bit of AI.

These two Airbnbs, the four-bedroom and the one-bedroom, helped us save up to buy other properties. We now have four more, including a pool cabin in Gatlinburg, TN, a property in Blue Ridge, GA, and one in Broken Bow, OK.

There are a lot of things I love. Part of it is sentimental because this was our first one. Also, if you’re going to invest in a property, it’s nice to have an asset that you can use and enjoy. You don’t get that with a long-term rental. I never bought any STRs locally because I wanted vacations. I take my brother and his family every year to this property, and that’s the most fun part.
