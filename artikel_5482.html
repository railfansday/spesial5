Foreign Buyers Pull Back From U.S., but Canadian Snowbirds Widen Their Lead

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Foreign homebuyers retreated from the U.S. housing market this fall, driven by an exodus of European shoppers. This shift helped Canadians cement their dominance.

Between July and September 2026, global house hunters accounted for 1.3% of traffic on Realtor.com®, down from 1.5% a year ago, according to the latest quarterly International Demand report released on Wednesday.

Realtor.com senior economist Jiayi Xu attributes the broader slowdown to a combination of factors, including geopolitical uncertainty, shifting trade policies, and persistent inflation challenges.

Yet against that downturn, Canada's share of demand has expanded, climbing to 35.9% of overall international traffic and reinforcing its hold on the top spot among foreign buyers.

This development marks a turnaround from a year ago, when Canadian interest in U.S. real estate dipped to 32.3% amid President Donald Trump's aggressive tariff policies, which defined the beginning of his second term in office.

Sun-soaked Florida remains the premier destination for Canadian snowbirds escaping freezing Northern winters, commanding nearly 29% of Canada's search traffic.

Meanwhile, Texas edged past California as the second-most popular market for Canadians, drawing 8.8% of traffic compared with the Golden State's 8.6%.

"I’m seeing continued interest from Canadian buyers in Southern California," Daniel Zakharia, an agent with Douglas Elliman Beverly Hills, tells Realtor.com. "While political and economic uncertainty prompted some Canadian homeowners to reconsider their U.S. real estate holdings in 2025, particularly in traditional snowbird markets such as Palm Springs, recent reporting following the November midterm elections suggests that some Canadians are reconsidering their decisions to sell or stay away."

The third-quarter 2026 data also highlights Montana's growing appeal, with the mountain state drawing 2% of Canadian search demand, up from 1.3% in 2019.

By comparison, Montana accounted for just 1.2% of overall international traffic during the same period.

"My experience has been that, typically, the Canadian buyers buy in the northern sections of Montana, as it has greater proximity to the border," Chase Heiland, real estate broker with Keller Williams Montana Realty, tells Realtor.com.

Echoing Xu, Heiland maintains that economic uncertainty surrounding tariffs on imports, elevated consumer prices, and an unfavorable exchange rate for the Canadian dollar are keeping many prospective buyers on sidelines.

However, those who do buy are taking a long-term view.

Heiland recently helped Canadians moving to Bozeman, MT, for a job opportunity to purchase a home in the area.

"We walked through the pros and cons of single-family homes versus townhomes versus condos," he recounts. "They opted for a single-family home to best preserve its value moving forward, as well as being a good long-term investment into Bozeman and Montana as a whole."

Despite cooling international demand nationwide, Heiland notes that he has worked with more overseas buyers this year than at any other point in his career.

"Montana and Bozeman are very much on the map in a way they weren't before," says the agent. "I've had clients not only from Canada this year, but [also] from Russia, Mexico, and Pakistan. They all gravitated toward single-family homes in the city center, with access to everything Bozeman offers and easy access to the area's recreation."

At the metro level, Cape Coral, FL, retained its strong pull among Northern neighbors, with roughly 66% of its international traffic coming from Canada in the third quarter, up 8.9 percentage points from a year ago for the largest annual gain among major markets.

Known as "the Venice of America," Cape Coral boasts over 400 miles of canals, many of them with golf course access, along with dozens of parks and family attractions.

Naples, FL, saw 65.4% of its foreign traffic coming from Canada in the third quarter, followed by Phoenix (62.6%) and North Port, FL (61.4%).

While Canada's share of international demand has grown year over year, Europe's has declined.

Europeans accounted for just under a quarter (24.5%) of all international homebuyers this fall, the lowest level since 2019, according to the quarterly data.

U.K. house hunters made up 6.2% of foreign traffic, followed by Germany at 3.7%, both marking seven-year lows for the third quarter.

"The decline coincides with weaker European travel to the U.S., suggesting that European interest in U.S. homes may be cooling alongside interest in visiting," says Xu.

However, Zakharia points out that international buyer behavior is not uniform, and that it is important to distinguish between discretionary second-home purchases and those driven by professional or business opportunities.

"I’m currently in discussions with a prospective buyer from the United Kingdom who is an actress considering relocating to Los Angeles and acquiring property because she sees greater opportunities for career advancement within the entertainment industry," says the agent. "This illustrates how international buyer demand can vary considerably depending on a buyer’s profession, industry, and long-term objectives.

Among the Europeans still looking to scoop up properties stateside, a growing share focused on New York and Texas.

In the third quarter, New York drew 8.1% of European traffic, up from 6% in 2019, while Texas attracted 8%, up from 7.1%.

Smaller states also gained ground among Europeans, with Montana's share nearly doubling, from 0.5% in 2019 to 0.9% this year, and Tennessee’s rose from 1.7% to 2.0%.

"International real estate demand is not simply a function of economics or politics," adds Zakharia. "It is also a function of where individuals see the greatest opportunities to build their careers, businesses, and lifestyles."

The third-quarter traffic slowdown aligns with the National Association of Realtors® 2025 International Transactions in U.S. Residential Real Estate report, which found that international buyers purchased 14% fewer homes in U.S. compared to the prior year.

"The decline in foreign homebuyer activity mirrors the decline in international visitors and tourists to the United States," said NAR Chief Economist Lawrence Yun. "Even a slightly weaker U.S. dollar over the past year, which provides more purchasing power for foreigners, did not induce more activity."

In the NAR data covering a period from April 2025 to March 2026, Canadians led foreign buyers, accounting for 16% of international transactions.
