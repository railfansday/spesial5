Harry Kane spot on to seal England win over Croatia on his record-breaking night

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

It was an historic occasion for Harry Kane, the one when he made his 126th England appearance and overtook Peter Shilton as the nation’s most capped men’s player. And for much of the night, it felt as though the desire to celebrate him, certainly beforehand, was the main thing. The actual football in this Nations League group game felt like a sideshow.

England had demolished Croatia 7-0 in Rijeka last month; it was Croatia’s heaviest defeat. It was a different story here. England lacked cut and thrust. There was a flatness to the Wembley atmosphere. Croatia were never likely to be humbled again and they sensed redemption when Petar Sucic capitalised upon a defensive aberration from Jarell Quansah to equalise for 1-1 just before the hour.

It was at that point that Kane got a hold of the script. It was supposed to be his night during the 90 minutes, wasn’t it? There was a tussle on an England corner inside the Croatia area, Quansah feeling Ivan Smolcic tug at his shirt and when he went down, it felt as though the video assistant would be interested. He was, deciding that it added up to a penalty – and not that Quansah went to ground too easily.

It was over to Kane from the spot. There was the familiar stutter and the conversion was clipped up the middle, narrowly avoiding the trailing leg of the goalkeeper, Dominik Livakovic. It was Kane’s 92nd England goal; his 27th successful penalty.

Croatia fought until the end and they had a clutch of late chances, none better than when Nikola Vlasic took a touch and shot just wide in the 82nd minute. England were good value for the result. They are guaranteed a place in the quarter-finals of this competition in March and will go to Madrid to play Spain on Sunday with a shot at winning the group. They would have to beat the world champions to do that. And, really, play better than this.

Kane had walked out for kick-off with his four children and there was a lot for him – and them – to take in before a ball was kicked. First of all, there was the giant tifo behind one of the goals, which acclaimed Kane as “England’s finest”. Then, after he had hoisted aloft his Ballon d’Or trophy, there were presentations from Shilton, Luka Modric and Prince William. The gifts they bore, respectively, were a small trinket, a replica of the first football brought to Croatia in 1880 and a commemorative, framed Kane shirt.

It was quickfire. Rarely has so much been handed out so swiftly. Somewhat inevitably, there was a hangover when the game got under way. Was the show not already over? The Wembley crowded slumbered. Thomas Tuchel always wants intensity at the outset. It was not there.

Kane was involved in the breakthrough goal midway through the first-half, floating a lovely diagonal out to Jude Bellingham on the right-hand side of the Croatia area. Bellingham volleyed over first-time and what followed was a personal disaster for Josko Gvardiol. He got his feet into a tangle and allowed the ball to get to Morgan Rogers, who reached back and slashed it down and in from close-range.

Tuchel’s most eye-catching selection was to start Trent Alexander-Arnold over Ben White at right-back and the Real Madrid player got into advanced positions. He played some good balls. He wanted to quicken the collective pulse. As did Bellingham with those surges. It was all rather room temperature in the first half; England in control but not an awful lot happening.

Kane dropped deep. He wanted to spark England’s moves. He even took throw-ins. Rogers, who Tuchel played on the right wing, had the license to get into central attacking areas. Alexander-Arnold pushed high; sometimes as far as the centre-forward position.

Kane went close at the start of the second-half, seeing a close-range shot blocked and Bellingham did not get hold of a drive after Anthony Gordon had tried to buy a penalty with an obvious dive.

England were sleepwalking. It felt as though complacency set in. Elliot Anderson played a couple of loose passes. Lewis Hall needed to win a one-versus-one against Igor Matanovic, conceding a corner. From it, England got their wake-up call. Gvardiol challenged for the high ball and when it broke, Quansah tried to head back for Jordan Pickford; a high-risk move. He did not see Sucic, who stretched out a leg to touch home.

Enter Kane. The closing stages were notable for the booing of White when he came on to replace Alexander-Arnold and Croatia threatening a second equaliser. Quansah redeemed himself with a saving tackle on the substitute, Ante Budimir. At the other end, James Garner, on as a substitute, had the ball in the net only for Bellingham to be flagged offside. For England, it is on to the showdown with Spain.
