Home Assistant Connect AUX-2: ESP32-S3R8 PoE & RS-232 Hub

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Posted on Nov 22 • Originally published at embedsbc.com

Nabu Casa's Home Assistant Connect AUX-2 is an ESP32-S3R8 edge bridge that brings legacy Hi-Fi gear, RS-232 serial devices, and Bluetooth peripherals onto an Ethernet/PoE network. It cuts down rack-side cabling, though audio caps out at 16-bit/48kHz with mutually exclusive analog and optical outputs.

Integrating older AV receivers, projectors, UPS units, and traditional Hi-Fi amplifiers into a modern automation stack usually hits the same snag: no native Ethernet, forcing engineers to deal with local RS-232/RS-485 runs and analog audio cabling. Nabu Casa packs PoE networking, DAC conversion, dual serial ports, and wireless proxying onto a single microcontroller board. Here is the hardware breakdown:

Home Assistant Connect AUX-2 pairs a W5500 Ethernet controller with IEEE 802.3af PoE to deliver wired network reliability and power over a single Ethernet drop.

Inside metal AV racks, structured wiring panels, or ceiling projector mounts, RF shielding and electromagnetic noise routinely cause 2.4GHz Wi-Fi packet loss, while pulling dedicated AC power adds installation overhead. Offloading wired networking to a discrete W5500 Ethernet controller takes the control link off congested wireless bands. Where a PoE switch isn't available, the unit falls back to a 5V/2A USB-C power input alongside either wired Ethernet or 2.4GHz Wi-Fi.

Thermally, a typical power draw under 1W inside a 106 × 63 × 25mm, ~104g polycarbonate enclosure keeps heat dissipation negligible. It runs passively in enclosed, fanless cabinets without thermal throttling concerns or acoustic fan noise.

Compared to standard Wi-Fi-only serial or audio bridges, Home Assistant Connect AUX-2 eliminates RF attenuation inside metal racks and removes the need for local wall adapters, at the cost of requiring a PoE switch (or separate USB-C supply) and sourcing all interconnecting cables separately.

Home Assistant Connect AUX-2 combines a Texas Instruments PCM5122 DAC (112dB SNR) with a shared 3.5mm jack to output up to 16-bit/48kHz stereo audio, staying in sync with compatible smart speakers via Music Assistant and Sendspin.

MCU-based audio streamers often suffer from dropouts or clicks when network jitter outpaces limited on-chip SRAM. Using the ESP32-S3R8 with 8MB PSRAM provides buffering headroom for FLAC, MP3, and WAV streams, while handing conversion off to the external PCM5122 avoids the high noise floor typical of integrated MCU DACs.

The physical interface introduces a strict multiplexing constraint: the single 3.5mm jack handles both analog stereo and optical S/PDIF, but only one output mode is active at a time. Simultaneous analog and digital output is not supported.

Compared to full Linux SBC streamers (such as a Raspberry Pi paired with a DAC HAT), Home Assistant Connect AUX-2 lowers power consumption, boot latency, and OS maintenance overhead, but locks audio output to 16-bit/48kHz—ruling out native high-resolution master playback.

Home Assistant Connect AUX-2 consolidates low-speed local control buses and short-range radios at the network edge through dual pin-configurable 3.5mm RS-232 ports, a USB-A expansion port, and a Bluetooth 5 LE proxy.

Anyone wiring commercial projectors, AV receivers, TVs, or UPS units knows RS-232 pinouts vary wildly between straight-through and null-modem conventions. Having software-configurable TX/RX pin assignments on both 3.5mm RS-232 ports cuts down on custom harness crimping and field-side crossover debugging.

For longer runs or alternative protocols, the onboard USB-A port accepts the Home Assistant Connect ZWA-2 Z-Wave adapter as well as USB-to-RS-485 converters. This architecture lets you decouple Z-Wave radios or RS-485 buses from the physical location of the main Home Assistant server and place them wherever coverage or wiring dictates over Ethernet. On the wireless side, the Bluetooth 5 LE proxy bridges nearby BLE sensors to the network, though single-MCU radio scheduling and memory limits cap it at 3 active connections.

Compared to deploying separate serial-to-Ethernet servers, BLE gateways, and USB extenders, Home Assistant Connect AUX-2 reduces hardware count and power drops at the edge, but introduces a hard ceiling of 3 active Bluetooth proxy connections that won't scale to dense BLE clusters.

Home Assistant Connect AUX-2 ships with ESPHome-based firmware for direct Home Assistant integration, while leaving an unlocked bootloader and a Grove connector (I²C + GPIO) open for custom firmware and peripheral expansion.

For integrators who want to avoid writing low-level drivers from scratch, the ESPHome stack streamlines device configuration as long as the host runs Home Assistant 2026.9 or later. For embedded engineers who need direct timing control, custom protocol handling, or standalone operation outside the default stack, the unlocked bootloader permits flashing custom firmware without fighting signature locks. Physically, the Grove port breaks out I²C and GPIO lines for solderless sensor or I/O hookups.

Hard Limits (Look Elsewhere If You Need):

Q: Can Home Assistant Connect AUX-2 output analog stereo and optical S/PDIF simultaneously?

A: No. Both share a single 3.5mm jack and only one mode can be active at a time, capped at 16-bit/48kHz stereo.

Q: What are the power and host software prerequisites for deploying Connect AUX-2?

A: It runs on IEEE 802.3af PoE or 5V/2A USB-C power, and requires Home Assistant 2026.9 or later.

Q: What is the Bluetooth proxy connection limit, and can the device run custom firmware?

A: The Bluetooth 5 LE proxy supports up to 3 active connections. The bootloader is unlocked, allowing custom firmware flashing.
