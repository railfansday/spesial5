Is Nu Holdings Stock a Buy, Sell, or Hold After Falling 24% in 2026?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Brazil's election rattled Nu Holdings stock. Is the U.S. launch enough reason to buy the dip?

A flight from Brazilian assets has pulled Nu Holdings (NU -4.08%) down 24% in 2026. The third-quarter report did little to settle the debate.

The Brazilian neobank's shares closed 2025 at $16.74 and wrapped up Nov. 16 at $12.68. The path was jagged. Nu's stock jumped 13% on Oct. 5, when Flávio Bolsonaro took a surprise lead in Brazil's presidential race. It then fell 17% on Oct. 26 after Lula won reelection, notching one of the stock's worst days on record.

That was a retreat for an entire country's stock market, and Nu got swept along. So I bought my first shares on Oct. 26.

Spicy timing, I know. But I expect the Nubank parent to serve my portfolio well in the long run, regardless of the political winds in Brazil.

Nu's Q3 report landed on Nov. 12 and it was a mixed bag. That's my favorite kind of report, because the market often overreacts to both halves.

Sales rose 37% year over year, beating consensus estimates by 6%. Earnings per share jumped 40% but missed analyst targets by 3%. Headlines mentioned slowing loan growth and rising late-stage delinquencies, and yes, I read those complaints twice. Meanwhile, I noticed that the company added 3.7 million customers during the quarter, customers' credit quality improved, and profit margins widened.

The stock jumped 12% the next day, then gave back 4% on Monday. Hold on to your Copacabana straw hats!

At a market cap of $61.4 billion, Nu trades at 15.9 times trailing earnings and 11.6 times next year's estimates. That's pricey compared to local fintech rivals XP (XP -4.26%), PagSeguro Digital (PAGS +0.13%), and StoneCo (STNE +1.14%), whose earnings-based multiples are in the mid-single digits. It's also affordable from a historical perspective; Nu traded at triple-digit P/E multiples at the end of 2023.

So why did I still take the plunge in October? Because Nu is a cash machine, and I think it's only getting started.

Two developments matter for the long-term case. First, Nu launched in the United States on Sept. 10 through partner Lead Bank, offering a 3.5% yield on deposits and a credit card with 1.5% unlimited cashback. The company holds conditional approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency for a national bank charter, granted in January. American Banker reported that the service reached all 50 states within its first two months. Robert Livingston, formerly the CFO of Visa's (V +0.16%) North American business, became Nu's CFO in July. The company is setting up a serious shop in the world's largest economy.

Second, Brazil's central bank approved Nu's acquisition of Banco Porto Real on Nov. 13, securing a banking license in its home market. According to management, the license adds no extra capital or liquidity requirements. Nu's first investor day follows on Dec. 8 -- in New York, of course. There's an American expansion to support, you know.

I'll check in on the Dec. 8 webcast to see how management discusses U.S. economic conditions and Brazilian credit quality. Those things could surprise me. And the risk list is real: another Lula term, shaky late-stage credit, and a U.S. business that's only a few weeks old.

But at 11.6 times forward earnings, I'm a Nu buyer who's watching for global growth. This Brazilian digi-bank is literally going places.
