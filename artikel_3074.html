Daqo: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Daqo is betting its future on two fronts: stabilizing polysilicon margins while building a new revenue stream in AI data center power.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 27, 2026, Daqo (DQ +6.48%) announced a substantial increase in quarterly sales volume following a shift to market-oriented pricing. The strategic pivot helped narrow operating losses while the company maintained a debt-free balance sheet with $2.06 billion in liquid assets. For long-term investors, navigating severe solar sector headwinds requires balancing low-cost manufacturing efficiency with disciplined capital allocation.

Three takeaways from the call stand out for long-term investors.

Daqo abandoned its previous wait-and-see posture in June, choosing instead to align pricing with prevailing market dynamics. This shift helped third-quarter sales volume rebound to 46.9 thousand metric tons from 15.2 thousand metric tons in the prior quarter, narrowing operating losses despite soft solar industry conditions.

Elevated solar supply chain inventory of roughly 700,000 metric tons continues to weigh on selling prices across the market. In response, Daqo plans to lower its fourth-quarter production volume to between 35,000 metric tons to 45,000 metric tons, aligning plant output with actual customer demand to support broader market recovery.

To hedge against volatile solar market cycles, Daqo is expanding into power equipment for artificial intelligence data centers (AIDC), such as solid-state transformers and energy storage systems. Partnering with an affiliated parent company that brings four decades of manufacturing expertise, the company expects initial revenues from this segment in 2027.

Solar market demand is stabilizing as price floors take effect, and Daqo maintains strategic flexibility with $2.06 billion in cash assets and zero debt. Low production costs across its two facilities support long-term durability during cyclical industry troughs.

Management is evaluating potential stock repurchases while observing regulatory enforcement and peer output cuts. Operational focus remains on digital automation to optimize unit costs.
