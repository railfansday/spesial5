Ferrovial: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The company's toll road concessions generate steady cash while its construction margins expand, creating a rare dual-engine growth model.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 22, 2026 at 3:45PM EST

On its third-quarter of 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 28, 2026, Ferrovial (FER -2.86%) announced revenue growth across its highways and construction divisions, backed by major express lane awards in North America. Long-term investors should pay attention because the infrastructure manager is securing multi-decade toll road concessions that generate cash flow while expanding its high-margin construction backlog.

Three takeaways from management's call stand out for long-term shareholders.

The company's construction division expanded its backlog to an all-time high while improving profitability. Earnings before interest and taxes (EBIT)--a metric showing operating profitability before debt costs and taxes--reached a 4.1% margin in the first nine months of 2026, up from 3.7% in the prior-year period, driven by execution across North American and European contracts.

Management secured major express lane awards in Atlanta and Nashville, expanding its North American toll road network. Rather than relying on future population growth, these projects target highway corridors where heavy daily congestion creates immediate driver demand for reliable travel times.

Even as it pursues large public-private partnership (P3) infrastructure projects, management maintains strict financial hurdles. The company targets an equity internal rate of return (IRR)--an annualized metric measuring project profitability--in the double digits to compensate for development risks.

Cash flow from operating toll roads and construction projects keeps the balance sheet flexible. Ferrovial maintains competitive advantages through proprietary traffic data, local construction platforms, and dynamic toll pricing. Management focuses on execution certainty and pricing discipline across core North American markets.

With recent concession wins in Georgia and Tennessee, the company continues to pursue major infrastructure opportunities in high-growth U.S. transit corridors.
