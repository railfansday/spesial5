Celsius vs. e.l.f. Beauty: Which Consumer Goods Stock Is the Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Both of these consumer brands have taken market share from legacy rivals, but one looks easier to hold for the next decade.

Investors looking for growth often turn to the consumer sector to find brands with staying power. Comparing Celsius (CELH +1.15%) and e.l.f. Beauty (ELF +4.93%) reveals two very different paths to market dominance.

Celsius provides functional energy drinks aimed at health-conscious consumers, while e.l.f. Beauty offers prestige-quality cosmetics at mass-market prices. Both companies have disrupted their respective markets by using savvy digital marketing and strategic retail partnerships to gain significant market share from established legacy competitors.

Celsius provides functional energy drinks to a global audience, focusing on fitness-minded consumers who want sugar-free options. The company sells its products through leading retailers and e-commerce platforms like Amazon and Walmart. The energy drink portfolio, which includes brands like Alani Nu and Rockstar Energy, currently holds the number three spot in the United States energy drink market.

Celsius continues to benefit from its distribution partnership with Pepsi, which accounted for nearly half of net revenue in the most recent fiscal year. On a trailing-12-month (TTM) basis through the September quarter, revenue reached nearly $3.2 billion, representing an increase of 49% year over year.

This growth pushed net income to roughly $230 million during that period. The net margin, which represents the percentage of revenue remaining after all expenses are paid, was 10%. The growth in sales and net income suggests the brand is capturing significant consumer interest among beverage stocks.

Its September 2026 balance sheet showed a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.2x, suggesting the company uses very little debt relative to its equity. The current ratio was roughly 2.0x, which indicates the company's ability to meet short-term obligations with assets that can be converted to cash within a year.

TTM free cash flow was approximately $269 million, the cash a company has left over after paying for its operating costs and equipment investments.

e.l.f. Beauty offers a multi-brand portfolio including e.l.f. Cosmetics and Naturium, focusing on high-quality beauty products at accessible prices. The company reaches customers through its own website and major retail partners such as Target, Walmart, and Amazon. By leveraging social media platforms like TikTok Shop, the business maintains high visibility among younger consumers and consistently gains shelf space in specialty retailers.

Through the September quarter, TTM revenue reached approximately $1.9 billion, representing nearly 40% year-over-year growth. The company reported net income of roughly $87 million. That left a net margin of approximately 4.5% -- an improvement over the year-ago period's 1.6% but still down from the previous margin of 12.5% in fiscal 2024 ending in March.

Its September 2026 balance sheet shows a debt-to-equity ratio of nearly 0.7x. This ratio measures how much debt a company uses to finance its assets relative to the value of shareholder equity. Its current ratio was approximately 2.6x, indicating a strong position to cover short-term liabilities.

The company generated roughly $276 million in free cash flow during the period.

Celsius faces substantial risks from its heavy reliance on a single distribution partner for its core products. The company also faces intense competition from established players like Monster Beverage. Additionally, integrating newly acquired brands like Alani Nu poses challenges related to brand overlap and potential cultural friction. Fluctuations in the costs of aluminum cans and raw ingredients could also impact future profitability.

e.l.f. Beauty operates in a crowded market where it must compete with legacy giants like Procter & Gamble and Coty. The company is highly dependent on manufacturing in China, which makes it vulnerable to shifts in trade policy or tariffs. Furthermore, the recent acquisition of Rhode carries risks related to integrating the business and retaining key personnel.

Celsius appears to be the more attractively valued option for investors right now because it trades at lower multiples than its peer.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

The Forward P/E ratio measures the current stock price relative to future earnings estimates for the next year. Meanwhile, the P/S ratio measures the market value of a company relative to its sales over the past twelve months. Both metrics suggest that investors are currently paying a higher premium for the beauty brand's growth profile than for the energy drink maker's.

It's more difficult to gauge which cosmetics brands will remain durable and popular with consumers over the next 10 years. In contrast, beverage consumption among leading brands tends to be more predictable and stable over time. This is why I would rather buy Celsius, especially given that the stock is trading well off its previous highs.

Celsius shares are down about 28% over the past year and trading at about 23 times forward earnings estimates. This is attractive as its energy drink portfolio continues to hold a top market share spot among leading competitors. A stronger pipeline of new products releasing in 2027 provides a growth catalyst that could drive solid revenue and earnings growth.

Long term, analysts expect earnings to grow at an annualized rate of about 16%, which could support market-beating returns.
