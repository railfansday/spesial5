YouTube bets on creator-led shows with a new slate

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Streaming services and social platforms are giving internet creators more places to make their next hit show. Disney+ is adding videos from TikTok creators, Netflix is expanding its lineup of creator-led podcasts, and Instagram is testing videos up to 90 minutes long.

But when it comes to what Americans watch on TV, YouTube is still king. In August, it accounted for a record 15.8 percent of U.S. TV watch time, more than twice Netflix’s 7.7 percent and the biggest share of any company in the rankings.

This week, YouTube gave us a look at what some of its biggest creators are making, from shows that are already live to those coming in the months ahead. Here's what’s on the slate.

Emma Chamberlain — What Are You Waiting For? Emma Chamberlain hates waiting in line, which makes her an especially good person to ask New Yorkers why they do it. The 2026 Mashable 101 honoree visits some of the city's busiest lines and talks to the people in them about what’s worth giving up part of their day for. The line is just the starting point for a closer look at the fandoms and subcultures that bring people together in person. The series, produced by Amplify Pictures, premieres on Chamberlain's channel in early 2027.

Grace Reiter — Twilight (but better). After sending up Panem, Mashable 101 creator Grace Reiter is headed to Forks. The Saturday Night Live cast member and 2026 Mashable 101 honoree is directing and improvising her way through a Twilight parody, her second unscripted feature after The Hunger Games (but better), which drew more than 18 million views. The new one premieres Feb. 18 on Reiter's channel.

Aryna Sabalenka — Aryna’s Arena: Unfiltered. What does wedding planning look like when most of the year is spent living out of a suitcase? Sabalenka's new docuseries follows the tennis champion from the tennis tour to dress fittings and brand meetings, including a project to design her own $250,000 diamond necklace. The show expands on her existing YouTube series, Aryna’s Arena, and premieres on her channel in spring 2027.

Wesley Wang — Response, Extract 14, and Absolute Cinema. If your group chat is always trying to solve the murder before the big reveal, Extract 14 will give you all something to work with. The six-episode mystery hides clues in found footage and offers $100,000 to the first viewer who cracks the case. Wang also has Response, a sci-fi horror short about park rangers who find something unsettling during a welfare check, and Absolute Cinema, which brings 10 aspiring filmmakers to New York for a weeklong boot camp. Viewers get the final say on who wins $100,000 to make their movie. Response is due in early 2027, with the other two projects following in the spring.

Nick DiGiovanni — Chef. Nick DiGiovanni went from MasterChef finalist to YouTube food star, building an audience with step-by-step recipes and stunts like making the world’s largest sushi roll. Now he's turning the camera on other chefs. His new channel, Chef, takes viewers inside Michelin-starred restaurants and street-food kitchens, with full-length episodes about the people behind the food and daily recipe Shorts filmed from the cook’s POV. The first two videos are already live: a visit to a pizza restaurant and a quick lesson in making butter chicken shawarma.

Morgan Jay — Music Therapy with Morgan Jay. Comedian, musician, and 2025 Mashable 101 cover star Morgan Jay is bringing his musical improv to the therapist's couch. In each episode, he plays therapist to a celebrity guest, and whatever comes up in their unscripted conversation becomes material for an original song they write and perform together. The first season’s guests include Mara Wilson, Yung Gravy, Ron Funches, and Josh Johnson. The series premiered Nov. 18 on Jay's YouTube channel.

The slate also includes Lilly Singh's Eligible Aunties, a dating series about older single women, and Track Star Tonight, a live music series from the team behind Jack Coyne's street quiz. Scott Evans heads to Lagos, Nigeria, for the Jan. 8 premiere of House Guest Vacay. Before then, Happy Hour with Ina Garten has a Thanksgiving special on Nov. 25, and Sam Eckholm goes behind the scenes with the Royal Air Force's Red Arrows on the Nov. 27 episode of Access Granted. New episodes of the docuseries Created with Jon Youshaei are also due this winter. That's a lot of new, original content!

YouTube has been working on two things a recurring show needs.

The first is a way for viewers to find the next episode. Creators can organize longer videos into seasons and episodes, and in September, YouTube announced a similar feature for Shorts, allowing viewers to watch them in order.

The second is a way to find sponsors. In March, YouTube introduced Creator Partnerships, which connects creators in YouTube Studio with marketers using Google’s ad tools. At the showcase, YouTube pointed to NYX's takeover of Mashable 101 star Brittany Broski's Royal Court and Expedia's partnership with another Mashable 101 creator, iShowSpeed.

One of the clearest examples is a show that aired for eight seasons on Netflix. Phil Rosenthal's Somebody Feed Phil will premiere its ninth season on his own YouTube channel this spring. United Airlines is the title sponsor and plans to carry the series on its in-flight screens, giving the show yet another place to reach viewers.
