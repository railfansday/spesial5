US intelligence agencies reportedly brief Congress about Alice Huang

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

U.S. intelligence agencies reportedly briefed Congressional staffers about Alice Huang, the cloud executive at the center of the Jensen Huang controversy.

The classified discussion took place on Tuesday, sources told the New York Times and the Wall Street Journal today. It was attended by staff members of the House and Senate intelligence committees. The briefing is said to have included representatives of the FBI, CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence.

Alice Huang is a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singaporean cloud provider that has reportedly bought billions of dollars worth of Nvidia Corp. chips. The company is under investigation for allegedly breaching U.S. export controls. Those controls prohibit companies from selling cutting-edge artificial intelligence chips to customers in China.

On Oct. 29, the Times revealed that Nvidia Chief Executive Jensen Huang had an affair with Alice Huang. The paper stated that she had been “found to be passing intelligence” to China. A few days later, other outlets reported that U.S. officials had not yet concluded that Alice Huang is a spy. The U.S. Justice Department reaffirmed that’s the case shortly thereafter.

According to the Times, this week’s classified Congressional briefing included a summary of the information that U.S. intelligence agencies have collected about Alice Huang. Those agencies’ representatives reportedly “did not dispute” that she had gathered information for China. However, they stated that there’s no evidence China’s MSS spy agency directed her to initiate the affair.

Some U.S. officials reportedly believe Alice Huang can still be considered a spy. Others are said to see her ties to the country’s intelligence service as ”more circumstantial.” According to the Journal, officials don’t believe that Alice Huang passed any “intelligence of significance” to China.

The briefers reportedly told Congressional staffers that Kang Wei, a businessman believed to be Huang’s husband, is a “more significant” figure. They stated that Kang is a Chinese intelligence asset who was “often” in touch with the country’s spy agency.

On Nov. 12, the Journal reported that Kang had known Jensen Huang for over a decade. The publication later revealed that he had built a data center network in China.

The briefing reportedly did not cover the most sensitive information the U.S. intelligence community has gathered about Alice Huang. Additionally, it didn’t include a classified memo about her relationship with Jensen Huang that was drafted under the supervision of U.S. State Secretary Marco Rubio. Congressional staffers requested a copy of the memo.

Jensen Huang is set to appear before three House committees on Dec. 1. Prior to the spy controversy, he was reportedly scheduled to make a product announcement that day. The closed-door briefing will be followed by a public hearing before the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 3.
