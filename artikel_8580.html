To lower this year’s taxes, extra pretax 401(k) contributions, charitable gifts, and sales of losing investments all have to happen by December 31. IRA and HSA money can go in until the April filing deadline. Go through your list now, because a 401(k) change has to take effect before your last paycheck of the year.

By April, your return mostly records what you did before the year ended.

These count in the year you make them, so finish them by December 31:

If you’ll owe tax in April, check your withholding and raise it for December. Withholding counts as paid evenly all year, so it can erase an underpayment penalty for earlier months that an estimated payment due January 15 can’t.

Time your income, too. If you expect a lower tax bracket next year, ask whether a bonus can be paid in January and hold a sale until then, or do the reverse if you expect a higher one.

Take a couple filing jointly in the 22% bracket who itemize and sold shares this year at a $4,000 long-term gain, taxed at 15%. Adding $2,000 to a pretax 401(k) from each of their last three paychecks saves $1,320. Harvesting a $4,000 loss erases the $600 tax on that gain. Giving their church $5,000 of shares that cost $1,000 years ago, rather than selling them to give cash, avoids another $600 of capital gains tax. That’s $2,520 off this year’s federal income tax.

After New Year’s, a deductible IRA or HSA contribution made by the April filing deadline, and marked for the year just ended, still cuts that year’s tax.
