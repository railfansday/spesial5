15 Jobs With Student Loan Forgiveness or Repayment Help – Pick One for the Work

Jobs in public service, teaching, health care in shortage areas, government law offices, the military, and national service can come with help repaying your student loans. Choose the job for the work, though, and count the loan help as a bonus. Most programs require years of service, and some depend on yearly federal funding.

A job title alone rarely qualifies you for this help. Most programs also look at who employs you or where you work, so the same nurse can get help at a nonprofit hospital in a shortage area and nothing from these programs at a for-profit clinic in a well-staffed suburb.

The help comes in two forms. Forgiveness programs cancel some or all of what you still owe on federal loans once you’ve put in the years. Repayment programs pay toward your loans while you work, usually under a contract to serve two or three years.

Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF) reaches the most jobs, since it looks at who you work for rather than what you do. It forgives what’s left on your Direct Loans, the only federal student loans made today, after 120 qualifying monthly payments. Those take at least 10 years, and each must be made while you work full time for an eligible employer.

Most of the health care programs below go to clinicians who work where providers are scarce. The National Health Service Corps (NHSC) and NURSE Corps take applications once a year and require a job in or serving a Health Professional Shortage Area. That’s a place, population, or facility the federal government has designated as short of primary care, dental, or mental health providers.

Take a nurse with $40,000 in nursing school loans who works full time for three years at a hospital in a rural shortage area. NURSE Corps would repay $24,000 over the first two years and $10,000 for the third, 85% of the balance. NHSC awards are tax-free, but NURSE Corps payments are subject to federal income and payroll taxes, so set money aside for the tax bill.

Only take a job you’d want without the help. Most programs above ask for two or three years of service, PSLF takes at least 10, and several depend on funding that can shrink or stop from one year to the next. Leaving early can cost you the rest of the help, and some programs make you repay what they’ve already paid.

Before you accept an offer, ask HR which of these programs the job qualifies for, and check state repayment programs and the employer’s own student loan benefit too. Then compare the offers’ total pay without counting any of that money.
