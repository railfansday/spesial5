The Lucid Gravity robotaxi is one step closer to a commercial launch.

Nearly a year after showcasing their “luxurious” robotaxi at CES, Uber, Nuro and Lucid have started fully driverless testing on public roads in the San Francisco Bay Area, the companies said Tuesday.

The partnership, announced in July 2025, combines Uber’s ride-hailing platform, Nuro’s self-driving technology and Lucid’s electric vehicles. Autonomous rides take place aboard the Lucid Gravity robotaxi, which I had the opportunity to see first at a CES showcase in January (in the form of a “production intent design”), and then on the road this summer, with a safety driver behind the wheel.

Now, the partnership is ready to take its next big step, beginning with employee rides “soon,” the companies said. They plan to launch commercial robotaxi operations in the first quarter of 2027 in the San Francisco Bay Area, before expanding to Houston later next year. Lucid will begin manufacturing the first vehicles designed specifically for the commercial launch fleet “in the coming weeks.” Uber plans to deploy around 35,000 Lucid vehicles equipped with the Nuro Driver in the coming years in “dozens” of cities.

“Removing the safety operator from behind the wheel is an important milestone in our partnership with Nuro and Lucid, and a testament to all three partners’ focus on executing against our shared roadmap,” Samarth Kejriwal, Uber’s global head of autonomous mobility, said in a statement. “As we prepare for our first driverless passenger rides in the coming months, we’ll be working closely together to refine our operations, infrastructure and the in-cabin experience to ensure a safer, more comfortable ride.”

Nuro says it’s been conducting “round-the-clock autonomous testing of the Lucid Gravity robotaxi on public roads in the San Francisco Bay Area and Houston” since December 2025, with a safety driver in the vehicle who can take over if needed.

In May, Nuro received a California Public Utilities Commission Drivered Pilot Permit, giving the company the green light to conduct pilot testing for an autonomous passenger service with a safety driver. Nuro also received a California Department of Motor Vehicles Driverless Testing Permit in April, allowing it to test self-driving vehicles without a safety driver.

Uber’s partnership with Nuro and Lucid is just one of dozens it has with other AV companies, including Waymo, Zoox, Avride, Wayve and May Mobility. Adding driverless options to an app that’s likely already on someone’s phone could increase their chances of giving the experience a try — despite the trepidation many people have about self-driving vehicles.

The Lucid Gravity robotaxi can seat up to six passengers, unlike rides from Waymo and Zoox that seat up to four. It has plenty of legroom and ample space for luggage in the back. The vehicle’s sensor suite is equipped with cameras, lidar sensors and radars for autonomous navigation.

It’s been a big year for autonomous vehicles, and it appears 2027 will be no different. The rapid expansion of AVs comes amid heightened scrutiny. Last week, the California DMV banned Waymo’s robotaxis from state freeways after a series of high-profile incidents — after which Waymo paused all of its freeway rides. Waymo, the largest player in the AV space, has been involved in a number of other safety incidents as it expands to more cities. Over the years, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration has launched investigations into other AV companies, including Zoox and Tesla.

It’s against this landscape — and the full-speed-ahead race toward autonomy — that Nuro, Uber and Lucid will soon publicly debut their own robotaxi.
