Why Sunrun Stock Surged 33.4% This Week

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Investors warmed up to this solar stock after the company reported strong financial results.

A third-quarter earnings report featuring $1.1 billion in revenue sent Sunrun (RUN +1.11%) soaring 33.4% this week.

Sunrun stock changed hands at $9.98 as of the close on Friday, Nov. 6, up 33.4% from Friday, Oct. 30's close, according to data provided by S&P Global Market Intelligence. Across those five sessions, the S&P 500 (^GSPC +0.31%) fell 2.7%, and the Nasdaq-100 fell 4.9%.

On Wednesday, Sunrun reported third-quarter revenue of $1.1 billion, a 51.7% year-over-year jump that topped Wall Street estimates. Diluted earnings per share reached $0.56, beating the consensus expectation of $0.32 per share. That top-line surge came even as installed solar capacity dropped 15% year over year, because Sunrun completed a strategic shift toward direct-channel volume while scaling higher-margin battery storage attachments.

Management trimmed its full-year cash generation outlook to roughly $200 million, the bottom of its previous $200 million to $375 million target range. Even with that reduced guidance, direct sales volume grew 9% year over year as battery attachment rates hit a record 75%. This week's quarterly beat proves Sunrun can expand system margins through battery storage, but trimming guidance to the bottom of its range keeps cash generation on a tight leash.

Even after this week's rally, Sunrun remains down 55% from its 52-week high of $21.95, reflecting long-term investor anxiety over rising interest rates and complex project financing.

Sunrun trades at a trailing price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 5, though that low multiple is heavily influenced by non-cash accounting adjustments and derivative gains. The company carried $15.3 billion in total debt, including lease obligations, as of Sept. 30, 2026, against $699.4 million in cash and short-term investments as of Sept. 30, 2026. Investors will watch the next quarterly earnings report scheduled for Feb. 25, 2027, to see if direct installation growth can offset higher funding costs.

Although Sunrun reported a strong quarter, investors must be cautious about warming up to Sunrun stock. While it shone brightly last quarter, reporting profits that exceeded analysts' expectations, the company is hardly in sound financial health.

Sunrun relies heavily on leverage to keep the lights on. As of the end of September 2026, for example, Sunrun had a net debt-to-earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) ratio of approximately 11.6. Taking this into account, along with the fact that the company has an interest coverage ratio of about 0.44, according to Morningstar, it's clear that Sunrun is best suited for those comfortable with a more speculative investment. For those who have lower risk tolerances, there are plenty of other solar energy stocks to consider.
