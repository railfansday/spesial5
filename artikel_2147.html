Two EFL club takeovers collapse at final hour

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Both AFC Wimbledon's and Bromley's proposed investment plans have hit a roadblock at the eleventh hour.

The League One duo were expected to struggle in the third tier this year. Bromley are in the division for the first time in their history, having only just been promoted into the EFL for the first time three years ago, while AFC Wimbledon are proudly fan-owned.

Ultimately, with the finances in football only increasing, plus some of the juggernauts in the division this year, such as Leicester City, Huddersfield Town and Sheffield Wednesday, who can afford to invest in the squad and the infrastructure at the club, their backs were always going to be against the wall.

It seemed as if they'd be given some financial help in the not-so-distant future, though. AFC Wimbledon were approached by a consortium led by ex-Chelsea defender Ryan Bertrand to take a minority 34% of the club's shares, while a consortium led by former Everton frontman Victor Anichebe was heavily linked with a takeover at Hayes Lane.

However, in recent updates, AFC Wimbledon have confirmed that the Sloane Street Capital investment group has withdrawn from their deal to invest up to £87 million over five years, one week ahead of what would have been an important fan vote to pass it.

Bertrand needed the financial backing of British technology expert Omar Mir to go through with the minority investment, but his 'investment priorities have changed,' according to a statement.

The collapse hasn't deterred the 37-year-old ex-England international, though, with it said that he, alongside his partners at Sloane Street Football, will be seeking fresh investment and returning to the Dons with a proposal on the same terms.

Meanwhile, Pete O'Rourke has reported that the African consortium's takeover of Bromley, fronted by Anichebe, is on the verge of collapse, which, to some, can be seen as a bullet dodged.

The Shoreline Limited group has been the main avenue of blame attributed to the state that National League side Gateshead are currently in.

They attempted to buy the North East club a couple of seasons ago, but after it failed, the group needed to sign waivers over money they claim they are owed, so the side currently at the bottom of the fifth tier can be taken over by another group and avoid the possibility of liquidation.

While not a concrete fact, it's strongly inferred that Andy Woodman's dismissal after five-and-a-half years in charge of Bromley, during which he won the FA Trophy and two promotions to the highest level the Ravens have played at, was down to the proposed takeover.

Many feel that the sacking of Woodman just 10 games into the League One campaign was in favour of a new brand of football that this new consortium wanted to see at Hayes Lane. Now, some feel that an unpopular dismissal was for nothing.

One fan claimed that the group had "turned Gateshead into near collapse," and "ruined 2 football clubs," after this breakdown.

Some are now worrying that, as with Gateshead, Shoreline Sports could end up suing the club over financial claims against shareholders, which could put any future investment into the club in jeopardy.

Ultimately, it's been quite a mess at Bromley in the opening stages of the season. Karl Robinson lost his first game in charge in the FA Cup against a Rotherham United side experiencing troubling times themselves in League Two.

This failed takeover will just create more confusion and uncertainty about the club's future, which will detract from what was set to be a historic campaign in the club's history.

In an ideal world, AFC Wimbledon wouldn't need any minority investment, and they'd be able to compete solely as a fan-run club.

The reality isn't that, though, and it's something that the Dons fans have come to accept if they are to break through the glass ceiling currently encasing their ambitions.

As long as any funding doesn't turn into a full-blown takeover, that's the compromise that a club like AFC Wimbledon has to take, and this proposed 34% investment wouldn't have done so. Therefore, some Dons fans were actually looking forward to getting it over the line.

The news surrounding SSC pulling out is a disappointment, but there can be some optimism in the fact that Bertrand and co are still seeking investment routes into the club. Now all they have to do is wait to see if that comes.

Meanwhile, both sides will continue to punch above their weight and battle bravely against the third-tier drop this season.
