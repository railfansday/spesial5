American Airlines Drops Its Chicago Gate Lawsuit—But United Isn’t Getting The Retreat It Wanted

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

American Airlines is dropping its lawsuit over Chicago O’Hare’s gate redistribution. The airline tells me it has filed to dismiss the underlying contract case after losing its injunction appeal — even as it continues to insist Chicago broke its lease. The airline has recovered gate space, added flights, and is in a much stronger position in Chicago than when the case began. American says two subsequent gate redeterminations have increased its allocation, and it also bought two gates from Spirit Airlines. They’re putting their attention into growing the hub, including a new Admirals Club that doubles the current L concourse lounge’s footprint. In a statement shared with me, American says: We stand by our decision to advocate for a fair process, and today, we believe the best way to continue delivering on that commitment is to look ahead. As rebuild our Chicago hub from the pandemic, we have continued to invest in our operation, network and customer experience. Our focus now is on continuing that momentum and building an even stronger future at O’Hare. American’s filing goes further – it asks to dismiss the entire lawsuit with prejudice, including its claim for money damages. Chicago and United don’t object, and each side would bear its own costs and legal fees. Once approved, that closes the door on American returning to collect damages over the same 2025 gate redistribution. American Is Choosing To Drop The Case Earlier today I explained that losing the appeal didn’t end American’s lawsuit. The appeals court upheld the denial of a preliminary injunction because it accepted that undoing the gate changes would cause greater disruption than leaving them in place. It didn’t finally decide whether Chicago breached the 2018 lease, or whether doing so damaged American. The airline is now choosing to stop pursuing that underlying case. The airline maintains that the 2025 process violated its agreement and gave United an unfair head start, but says the changed circumstances at O’Hare make investing and competing there a better focus. I still think American has the better contract argument. The city promised additional gate capacity and a protected period to grow into it before another redistribution. The trial judge accepted that the promise had been made, but suggested that even if Chicago was breaking that promise it might not be enforceable. But the evidence in the case was pretty clear that Chicago was redistributing gates earlier than they’d promised to, and that the timing was part of American’s bargained-for consideration in signing the lease. I was skeptical from the beginning that American would get an injunction, because its losses seemed potentially compensable with money. But I also suggested that American had recovered its gate position so the value in litigation seemed almost beside the point at this point. More Flights, More Destinations, And Gates Coming Back Before American sued, it planned forup to 480 daily departures and a network of about 150 destinations. Summer 2025 represented 22% more departures and 25% more seats than the prior year. American now has more than 500 daily departures and service to more than 185 destinations in 20 countries and territories. Chicago is its third-largest hub, with nearly 10,000 employees. And their spring 2026 schedule called for more than 500 peak daily departures, with 30% more departures than spring 2025. American counts more than 30 new destinations added or announced from Chicago since the start of 2025. Naples launched in May 2025 and Honolulu that October. Maui starts December 17, while Tokyo Narita starts March 19, 2027. This growth earned back gate space in this October’s redistribution. Separately, it paid $30 million for Spirit’s G8 and G10 gate leases, which I was first to report. A Bigger Admirals Club And Better Facilities The new L concourse Admirals Club will replace American’s smallest and weakest O’Hare lounge next year. The current space is basically a sad room with a bar and a small conference room. It’s convenient to the L gates, but it isn’t much of a reason to choose American. The replacement will exceed 10,000 square feet and double the current footprint, with floor-to-ceiling windows, airfield views, and the newer design American has introduced in places like Washington National, Denver, Newark, and Philadelphia. The existing club remains open during construction. American’s rendering shows what they’re building: Refurbished gates K1 and L2A have reopened with larger waiting areas, and baggage claim carousels 30 through 33 are operating. American has also invested in the product passengers get once they board: Its O’Hare operation now offers a premium cabin on every flight, after replacing single class regional jets with aircraft offering first class and Wi-Fi. Chicago received the new Boeing 787-9s with 51 business class suites first. United Still Has To Compete With American In Chicago United wanted American to back away from O’Hare. Scott Kirby talked up American’s losses and tried to get financial analysts to pressure the airline to retreat. United filed more flying to keep American from improving its gate position, even though it didn’t independently want all of that additional capacity. The Federal Aviation Administration concluded that the airport couldn’t handle the schedules airlines had filed. Its summer flight caps used approved 2025 schedules as the baseline, rather than rewarding United for its much bigger proposed buildup. That protected American’s competitive position. The federal intervention and American’s recovered gate space have mattered more to its ability to stay and grow than the injunction appeal. American still has to make the flying work. But Kirby’s preferred answer—have American leave so United can dominate—would also cost American connecting traffic, local customers, and credit card spending. The airline tells me it’s seeing growth in local and business customers, AAdvantage enrollments, and credit card acquisitions. It’s also bringing in Margaret Mueller, former head of the Executives’ Club of Chicago, as vice president and managing partner for the market starting November 30. Her job includes local sales and business relationships. That’s where the larger schedule and lounge spending have to pay off. Chicago is one of the biggest credit card spending markets in the country. American needs a schedule customers can use and an experience they’ll choose if it wants them earning AAdvantage miles on their everyday spending. The new Admirals Club also gives them a more tangible reason to keep paying for the Citi AAdvantage Executive card. Litigation is quite often a pressure point. In this matter, American may have lost the case (or is choosing not to pursue further), but gained significant benefits from having brought the litigation and keeping the pressure on. It doesn’t sound like from this article that any formal settlement was reached over this matter, but it did demonstrate to the powers that be in Chicago that AA is serious about competing at ORD and is not going anywhere.

American won by not giving up and refocusing its efforts on being relevant in Chicago.

Their new management has a “we have to fight to win” mindset that they will need in Chicago more than any other market.

The real question is where Chicago comes in priority compared to other opportunities; AA has lost share in NYC, BOS and LAX among large markets that are competitive w/ DL and UA. I don’t think AA has the bandwidth or the earnings to take on all of those markets and win given that UA and esp. DL have much higher earnings.

UA”s earnings will continue to be diluted because of having to fight against AA where average fares are much lower than competing hubs such as DTW and MSP as well as ATL and DFW.

in an industry where air travel is increasingly concentrating around the big 4, AA cannot afford to give up any more share and has to claw back what it can.

Man, I really want American to come back. They seem to have a change in attitude and perspective and intelligence. I hope it’s for real. I hope it sticks.
