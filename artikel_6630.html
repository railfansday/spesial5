Azalea Cocktail

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Recipe by Kate McKiernan, Chicago, Illinois

The Azalea cocktail is the signature bright pink, lemonade-and-vodka drink of the Masters Tournament, delivering spring and summer party refreshment.

The Azalea is the signature cocktail of the Masters Tournament, and as a hostess with the mostest, I love leaning into a theme. This drink is a hole-in-one crowd pleaser, whether you’re a golf fan or not. No one can deny the lure of a pretty-in-pink, citrusy vodka cocktail like this one, a perfect sipper on a warm, breezy day on or off the green.

Named for the stunning azalea blooms that famously line the grounds of the 13th hole at the Augusta National Golf Club, the Azalea cocktail is refreshing and perfectly sweet. It’s simple on the page with only a few ingredients, but the bright acid of the lemonade and the sweet kick of the grenadine go perfectly together and work well with a neutral spirit like vodka. It’s an easy cocktail to scale up for a crowd, making it perfect for Masters watch parties, spring brunches or any gatherings that could use a little extra pink charm.

Fill a chilled rocks glass with fresh ice.

Add ice, vodka and grenadine to an empty cocktail shaker. Fill the shaker with ice, and shake the cocktail until it’s well chilled, 15 to 20 seconds.

Editor’s Tip: For a more layered look, add the grenadine directly to the glass and only shake the vodka and lemonade, allowing the pink color to settle to the bottom.

Strain the cocktail into the prepared glass.

Garnish the cocktail with an orange slice and a cocktail cherry.

Editor’s Tip: You can also use the syrup from the cherries to stand in for grenadine in a pinch.

To make Azalea cocktails for a crowd, multiply the portions by the number of people you’re serving. Combine the vodka, lemonade and grenadine in a large pitcher or beverage dispenser ahead of time, then refrigerate the mixture until you’re ready to serve. If you don’t plan to shake each drink individually, you’ll want to add water to the mixture to compensate for the dilution you get from shaking a cocktail over ice. To achieve the proper dilution, add water equal to 20% to 25% the total volume of the base mixture.

Another way to gauge: Make a single serving of your cocktail. Measure the total volume of liquid, stir the mixture with ice until it’s chilled, and measure it again. The difference is the exact amount of water you need to add per serving in your batch.

Stir the mixture well before pouring it over ice, and wait to add garnishes until serving so everything feels fresh.

A rocks glass, aka a double old-fashioned, is the traditional glassware choice for an Azalea cocktail, giving it a classic, country-club feel while keeping things simple and elegant. If you want to dress things up for brunch or spring entertaining, a highball glass also works beautifully, offering extra room for ice and garnishes.
