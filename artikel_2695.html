Patriots’ biggest problem to fix during 2026 bye week

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Patriots' biggest problem to fix during 2026 bye week appeared first on ClutchPoints. Add ClutchPoints as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

There are plenty of problems the New England Patriots need to address during their Week 11 bye. However, one stands above the rest: What happened to the running game?

The Patriots' rushing attack has become almost nonexistent, and the numbers don't tell the entire story. New England is averaging 114 rushing yards per game, but it doesn't have a single running back averaging more than 40.5 yards per game.

Rhamondre Stevenson is averaging 40.3 yards per game, Corey Kiner is at 26.9, and TreVeyon Henderson is averaging 33.8. Henderson has appeared in only six games and is currently sidelined, leaving Stevenson and Kiner to carry the workload.

Against the Detroit Lions, Kiner received 10 carries while Stevenson had just six. The Patriots finished with 61 rushing yards as a team, and backup quarterback Tommy DeVito accounted for 19 of them. That's simply not good enough, especially for a team that could be without its starting quarterback for an extended period.

Drake Maye suffered an ankle injury against Detroit and will undergo further testing to determine the severity. While his recovery timeline remains uncertain, New England has to prepare for the possibility that he misses significant time. That makes fixing the running game more important than ever.

If the Patriots want to salvage their season, they need to establish some consistency on the ground. That is especially true if Maye is unavailable when they return from the bye. DeVito struggled after taking over against Detroit, and New England cannot expect its backup quarterback to carry the offense with a poor receiving core.

Mike Vrabel needs to find a way to get more out of his running backs. It's not as if this group lacks talent. We have seen Stevenson and Henderson produce at a high level, while Kiner has shown enough to suggest there is something worth developing.

Kiner averaged 4.8 yards per carry on 12 attempts with the Arizona Cardinals last season. This year, he is averaging 4.5 yards per carry on 60 attempts. That's respectable production, especially for a running back still finding his footing in New England.

Stevenson, meanwhile, is averaging just 3.7 yards per carry. Henderson isn't much better at 3.9, although his opportunities have been limited by his absence. The Patriots need more from all three. Stevenson has to be more effective with his touches, Kiner needs to take advantage of the opportunity in front of him, and Henderson's eventual return should give the backfield another boost.

If things don't improve, New England may have to explore bringing in another running back. There is no reason to keep running the same offense if the current personnel cannot consistently move the football.

Vrabel has an entire bye week to evaluate what isn't working. He needs to communicate with his running backs, figure out how to get them more involved, and make the necessary adjustments up front.

The Patriots have bigger questions to answer with Maye's health, but the running game is something they can begin addressing immediately. If New England wants to stay competitive over the second half of the season, Stevenson and Kiner need to step up. Some believe New England isn't a playoff team anyway.

The bye week couldn't have come at a better time. Now it's up to Vrabel to make sure the Patriots come out of it with a much better plan on the ground when they face the winless Los Angeles Chargers in Week 12 (which could be flexed out of Sunday Night Football).
