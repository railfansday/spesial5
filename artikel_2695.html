Need for stronger start an emphasis for Magic, who host Pistons next

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

For the fifth time this season, the Magic failed to score 100 points against the Wizards on Sunday night.

And also for the fifth time this season, Orlando lost in that low-scoring situation.

But it wasn't just the fact that the Magic were unable to play well enough on offense against a Washington squad that entered the game with the 20th-ranked defensive rating (115.3) in the NBA. Orlando also couldn't slow down a Wizards team that featured the fifth-worst offensive rating (110.3) in the entire league.

The Wizards led wire-to-wire in their 20-point victory, 116-96 over the Magic at Capital One Arena, and they knocked down a season-high 22 three-pointers, which were tied for the second-most in franchise history. Meanwhile, Orlando shot 30.8% from 3-point range and fell to 2-8 when it shoots below 36% from distance this year.

Simply put, the Magic did themselves no favors on defense and had no answers on offense against the Wizards, who won their third game in a row and second over Orlando in the month of November.

"I think our closeouts in a number of areas were a little bit short," Magic coach Sean Sweeney said about Orlando's defensive effort on the perimeter at Washington. "We let guys who have been good shooters this year get good looks. So, we've got to do a better job there."

"With some of those guys that are good shooters like (Kyshawn) George, we've got to do a better job," Sweeney added. "A guy like that can't get up 11 threes up."

To Sweeney's point, George entered Sunday night shooting 40% from distance on 5.9 long-range attempts per game. Against the Magic, he shot 6-of-11 from beyond the arc.

George was one of seven Wizards who made made multiple 3-pointers, tying for the most in team history in one game. Beyond George making half as many triples as the Magic made total (12), Washington's Bilal Coulibaly hit four and Tre Johnson made three. Four-time All-Star guard Trae Young added three and the trio of Khris Middleton, Alex Sarr and Anthony Davis each made two.

It didn't matter if it was an accurate shooter such as George or an inconsistent wing such as Coulibaly (who shot 16.7% from 3-point range entering Sunday) — the Wizards were hitting from distance more often than not when they shot 56% as a group on Sunday.

"There (were) guys out there that were hitting threes that we're kind of living with and then there (were) also guys that we had as 'hot shooters' on the scouting report," Magic forward Tristan da Silva said about the Wizards. "With some of them, you've got to live, but I think we gave up too many that we don't want to give up."

As Washington (7-11) won its third game in a row after previously dropping eight straight, the Magic (7-9) fell for the second time in three games.

Orlando will return to the nation's capital on Saturday for a third November meeting with the Wizards, who've won both Southeast Division matchups over the Magic.

Before the Magic return to Washington, however, they'll have a chance to bounce back against the Pistons (10-6) on Tuesday night in a high-stakes NBA Cup group-play contest.

With a win, Orlando, which has started 3-0 in East Group A action, would clinch its spot in the Cup quarterfinals. A loss and the Magic would likely have to rely on point differential in order to advance to the knockout rounds as the East's wild card team.

Eight teams will advance to the knockout rounds: the team with the best standing in group play games in each of the six groups and two "wild cards" (the team from each conference with the best record in group play games that finished second in its group), according to the NBA.

The Magic have advanced to the knockout rounds the past two seasons, but this year, they haven't been as sharp at home as they've played in the past.

Entering Tuesday's game inside Kia Center, Orlando is 1-5 at home this season and Detroit is 7-3 on the road. There were only three others teams in the East still with just one win on its home court entering Monday's slate of games: the 4-12 Bulls, the 3-12 Nets and the 1-16 Bucks.

There was also only one other team in the East with a better road record than Detroit, and that was Philadelphia, which beat the Magic at Kia Center last Wednesday.

Asked about his message to the Magic after Sunday's loss at Washington, Sweeney emphasized the need for a stronger start after Washington scored the first 11 points of the contest and led by 17 points after the first quarter inside Capital One Arena.

"I think just start with how we come out," Sweeney said. "Make sure we're ready to go at the beginning and then make sure we sustain what we do well. And if we make a mistake, correct it (and) move onto the next one."

Added da Silva about the team's task of bouncing back versus Detroit: "We've got to definitely be on our best behavior. We've just got to get in the film room, clean some stuff up and be ready to go. It's a good team coming in and it's a Cup game."
