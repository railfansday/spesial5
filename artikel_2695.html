5 Bills to watch vs. Chiefs

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The Buffalo Bills host the Kansas City Chiefs in the Thanksgiving nightcap this year, and what a game it should be. The 9-1 Bills are the current No. 1 seed in the AFC playoff picture. Thanks to tiebreakers, the 8-2 Chiefs trail the 8-2 Denver Broncos in the AFC West, so Kansas City is currently the No. 5 seed in the conference. However, a win over the Bills would go a long way towards ensuring that the AFC playoffs run through Arrowhead Stadium.

The Bills are coming off a sloppy divisional win over the Miami Dolphins on Sunday. The Chiefs, on the other hand, thoroughly thrashed the Arizona Cardinals. Kansas City has won three games in a row; Buffalo has won six consecutive games. Unless there's a tie, someone is going to take a loss for the first time in a long time this week.

If Buffalo wants to emerge with a win over their bitter conference rival, they'll need some big performances from their key players. While everyone wants to talk about the quarterbacks in this game, we're intentionally leaving both Josh Allen and Patrick Mahomes out of the columns this week. Those two are pretty obvious. There are plenty of other players worthy of mention, though.

Here are five Bills to watch in this critical AFC contest._____________________________________________________________________________

After a slow start on Sunday, Cook extended his streak of scoring at least one touchdown and rushing for at least 75 yards to nine games, which is a new NFL record. He had previously shared the record at eight games with John Rigging and LaDainian Tomlinson. Cook surpassed the 1,000-yard rushing mark for the fourth consecutive season, and he joins two Hall of Fame former Bills in Thurman Thomas and O.J. Simpson as the only players to achieve that feat in franchise history. The sledding won't be easy against an outstanding Kansas Cigy tush defense that allows just 3.7 yards per carry on the season, but in a game that's set to feature horrendous weather, finding space and keeping the offense ahead of the sticks will be of the utmost importance.

It's something we've said ever since the Bills made Kincaid their first-round pick in the 2023 NFL Draft. When the big tight end is healthy, Josh Allen's life is much easier. When he doesn't play, Allen doesn't have nearly as much space to operate in the passing game. Last week, Allen threw for 304 yards without Kincaid, but it took 45 passing attempts and some ugly possessions to hit that milestone. If Kincaid is healthy, it gives the Bills their full cadre of pass-catchers against an elite defensive unit. Allen's quarterback rating when targeting Kincaid was 149.2 last season. Since Kincaid joined the club, Allen has a passer rating of 99.9 with Kincaid in the lineup and 91.2 without him. Allen averages 237 passing yards per game with Kincaid in the lineup; he averages 206 passing yards per game without him. He has thrown 77 touchdowns against 29 interceptions in 47 games with Kincaid, and he's thrown 14 touchdowns against 9 interceptions in the ten games Kincaid has missed in his career. No. 86 is a big part of Buffalo's offense, and having him on Thursday will go a long way towards helping the Bills earn a victory.

Greg Rousseau is obvious. Someone else has to offer some juice in the pass rush, and while I'm tempted to spotlight rookie T.J. Parker, I think it has to be the veteran edge rusher to come in and make a play. Chubb has been pretty quiet this season, totaling just 1.5 sacks, 10 quarterback hits and 14 pressures while playing on 69% of the Bills' defensive snaps. With the Miami Dolphins last season coming off an ACL injury, Chubb totaled 8.5 sacks, 22 pressures, and 20 quarterback hits. No matter how many good things the Bills have to say about him, they definitely expected more when they handed him a three-year deal worth $43.5 million this offseason. I expect that the Chiefs will look to double-team Rousseau, and they'll likely leave Chubb blocked by one man. Chubb has to win that matchup and impact Patrick Mahomes in some way.

With the defense settling down over the last month, defensive coordinator Jim Leonhard can really start to use his chess pieces to their athletic strengths. Bishop can operate at every level of the defense for the Bills, and I would love to see Leonhard deploy him in a variety of ways. Use him to guard Travis Kelce. Use him as a deep-third centerfielder when Mahomes expects newly-acquired Amani Hooker to pkay that role. Use him as a blitzer off the edge to create pressure on the run game and the pass game. Bishop is an elite athlete who is allowing a career-low 55.8% of the passes thrown while he's the closest man in coverage to be completed. Bishop has a chance to be eating a turkey leg on National television after this one because he can change the game in a variety of ways. Look for him to notch his first interception of the season while also collecting his second sack of the season at some point in this game.
