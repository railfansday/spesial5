The negatives of positive thinking

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Of all the cringey golf clichés, I can't stand the one that says a bad day on the golf course is still better than a good day at the office. Consider me skeptical. I get paid to be at work, whereas my worst golf days tend to cost me time, money, and self-esteem.

Even as a golfer who prides himself on embracing imperfection, I am also allergic to what I might describe as "artificial positivity."

Let's agree some days golf can feel really hard, so there's no sense pretending we're happy when we're not. The other extreme is also dangerous because no one's ever complained their way out of a bad round. But at least your whiniest playing partners aren't trying to be something they're not.

So what is artificial positivity? It's a partner insisting you enjoy the beautiful day, or congratulating you when you make a putt for double bogey. Among my pet peeves are backhanded compliments like, "Best drive of the day!" or "There you go!" I'm sure everyone means well, but sometimes the best reaction is silence.

When sports psychologist Julie Elion works with her tour player clients, she says she doesn't want them to suppress what they're experiencing on the course. If players are frustrated or tentative during a round, it helps to at least recognize it and work from there.

" I never say, 'Be positive.' It's so ridiculous and empty, you know?" Elion says. "But I think what we try to do is take a step back and find neutral. Let's not confabulate what's happening out there and make it bigger than it is. What mental state works best for you? Let's try to figure out how to get back there sooner."

No matter which end of the spectrum you occupy, "finding neutral" is a worthy objective. When we feel as if nothing is going right, we probably need a sliver of encouragement or motivation to pull us out of a rut; and when everything is clicking for you, finding neutral means focusing on the next shot instead of the course record.

The trap many golfers fall into is focusing on everything other than the present. This is why Elion believes bounce-back stats are so valuable, because they are windows into how golfers channel their energy into whatever's next.

"If you've had a bad hole, how are you going to get back? And to me, that's getting back to neutral, whatever neutral is for you," she said.

The next time you are fighting it on the course, the answer isn't to pretend you're thrilled, nor is it to throw up your hands in despair. Like picking a target to a fairway or green, somewhere in the middle is usually best.
