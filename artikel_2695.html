Lakers surge in fourth quarter to beat Jazz, win for 11th time in 12 games

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

It took some work for the Lakers to win Monday night against the Utah Jazz.

It took playing as a whole unit, and it took a strong defensive effort when the offense struggled.

Five players scored in double figures, and the Lakers needed every bit of their contributions to pull off a 109-94 win at Crypto.com Arena.

Luka Doncic led the Lakers with 28 points, but he was just eight for 24 from the field and three for 14 from three-point range.

Walker Kessler had a double-double with 10 points and 12 rebounds to go along with two blocks. Jake LaRavia had a double-double with 15 points and 10 rebounds. Matisse Thybulle scored 14 points and Collin Sexton had 12 points off the bench.

The Lakers shot 43% from the field and just 29% from three-point range. But they held the Jazz (3-16) to just 32% shooting from the field in the fourth quarter, outscoring them 31-17.

"I would say that's pretty good defense," Kessler said. "I think our transition defense has gotten better, which is nice to see. Our limiting second-chance points has gotten better. Obviously, we can still improve on that. We're kind of struggling right now with open threes, but I feel like, you know, we had a kind of a tough stretch where they kind of came back in the second half. ... I think we're trending upwards and that's all you want to see."

Despite their shooting struggles, the Jazz threatened to beat the Lakers for the second time in less than a week before a fourth-quarter surge helped the Lakers pull away. The group of Sexton, Thybulle, LaRavia, Kessler and Quentin Grimes started the fourth quarter and helped the Lakers build a 13-point lead.

By the time Doncic returned to the game with 6:01 left, the Lakers led 95-84 and were on their way to their 11th win in 12 games.

Lakers star Luka Doncic controls the ball in front of Utah Jazz forward Kyle Filipowski during the first quarter Monday. (Ronaldo Bolaños / Los Angeles Times)

"I think the fourth quarter determines a lot of the game," Sexton said. "And at the end of the day, we got to come out with that juice, with that fire. And I feel like the last couple games, we've been doing that. I think a big part of it is just that start. And I think that whoever starts that fourth quarter has to set the tone for the rest of the game. So, I think we got to try to change the flow, change the way the game was going."

Now the Lakers prepare to face the Spurs in San Antonio on Friday in an NBA Cup group play showdown.

The teams are tied for the best record in the NBA at 15-3. They also have the league's biggest stars in Doncic and Victor Wembanyama for the Spurs.

"We're going to prepare for it the same way we prepare for every other game and we're going to game plan the same way we have and we're going to go in there and play the same way that we've been doing," LaRavia said. "So, we got three days, obviously, to kind of rest and recover and game plan for this game on Friday, 'cause you're right, it is a big game."

Bob Iger, far left, sits next to Jeanie Buss and Josh Kushner, second right, sits next to Rob Pelinka during Monday's game. Kushner and Iger have entered into an agreement with Lakers owner Mark Walter to buy the team. (Ronaldo Bolaños / Los Angeles Times)

Austin Reaves missed his fifth straight game with a left heel bruise, but he is making progress, coach JJ Redick said. Reaves was able to participate in the Lakers' stay-ready-games on Monday.

"We want to be consistent with our RTP [return to play] stuff," Redick said. "I think today was a big step just in terms of getting a stay-ready game in. And he played great, moved great. I think we're optimistic for the rest of this week going forward."
