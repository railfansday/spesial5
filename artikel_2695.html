Jonathan Taylor Trade Rumors: Vikings, Broncos, Packers among potential landing spots as deadline approaches

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Jonathan Taylor is the talk of the town after the Colts shifted their stance on trading him. I'll be cataloging every trade rumor and update we get on the situation in this article, starting with a big update.

11/7 Update: The Ringer's Tom Pelissero believes Taylor is staying in Indy. The Colts are looking for a blockbuster haul and no team has made them an offer. This is by no means the end of the saga, but it's increasingly likely that the Colts' unreasonable ask for Taylor prices out all interested parties.

The Vikings make too much sense. They have possibly the best defense in the NFL and a weak passing attack. Given that a superstar quarterback isn't walking through the door anytime soon, they should be an old-school, ground-and-pound offense that complements the defense for low-scoring wins. They don't have the horses for that in their backfield right now. Aaron Jones has had a solid season while averaging 4.2 yards per carry, but he can't make it through a game without picking up a new injury. Jordan Mason, on the other hand, has missed time with an injury of his own and is plodding his way to 3.9 yards per attempt. Minnesota also has extra draft capital in 2027 via the Jonathan Greenard trade, which netted them the Eagles' third-round pick.

The Broncos were reportedly the team that nearly outbid the Texans for David Montgomery in the offseason. Jonah Coleman has emerged as a solid option for them, but RJ Harvey is on injured reserve and J.K. Dobbins—now relegated to backup duties—is averaging a dreary 3.3 yards per carry. Taylor would immediately take over as the workhorse back on a team that has failed to create a successful ground game for Sean Payton's entire tenure as head coach. Local reporter Zac Stevens makes it sound like they are pushing hard to get a deal done.

11/8 Update: NFL insider Josina Anderson says that the Giants have pulled out of conversations to acquire Taylor. Things change quickly in the NFL, but this sounds fairly definitive.

11/9 Update: While the Giants are reportedly out of the Taylor trade talks, their offer has been revealed. They were reportedly willing to part ways with second- and fifth-round picks for Taylor. The Colts declined, though that was likely to continue fielding offers. This might be the best deal they get. It would not be surprising for the Colts to circle back on this offer ahead of tomorrow's deadline.

Cam Skattebo's weird season makes the Taylor trade even more viable. He leads all running backs in fumbles and was benched after losing the ball in overtime versus the Commanders. Skattebo came back with a vengeance in Week 7 with a 17/124/1 rushing line, but he also went down with a hamstring injury at the end of the game. It held him out of Week 9's contest, though it looks like he will be back for Week 10. With his minor injury and Tyrone Tracy on IR, the Giants could view running back as a need regardless of the availability of Taylor. The fact that a superstar happens to be up for grabs is simply fortuitous timing.

The Packers are the last of the big four teams routinely linked to Taylor, including by NFL Network's Ian Rapoport, who didn't list the Seahawks or Bengals. Though that's simply pulling from the ticker on his Sunday morning segment.

11/9 Update: The Packers were already getting nothing out of their ground game, and now Jacobs is hurt. He has a fractured finger, though there's a chance he manages to play through it in Week 10. Still, Jacobs has been dreadfully inefficient this year and has ongoing legal troubles.

The injury could be the nudge Green Bay needed to pull the trigger on Taylor.

Schefter didn't specifically mention the Bengals in his Sunday morning hit, but NFL insider Jordan Schultz has included them as a team to watch.

Cincy's rushing attack is struggling this year. They are 22nd in EPA per rush attempt and Chase Brown ranks 38th in yards after contact per carry. Samaje Perine is shockingly ahead of him in that metric, though he only ranks 30th. The Bengals' defense has improved this year, but it's not going to carry them to a title. Their path to hoisting the Lombardi Trophy ultimately runs through having the league's best offense. Taylor would round out an offense that already has an unstoppable passing attack.

This destination continues to look less likely by the week. Schultz included Seattle in his list of teams interested in Taylor, but he's in the minority of reporters in that regard. The Seahawks just got first-round pick Jadarian Price and Zach Charbonnet back from stints on IR. Seattle trusts Charbonnet to do the dirty work and doesn't have much tape on Price because of the month he spent on injured reserve. It would be surprising for them to throw in the towel on Price after just four games.

At 4-4, they might not feel confident in pushing their chips in for the 2026 season either. The Giants at -160 (61.5 percent) are considerably more likely to make the playoffs than Seattle at -138 (58 percent), if you take DraftKings' numbers at face value. Seattle is undoubtedly a better offense when they have an elite running back to lean on. Given Sam Darnold's middling play this year, they are likely a team that benefits more than the average roster from a great running back. But their investment in Price and shaky start make a trade unlikely.

The Commanders stick out as a logical fit for Taylor, but reporters aren't bringing them up at all, so I'll keep this brief. Even at 2-4-1, they're still in the hunt for the NFC East crown. Their Week 10 game versus the Giants will have a massive impact on their place in the standings. With Jacory Croskey-Merritt on IR, Kaytron Allen waived, Jeremy McNichols potentially done for the year with a foot injury, and Jerome Ford waived, almost every notable member of the backfield is out of the picture. Rachaad White and Austin Ekeler are the last ones standing. For a team with a dynamic, dual-threat quarterback, the Commanders do not have a running back who can take advantage of the extra attention defenses pay to Jayden Daniels. The Commanders will likely do their due diligence regarding Taylor, but the lack of buzz connecting them to the trade talks likely means they're not in the running.

It's worth mentioning that the Colts aren't obligated to trade Taylor. Their asking price of a CMC-level haul could price all potential partners out.

Taylor is an elite runner, but McCaffrey's abilities as a receiver haven't been matched by anyone since Marshall Faulk. McCaffrey was also 26 when he was traded. Taylor is 27 and will turn 28 at the end of the regular season. There are enough interested parties and a desire to make this happen from the Colts' end that I bet a deal gets done, but it's not a foregone conclusion.
