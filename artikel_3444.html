Watch the ‘Backrooms’ VFX Supervisor Explain His Unique Process of Collaborating with Kane Parsons

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

When you hire a 20-year-old director to adapt his own viral YouTube series into a major Hollywood film, you’re not going to end up with a traditional movie. That’s part of what made Kane Parsons’ “Backrooms” such a massive critical and commercial success for A24. Audiences were eager to embrace Parsons as a major new filmmaking voice — and his crew members were just as excited for the chance to work with him.

During IndieWire’s recent Craft Roundtables, “Backrooms” VFX Supervisor Edward Douglas broke down the unique workflow that he developed with Parsons during pre-production. He explained that Parsons was used to making his own effects in Blender, so the team started communicating with him using the popular VFX software. “The first conversation I had with Kane Parsons was ‘How do you like to work?’ And we embraced his process,” Douglas said. “Because his way of communicating with the entire crew was first, through Blender. He built 100,000 square feet of sets in Blender and passed it off to the production designer. And it immediately crashed his computer. But that let us start breaking down everything.”

Despite costing less than $10 million, “Backrooms” contained a significant number of VFX shots. Douglas explained that the process became a unique mix of traditional effects work by seasoned pros and found-footage-style shots that Parsons did himself.

“It was an indie budget film, but it had over ten minutes of full CG,” Douglas said. “Some of it was traditional, and some of it was Backrooms found footage exploration. That ended up being a hybrid VFX process. We had very traditional sequences when we were working with VFX Blenders. And then we had more of our Kane Parsons DIY pipeline for scenes that he would really be touching himself. And we set it up so that Kane could actually log into the VFX Blender servers and tweak things, set up shots. Because that was his way. He could say, ‘Here are the notes,’ or Can I show you?’ And we’d want to create a way for him to show us. Because he is, at his heart, a VFX artist.”
