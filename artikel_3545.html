Warby Parker and Gentle Monster Are Launching Smart Glasses. Does Anyone Want Them?

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

This is the November 11, 2026 edition of Fit Check, your front row seat to the power players, peacocks, and deep pockets of fashion—and how they influence the rest of the world.

It’s been a meme-worthy news week: the new Met Gala theme launched endless online discourse, Jensen Huang ignited the internet with news of his affair with an alleged Chinese spy, then fashion netizens had a time memeifying him as a Hedi-boy, and Maybelline changed its slogan with some very confusing punctuation.

IRL, Gentle Monster and Warby Parker are getting into the smart glasses space. Like Meta, they’re working hard to get fashion’s co-sign in order to have tastemakers influence everyone else to wear them, though I have yet to find a fashion person happy to wear these without a check involved. And this week, Alex Consani video-called me to discuss her acting ambitions after her debut in American Horror Story: 13. She’s already filmed two movies coming next year.

Mentioned in this issue: Iron Man, Robert Downey Jr., Kylie Jenner, Timothée Chalamet’s security guard, Steven Meisel, Martine Rose discourse, Luca de Meo, Woke 3.0, Hedi Slimane, Slimaniacs, Sarah Paulson, Ryan Murphy, and many more.

Amelia Gray at the Gentle Monster launch dinner.

Last night Warby Parker and Gentle Monster celebrated the launches of their “intelligent eyewear” lines (not to be confused with Meta’s “smart glasses”), both developed with Google and Samsung. Warby Parker hosted a literary activation with Vogue, and Gentle Monster held a “preview experience” and dinner attended by Alex Consani, Anok Yai, Charles Melton, Joaquin Phoenix, and more.

Snap also tapped Robert Downey Jr. to star in videos showcasing its technology, Iron Man–style. Phoenix appeared in Gentle Monster’s launch campaign in a callback to his role in Her, in which he plays a man who falls in love with an AI voiced by Scarlett Johansson. (In 2024, she had a dispute with OpenAI after learning the company had created a voice that sounded similar to hers for its AI, which she had declined to license for ChatGPT. Sam Altman denied any intentional imitation.) Leveraging two movie stars who’ve been the face of this kind of technology in film to bring their products to the masses is a cunning move from Google and Samsung. Yet it’s possible that no co-sign can combat the anxiety around AI and its surrounding gadgets. There is a budding battle in the smart glasses space—but does anyone actually want them?

Martine Rose is officially taking over from Pierpaolo Piccioli at Balenciaga. I still think he could have been right for the brand, but he came at the wrong time. Balenciaga is essentially Kering’s youth vehicle after a decade under Demna, and Piccioli just wasn’t the designer to fulfill that brief. That his collections seemed to try to honor both Demna’s casualwear range and his own couture-like sensibility only muddled the brand’s remit further. But Rose, who worked with Demna on his Balenciaga menswear early on, is a much better fit in this context, and she has the industry’s goodwill—fans have been advocating her ascent to a role like this one for years.

Rose is the first official hire under Luca de Meo’s tenure as Kering’s CEO, and she is walking into a brave new world. De Meo believes Kering has been overly dependent on its creative directors, and is looking to streamline operations with a new strategy dubbed “ReconKering.” If reports are confirmed that Chemena Kamali will head to Saint Laurent and Virginia Burlina to McQueen, four out of five creative directors at Kering will soon be women. Along with Rose and Louise Trotter at Bottega Veneta, these are designers who are better known as prolific workhorses rather than buzzy household names. De Meo is evidently looking to grow beyond the star creative director strategy that defined fashion in the 2010s, with Demna as an exception. What this means for Rose and Balenciaga, and for Saint Laurent and McQueen, which have historically depended on the persona of their creative leads, remains to be seen.

Balenciaga is back in the news this week with the opening of the first of several activations around the 25th anniversary of its Le City bag, arguably the most enduring of It bags after the Birkin. Damien Hirst has hand-painted 25 bags for the occasion, and Nicole Richie is fronting the campaign in a callback to the aughts. This was planned prior to Piccioli’s exit, yet it’s taking place in between creative directors speaks to an undeniable fact that plays right into ReconKering: These brands are bigger than their designers, despite how important these appointments are to industry insiders and fanatics.

A look from Jun Takahashi’s fall-winter 2024 collection for Undercover, which will be included in the upcoming Met Costume Institute exhibition.

In case you missed it: The Met announced on Thursday the replacement exhibition for the John Galliano blockbuster postponed earlier this year. “ExtraORDINARY: Fashion and the Poetics of the Everyday” will focus on how designers have explored familiar, ordinary garments: jeans, T-shirts, workwear, and more. It sounds like both an ode to normcore and to the ingenuity with which designers like Matthieu Blazy, Demna, Jun Takahashi, and more have reimagined the most simple of clothing. Andrew Bolton, the curator in charge of the Costume Institute, told Vogue he “didn’t have a plan B” after Galliano and that he wanted the replacement show to be “challenging and provocative.” The announcement drew both positive and negative reactions. Its main criticism is its simplicity, especially when compared to Galliano’s extravagance. Bolton has a singular way of placing fashion in larger cultural contexts to underscore its historic importance—I’m curious to see what he’ll do when the fashion he’s dealing with already exists in an atmosphere that requires little explanation.

Maybelline has updated its famous slogan, “Maybe she’s born with it. Maybe it’s Maybelline,” which was first introduced in 1991, to a more democratic, inclusive riff: “Maybe. We’re born with it. Maybe. It’s Maybelline.” Confusing punctuation aside, the change feels like it belongs to the late 2010s era of DEI marketing. It’s true that binary pronouns have fallen out of style, particularly at the makeup counter, even if brands shied away from this kind of revamp in the Trump era. But then again, after last week’s blue wave in the midterm elections, maybe Maybelline is just in time.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang waves to the media as he arrives at Gimpo airport in Seoul on June 5, 2026 wearing a Dior jacket. Images from this same outing were modified with AI by fashion netizens.

The news of Jensen Huang’s affair filled X with memes about Chinese spies and mistresses in a variety of configurations. By Monday evening, Huang had reached the fashion world’s stylish netizens: the Instagram account @hidden.ny, an anonymous digital mood board and cultural platform, posted a series of images of Huang in a studded leather jacket. The caption read: “NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang in Hedi Slimane SLP Studded Leather Jacket after admitting to affair with alleged Chinese spy.”

It’s well-known that Huang is a leather jacket enthusiast. But despite Huang’s penchant for a black leather moment, it appears he was not, in fact, wearing the Saint Laurent jacket. A quick run through an AI image detector debunked Huang being a “Slimaniac,” at least in this particular case. SynthID detected that the images posted by Hidden and other users online were generated or edited with OpenAI. To burst the bubble even further, it appears the images used as the base for this edit weren’t taken after the disclosure of Huang’s affair, but during a trip to South Korea in June. Apologies to the menswear crowd for ruining the fantasy.
