People Are Just Straight-Up Illicitly Dumping Games Like Undertale Into Roblox Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Counter-StrikeDeltaruneParappaRobloxUndertale

Someone has taken the entirety of indie hit Undertale and unceremoniously cloned into Roblox without permission. And Deltarune, all five chapters.

And I don’t mean someone did fan recreations of these games in Roblox with Roblox assets. Nor do I mean they created a unique fan spinoff sort of thing within Roblox using assets from those games. I don’t even mean someone directly ripped all of Deltarune‘s battles and made them playable in Roblox, though someone did do that in August and nearly 200 million people have played it since. I mean someone just straight up took the entire games of Undertale, Deltarune, and others, and stuck them in Roblox.

I first learned about this on Reddit via a post from Personal-News3260 regarding the porting of Deltarune, and it admittedly took a bit of digging to find the port (I’m not going to link any of the games I talk about here for obvious reasons). But I did eventually find it and play it (alongside, apparently, 58,000 other people) and yeah, that’s Deltarune all right. While I obviously was unable (and, frankly, unwilling) to test the game in its entirety, I did play the first half hour or so of Chapter 1 and started all the other chapters, and can confirm that what I saw is undeniably just Deltarune. There are some notable issues, like audio glitches and screen tearing, and one of Susie’s early lines (“Quiet people piss me off”) has been softened to “Quiet people tick me off,” presumably so as not to run afoul of whatever rules still exist on Roblox anymore.

From there, I was able to track down a similar version of Undertale (“created” by the same person), and a community they’re part of where someone has put in what looks to be Parappa the Rappa. I booted both up and from the first few minutes, both definitely look like the actual games.

I also tapped my colleague Zack Zwiezen to look at a Counter-Strike 2 port that I saw being discussed and reported about in multiple places. Zack told me that the game I tracked down and sent him looked, sounded, and played like Counter-Strike 2, but did have different models and a different map, so it wasn’t quite as egregious as the stuff above. From what I can tell, the version of this game shown in the tweet below is the same game I sent him, but it seems to be regularly updated to change models around or restrict access in order to avoid being taken down. At various points, a number of individuals online have shown and reported that it’s largely indistinguishable from Counter-Strike 2.

When I reached out to Roblox for comment on this practice, I received the following back:

As a platform built by creators, we’re committed to respecting intellectual property rights of independent developers and global brands alike, and every Roblox user agrees to abide by our Terms of Use when they create.

Our Community Standards prohibit content that’s confusingly similar to another Roblox creator’s work, along with repetitive and misleading content, and our moderation systems are built to find and can act on copied games and misleading copycats of Roblox games. For off-platform rights holders, itâs their choice on if and how they would like their IP to be used on Roblox. Rights holders can manage this with tools like our License Manager, which lets them provide and manage licenses at scale, and our Rights Manager, which lets IP holders report content they believe infringes their rights. For those who choose, both Rights and License Manager also allow IP holders to provide reference material that enables Roblox to surface content that matches their IP.

We don’t tolerate IP infringement and act quickly on valid takedown requests, including DMCA notices. Once a game is published to Roblox, it’s held to the same platform-wide IP and clone policies as any other Roblox game, and creators remain responsible for what they choose to publish.

So Roblox’s official policy, of course, is that people are not allowed to just lift other people’s games and drop them into its platform. But they do. And Roblox here is essentially saying it’s not going to sit there and check games to see if they violate policy, until and unless the rights holders themselves get involved and use its tools to report games that violate their own copyrights.

But Roblox is massive! Thousands of games are being updated daily! And none of the games I mentioned above are findable just by searching “Deltarune” or “Parappa” in the search bar. So even though thousands of Roblox users are liable to stumble upon these games by accident or recommendation, because their “creators” have gone to great lengths to use vague names and descriptions in order to hide them from people actively looking for them; they’re deliberately difficult to track down in any specific way. I can imagine a company like Sony might have the resources to assign someone to engage with Roblox’s rights platforms. But I cannot fathom that small indie devs would have that kind of time. I’m sure Toby Fox, for instance, has better things to do than painstakingly search Roblox every day to see if anyone’s dumped his games in there.

With this policy and the advent of AI coding enabling more and more individuals to do such things with little time or effort, Roblox appears to be taking more of a YouTube approach to game uploads. It’s outsourcing the work of whack-a-mole-ing rights violations onto rights holders rather than performing any level of basic screening of a game before it’s allowed to be uploaded.

And that’s by design. In its latest earnings call, Roblox talked extensively about multiple AI-driven initiatives, including one called “The Universal Player.” Essentially, The Universal Player’s goal is to â[enable] the capability to import any existing console, PC, or mobile game onto Roblox.â Per Roblox, what the company wants is for developers who make their games elsewhere to be able to bring them to Roblox and monetize them there, as well as on other platforms. At the moment, this specific feature is not what’s being used to get Deltarune and sundry onto Roblox. “Our tools are still in development, so weâre starting with a select group of partners who hold the necessary rights and have a game that has been released on a major storefront,” wrote Roblox in an email to me. “We have a multi-step process to vet applications, including vetting for ownership. Selected partners must then sign an agreement with us before moving forward.”

But that’s just for now. What’s coming is, again per Roblox, a “new AI-based import technology, which can bring [a developer’s] existing console, PC, and mobile games to Roblox. It uses AI tools built by Roblox to convert a game into a Roblox version.” Assuming that the goal is to eventually roll this out widely, we’re only going to see greater proliferation of vibe-coded renditions of existing games uploaded to Roblox without permission. Nintendo, Steam, PlayStation, and Xbox are already having enough trouble controlling the flood of AI slop, including from “vetted” developers. Roblox’s current tactics do not provide much reassurance that it will do a better job of curation. Given that the “creators” of unauthorized ports like these can and will monetize such endeavors, thereby making money for themselves and for Roblox off of other people’s hard work, I’m not sure it even wants to.
