The 12 Best Gifts For RPG Lovers In 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

I think it must be pretty hard to buy gifts for folks who love RPGs if you yourself are not familiar with them, because as I’ve discovered today while perusing the internet for ideas, most searches for RPG-themed gifts get you into the territory of tabletop RPGs rather than video game RPGs. Don’t get me wrong, I love a good TTRPG, but sometimes that’s not what you’re looking for exactly.

This is a guide to shopping for the RPG-lover in your life, someone who you know really likes a good role-playing video game with stats that go up as you level up, a nice big fantasy world of some sort, stuff you can equip to make yourself stronger, probably a party of heroes adventuring together, and possibly some interesting moments where you can make choices that influence what happens next. There are a lot of games that fall into this category, and the RPG-lover in your life may or may not like some or all of these. So as with all gifts, try to get to know the person you’re gifting something to just a little and make sure you’re getting, say, a game for a system they actually own, or a piece of merchandise for one they already played and liked.

With that in mind, here you are! 12 ideas!

Don’t worry, this list won’t be all straight-up games, but I did want to sprinkle a few recent releases in here to hopefully give you some options of newer games to gift that might broaden your giftee’s horizons. Trails in the Sky 1st ChapterÂ is a remake, released last year, of the original Trails in the Sky, which is the start of an excellent RPG that’s nonetheless (I think) overlooked by a lot of general RPG fans. The remake itself looks really nice, and the music is incredible. If you know someone who already has 1st Chapter in their library, 2nd Chapter came out this year too!

I’m not a huge collector of video game-themed merchandise, especially stuff that’s really obviously “gamer”, you know? But I have to admit these towels get me every time. It’s a genuinely funny play on a death screen from an iconic RPG series that anyone who’s touched a Souls game will probably find at least somewhat amusing.

The Adventures of Elliot is another release from this year, and while it’s not my personal favorite RPG, it’s got a lot going for it and a ton of RPG fans seemed to really dig it. Not a traditional turn-based RPG, this game instead has a singular main character, Elliot, who can switch between several different weapon types in action combat to dispatch monsters. It’s got that gorgeous HD-2D look that Square Enix has been using to marry a retro feel with modern visuals, and a pretty interesting story involving time travel to see the same world across multiple eras.

I’ve been seeing these little music boxes around for a bunch of different games, and I think they’re a really neat idea. But for an RPG fan, the absolute safest and best bet for a theme they’ll recognize is one from Final Fantasy. FF7 has several different ones, including both Tifa and Aerith’s themes, and there are more on the Square Enix store if you want to check that out or look for a music box for a specific game.

I tried to recommend several different kinds of RPGs on this list, depending on what the RPG gamer in your life most often plays. Beast of Reincarnation is a new game from the Pokemon developers, out this year, but it’s a far cry from what Pokemon is. Instead of capturing monsters, you and a single creature partner fight off blight-diseased enemies and explore a post-apocalyptic world using nature-based powers. It’s a game about humans, nature, and human nature, and might appeal especially to fans of the NieR games on aesthetic alone.

Hell yeah I’m recommending a dang notebook and pen. Who doesn’t love these? Seriously. As an RPG lover I frequently end up taking notes while playing games, either to solve puzzles, remember who the heck characters with fancy names are, or just doodling. And there are so many very cool notebooks and pens out there! You can go two directions with this. You can get something that has a very fantastical outward appearance, like this journal, or you could get a really nice-quality, plain notebook (the paper quality on this one is excellent and feels good to write on). For pens, you could get some colorful gel pens (everyone loves colors) or get a little fancy with it and introduce them to the world of fountain pens. This is coming out on December 3, so in just a few days: it’s Xenoblade 3 on Switch 2! Xenoblade 3 is already an excellent game, its Switch 2 version will include a new recruitable character and a quest to obtain them, a new battle mode, newly-voiced event scenes, and it’s in 4K with a glorious 60fps. Check in first to see if the person already has this game on Switch, though, as the upgrade pack by itself to the Switch 2 edition is admittedly a bit cheaper.

I love video game cookbooks. The ones by author Chelsea Monroe-Cassel are responsible for teaching me to cook in the first place! My two favorites of hers are the World of Warcraft Official Cookbook (and its sequel, New Flavors of Azeroth) and The Elder Scrolls Cookbook. All three are fun, easy to follow for novice cooks, and the recipes you end up with are both tasty and unique, often introducing me to ingredients I had never used before. The photography is also lovely, so even if your giftee doesn’t cook, they make a nice collector’s item for fans of the games. If neither of these games appeal, there’s a whole host of other video game cookbooks out there to choose from.

I’m genuinely not sure what it is, but every time I go to Japan for work, someone asks me to get them one of these Dragon Quest slimes. They come in several different versions at varying prices, so take your pick if you know someone who loves Dragon Quest.

Oh my God. These…are hilarious. For those who loved last year’s hit RPG Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, these slippers are sure to get a laugh when unwrapped. They’re a hilarious tribute to a character who was obsessed with collecting monster feet, and they look extremely comfy to boot. Possibly a good gift even if someone didn’t play Expedition 33 on sheer utility.

Yeah, really. I’m personally ambivalent about getting gift cards, as if that’s all you’re getting someone it can seem a tad thoughtless. But if you’re shopping for a kid who’s getting multiple things, or a secret Santa you don’t know well, this can be an okay choice. A Steam gift card will go over well with almost anyone who plays games on their PC because they can buy whatever RPG has been on their wishlist (though you can also just peek at their wishlist and buy one for them). You can also get cards for the digital storefronts on PlayStation,Â Xbox, or Nintendo consoles for those that own them. Take your pick.
