4 Chip Stocks Down as Much as 36% From Their Highs to Buy and Hold for the Next Decade

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Four AI chip stocks trade as much as a third under their records. Which would I want to own for 10 years?

Most chip stocks are far off their highs. As I write, Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -5.58%) trades around 18% below its record close, and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM -6.34%) sits about 18% below its own. ASML (ASML -5.91%) is about 25% under its June record, and Micron Technology (MU -7.62%) is about 36% under its own.

Each handles a different link in the chain that turns artificial intelligence (AI) spending into hardware. AMD designs chips. TSMC makes them, partly using ASML's lithography machines (which print a chip's patterns on silicon wafers). And Micron provides the memory that sits next to them.

For a 10-year hold, I think this matters. Some of these companies get paid almost no matter whose chip wins. Others have to keep winning.

Image source: Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company.

AMD's data center segment sells server processors and AI chips. Showing how fast this business is scaling, its revenue climbed 97% year over year in the third quarter to a record $8.5 billion, up from $6.7 billion in the second quarter and $5.8 billion in the first. Growth from the previous quarter accelerated from 16% to 27%. And the segment now makes up 64% of AMD's sales, up from 47% a year earlier.

That's well above the more than 60% a year AMD targets for the segment over the next three to five years.

The biggest risk, I think, is the price. At around $535, AMD trades at about 30 times the earnings analysts expect for 2027. Even its own target of adjusted earnings over $20 a share in three to five years puts today's price at about 27 times those earnings.

AMD depends on TSMC for all its processor and graphics chip wafers at 7 nanometers or less (a lower number means a more advanced chip), according to AMD's last quarterly filing.

Notably, chips built at 7 nanometers or below brought in 79% of TSMC's wafer revenue in the third quarter, as total revenue grew 41% year over year in U.S. dollars and net income climbed 69% in Taiwan dollars. Then October's sales set a monthly record of around 587 billion Taiwan dollars, up about 60% from a year earlier.

Of course, most of TSMC's main plants are in Taiwan. A conflict or trade fight involving the island is the biggest risk, because it might disrupt the factories AMD relies on.

The most advanced chips rely on extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines, and ASML is now the world's only maker of them.

Its third-quarter sales hit 11.7 billion euros, up 55% year over year. And management expects 2026 sales of 44 billion to 45 billion euros, around 36% above 2025's total at the midpoint.

"In light of their demand visibility, some customers are expressing an interest in placing orders with us for 2029 and even 2030," CEO Christophe Fouquet said in ASML's third-quarter results release.

But the chip industry has historically been cyclical, and ASML's sales rise and fall with it. At around 24 times the earnings analysts expect for 2027, the stock arguably prices in a long upturn.

Micron's link is high-bandwidth memory (HBM), stacked memory chips that sit next to an AI processor and deliver data to it. In June 2025, Micron said its HBM had been built into AMD's Instinct MI350 Series.

And every new generation needs more of it. AMD's latest AI chip, the MI455X, has 12 HBM stacks totaling 432 gigabytes, versus 8 stacks and 288 gigabytes in the MI355X, one of the MI350 Series chips.

Micron's cloud memory unit sells HBM to data center customers. Its revenue climbed to $16.3 billion in the fiscal fourth quarter of 2026 (the period ended Sept. 3, 2026) -- up from $13.8 billion in fiscal Q3 and $4.5 billion a year before.

But memory has a long history of sharp price swings. Micron's gross margin surged to 81% in fiscal 2026 from 40% a year before, and a margin that can double in a year can fall just as fast when the industry adds supply.

All four look like companies that can continue to grow through 2036. But TSMC and ASML mostly get paid as long as the industry keeps making advanced chips, whoever designs them. AMD has to keep winning business from rivals, and Micron has to deal with memory prices.

So, I think all four are worth considering over the next decade, but I'd put the most money into TSMC and ASML. I'd start with TSMC, at around 18 times expected 2027 earnings versus about 30 times for AMD.

I'd add AMD and Micron in smaller amounts over the months ahead. Ten years is plenty of time to buy in stages.
