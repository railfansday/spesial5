Uber Riders Can Now Request to Have Their Trips Recorded

In four cities, Uber riders can request rides with drivers who use the Record My Ride feature.

Starting this week, Uber riders in select markets will be able to specify that they want their trip recorded on video. The feature, which is being introduced in Baltimore, Indianapolis, St. Louis, and Raleigh, North Carolina, adds a new Recorded Rides choice to the app alongside other familiar ride options like UberX and UberXL. Uber says that the new feature gives “greater confidence that a recording will be there if something doesn’t go as planned.”

After a series of rider safety investigations and sexual assault lawsuit settlements, Uber began looking for ways to boost passenger safety and confidence in the service. The ridesharing platform first tested in-app trip audio recording in late 2019, then piloted the Record My Ride feature in 2022, before finally rolling out the video recording function in earnest, first in San Francisco in 2023, then nationally across the US in 2024.

According to Uber, the knowledge that a trip is being recorded gives both riders and drivers peace of mind.

While drivers can opt out of using the feature in their settings, more than half of its active drivers in the US choose to record their trips in the Record My Ride feature.

Starting this week, Uber riders in those four cities will be able to request a recorded ride during the hailing process in the Uber app and, when possible, be automatically matched with a driver using the feature. Choosing a recorded ride doesn’t affect the fare.

Record My Ride has been available to Uber’s drivers, though not passengers, for a few years now. The feature uses the front-facing camera on the driver’s phone to capture trip audio and video.

“Uber can only review recordings uploaded as part of a safety report or insurance claim,” a representative told CNET.

Not selecting a Recorded Ride doesn’t guarantee that your trip won’t be recorded in some way. Many Uber drivers choose to install separate dash cams in their vehicles, often with multiple cameras recording into the cabin. Uber offers drivers the option to register their dash cam with the service, but this is separate from the pilot.

“Drivers can use a separate dash cam, provided they comply with local laws,” an Uber representative told CNET in an email, “but Recorded Rides specifically matches riders with drivers recording through Record My Ride.”

Uber says it’s exploring additional ways to use recordings to keep its riders and drivers safe. It will soon begin proactively activating the Record My Ride feature on drivers’ devices during trips that may benefit from additional oversight, such as late-night or trips involving teenagers. Uber is also looking into automatically turning on video recordings “when trips are not proceeding as expected,” such as when a trip goes significantly off the projected course or if a crash is detected.

Uber says it’s also looking into testing the automatic activation of audio recording on riders’ phones during nighttime trips. Passengers would have to consent to this, which would require the app to use their phone’s microphone.
