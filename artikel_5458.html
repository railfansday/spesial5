The next AI question is who controls the intelligence

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

This article has been reviewed by the Quartz editorial team. The content may be licensed for reuse.

As AI becomes part of everyday interactions, the handling of information shared with conversational systems is becoming an important area of public and regulatory attention. In 2025, the Federal Trade Commission launched an inquiry into consumer-facing AI chatbots, seeking information about how companies process user inputs and how they use or share personal information obtained through users' conversations.

The scrutiny reflects a broader question about the relationship between users and increasingly capable AI systems. The FTC’s inquiry also examined issues including user engagement, monetization, safety, and the potential effects of AI companions, demonstrating that questions around conversational AI extend beyond the performance of the underlying technology.

Public attitudes provide another indication of the challenge facing the industry. A 2026 Pew Research Center survey found that 71% of U.S. adults believed greater use of AI would make their personal information less secure, while only 3% believed it would make their information more secure. The survey also found that 67% of Americans had little or no confidence that the federal government would effectively regulate AI.

That is the environment in which Michael Folk, founder of FolkTech AI, has chosen to build Serena. FolkTech AI describes its approach as local-first agentic AI, with Serena designed to run on the user's own hardware. The company states that Serena operates entirely on the user's machine, that conversations do not leave the device, and that an account is not required. Serena Business is designed to operate on hardware controlled by an organization.

For Folk, the architecture reflects a principle he has carried from his earlier career as a paramedic. He says his experience in emergencies showed him how consequential technology failures can become when decisions have to be made under pressure. That experience helped shape his determination to create an assistant that could remain useful without depending on a remote cloud connection.

“I learned early that reliability is not an abstract engineering goal,” Folk says. “When people are depending on technology in a critical moment, the system has to be there, and it has to behave in a way they can understand and trust. That experience stayed with me when I began thinking about what an AI assistant should be.”

Folk argues that the location of processing changes the relationship between an individual and an AI assistant. If conversations are processed locally, he says, the information does not have to cross an external network boundary to reach a vendor-operated endpoint. According to him, that approach does not eliminate every potential security risk associated with software or hardware, but it changes the data pathway that a user must consider.

Folk also sees continuity as an important part of the assistant experience. He describes Serena as being designed to retain relevant context over time, allowing interactions to build on previous conversations rather than treating every exchange as completely isolated. He also envisions proactive follow-up as part of the product's usefulness, with the assistant able to return to matters the user has previously discussed.

Those capabilities are part of Folk's broader view that an AI assistant should develop an understanding of context rather than simply retrieve information on demand. For businesses, that concept becomes the basis of Serena Business, which Folk describes as a "digital brain" capable of building relational knowledge around an organization's documents, procedures, and operational context. FolkTech states that Serena Business answers from an organization's own documents on hardware the organization controls.

The progression from Serena for individuals to Serena Business reflects FolkTech AI's stated strategy of building a broader ecosystem incrementally. Folk describes the company as deliberately product-led, with each stage extending the same local-first philosophy into another use case.

The company also has a social objective attached to that growth. Folk says FolkTech AI intends to donate a Serena consumer system with advanced educational content to communities without reliable internet access for every Serena Business product sold. He views local processing as particularly relevant to communities where continuous connectivity cannot be assumed. The initiative remains a company commitment, with its eventual reach dependent on execution and scale.

“AI should earn trust through what it is built to do,” he says. “If the intelligence is going to become part of your life, you should know where it lives, who controls it and what happens to your information. My goal is simple: build technology that works for people without asking them to give up control.”
