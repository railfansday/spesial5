7 bizarre things crypto whales spent a fortune on

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The crypto rich have spent their fortunes on everything from dinosaur skeletons to rotting fruit, with some purchases defying belief.

They say a fool and his money are soon parted, but perhaps the idiom should be extended to crypto multi-millionaires and billionaires, who get their kicks from spending their fortunes on some truly bizarre things.

From a trip to space funded with Bitcoin to a $69 million nonfungible token, check out the seven weirdest ways crypto fortunes have been allocated so far.

For some early Bitcoin adopters, the ultimate luxury purchase wasn’t a Lambo or a Malibu mansion; it was a ticket off the planet.

In November 2013, British entrepreneur and Virgin Group founder Richard Branson announced that Virgin Galactic would accept BTC for spaceflight tickets.

At $250,000 a pop, the announcement piqued the interest of some of crypto’s earliest investors. In March 2014, Gemini co-founders Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss each purchased a ticket using Bitcoin to fund the space plunge — which they never actually took.

Bitcoin, space and beyond. Source: Winklevoss Capital

After stumping up 312.5 BTC each, the flights were repeatedly delayed until the Virgin Galactic VSS Unity was officially retired from spaceflight in June 2024.

Tron founder Justin Sun did make it into space briefly on Blue Origin in 2025 for $28 million, while f2pool co-founder Chun Wang actually commanded SpaceX’s Fram2 mission.

It was the first human spaceflight to fly over both poles of the earth. It’s not known how much he paid for the privilege but estimates suggest the four person mission would cost around $220 million.

Everyone’s heard of Bitcoin Pizza Day, when developer Laszlo Hanyecz infamously spent 10,000 BTC on two pizzas, but Bitcoin Banana Day gets considerably less attention.

Yet few examples capture the intersection of crypto and wealth quite like Justin Sun’s outrageous spend on, well, a banana.

In November 2024, the founder of Tron paid $6.24 million for Italian artist Maurizio Cattelan’s Comedian, which consists of a banana duct-taped to a wall.

Sun subsequently followed through on a promise to eat the artwork, holding a press conference in Hong Kong where he took a bite out of the yellow fruit. If it were anyone else it might have been a Dada-esque artistic statement about the transience of worth in a consumption-based society, but it was Justin Sun, so it was probably just a publicity stunt.

He could have just gone to the 7-Eleven down the street and scored a banana for $1.50, but then he wouldn’t have been able to attract so much attention by eating it. The actual work he bought wasn’t the banana or duct tape, it was a certificate of authenticity for instructions on how to tape the banana to a wall.

Some people spend their fortunes trying to live more comfortably; others are more interested in the chance to live again.

Hal Finney, one of Bitcoin’s earliest contributors and the recipient of the first Bitcoin transaction from Satoshi Nakamoto, was cryopreserved after his death in August 2014.

Finney was a member of the Alcor Life Extension Foundation for more than two decades, and after his death, his body was preserved in the hopes that future medical technology might one day make revival possible.

Even better? Finney’s cryopreservation was paid for through a combination of life insurance and Bitcoin donated by fans. Crypto investor Peter Theil and “Bitcoin Jesus” Roger Ver are also set to undergo the process. Ver told Magazine back in 2021 he’d actually seriously considered killing himself and being cryptopreserved to be revived later, to avoid being sent to jail as a 23-year old.

Some collectors want paintings on their walls, others want bananas, and others still want something that has been dead for 69 million years.

In 2025, a group of investors, including Yoann Turpin, co-founder of cryptocurrency market maker Wintermute, spent $5 million on a dinosaur skeleton.

Yoann Turpin spent $5 million on a dinosaur skeleton. Source: Bloomberg

The Triceratops fossil is one of only two dozen or so known specimens, and was shipped from Wyoming to Turpin’s storage facility in Singapore.

Beyond the obvious novelty value, he reportedly said its appeal includes the fossil’s scarcity, which he likened to Bitcoin’s limited supply. One dino-skeleton will always equal one dino-skeleton.

From one enthusiastic collector of old relics to another. Patrick Gruhn, former head of FTX Europe, spent a chunk of his crypto fortune on a gold pocket watch recovered from the body of John Jacob Astor IV, the wealthiest passenger aboard the Titanic.

Gruhn spent $1.5 million at auction in 2024 and reportedly bought the old timepiece as a gift for his wife, partly because of his fascination with the Astor family’s history, and perhaps partly due to an affinity for sinking ships.

HEX founder Richard Heart’s taste in luxury goods could hardly be called understated.

In February 2022, he acquired The Enigma, a 555.55-carat black diamond, for approximately $4.3 million.

The enormous stone, also known as a carbonado diamond, is one of the largest faceted diamonds ever offered at auction, and the theories of its origins stretch from deep inside the Earth’s core to outer space.

Richard Heart bought a 555.55-carat black diamond. Source: Rapaport

Heart later renamed it the HEX.com diamond, connecting the unusual purchase to his cryptocurrency project. The US Securities and Exchange Commission launched a civil case in 2023 alleging that he misappropriated investor assets, including funds used to purchase the diamond. Heart was arrested in Turkey earlier this year due to an Interpol Red Notice connected to separate Finnish tax fraud charges.

What to buy for the person who has it all?

A $69 million NFT, perhaps. That’s what cryptocurrency investor MetaKovan paid in March 2021 for EVERYDAYS: THE FIRST 5000 DAYS, a digital collage by artist Beeple of 5,000 images created over 13 years.

At 42,329.453 Ether, the sale made it not only the most expensive NFT ever sold at the time, but also one of the most expensive artworks by a living artist.

The owner has no intention of selling the work, but it seems unlikely to fetch a similar price today. But he has purchased himself a notable place in art and crypto history.
