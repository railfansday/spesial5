Carrie Coon Calls for Women to Start Their Own Studio as Elle Women in Hollywood Honorees Push for Change

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Coon was honored alongside Florence Pugh, Julianne Moore, Penélope Cruz, Elizabeth Debicki, Rosalind Eleazar, Samantha Morton and Inde Navarrette at the Beverly Hills event.

Some of Hollywood’s biggest stars gathered Monday night for Elle’s annual Women in Hollywood celebration, where the heartfelt speeches toasted women’s achievements while also acknowledging how far the industry still has to go.

This year’s honorees included Carrie Coon, Florence Pugh, Julianne Moore, Penélope Cruz, Elizabeth Debicki, Rosalind Eleazar, Samantha Morton and breakout star Inde Navarrette, who received the Ralph Lauren Spotlight Award.

Pugh, introduced by Dune director Denis Villeneuve, used her speech to thank the women behind the scenes who helped her navigate the industry from a young age. “To be a woman in Hollywood is a bonkers ride,” she said, recognizing the costume designers, makeup artists and crew members whose work often goes unnoticed. Earlier on the red carpet, Pugh told The Hollywood Reporter that many of those women had looked out for her before she knew how to advocate for herself. “It’s the unseen, it’s the unnoticed work that shapes the women that we grow up to be,” she said.

Coon took a more pointed approach in her speech, questioning the systems that determine whose stories get told and who holds power in Hollywood.

“Patriarchy, capitalism. These are hierarchical systems of domination that rely on fear, and in this paradigm, women and girls, like our natural resources, are a disposable commodity,” she told the room at the Four Seasons Beverly Hills.

Coon pushed back against the idea that women’s stories are interchangeable, drawing on the writings of feminist scholar Bell Hooks to describe how fully inhabiting one’s body through art can itself be an act of resistance. Of her fellow honorees, she said: “I see the fight for the living world. I see moral clarity. I see imagination. I see courage. And these are, in fact, the antidotes to fascism. That’s how important this storytelling is.”

She also challenged the notion that women should have to fight for space in an industry where they remain underrepresented in positions of power.

“As my friend Julianne Moore said recently, we’re 52 percent of the population. That’s not a special interest group,” she said. Rather than simply celebrating women’s achievements for one night, Coon urged the room to consider what they could accomplish together. “I’m going to start with a really stupid question,” she said. “Can we start a studio?!”

Speaking to THR on the red carpet earlier in the evening, Coon had expressed concern about the recent departures of prominent female Hollywood executives and what that could mean for the future of women-led projects.

“When women don’t have greenlight power, then fewer female-led films with either directors or female protagonists get made,” she said. “We’re going to see a drop. We know this. It’s just statistics. It’s just math.”

Cruz also had a few things to say about Hollywood’s treatment of women behind the camera. Reflecting on her experience working with Olivia Wilde on The Invite, she questioned why she’s still asked whether working with a female director feels different. “After 30 years working, when are we gonna get to the day where that is not worth asking anymore?” she questioned.

Moore, meanwhile, thanked Jesse Eisenberg for writing her a leading role in The Debut, a story inspired by his own experiences that he chose to tell through the perspective of a middle-aged woman. “He did what female actors crave. He put me in the center of the narrative,” she said.

There were plenty of laughs throughout the night, too, with introductions from Seth Rogen and Alexander Skarsgård, along with tributes from Daniel Kaluuya and Bryce Dallas Howard. Debicki recalled the casting director who helped launch her career, while Eleazar spoke about her mother, who raised her on her own and shaped her understanding of strength.

Matt Damon paid tribute to Morton, who was unable to attend due to illness. In prepared remarks read on her behalf, Morton spoke about the importance of women supporting one another. “To truly be a woman in Hollywood is not about location, but community,” her statement read.

It was a sentiment that echoed Coon’s earlier call for women to turn the night’s celebration into something more lasting. “Nights like these shouldn’t be one-off nights where we celebrate,” Coon said. “They should be the beginning of those conversations.”
