Anthropic prices IPO at $83 per share, giving it a $1.5 trillion valuation

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Anthropic priced shares for its initial public offering on Wednesday at $83 per share, above its initially guided ranged of $70 to $78 per share, and valuing the AI company at $1.5 trillion.

Anthropic’s stock will begin trading Thursday on the Nasdaq exchange under the ticker ANT, marking one of the most highly anticipated IPOs in years as well as one of the largest ever.

The San Francisco-based AI company said Wednesday that it increased the number of shares sold in the offering to 980 million, instead of the 825 million shares it initially indicated it would sell. The expanded float means Anthropic will raise roughly $79.4 billion in the IPO—and as much as $91.6 billion if the deal’s underwriters choose to exercise an over-allotment option.

The IPO will also make instant billionaires out of numerous Anthropic employees and early investors, particularly the seven cofounders who created Anthropic in San Francisco just five years ago. Some—including CEO Dario Amodei, President Daniella Amodei, and Chief Science Officer Jared Kaplan—are cashing in small portions of their equity through the offering, with existing shareholders selling 23.6 million shares.

At a $1.5 trillion valuation, Anthropic would be the second most valuable IPO of all time, behind SpaceX’s $1.77 trillion IPO in June. Measured by capital raised however, Anthropic’s $79 billion would top the $75 billon raised by SpaceX.

The offering will provide Anthropic with a vital infusion of capital as it seeks to stay ahead in the AI race and to invest in the computing infrastructure to train its models. Seven strategic partners, including Google and Nvidia, have also agreed to buy $26 billion of non-voting Anthropic stock at the IPO price—meaning that, all told, Anthropic could exit the deal with proceeds of $118 billion.

The high share price straight out of the gates will put immense pressure on Anthropic as it seeks to justify its valuation to investors and to prove that AI tools can deliver the massive payoff Wall Street expects.

Anticipation for the IPOs of Anthropic and its arch-rival OpenAI has been building ever since OpenAI ignited the generative AI boom in late 2022 with the launch of ChatGPT. With OpenAI holding off on its IPO until at least 2027, Anthropic becomes the first pure-play maker of frontier AI models to go public (SpaceX also does rockets, and Google and Meta both have massive businesses in search and social media).

Anthropic’s Wall Street debut is being closely watched as a barometer for the health of the broader AI industry. The IPO comes as the AI boom is in a period of uncertainty following a leadership shake up at AI chipmaker Nvidia and growing public backlash to AI data centers. High profile incidents of rogue AI agents, by OpenAI as well as Anthropic, have added to the unease over the technology.

“I think if we do this right we can create an age of miracles, where humanity is able to solve problems that have eluded us for millennia,” said Amodei, the CEO, in a video the company posted online for its IPO investor roadshow.

Amodei, once the VP of research at OpenAI, left to cofound Anthropic at the end of 2020, along with sister Daniella and several other OpenAI employees, when they became disillusioned with OpenAI’s approach to AI safety. Still, the company’s IPO prospectus is replete with warnings of the potential dangers posed by artificial intelligence including “catastrophic or existential risks to humanity.”

Anthropic’s revenue has soared thanks to strong demand from business customers, a market in which Anthropic has been especially successfull. In the nine months ending September 30, Anthropic generated $33.2 billion in revenue, up from $2.5 billion in the same period a year ago. The company posted a $104 billion net loss in its most recent nine-month period, though the bulk of the red ink is because of the way it accounted for its convertible notes.

“Anthropic will need to sustain rapid growth while managing the enormous cost of developing and running its models, and there is still considerable uncertainty around how the competitive landscape and economics of generative AI will evolve,” said Morningstar analyst Malik Ahmed Khan in an emailed statement on Wednesday.

Despite the risks, and the already massive valuation for a newly public company, Khan said he believed Anthropic’s fair value is actually closer to $92 per share. “Our valuation reflects the significant growth we expect from AI adoption and Anthropic’s position at the frontier of model development, particularly in areas such as coding and cybersecurity,” Khan said.

When the stock begins trading, likely around midday on Thursday, investors will finally have their chance to opine on the “fair” share price.

Alexei Oreskovic is the Tech editor at Fortune.
