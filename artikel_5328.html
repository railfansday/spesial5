Coca-Cola CFO John Murphy to retire. Here are his 3 pieces of career advice after nearly 40 years at the company

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Good morning. CFO turnover continues across the Fortune 500, and one of the latest changes is at The Coca-Cola Company, where longtime finance chief John Murphy is preparing to retire after nearly four decades at the beverage giant.

Murphy, who has served as CFO since 2019 and president since 2022, will step down from his CFO role on April 2. He will remain president until his retirement later that year, although a specific date has not yet been announced.

To succeed Murphy, Coca-Cola has tapped another Fortune 500 finance chief: David Elkins, who has served as executive vice president and CFO of Bristol Myers Squibb since 2019. His previous roles include positions at Johnson & Johnson and AstraZeneca. At Coca-Cola, Elkins will oversee finance, global strategy and enterprise services.

Elkins has a “track record of success across complex, global companies,” Coca-Cola CEO Henrique Braun said in the company’s announcement Monday. Braun also highlighted Elkins’ ability to develop future leaders.

Murphy, meanwhile, has been a transformational leader during his 38 years at Coca-Cola, Braun said.

Although Coca-Cola selected an external candidate for its CFO role, Braun is a product of the company’s own leadership pipeline. He became CEO in March after spending 30 years at Coca-Cola, reflecting the company’s history of developing leaders from within.

Murphy, 64, joined the company in 1988 and held a variety of general management, finance and strategic planning positions around the world. His roles included vice president of business systems for Coca-Cola North America, executive vice president and CFO of Coca-Cola Japan, and vice president of strategic planning for the company.

I interviewed Murphy in September to discuss Coca-Cola’s planned $10 billion infrastructure investment from 2026 through 2030, which will span multiple projects across its system. Coca-Cola contributed $85 billion to U.S. GDP in 2025, or roughly $10 million every hour, according to an independent study commissioned by the company.

After our conversation about the investment, I asked Murphy what career advice he would share with people early in their careers, drawing from his own experience at Coca-Cola.

“The first thing I say to younger folks is to have the courage to get uncomfortable. I think you grow and learn more in positions where you are uncomfortable than when it’s easy, and sometimes it’s a tough decision for younger folks to make. Do that a few times, and you then start to differentiate yourself from those around you.”

“The second thing I would encourage people to do is focus on today—and tomorrow will take care of itself. In other words, do the very best that you can with what’s on your agenda for today, and don’t worry too much about what comes tomorrow or next week. And then, in a strange way, the better you do today, the better tomorrow and next week and next month will be.”

“The last thing I say to people is, from early on, build your network. You can never have too many people in your network. And the last time I checked—I’ve never yet been punished for being nice to people.”

As Murphy prepares to close a nearly four-decade career at Coca-Cola, his advice is a reminder that career growth isn’t necessarily about having a perfect plan. It’s about being willing to take on challenges, doing today’s work well and building relationships along the way.

The annual survey released by the National Retail Federation and conducted by Prosper Insights and Analytics, estimates that 182.7 million consumers plan to shop over Thanksgiving weekend. That is slightly below last year's 186.9 million but still historically elevated.

Holiday shopping keeps starting earlier: 60% of shoppers begin by the start of November, up from 56% before the pandemic. Black Friday is still the most popular day, with 69% (126 million) planning to shop. Of those, 89% plan to shop online and 67% in stores. Cyber Monday follows at 36% (66.1 million).

Almost half of respondents said deals are the top motivator, ahead of tradition (26%). About eighty-two percent said they are taking steps to control costs, such as buying cheaper gifts or buying for fewer people. About 59% plan to use AI for holiday shopping, mostly for product recommendations, gift ideas, and price comparison.
