Shai Gilgeous-Alexander lost a trash-talk battle to a Pacers bench warmer

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander is one of 14 players in NBA history to win back-to-back MVPs. Jalen Slawson doesn't even have a real roster spot on the Indiana Pacers, instead playing on a "two-way" deal designed to shuffle him between the G League and the big leagues. On Monday night, two unlikely adversaries produced some of the best trash talk of the NBA season, and somehow Slawson came out on top.

The Pacers beat the Thunder, 125-120, in a 2025 NBA Finals rematch for two teams missing some of their best players. Indiana continues to be without Tyrese Haliburton as he works his way back from a hamstring injury. OKC is trying to survive long-term injuries to Jalen Williams, Chet Holmgren, and Cason Wallace.

The trash talk started at the end of the second quarter when Slawson had an incredible block on an SGA drive to the rim. When Slawson hit a three over an SGA contest two possessions later, he immediately started taunting the two-time MVP again. Gilgeous-Alexander would strike back before the end of the half by hitting a three over Slawson and immediately doing the same celebration he had just done to him. Watch the sequence here:

Slawson and the Pacers would get the last laugh even though he didn't score in the fourth quarter. SGA only shot 3-of-8 from the floor in the final period for seven points, and also had two turnovers. SGA finished the night with 38 points on 13-of-30 shooting with seven assists. Slawson had 16 points, 10 rebounds, and four blocks in 20 minutes off the bench.

Slawson was on our list of players who looked too good for Las Vegas Summer League this year. He's a big, athletic forward out of Furman who can get downhill and provide some supplemental rim protection, and he's been making steady progress with his three-point shot over the last couple years in the G League.

With so much attrition on both sides, players are being forced into unnatural roles. For Gilgeous-Alexander, that means putting up the highest usage rate of his career, a ridiculous 37%. For Slawson, that means playing real NBA minutes for the first time in his career. For one night, it produced the most unlikely showdown possible.
