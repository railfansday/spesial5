The Bronco Raptor takes the already highly capable Bronco platform and cranks up the intensity across the board: the twin-turbo 3.0-liter V-6 is good for 418 ponies, the beefed-up suspension features Fox adaptive dampers and a slightly wider track, and it rolls on standard 37-inch BFGoodrich K02 all-terrain tires. If that sounds like the ideal recipe for blasting through the desert, performing low-level aerial maneuvers, and breaking trails, well, that’s the point.

But arguably the most impressive thing about the Bronco Raptor is how well it settles down in less demanding situations—like your drive home from the off-road park. There’s room for five, it comes equipped with contemporary technology and infotainment, and it looks the business whether you’re in the urban jungle or the real one.

The 2027 Bronco Raptor is largely unchanged; minor standard updates include ambient footwell lighting and a 5G modem.

Ford didn't put a V-8 in the Bronco Raptor, but it did manage to coax 418 horsepower from the twin-turbo 3.0-liter V-6 under the hood. The engine pairs exclusively with a 10-speed automatic transmission and an advanced four-wheel-drive system that works in concert with selectable drive modes named with the clever acronym "G.O.A.T." (Goes Over Any Type of Terrain).

The Bronco Raptor has a beefier suspension setup than the standard SUV, with a wider track, Fox adaptive dampers from the F-150 Raptor, and standard 37-inch BFGoodrich K02 all-terrain tires—all of which endow it with better high-speed stability for fast desert running, high-jumping sand dunes, and powering through rough off-road trails.

During our test drive, we were impressed with the Bronco Raptor's off-road prowess, both when bounding over bumpy terrain at speed and while crawling through rocky passes with the low-range gearing engaged.

In Comparison: While the Bronco Raptor’s twin-turbo 3.0-liter V-6 doesn't have the same shove as the F-150 Raptor's boosted 3.5-liter, it has enough punch to get the Bronco Raptor to 60 mph in 5.6 seconds. The F-150 Raptor hits the mark in 5.2 seconds.

In Comparison: The last Jeep Wrangler 392 we tested left the Bronco Raptor in the dust, hitting 60 mph in 4.0 seconds.

In Comparison: The Raptor can tow more than the regular Bronco—which has a maximum tow rating of 3500 pounds—but it’s still no match for Jeep’s and Toyota’s 4x4s. A V-8 Wrangler can tug a 5,000-pound trailer behind its bumper, while the 4Runner is good for 6000 pounds.

The Ford Bronco Raptor earns EPA ratings of 15 mpg city, 16 mpg highway, and 15 mpg combined. On our 75-mph highway fuel-economy route, the Bronco Raptor surprised us by returning 17 mpg—one mpg more than expected. For more information about the Bronco Raptor's fuel economy, visit the EPA's website.

In Comparison: Compared to the 470-hp 6.4-liter V-8 Jeep Wrangler 392, the Bronco Raptor has a marginal two-mpg advantage in city driving; both are EPA-rated for 16-mpg highway.

First Row: The extra-capable Raptor shares its purposeful interior design and its dimensions with the regular Bronco and comes standard with rubberized flooring, marine-grade vinyl upholstery, and a set of overhead auxiliary toggle switches. Those who want a more luxurious cabin can opt for leather-trimmed seats.

Second Row: The Bronco Raptor is a four-door-only proposition, and the second row offers more leg-, shoulder, and hip room than the two-door versions. Two adults will be perfectly happy sitting in the back of the Bronco Raptor, but even with the additional space, three would be a squeeze.

Cargo Capacity: The cargo hold is pretty sizable, with a claimed volume of 38.3 cubic feet behind the second row. It can fit all the SUV’s removable doors behind the back seats, but we recommend using the available protective bags to keep them safe, and they consume every inch of the cargo area when doing so.

The Bronco Raptor has a 12.0-inch vertically oriented touchscreen, running Ford's latest Sync 4 software. The infotainment system supports wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto functions, too. A one-time purchase activates seven years of unlimited Wi-Fi hotspot service, connected navigation, audio and video streaming, Alexa voice assistant, and other entertainment options. There's a rack built into the top of its dashboard for mounting smartphones and GoPros, which can be plugged into nearby 12-volt outlets; there’s also a 120-volt household outlet.

The Bronco Raptor comes standard with lots of driver-assistance technology, from blind-spot monitoring to lane-keeping assist to adaptive cruise control.

For information about the Bronco Raptor’s crash-test results, visit the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) and Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS) websites.

Ford offers competitive limited and powertrain warranties. However, the company doesn’t provide complimentary scheduled maintenance, whereas Jeep does.
