House Ethics panel adopts changes aimed at speeding up investigations

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Ethics Chairman Michael Guest, left, and ranking member Mark DeSaulnier arrive for a committee meeting on Capitol Hill in April. (Tom Williams/CQ Roll Call)

In a year roiled by sexual misconduct allegations in the House, the Ethics Committee approved a series of changes Thursday that its members say are intended to improve how the panel handles its investigations.

In a rare public meeting lasting less than 10 minutes, the committee discussed proposed tweaks aimed at speeding up its processes, after some observers have criticized its pace.

“My problem with the Ethics Committee is it just takes too damn long,” Rep. Madeleine Dean, D-Pa., said earlier this year.

The internal rule changes were adopted in a voice vote by the committee, which is evenly divided between Republicans and Democrats.

“We believe that these rule changes will provide more transparency. They will expedite our investigative process while maintaining protections for witnesses and victims, while also making sure that … due process is protected,” Chair Michael Guest, R-Miss., said during the meeting.

When former Reps. Eric Swalwell, D-Calif., and Tony Gonzales, R-Texas, resigned earlier this year amid misconduct allegations, it sparked outrage as some called to rethink how the House polices bad behavior. Both men were on the radar of the Ethics Committee at the time, but such investigations have tended to move slowly.

“I want the Ethics Committee to move faster,” said panel member Rep. Suhas Subramanyam, D-Va., back in April.

The committee did not immediately make available the exact text of the changes it adopted Thursday. But a summary sent in a news release earlier this week included one proposal aimed at “consolidating deadlines” for a member to respond to an investigative subcommittee’s findings. Another tweak aimed at speeding things up would give the chair and ranking member subpoena power on behalf of the committee, rather than requiring a full committee vote.

The summary described “codifying the Committee’s policy of publicly disclosing any findings that a Member engaged in sexual harassment or other sexual misconduct” while “prohibiting publication of the identity of any victim.”

Also on the list: “Expanding the circumstances under which fines and denials of privileges are considered an appropriate sanction to include any abuses of position.”

It comes as women on both sides of the aisle have formed a partnership aimed at reviewing how Congress deals with sexual misconduct and suggesting concrete ideas for an overhaul. Rep. Teresa Leger Fernández, D-N.M., has been co-leading that effort as the chair of the Democratic Women’s Caucus alongside Republican Women’s Caucus head Kat Cammack, R-Fla.

According to Leger Fernández, the Ethics Committee has “consulted” with her group as it weighed the changes to its internal rules.

“The Ethics Committee was clearly listening to women staffers, and has been a great partner in our work. We appreciate how they’ve consulted DWC and that these proposals will improve transparency and help protect the identities of those who report alleged misconduct. Together with RWC, we will continue to work on additional legislation in our sexual misconduct reform agenda,” she said in a statement earlier this week.

Cammack said that, while “the entire system on Capitol Hill has been designed to create a chilling effect for victims,” the Ethics committee rule changes would help.

“Putting public disclosure of misconduct findings in writing, while protecting every victim’s identity, gives staffers a commitment they can count on. Faster investigations mean they get answers in time to matter,” Cammack said in a statement. “This is exactly the kind of change our bipartisan task force has been pushing for.”

Ethics ranking member Mark DeSaulnier, D-Calif., said everyone in the House should have “zero tolerance” for sexual harassment.

“We’ve worked very hard to look at ways that we could make the committee’s process fair, transparent and accountable, and I think we’ve done that,” he said at Thursday’s meeting.
