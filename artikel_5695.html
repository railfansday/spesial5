Next-Generation Oral SERD Approved for Breast Cancer

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

— Combination of giredestrant plus everolimus reduced risk of progression or death by 62%

The FDA approved the next-generation oral selective estrogen receptor degrader (SERD) giredestrant (Wilserdly) in combination with everolimus for previously treated estrogen receptor (ER)-positive, HER2-negative, ESR1-mutated breast cancer.

The all-oral combination is indicated for patients with locally advanced or metastatic cancer whose disease recurred during or after completion of endocrine-based adjuvant therapy or progressed on at least one endocrine-based regimen in the metastatic setting.

"As oncologists we are always looking for better and more effective treatments for patients with metastatic breast cancer, particularly when their disease progresses on endocrine therapies and CDK4/6 inhibitors," said Erica Mayer, MD, MPH, of the Dana-Farber Cancer Institute in Boston, in a press release. "Today's FDA approval of giredestrant gives patients an important new therapeutic option, which substantially improves disease control while also providing the convenience of an all-oral regimen."

Approval was based on results from the global phase III evERA study, which evaluated giredestrant plus everolimus versus standard endocrine therapy plus everolimus in patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer, more than half of whom had ESR1 mutations.

The study showed that among those with ESR1-mutated cancer, median progression-free survival (PFS) nearly doubled from 5.5 months with standard endocrine therapy to 10.0 months with giredestrant and everolimus (HR 0.38, 95% CI 0.27-0.54, P<0.001).

In the overall study population, median PFS was 8.8 months for patients receiving giredestrant plus everolimus (HR 0.56, 95% CI 0.44-0.71, P<0.001).

The most common adverse reactions in patients receiving giredestrant plus everolimus were stomatitis, rash, fatigue, diarrhea, musculoskeletal pain, pneumonitis, and nausea, while the most common laboratory abnormalities were decreased hemoglobin, lymphocytes, white blood cell count, neutrophils, platelets, calcium, potassium, and sodium, and increased aspartate transaminase, alanine transaminase, and creatinine.

Prescribing information includes warnings and precautions for bradycardia and embryo-fetal toxicity.

The FDA also authorized the FoundationOne Liquid CDx assay as a companion diagnostic device to identify patients with breast cancer with ESR1 mutations.
