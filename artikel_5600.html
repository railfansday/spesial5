NAR moves from survival to ‘showing receipts’ on member value

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

NAR leaders used the 2026 NXT board meeting to outline year 1 of the 2026 to 2028 strategic plan. Updates included outreach to 450+ MLSs, Tuccori settlement participation, MLS policy changes, advocacy wins and education platform upgrades.  AI Summary

A little over two years have passed since Nykia Wright was named permanent CEO of the National Association of Realtors (NAR). Her tenure as the CEO of the nation’s largest trade association began in a period of unprecedented turmoil for the over 100 year old organization.

During an address to NAR’s board of directors Monday morning at NAR’s 2026 NXT conference and board meeting, Wright attributed much of the association’s success in navigating this turmoil to 2025 NAR President Kevin Sears, who began his term early, in January 2024 after Tracy Kasper’s resignation.

“I wanted to remind us just how much leadership matters. The two years of Kevin Sears’s leadership was defined by leading us through never before experienced turbulence,” Wright, a HousingWire Woman of Influence, said. “Not just the turbulence of survival versus extinction, which is singularly difficult on its own, but also cultural turbulence. And under his leadership, we set the groundwork for what we were promising we were going to do if we were given the chance.”

For the past year, however, it was 2026 NAR President Kevin Brown, who was tasked with carrying out the plan, namely the first year of NAR’s 2026-2028 strategic plan.

“Kevin Brown’s leadership was defined by the showing of actual receipts. January 1st of this year represented the official starting of the clock for our three-year strategic plan that was authored by the membership. They asked, and he began to deliver,” Wright said.

Under Brown’s guidance this year, Wright said that this was the first year NAR made contact with all 450+ MLSs across the country. In addition to this, NAR settled the homebuyer commission lawsuits by opting into the Tuccori settlement, which is awaiting final approval, added 30 new co-sponsors to the More Homes on the Market bill and the trade group implemented zero-based budgeting.

“I want to thank Kevin Brown and his leadership team for every single week continuing to hold us accountable, continuing to cross-examine our thinking, and for using every opportunity when he traveled across the country to bring back new and fresh feedback as we needed to be considered as we strive to continue to add value for our members,” Wright said.

In taking the helm from Sears last fall, Brown said his primary focus was on getting Realtors “back to business” by helping members “navigate the issues that impact their day-to-day business like financing, inventory, insurance, zoning restrictions and other barriers to home and property ownership.”

This translated into tackling a variety of challenges. Building off the changes approved last year, under Brown’s leadership NAR continued its work focused on supporting and strengthening MLS, but without trying to “run the MLS ecosystem.”

“We’ve strengthened the MLS collaboration through outreach to nearly 500 MLS executives, forming a new MLS work group to address emerging issues, help modernize the policies and shape resources and help members better serve clients and collect feedback to understanding the on-the-ground impact from members and the industry through surveys, focus groups, and in-person meetings,” Brown said.

These efforts came to fruition this week when the association approved four MLS policy recommendations that increase MLSs’ options to claim copyright in listing content, expand local flexibility on data feeds, clarify permissible uses of MLS data and update qualifications for NAR’s MLS Committee.

Beyond this, Brown aid NAR is working with federal lawmakers and MLS leaders on things like modernizing the copyright registration process, as well as advocating to antitrust regulators that the MLS helps increase competition in the brokerage space.

Brown also highlighted NAR’s advocacy wins this year, which included the passage of the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act and the expanded use of newer credit scoring models by Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, and the Federal Housing Administration (FHA).

Looking ahead, Brown said NAR remains focused on the implementation of the ROAD Act and working on gaining more support for the More Homes on the Market Act, which would update the capital gains tax exclusion on the sale of a primary residence.

The past year also saw NAR work to improve  its education for agents, building a “unified education experience.” This included modernizing education classes, improving the quality of instructors and creating an improved digital learning experience with a new learning management platform.

“NAR is enhancing the Center for Realtor Development so we can raise the professionalism of our industry through education and provide better programs and resources members can use to get to their next transaction faster with greater confidence,” Brown said.

Finally, Brown noted NAR’s efforts to deepen its relationships with its brokers, expanding broker engagement and “turning direct broker input into dedicated programming, peer connections and business resources.”

“NAR is connected with more than 15,000 brokers through state and local brokers for summits. And we’re on track for participating in more than 85 broker summits by the end of the year,” Brown said. “We also organized the inaugural broker innovation forum here at NXT based on the success of the broker policy forum at the Realtors Legislative Meeting. The first broker mastermind event drew more than 460 participants to discuss, strengthen the talent pipeline, modernizing the brokers using AI and broker tech essentials.”

Brown said that this was clearly the type of content brokers found helpful and that positively contributed to their business.

As he departed the presidency, Brown thanked NAR’s advocacy team for all of their work this year, as well as the leadership team, staff and Wright.

“It has been an honor to represent NAR and serve as your 2026 president because each day Realtors work hard to make a positive difference and change people’s lives for the better. I’m proud to be a Realtor and I hope you are too. Let’s keep working together so we can continue making a difference for our communities, our organization and our industry.”
