Here’s what happened in crypto today

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Need to know what happened in crypto today? Here is the latest news on daily trends and events impacting Bitcoin price, blockchain, DeFi, Web3 and crypto regulation.

Today in crypto, Bitcoin whales have added about 121,000 BTC since the end of September, defying historical year-end selling patterns. Meanwhile, the EU’s securities watchdog plans to make it easier to verify which crypto companies are authorized to operate in the bloc, and crypto bug bounty platforms are expanding their use of AI to identify genuine security threats amid a surge in submissions.

Bitcoin whales are bucking the years-long trend of trimming their balances toward the end of the year. Instead, they are accumulating the cryptocurrency despite falling prices and dwindling unrealized profits.

According to onchain analytics firm CryptoQuant, whales added about 121,000 Bitcoin (BTC) to their holdings between Sept. 30 and Nov. 21, taking their combined balance to nearly 2.95 million BTC, despite a price decline of nearly 30% over the same period.

CryptoQuant defines whales as addresses holding between 1,000 and 10,000 BTC. Unrealized profits across addresses holding at least 1,000 BTC have shrunk 81% to $23.8 billion from $124.7 billion on Sept. 30.

The report, supported by crypto lending platform CoinRabbit, notes that whales have historically reduced their holdings toward the end of the year in both bull and bear markets. Since 2017, whale balances have declined in seven of the nine previous fourth quarters, with 2019 and 2020 being the only exceptions.

Meanwhile, dolphins, a term referring to addresses holding between 100 and 1,000 BTC, have slightly reduced their holdings. The group shed about 15,169 BTC, representing 0.28% of its holdings.

The European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) is preparing an overhaul of its public crypto register nearly two years after its launch.

The European Union’s securities watchdog plans to replace its manual interim register under the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA) with a searchable web-based platform around mid-2027, an ESMA spokesperson told Cointelegraph.

The new system will also maintain a version history for individual white papers to provide archives of changes.

MiCA’s transitional period ended in July, which meant that unauthorized crypto service providers had to wind down their EU operations or face multimillion-euro fines or other enforcement action. ESMA’s interim register serves as a public reference point for checking whether a crypto company is authorized in the bloc.

Crypto bug bounty platforms have expanded their use of artificial intelligence to keep up with a growing mountain of AI-assisted security reports.

Crypto-focused HackenProof said in October that it was using AI to triage 70% of submissions fully autonomously, aiming to hit 85% by the end of 2026. It said it received an average of about 6,100 bug reports a month in the third quarter, 2.4 times the reports from December last year.

Rival Immunefi last week said submissions to its platform rose 136% year-over-year in the first nine months of 2026, as it introduced a tool that can flag duplicates before a researcher submits a report.

Bug bounty programs pay independent researchers to find and report potential security flaws. AI has helped researchers uncover more genuine vulnerabilities, but has also swelled the volume of invalid and duplicate submissions, making it harder for security teams to identify the threats that need urgent attention.

It’s like “bringing a sword to a gunfight,” Dmitry Tokarev, founder of crypto wallet provider Bron, told Cointelegraph, referring to security teams that haven’t already adopted AI to sift through reports.
