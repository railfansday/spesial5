The Winter Arc Challenge Is Trending. Here Are 3 Ways to Crush Your Fitness Goals When It’s Cold and Dark.

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Many people press pause on their fitness goals this time of year as holiday plans take precedence, cold weather steamrolls gym motivation, and “I’ll get back on track in January” becomes a common refrain.

The Winter Arc Challenge urges the opposite. This viral social media trend, which first took off in 2024 and is buzzy again, encourages people to lock into their health and fitness goals for the last three months of the year, from October 1 to December 31. TikTok influencer Carly Berges came up with the term “Winter Arc,” according to an article published in Today.

“The Winter Arc is all about just dialing in and getting super laser-focused on your goals, on your personal development, on your growth so that way by the time of first of January rolls around, you are already coming out a whole ’nother beast,” Berges said in a TikTok video explaining the trend.

As a certified personal trainer and behavior change specialist, I’m a big proponent of sticking with fitness goals year-round, but I acknowledge this time of year can be especially tough for people—so I love the idea of a social media movement designed to help folks stay on track.

For anyone who wants to give it a whirl, I pulled together three of my favorite tips to boost gym motivation from now through December 31. Read on for the simple, effective hacks that can help you smash your winter fitness goals.

When it comes to fitness goal-setting, many people just fixate on their long-term objectives, such as running a marathon, completing a multi-day hike, or doing 100 push-ups in a row. But having a combination of long- and short-term goals is important if you actually want to improve exercise performance and spur positive behavioral changes.

That’s because short-term goals—for example, run three times this week or do 20 perfect push-ups from your knees—can boost your confidence, make the journey to achieving your bigger goal less intimidating, and provide valuable feedback on whether you need to adjust the parameters of your bigger goal.

So when setting up your Winter Arc Challenge, make sure to include lofty goals, as well as smaller ones you can realistically achieve in a short time frame.

Developed by psychologist David Premack, premacking is a concept in behavioral psychology where you entice yourself to complete an undesirable task by promising yourself a compelling reward afterward. For example, you may not be all that thrilled about a 6 A.M. spin class, but if you decide that afterward, you’ll treat yourself to a latte at the nearby coffee shop, you’ll be more likely to get your butt to the gym.

Using this type of reward system can, over time, help behaviors you’re initially resistant to, such as going on a trail run when it’s below freezing, or showing up to the gym when it’s pitch black out, become a habit.

The keys with premacking are that you don’t give yourself the reward unless you actually follow through on the behavior and that your reward is enticing enough to spark compliance in the first place. So when employing this tactic, take a few minutes to brainstorm treats that will genuinely motivate you. Possible rewards? Watching an episode of your favorite Netflix show, soaking in a hot bath, or reading the next chapter of that page-turning book.

For years, elite athletes have used imagery to improve performance in high-stakes competitions. But this mental technique can also help the everyday outdoor athlete boost motivation so they can more effectively follow through on their Winter Arc goals.

Try it with the following exercise. Close your eyes, imagine the challenges you regularly face that make it hard for you to stick with your goals, and then visualize how you overcome those hurdles. For example, maybe you mentally resist going for a bike ride when it’s cold out, but donning enough layers and asking a friend to join you helps you overcome that inertia. Spend about ten to 15 minutes imagining this specific scenario in vivid detail, using as many of the five senses as possible. Try to do these visualization sessions three to four times a week to improve motivation and increase your ability to persevere through roadblocks.
