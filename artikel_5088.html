Claude Gets Personal Finance—Anthony Pompliano Accuses Anthropic of Copying His Startup

Anthropic launched a personal finance feature for Claude on Thursday, letting users connect bank accounts and ask the chatbot what they have spent, what bills are due, and how much money they have left.

The announcement came with an accusation from crypto investor Anthony Pompliano, who says Anthropic tried to get inside information from his competing startup, Silvia, months before the rollout.

Claude can then summarize spending, spot duplicate charges or unexpected fees, explain interest payments, and build charts or bill calendars. Anthropic's example shows a user asking where their money went this month, then following up to find out what remains before payday.

"Access is read-only, and Claude can't move, manage, or invest your money," Anthropic wrote on X. "Like the rest of Claude, Personal Finance has no ads or sponsored recommendations," the company added.

The assistant can see balances, credit limits, account names and types, the last four digits of account numbers, and up to 24 months of transactions from accounts users approve. According to Anthropic's documentation, conversations inside the Personal Finance hub are not used to train its models, but account balances may lag and AI-generated figures should be checked against bank records.

Also, how Anthropic tracks its users’ financial data and uses it beyond model training is not completely clear—and this is extremely valuable information for big data companies.

Pompliano, whose Silvia product bills itself as a personal AI chief financial officer, posted a lengthy complaint on X shortly after Claude's announcement. He alleged that Anthropic personnel began approaching Silvia engineers in April, supposedly because they were interested in the startup's growth and computing needs.

"Anthropic was fishing for details on how we built our product and why it was so popular," he wrote. Pompliano also described an alleged steak dinner involving Anthropic representatives, claiming someone told the Silvia team that the meal was on CEO Dario Amodei's tab.

His engineers declined to divulge sensitive information, he claimed, adding that someone at Anthropic warned his team about the upcoming launch days earlier.

"Today, Anthropic launched a Claude wrapper that is masquerading as a personal finance product," Pompliano wrote. He also claimed Silvia's computing costs are roughly 97% lower when using its own models and hardware instead of frontier AI models, which most likely mean they are using a local model, likely finetuned for the startup’s specific purpose.

That said, Anthropic was not the first general-purpose chatbot maker to move into personal finance, either. OpenAI launched similar bank connections for ChatGPT in May, also using Plaid for read-only access to financial information, and Agents like Hermes, Open Claw, or Grok Bot can also do pretty similar tasks given the proper skills, permissions, and instructions.

Anthropic says Personal Finance is a first step and is exploring partnerships with licensed professionals and regulated financial firms. For now, the beta is restricted to U.S. customers on three paid Claude plans.
