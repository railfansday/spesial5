Happy Thursday! The Block and The Hill hosted the Capitol Crypto Forum today in Washington, D.C., bringing together lawmakers, regulators, and industry leaders to discuss the future of U.S. crypto policy.

In today's newsletter, CFTC Chair Michael Selig outlined how Hyperliquid and other onchain venues could enter the U.S., Ripple's Stuart Alderoty weighed in on Elizabeth Warren's influence over crypto policy, Kalshi's Brian Quintenz discussed insider trading in prediction markets, and more.

Meanwhile, senators urged the Treasury to ensure equal treatment for stablecoin issuers under the GENIUS Act, and bitcoin finance protocol Hashi went live on Sui mainnet.

CFTC Chairman Michael Selig said onchain derivatives platforms such as Hyperliquid can enter the U.S. retail market by registering as an exchange or partnering with an already registered exchange.

Ripple Chief Legal Officer Stuart Alderoty said he does not expect Sen. Elizabeth Warren to determine U.S. crypto policy, even if she becomes chair of the Senate Banking Committee.

Kalshi board member Brian Quintenz said there are far more insider trading cases in stocks than in prediction markets, where the link between a trader and an event's outcome can be easier to identify.

Rep. French Hill and Sen. Bernie Moreno said Congress missed an opportunity to pass the Clarity Act after the GENIUS Act, but differed on how much federal regulators can accomplish without legislation.

Swiss crypto bank Sygnum expects bitcoin carry trades, which involve buying spot bitcoin and shorting futures, to generate higher average returns than U.S. Treasury bills in 2027.
