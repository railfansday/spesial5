Stripe is exploring a tender offer that would value it above $200 billion

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The payments company's offer would let employees sell stock at a valuation at least 20% above its prior $159 billion mark

David Paul Morris / Bloomberg via Getty Images

Stripe is in talks on a tender offer for its outstanding stock, one that would put a value of more than $200 billion on the private payments company, according to The Wall Street Journal.

The valuation would mark a rise of 20% or more over the $159 billion at which Stripe was previously valued, according to The Wall Street Journal. The offer has not been finalized, and Stripe's conversations with investors could shift the figure.

The offer is meant to let employees cash out stock, according to The Wall Street Journal. Stripe has bought back employee stock each year.

Existing investors are expected to buy some of the stock in the offer, according to The Wall Street Journal. In its previous tender offer, Stripe said part of the buyback was funded with money from its own balance sheet.

Stripe has been dealmaking to establish itself as a key player in the way companies work with AI, according to The Wall Street Journal. In August, it bought OpenRouter, a startup that directs user queries to a range of AI models, for upwards of $7 billion. That was the largest acquisition in Stripe's history.

Stripe also bought Metronome, a startup whose tools let software firms charge customers according to how much they consume, and Parafin, which provides loans to small businesses, according to The Wall Street Journal. Stripe joined private-equity firm Advent International in a bid for PayPal $PYPL that did not go forward.

Stripe's Link, a digital wallet that also serves as a checkout service, is among the default ways to pay in several AI agent tools already on the market, according to The Wall Street Journal. Those tools allow a bot powered by AI to buy goods for a person.

Brothers Patrick Collison and John Collison founded Stripe in 2010. The company has remained private partly because that status lets it periodically offer employees and investors a way to cash out some of their holdings, according to The Wall Street Journal. Executives have indicated that remaining private is the better course amid the unpredictability surrounding AI.
