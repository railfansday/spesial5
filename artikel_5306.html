‘Voluntary, revocable’: The fine print behind Anthropic founders’ $112 billion giving pledge

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Anthropic’s record-setting IPO may have created one of the largest pools of charitable capital ever assembled. Its size depends on a stock that has swung more than $30 a share in its first three sessions.

The Claude maker priced 980 million shares at $83 on Nov. 18, above its marketed range of $70 to $78, raising a record $81.3 billion and valuing the company at about $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis. The stock opened at $111.50 on Nov. 19, closed its first day at $97.17, then fell 8.5% to $88.91 on Friday.

On Monday, as the Nasdaq jumped roughly 6% on Jensen Huang’s reinstatement as Nvidia CEO, Anthropic went the other way. Shares closed down 1.4% at $87.64, about 5.6% above the IPO price.

TechCrunch estimated Thursday that Anthropic’s charitable commitments could be worth about $156 billion at roughly $95 a share, where the stock traded early in its debut. The figure combines the company’s Public Benefit Fund, employees’ pledged shares, and Anthropic’s match on those pledges.

Because all three are paid in stock, the total moves with the share price. At Monday’s close, the same math yields about $144 billion, down $12 billion from Thursday. At the $83 IPO price, it would be roughly $136 billion.

Even the lower figures are enormous. By TechCrunch’s count, Americans gave $617 billion to charity in all of 2025, so Monday’s value equals nearly a quarter of a year of U.S. giving.

The largest piece is the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, a wholly owned LLC the company announced in October. Anthropic’s S-1 filing shows it contributed 846.8 million shares to the fund, 5% of fully diluted shares as of June 30, a stake worth about $74 billion at Monday’s close.

The fund can grow. Anthropic says its board may add another 2% of the company for every 50% increase in value above a set baseline; TechCrunch reported that baseline is the $83 IPO price, which would put the next trigger near $124.50 a share. The company said it will disclose more about the fund’s governance and valuation milestones in the coming months, and it has not announced a distribution timetable.

The second channel is employees. About half of Anthropic’s staff take part in its equity donation program, and the company matches pledged shares 3-for-1 for longer-tenured employees and 1-for-1 for newer ones, according to plan documents filed with the S-1. TechCrunch estimated employees have pledged about $23 billion in stock, which would draw roughly $53 billion in matches at $95 a share.

That money is closer to moving, but not yet. Most holders face a lockup of up to 180 days, which could end sooner after first-quarter 2027 results, and many employees cannot sell or gift most of their shares until next spring, the Associated Press reported. Shares parked in donor-advised funds, which have no annual payout requirement, may take even longer to reach working charities.

The third channel is the least certain. Anthropic’s seven cofounders have pledged to give away 80% of their personal equity, a commitment the AP valued at $112 billion at the IPO price. But the S-1 describes the pledges as “voluntary, revocable expressions” of intent that are not legally binding, and timing, amounts, and recipients remain undecided. TechCrunch’s $156 billion estimate excludes them.

The cofounders also face the longest lockup: up to 365 days from the Nov. 18 prospectus, or earlier after third-quarter 2027 results, with a portion of their shares eligible for release around the 180-day mark.

Some money is already committed. Earlier this month, Anthropic pledged $50 million to a donor-advised fund at the San Francisco Foundation for Bay Area nonprofits through 2027. The first $500,000 went to the SF-Marin Food Bank, GLIDE, the Interfaith Winter Shelter, and the San Francisco Firefighters Local 798 Toy Program, and a company spokesperson told TechCrunch the gift did not come from the Public Benefit Fund.

Anthropic has also committed $200 million to study and address AI’s effects on the labor market. In June, it pledged $150 million to Claude Corps, a fellowship that places early-career workers at nonprofits to help them use AI.

Separately, Anthropic has given $40 million this year to Public First Action, a bipartisan group that advocates for AI safeguards. That is policy spending, not charity, and Anthropic says it will not use equity held by the Public Benefit Fund for lobbying or political giving.

The next test for the share price comes Dec. 15, when up to 303 million shares can become eligible for sale under the prospectus’s lockup schedule, followed by another 303 million on Jan. 5. For charities, the date that matters more is spring 2027, when employee pledges and the company’s matches can start to move.

Until then, Anthropic’s philanthropic promise is marked to market every trading day. A return to the $115 debut high would lift TechCrunch’s measure to nearly $190 billion; a slide below $83 would take it under $136 billion.
