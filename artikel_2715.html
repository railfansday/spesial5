Wizards beat Magic for third straight victory

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Naturally, on a night I go to the movies, the Wizards have their biggest win of the season, defeating the Orlando Magic by 20. It was Washington's third straight win, which sure feels better than the losing streak that preceded it.

In case it's not clear from the preceding paragraph, I haven't watched this game yet. The article below comes from examining the available stats.

The Magic were missing Paolo Banchero, but were otherwise healthy. In other words, unlike the last two wins, this one was at least somewhat easy, pretty, and satisfying.

The Wizards controlled this one pretty much from start to finish. They were up 17 at the end of the first, gave back five points in the second, but ended the seriously competitive portion of this by winning the third quarter by eight. In the fourth quarter, the Magic never got closer than 16, and the Wizards led by as much as 25.

Garbage time officially began at the 3:23 mark of the fourth quarter. In reality, it started considerably earlier.

Worth mention that the Wizards are sticking with a shorter rotation. Last night, eight players got double-digit minutes. Deandre Ayton got just five minutes — all of which came in the first half. Which is…interesting.

It was a strong offensive night for the Wizards. They shot 22-39 from three-point range (56.4%) — so well they could get by with just five offensive rebounds. They also did a better job on the defensive glass, with a defensive rebounding rate north of 80%.

The Magic helped with a night of brick laying. They hit 30.8% from three and just 49.0% on twos. It's tough to wins games in the NBA with an effective field goal percentage of 47.7%.

Kyshawn George | This was the actualized George the Wizards need him to be — 24 (efficient) points, 3 rebounds, 4 assists, a block, just 2 turnovers, and no fouls. He hit 6-11 from three-point range.

Anthony Davis | Another dominating performance for Davis against a subpar opponent big man rotation. That's not a knock — you can only play the guys the other team puts on the floor, and it's good that he thoroughly outclasses them. Davis had 22 points, 7 rebounds, an assist, 2 steals and a block. I've been largely impressed with Davis' defensive hands this season.

Alex Sarr | Productive in just 26 minutes — 10 points, 3 rebounds, 2 steals, and a block. Sarr hit 2-3 from three-point range.

Tre Johnson | 11 points on 5 shots in just 15 minutes.

Trae Young | Another poor shooting night, but 11 assists vs. 3 turnovers.

Below are the four factors that decide wins and losses in basketball — shooting (efg), rebounding (offensive rebounds), ball handling (turnovers), fouling (free throws made).

eFG% (effective field goal percentage, which accounts for the three-point shot)

TOV% (turnover percentage — turnovers divided by possessions)

FTM/FGA (free throws made divided by field goal attempts)

PPA is my overall production metric, which credits players for things they do that help a team win (scoring, rebounding, playmaking, defending) and dings them for things that hurt (missed shots, turnovers, bad defense, fouls).

PPA is a per possession metric designed for larger data sets. In small sample sizes, the numbers can get weird. In PPA, 100 is average, higher is better and replacement level is 45. For a single game, replacement level isn't much use, and I reiterate the caution about small samples sometimes producing weird results.

POSS is the number of possessions each player was on the floor in this game.

ORTG = offensive rating, which is points produced per individual possessions x 100. League average so far this season is listed in the Four Factors table above. Points produced is not the same as points scored. It includes the value of assists and offensive rebounds, as well as sharing credit when receiving an assist.

USG = offensive usage rate. Average is 20%. Median last season was 17.7%.

ORTG and USG are versions of stats created by former Wizards assistant coach Dean Oliver and modified by me. ORTG is an efficiency measure that accounts for the value of shooting, offensive rebounds, assists and turnovers. USG includes shooting from the floor and free throw line, offensive rebounds, assists and turnovers.

+PTS = "Plus Points" is a measure of the points gained or lost by each player based on their efficiency in this game compared to league average efficiency on the same number of possessions. A player with an offensive rating (points produced per possession x 100) of 100 who uses 20 possessions would produce 20 points. If the league average efficiency is 115, the league — on average — would produced 23.0 points in the same 20 possessions. So, the player in this hypothetical would have a +PTS score of -3.0.

Players are sorted by total production in the game.
