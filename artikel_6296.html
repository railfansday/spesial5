Why Your Seatback Screen May Be Dark On Your Next United Airlines Flight

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

If you board a United Airlines flight and find a placard covering an inoperative seatback screen, it may be a consequence of an upgrade rather than an unexpected malfunction. United is installing Starlink ahead of cabin renovations on some aircraft, leaving passengers with excellent Wi-Fi but no working seatback entertainment.

An internal United memo reviewed by Live And Let’s Fly, explains that a small number of Boeing 737-900 aircraft, identified internally as the 73C configuration, have received Starlink before their United Next interior refresh.

That installation has deactivated the existing DirecTV system. The result is that the old seatback monitors no longer function.

In other words, the screen in front of you is still there, but your entertainment will need to come from your own device…at least temporarily.

United says affected screens carry placards explaining the entertainment upgrade and directing MileagePlus members to free Starlink Wi-Fi. The aircraft also have Starlink signage beside the boarding door.

With the screens disabled, flight attendants must perform a live safety demonstration instead of playing the safety video.

I’d trade Starlink for DirecTV any day of the week and twice on Sundays…

United’s Starlink rollout is accelerating. Speaking at the CyberSat conference this week, United Chief Information Security Officer Deneen DeFiore said the deployment timeline had been reduced from two to three years to 18 months.

As of November 5, United’s official Starlink tracker lists 677 equipped aircraft and says Starlink is available on 50% of its daily flights. The tracker continues to list completion by the end of 2027.

You can also use that tool to check an upcoming flight within three days of departure. Aircraft assignments can change, so check again closer to travel.

The five aircraft in the memo illustrate a consequence of moving the connectivity installation ahead of the interior work but considering you can stream TV from your phones or computers I don’t see it as a loss of any sort.

I would much rather have fast, reliable, free internet than an aging DirecTV system. For working onboard, Starlink is a far more useful amenity to me.

That calculus changes slightly when traveling with my children. They don’t have phones so the setback monitors are very helpful to keeping them occupied during the flight. Having internet on your phone also does not replace the convenience of watching something on the screen in front of you while using that phone for something else.

But there are just growing pains in the ex-DirecTV fleet and I would not want United to delay Starlink simply to keep the old television system running. Still, passengers should know what to expect. Bring a charged device and headphones if you plan to watch something.

Some United Boeing 737-900s now have Starlink but inoperative seatback screens because the connectivity installation preceded their United Next cabin refresh. The long-term improvement is welcome. In the meantime, enjoy the better Wi-Fi, but expect to supply your own screen on these aircraft.
