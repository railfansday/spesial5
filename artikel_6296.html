10 ways to use points and miles for a fabulous trip to London

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

London routinely ranks among the world's most-visited cities. It is home to incredible museums, a fabulous theater scene, great restaurants, luxurious hotels and its main airport, Heathrow (LHR), is a hub for multiple airlines, making it a great stopping-over point on a larger trip.

That said, the British capital is well worth visiting on its own for anything from a few days to a few weeks, depending on how much of an Anglophile you are.

Despite the strong U.S. dollar, London is still an expensive destination, so if price points have been putting you off lately, here are some easy strategies to stretch your London trip budget even further using miles and points.

And if you have some strategies of your own to use airline miles, hotel points and credit card rewards to maximize a trip to London, please share those in the comments below!

Where you should stay in London depends on the purpose of your visit. If you are in town for business, you might want to stay near the City of London, while folks who want a quieter neighborhood might like Kensington or Notting Hill. Knightsbridge is best for those who want upscale shopping, while Mayfair has fantastic restaurants and boutiques.

London can get dreary and gray during the winter months and crowded during the summer, so your best bets are fall from September-November and spring from March-May when the temperatures tend to be mild and days will include a mix of sun and rain.

The number of miles you need to fly to London depends on which airline you take and which miles you redeem, as well as the time of year since some mileage programs require more miles during peak travel times. That said, you can find flights on some airlines, like Virgin Atlantic, by redeeming as few as 6,000 miles each way.

American Airlines, Delta Air Lines and United Airlines all fly to London from various U.S. airports. You'll also find plenty of flights from over a dozen U.S. cities from the U.K.'s two main carriers, British Airways and Virgin Atlantic.

The cost of a trip to London depends on when you visit, how long your trip is and where you want to stay. The city has options for travelers ranging from budget flights and accommodations to luxury hotels that can cost well over $500 a night. Luckily, many hotels are accessible using credit card rewards and hotel points.

Now that the FAQs are out of the way, here's what else you need to know to start planning your trip to London.

Virgin Atlantic flies to London Heathrow from 11 U.S. cities and using Virgin Atlantic Flying Club points can be a real bargain. Just beware of taxes and fees that can be several hundred dollars, even for economy tickets.

I like using the airline's Reward Seat Checker tool to price out tickets between city pairs over a month at a time. You can often find economy tickets between the East Coast and London for as few as 6,000 miles each way. Yes, you read that correctly — you can cross the Atlantic for just 6,000 miles. Even better, premium economy starts at just 10,500 miles and Upper Class can require as few as 29,000 miles (though it's often much higher).

Unfortunately, you'll be on the hook for anywhere from $450-$1,700 in taxes and fees round-trip depending on your class of service. That's a lot, but perhaps not so bad considering even some economy flights are costing upwards of $1,400 at the moment, saving a few hundred dollars by redeeming a few thousand miles can be well worth it.

British Airways is one of the most generous airlines in the world when it comes to releasing award seats in its four classes of long-haul service: economy, premium economy, business class and first class.

The downside is the hefty fuel surcharges and taxes on the airline's flights to and from its London Heathrow hub. There are, however, some ways to mitigate those.

The key is to redeem points and miles from the right programs. The extra charges will still be relatively high, but you can end up saving hundreds of dollars per ticket — each way! To illustrate this most clearly, let's focus on British Airways business class.

While American Airlines AAdvantage would only require 57,500 miles each way in business class for British Airways flights between the U.S. and London, the taxes and fees can cost between $750-$1,000 each way. The same is true if you were to redeem 45,000-70,000 Alaska Airlines Atmos Rewards points each way – with taxes and fees that can top $1,100.

Instead, you might want to consider redeeming Cathay Pacific Asia Miles. Flights from the East Coast start at 63,000 miles plus $200-$450 in each direction, while those from the West Coast incur the same taxes and fees, but require 93,000 miles.

For its part, British Airways Club would require 88,000-99,000 Avios in each direction depending on which city in the U.S. you're flying from, plus taxes and fees closer to $400-$500. Yes, that is still a chunk of change, and it's a lot more Avios or Asia Miles, but it can be well worth it to save close to a thousand dollars per person.

Another way to save on taxes and fees on award tickets as well as sparing yourself a few miles is to redeem United MileagePlus miles for a ticket on United. Not only does United charge far lower taxes and fees than British Airways and Virgin Atlantic, but if you are either a United MileagePlus elite or carry a United cobranded card, you might be eligible for preferential redemption rates on award tickets booked through MileagePlus.

You'll pay just $5.60 flying from the U.S. to London, in any class of service, and around $200-$400 on the return, depending on which cabin you're in.

Plus, you can save on the mileage portion of your ticket if you're an elite or a credit cardholder. Take these flights from London to Chicago O'Hare International Airport (ORD), for instance. Regular MileagePlus members would have to redeem 40,000 miles for coach, 50,000 for premium economy and 80,000 for United Polaris business class. But if you have access to preferential rates, you'll save at least 10% of the miles and need just 36,000 for coach, 45,000 for premium economy and 72,000 for business class.

As you might expect, London is lousy with points hotels, and more are on the way. I'm most excited about the Waldorf Astoria London – Admiralty Arch and the St. Regis London, both of which should open in early 2027.

I'd suggest picking the area you want to stay in and then doing a search with your preferred hotel programs around there. In the meantime, here are some of my top picks with each program.

The swanky NoMad London opened in 2021 and immediately gained a following thanks to its maximalist decor and popular bars and restaurants, not to mention its central Covent Garden location. It's now part of Hilton, so you can redeem points for stays. Nights start at $550 or 100,000 Hilton Honors points.

I also love the colorful BoTree Hotel, which is part of Hilton's Curio Collection. It opened in a quiet pocket of Mayfair in 2023 and has bright but sophisticated rooms and is home to an outpost of the see-and-be-seen Italian eatery Lavo. Rates start at $400 or 108,000 Hilton Honors points per night.

I love Mayfair, so my top Hyatt pick for the city is Hyatt Regency London – The Churchill, which sits right on stately Portman Square and is turning 50 years old next year. The room decor is not exactly overawing, but the public spaces are grand indeed and the service is impeccable. Nights start at $440 or 25,000-55,000 World of Hyatt points per night.

TPG staffers also love the Great Scotland Yard Hotel, which is part of Hyatt's Unbound Collection. Rooms are on the small side, but you can't beat the history since it's in a heritage-listed building that was once the Metropolitan Police headquarters. Rates start at $380 or 20,000-40,000 World of Hyatt points per night.

For something a bit hipper as well as budget-friendlier, try the Standard. It's in a very busy area right near Kings Cross, but chic, 70s-inspired room decor and several buzzy bars and restaurants will make your time here feel like an upscale city experience. Rates start at $305 or 20,000-40,000 points per night.

Even among quirky Kimptons, the Kimpton Fitzroy is a standout thanks to the magnificent 1900 landmark building that houses it and its central Bloomsbury location. Rates start at $319 or 66,000 IHG One Rewards points per night.

The InterContinental London Park Lane has an utterly convenient location at Hyde Park Corner and features spacious rooms, specialty drinks inspired by the royal family at Arch Bar and an expansive spa and fitness center. Rates start at $433 or 83,000 points per night.

It might occupy a heritage-listed building from 1910, but the London Edition feels utterly of the moment (even though it opened over 13 years ago). Rooms have Edition's signature look of white textiles, wood paneling and minimalist bathrooms. It's worth spending an evening admiring the sculpted ceiling at Berners Tavern while enjoying dishes from chef Jason Atherton's menu. Rates start at $485 or 70,400 Marriott Bonvoy points per night.

Formerly a Renaissance hotel, St. Pancras has since joined Marriott's Autograph Collection and is a favorite among train enthusiasts thanks to its location in a landmark neo-Gothic edifice attached to the train station of the same name. Rooms feel opulent in a Victorian way, and its Moorish-inspired spa is a treat. Rates start at $280 or 52,000 Marriott Bonvoy points per night.

Although British food was long a punchline, the city has become one of the world's premier dining destinations in the past two decades or so. These days, you can find everything from Sri Lankan hoppers and Scandinavian smoked fish to French haute cuisine and sustainable farm-to-table fare.

Prices are high, though, so it's best to maximize rewards on your spending and any statement credits your credit cards might afford you.

Another favorite tip of mine: You can often find discounts of up to 50% at some restaurants by booking reservations via The Fork. Then, just show your reservation details at check in, enjoy your meal, and the savings will be automatically applied to your bill.

Taxis and Ubers are easy to come by in London, though rides can be expensive. So if you do go this route, so to speak, make sure to use a card that earns bonus points on ride-hailing services.

It's also easy to hop on the city's Tube lines and buses, both of which are easy to navigate and affordable, by simply tapping on with your credit card. If you decide to do so, keep a card that earns bonus points on public transportation at the top of your wallet.

The World of Hyatt Credit Card (see rates and fees) earns 2 points per dollar on local transit and commuting, for example, and the Atmos™ Rewards Ascent Visa Signature® credit card earns 2 points per dollar on local transit purchases.

You might be able to use the Chase Sapphire Reserve's $300 in annual travel statement credits toward transit purchases as well as taxis and Ubers since these charges often code as "travel."

No matter whether you spell it "theater" or "theatre," no trip to London would be complete without seeing a production in one of the West End's magnificent venues. Ticket prices tend to be more reasonable than in the U.S. but can still put a dent in your budget. Luckily, you can take advantage of the Chase Sapphire Reserve Stubhub credit to offset some of that cost.

Chase Sapphire Reserve cardholders receive up to $300 per year to use on purchases made through StubHub or its sister site, Viagogo. The credit is split into two biannual credits of up to $150 (activation required; through Dec. 31, 2027).

Among the productions currently available through Stubhub in London are "Paddington: The Musical" (just beware it's mostly sold out except for last-minute tickets) and "Oh, Mary!".
