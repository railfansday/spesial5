'Not a good sign' - Stefan Borson raises alarm bells over PIF's future at Newcastle United

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Newcastle United's owners, PIF, could be on the verge of a "trickier" period in charge at St James' Park in the coming seasons.

That is according to former Manchester City financial adviser Stefan Borson, who exclusively told Football Insider that recent dips in attendance could spell bad news for the Magpies.

Newcastle are currently sitting 12th in the Premier League table during the international break, with Matthias Jaissle stumbling in his first few weeks.

Question marks have been raised over the manager's tactics, with the players not entirely certain of what is being asked of them on the pitch.

However, more serious questions need to be asked of PIF and their future in the North East, with new rumours emerging at St James' Park.

A new study found that at least eight Premier League clubs are, officially or unofficially, up for sale right now, with owners concerned by "ongoing global uncertainty".

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Borson believes that "signs are there" that PIF could soon consider their future as Newcastle owners.

"It's another expensive deal, isn't it? So it's going to be over £1bn. It's another club that's certainly not got a guarantee of Champions League," he said.

"It probably is insulated from relegation, so that's positive. I did see there was a little bit of a dip in attendance and demand for the Everton game.

"You could buy tickets on the day, which is not a good sign. It could be the start of a trickier time at the club.

"And you can't ignore the LIV Golf situation and PIF's change of direction on sport. The signs are there. Now, I don't think that necessarily means that they're looking for a sellout right now.

"But I do think it will lead to probably a more prudent period of investment, a more careful, new strategy."

It is no secret that Amanda Staveley is eager for a return to the Premier League, being heavily linked with a move to Everton this season.

Borson has already discussed the possibility of Staveley coming back aboard at St James' Park and has refused to rule it out completely.

However, as stated, a takeover would come with a very hefty price tag, one that Staveley and her consortium might not be able to afford.

It has been suggested that PIF would want upwards of £1.5bn to complete a sale at St James' Park. That's an eye-watering amount of money.
