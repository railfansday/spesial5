Keep the lighthouse on and the villagers obedient in Glagstone, an eerie island city-builder with Macintosh-style graphics

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Now coming to Steam, following a Playdate prototype

Glagstone's "1-bit" aesthetic is a homage to Macintosh games of the late 1980s, cut with elements of art deco, Expressionism and film noir, but it looks like that mainly because it began life on lo-fi indie handheld Playdate.

In transplanting this eerie, story-driven city-builder to Steam, developers Valerii Krasnoselskii and Evgenii Kirichenko have fleshed out the tasty throwback art and shored up connections between the game's management and story layers, while leaving the crux of the experience untouched: you are the newly appointed governor of a remote northern island, contested by rival nations. You have come here to harvest the island's rich mineral resources, and now, the island is trying to drive you nuts.

You start by building homes and roads, getting fish to the warehouse, and churning out coal for the Crown. You'll also need to get the local lighthouse operational, so that you can see the city you're maintaining amid recurring fog, storms and darkness.

According to the devs, who've written a longer account of their project for 80.lv, it's supposed to feel like a simple sandbox, at first. But it has a creeping undertow. Visiting ships bring both opportunity and peril – admitting them to port is a gamble. The coastline is worryingly indefinite, in ways that can't quite be attributed to the tides. The locals are secretive, demanding and a little sinister. They talk of strange masked rituals, Silver Ladies, Shed Skins, and coves where you don't go during an eclipse. The Crown's orders, meanwhile, make less and less sense, and the waves keep coughing up peculiar objects. Sometimes, you could swear you hear singing through the surf.

A lot of the associated Dreadful Lore appears to arrive in the form of notebook marginalia, item descriptions, and sailor anecdotes that need to be coaxed forth with the promise of liquor. There are also pop-up events that may shape the morale and loyalty of your townsfolk. Those masked festivals, for example: they'll cost you production, but the glint in the village elder's eye suggests that refusing to give people time off for the Old Listening might be… reckless.

It all feels like an elaborate port fable from Failbetter's Sunless Sea. Or a standalone episode from fellow "1-bit" coastal sojourn World of Horror. You probably don't need me to tell you that Poe and Lovecraft are among the explicit influences. The developers – who are based in Belgrade and Basel - have also taken inspiration from the Svalbard and Novaya Zemlya archipelagoes. I've just googled Svalbard. It has very pretty reindeer. Haven't dug up any reports of kraken and mermaids just yet, but I'm sure they're out there.

Glagstone is coming to PC next year. Catch it on Steam - they're accepting playtest requests. Speaking as a fan of impenetrable marine murk, I think this looks wonderful. The 80.lv piece is worth reading through, however self-promotional - it includes some savoury titbits on how they've modelled the play of radiance between lighthouse beam and roadside lantern. Did you try the Playdate original? Any comments?
