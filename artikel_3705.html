Paradox grand strategy Afterworld has a wasteland band mechanic that sounds worryingly like a continent-sized game of The Sims

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Unify your tribe with carrot, stick or many gradations thereof

Much like a huge dust cloud gradually resolving into an army of skull-painted bikers, Paradox's post-apocalyptic grand strategy game Afterworld continues to take shape in the course of developer blog posts. The latest Parablog digs into what I think is one of the game's most interesting aspects – you don't control one single unified civ. Instead, you amass a motley tribe by befriending, conquering, or otherwise assimilating a range of individual wasteland bands.

Each band has needs, satiation of which makes them feel more motivated and thus, more willing to follow your orders and work for the good of the tribe as a whole. Some of the needs are more generic – for example, shelter, nutrition, hydration, medicine, social standing, and security against threats. Others are bespoke, shaped by Custom - another in-game social metric - and by that band's origins.

It appears to make for some alarming diversity. For example, you could have a band that are full vegetarian, and excel at foraging - they won't even eat dried insects, the hippies! You could also have a band of cannibals who have a literal Human Flesh consumption gauge. I see nothing in the latest dev diary to forbid you from assimilating both the cannibals and the vegetarians. Let's, er, not seat them next to each other at the next council meeting, unless we're really running low on finger food.

"All needs can scale by band size, level or other game state elements that make sense," the developers write. "Another thing to keep in mind is that different bands will value different needs depending on who they are or recent events. For example a band that’s experienced extreme hunger will get more motivation from nutrition, while a band on the council will expect to get more standing because of where they are in society, not just because of who they are.

"When all of these things come together, you are invited to pick which bands are important for your tribe’s goals," the blog continues. "Those bands are the ones you will pay the most attention to motivate. When you look more closely at what those bands actually want you will find narratively and systemically immersive choices to make. At one point a band may care about food because you are facing a famine, but then rumors of some dustland horror beyond the tribe borders scares everyone into caring more about security and downplaying their previous preoccupation with food."

The most "work-in-progress" part of Afterworld's band motivation system is managing the potential gulf between an individual band's needs and overall tribe policy. You can, for example, put troops in settlements to make bands feel "dominated", which "increases their motivation, bypassing the needs, and allows you to run parts of your tribe with the stick, while others get the carrot."

The developers are "still iterating on this side of the system, but the intent is for such tactics to [offer] various degrees of sustainability for your tribe, depending on your set up and who your bands are". If you try to coerce a band, you run the risk of them blowing up in rebellion as and when the stick is removed. "This can result in cool moments like you taking down a bigger threatening tribe who’s keeping their lower classes under control through forced medical sedation, but it all comes apart when you find a way to sabotage or raid the source of the sedatives," the blog explains. Before writing this piece, I asked Mark for his take on Afterworld based on many hours desecrating the Catholic Church and/or wine trade in Crusader Kings 3. He observed that the game sounds a bit like playing the unlanded leader of some adventurers in CK3. Myself, I wish I could make some precise references to the Europa Universalis series, but our resident expert Brendy (RPS in peace) left many moons ago to found some appalling indepedendent website or other. So I'm going to swing for the fences and say this feels like a continent-scale game of The Sims, except with groups of irradiated and tattooed, neo-Bronze age pillagers instead of smooth-faced burbanites.
