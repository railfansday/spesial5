Here's Everything We Saw During The MagicCon Atlanta Preview Panel

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

We've got all the big news from the MagicCon Atlanta Preview Panel, which focuses on in-universe updates for 2027.

The Magic: The Gathering preview panel from MagicCon Atlanta brought us a much-needed deeper look at Nauctis: The Sunken Realm, the first Magic set on the 2027 release calendar, and also confirmation of a Netflix animated show.

In this blog, we'll summarize all the big announcements from the panel, hosted by British radio and TV host (and, most importantly, Magic fan) Jack Saunders, joined by head of story Roy Graham, head designer (and vision designer for Nauctis) Mark Rosewater, and Nauctis world building lead Laurel Pratt.

The stream kicked off with the reveal that the Magic: The Gathering community has raised $10 million for Extra Life through the ongoing philanthropic efforts, including Secret Lair drops, specific Extra Life products, and straight-up donations.

It was also revealed that 100% of the money from purchases of Extra Life products at MagicCon Atlanta will be donated to Extra Life; for products purchased in any other way, the split is a smaller percentage. So, if you're at the show and want to pick up some of the collection, your money goes further with a direct purchase!

Mark Rosewater kicks this off by hyping up the move to an underwater plane and says this has been in the works for 20 years. There were a lot of challenges, including how to represent the flying mechanic! The answer... there's no flying!

Then it was confirmed that a new mechanic is coming, called Dive, and it won't be as straightforward as a creature feature.

Laurel Pratt then takes us through how building a set underwater works, and how the team has to use research to represent the color pie. Roy Graham then takes over to talk about The Shallows, the first area within Nauctis. Dungeons & Dragons gets a callout for being the inspiration for this set, for how the tabletop game handles similar situations.

We finally get our first card for Nauctis: Calmwater Mentor, a Seal Monk! It's a four-mana mono-white card, and when it enters, you search your library for up to two creature cards with power 2 or less, reveal them, put them in your hand, then shuffle your library. We also get a look at the card in the Coral Frame, which is available in boosters and collector boosters.

The next card is Goramou the Coral Taken, a green-red Shark that confirms Blood tokens are coming back as a big mechanic.

Next, we're heading to a new area: The Grand Marine, home of the Merfolk and Hominids. We get some talk about the importance of Merfolk and their history in Magic, and how the team plans to fairly represent the creature type in this set. We then get a look at Shimmering Citadel, a white-blue dual land that enters tapped. It also has the Exhaust ability, returning from Aetherdrift, which costs five mana to let you draw two cards and gain one life, but you put a depletion counter on the land.

We go straight into another card, Salinae, the Ocean Mother, an eight-mana mono-blue Whale God with devotion to blue, but it's pointed out that this is the only card with it in the set. Five Whale Gods will feature in the set and be hugely important to the colors they are associated with.

Box Toppers are confirmed as returning for all booster types, and they will be known as Sunken Treasures.

We move on to The Abyss next, and start exploring the hulking, terrifying creatures that occupy this corner of Nauctis. The first card we see here is Tecton, Malignant Ruin, which sees the return of Descent. This 5/5 costs one mana less to cast for each card in your graveyard, has Menace and Deathtouch, and whenever you sacrifice a card, you draw a card. This is an example of a Desolator, the creature type revealed in the MagicCon Detroit ticketed play events.

Abyssal Lotus has been revealed: a zero-cost artifact that you can tap and exile for three mana of any color if you Descend 8. It is the headline card of the set and comes in three variants: regular, Coral Frame, and Raised Foil Headliner.

Following the Reality Fracture panel at MagicCon Amsterdam, we know exactly what 2027 looks like for MTG, including all sets and big events.

That's everything we're getting for Nauctis! Roy Graham stays behind with Jack Saunders, and they're going to talk about publishing updates for Magic. There's a recap of Omens of Chaos, and confirmation that more novels are coming, tied into in-universe sets!

We then get the recap of Untold Stories, a comic series in partnership with Dark Horse, that focused on Jace, Elspeth, and Liliana, but Roy confirms that Chandra is up next!

Mage of Zhalfir is then hyped, with a few pages shown from the first issue, along with some backstory for the narrative.

Kamigawa: Titanbreach then has its Moon Over the Neon City novel confirmed, and it will get a Command Tower promo card with it! Author Akemi Dawn Bowman returns, having previously worked on Kamigawa: Neon Dynasties. It's coming on July 27, 2027.

Then comes a surprising reveal: The Incoming Student's Guide to Strixhaven, an in-world visual history of the hugely popular plane.

Two more books are then revealed: a Planeswalker Encyclopedia and a Magic: The Gathering Bestiary, both coming in September 2027.

While only in early development, Magic: The Gathering is finally getting an animated Netflix series. The show is called Planeswalkers, and it's coming in 2027!

The story team at Wizards has been involved with the production of the series since day one, and based on the early sneak peeks we just saw, it's looking incredible.

Our favorite Boggarts are here to show everything that they've been getting up to on their podcast over the past year. This includes a sizzle reel of the videos they've been featured in since Lorwyn Eclipsed was released.

It's a bit of a comedy break, with nothing notable actually being revealed during this section of the show.

Jack Saunders then comes back and introduces Brian Henson of The Jim Henson Company, who talks about how Squen & Cragg came to life and the experience of working with Magic: The Gathering as a brand.

We then get a detailed look at how Squen & Cragg work, plus an amazing look at the puppeteers involved.

Squen & Cragg are then revealed to have stickers and pets that have shadowdropped in MTG Arena

The show just kinda ends from there; it was a weird and slightly disappointing end to a preview panel that should have been about in-universe sets for next year, but didn't really go any further than Nauctis. We also don't feel like we got the monumental announcements promised in the lead-up, as cool as the Netflix show is.

A good show, but not what it was hyped up to be at all.
