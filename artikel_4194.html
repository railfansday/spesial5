In Star Wars: Zero Company’s case, developers Bit Reactor had some of the raddest sections of Star Wars’ mythos to pull on, tools they gladly toss straight to players. With that said, recently released community stats have showcased exactly how the player base liked to deal with the problems posed by the Infinite Coil: Total Clone Devastation.

Stat sheets like this one are always fun, showcasing how exactly the larger community picked their way through Zero Company and where they stumbled. For a black sheep like Zero Company, which came out of nowhere between other big-budget Star Wars titles, it swung far above its weight class, giving the larger gaming space tactics they haven’t played with since the last XCOM title.

Trick, the resident Clone in the playable roster, was by far the most popular pick, standing as the most deployed operator with a whopping 74.5% use case, followed by Cly the Mandalorian at only 55%. It’s clear that gaming fans still adore the Clone era of Star Wars, with Trick standing tall amidst his same-faced brothers.

Trick’s optional Bond with Hawks is almost the most popular in the game, really setting him up to be the fan favorite. It wasn’t just Operator stats explored, with the larger community whipping out over 226,056,072 battle droids, not counting elites or special variants.

With a genre so focused on how its myriad mechanics intertwine, it’s up to the developers to give every kind of player the tools they could want.

Standing high above the chaff, the Coil Enforcer beat the most players down, while Visser took his throne as the deadliest boss by far. He alone took down over 35,000 Operators, serving as a rather infamous difficulty spike. Through these foes, around 21% of the player base lost their first recruit, adding even greater stakes to the campaign’s end.

When it came to major choices, the community fell in line more often than not, with an overwhelming 97% hiring on Cly as soon as the opportunity presented itself. Hawks’ playstyle also seemed to coalesce for most, setting them up as a Heavy/Gunslinger DPS monster. With tactics like this, its no wonder the community tore through so many.

When it comes to games, Zero Company is doing wonders to keep Star Wars’ head above water after the hectic swirl of news surrounding Battlefront 3. Between it and Galactic Racer, fans are showing up for the smaller-scale Star Wars adventures, reminding the larger industry, as well as EA, that not every title needs to be some massive budget epic.

Star Wars as a setting has always benefitted from feeling truly lived in, and for an entire galaxy to exist, most days need to be occupied with smaller action. Pod Racing, tight tactical skirmishes, maybe some space wizards clashing behind the scenes. While the big releases are hotly anticipated, fans are more than willing to step into the shoes of smaller players.

Clones especially provide a really great niche for games, allowing players to hop right into a recognizable face without needing to give them crazy story significance. You can’t always play as a Skywalker, but you can always hop straight into the shoes of a Clone Trooper, even outside of their era. At the end of the day, every Star Wars game just needs to bring something new, and the tactics of Zero Company are perfect.
