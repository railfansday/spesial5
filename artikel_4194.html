10 Abilities That Ruin Good Pokémon

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

There are some amazing Pokémon out there that are great for both playing through the game and using them in competitive battles. These Pokémon are often the go-to for those who like to play competitively.

However, there are also Pokémon that are good, but they are absolutely ruined because they have a bad ability. Think of Pokémon like Slaking, who has a high base stat total, but the fact that it has the Truant ability makes it so it can only attack every other turn.

Otherwise, this Pokémon is just loafing around. There are several Pokémon like this that would be amazing if it wasn’t for a terrible ability holding it back.

Swampert is a great Pokémon, and one my go-to starter Pokémon line in Ruby and Sapphire. It has solid stats, with it being a good attacker who only gets better when it Mega Evolves.

The only thing lacking for Swampert is its speed, as it only has 60 speed, but the rest of its stats more than make up for its lack of quickness. Unfortunately, it has two abilities that somewhat ruin it. These abilities are Torrent and Damp.

Damp just prevents explosions, and Torrent increases Swampert’s water-type moves, but only when it is low in health. Thankfully, Mega Swampert gets Swift Swim, which doubles its speed in the rain.

I mentioned it in the intro, but it needed to be an entry on this list, because Slaking is one of the all time greatest Pokémon stats-wise, but its ability makes it so it can’t do anything.

If you weren’t aware, Slaking has a base stat total of 670, which means that it rivals legendary Pokémon like Rayquaza. With power like that, Nintendo and Game Freak needed a way to make it so Slaking didn’t destroy the world, so they gave it Truant.

Truant makes it so it has to loaf around every other turn, leaving it open to attack and set-up moves. This ability absolutely destroys Slaking, making it a complete pass in most circumstances.

Regigigas is a powerhouse, and there is no denying it. With an attack stat of 160, this Pokémon dominates with physical attacks. However, it is ruined by its horrible ability, Slow Start.

Slow Start may sound like it simply slows a Pokémon down, and that is partly true, but it is worse than that. This ability makes it so the Pokémon’s speed and attack stats are both halved for the first five turns of battle.

Five turns is a ridiculously long time to have half of your attack, and it basically ruins Regigigas, as it can be defeated within those first five turns by any competent trainer. Just bring out a special attacker, and Regigigas doesn’t stand much of a chance.

A complementary Ability can turn a weak, stat-challenged Pokemon into a force to be reckoned with and here are the best.

Machamp is a fan-favorite Pokémon that is an absolute powerhouse. It rocks an overall base stat total of 505, with a high attack stat, decent defense, and decent HP. Unfortunately, it is brought down by the ability No Guard.

No Guard was the signature ability of the Machamp line prior to Generation V, and it makes it so enemy and player attacks always land. This means that accuracy suddenly doesn’t matter as both Pokémon will always hit their target.

This means that even 1-hit KO moves are guaranteed to land, and if Machamp is outsped, then it is curtains for this Pokémon. It doesn’t help too much that Machamp also has Guts, which increases its attack by 50%, but only when it has a status condition.

To be fair, Illuminate isn’t the worst ability out there, but it has absolutely no effect in battle, making it completely useless to have on a strong Pokémon. Starmie is one such Pokémon that has a useless ability.

Instead of Illuminate affecting anything in battle, it simply increases your encounter rate outside of battle. This is okay whenever you are trying to farm for a specific Pokémon, but it is otherwise useless.

It would be nice for this to be a hidden ability on Starmie instead of its primary ability alongside Natural Cure (which has a little more value). Thankfully, it gets the ability Huge Power whenever it Mega Evolves, as this amplifies its power.

Imagine that you caught a Wimpod. You stuck with it despite its flaws, raised it to level 30, and you were rewarded with it evolving into Golisopod. This is a significant power boost. After all, it has a base stat total of 530.

Unfortunately, the ability Wimp Out, which is the main ability of Wimpod, has changed into Emergency Exit. While the name of the ability has changed, the effect of the ability has not, making it so Golisopod will switch out whenever its health falls below 50%.

This ability isn’t the worst, but it does make using Golisopod a bit of a nuisance. After all, it may be a great match-up against a Pokémon you are fighting, but then it switches out because its health got too low.

From Ultra Necrozma’s stat boosts to Volo’s grueling final battle, these Pokémon encounters bend the rules to deliver memorable challenges.

Gallade is a great Pokémon that matches the 518 base stat total of Gardevoir, is psychic and fighting type, and hits like a truck with its 125 attack stat. However, the ability Steadfast is pretty pointless.

Steadfast is an ability that increases the Pokémon’s speed by one stage whenever it flinches. This is an okay ability, but it doesn’t come into effect very much, which is unfortunate since Gallade doesn’t have the best speed.

It has an 80 speed stat, which means there are a lot of Pokémon that will outspeed it. However, they have to specifically use flinching moves to make Gallade’s speed increase, and if that doesn’t happen, then the ability never gets used, making it a complete waste.

Rhyperior is a hefty Pokémon with a base stat total of 535, making it very powerful, especially with an attack stat of 140. It also boasts high defense, with a score of 130, and great health that has a score of 115. It is a tank, and it needs to be since its speed stat is only 40.

However, the worst part about Rhyperior is the ability Lightning Rod. This ability makes it so any single-target eclectic attacks are redirected to Rhyperior. Since Rhyperior is a ground-type it isn’t affected by electric attacks.

However, the only way this ability is effective is if you are doing double-battles. After all, nobody would dare bring out an electric-type Pokémon against a ground type, unless the sprinklers are on. Solid Rock does help its defences, but with those pesky x4 weaknesses, it's not going to do enough.

Archeops is coming in with a staggering 567 base stat total. This is very impressive, especially when you look at its attack and speed stats, which happen to be 140 and 110. It hits fast, and it hits hard.

Unfortunately, Archeops is a bit of a Defeatist, and by that, I mean it has the ability Defeatist. This horrible ability makes it so that the Pokémon’s attack and special attack are halved whenever its health falls below 50%.

One solid hit on Archeops that takes it below 50% health, and suddenly its attack is heavily nerfed. It wouldn’t be so bad if it could fall back on its special attack, but that is also halved.

Camerupt is a solid Pokémon with a base stat total of 460. This isn’t as strong as some of the other Pokémon on the list, but it is up there. It has both a solid special attack and attack, with them being 105 and 100.

As is the case with every Pokémon on this list, it has an ability that is pretty much useless. It gets Magma Armor, which prevents the Pokémon from becoming frozen. While this sounds good, you have to think that Camerupt is a fire type Pokémon.

If you know type effectiveness, then you know that fire-type Pokémon are super effective against ice types. In other words, nobody would dare use an ice-type move against Camerupt, even if it doesn't resist it because of its secondary ground typing. Solid Rock serves it better, though again its 4x weakness to water will usually be too much with or without it.

This list includes some Pokémon that are stronger than their evolutionary forms.
