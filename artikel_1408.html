Premier League ‘big six’ head-to-head table 2026-27: Arsenal flying, Man Utd flailing…

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

We’ve put together a Premier League ‘big six’ mini league for the 2026-27 season, tracking the results between Arsenal, Liverpool, Manchester City, Manchester United, Chelsea and Tottenham.

The concept of the ‘big six’ feels increasingly outdated. Newcastle United and Aston Villa have challenged the established order in recent years, while Man United, Chelsea and Tottenham have all endured miserable campaigns miles off challenging at the top.

The last time all six clubs actually finished in the top six was back in 2021-22, and it doesn’t appear likely to happen again soon, with the likes of Brentford and Brighton threatening to upset the apple cart this time around.

But the distinction still holds some weight. These six clubs remain among the Premier League’s biggest spenders in terms of wages and transfer fees, and their financial muscle means they’re rarely far from the conversation. For now, we’ll stick with the traditional definition.

Last season, Manchester United topped our 2025-26 ‘big six’ mini league, despite finishing well behind champions Arsenal in the actual table. The Red Devils took 22 points from their 10 matches against their fellow heavyweights, including doubles over Liverpool and Chelsea, with Michael Carrick landing the job on a permanent basis in large part due to some statement victories.

It’s been a different story this season. United have lost all four of their ‘big six’ encounters, most recently suffering a humiliating 4-0 defeat to Liverpool at Anfield. That result proved to be Michael Carrick’s final game in charge, with Mauricio Pochettino appointed as his successor.

Unfortunately for the new boss, his first match could hardly have gone much worse, with United comprehensively beaten on Merseyside. Pochettino has a huge job on his hands, with his side having conceded eight goals in four heavyweight encounters without managing a single victory.

Manchester City, meanwhile, have made a flying start in these fixtures. Enzo Maresca’s men followed up their controversial derby victory by thrashing Liverpool 3-0 at Anfield, an emphatic statement victory that struck a defiant tone following the news of the Premier League finding them guilty of the vast majority of their 115 charges. That stands in stark contrast to their subsequent run of form against teams lower down the table, with dropped points against Aston Villa, Nottingham Forest and Fulham.

Tottenham have also made an encouraging start, taking four points from their first two outings. A late equaliser earned them a draw at Old Trafford before they registered a particularly impressive 2-0 victory away to Chelsea. Things finally seem to be headed in the right direction under Roberto De Zerbi, with a rare victory at Stamford Bridge potentially a turning point.

Xabi Alonso’s Chelsea have endured a mixed start, losing 2-1 at Arsenal and suffering a disappointing 2-0 home defeat to Tottenham before bouncing back with a 2-0 victory over Manchester United.

Liverpool have had a mixed start to their big-six fixtures, losing 3-0 to Manchester City and failing to score against Arsenal. But they produced a statement response against Manchester United, running out 4-0 winners at Anfield in a result that gives Andoni Iraola’s project some momentum.

Mikel Arteta’s champions, meanwhile, have maintained their perfect record with a statement 1-0 victory at Anfield, their first league win there since September 2012 and the first under the current boss.

Kai Havertz’s early goal proved decisive, with the Gunners producing something of a Mourinho-esque defensive masterclass to keep Liverpool at arm’s length. Six points from six against Chelsea and Liverpool is an excellent return, and it shows their big-game mettle after their excellent display in the Community Shield curtain-raiser against Manchester City.
