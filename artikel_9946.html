Keeping and growing your coding skills seems like a useful thing to keep yourself employed in the long run. AI usage has been shown to lead to deskilling, and why would a business hire an expensive senior to push the buttons and make the AI do its thing when you can also get a cheap junior with the same skill set to do it?

Actually enjoying your work and understanding the code in your project can give more meaning to your work than rubber-stamping AI-generated PRs that come in by the firehose. And debugging code you actually understand when the shit hits the fan is easier than debugging someone else's code.

The switching between longer bouts of code writing and code review can contribute to that. Regarding choosing software as a user: it's often been shown that people have trouble adjusting to change. This leads to a more manageable pace of change that you can keep up with more easily. There's nothing so nice as software that's more or less "done", as you also point out in the case of Helix.
