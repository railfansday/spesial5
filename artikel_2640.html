Lions vs. Buccaneers Song of the Game: “Showdown” by ELO

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

For this week's Song of the Game, there will not be much talk about the actual Detroit Lions' win over the Tampa Bay Buccaneers. It was a big win. It was an impressive performance. But it's a short week, and a big one awaits on Thanksgiving.

She cried to the southern windAbout a love that was sure to endEvery dream in her heart was goneHeadin' for a Showdown

Despite a big win in Munich, Germany, Detroit Lions fans weren't exactly in a celebratory mood heading into Week 11. Aidan Hutchinson had gone down—Detroit's second massive defensive loss in three weeks—and with the trade deadline expired, there were no reinforcements coming to save the defensive line. With the Vikings seemingly on the rise with Kirk Cousins acquired and the Bears riding high off the Jeffery Simmons trade, Detroit seemed to be playing third-fiddle in their own division, despite holding the top spot. Another season of opportunity lost to injuries.

It's unreal, the sufferingThere's gonna be a Showdown

But midweek, Dan Campbell shook the fanbase straight. Yeah, the injuries suck. Yes, you can't replace Hutchinson or Jack Campbell. But what good is bitching about it? There are games to be played. We owe it to those who can't help the team anymore to carry the torch.

And the Buccaneers were not a team to bring a half-assed effort. Detroit needed to turn the page, put the jetlag behind, and get ready for a showdown.

In Year 6 under Dan Campbell, the Lions are well-trained in adversity. The Bucs never stood a chance.

But Sunday's game will be just a footnote of the Lions' season, and with a short week ahead, the page must be turned promptly yet again. Another showdown awaits.

Bad dreamer, what's your name?Looks like we're ridin' on the same train

This offseason, Ben Johnson was already being hailed as one of the league's best coaches in the NFL. An NFC North title and an unexpected Divisional Round run in Year 1 will do that.

But it feels like those accolades quickly got to Johnson's head. He immediately declared that winning wasn't enough for 2026. He dreamed of sporting the highest-scoring offense of all time. After convincing the already-convinced with a 59-point Week 1, Johnson and the Bears fell back to reality and have only scored four touchdowns in a game twice since. For the season, they rank a modest 10th in DVOA, eighth in points per game, and 10th in EPA/play—not really that far off from last year's numbers, albeit with three games of backup quarterback play.

Meanwhile, the Lions offense has not been missing Johnson. Detroit's offense ranks higher in points per game (28.8 vs. 26.2), yards per play (6.0 vs. 5.4), EPA/play (0.120 vs. 0.034), success rate (49.2% vs. 46.8%), and DVOA (14.8% vs. 4.8%). Ben, who?

But I don't mean to diminish the opponent. The Lions are 8-2. The Bears are 8-2. The Lions are on a six-game winning streak. The Bears have won five in a row. Both fanbases are living in the golden times after dwelling in the basement of the NFC North for the better part of two decades.

From 2012 to 2025, either the Bears or the Lions finished last in the division in 13 out of 14 years. Now, the two will jockey for the NFC North for the foreseeable future. Thursday is going to be a a great one.

Looks as though there'll be more painThere's gonna be a Showdown
