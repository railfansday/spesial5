Rodgers Breaks Silence On McCarthy’s Snoring And Team Betrayal

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Aaron Rodgers has made his final stance clear regarding his time with the Pittsburgh Steelers. The four-time MVP told the world on Wednesday that he will not return to the team after a shocking benching decision. Rodgers said on "The Pat McAfee Show" that "that ship has sailed" regarding his future in Pittsburgh. This statement ends any hope of a quick fix for the fractured relationship between the quarterback and the coaching staff. The situation has escalated far beyond a simple roster move into a full-blown leadership crisis.

The core of the issue lies in how Rodgers learned he was being benched. He did not hear it from head coach Mike McCarthy or general manager Omar Khan. Instead, tight end Pat Freiermuth called Rodgers to break the news. According to ESPN, he said "I can't believe they're doing this,", while Rodgers answered "'doing what?'" This breakdown in direct communication shows a deep disconnect within the locker room. Rodgers claimed he had extensive talks with McCarthy over the past two days, yet the word "benching" never came up. The lack of a formal conversation suggests a failure in basic respect for the player.

Rodgers went further by describing the coach's behavior during team meetings. He noted that McCarthy was either "snoring" during team gatherings or had skipped them completely in the last week. Rodgers recounted a specific moment from last Wednesday when the coach came in late and began snoring within ten minutes. He woke himself up and rattled his papers before the noise started again. This level of inattention is hard to ignore for any professional athlete. Rodgers noted this was "not an abnormal week" for the coach, implying a pattern of disengagement.

The tension has spilled over into how the team handles its identity and morale. During halftime of the loss to the Philadelphia Eagles on Nov. 22, Rodgers claimed McCarthy led a cheer by saying, "All right, one, two, three Packers." This reference to Rodgers' former team in the middle of a game for Pittsburgh caused deep offense. Rodgers argued that defensive players felt the coach did not want to get to know them or their unit. He told McCarthy to lean into the defensive guys, but the coach replied, "Well, I don't think I need to." This refusal to connect with the defense has seemingly cost the coach the trust of the locker room.

The Steelers offense has struggled immensely leading up to this moment. The team has dropped to seventh in the AFC standings after losing to the Cincinnati Bengals and the Philadelphia Eagles. Over the previous three contests, DK Metcalf accumulated only 60 yards, the majority occurring during garbage time. Injuries to Michael Pittman Jr. and the absence of left guard Mason McCormick have stalled the running game. In the last two games, Warren has recorded merely 43 rushing yards total. These numbers show a unit that has fallen apart without clear direction from the top.

Although meetings continued until Tuesday, Rodgers affirmed he was voicing team concerns regarding McCarthy. He said McCarthy denied the behaviors Rodgers observed, but claimed multiple other players would vouch for having witnessed them. Rodgers asked if the coach understood the strategy at all, remarking, according to Yahoo Sports, "I think he learns the plan during the week." He called this part "disrespectful to the game itself." The Steelers announced late Tuesday night that Mason Rudolph would start the upcoming game against the Denver Broncos on Black Friday.
