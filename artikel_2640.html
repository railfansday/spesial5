Ohio State football strongly believes CFP bid is in reach

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Ohio State football is heading to Nebraska believing its College Football Playoff hopes are still alive, with the playoff committee ranking the Buckeyes at No. 14.

"We can't change nothing about the past," Ohio State linebacker Riley Pettijohn said. "But we can always just learn from it. So, we just keep stacking days, and there's definitely hope."

The Buckeyes were 6-1 going into their game against USC, the final game before the first College Football Playoff rankings were released on Nov. 3. When OSU fell 30-18 on the road, it opened this year's rankings right outside the playoff picture at No. 12.

Ohio State dropped to No. 20 following their 41-28 home loss to Oregon on Nov. 7.

On Nov. 14, OSU snapped its two-game losing skid, defeating Northwestern 28-19 for its first win in nearly a month.

"When you lose two weeks in a row, you don't have any momentum," Ohio State coach Ryan Day said. "You have to build it, and we did that last week. So now, we've got to build on that. ... We're back at it, and we're obviously a different team than we were at the beginning of the season, a different team we were midseason, and now we're a different team than we were a couple of weeks ago."

The Buckeyes climbing six spots in the latest rankings was partly attributable to five teams sitting above Ohio State losing, including the former No. 18-ranked USC, who fell to Indiana. Ohio State handed Indiana its only loss earlier in the season, defeating the Hoosiers 19-15.

With the Buckeyes facing unranked Nebraska and Michigan to close out the regular season, Ohio State will likely need additional help from higher-ranked teams, along with statement wins, to move into the top 11.

"The first thing is all that matters is winning these two games. The guys understand that part of it," Day said. "But also, the committee and people, they're watching everything. They're watching the offense, they're watching the defense, they're watching special teams. So, everything we do matters."

Ohio State is coming off one of its strongest defensive performances, notching a season-high five sacks and holding Northwestern to just 53 rushing yards. Quarterback Luke Fahey looks increasingly comfortable since replacing Julian Sayin, throwing for 201 yards, three touchdowns and one interception with a 65.6% completion rate (21-for-32) against the Wildcats.

However, history is still against the Buckeyes, as no three-loss regular-season team has made the playoff bracket. And this year, the spots are even more limited as a Group of 6 team has to make the 12-team field, which would currently be the Pac-12's Boise State.

The Buckeyes could earn an automatic bid by winning the Big Ten title, but they need multiple teams, including No. 8 Indiana and No. 13 Oregon, to fall in the final two weeks to make the conference championship.

"We need help, but we know there is a chance," Day said. "We have two really good road wins this year against top-15 teams. So, to go get this win on the road again would go a long way. ... We don't know what's going to happen here in two weeks, in three weeks."

Ohio State kicks off against Nebraska at noon on Nov. 21.
