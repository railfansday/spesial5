Global South Forum on ATHIS

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Read the original version in Portuguese here.

Heliópolis, one of the largest favelas in the city of São Paulo, will host an international gathering in defense of dignified housing from December 6 to 8. The Global South Forum on ATHIS will bring together representatives from governments, international bodies, universities, social movements, civil society organizations, and community leaders from countries across Latin America and Africa at the Instituto Baccarelli to discuss ways to strengthen social interest housing policies. The program includes technical visits to five territories in São Paulo on the 6th, followed by two days of debates on the 7th and 8th.The event is organized by the Council of Architecture and Urbanism of São Paulo (CAU/SP), the United Nations Human Settlements Programme (UN-Habitat), and the Brazilian Cooperation Agency of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (ABC/MRE) as part of the project ATHIS en Rede: fortalecendo a moradia digna no Sul Global (ATHIS in Network: Strengthening Dignified Housing in the Global South). The initiative seeks to expand the exchange of experiences and strengthen cooperation between countries to advance the implementation of Technical Assistance for Social Interest Housing. Admission is free, and registration is available via the link https://www.forum.athisemrede.causp.gov.br/ "Addressing the housing crisis requires more than just increasing the supply of homes: we need integrated solutions, consistent public policies, and cooperation between different actors and countries, always considering the realities of each territory. The Global South Forum on ATHIS is an opportunity to bring experiences closer together, share knowledge, and strengthen joint paths to advance this agenda and turn the right to adequate housing into reality," explains Rayne Ferretti Moraes, head of UN-Habitat in Brazil.Program HighlightsThe opening ceremony on December 7 will feature representatives from UN-Habitat, CAU/SP, and ABC/MRE, as well as federal government authorities and representatives from state and municipal governments, international organizations, and partner institutions.The federal government will be represented by the Chief of Staff of the Presidency of the Republic, the Secretariat of Institutional Relations, and the Ministries of Cities; Foreign Affairs; Management and Innovation in Public Services; Agrarian Development and Family Farming; and Environment and Climate Change, in addition to the Caixa Econômica Federal. The agenda also includes representatives from the Government of the State of São Paulo and the City of São Paulo.The meeting will also host delegations and representatives from countries such as Cape Verde, Mozambique, South Africa, Mexico, Uruguay, Cuba, Ecuador, Peru, and Colombia, as well as representatives from the BRICS nations. This international participation will allow for a dialogue between experiences from different contexts and a discussion on ways to strengthen social interest housing policies."Countries in the Global South share significant challenges: rapid urbanization, socio-spatial inequalities, housing deficits, urban informality, and increasing exposure to climate risks. However, they also share something very valuable: experiences, knowledge, and solutions developed from their own realities through South-South Cooperation," highlights Mônica Salmito, Lead Analyst for the Brazil-UN Habitat Program at the ABC/MRE.Among the guests for the dialogue panels are prominent figures in the field of the right to housing and urban reform, such as Koldo Kasla, UN Special Rapporteur on the right to adequate housing; references in Brazilian urbanism such as Raquel Rolnik, Ermínia Maricato, and Nabil Bonduki; historical leaders of popular movements for housing and urban reform, such as Evaniza Rodrigues and Benedito Roberto Barbosa (Dito); as well as members of the Executive and Legislative branches, such as Ediane Maria, Luiza Erundina, Inês Magalhães, and José Police Neto. The program also includes representatives from the Landless Workers' Movement (MST), the João Pinheiro Foundation, and the Institute for Applied Economic Research (Ipea). The academic community will also have a significant presence, with representatives from universities in São Paulo, Paraíba, Santa Catarina, Bahia, Brasília, and Minas Gerais.Global South Charter and ATHIS in Network PlatformThe closing of the Forum on December 8 will feature the reading of the Global South Charter, a document containing recommendations and commitments developed throughout the debates. The ATHIS in Network Platform will also be launched, serving as a permanent space for exchange and coordination among participating countries. On this occasion, the 2026 edition of the publication Vivenciando ATHIS, by CAU/SP, which presents projects fostered by the council throughout the year, will also be released.Exhibition Presents ATHIS ExperiencesIn addition to the debate panels, the Forum will feature the Vivenciando ATHIS Exhibition, which will showcase projects supported by CAU/SP and other national and international experiences related to housing improvement, urbanization, land regularization, and community strengthening. Through panels and presentations, the initiative aims to bring the public closer to solutions developed in different territories and will allow attendees to learn how technical assistance can contribute to improving housing conditions and strengthening communities. Technical Visits and Territorial ExperiencesOn December 6, the program will take place in five territories in São Paulo. Through simultaneous itineraries, participants will be able to see in practice the transformation promoted in communities through ATHIS. These include:Diadema: experiences in housing renovation and qualification carried out with technical assistance, developed by the Housing and Human Settlements Laboratory of FAU-USP (LabHab) and Peabiru Community and Environmental Works in partnership with the City of Diadema.Mauá: experiences that link housing, urban infrastructure, and adaptation to climate change, developed by UN-Habitat within the scope of the Periferia Viva Mauá project and the Pólis Institute. São Paulo - Jaraguá Neighborhood: experiences linking housing and sanitation in the Guarani indigenous territory of Tekoa Pyau. The activity addresses the implementation of sanitation solutions and their relationship with the ways of life, community organization, and cultural specificities of the Guarani territory, highlighting a technical assistance practice built in dialogue with the knowledge and needs of the community.São Paulo - City Center: housing experiences in occupations and tenements located in the central region of São Paulo, monitored by Fio Popular Technical Assistance. The visit will allow for an understanding of the processes of community organization, social production of habitat, and the promotion of the right to housing.Santos: experiences developed by the Elos Institute that link housing, community development, social inclusion, and food security. The visit will highlight approaches to strengthening vulnerable communities, showing how actions aimed at improving living conditions can contribute to building more resilient, healthy, and inclusive territories.Together, these itineraries demonstrate the different forms of ATHIS action and the importance of building solutions with communities, based on the needs of each territory.Why ATHIS?Technical Assistance for Social Interest Housing (ATHIS), guaranteed by Federal Law No. 11.888/2008, ensures that low-income families have free access to architecture and urbanism services for the construction, renovation, expansion, and regularization of their homes. The policy recognizes that dignified housing goes far beyond providing a roof: it involves structural safety, health, accessibility, comfort, infrastructure, and adequate territorial conditions.Despite its importance, the implementation of ATHIS still faces obstacles, such as a lack of budgetary resources, low institutionalization of the policy, and the limited technical capacity of many municipalities. "In this context, the exchange of experiences between countries and institutions can help identify solutions, strengthen local capacities, and expand the reach of public policies," states Camila Moreno de Camargo, president of CAU/SP.It is in this scenario that the ATHIS in Network project bets on South-South and Trilateral Cooperation as a strategy to bring together governments, professionals, universities, and social organizations, promoting the circulation of knowledge and experiences developed within the territories of the Global South itself. "More than just a meeting, the Forum intends to leave behind a legacy of coordination tools and commitments to advance the realization of the right to adequate housing," adds Camila.Heliópolis and the Instituto BaccarelliThe choice of the Instituto Baccarelli as the venue for the Forum goes beyond infrastructure: it translates the principles of ATHIS by showing that the right to dignified housing includes access to culture, education, and quality public facilities. "The Institute starts from the needs of the territory, values community leadership, and invests in permanent actions for social transformation. Holding the Forum within Heliópolis means debating dignified housing where housing challenges are experienced every day and where collective responses are also being built," says Nilcio Regueira Dias, coordinator of the ATHIS in Network project for UN-Habitat.Founded in 1996, after a fire left families homeless in Heliópolis, the Institute was born from the initiative of maestro Silvio Baccarelli to provide music lessons to 36 children affected by the tragedy. Three decades later, about 1,650 students receive free musical training at the site. In addition to music, the Institute, which also distributes 3.5 tons of food per month to students and their families, maintains social and inclusion actions and manages public facilities in Heliópolis and other regions of São Paulo.
