Aston Martin Takes On Dakar 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Fernando Alonso is returning to the Dakar Rally, this time as part of an Aston Martin team hoping to win the event on its first attempt.

The manufacturer has recruited a formidable driver line-up for its 2027 entry, with nine-time World Rally Champion Sébastien Loeb and reigning World Rally-Raid champion Lucas Moraes also joining the project.

At the centre of it all is the Dreadnought, a rally-raid car developed by Aston Martin and Prodrive. The latter will build and run the cars, drawing on its own experience competing in the Dakar.

It has been seven years since Alonso last competed in the Dakar, although his debut campaign in 2020 was a reasonably successful one. He finished 13th overall, having taken on the event after a career spent almost entirely on circuits.

Marc Coma will once again sit alongside him. The Spaniard knows the Dakar better than most, having won the motorcycle category five times before switching to co-driving duties.

Fitting the rally into Alonso's calendar could be an interesting exercise. He's still committed to Formula 1 with Aston Martin, having extended his contract with the team through 2027.

"Dakar was one of the most challenging and enjoyable experiences of my career," Alonso said. "I have enormous respect for this race and the specialists who have mastered it."

Loeb will have Fabian Lurquin alongside him, while Moraes is paired with Pablo Moreno. Across the three crews, Aston Martin has assembled considerable rally-raid experience.

Aston Martin isn't going into the Dakar alone. Prodrive has been brought in to develop the Dreadnought, construct the cars and oversee the competition programme.

It's a familiar name in rally-raid circles. The Banbury-based outfit has finished on the Dakar podium several times and secured an overall victory earlier this year with a car of its own design.

Adrian Hallmark, Aston Martin's chief executive, said the decision to enter the event was in keeping with the company's desire to take on different motorsport challenges.

"There is in Aston Martin's nature a desire to explore new frontiers and test ourselves against the toughest competition," he said.

Prodrive chairman David Richards said the brief was straightforward: build a car that could compete for the win.

That's a fairly sizeable task. The Dakar is notorious for mechanical failures, difficult navigation and stages that can stretch across hundreds of kilometres. A fast car is only part of the equation; it also needs to survive two weeks of punishment.

The Dreadnought didn't originate in a motorsport design studio. Its original concept was created as a fictional military vehicle for Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4.

Aston Martin has since taken the idea and turned it into a real competition car, although the demands of the Dakar have dictated how the finished machine has been developed.

The company's racing experience is extensive, but this is unfamiliar territory. Its GT and endurance programmes have generally involved racing on established circuits, rather than picking a route through some of the world's harshest landscapes.

The next Dakar Rally gets underway in Portugal on 9 January 2027. A 129km prologue around Loulé will decide the initial running order before the competitors head to Morocco.

There are 12 stages planned, with a total distance of 6,805km. That includes 4,341km of timed competition, and the finish is scheduled for Marrakech on 23 January.

For Alonso, it'll be another opportunity to test himself in a discipline very different from Formula 1. For Aston Martin, there's a much bigger question: whether its new Dreadnought can deliver the result Prodrive and its drivers have been brought in to achieve.
