Future Eagles Fits: Jeremiah Smith headlines an explosive 2027 WR class

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Future Eagles Fits: Jeremiah Smith headlines an explosive 2027 WR class originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

Welcome back to Future Eagles Fits, our weekly deep dive into the prospects who could eventually find their way to Philadelphia.

Every week, we're going inside the tape and looking at prospects through an Eagles-centric lens — breaking down the athletic profile, positional versatility and scheme fit that could ultimately determine whether a player makes sense in Philly. Along the way, we'll examine how prospects are being evaluated across the league as draft boards begin to take shape and conversations are had with representatives across the scouting landscape.

For the Eagles, it's been a mixed bag on the outside this fall. DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks have missed significant time, Darius Cooper has shown some flashes albeit in small spurts, but rookie Makai Lemon, who currently leads the team in targets, receptions and receiving yards through the first nine weeks of the season, has done an excellent job all things considered. The first-year wideout has been a focal point in the offense and looks like a legitimate complementary WR2 moving forward. Still, however, there are questions about the overall composition of the group and whether Philly has enough explosiveness on the outside to consistently threaten defenses at all three levels. You know what Smith offers, and Lemon has provided a reliable presence, but adding another dynamic playmaker with the ability to create separation, stretch the field and generate yards after the catch could give this offense another dimension.

Currently, the Eagles hold the fifth overall pick in the 2027 NFL Draft, along with the 25th overall pick following the trade of Jordan Davis to Jacksonville. That gives Howie Roseman unique flexibility at the top of the draft, particularly if Philadelphia wants to make another significant investment in the receiver position after drafting Lemon on Night 1 last spring. Whether it's targeting a potential franchise-caliber weapon with an early first-round selection or finding another explosive playmaker later in the round, there are several intriguing options to consider.

While there is still a ton of football left to be played and the entirety of the pre-draft process still to play out – which will alter draft boards – here are six pass-catchers to know in the upcoming class — two potential Day 1 targets, two Day 2 prospects and two Day 3 options who could provide some pop on the outside.

Day 1: Jeremiah Smith, Ohio State | 6'3″, 225 lbs, estimated 4.43 40 time (via summer testing)

If the Eagles are fortunate enough to have Jeremiah Smith available with the fifth overall selection, sprint the card up to the podium.

Simply put, Smith is as elite a wide receiver prospect that the NFL has seen since the likes of Julio Jones coming out of Alabama years ago. A "potential All-Pro as a rookie" as a source said, his combination of size, physicality, and explosiveness is as impressive as you'll find at the position in this draft class, or any other. At 6-foot-3, he has the ability to overwhelm defensive backs physically while maintaining the athleticism and explosiveness to separate at the top of routes, the strength to consistently win through contact and the type of ball skills that allow him to make plays far outside his frame hauling in passes like a Venus flytrap above the helmet of opposing corners.

A former five-star recruit and Heisman trophy candidate this fall, what separates Smith from other physically imposing receivers in the past is his ability to dominate at all three levels of an offense. He's not simply a big-bodied possession receiver who wins in the red zone. He can threaten defenses vertically, create separation on intermediate routes, and turn underneath opportunities into explosive gains with his ability to run through contact and generate yards after the catch.

There's also a level of polish to his game that makes his evaluation particularly exciting. Smith has the physical tools to dominate NFL corners immediately, but he's also shown the ability to consistently win in different situations and against different coverage looks. He's the type of receiver who doesn't need a perfect quarterback situation to produce, making him about as close to a quarterback-proof offensive weapon as you'll find. Quite frankly, he's a player who could have made the jump to the NFL a couple of years ago and still been one of the more talented receivers in the league. He's that special of a talent.

For the Eagles, Smith would represent an opportunity to add a legitimate cornerstone piece to the offensive skill group and showcase what would be arguably the NFL's top wide receiver tandem alongside DeVonta Smith. With questions surrounding the long-term composition of the receiver room and the potential need for another dominant outside presence, his ability to win vertically, create after the catch and consistently command attention from opposing defenses would immediately change the way teams have to defend the Eagles. If he's available at No. 5, no matter what your depth chart looks like, you turn in his card and don't think twice. He's the top player in the 2027 class.

Day 1: Jackson Harris, LSU | 6'2″, 205 lbs, 4.45 estimated 40 time (via summer testing)

A transfer to Baton Rouge from Hawaii, Harris has gone from being a likely Day 3 pick to an athlete that will likely hear his name called on Night 1.

At his size, Harris brings an appealing combination of outstanding route-running and playmaking ability on the outside. He's a receiver with the type of frame NFL teams covet, particularly when evaluating players who can operate outside the numbers and give quarterbacks a legitimate target in contested situations. His transition to the SEC has been particularly impressive, and the production he's put together this season has helped elevate his draft stock. After announcing himself on the SEC stage in Week 4 with 195 receiving yards against Texas A&M, Harris is coming off of electric back-to-back performances against Auburn and Alabama to the tune of 21 catches for 359 yards and two TDs combined in the last two weeks. Defenses know the ball is going his way, and they still can't do anything about it.

For the Eagles, the 25th overall selection could be an interesting range to explore if Harris remains available. His size and ability to provide another outside presence would give the Eagles some additional flexibility with its personnel groupings.

Day 2: Deuce Alexander, Ole Miss | 5'11", 180 lbs, 4.33 estimated 40 time (via summer testing)

If you're looking for an explosive weapon who can immediately add a completely unique dimension to an offense, Alexander is the guy here on Day 2. A Wake Forest transfer, Alexander brings a completely different physical profile than some of the bigger-bodied receivers at the top of the board as his burst and top end speed will force opposing coordinators to know where he his on every snap.

He's not going to overwhelm defensive backs with size, but his fundamental explosiveness and ability to create separation are what make him such an interesting prospect. There are some similarities to Lions WR Jameson Williams in the way he moves and threatens defenses vertically, as Alexander has the type of acceleration and track speed that can force opposing defenses to respect the deep ball, while his ability to create separation gives him opportunities to make plays underneath, as well. He's the type of receiver who can force advantageous matchups for an offense simply by being on the field.

For the Eagles, Alexander would provide a different element to the receiver room. Philadelphia already has a few established options who can operate in the intermediate areas of the field, but adding another receiver with legitimate vertical speed could open up opportunities for the entire offense by forcing defenses to cover every single blade of grass. His ability to threaten defenses over the top and generate explosive plays would make him an appealing complementary piece, particularly if the Eagles are looking to add more speed without allocating first-round capital.

Day 2: Ryan Wingo, Texas | 6'1″, 214 lbs, 4.37 estimated 40 time (via summer testing)

Wingo is another receiver who makes just about everything look easy when watching the tape. Operating in Steve Sarkisian's offense at Texas, he's been utilized in a variety of ways, and that versatility is one of the more appealing aspects of his evaluation.

At 6-foot-1, Wingo has an impressive combination of size, strength and elite athleticism. He's a well-built receiver who can operate outside, but his skill set extends beyond simply lining up on the perimeter and running traditional route concepts. Texas has done an excellent job finding different ways to get him involved, whether that's through jet sweeps, the screen game, vertical opportunities or allowing him to create after the catch to showcase his track speed (as a former All-State sprinter during his prep years in Missouri).

His versatility is highly intriguing as he doesn't need a specific type of play call to make an impact. He's comfortable working with the ball in his hands, has the speed to threaten defenses vertically, and also possesses the physical profile to create problems when defensive backs attempt to tackle him in space. That alone is unique. He's the type of player who can provide an offense with multiple answers depending on the defensive personnel and coverage they're facing.

There's also something to be said about how naturally he operates within the offense. Wingo doesn't necessarily need to be force-fed opportunities to become an impact player, and his ability to contribute in different areas makes him an appealing evaluation for teams looking for more than just a traditional outside receiver.

For Philly, that versatility could be particularly valuable. The Eagles have continued to look for ways to create more explosive opportunities within its offensive structure, and Wingo would provide another player capable of contributing both on designed touches and as a legitimate downfield threat. His skillset expands a playbook.

Day 3: Cayden Lee, Missouri | 5'10", 175 lbs, 4.55 estimated 40 time (via summer testing)

Don't dismiss Lee because of his size, as his ability to consistently make plays regardless of the situation is what makes him worth monitoring. An Ole Miss transfer, Lee has established himself as one of the most productive receivers in the SEC and currently leads the conference in receiving yards with 835 through nine games.

At 5-foot-10, his game isn't predicated on overwhelming defensive backs physically. Instead, his game is built around nuance – the ability to consistently catch the football, mirroring similar routes, create separation and make plays beyond his frame. He has some of the best hands in the country and understands how to find openings in coverage and give his quarterback an opportunity to complete passes, and there's a level of consistency to his game that stands out when watching him. There are some similarities to Lemon in the way he operates as a receiver, as well. He's not the biggest, fastest, or most physically imposing player on the field, but his ability to consistently get involved, make difficult catches and produce within an offense showcases a heck of a football player.

The Eagles don't necessarily need another receiver who profiles as a traditional outside target, but finding a reliable player who can contribute as a WR4 or WR5 while providing some additional explosiveness to the offense potentially on special teams is never a bad investment. His ability to consistently make plays and his production against SEC competition make him a name worth keeping an eye on as the process unfolds.

Day 3: Justin Bowick, Oklahoma State | 6'3″, 203 lbs, 4.50 estimated 40 time (via summer testing)

Bowick is a name whose draft stock could continue trending upward as the evaluation process moves along. Playing at Oklahoma State and catching passes from Drew Mestemaker, potentially the first QB off the board in April, Bowick has an opportunity to continue building his profile as a big-bodied perimeter target.

At 6-foot-3, he brings a physical profile that immediately pops on tape. He's a large presence on the outside who can present matchup problems for opposing defensive backs, particularly when working in contested-catch situations and giving his quarterback a bigger window to throw into. His size alone makes him an interesting developmental prospect, especially for the Eagles who could eye another outside receiver who can provide a different physical element to their offense.

Right now, he has mid to late Day 3 grades from teams but is likely a player to fully indulge himself in the pre-draft process, meaning a good showing at an All-Star game (Senior Bowl or East-West Shrine), the NFL Combine, and pro day circuit could move him up boards significantly.
