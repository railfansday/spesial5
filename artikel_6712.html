I Went To Bermuda &amp; Fell In Love With The Dark ‘N’ Stormy

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

You know an island is really fun when it has a national cocktail. Before heading to Bermuda, I didn’t even realize its national drink was the Dark ‘n’ Stormy. I should have, given its deep roots and ties to rum, especially Gosling Rum. And even though I knew what the drink was, I had never had one. I was excited to try my first one right where it was created!

Long story short: I was not disappointed. The Dark ‘n’ Stormy isn’t too sweet, has a strong ginger kick, and, best of all, is easy to make at home. I also love a layered drink. Even one that looks like an eerie cloud is so pretty to look at. I think this drink finally turned me into a rum lover.

When I normally think of a rum drink, I think of all the fun, tropical ones, like a piña colada or strawberry daiquiri. Perfect for sipping on the beach or by the pool.

But the Dark ‘n’ Stormy is made for the fall and winter. It’s spicy from the ginger beer, which pairs so well with the sweet molasses flavor of the dark rum. Get the spiciest ginger beer you can get your hands on. The more ginger flavor, the better, I think. A chilly fall night deserves this drink. If there's a thunderstorm, major bonus points.

Bermudians are natural storytellers, and after hearing any of their stories, you’d be hard-pressed not to try their favorite drink. Legend has it that after being served this drink, a sailor thought it looked like the worst kind of cloud, one only a fool or a dead man would sail under. It makes you shudder to order one, but it’s too good to stop you.

You need two ingredients to make a Dark ‘n’ Stormy. Gosling Rum and ginger beer. It is common to add a squeeze of lime, which is always a good idea. I don't find it totally necessary because I like keeping it on the warm, darker side. The simple, straightforward version is incredible as is. If you have a lime on hand, then go for it. Don’t go out of your way to buy some limes, though.

I had one riff on the classic that was worth the extra ingredients, though. When we stopped into the Rosedon Hotel, they made us one that included burnt honey, cardamom, and orange zest. It was so delicious, and I could have had multiple.

A true Dark ‘n’ Stormy must be made with Gosling Rum. It’s officially trademarked by them, and it’s hard to argue with the original recipe. If you are going to make one, it’s worth getting the correct ingredients, especially when there are so few.

To make the rum floater stay on top, pour the rum in slowly. As with most layered drinks, the balancing act is delicate. A lot of ice helps, too. If you pour slowly, the rum will stay toward the top. I like to give it a gentle stir before I start drinking, without fully combining the two. I like getting drinks of pure ginger beer every once in a while.

Find your perfect rum-to-ginger-beer ratio. Dark ‘n’ Stormies are often 2-to-1 ginger beer to rum. If you like yours stronger, add a bit more rum. It all depends on how “stormy” you want your drink. When you make drinks at home, you are the bartender and can find your perfect ratio.

Now, if only I could sip on one of these back in Bermuda. That would be ideal. I wouldn’t even mind if it was during a storm.
