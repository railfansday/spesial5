Groups Decry Trump EPA’s Decision to Grant Louisiana Oversight of Toxic Coal Ash

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Testing at all Louisiana coal plants found unsafe groundwater contamination.

BATON ROUGE, La. — The Sierra Club, Earthjustice, and the Alliance for Affordable Energy Louisiana decried the Trump administration’s final decision today to put the State of Louisiana in charge of oversight of toxic coal ash waste. The Louisiana Department of Environmental Quality (LDEQ) has repeatedly allowed coal plants to violate federal coal ash protections.

Coal ash is generated when coal is burned, and massive quantities are stored at coal-burning power plants near Baton Rouge, Alexandria, and Shreveport.

At all three active Louisiana coal plants (Big Cajun 2, Brame Energy Center, R S Nelson), and the state’s one retired plant (Dolet Hills), monitoring data shows that coal ash is contaminating groundwater at levels above federal safe drinking water standards. These toxic chemicals include arsenic, boron, cobalt, lead, lithium, radium, sulfate, and thallium. More than 61% of Louisiana’s population relies on groundwater for all or part of their drinking water supply.

“Louisianans know all too well that we can’t trust our state regulators to hold polluters accountable, so it’s nonsensical that the Trump Administration just gave LDEQ even more authority to handle something as dangerous as coal ash,” said Darryl Malek-Wiley, a Sierra Club senior field representative in Louisiana. “It’s clear this decision is meant to favor wealthy coal-owning utilities at the expense of our health.”

“The Trump administration chose polluters over the people of Louisiana,” said Earthjustice senior attorney Mychal Ozaeta. “An alphabet soup of toxic chemicals from burning coal is contaminating groundwater in locations across the state. The right thing to do is make the polluters clean up their toxic mess, and protect sources of drinking water. Instead, the Trump administration is handing over authority to Louisiana’s state government which won’t lift a finger to clamp down on the concerning levels of contaminants poisoning the state’s groundwater.”

“Our state regulators are dangerously inadequate in their oversight and enforcement of polluting industries, which has already cost Louisiana our safe air and water. We simply don’t think LDEQ can handle even more responsibilities they won’t take seriously,” said Logan Burke, Executive Director of the Alliance for Affordable Energy.

Public interest groups submitted formal comments to EPA urging the administration to reject Louisiana’s request to handle coal ash oversight in the state.

Coal ash contains arsenic, mercury, chromium, lead, lithium, radium, and other heavy metals, which have been linked to numerous types of cancer, heart and thyroid disease, respiratory illness, reproductive failure, and neurological harm.

As an example of the state’s abysmal track record on coal ash, LDEQ has allowed power plants to close coal ash dumps with ash in groundwater, ensuring that the dumps will contaminate groundwater with toxic chemicals indefinitely. LDEQ should not be in charge of issuing permits that let coal plants continue to violate the law. These permits will shield power plants from federal enforcement and prevent Louisiana residents from stopping coal ash pollution.

LDEQ is a severely understaffed and extremely lenient environmental regulator. In addition to its coal ash failures, its air monitors are far away from Louisiana’s heaviest air-polluting facilities, and the agency has for years been criticized for approving permits for wealthy polluters, including LNG terminals and a massive Formosa plastics factory. Louisianans know all too well that LDEQ does not protect low-income communities of color, and creates a lax regulatory environment that has enabled disasters like the recent Smitty’s Supply explosion and spill.
