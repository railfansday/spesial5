Novartis has acknowledged seven previously unknown instances in which tumors were found in children who were treated with its spinal muscular atrophy therapy, a surprising development that has raised concerns among families, physicians, and patient advocacy groups, STAT writes. The cases, which for the moment are being seen as only a potential safety signal, were publicized on Thursday by an advocacy group and on a Facebook page after Novartis earlier this week disclosed the information to neurologists. One advocacy group, SMA Europe, issued a statement saying that regulatory authorities are now examining the cases.

Four of the eight people appointed by U.S. Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. to an influential health panel in September own stock in companies that make drugs and other products affected by the group’s decisions, The New York Times reports. The investments were described in conflict-of-interest waivers that gave the members permission to serve on the panel, the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force, which evaluates evidence for services like colonoscopies, HIV prevention drugs, depression screening, and more. Its recommendations both inform physician decisions and determine what insurers must pay.
