Lot Supply Rises for Ninth Consecutive Quarter

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The supply of vacant developed lots continued to loosen in the third quarter as slower construction activity brought the national lot market further into balance, according to Zonda.

Zonda’s New Home Lot Supply Index rose to 88.5 in the third quarter, up 20.8% from a year earlier and 3.9% from the second quarter. It marked the ninth consecutive quarterly increase in the index.

The index measures the supply of single-family vacant developed lots relative to the rate at which builders absorb those lots through housing starts. Zonda rated the national market “appropriately supplied” in the third quarter.

In the second quarter, the index sat at the boundary between “slightly undersupplied” and “appropriately supplied.” The third-quarter increase placed the national market in the latter category.

“The national lot market is moving more firmly into balance,” says Sean Fergus, vice president of economic research for Zonda. “But that balance needs to be viewed in the context of today’s housing market.”

Fergus noted that affordability pressures and widespread incentives continue to challenge builders’ ability to convert demand into sales. An appropriately supplied lot market indicates that lot availability is better aligned with the current pace of construction, he said, but does not mean the industry’s longer-term land constraints and cost pressures have disappeared.

Lot supply increased year over year in every major metropolitan area tracked by Zonda except Boise, Idaho. Among the markets where supply increased, the average annual gain was 32%.

Baltimore, Portland, and Seattle posted the largest year-over-year increases in lot supply. Boise was the only market where supply tightened, with its index falling from 65.7 in the third quarter of 2025 to 51.9.

Despite the broader improvement in availability, 12 of the 30 markets tracked by Zonda remained “significantly undersupplied.” Eight markets changed supply ratings during the quarter, Zonda points out.

Zonda also reported a modest increase in the future lot pipeline. Total upcoming lots increased 1% year over year and 1.6% from the second quarter.

Upcoming lots include properties in the later stages of development that are typically expected to become vacant developed lots within 12 to 18 months.

The increase suggests additional lots are moving toward delivery, although the pipeline is expanding much more slowly than the current supply index. The divergence reflects the role of softer starts in loosening lot conditions: available lots are lasting longer as builders respond to weaker demand and elevated mortgage rates.
