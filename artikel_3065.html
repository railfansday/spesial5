I asked Gemini Notebook to ‘make me a site’ from my notes – it was satisfying

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A new Gemini Notebook feature lets you create interactive sites from intricate data.

Google’s Gemini can now turn your notes and other sources in your Gemini Notebook (previously called NotebookLM) into interactive pages or sites to help you better process complicated information.

Complete with flashcards, dashboards, and colorful panels, the sites make it easier to study and understand intricate concepts. I tested the feature, announced Tuesday, to see how efficient it is at creating a navigable site that is actually helpful and stays loyal to sources.

Because Notebooks are built with the sources you provide, like textbooks, files, or notes, the sites are created with the data that is in your Gemini Notebook. Notebook has three tabs: Sources, Chat, and Studio. The site option is in the Studio tab, listed under what you can create.

If you’re taking a college class, you could put all the information you need to prepare for a test and use the interactive site as a study guide that you can share with others.

To test this, I gave Notebook the following prompt:

“Make me a site that visually explains how cellular respiration works, breaking down each stage with interactive diagrams and self-check flashcards.”

Since I’m not in a class that would give me those study materials, I gave Gemini online sources to use for this. Gemini cited the sources in my notebook, then spent a few minutes creating the interactive site. Gemini will give you a quick update when it’s done, including a summary of the source information as it pertains to your request and instructions on where to find the site.

As promised, my cellular respiration notes were complete and organized on an interactive, visually appealing site that was easy to navigate. The coolest feature on the site was the flashcards, which you can hover over to see which ones you’ve studied.

The interactive site feature is now available to Gemini Notebook users and will soon come to the mobile app. As someone who knows very little about coding, I was pretty satisfied to see the information Gemini put together presented in explorable, easy-to-understand tabs on a site.
