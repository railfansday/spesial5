The Free World Award Is Made From Weapons Metal. The First Winner: Iraq’s Oscar Entry

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Ukraine's Odesa and Estonia's Tallinn film festivals have partnered on the new honor symbolizing "the principles of peace and justice" and part of a three-year memorandum.

It looks very different from most film awards. And it sure as hell is forged very differently from any film award you may have ever seen or even heard about.

The Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival (PÖFF) in Estonia and Ukraine‘s Odesa International Film Festival have partnered on the inaugural Free World Award, a new annual honor launched “to recognize cinema that champions freedom, human dignity and human rights.”

The news of the award was unveiled on Thursday at the Industry@Tallinn & Baltic Event (ITBE), the market of PÖFF, whose 30th edition runs through this weekend. Andrii Osipov, head of the Ukrainian State Film Agency, said during an event at the festival: “This award was established by representatives of a nation that today stands on the front lines of Russia’s war against Ukraine. We hope to spread the strength and resilience of the Ukrainian people across the world.”

But wait, there is more that is unusual about this one. “The award carries profound symbolic significance, as it is made from metal melted down from the remnants of weapons, missiles and ammunition,” Osipov shared. “For us, it symbolizes the unity of our efforts and intentions to uphold the principles of peace and justice, counter armed aggression and protect human rights.”

The winner of this year’s inaugural award is Halkawt Mustafa’s No Paradise If You Are Killed by a Woman, Iraq’s international Oscar contender. Writer and director Mustafa will receive the honor on Friday. The film is a co-production between Norway, Iraq, the United Arab Emirates and Sweden. The story follows a female sniper returning to the battlefield to rescue her younger sister from ISIS captivity. The film premiered at the Venice, where it won a special jury mention.

The Free World Award is part of a three-year memorandum, which the Ukrainian State Film Agency and PÖFF signed on Thursday that is designed to “strengthen the international visibility of Ukrainian cinema and develop closer ties between Ukrainian, Estonian and international film professionals.”

The partners added: “The agreement supports artistic freedom and democratic values, while expanding opportunities for Ukrainian films, projects and filmmakers to participate in PÖFF and its industry programs, with particular attention to emerging talent. Planned cooperation includes professional exchanges, joint discussions and presentations, and the development of international partnerships.”

PÖFF has been proudly saying that it was among the first festivals to promote Ukrainian films following Russia’s full-scale invasion in 2022, while encouraging audiences to donate in support of Ukraine. Since 2024, PÖFF has dedicated its Standing With Ukraine program to Ukrainian cinema, giving filmmakers a platform to share their work with audiences.
