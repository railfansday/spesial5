Huddersfield Town v Leicester City: Drury's vow regarding Sanches Fernandes plus empathy for Foxes rival

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

They can also rest assured that the talismanic winger will not get carried away - or 'giddy' as they say in these parts.

Footage of Fernandes cutting inside from the position he loves on the right flank before unleashing a sweet left-foot drive into the net - just 15 minutes into his bow for the Blue Sharks - did the rounds late last week.

The 25-year-old came off the bench in the final quarter of the Africa Cup of Nations qualifier against Liberia and was afforded a memorable moment in his burgeoning career.

Unfortunately, a bout of gastroenteritis ensured that Fernandes did not feature in the second leg of the double-header on Tuesday. After returning to the UK early, he is now over his sickness bug and in the reckoning for today's juicy fixture.

On the recognition, head coach Martin Drury said: "I think it's nothing less than he deserves for the level of performance he's put in this season. Not just with the ball, but without the ball.

"I think he's been outstanding. You see the goal he scores, he comes on the pitch for 15 minutes and does what I see him doing in training every day and what we've seen him doing in the league.

"So, full credit to him. But I think the big thing with him is he's so level-headed. He doesn't go there and get carried away with it. He uses it for what he needs to use it for in terms of his development and his learning.

"I'm sure he'll become a better player and person as a result of that experience."

Town face a Leicester side who are starting to make their move in third-tier circles after a tough start to the campaign which saw head coach Russell Martin receive no end of vitriol on social media platforms in particular.

Drury remains a big fan of his work, although he will not want to see too much evidence of it today.

Regarding how Martin blanked out the 'noise' early in the season, the Town chief is full of respect.

He continued: "In the industry that we live in, there's a platform for people to say whatever they want. And when it's really positive, that can be really good. When it's not positive, sometimes we just have to remember we're talking about human beings here.

"Football coaches and football players aren't abnormal; they're human beings. And I've seen some of the stuff that he's had labelled his way.

"I'm not sat here saying he's perfect, he's doing everything right. But I can only speak from a coaching point of view and personal view, having met him. He's a really good human being. I think he's a really talented coach.

"I've got a lot of respect for him - for the fact that he's got the mental strength to deal with that. And I think he's got an inner belief in himself that the work that he does is good."
