Bowen’s West Ham Future | We’ve Been Here Before

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

West Ham United captain Jarrod Bowen has revealed that he will sit down with the club to discuss his future at the end of the season should the Hammers fail to secure promotion. The England international insists he never wanted to leave following relegation and remains determined to get West Ham back into the Premier League.

However, speaking in an interview with Sam Tabuteau for The Standard, Bowen made it clear that another conversation would be necessary if the promotion bid failed.

With Scott Parker’s team languishing in 13th position ahead of tomorrow’s visit of Stoke City, Bowen’s latest admission is particularly significant. After six games without a win, the prospect of West Ham spending another season in the Championship is becoming increasingly realistic.

As far as I’m concerned, nobody could blame Jarrod Bowen for leaving West Ham. The truth of the matter is that I wouldn’t have held it against him had he decided to move in the summer. After all, he’s given everything for this football club.

I also think it’s important to state on these pages that whilst I don’t particularly believe him to be captain material, that in no way clouds my view of him as a player. Bowen is already somebody I would consider to be a West Ham legend.

Let’s be fair, there are plenty who have achieved icon status at this football club without having done a quarter of what Jarrod has in claret and blue.

Kretinsky and Bowen in Prague as the skipper committed to life in the Championship

I do find it intriguing that Bowen is once again talking about sitting down with the club at the end of the season. It was only in the summer that Jarrod flew over to Prague to meet Daniel Křetínský and do precisely that.

One can only imagine what assurances Bowen was given during that meeting to persuade him to stay. And I wouldn’t be an inquisitive Hammers fan if I didn’t wonder whether the recruitment since then has lived up to the ambition outlined to him of building a Championship-winning team.

Personally, I’ll take the talk of another meeting with a pinch of salt. I can’t see any way Bowen plays a second season in the Championship, and nor should he.

It’s always been my suspicion that failing to secure promotion at the first attempt would likely result in an extended stay in the lower division.

And quite frankly, Bowen is better than that.
