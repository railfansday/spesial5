New Museum Inks Multiyear Partnership with Video Art–Focused Vega Foundation

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

As part of its expanded programming at its recently enlarged New York home, the New Museum is launching a multiyear partnership with the Vega Foundation, an organization known for supporting major video art commissions that was launched by Elisa Nuyten, a board member.

The foundation has a reputation for providing opportunities to emerging and mid-career artists working in film and video, two mediums that are under-supported and difficult to monetize. Artists to have received Vega commissions include Ali Cherri, Diego Marcon, Martine Syms, and Yuyan Wang. (Marcon is currently the subject of a New Museum show that features a reformulated Vega Foundation commission.) Based in Toronto and begun in 2022, the Vega Foundation has often brought its commissions to Canada’s institutions; that will happen once again with the New Museum’s commissions. “The New Museum has played such an important role in making space for new forms of art and ideas, and I hope this partnership will build on that history,” Nuyten, a recent addition to ARTnews’s Top 200 Collectors list, said in an email. “Just as importantly, I hope these projects will have lives beyond their initial presentations in New York. Bringing this work to Canadian audiences is central to what we do at Vega.”

She continued, “For Vega, supporting artists also means thinking about how their work continues to reach new audiences and find new contexts over time. That’s something I’d really like to see come out of this partnership.”

Massimiliano Gioni, the New Museum’s newly named director, said in an email, “Vega’s work creating a network of possibilities for works that might otherwise never be realized is perfectly aligned with what the New Museum was founded to do nearly fifty years ago, giving artists the freedom and resources to take risks, experiment, and pursue ideas that might initially seem strange or even impossible. To me, that is precisely what contemporary art needs.”

He added that the foundation, with its ability to offer budgets and support tailor-made to each artist, is “not a vanity project: it’s all dedicated to artists and their work. I also consider myself an honorary Canadian, having studied there when I was a teenager, so any excuse to partner with our neighbors, particularly today, is just very special.”

First up at the New Museum is a Vega-funded commission by Korakrit Arunanondchai that will debut in a solo show by the Thai artist opening in February. His Vega commission—which features the artist Tosh Basco, who will do a three-day performance series within the New Museum exhibition during its run—deals with the phenomenon of ghost cinemas in Thailand, where, after the American War in Vietnam, monks used portable projectors to stage screenings for specters.

At the New Museum, Arunanondchai will create a video installation that includes fog, light, and a soil floor. “The way that the sound and the atmosphere [are] functioning, it almost feels like you’re in a room with ghosts,” Madeline Weisburg, the exhibition’s curator, said. “So, there’s this very spatialized interest in this history that he’s bringing into this space.”

Of partnering with the Vega Foundation, Weisburg said, “We’re not here to preserve history; we’re here to make this new work and really to produce this history. … We’re able to the best of our ability to support the most ambitious works that an artist can make.” Without partners like the foundation, she added, those artists “might not be able to make that work.”
