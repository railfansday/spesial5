‘I am deeply sorry’: Nvidia stock falls as Jensen Huang’s affair and an alleged spying scandal rocks the AI industry

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Shares in Nvidia Corporation (Nasdaq: NVDA) are sinking today, less than 12 hours after CEO Jensen Huang publicly confirmed that he’d had an extramarital affair, and after the chipmaker confirmed that it was investigating Huang’s relationship amid reports that the woman he’d had an affair with was allegedly spying on behalf of Beijing.

The story has rocked the AI industry at a time when related tech stocks are already facing their most significant headwinds since the artificial intelligence boom first took off. Here’s everything you need to know to get caught up.

Huang and Nvidia confirm affair and investigation

Last night, Nvidia published a statement from its board of directors confirming that the company has launched an independent investigation into its CEO, Jensen Huang.

The statement came following earlier reports by the New York Times and the Financial Times alleging that Huang had been having an extramarital affair with Alice Huang, a senior leader at a Singapore-based cloud company called Megaspeed.

U.S. intelligence services believe the woman was spying on behalf of China, the reports said.

Nvidia makes the world’s most advanced chips and graphics cards, which are now the backbone of the AI revolution.

For years, the company’s most state-of-the-art technology has faced export restrictions to China over national security fears. The United States and China are locked in an AI arms race, and U.S. lawmakers and national security experts worry that the sale of Nvidia’s hardware to China would give the country an edge economically and militarily.

While the extramarital affair stands to damage Huang’s personal reputation, AI industry insiders, Wall Street, and security experts are most concerned about the fact that Huang’s companion may have also been working as an intelligence operative.

There are fears that any industry information or Nvidia secrets that Jensen Huang may have discussed with Alice Huang could have been passed along to foreign intelligence services.

After Huang confirmed the affair—and Nvidia confirmed the investigation—Nvidia’s stock sank in premarket trading on Monday. This is more bad news for investors who have already seen AI-related stocks get hammered in the past week.

Fast Company reached out to Megaspeed for comment. It was not immediately clear if Alice Huang, who could not be reached for comment, still worked there.

What has Jensen Huang said?

When reports of the affair first broke, Jensen Huang suggested that he’d had only had normal business engagements with Alice Huang. The two had been photographed together at multiple business and social events.

But last night, Huang sent an email to Nvidia employees confirming the affair. Nvidia later published the message on its website. 

“I owe my family and all of you an apology,” the message began. “I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry.”

Addressing Nvidia employees directly, Huang said, “you deserve better from me. Leading NVIDIA is the privilege of my life. My responsibility is to serve you and be worthy of your trust. I have fallen short of that responsibility. I am fully committed to NVIDIA and will work to earn back your confidence.”

Huang also addressed the national security concerns about his affair, saying the company had launched an independent investigation.

“I support the investigation and will cooperate fully. I love our country and our company. I am committed to protecting NVIDIA and supporting America’s national security. I am proud and grateful to be an American,” Huang added.

Huang, who cofounded Nvidia in 1993, has been seen as one of the single most influential tech leaders behind the AI revolution.

What has Nvidia said?

Nvidia confirmed that its board of directors has initiated an independent investigation into the reports that Huang may have “been the target of a foreign intelligence effort.” 

The investigation will be led by “independent directors, assisted by outside independent counsel,” according to the company, with the board noting that it “prioritizes U.S. national and economic security, which is paramount to the interests of NVIDIA and its shareholders.”

The company also stated that its board of directors has no reason to believe the company’s secrets or national security were compromised. 

Nvidia says that it still has “confidence in Jensen and his leadership team.”

What have China and the U.S. said?

Officials in China were quick to dispute the reports that it targeted Nvidia’s CEO in any way. Reuters quoted Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun as saying, “The so-called Chinese spy narrative is purely a groundless accusation, slander, and a smear.”

As for U.S. officials, when reporters asked Secretary of State Marco Rubio about the matter, he replied: “Yeah, I can’t comment on any of that. I just can’t comment because of the nature of it.  And I also can’t comment on an internal corporate matter that’s going on with Nvidia.”

President Trump told reporters, “I have not spoken to [Jensen Huang] about it. I just know he’s a fine person, but you never know what is going on.” He added: ”I hope everything’s fine.”

The president also said, “I think he’s an honorable guy,” noting that the Nvidia CEO had “done an amazing job for our country.”

How has NVDA stock reacted?

Not well. As of this writing, in premarket trading, Nvidia shares are down more than 5% to just above $177. If today’s premarket drop holds, Nvidia will have lost more than 26% of its value in the past month alone.

Like many AI-related stocks last week, Nvidia shares sank in the days after the 2026 U.S. midterm election results, amid fears that the incoming Democratic-led Congress would take a more cautious approach to AI data center buildouts than the current administration. Voters have overwhelmingly shown their distaste for the massive server farms.

Huang’s scandal is sure to do no favors to the larger AI industry, which is already facing worries about a stock market bubble and growing unpopularity with the broader public.

What happens next?

Nvidia likely won’t be the only entity investigating the allegations.

Dan Ives, senior managing director at Yorkville Ives & Co, told CNBC, “This is a black eye moment for Jensen and will spark multiple congressional inquiries at a minimum and will create an overhang on the stock for a myriad of reasons.”

Ives believes there is a risk Jensen could be removed from his CEO position at Nvidia. 

“[T]his will add risk to the stock as he is so important to the Nvidia story and strategy globally.”
