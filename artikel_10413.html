Do this crucial thing to thrive during unemployment

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

A job search, whatever the length, is demoralizing.

After losing a job, you’ll start asking yourself challenging questions about finances, childcare and more. You may also feel the pang of an identity crisis—particularly if you’re a longtime employee whose daily life has become tied up with work.

On top of that, there’s the hours of applying, networking, interviewing, and dealing with modern-day job hunt maladies, from AI and ghost jobs. Lack of communication and recruiter bait-and-switch make the process feel useless. 

The mental battle can be a monster. But experts suggest a possible sanity-saving sword to slay that monster: the power of having a routine. That can be key, especially when navigating a process that may drag on past six months or more.

Melissa Dorman, an organizational psychologist and executive coach, shares that in today’s anonymous market, “no matter how much you get done, you can feel behind or get sucked into the vortex of applying for one job after another.”

Crafting (and sticking) to a routine during this period is not only a way to stay positive during a job search—but it could also be a means to open up job seekers to new opportunities.

Routines and productivity

The salve to some of this strangeness may come down to how seekers control what is within their power. A routine can serve as that support and stability that can put a period of uncontrollable unpredictability into perspective. 

“Stability is everything when your world feels like the bottom has fallen out, especially when losing a job and searching for a job in a volatile market,” says Dorman.

By setting routines, what matters is, “you can know how your day will start tomorrow morning.”

As Dorman recommends, try to set specific times for job applications. Outside of that time, focus on developing new skills, conducting networking outreach, or simply giving yourself some grace to reconnect with what brings you joy. These new activities can not only bring you joy—but serve as anchors throughout your day. Over time, you may see that your routine gives you purpose.

“One sanity-protecting exercise is to implement an ‘office hours’ approach for apps,” adds Dorman, who frequently coaches managers on keeping their skills sharp. 

With these kinds of consistent routines, you can free up mental space so you are able to meet the perfect opportunity when it presents itself: “You can’t be productive or maintain motivation long-term if [only] completing job applications is your full-time job.”

Benefits of consistency

Routines can be tricky to make stick, though—the human brain is wired to feel lit up by novel experiences.

“Our brains love new things. When we start a routine, we get a bit of a dopamine hit, or a burst of the motivational chemical that craves rewards. But when the routine becomes familiar, what once felt exciting starts to feel like work,” says Dorman.

That’s why being intentionally consistent is key: blocking off time on your calendar for that 4:30 p.m. jog, or booking a hot desk at your favorite coworking space, where you specifically research interesting companies or dig into building new skills.

With repetition, not only does the craving for novelty become less pronounced, but the routine itself starts to reinforce itself from positive associations. Your weekly workout may suddenly be the most significant time to relax and refresh yourself, for example.

Make time in the first few weeks to feel a little uncomfortable when cementing your new routine—but stick to a regular schedule. And if you’re comfortable sharing your new routine, recruit a close friend to keep you accountable through regular check-ins.

“The more often you do something, the less you have to think about it,” says Dorman.

Being consistent also makes us feel more productive. According to long-cited research by Teresa Amabile, a psychologist and professor emerita at Harvard Business School, the act of making progress on a project of any size involving personal investment leads to feelings of productivity. Her team dubbed this concept the “progress principle.” Amabile’s research shows this ability to make even a small dent on a project improves workers’ motivation, emotional health, and sense of personal performance. The frequency of these feelings also affected the duration subjects remained productive.

In essence, building up wins throughout the day equaled a feeling of motivation for people over the long-term.

Crafting your successful routine

  • 1. Make it a habit


Habits are borne from planning ahead. One place to start when you’re still looking for work? Take an audit of your sleep schedule. Laura Vanderkam, a Fast Company contributor who has written about productive scheduling, stresses the importance of a consistent sleep schedule in her 2022 book, Tranquility by Tuesday.

Unorganized sleep schedules are hidden saboteurs, causing people to crash out and squander productivity in order to make up for rest. It’s too easy to get into a cycle of stretching yourself thin during the work week—only to oversleep on weekends and throw off your rhythm on weekdays. With a consistent sleep and wake-up time, you not only regulate your flow of energy for the day, but you’re setting yourself up for success tomorrow on your terms.

  • 2. Place rewards alongside hard work

Harvard behavioral scientists Katy Milkman, Julia Minson, and Kevin Volpp point to the practice of bundling temptations alongside tedious or daunting work to fuel motivation. Pair activities that are a chore or bore you with a motivational pick-me-up—like your favorite coffee or a few minutes of vinyasa flow.

And Thomas Davies, a previous director at Google for Work and Fast Company contributor, writes that engaging in activities that genuinely light you up will make an onerous or soulless job search feel more fruitful or at least seem like one step to something better.

MIT researchers also draw a connection between the importance of mental hints when developing habits. They claim habits are often associated with a physical cue that triggers a habit response—so go ahead and reach for your noise-cancelling headphones or shift over to your standing desk when hard work calls.

  • 3. Don’t ignore the paths of least resistance

Finally, keeping a routine doesn’t mean being inflexible.

According to a 2026 study carried out by Columbia researchers, people often get stuck in inertia (fixed mindsets, anyone?), which can cause them to disregard new pathways to their goal. Sometimes, the more interesting route can be more fun and lead you to the same result. So stay open to changing your set routines when it feels right. Maybe this means changing the ratio of your networking efforts to application efforts. Maybe this means searching for skills over strict job titles. 

Whatever the shift looks like, the idea applies to before you get the job… and when you land it.

Keep these tips to make your routine a steady one—and to make your unemployment period a productive one.
