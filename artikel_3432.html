Ridley Scott is coming off a major setback in the form of The Dog Stars, his first sci-fi movie in nearly a decade. The film underperformed at the box office, grossing around $40 million worldwide against a reported budget of around $100 million. As big a flop as it was, The Dog Stars isn't the biggest bomb that Scott has directed this decade. That unenviable honor goes to a period epic released five years ago with a budget similar to several of the filmmaker's recent movies. The period epic is currently streaming for free in the United States, but not for much longer.

Not only was it marketed as Scott's return to Gladiator-style filmmaking, but it also marked an on-screen reunion of Ben Affleck and Matt Damon. More interestingly, the two also worked on the script, making this their first new project as writers since 1997's Good Will Hunting, for which they won an Oscar. This time around, they collaborated on the script with filmmaker Nicole Holofcener and crafted a movie that owes its structure to the Akira Kurosawa classic Rashomon.

The movie features Damon as one of the three leads, along with Jodie Comer and Adam Driver. Affleck, on the other hand, plays a villainous supporting role. The movie is based on the true story of the last recorded duel in history, which was fought over a woman's honor. The film presents the same incident from three different perspectives, asking the audience to arrive at their own judgments.

Four houses, four completely different theories about how you survive a world like this one. Here is where your banner belongs, what the name would cost you, and what it would buy.

You belong in the North, where the weather does most of the arguing and nobody is impressed by a title. The Starks are the only great house on this list who genuinely believe that being right matters more than winning, which is exactly why the show spent eight years punishing them for it.

You belong to the house that worked out early on that gold moves faster than armies. Casterly Rock runs on leverage, reputation and the certainty that everybody at the table owes you something, and you are perfectly comfortable with all three.

You belong to the house that conquered a continent and never quite got over it. Targaryens do not ask for a seat at the table, because in their version of events the table was theirs to begin with, and that certainty is either magnificent or catastrophic depending on the week.

You belong to Highgarden, where nobody raises their voice and everybody gets exactly what they came for. The Tyrells feed the realm, charm the realm and quietly steer the realm, and they have never once needed to win a battle to get their way.

We're talking, of course, about The Last Duel. Produced on a reported budget of $100 million, the film grossed $30 million at the worldwide box office. Ironically, it happens to be the best-reviewed film that Scott has made since The Martian. The movie holds a "Certified Fresh" 85% score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, where the consensus reads, "The Last Duel's critique of systemic misogyny isn't as effective as it might have been, but it remains a well-acted and thought-provoking drama infused with epic grandeur."

The Last Duel will remain available on Tubi until December 1. Meanwhile, Damon and Affleck recently collaborated as producers on the political thriller Animals, which Affleck also directed and starred in. Like The Last Duel, the movie effectively serves as a reimagining of a Kurosawa classic. In this case, Affleck adapted the Japanese filmmaker's High and Low, which was remade by Spike Lee only last year. You can watch both The Last Duel and Animals at home, and stay tuned to Collider for more updates.
