Palmer and Rogers complete 12-minute rescue mission to save Chelsea

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Xabi Alonso needed the cavalry – and referee Jarred Gillett – to stop Chelsea making a quick exit from the Carabao Cup and, boy, did they come up trumps for him.

Chelsea had looked to be heading out of the competition shortly after half-time, but, in Cole Palmer and Morgan Rogers, Alonso has a cheat code that is capable of masking even the worst defending.

Alonso’s side have shared 26 goals in the five games they have played this season. It has certainly been an entertaining start by Chelsea’s latest manager.

Palmer, Rogers and Pedro Neto stepped off the substitutes’ bench to complete a stunning rescue mission against Leeds United. Palmer scored twice, Neto once and Rogers helped himself to at least a hat-trick of assists. He claimed it was four, but the record books may say three.

When it was announced Palmer had been awarded the player of the match, Rogers responded by saying: “Mate, I got four assists. I can’t believe that! That’s mental. He scored a pen!”

"Mate, I got four assists!" "That is criminal!" Morgan Rogers and Cole Palmer arguing over the POTM award! 🤣 pic.twitter.com/X2PkPnSGDk

Chelsea had looked down and out, when, just three minutes after Alonso’s quadruple half-time change, Leeds doubled their lead. But four goals in an incredible 12-minute spell turned the tie on its head and it was Alonso’s substitutes who Chelsea had to thank.

Palmer, with some help from Gillett, was the first to come to the home side’s rescue. Four minutes after Brenden Aaronson had put Leeds 2-0 up, Gillett pointed to the penalty spot after Daniel James had stood on the foot of Pep Chavarría.

It looked like a harsh decision on Leeds and proved to be almost as big of a game-changer as Alonso’s substitutions. Palmer stepped up to score the resulting penalty.

Cole Palmer pulls one back for Chelsea from the spot! 🎯 pic.twitter.com/BUv6h1wTPQ

“Nobody in the stadium, not one player was asking for a penalty and he gave the penalty,” Daniel Farke, the Leeds manager, said. “All of a sudden their players came into the game. The momentum was on their side. Situations can change the whole momentum of the game.”

Stamford Bridge sensed a comeback and in the 55th minute, Rogers nudged the ball into the path of Palmer who produced a brilliant curling finish to beat Michael Zetterer, the Leeds goalkeeper.

Reece James then went close, before Neto completed the turnaround on the hour mark. Palmer found Rogers, who in turn picked out Neto and he sent Stamford Bridge wild by sending his shot into the corner of the net.

"This is a comeback of comebacks!" Pedro Neto scores and Chelsea have turned it around eleven minutes after going 2-0 down! 🤯 pic.twitter.com/FTPuWcFqEX

As Farke waited to make a quadruple substitution of his own, Danny Welbeck got in on the act by collecting the ball from Rogers, spinning and scoring.

Class from Danny Welbeck 😤 pic.twitter.com/FUFKxlzXDz

That should have been that and the atmosphere could not have been more different from half-time, when Chelsea fans had booed off their side after watching them fall a goal behind.

Reggie Watson, Chelsea’s 16-year-old debutant, Malo Gusto, Estêvão Willian and Jamie Gittens were hooked at half-time by Alonso, who said: “For sure, I take responsibility for all of them. No player to blame. It’s the decisions that we took.

“I know that probably I haven’t taken the right decisions for them to show their best level. It’s something that I will analyse and learn from. So it’s my responsibility and we had to make decisions at half-time. And we have seen another second half with another quality.”

Goalkeeper Mike Penders was one of the nine changes made by Alonso. He produced a superb double-save to first keep out Aaronson and then recover to tip Tarik Muharemovic’s header out from the corner.

But the Belgian flapped at the corner, which gave Harry Wilson a second chance to get his delivery right. And he did just that by finding Muharemovic at the back post to head the ball into the net.

Valentín Barco survived Alonso’s changes, but was partly at fault for Leeds’s second goal just after the break. He lost the ball and Ethan Ampadu galloped away from Rogers before crossing. Lukas Nmecha got the slightest of touches and Aaronsen smashed the ball into the net.

The substitutes then took over, as Chelsea shocked the visitors before Leeds substitute Dominic Calvert-Lewin gave his team hope by reducing the deficit with 15 minutes remaining.

Any thoughts of a Leeds comeback were quickly erased, however, as Barco netted his first Chelsea goal from a Rogers pass, which may have taken a slight touch to deny him a fourth assist in the record books. Welbeck added a sixth in time added on.

On the defeat by Chelsea, Farke said: “The referee had the idea to give a penalty. No one in the stadium was asking for it, no one of their players was asking for it, but he spotted a situation where he thought he would give a penalty.

“All of a sudden they scored the goal, then their players came into the game at half-time, the whole stadium after being relatively quiet for 60 minutes they of course were there then. The momentum was all of a sudden on their side. In football small situations can change the whole momentum of a game and this happened.”

On the message from the manager at half-time, Palmer told Sky Sports: “Just up the intensity, play a bit more, become more alive because I think the first half was flat, everyone agreed in the changing room, so we stepped up.

Asked whether Alonso’s message was delivered calmly, he said: “At the start, but then he got a bit angry. But it was alright.”

On Chelsea’s half-time changes, Conor Coady said on Sky Sports: “I think the credit goes down to [Alonso]. He made a bit of a statement by doing that at half-time. Four subs is a lot to do at half-time but it was needed. Listen, we said about the first half that it was kind of like the players hadn’t played with each other, and then the big guns came on... They were electric at times.”

As recently as March, a giant mural of Estêvão Willian appeared on the side of a building outside Stamford Bridge as the teenager threatened to become Chelsea’s number one poster boy.

He may no longer hold top billing outside the club, following a summer in which Chelsea spent a club-record £117m on Morgan Rogers to complete a fearsome front three alongside Cole Palmer and João Pedro.

A giant mural of the Brazilian teenager appeared on the side of a building close to Stamford Bridge in March.

But inside Stamford Bridge, nothing has changed. Estêvão remains one of Chelsea’s star boys and is part of an “untouchable” group of players that also includes Palmer, Rogers and Pedro. Sources believe Estêvão is a player the club will look at rewarding with an improved contract in the coming months.

Having already earned his Chelsea “untouchable” status, the next challenge for the 19-year-old winger – once nicknamed “Messinho” – is to make himself undroppable.

Estêvão may not be a perfect fit for Xabi Alonso’s three-at-the-back formation that features two 10s – Palmer and Rogers – behind a striker. But the boy from Brazil is certainly not being squeezed out and Chelsea’s manager has spoken of the need to be flexible.

Good evening and welcome to live coverage of tonight’s Carabao Cup third-round tie between Chelsea and Leeds United. This is a rematch of last season’s FA Cup semi-final which Chelsea edged by a single Enzo Fernández goal, but plenty has changed since then. Xabi Alonso has taken over the reins at Stamford Bridge and made a promising start, reeling off three wins before losing to Arsenal in an absorbing contest last Sunday. Chelsea are not involved in European competition this season, so the domestic cups represent an avenue to silverware, as well as an opportunity to tinker.

In Alonso’s way, though, stands a Leeds team whose manager, Daniel Farke, told the media on Tuesday that his side are aiming to win the Carabao Cup. Farke has overseen an impressive run of form, having lost only two of their previous 16 competitive matches in a stretch that extends back to March. Two wins at Nottingham Forest preceded draws against Brentford and Brighton to keep Leeds undefeated this term. Farke has suggested he will rotate in west London to “integrate new players” having brought in seven new recruits during the summer.

If Leeds are to reach the next round, they must reverse a heavily one-sided head-to-head record against their hosts, who have won each of their past 10 cup meetings (three in the League Cup, seven in the FA Cup). In fact, Chelsea have lost at this stage of the competition just once in the past 15 years, and that was against Pep Guardiola’s Manchester City four seasons ago.

Alonso broke up the exhilarating “JP Morgan” band (João Pedro, Cole Palmer and Morgan Rogers) for Chelsea’s victory over Luton Town in the previous round, handing starts to Danny Welbeck and Estêvão. Players such as Jamie Gittens and Geovany Quenda are also waiting in the wings and may get chances to impress tonight. Mike Penders could also be in line for a start between the sticks.
