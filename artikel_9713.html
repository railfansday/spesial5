Webflow Maintenance: What Still Needs Doing

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Posted on Nov 19 • Originally published at themazsolutions.com

Webflow sells itself partly on "no plugins, no server updates." That is true, and it is a real advantage over a self-hosted WordPress site. But it leads a lot of owners to a wrong conclusion: that a Webflow site needs no maintenance at all.

It needs less. Not none. The work just moves from the server to the things Webflow can't see, like your content, your forms, your integrations, and how the site is doing in search.

Here's what Webflow already handles, what you still need to check, and a monthly routine you can run yourself.

Webflow forms send notification emails to the addresses set in your site settings. Those addresses go stale when someone leaves, changes roles, or a mailbox gets shut down. If your form also posts into a CRM or a tool like Zapier or Make, that connection can break when someone changes a password, an API key expires, or a field is renamed.

Once a month, submit every form yourself and follow the message all the way to where someone actually reads it.

Open Site settings, look at the custom code in the head and footer, and ask of each snippet: do we still use this service? If nobody can say, it's a candidate for removal.

The CMS is where Webflow sites drift. Old blog posts with outdated prices, team pages listing people who left, event pages for events that already happened, and draft items nobody finished.

Look for items that are out of date or contradict each other, placeholder text that ended up live, and, if you publish often, how close you are to your plan's CMS item limit.

When you rename a page or change a CMS slug, the old URL stops working unless you add a 301 redirect in Site settings. Over a year of small edits, this adds up, and links from Google, social posts, and other sites start landing on your 404 page.

After any slug change, add the redirect at the same time. Every month or so, check Google Search Console for new "Not found (404)" pages.

Webflow serves images through its CDN, but it can't stop someone uploading a huge photo straight from a camera. Compress before upload, and use the Assets panel's option to convert images to WebP. Heavy interactions and background videos above the fold are worth a second look too.

Webflow gives you the fields: titles, meta descriptions, Open Graph images, and per-page indexing settings. It doesn't check that they are filled in sensibly. Collection templates are a common gap, where every CMS page ends up with the same title or no description.

Search Console is where you find out how Google actually sees the site. Look at the Pages report for pages that aren't indexed and why, and at the Performance report for which queries bring people in.
