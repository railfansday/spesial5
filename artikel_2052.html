Barnsley relegation problem identified as January transfer demand emerges

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

EFL content creator and Barnsley fan Callum Fowler believes that Barnsley are a striker away from climbing out of the bottom four in League One.

The Tykes' winless league run reached 10 games on Friday night at Oakwell against Sheffield Wednesday, as the Owls were clinical when needed, scoring twice in the opening 14 minutes and keeping the hosts at arm's length thereafter.

But it was another game in which Daniel Stendel's side outshot their opponents and had more touches in their box, and that's been the story of their season so far. Barnsley are poor in both boxes, hence their position at the foot of the League One table.

January is going to be crucial if the Reds are going to avoid a first fourth-tier campaign since the late 1970s, and a striker must be atop their shopping list.

Speaking on the weekly Pyramid podcast, EFL content creator and Barnsley supporter Callum Fowler admitted that he doesn't feel his side are playing badly, and that a striker is all that separates them from being adrift at the bottom of the third tier and mid-table.

The Reds have relied heavily on goals from the midfield so far this season. In all competitions, Cameron McGeehan is their top scorer with nine, closely followed by Vimal Yoganathan, who has eight, and Reyes Cleary is below on six.

Meanwhile, Portsmouth loanee Makenzie Kirk sits on four in all competitions, and just two in League One action. The Tykes fan ran through the squad and admitted, especially in midfield, that they're not a bad side, but don't have that focal point leading the line.

"When our nines are worse than me and you, and our depth is non-existent. The striker…imagine you replace your most important player with me up front; we don’t have a striker," he said.

"I look at the scores coming in, and Wycombe have scored, and every time it’s [Jayden] Wareham. If we just had someone like him up front...every team has a better striker than we do, 100%."

Fowler is almost resigned to Cleary leaving the club in the upcoming January transfer window, as the higher-ups look to maximise his value in case they do fall into League Two in the summer, and he hopes that they use whatever money they get from the deal to buy a recognised striker.

"It's got to be a thing in January; we know Cleary’s going to go, five million, whatever, if they do not spend that money..." he continued.

"They [Sheffield Wednesday] were better than us for 15 minutes; they had two shots all game. I’m saying, I know we’re bottom, but seriously, if we had a striker with legs we’d be about 14th, 13th."

This time last year, Barnsley were lining up with Davis Keillor-Dunn and David McGoldrick as their strikers. In less than 12 months, Tykes fans can't believe how they've been downgraded.

This past summer, the only striker who was bought at Oakwell was Ben Hamed Toure, whose time in South Yorkshire has yet to get going due to a devastating family bereavement. Maybe he can hit the ground running now he's back, but Barnsley fans aren't holding their breath.

The fact that they've had to resort to two free agents since the summer window closed is pretty telling in itself. But in Tobi Adeyemo, who scored one goal in League Two last season for a poor Crawley Town side, and Rylan Brownlie, who, in fairness, has looked lively, they've continued to sign unproven youngsters.

And they've been made to pay the price. Barnsley's attacking numbers indicate they'd be one of the best sides going forward in the division. Per Opta, they've taken the sixth-most shots, the 11th most on target, and have the seventh most touches in the opposition's box.

In reality, only AFC Wimbledon, Bradford City, Notts County and Peterborough United have scored fewer.

Sheffield Wednesday showed that you don't have to do much to beat Barnsley. Their two goals were incredibly easy to score, and while the Tykes controlled the game after that, they didn't do much to trouble the Owls' backline.

Wigan Athletic boss Gary Caldwell admitted after their 2-1 win over the Reds that he was happy to let Daniel Stendel's side have the ball and come onto them, as they often lose games when that's the case. In league games where Barnsley have had over 55% of the ball, their record is P8, W1, D1, L6.

And, ultimately, if Barnsley had that potent goalscorer leading the line, games in which they have the ball would see them punish sides much more often, and Daniel Stendel's playstyle, which isn't bad, would get much more out of this set of players.

Strikers are the ones who cost the big bucks, and while Neerav Parekh and co haven't been the best spenders in recent years, they need to pull out all the stops if their side is to remain in this division this season.
