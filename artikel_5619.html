The Trump administration proposed new regulations Monday that top officials say would “prevent fraud, remove bad actors, and protect the Americans who rely on” Medicare, Medicaid, and the Affordable Care Act marketplaces.

Trump’s health agencies are promising $90 billion in savings that would come back to taxpayers over the next decade. But that money would not come from stopping seedy criminals who overbill for hospice care or submit fraudulent claims for wheelchairs.

All of the savings, according to Medicare agency officials, would come from one policy that has been tossed around for years: more aggressive audits of Medicare Advantage plans.
