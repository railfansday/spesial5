What Are Thanksgiving Sides That Don't Need to Go in the Oven?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

There are millions of recipes on the internet, but you still don’t know what to cook. Tell us about it! Dear Bon Appétit is an advice column where we answer your culinary conundrums with handpicked recipes and well-seasoned advice. Submit a question here.

On Thanksgiving the oven is always precious real estate for the quintessential spread—stuffing, green bean casserole, sweet potatoes, and, of course, the turkey. In years past, the stress of getting everything in and out of the oven with the right amount of time has been a nuisance that I’d like to avoid this year. Are there any Thanksgiving sides that feel classic but don’t need the oven? —Oven-whelmed

If there was a proverbial dartboard of Thanksgiving problems, you have just hit the bull’s-eye. (And as someone who has never hit a real bull’s-eye in her life, I mean this as quite the compliment!) Even if you place oven racks strategically—even if you have one of those fancy double ovens—there’s always a bottleneck of dishes that need to be roasted and baked.

The solution is not to plot out an overwrought schedule of when things go in and out, and in and out, but to ruthlessly reduce the number of recipes that require the oven in the first place.

With that in mind, I would like to point out that turkey can thrive in other settings. And no, I’m not talking about deep-frying it in your yard, which we all know is a rowdy and risky idea, as proven in Gilmore Girls season three, episode nine. You could grill it. You could smoke it. Both delightful.

But you asked about sides specifically, so sides are what we will tackle today. Beyond the classics like mashed potatoes, boxed stuffing, and cranberry sauce, here are 10 recipes that do not need an oven at all—and brazenly, boldly get away with it.

Green Beans and Mushrooms With Crispy Shallots. The same cozy flavors as a classic green bean casserole—earthy mushrooms, frizzled shallots—but it comes together entirely on the stovetop. Now is the time to branch beyond white button or baby bella; opt for shiitake or oyster, the wonkier the shape the better.

Green Beans With Tahini Sbagliato. For something less traditional, from Samin Nosrat’s recent cookbook, this recipe is make-ahead-friendly on multiple fronts. You can blanch the green beans a day ahead and whip up the tahini-miso sauce up to four days ahead. All that’s left on Turkey Day is assembly.

Brussels Sprouts Stir-Fry With Cheddar and Golden Raisins. Thinly sliced and swiftly sautéed, brussels sprouts take on a savory char. The cheddar could be swapped out for Gouda, or any aged cheese you love. Likewise, the golden raisins could be replaced with dried cranberries or chopped dates. It’s flexible!

Air Fryer Crispy Brussels Sprouts With Honey Butter. Or free up the stove and turn to your trusty air fryer instead. In the concentrated and circulating heat, the sprouts’ outer leaves become crackly like potato chips, craveable enough to go back for thirds.

Sweet Potatoes With Tahini Butter. Most sweet potatoes in our archive are roasted, so isn’t it ironic that one of our most popular recipes is steamed. Author Carla Lalli puts it perfectly: “Steaming offers extreme speed and high hydration, which means that whatever you cook will be done quickly and always moist. Sweet potatoes in particular benefit from this treatment, which makes their flesh pudding-like and fluffy.”

Glazed Carrots. Li’l retro! These saucy carrots are sweet but not too sweet thanks to a floral mixture of orange juice and honey, plus a welcome zing from fresh ginger. I could see parsnips swapping in happily here.

Parsnip Purée. Speaking of parsnips, from the Barefoot Contessa herself, this is a cousin to mashed potatoes that, in my humble opinion, is a whole lot better. Parsnips have a je ne sais quois, needing no more than butter, salt, and pepper, to shine bright.

Adult Mac and Cheese. Despite its title, this dish will be a hit with grown-ups and kids alike. It comes together on the stove with just six ingredients (and that’s including salt!). A few of its rave reviews: “Staple now in my house,” “Super easy and always delicious!” and “Lovelovelove. I’ve made this so many times.” Medium shells catch the sauce like little canoes; or could be a fun moment for radiatori.

Kale Salad With Pecan Vinaigrette. Pecans are good for more than pie. Here they turn into a nutty dressing for ruffled kale and perky radishes. Though salad is not the first (or even second) thing one might think of for Thanksgiving, it has become an absolute must on my family’s table. A refreshing reprieve to all the richness. We have more great options here.

Instant Pot Collard Greens. On a holiday table, when there’s so much going on, sometimes a simple dish is just the thing. Bacon, greens, onion, chile, done. An Instant Pot is like a Thanksgiving cheat code, freeing up the oven and the stove too.
