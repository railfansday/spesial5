Make the Skirt Your Fall Personality

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Every product on this page was chosen by a Harper's BAZAAR editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

Thanks to Prada, the skirt has never felt fresher

The collection seen around the world this year was, by all accounts, Prada’s Spring/Summer 2027 show, which served as something of an edict on the skirt. Composed of 63 looks, each featuring a distinct iteration in varying colors and fabrications, the presentation was nothing short of a skirt master class from Miuccia Prada. Following that spectacle, you would have been hard-pressed not to spot a skirt on nearly every runway, be it the gold-leaf pencil skirts at Saint Laurent or the many offerings from Julian Klausner’s Dries Van Noten, which brought to light another unsung hero as of late: the ocelot print!

Though the silhouette felt decidedly new under the glamour of the spring collections, the truth of the matter is that skirts made a similar splash during the fall shows. However, the garment itself wasn’t necessarily the prevailing message. Rather, fall/winter 2026 signaled a return to a more considered approach to dressing, with perhaps no better champion of this sensibility than Matthieu Blazy at Chanel, whose interpretation of the wardrobe staple helped make it feel novel once again.

Of course, as temperatures drop, the natural inclination is to reach for clothing that provides maximum coverage, which often means an onslaught of pants, whether in heavy wool or denim. However, all hope is not lost for the garment of the moment as we inch towards brisker weather. The key to making it work during the colder months comes down to a thoughtful approach to layering, as well as opting for styles in substantial fabrics that can stand up to the chill.

Below, you’ll find eight skirt styles, like those in leather or denim, and how to wear them for everything from weekend adventures to boardroom meetings without feeling too buttoned-up.

Perhaps Matthieu Blazy’s design prowess could be likened to the Midas touch, because just as he has helped bring the skirt back into relevance, so too has he turned to the quarter-zip. Channel a bit of Chanel’s Métiers d’Art 2026 collection by pairing an oatmeal-colored cashmere-blend quarter-zip with a tartan skirt adorned with fringe, and introduce some daytime drama with a pair of shoulder-duster earrings. To finish things off, reach for croc-embossed Mary Jane pumps and a stark white shoulder bag for just the right amount of ladies-who-lunch panache.

One of the great pleasures of fall is venturing outside the city for some much-needed fresh country air. Channel a more pastoral approach to dressing with a long, box-pleated corduroy skirt paired with a lightweight cashmere turtleneck and a plaid shirt. Top everything off with a cropped puffer jacket featuring an aviator-inspired shearling collar, a detail echoed in the retro sensibility of a pair of shield sunglasses.

Though Western dressing seems to be an ever-present trend, there’s no denying that the aesthetic has become increasingly dominant in recent seasons. To channel it without looking like an extra in a rodeo show, the key is to mix hallmarks of the style, such as suede and turquoise accents, be it in the form of a concho belt or jewelry, with more everyday pieces. Consider a vintage-inspired gray hoodie and croc-embossed high-shaft boots that evoke the old West without feeling overwrought. Finish with a cropped bomber jacket in a similar hue to the skirt to create the impression of a matching set.

When it comes to fall dressing, it’s quite natural to gravitate toward a palette of muted tones and neutrals, perhaps punctuated by an interesting pop of red or even the color of the season, purple. However, don’t be afraid to go against the grain and embrace a more saturated palette, particularly with holiday gatherings on the horizon. Consider this hostess-ready taffeta ball skirt in a shocking pinkish-red hue, paired with a pink button-down shirt and a studded enamel starfish ring set with peridot. To temper the abundance of color, introduce touches of white in the form of a topcoat and retro-inspired kitten heels finished with a bow, lending the outfit a welcome sense of balance.

Give the traditional prep-school uniform a contemporary twist by pairing an A-line denim skirt with a striped long-sleeve polo shirt and an unconventional gray wool blazer with double lapels. Instead of the typical boot, consider opting for the trendy optic-white jazz shoe for an unexpected touch. For some added visual interest and warmth, introduce a scarf in a complementary hue.

A leather skirt can, at times, feel a bit severe, so consider softening its appearance with your go-to white T-shirt and a V-neck sweater in a complementary shade of gray. For a downtown cool factor, reach for a pair of slip-on loafers in lieu of heels or a boot. Finish with a statement cuff for added polish.

Office attire has become considerably more relaxed in recent years, letting personal style shine. A utilitarian skirt turns traditional workwear on its head, but pair it with familiar staples, such as a striped monk-collar shirt and a knitted vest, both rendered in unexpected color combinations. Finish with a herringbone overcoat and second-skin wine-colored booties for a stylish ensemble that feels anything but corporate.

In recent seasons, we’ve seen a variety of animal prints come into play, well beyond the ubiquitous leopard, making room for everything from zebra and ocelot to tiger. These patterns are statement makers on their own, which is precisely why they work so well next to understated pieces. Consider styling them with a black short-sleeved cashmere T-shirt and wool cardigan, which allows the print to command attention without overwhelming. Add a waxed-cotton jacket and riding boots for an interesting counterpoint that feels both practical and playful.
