I Own 200 Perfumes and Baccarat Rouge Is Still My Favorite of All Time

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

It's refined and fresh, but also woodsy and sexy...and smells a little bit different on everyone.

When I first moved to New York, my dream was to become the chicest version of myself. I wanted to Carrie Bradshaw-ify my life, if you will. I vowed to take pilates classes, read literature on the subway, drink extra dirty martinis, and smell like a grown adult. This meant I needed to invest in a ~grown adult~ perfume, and I had my sights (errrr, nose?) set on Maison Francis Kurkdjian's Baccarat Rouge 540.

This was before Baccarat Rouge became an iconic perfume in the eyes of PerfumeTok. I'd go to Nordstrom every week to sneak a few spritzes from the displays. At the time, there was nothing else that smelled like it. There were no other jasmine perfumes laced with sugar and woods. It felt like lightning in a bottle back then.

Fast forward five years, and although I haven’t reached fancy New Yorker-status (yet!), I am a beauty editor with an expert nose. In that time my perfume palette expanded, and so did Baccarat Rouge 540's popularity. What once was known as this $$$$ scent that was worn by royalty and A-listers developed a cult-like following on TikTok for being the sexiest perfume. But does it still hold up to those high standards? After years away, I finally bought my own bottle to see if this ultra-famous scent is still as beautiful as I remember.

Key notes: Saffron, jasmine, amberwood, ambergris, fir resin, cedar, ambroxan, sugar, oakmoss

It’s nearly impossible to put Baccarat Rouge 540 into one box or fragrance category because it has a kaleidoscopic aura to it. It’s airy, spicy, woody, metallic, and warm, but also so much more than these descriptors.

The scent, which launched in 2015, was inspired by the 250th anniversary of luxury crystal brand Baccarat. There's a dazzling quality to the perfume that evokes the shimmery look of Baccarat fine crystal—it's like a champagne toast in the opening, with bright saffron and jasmine that create a twinkling essence. Then, it goes a bit mineral and fresh with amber wood and ambergris. But as it dries down, you get a more woodsy, warm tinge of the fragrance. Amber wood, cedar, and fir resin create depth, but instead of going smoky, sugary notes add a sweetness that brings you right back to that glistening quality.

It is definitely polarizing, though. In reviews, some people say it smells really sweet—faintly of cotton candy or vanilla—and nothing else. Others mention it smells like a fancy hotel lobby that makes them feel like they have their whole life figured out.

When I first saw the price of BR540, my jaw literally dropped. (I was fresh out of college, with an entry-level salary, FYI.) However, the high price tag could make anyone’s eyes bug out of their head. Why on earth is this perfume so expensive? Well, it’s a combination of things, including its potency, performance, rare ingredients, and the hype behind this scent.

First, Maison Francis Kurkdjian uses the highest-quality ingredients—some of which take years to source—to create the immaculate juice. No penny is spared in the final product, from the hefty glass bottle to the strong potion inside. The high-end ingredients are part of the reason why this has such impressive longevity. You’ll still be able to smell this at the end of the day even if you only applied one or two spritzes. And, of course, the celebrity fans, as well as social media hype, have increased demand—and also the price.

Baccarat Rouge 540 really does smell a little bit different on everyone, thanks to the ambroxan note (which is a synthetic compound that has a warm, musky scent that interacts a lot with your skin chemistry, so it smells a bit different on everyone). Once dry, it goes really sweet on my skin. I get all the sugary, jasmine sweetness. It's yummy and gourmand—not really this spicy, fir, woodsy scent that it seems like it'd be from the note breakdown.

It's kinda warm and cozy, but not in that fall-perfume way you'd expect. It reminds me a lot of how my skin would smell after taking a shower. There's a clean, subtle aroma that sits right against my skin. It makes me feel powerful, but not because it's strong or intense. Rather, it simply feels like an enhanced version of my own skin. In my opinion, that's the sexiest way to smell.

One thing's for sure, though: Baccarat Rouge 540 definitely leaves an impression and gets compliments. I spritzed this on at the office—on a day when basically no one was in, by the way—and multiple people from all over our floor came up to my desk to say it smelled good "over here."

It's been years (and a lot of bottles of other, similar scents) since I've played with Baccarat Rouge 540. So, admittedly, I thought I’d feel meh about it at this point. But my obsession has been fully renewed. I can't stop spraying the perfume, and every time I wear it, I smell myself all day long (something that doesn't necessarily happen with many of the other perfumes I own). I've been keeping the bottle by my desk, and I had to move it, because I was spraying it too often and wasting the bottle (lol, even a beauty editor doesn't wanna waste precious BR540).

Here's the thing, though: Obviously, it's expensive as heck. You don't need to spend hundreds of dollars on a perfume. But if you want to invest in a fragrance that you can wear legit all the time, for every occasion, during every single season—I'm a believer in the Baccarat Rouge hype.
