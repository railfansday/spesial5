The Porsche 911, Taycan, Panamera and Cayenne Are Quintupling Their Infotainment Power

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

The carmaker's latest system packs AI-powered voice controls and a host of other new features.

Raw power has never been what the Porsche 911 is all about. After all, a $73,795 2027 Corvette Stingray makes more horsepower than a $239,850 911 GT3. However, Porsche has just revealed a major power upgrade coming to the 911, as well as the Taycan, Panamera, and Cayenne. Unfortunately, it won't help any of them set quicker lap times, because the extra power is going straight to the infotainment system.

Porsche claims that the new generation of Porsche Communication Management (PCM), announced Tuesday, will bring up to five times more computing power than its predecessor. That means new hardware architecture delivers faster startup times, shorter loading times, and smoother operation when running multiple applications simultaneously.

Of course, quicker response times are good, but the PCM upgrade goes far beyond that. The new system brings Porsche Digital Interaction—first introduced in the electric Cayenne—to the 911, Taycan, Panamera, and internal combustion-powered and hybrid Cayenne models. The interface features five selectable themes, customizable widgets, and a three-dimensional representation of the owner's actual vehicle.

And Porsche owners that love to admire their car in the garage will be pleased to learn that they can now admire their car while they are sitting in it. Drivers can rotate a 3D virtual car in its actual exterior color on the display. Access to certain vehicle settings is available directly through manipulation of the virtual car.

Porsche is also introducing an upgraded AI-powered Voice Pilot. Built around a large language model, the system can understand natural speech, address follow-up questions, and take on requests involving multiple steps. Drivers can use it to control navigation, entertainment, selected vehicle functions, and compatible applications without taking their hands off the steering wheel. There’s also Navigation Plus, which will come with three-dimensional maps and real-time traffic information. (That's a far cry from trying to follow a paper map, as many longtime Porsche owners will undoubtedly remember.) For electric models, Porsche's Charging Planner can identify charging stops along a route while accounting for battery preconditioning.

There was a time when Blaupunkt radios were at the top of the Porsche audio food chain. Those days are long gone. With the newest PCM, Apple Music is available directly through the Porsche App Center, while compatible Bose and Burmester sound systems support Dolby Atmos spatial audio. Improved smartphone integration and over-the-air software updates round out the upgrades.

Porsche says the new infotainment system will gradually arrive in newly produced vehicles, with availability depending on the model, market, and production date. None of this will impress that Corvette driver in the next lane, but there's more to the Porsche driving experience than horsepower, and it's about to get even better.
