Anthropic's market debut comes in a week Wall Street is on pace for its worst in over a year

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Anthropic stock fell 6% before the opening bell, and the Dow is down 6.2% for the week after a drop of more than 2% on Thursday

Anthropic stock fell 6% before the opening bell on Friday, a day after the company's public market debut, as a selloff in technology stocks weighed on U.S. futures.

Futures tied to the Nasdaq $NDAQ-100 lost 1.5%, those for the S&P 500 edged down 0.7%, and Dow Jones Industrial Average contracts declined by 15 points. AMD $AMD stock slid 6%, Nvidia $NVDA stock fell 2% and Micron $MU Technology stock dropped 4.6%.

The major U.S. averages all dropped over 2% on Thursday, and the Dow ended beneath 50,000, its first finish under that level since June, according to CNBC. The Dow is down 6.2% for the week, on track for its steepest weekly loss since April 2025. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq have each lost 5% and are poised for their biggest weekly drops in over a year.

The S&P 500 is on track for a seventh straight down day, which would be its longest losing streak since 2020, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Anthropic's debut failed to calm worries over AI stocks, according to The Wall Street Journal. "The AI buildout still needs vast amounts of funding just as financing costs are rising and signs of credit stress are emerging," Swissquote's Ipek Ozkardeskaya said, according to The Wall Street Journal. She added that the possibility of stricter chip-export rules is a worry.

Deutsche Bank strategist Binky Chadha told CNBC that stocks look "very oversold" even though economic expansion and corporate profits are still solid. “The broader market, whether you look at positioning, whether you look at valuation ... we are sort of completely washed out,” he said. Chadha said the selloff started as investors moved money away from technology shares and then spread across the rest of the market.

Markets abroad fell. Japan’s Nikkei 225 fell 5.1%, finishing at 56,436.65, while South Korea’s Kospi lost 5.2% and ended at 4,869.96. Taiwan's TAIEX fell by over 6%, marking its weakest week since 1990, according to The Wall Street Journal. European tech stocks were down more than 3%.

U.S. Treasury yields fell as investors sought safer assets. The 10-year note's yield slid beneath 5%, and the dollar stayed close to its strongest level in a year.

Traders will watch the final November reading of the University of Michigan's consumer sentiment report on Friday. BJ's Wholesale is scheduled to release its earnings ahead of the opening bell.
