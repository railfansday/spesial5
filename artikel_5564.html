Qantas is cutting about 850 jobs in an AI-driven deal with Accenture

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Qantas expects the first wave of changes by mid-2027, with some corporate work moving to Accenture teams in India

Qantas Group signed a multi-year partnership with Accenture $ACN that will move some corporate work to India and affect around 850 roles over the coming years, the company said on Tuesday.

Qantas expects the initial round of changes to take effect by the middle of 2027. The company will consult with affected employees and their unions before individual outcomes are determined. It plans to redeploy people into other roles across the group where possible, including new roles being created in Australia.

Some services provided by Qantas corporate functions will move to Accenture, the company said. Most of the work will be handled by Accenture teams in India, with some done in Australia, and the services are to be changed through automation and AI over time. The changes will first reach the finance, marketing and procurement teams, according to the Australian Broadcasting Corp., which cited an all-staff email from Qantas Group CEO Vanessa Hudson.

Accenture will make an upfront investment to modernize the group’s data platforms, Qantas said. The airline relies on over 1,000 older software applications that were built without any intention of operating as a connected system.

"Standing still is not an option for us," Hudson said in a statement. "We know this will be difficult news for some of our people and this decision is not a reflection of their contribution or commitment."

Qantas stock rose in Sydney trading on Tuesday.

The Australian Services Union said it would hold a meeting on Tuesday with members who work at Qantas, according to ABC. Scott Cowen, the union’s assistant national secretary, said the union was told during earlier discussions that no plan existed to offshore 1,000 jobs, yet staff learned Tuesday that up to 850 could be sent offshore, according to ABC.

Cowen called the news shocking for members and said most of the affected jobs are based in Sydney. He said the union would fight for every job and warned that other companies could follow Qantas without regulation, according to ABC.

Qantas said it is a net employer and has created more than 4,400 operational roles since 2023, including pilots, cabin crew and engineers. The company said its Australian workforce totals 28,000.
