Two Directors, One Oscar: Can Lord and Miller, Los Javis, Fielder and Oppenheim or the Esiri Brothers Break Through?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Awards Circuit Column: The Academy’s Directors Branch has given best director to a duo only three times in 98 years. This season, four teams are vying for attention

Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim, Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi, Phil Lord and Chris Miller and Nigeria’s Esiri brothers are chasing a prize the Academy’s Director Branch has handed to a duo only three times in its history.

Looking at the two-name directing teams in this year’s Oscar race, artistic collaboration seems to be having a moment. Not everyone sees it that way. “If you need two of you to make the film, then I think that’s a problem,” one prominent member of the Directors Branch bluntly tells Variety.

Fielder and Oppenheim turned Elizabeth Holmes’ saga into the slippery A24 documentary “You Can See Everything.” Calvo and Ambrossi, the Spanish duo known as Los Javis, are carrying their country’s international feature hopes with “La Bola Negra.” Lord and Miller reunited behind the camera for Amazon MGM’s sci-fi swing “Project Hail Mary.” And twin brothers Arie and Chuko Esiri helmed Neon’s ambitious “Clarissa,” a reimagining of “Mrs. Dalloway.”

All four teams face a stubborn piece of Academy history: The Directors Branch does not like splitting statuettes. In 98 years, only four directing duos have received best director nominations (one of them twice). This year, several are within earshot of nods.

Robert Wise and Jerome Robbins got there first for 1961’s “West Side Story.” Their partnership was so combustible that Robbins was fired mid-production for running over schedule and budget. He retained his credit and collected the Oscar.

Warren Beatty and Buck Henry followed with “Heaven Can Wait” (1978), but lost to Michael Cimino (“The Deer Hunter”). Joel and Ethan Coen scored for the best picture winner “No Country for Old Men” (2007), and were nominated again for the Western remake “True Grit” (2010), losing out to Tom Hooper (“The King’s Speech”). The most recent winners, Daniel Kwan and Daniel Scheinert, aka the Daniels, took home the prize for “Everything Everywhere All at Once” (2022).

History suggests that longtime professional partnerships are the key to acceptance. Wise and Robbins were a filmmaker and a choreographer brought together by a studio. The Daniels met making music videos. The Coens remain the only sibling duo recognized.

The Academy has repeatedly overlooked pairings, even in the face of precursor support. Jonathan Dayton and Valerie Faris, the married filmmakers behind “Little Miss Sunshine” (2006), won SAG ensemble and PGA honors and earned a DGA feature nom. The Oscars’ directing branch still shut them out.

The Safdie brothers were similarly snubbed for “Uncut Gems” (2019), but when Josh Safdie made his solo debut with “Marty Supreme” last year, the branch gave him a nod. Even the Wachowskis couldn’t crack the category for the revolutionary technical achievement of “The Matrix” (1999).

This season’s teams fit familiar types. Lord and Miller and Fielder and Oppenheim resemble the Daniels’ professional partnership, while the Esiri siblings are following the Coens’ path. Los Javis are the wild card: The couple’s roughly 13-year romantic relationship ended while they were making “La Bola Negra,” their most personal and ambitious collaboration.

“The first question other directors will ask is ‘Why did you need two people to do this?’” one veteran strategist says. “The job is to convince a bunch of directors who often think they are the most creative artists alive that their genius can be shared. That’s tough.”

None of the four teams is a conventional frontrunner. Fielder and Oppenheim are competing in a documentary lane the Directors Branch has always kept separate. Los Javis are more likely to make noise in international feature and screenplay because theirs is a non-English-language film. Lord and Miller are the closest thing to a studio-scale contender, but genre films need to overperform in below-the-line races to enter the conversation.

Still, the sheer number of teams is the story. Four duos in one year reflects an industry increasingly built on shared creative enterprise, even as many directors cling to the myth of the solo genius.
