Marvel Officially Confirms Whether RDJ's Doctor Doom Is A Tony Stark Variant

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

When Marvel Studios announced Robert Downey Jr. would play Doctor Doom in "Avengers: Doomsday" fans used one word to describe it: desperate. But anticipation for RDJ's return has only grown as "Doomsday" has drawn nearer. Still, significant confusion remains. After all, Downey Jr.'s best-known role is that of Tony Stark/Iron Man in the Marvel Cinematic Universe. Now, he's back in that same franchise as a supervillain? It's all a bit confounding, really, and while Marvel Studios head Kevin Feige has just tried to clear it all up, he's also somehow made things even more confusing.

Speaking to Empire, Feige was asked whether RDJ is playing a Tony Stark variant in "Doomsday" or an entirely different character. The Marvel Studios boss seemed to indicate the latter is true. "Is Steve Rogers a Johnny Storm variant?" he said in reference to Chris Evans having played both characters at different points throughout Marvel's cinematic history. "People wondered, 'Is Doom just a variant of another character? Is this just a shortcut to bringing back another character?' The answer is no. He's Victor Von Doom."

Evans played Johnny Storm in a an entirely different cinematic universe, specifically portraying the Fantastic Four member in the 20th Century Fox Marvel movies. He then played Steve Rogers/Captain America in the Marvel Cinematic Universe. Downey Jr., however, played Tony Stark in the MCU and is now back as Victor Von Doom in that same universe. Sure, the multiverse has made all of this more nebulous. We saw Evans' Johnny Storm in "Deadpool & Wolverine," which means both he and Evans' Steve Rogers could, conceivably, appear together in the MCU. But it would surely feel like a letdown to bring back the most popular and important actor in MCU history as a villain and let that be that.

Doctor Doom was once Iron Man in the comics, and in 2010's "What If? Iron Man: Demon in an Armor" an amnesiac Tony Stark became the supervillain. But Kevin Feige has seemingly confirmed the Victor Von Doom of "Avengers: Doomsday" is like neither of these examples.

According to the Marvel Studios head, Robert Downey Jr. is playing an entirely separate character to Tony Stark, likely thanks to the multiversal incursions that threaten to collapse the universe in "Doomsday." What we'll essentially see, then, is a character named Victor Von Doom who hails from an entirely different timeline to the main MCU (likely the one that gave us 2025's "Fantastic Four: First Steps"), has no connection to Tony Stark, and just happens to look like the former Iron Man. I feel confident in saying that this only raises more questions. Is there another Tony Stark in Doom's timeline? Is there really no connection at all between RDJ's villain and the Iron Man of the MCU?

Regardless, "Doomsday" directors Joe and Anthony Russo promised Empire that this Doctor Doom will be a truly scary villain, with Joe saying he's "a Gothic character" and "a very powerful character with some very dark magic" who's "not above selling his soul to get what he wants. He's worth being scared of." Interestingly, the co-director added that RDJ's Doom is a "father who's a victim of trauma" and that he has "lost a child." The only parallel there between Downey Jr.'s Tony Stark and his Doom is that the former was once particularly shaken by losing Tom Holland's Spider-Man to Thanos' snap in 'Avengers: Infinity War." Otherwise, he's never lost a child, which is further proof that this is a different character and not a Tony Stark variant.

According to Joe Russo, those who've seen early screenings of "Avengers: Doomsday" have said "it's a very scary movie" due to Robert Downey Jr.'s Doctor Doom being "so profoundly damaged." Clearly, the loss of his child is what drives this particular incarnation of Doom — which puts some of the "Avengers: Doomsday" trailer footage in context. In the trailer, we hear Vanessa Kirby's Sue Storm/Invisible Woman saying, "Victor [...] used to be different. He used to be kind, he used to be caring. And then, everything he loved was taken from him." This is obviously in reference to the loss of his child. In that same trailer, Doom accuses all the heroes of having lived "stolen lives," suggesting that something they did in previous MCU installments might have triggered the event that led to his child's death.

Unfortunately, while Downey Jr. did talk to Empire, he didn't shed much light on the Tony Stark variant question. Instead he talked about Michael Douglas at the end of "Falling Down," saying "He goes, 'I'm the bad guy?' I had that realization a couple of days into shooting 'Doomsday,' because all the heroes are over there, and I'm the problem." The actor recalled having "leaned in" to being the villain and having a "glorious time." That's lovely Rob, but are you Iron Man or not? Otherwise, he described Doom as being on a "scorched earth" mission, adding "Whatever you think he may be entertaining, I would ten times it, and you'll probably be closer to the ballpark."

Did Marvel really bring back RDJ without intending to at least hint at Tony Stark's possible return? It seems unbelievable. For now, we'll have to wait until "Doomsday" hits theaters on December 18, 2026 to find out.
