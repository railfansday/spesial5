The 6 Best Thrillers Released Between 2020 and Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The thrill of a thriller is how the story makes you feel. A great thriller film relies on sustained tension, high stakes, and constant uncertainty that keeps you on the edge of your seat. A great thriller works by manipulating how and when the audience learns crucial information. Often featuring tight editing, sharp sound design, dim lighting, and deliberate pacing to suffocate the comfort zone and build toward a memorable climax, a thriller becomes legendary when it leaves a lasting impression.

The 21st century has provided some extraordinary entries within the genre, but this current decade seems to be turning into a profound golden age. Since 2020, the thriller has become brilliantly risky, mixing big-budget masterpieces with mind-blowing indie projects that have altered the landscape. With so many brilliant films over the last six years, the six on this list have emerged as the very best. From game-changing horror thrillers to psychological dramas that mess with your mind, these six films became instant classics the moment they were released. What’s your favorite thriller of the 2020s?

In the highly underrated Pig, directed by Michael Sarnoski, Rob Feld (Nicolas Cage) is a reclusive truffle hunter living in the Oregon wilderness whose beloved foraging pig is suddenly stolen. To rescue her, he must return to his past life in the Portland culinary scene and confront the grief he left behind. It is a quiet, meditative drama about loss, artistic expression, and connection, not a traditional revenge thriller. By subverting genre tropes, Pig became the antithesis of thrillers, honing in on themes of authenticity and the power of connection.

Initially set up to look like a gritty "John Wick with a pig," where a hermit tracks down his stolen pet, the film abandons blood and retribution in favor of deep conversations and emotional vulnerability. Cage delivers a grounded, mournful, and restrained performance, moving into a new chapter of his storied career. The dynamic between Rob and his young, status-obsessed truffle buyer Amir (Alex Wolff) acts as a mirror for both to face their traumas head-on. The film becomes a poetic examination of how people process loss, regret, and the difference between fake artifice and genuine passion. Pig is a mesmerizing and moving story of love and loss that redefined how a thriller can be presented. ​​​​​​​

Don’t sleep on foreign films! The 2020s have seen a rise of foreign films finding their way into the Hollywood mainstream. Such was the case for Anatomy of a Fall. Directed by Justine Triet, the psychological courtroom thriller follows Sandra Voyter (Sandra Hüller), a German writer accused of murdering her French husband, Samuel Maleski (Samuel Theis), after he falls to his death from their secluded alpine chalet. With their visually impaired son, Daniel Maleski (Milo Machado-Graner), as the sole witness, the trial transforms from a forensic investigation into an intense, public dissection of the couple's turbulent marriage and conflicting realities. Ultimately, Anatomy of a Fall explores the ambiguous boundaries of truth, language, and memory when a relationship is put on trial.

The film emerged as a legal and psychological thriller because it treats the "truth" as an elusive, multi-layered question rather than a simple puzzle to be solved. It turns a murder investigation into a surgical dissection of an ordinary, flawed, and decaying relationship. Through the ambiguity of truth, the narrative explores how personal history, bias, and fiction can be weaponized in a courtroom. The film put the sensational Hüller on the map thanks to her stoic, razor-sharp, and deeply vulnerable performance. Earning the Palme d'Or at Cannes, Anatomy of a Fall strips away genre clichés to offer a haunting look at how private grief is put on public trial.

You’ll never look at your food the same after watching The Menu. Directed by Mark Mylod and written by Seth Reiss and Will Tracy, the film follows a young couple, Tyler (Nicholas Hoult) and Margot (Anya Taylor-Joy), and a group of ultra-wealthy elites who travel to a remote island to dine at an exclusive restaurant. The evening takes a sinister turn as the enigmatic celebrity chef, Chef Slowik (Ralph Fiennes), reveals that each conceptual course is part of a calculated, deadly plan to punish his pretentious guests. The Menu serves as a sharp satire on class divide, foodie culture, and the exploitation of the service industry.

The Menu stands out as an acid-tongued examination of the pretentious, hyper-exclusive fine-dining industry, using it as a microcosm to skewer classism, economic inequality, and the vanity of the ultra-wealthy. Set almost entirely on a remote private island, the movie builds claustrophobic, uninterrupted tension where every course on the menu escalates the dread. With biting thrills and shocking twists, The Menu delivers a delicious dish in which the audience laughs wholly at absurd foodie culture one moment and feels genuinely disturbed the next. Fiennes commands the screen as the terrifying, cult-leader-like Chef Slowik, while Taylor-Joy masters the grounded outsider who becomes the beacon of promise. The Menu brilliantly keeps you off-guard until the very last dish is presented. ​​​​​​​

A masterclass in modernizing a classic text, The Invisible Man brings fear, terror, and profound social commentary. Written and directed by Leigh Whannell, The Invisible Man tells the story of Cecilia Kass (Elisabeth Moss) as she escapes her abusive, wealthy optics scientist boyfriend, Adrian Griffin (Oliver Jackson-Cohen). After Adrian seemingly commits suicide, Cecilia becomes convinced she is being stalked and terrorized by an unseen, invisible force. With no one else believing her claims, she must fight independently to uncover the deadly truth and save herself. The Invisible Man earns its honorable reputation by transforming a classic sci-fi monster tale into a tense, grounded study of domestic abuse and gaslighting.

Instead of focusing on the mad scientist, the narrative centers on the victim. It mirrors the psychological isolation of real-world coercive control and gaslighting, where no one believes that an abuser is still haunting you. Whannell frequently frames Cecilia off-center in wide shots, leaving empty hallways and seemingly vacant rooms on screen, forcing you to scan the background, generating constant paranoia and suspense. Moss delivers an intensely physical performance, reacting to empty space, silent dread, and invisible physical force with visceral realism. It’s one of her best performances to date. The film is a masterclass in thrills, putting Whannell on the visionary map. ​​​​​​​

2026 is the year of low-budget horror masterpieces. Leading the charge is the incomparable Obsession. Written and directed by Curry Barker, the story follows a socially awkward man named Bear Bailey (Michael Johnston) who uses a cursed novelty toy called the One Wish Willow to force his coworker crush, Nikki Freeman (Inde Navarrette), to fall in love with him. While his wish initially seems to come true, Nikki's affection quickly warps into an extreme, violent, and terrifyingly possessive fixation. As the supernatural compulsion takes over, Bear finds himself trapped in a dangerous nightmare of his own making that escalates into fatal consequences. Earning itself a spot as a towering cinematic phenomenon, Obsession brilliantly injects terror into a seemingly basic "be careful what you wish for" setup.

Shot on a microbudget, Obsession shocked the world by grossing over $500 million worldwide. The viral word-of-mouth campaign worked as audiences were taken by the brilliant premise and conversations it sparked. On the surface, the film plays with a classic Faustian premise that becomes a brutal critique of male entitlement. The true psychological horror doesn't come from a masked killer, but from Bear’s willingness to strip away Nikki's autonomy and stay with her terrifying, cursed doppelgänger just to preserve his own wish-fulfillment fantasy. The film’s instant legendary status was earned through the dynamic duo of Johnston and Navarette. Barker builds suspense through classic tricks and craftsmanship. The boxed-in frame physically traps the audience with the characters, amplifying the intense feelings of domestic captivity and dread. It’s a masterclass in making silence itself feel threatening, using subtle tonality shifts and unsettling audio rumbles to make viewers physically uncomfortable. Obsession continues the trend of new voices and faces reshaping the genre's landscape. ​​​​​​​

One of the most groundbreaking and masterful thrillers in decades, Weapons is sheer brilliance. Written and directed by Zach Cregger, the supernatural mystery-horror thriller centers on a small town thrown into chaos after seventeen third-grade children from the same classroom inexplicably vanish into the dark at exactly 2:17 AM. The story utilizes a non-linear, multi-perspective narrative to follow a pariah teacher, Justine Gandy (Julia Garner), a grieving father, Archer Graff (Josh Brolin), and other local figures as they unearth a sinister web of blood rituals, police corruption, and witchcraft. Beneath its terrifying mystery is a story centered on the eccentric Aunt Gladys (Amy Madigan). Weapons serves as a deeper, personal allegory exploring generational trauma, grief, and the destructive nature of addiction.

In a time when franchises and blockbusters dominated Hollywood, Weapons delivered something genuinely ambitious, unnerving, and profound. Cregger weaves an intricate web across six distinct chapters, building tension purely through withheld information. Shot by cinematographer Larkin Seiple, the film utilizes constant, creeping tracking shots and slow zooms that refuse to let the audience look away. The claustrophobic nature is matched by a haunting sound design, generating visceral dread even when the horrors occur entirely off-screen. The film effortlessly balances bleak, psychological terror with sharp, pitch-black humor. Its tonal agility lets it shift from pure terror to surreal absurdity without ever breaking its spell. The film earned high honors thanks to Madigan’s iconic performance. She ensured Aunt Gladys earned a spot in the horror villain hall of fame. Weapons is a thriller masterpiece that became the new gold standard.
