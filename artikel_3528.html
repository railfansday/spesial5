Democrats Plot (and Party) in Washington After Midterms Rout

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

This is the November 12, 2026 edition of Party Animals, a letter from the back rooms, barstools, and bacchanals of Washington, DC—with exclusive reporting, interviews, and analysis.

Good evening from Washington, where Party Animals is coming to grips with how the social scene in the capital will change amidst a dramatic transfer of power. Gone will be the heady days of President Donald Trump’s total control of the US government. The next two years will be defined by how his administration handles a muscular Democratic opposition.

At Crooked Con, a conference put on the weekend after the midterm elections by the media company founded by former Obama staffers and Pod Save America hosts Jon Favreau, Tommy Vietor, and Jon Lovett, I got a sense of just how confident the party is after almost two years in the wilderness.

Befitting the sensibilities of the incoming Congress, Crooked week kicked off with a surprise appearance from Senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed, who wore keffiyeh-patterned Air Force 1s to the live taping of his Pod Save America episode. The crowd went ballistic.

In this edition of Party Animals, the view from Washington’s new libbed-up social scene; what a Newsmax show says about Trump’s tumbling popularity among young Americans; and my colleague Joe Hagan’s interview with Beto O’Rourke, the original Democrat-who-might-win-Texas, about how the party finally pulled it off in the Lone Star State.

Also mentioned in this issue: Barrett Adair, Matthew Foldi, Robert Garcia, Katie Miller, Felicia Schwartz, Keith Edwards, Vaughn Hillyard, Marc Beckman, Marco Rubio, Michael Scherer, Chi Ossé, and more.

Got tips? Hit the Party Animals hotline: aidan_mclaughlin@condenast.com. Or text me—my number is 917-817-8266. Complaints will be aired on Newsmax. Praise will be debated and affirmed by a Crooked Con panel.

I doubt there were many places on earth more buoyant than Crooked Con in DC last weekend. The conference brought together a legion of newly elected Democrats, veteran members of Congress, journalists, and liberal content creators for a day of live plotting that culminated in a party at City Tap House, where Robert Garcia, the incoming emperor of Oversight, worked the room. “I was pretty optimistic going into the election, but this is beyond anything I imagined,” Favreau tells Party Animals. “The majority of the country is clearly ready to move on from the Trump show and give Democrats another chance.”

The creators were ecstatic. At an after-after-party nearby, Barrett Adair, a platinum blonde progressive with more than 200,000 TikTok followers, bounced around in a hot-pink latex dress. Pints were passed about so generously that once I had acquired three, I stopped bothering to tell which was which. Keith Edwards and Chi Ossé chatted intently in the corner. The night felt like a portent of what was to come: After nearly two years of the MAGA Huns marauding freely throughout the capital, the libs were soon to be back in charge—of Congress, at least. They celebrated accordingly.

“There's a lot to feel good about,” said Favreau. “Now we have to make it count.”

As the country grapples with the sheer magnitude of the blue wave that crashed down last week, our nation’s besuited analysts are seeking to identify the warning signs, like a postdisaster reconnaissance team examining the swell of a tsunami after it has decimated a Southeast Asian beach town. How did Trump lose the key voting blocs, like young men, that vaulted him back to power in 2024?

I’d like to propose a bellwether: The Big Beautiful Trump Show, an unsubtle program that debuted on conservative network Newsmax in October. It’s unclear whose brilliant idea it was to launch a North Korea–style propaganda show in support of a historically unpopular president weeks before he led his party into a generational election wipeout, but the show was hosted for three episodes by a DC-based influencer named Matthew Foldi. A source shared the ratings: The show debuted to 1,000 viewers in the younger demographic, the 25–54 age group coveted by advertisers. “Insanely low,” the source explained. A Newsmax spokesperson did not respond to a request for comment.

Pour one out (a pint of raw milk, perhaps) for Katie Miller’s podcast, which is going on hiatus. I was a loyal listener—she booked pretty much every Trump Cabinet member, even the shy ones, and the friendly setting elicited more insight than you’d typically get out of a guarded pol like Marco Rubio. In a post on X, Miller said she would be pausing the show due to her husband’s new role as Trump’s chief of staff, writing that “the time commitment is too great and now is the time to step away.” The next day, The Atlantic’s Michael Scherer reported that Miller had signed a contract with Paramount for a future podcast deal, but that she had ended that as well. Paramount spokesperson Melissa Zukerman told Party Animals, “I don’t see she has anything with us.” When asked if Miller ever had a contract with Paramount, Zukerman did not respond.

In a previously unreported part of a past interview, Whipple asked Miller: “Would you agree, though, that it’s also the chief of staff’s job to tell the president what he doesn’t want to hear?” Miller replied: “No, I wouldn’t say that.”
