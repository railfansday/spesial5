AI anxiety: 5 ways to cope when you’re scared of AI

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

If you’re feeling a mix of emotions about artificial intelligence (AI) technologies and how they’re shaping our world, you’re not alone. Chief Clinical Officer at Calm, Chris Mosunic, PhD, has tips and strategies to help tame a growing source of anxiety.

AI anxiety is the worry or unease some people feel about the fast growth of artificial intelligence and how quickly technology is changing. It isn’t a clinical diagnosis, but the feelings are real and common. They can focus on everyday risks, such as job loss and AI-powered scams, and bigger ones, such as AI getting out of human control.

AI research goes back decades, but progress has sped up sharply in recent years with the arrival of chatbots, AI image generators, and other tools. A 2023 Calm survey* found that nearly 1 in 3 U.S. adults (29%) felt anxious about AI, and 18% described their feelings as fear or dread. At the same time, 31% felt optimistic, and 31% felt excited. Many respondents felt a mix: amazed by AI and nervous about it.

Concern about AI has stayed high in the years since. In a June 2026 Pew Research Center survey, 52% of U.S. adults said they feel more concerned than excited about AI in daily life. That’s up from 37% in 2021 and about the same as in 2023. Just 9% said they feel more excited than concerned.

It’s hard to know how AI will affect our safety and our lives. You might wonder how it will change your job, what it could mean for areas like scientific research, and what the future will look like. Many of us find uncertainty hard to cope with, so feeling anxious or out of control is understandable. But when anxiety feels overwhelming, it can start to affect your sleep, mood, and focus.

These five ways to manage AI anxiety draw on mindfulness and common thinking traps, like catastrophizing. Start with whichever fits how you feel today.

The rapid rise in AI and automation can feel like a threat to our livelihoods (and even our lives), making many of us feel unstable and out of control.

Resist resisting: When confronted with uncertainty, our first instinct can often be to fight it. Try sitting with it instead. Notice how the discomfort shows up in your body and mind.

Go at your own pace: Give yourself time to adjust to the new realities of AI.

Talk it out: Process your anxieties with a friend or a coworker. You may find someone who feels the same way.

💙 On those days when you feel overwhelmed by the rapidly changing tech landscape, try a simple meditation practice like Pause to Breathe to help you feel more grounded.

If you’re feeling anxious or uneasy about AI, don’t invalidate or try to avoid those emotions — that approach can make things worse. Instead, let yourself feel anxious. Telling yourself you’re overreacting can damage your self-trust and self-confidence.

Identify the emotions: Get a better understanding of what you’re feeling. Your anxiety could mean you’re working through a more deeply rooted problem, like feeling threatened or inadequate.

Try meditation: Meditation can help you ground yourself in the present moment and tune into what you’re feeling and where it’s coming from.

Write down all your anxieties without censoring yourself: Set aside dedicated time each day to think through your anxious thoughts, helping you process and make sense of your emotions.

💙 Take some time to stop and do this short Emotions Check-In to make space for any anxious feelings.

When we’re feeling anxious about the unknown, many of us fall into the trap of catastrophic thinking, imagining the worst possible outcome, even without evidence to support it. This can cause low-level anxieties to spiral out of control.

Challenge your worst fears: What evidence do you have that they’re true or that they’ll happen?

Bring to mind times in the past that felt groundbreaking in the tech world: Think back to the creation of mobile phones, laptops, or even navigation apps, and consider how you’ve adapted to their existence despite any initial anxieties or concerns. The same could happen with AI.

Bring your mind back to the now: The future feels a whole lot less intimidating when you focus on the present.

💙 One of the best ways you can bring yourself back to the present is through mindfulness meditation, which you can learn in the Mindfulness for Beginners series.

Many of us find ourselves viewing situations as an all-or-nothing approach. This is a cognitive distortion called polarized thinking, and it often occurs because we’re focused on the extremes without considering the nuances or possibilities of any given situation.

The debate around AI is nuanced. Popular culture shows dystopian futures, so it can be easy to think there’s only one possible outcome with AI.

However, reality tends to exist in between the extremes, so remind yourself that it’s perfectly normal for opposing emotions or views to be present at the same time. And they can also all feel as strong, real, or true as each other. You can feel fascinated and terrified, anxious and excited, or resentful and grateful.

When we allow multiple truths to co-exist in our minds, we make space for more ease. So try to hold space for the contradictions and the possibilities.

💙 For a more in-depth discussion on how to use mindfulness to combat worrying and keep nuance at the forefront, check out the Overcome Stress & Anxiety series.

At the extreme end of AI anxiety, some of us may have a deep—even subconscious—fear about a machine taking over. We ask questions like: Is this the beginning of the end of humanity? Will humans be replaced by robots?

A fear of overdependence on technology can cause us to worry about the negative consequences of relying too heavily on artificial intelligence. One way to remedy this fear is to embrace our humanity.

Be present for the little things you often take for granted, like hugs, laughing until it hurts, or exchanging a look with your best friend.

Make time for the people in your life, and prioritize connecting with them face-to-face when you can.

Marvel at the awesomeness of human things like awkward silences or goosebumps when you’re listening to music.

Ask people how they’re really feeling and practice deep listening when they respond.

Disconnect from technology and spend time in nature.

💙 Embrace the power of being human and living in the now by reading a book, listening to a meditation, or taking a Mindful Walk.

*Calm research was fielded among n = 450 adults in the US between March 21, 2023 and March 22, 2023 and is nationally representative, with quotas across age, gender, race, ethnicity, and region.

Recent news stories have shown how general-purpose AI tools may respond in unsafe or harmful ways, especially when people are vulnerable or struggling with a mental health crisis. These situations are rare, but they highlight an important truth: AI is not human and can’t accurately recognize risk, provide crisis support, or understand the complexity of your emotional world.

AI can support mental health — especially when it’s part of a program designed and guided by licensed professionals. But general AI tools, like chatbots or large language models, are not a replacement for real human care.

If you’re struggling, please consider reaching out to a therapist or support line — many offer free or low-cost options. You deserve support that’s personal, thoughtful, and real.

Your mental health matters and you don’t have to navigate tough moments alone.

To manage anxiety about artificial intelligence, there are proactive steps you can take.

Educate yourself about AI to understand its capabilities and limitations better: Learning about AI can make it feel less intimidating.

Engage in conversations about AI: Talking with others about your concerns can offer new perspectives and reduce feelings of isolation.

Practice mindfulness and stress-reduction techniques: Managing your anxiety effectively can help you focus on the present and reduce worries about the future.

Stay informed about AI developments: Understand how it's being integrated into society to help you feel more prepared and less anxious.

Understanding these fears and addressing them through education and dialogue can help alleviate the anxiety associated with AI.

You don’t have to be, though some concern is understandable. AI is changing fast, and it raises real questions, from job loss and scams to whether people can keep control of it. Some risks deserve attention. Others are far from certain, and no one knows exactly how AI will unfold. When we’re anxious, our minds jump to the worst outcome and can treat every headline as proof of a disastrous future.

It can help to follow reliable news sources and take breaks from stories that leave you on edge. When your thoughts race ahead, try a mindfulness practice to bring your attention back to today. If worry about AI is getting in the way of sleep or daily life, consider talking with a therapist or doctor.

The fear of AI doesn’t have a widely recognized specific term but is often considered a form of “technophobia,” which is the fear of advanced technology or complex devices. When this fear centers on AI, it can stem from concerns that the technology could surpass human intelligence and from the ethical consequences of machines making decisions that affect people’s lives. AI anxiety is rooted in the unknown future impact of AI on humanity.

On a societal level, increased reliance on AI could reduce human interaction and erode certain skills as machines take over more tasks. Understanding these potential negative effects can help develop strategies to mitigate them and ensure AI is used responsibly and ethically.
