James Tarkowski's Brentford nightmare: Everton star handed controversial red card on miserable night for David Moyes - as rampant Bees boost European hopes

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Published: 17:31 EST, 23 November 2026 | Updated: 04:29 EST, 24 November 2026

David Moyes’ record-breaking night as Everton manager could not have been more miserable.

In his 593rd game as Toffees boss, Moyes broke Harry Catterick’s 75-year record for most games in the dugout.

But that was about the only positive he could take away from the Gtech Community Stadium, as high-flying Brentford battered ten-man Everton on an evening to forget for the 63-year-old Scotsman.

Daily Mail Sport's Bobby Manzi was at the Gtech Community Stadium on a torrid night for the Toffees...

Brentford, having missed out on European football on goal difference last season, are showing all the signs of being there or thereabouts again.

They climbed to sixth, leapfrogging Leeds — who have had far more praise lavished on them this season than Keith Andrews’ reliable side.

Brentford are disciplined with plenty of attacking flair, even with frontman and top goalscorer Igor Thiago suspended.

The Premier League lacks quality this season, but Brentford are consistent, losing just twice.

This well-oiled Andrews machine must take advantage of the floundering elite, with Manchester United, Chelsea and Tottenham all struggling, and finally secure Europe.

‘We set out this season looking to be better than last season, and if that happens, we will be flying to different destinations next season,’ Andrews said of their European hopes.’

‘We've got a very competitive squad that strives to get better. If we continue with that mindset, we'll hopefully be in a good place.

‘I enjoyed us,’ he added about the performance. ‘I thought we were very good. We played with real bravery, courage and energy.’

Brentford limited Everton to just three touches in the penalty area in the first half. They grabbed the game by the scruff of the neck and pinned their opponents back.

Jaidon Anthony was superb on the flank, providing two assists. Midfielder Vitaly Janelt capped off a brilliant display with a wonder strike from 25 yards, finding the top corner with his left foot.

It was a surprise to see Kevin Schade benched but this team is so cohesive, with more focus on the unit than any individual stars, that victory was light work for them.

Both of Keane Lewis-Potter’s goals had an element of fortune attached. But teams earn that luck when they manage ten shots in their opponents’ penalty area in just 45 minutes.

Lewis Potter starred at left-back, registering seven shots — the most of any player — and the joint-most touches in the opposition box. He was a menace and integral to every Brentford attack.

James Tarkowski endured a depressing return to his former club when he was shown the first Premier League red card of his career.

It will split opinion, but in this day and age it is a clear red card. Although the Everton defender won the ball, his studs connected with Yehor Yarmolyuk’s shin on follow-through.

The speed and intensity of the tackle endangered his opponent. He had to go.

Referee Craig Pawson brandished a yellow, but a quick trip to the monitor saw it upgraded. Tarkowski ran down the tunnel before Pawson could even get the red card out of his pocket.

The home support enjoyed it, having taunted their former player throughout the first half.

To compound Everton’s misery, the first game of Tarkowski three-match ban is next weekend’s Merseyside derby.

A deflated Moyes had few complaints. ‘My feeling at the time was it wasn’t a red because James easily got to the ball before the Brentford player,’ the Everton boss said.

‘But after I saw it again, Tarky's boot is a bit higher than I'd like, so I can understand why the decision went against us.

‘We didn’t play well. Brentford did and credit to them. A bit unfortunate with the goals, it made it look even worse than it was.’

Everton were unbeaten in four games at Brentford before this and their defeat came in the form of the sublime and the ridiculous.

The first goal was all about Anthony undoing Everton on the left before Lewis-Potter was given far too much space to get his shot away. The deflection was unfortunate, but the defending was poor to begin with.

Tarkowski blocked an initial effort from Lewis-Potter for the second goal but the ball fell straight back to him, and Jake O’Brien helped his second attempt past Jordan Pickford. Once again, Everton should have done better and cleared the initial free-kick.

Portuguese forward Fabio Carvalho could not believe his luck when he added a late fourth, tapping in as Mathias Jensen’s corner flew straight past the congested pack at the near post.
