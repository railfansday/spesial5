FDA Wants Liquid Statin Off Shelves

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

— Missing annual reports put simvastatin oral suspension on thin ice

The FDA wants a liquid formulation of simvastatin (FloLipid) withdrawn from the market, though the agency is giving the drugmaker an opportunity for a hearing.

Approval of simvastatin oral suspension is at risk because Salerno Pharma "has repeatedly failed to file required annual reports" to the FDA, according to a notice in the Federal Register.

According to FDA policy, these annual reports are supposed to convey labeling information and technicalities, such as a current list of approved chemistry, manufacturing, and control information.

Initially approved in 2016 for a host of indications, simvastatin oral suspension is marketed as the first LDL cholesterol medication in liquid form for U.S. patients. The product was developed for patients who have difficulty swallowing pills, and the recommended usual starting dose is 10 mg or 20 mg once a day in the evening on an empty stomach.

Regarding safety, an increased risk of myopathy, including rhabdomyolysis, has been observed in patients taking an 80-mg dose of the oral suspension. Other patients unable to hit their LDL cholesterol goal taking the 40-mg dose are advised to initiate alternative LDL cholesterol-lowering treatments. In 2020, the drug label for the liquid simvastatin formulation was updated with a warning of rare reports of immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy associated with statin use.

According to the Federal Register notice -- which came from Michael Davis, MD, PhD, the FDA's top drug regulator -- Salerno is being given a shot at requesting a hearing if it wants to argue why the approval should not be withdrawn.

Salerno's simvastatin suspension was previously manufactured for Rosemont Pharmaceuticals, a Perrigo company.

Oral suspension atorvastatin (Atorvaliq) is an alternative liquid statin on the market.
