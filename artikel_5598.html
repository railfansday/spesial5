First Hormone Therapy Approved for Early Breast Cancer in Over 20 Years

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

— Oral SERD giredestrant lands indication as adjuvant therapy for stages I-III disease

The FDA on Monday expanded the approval of giredestrant (Wilserdly) in breast cancer to include the adjuvant treatment of early-stage disease, just a week after greenlighting the next-generation oral selective estrogen receptor degrader (SERD).

The new indication covers adults with stage I-III estrogen receptor (ER)-positive/HER2-negative breast cancer and an intermediate-to-high recurrence risk following surgery. With the new indication, giredestrant becomes the first hormone therapy approved for early breast cancer in over two decades.

"For patients with early-stage hormone-driven breast cancer, the goal after surgery is to keep the cancer from coming back," said Angelo de Claro, MD, director of the FDA's Oncology Center of Excellence, in a statement. "Our review focused on whether Wilserdly could reduce that risk more effectively than the hormone therapies patients already receive. The evidence showed that it could, supporting today's approval of a new option for patients at intermediate to high risk of recurrence."

In a large phase III trial (lidERA Breast Cancer), adjuvant treatment with single-agent giredestrant reduced the risk of invasive cancer recurrence or death by 30% compared with standard of care: 5 years of hormone therapy with tamoxifen or an aromatase inhibitor (anastrozole, letrozole, or exemestane).

Three-year invasive disease-free survival rates reached 92.4% with giredestrant compared with 89.6% in the standard-of-care arm (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.57-0.87, P=0.0014). Distant recurrence-free interval also favored giredestrant (HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.54-0.89) while overall survival showed a trend in favor of the oral SERD (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.56-1.12).

"In lidERA, patients treated with giredestrant had a reduced risk of invasive disease recurrence, as well as fewer treatment discontinuations due to adverse events compared to standard endocrine therapy, thus representing a major clinical advance for patients with breast cancer," said investigator Aditya Bardia, MD, MPH, of the David Geffen School of Medicine at the University of California Los Angeles, in a press release from drugmaker Genentech.

Treatment discontinuations because of adverse events (AEs) occurred in 5.3% of the giredestrant arm versus 8.2% of the control arm. The most common treatment-emergent AEs (≥10%) included musculoskeletal pain; decreases in lymphocytes, leukocytes, neutrophils, hemoglobin, calcium, sodium, and potassium; increases in aspartate aminotransferase, alanine aminotransferase, alkaline phosphatase, triglycerides, cholesterol, calcium, potassium, and creatinine; hot flash; fatigue; headache; nausea; bradycardia; and COVID-19. Treatment-emergent AEs were primarily low grade, with a similar frequency compared with the control arm.

Bradycardia incidence -- a known class effect of oral SERDs -- was higher in the giredestrant arm (11.3% vs 3.2% among controls) and is listed in the warnings and precautions section of the drug's prescribing information, alongside a warning on embryo-fetal toxicity.

Giredestrant is also approved in combination with everolimus for previously treated ER-positive/HER2-negative breast cancer in patients with ESR1 mutations.
