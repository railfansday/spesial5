'It was really scary': Mother describes sheltering during Russia strikes on Kyiv bridge

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Masha was in her kitchen when she heard the explosions blast through the city

The alarms warning of a ballistic missile threat went off just before 23:30 on Monday in Kyiv. Moments later came the boom of explosions.

Once again, the main bridges over the broad River Dnipro that cuts through the city were the target and this time the Russian missiles hit what they wanted.

Close to one end of the Pivnichnyi (Northern) bridge there is a large crater and in the pouring rain, water now rushes through it to the river below. Chunks of tarmac and metal are scattered around.

At its broadest point, the jagged hole is about eight metres wide.

The crossing is closed to all traffic apart from the occasional fire engine or police car passing at high speed. By contrast, nearby roads are jammed with cars and trucks, forced to take long detours.

Anyone in a hurry to move around this city has to walk, or cycle, across the bridge, picking their way through the wreckage. In the miserable weather, that also means getting very wet.

Masha and her family moved to a flat next to the bridge because they had been living by a power plant that kept getting hit by Russian missiles.

Last night, she was in the kitchen of their new place when the Russians struck the bridge. She thought her windows would be blown out of their frames by the explosions.

"It was really scary. We had no idea they'd hit here so much. It's like we're cursed," Masha told me, describing grabbing her child and running into the corridor for protection.

But somehow, she still managed to sound cheerful.

"It's difficult, but we're used to it. And we try to take the positive from everything. Like: we survived today! So we can be happy about that."

The Northern Bridge crossing is closed to traffic while emergency response teams work to clear the debris

From the damage, it looks like Russia targeted the same spot on the bridge with a volley of three missiles, aiming to keep the road closed for as long as possible.

A major recovery operation is under way, with firefighters and engineers in fluorescent overalls working to evaluate the extent of the damage. But it looks like the disruption will continue for some time.

Further along the Dnipro River, the Pivdennyi (Southern) bridge was also hit on Monday night, with four missiles.

The metro line across it was suspended for a while, although by mid-afternoon it had been restored. The mayor, Vitali Klitschko, said road transport in one direction was also being partially restored.

On the Pivnichnyi bridge, a man with a bag of tennis rackets on his back was gazing at the crater in front of him. He was heading home from the courts after he had travelled on foot to the left bank.

"It is upsetting," Anatolii told us. "Then when you read the news of what our side did to the Crimea bridge, you do feel a bit better."

The main bridges over the River Dnipro - that cuts through Kyiv - were the target of Russian strikes

That is because there was a battle of the bridges overnight.

Ukrainian missiles hit the one Russia had built to occupied Crimea, and caused serious damage. That bridge is prized by Russian President Vladimir Putin personally, a symbol of his illegal 2014 takeover.

Ukrainians loathe it for the same reason and now a section has collapsed into the sea, along with its towering arch.

To many here, the timing feels significant: a blow to Putin's image, just as he wants to seem most powerful at this week's summit with the US and China.

But for Kyiv resident Antonina, today is just another challenge to overcome.

The 67-year-old, who we met pulling a shopping trolley through the debris on a bridge in the Ukrainian capital, was heading to see her son on the right bank, but taxi drivers were charging a fortune to go round, so she decided to walk.

"It was a very scary night. It felt like something exploded right over my head. We will get through this, but I just wish the war would end."
