World Bank Taps Circle and Citi to Test Stablecoins for Development Payments

The World Bank is turning to stablecoins to modernize how it moves development money, announcing Tuesday it will explore using Circle’s USDC and blockchain-based payment rails to speed up and cut the cost of disbursing funds around the world.

The institution said it will work with Citi and Circle to test how dollar-denominated, regulated stablecoins could operate alongside or improve its existing funds-distribution system, which runs on correspondent banking, the slow and costly network of intermediary banks that routes international payments.

Under the arrangement, Citi would supply its connections to established banking rails while Circle provides the infrastructure to mint and settle funds in USDC.

The logic is that even small efficiency gains, applied across the vast sums the World Bank moves, could free up meaningful resources for development. “This initiative is about exploring ways to make the financial plumbing of development work better for the people who need it most,” said World Bank Group President Ajay Banga, arguing that faster, cheaper money movement means “a health clinic gets built on time, a farmer gets paid before the harvest season ends.”

The effort builds on FundsChain, a World Bank blockchain platform designed to make fund disbursements more traceable. More than 350 projects across 96 countries are already onboarded to its digital ledgers, letting everyone involved in a project see how money is being spent.

The stablecoin exploration would focus initially on an intermediated, interoperable model, limited to borrower countries where digital assets are permitted, with plans to involve other banks over time.

Circle CEO Jeremy Allaire framed development finance as one of the most important applications for his company’s technology, saying money that moves “at the speed of the internet shouldn’t be reserved for the world’s biggest banks and corporations.”

Citi’s Shahmir Khaliq said the collaboration would deliver “24/7 capabilities at scale so that critical funds move in real time.”

The move hands the stablecoin industry a powerful institutional validator, and it fits a broader wave of traditional finance embracing the technology.

Wall Street is currently racing to claim the onchain dollar, with Goldman Sachs, Citi and other banks committing roughly $270 million to a joint stablecoin venture, while Deutsche Bank recently went live with crypto custody after a five-year buildout.

For Circle, whose USDC is the second-largest stablecoin, a role in the World Bank’s payment plumbing marks a notable step toward mainstream legitimacy, even as the broader crypto market endures a rough stretch of ETF outflows and falling prices.
