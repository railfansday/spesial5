Ukraine allies warn ‘Yalta format’ will not bring about peace – as it happened

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Berlin meeting of the ‘coalition of the willing’ comes after Trump declared Putin a ‘friend’ during trilateral meeting with Xi in China

He says the leaders want “a peace for Ukraine that is both just and lasting,” with “more, not less, security for Europe and the world.”

“We will continue to support Kyiv for as long as is necessary. Should peace negotiations take place, we will be involved. Negotiations can only succeed if Ukraine and Europe are part of the process together. A “Yalta-style” format will not bring about peace.”

He says that as a gift he gave each of the guests a piece of the Berlin Wall, as an example of “what the Yalta arrangement represented for Berlin.”

“You may recall that in 1987, President Reagan stood here in Berlin and said: ‘Mr. Gorbachev, tear down this wall!’ Today, we say to President Putin: ‘end the war in Ukraine, and let us work together to find diplomatic solutions.’ We never again want to live in a divided Europe. We want to live in freedom and in peace – together.”

And, on that note, we will be closing the blog for today.

European leaders showed a united and defiant front as they repeated their backing for Ukraine “for as long as it takes” at the latest summit of the Coalition of the Willing in Berlin, declaring no decisions on Ukraine and Europe can be made without their participation (17:58, 18:06, 18:11).

Their comments appeared to play down the importance of Donald Trump’s summit with Russia’s Vladimir Putin and China’s Xi Jinping in Shenzhen (16:34), with German chancellor Friedrich Merz saying “a Yalta format will not bring about peace,” in a reference to secretive discussions between superpowers above the heads of those most affected (17:45, 17:50).

European leaders and US peace envoys insisted that some progress has been made in discussions on ending the Russian war of aggression in Ukraine, although they struck a cautious note about Putin’s intentions in the coming months (15:46, 18:38, 18:44).

US envoy Jared Kushner claimed that Russia’s leadership appears to be “comfortable” with the concept of Article 5-like security guarantees for Ukraine in any postwar settlement (18:29).

Meanwhile, Russia’s Putin struck an uncompromising tone at his press briefing in Shenzhen, vowing “painful” response to Ukraine’s attacks on Kerch bridge and defending his maximalist demands (13:09, 13:34).

Andy Burnham is meeting western leaders in Berlin and Brussels this week, to discuss Ukraine and closer ties to the EU. Meanwhile, Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping have met at the Apec summit in China.

Is this the start of a global shift of power away from Europe, or was it just a hyped-up photo opportunity?

Separately, Poland’s Donald Tusk also told reporters on his way back to Poland that Trump’s comments after his meeting with Putin and Xi sounded “quite optimistic.”

But Tusk quickly added that Trump’s “faith in Putin’s good will is probably unshakeable,” and he was a bit more sceptical about it.

“It would be good if indeed sooner or later it turned out that Russia is seriously ready to undertake peace talks,” he said.

“I don’t ‌think it’s likely ‌that Russia will sit down at the negotiating table in any serious ‌way before the winter is over.”

Zelenskyy has the last word, as he says that today’s meeting shows that “Russia did not get anywhere” with her attempt to “divide Europe.”

“A strong Europe is not only important for Ukraine, but for Europe itself,” he says.

The European leaders also get asked about Welt/Politico’s report earlier and the tone of Trump’s comments.

Burnham says that Trump’s comments focused on “horrific” loss of life during the war, saying “he spoke quite passionately about that subject.”

He stresses “nobody is winning this war,” and stresses that in his view there has been “huge progress” in now consensus that “there is no decision about Ukraine without Ukraine in the room and no decision about European security without Europe represented in the room.”

He says “there is a greater degree of alignment.”

“I would say for the first time in my period as prime minister of the United Kingdom, I see alignment; I see people saying the same thing; I see solidarity across the board in Europe; a strong, united European position.

And that gives some grounds for optimism that there can now be progress. But obviously, there is one person who can determine that more than anybody else, and that is president Putin.”

Witkoff jumps in there to say that Trump has “adamantly supported a peaceful end to this war,” with clear instructions to engage with the coalition of the willing and work in all formats that can be helpful to end the war.

“What I heard from him on the call today was that he mentioned these very same principles to the other two leaders who he was meeting with [Xi and Putin], so he made Ukraine, and a peaceful end to this war, a principal part of that trilateral today, and I think he’s to be commended for that.”

Macron gets asked about the Shenzhen talks and confirms that leaders were briefed on the discussions.

But he focuses on what’s ahead and stresses the need to get the sequence of agreements right to get a deal.

He also repeats his warnings that the war in Europe “threatens the security of Europe, and we have to be aware of that.”

Kushner says that the US managed to get Moscow comfortable with the concept that any security agreement for Ukraine would be based on Article 5-type arrangement, like in Nato.

(Not sure the Russians would say the same.)

He says “I know that there’s a lot of, scepticism and disbelief on that concept in Russia, but that’s why it’s important to restart the dialogue between Europe and Russia.”

“I will say from America’s perspective, giving an Article Five like security guarantee is a really, really big deal. And I think that that’s something that President Trump is open to. but obviously it has to be in America’s interest to do that and … we have to make sure it is part of a broader security package.”

Merz gets a question on potential multilateral talks with Ukraine, Russia, Europe and the US, and confirms that’s the plan and adds that finding the right place for it shouldn’t be too difficult.

He also goes back to his Yalta reference earlier, saying Europe was “parcelled up” by powers, and “we will not allow anything like that to happen again.”

Macron says the focus is on safeguarding Europe’s security.

He said that a peace declaration agreed by Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping in China “does not lead to a reorganisation of the world”.

“Today, if there is one critical factor in Europe’s reorganisation, its security, it is precisely the meeting that took place in Berlin,” he said.

He says the meeting was “very substantive and successful,” and “essential in helping us shape our point of views,” as he confirms that Trump dialled in for parts of the talks.

He pointedly praises Italy’s Meloni, Poland’s Tusk, European Commission’s von der Leyen, Nato’s Rutte, Finnish president and Norwegian PMs, Stubb and Gahr Støre.

He says Europe is “very united, and very, very strong in their support of Ukraine,” which he and Witkoff “relayed to president Trump.”

He says, without revealing details, that Trump came away from the trilateral China meeting thinking “peace is achievable”.

“For us, success in an outcome would be to have a sovereign, secure, prosperous and free Ukraine at the end of the war,” he says.

He says the talks have been making “a lot of progress” on postwar prosperity plan, security guarantees, and a plan to exchange prisoners of war.

On postwar security, he says that “Nato will be a central pillar” of proposals, with the right structures to avoid accidental escalations.

Kushner also says that Russians appear to be “willing to trust” the US suggestion that Europeans need to be included in the process to make sure that whatever agreement is struck will be respected.

The US envoys will be meeting with national security advisers next to agree on the detail of their proposals.

“President Trump wants to make a deal with Europe by his side,” he insists..

“Our goal is really to end this war and to really help build a durable peace. We are very committed. President Trump, will do whatever it takes to do that. He’s instructed us to commit as much time as possible, and we will continue to do that in full coordination, and unity with our European partners.”

He says today’s meeting “has put on a huge show of support” for Kyiv, and “the mood was stronger, more united, more determined.”

“Europe has a clear and consistent and united negotiating position. It’s not for the aggressor in this conflict to set out wide ranging and unrealistic security demands. That much we are all absolutely clear about.”

He says the coalition is ready for discussions on European security, but only “if there is a peace agreement between Ukraine and Russia.”

He says the UK’s support for Ukraine will continue for “as long as it takes.”

“Europe has a clear and consistent and united negotiating position. It’s not for the aggressor in this conflict to set out wide ranging and unrealistic security demands. That much we are all absolutely clear about.

And I hope that message is heard very clearly, in Moscow: for as long as it takes.”

“If we want to be credible in peace negotiations, then it is important that Ukraine defends itself and goes into these negotiations in a good position, and Ukraine can only do so if we continue supporting Ukraine.”

“Everyone who is here today have to do everything in their power so that Ukraine has everything it needs in order to resist and to defend herself …”

He says that faced with Russia’s maximalist demands, it’s time it is “the right point in time to discuss [Ukraine’s] Nato membership.”

Commenting on the current state of talks, he says Russia “did not submit any concrete and tangible proposals for peace.”

He acknowledges that “the coming weeks will be critical” for Ukraine, and so the coalition gathered at the right moment.

He says there was every attempt made to “make this meeting as concrete as possible.”

He thanks all partners for helping Ukraine and highlights “active efforts to ensure that we stand firm.”

He thanks the EU for its “very substantial” proposed loan of £45bn.

He also highlights some of the priority areas for Ukraine, including the move to stop daily drone attacks (which he confirms hit the Austrian and the Dutch embassies), and deploy more air defence systems.

He also says the next round of talks should include European leaders, and highlights the importance of security guarantees and Ukraine’s future membership in the EU.
