10 JRPGs Launching in 2027 You Should Keep on Your Radar

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Murillo Zerbinatto is a JRPG enthusiast, writer, and editor. He has played over 300 RPGs during his gaming career, with 90% of them being JRPGs.

The pandemic sure threw a wrench into many developers' plans, which was understandable. However, as soon as the situation began to normalize, many games that were delayed or suffered internal setbacks started getting revealed with official release dates. It feels like ever since 2023 we have been living through a run of great releases, with banger after banger.

Furthermore, JRPG fans have been well served since then. A new title keeps coming out, whether from indie developers, veterans who left their former companies to start their own studios, or fresh remasters and remakes popping up. I, for one, would love some peace and downtime to just play casually, and I thought 2027 would bring that relief, but I was mistaken. The year hasn't even begun, and my backlog is already clogging up again. Let us take a look at a few upcoming JRPGs in 2027 that you should definitely keep on your radar.

Which 2026 JRPGs are the best according to their Metascore?

Terranigma brings back a classic from the SNES, a JRPG that formed part of the legendary Quintet Trilogy. It functions more as a direct port than a full remaster, but it remains extremely welcome nonetheless. Terranigma never originally launched in North America, only in Japan and Europe, meaning many players from back in the day never had the chance to experience this action RPG.

In Terranigma, we play as Ark on a mission to restore Earth. And by Earth, I mean our actual planet, as all the continents are based on real-world geography that we gradually bring back to life. To do this, Ark uses a real-time combat system driven by level-ups and spells you buy along the way. The most enjoyable aspect of Terranigma is witnessing the world-building sidequests unfold as you visit villages and restore them. It is a fantastic treat for anyone looking to revisit or experience a past JRPG gem through an official release.

Against all odds and to my utmost surprise, SaGa is becoming one of the longest-running and most sprawling series in the Square Enix catalog. I know it is unorthodox and not everyone enjoys its unconventional design, but I love seeing Square keeping the franchise alive, especially through its remakes. Following Romancing SaGa 2: Revenge of the Seven, Square Enix is delivering a full remake of the third entry titled Romancing SaGa 3: Destinies United.

The remake will include similar enhancements to Revenge of the Seven, such as a revamped turn-based system that heavily emphasizes targeting enemy elemental weaknesses, while retaining core SaGa staples like Glimmer mechanics and open-ended progression where players shape the narrative path. I don't know if it will be as brutally difficult as the original Romancing SaGa 3, which was notorious for softlocking players with its complex progression systems. Still, it will certainly be a feast for fans of the series.

Continuing with upcoming remakes, Atlus is bringing another project to the table: Persona 4 Revival. As the name implies, this is a ground-up remake of the fourth Persona entry, fully modernized with current-gen graphics, overhauled combat mechanics, a re-orchestrated soundtrack, new social activities, extra story events, and expanded dungeon crawling elements inside the TV World.

The best part is being able to revisit what many consider the best Persona game (a statement that could honestly apply to almost any entry in the franchise). Returning to the town of Inaba, we play as the canonically named student Yu Narukami. While keeping up with his daily routine of school life, part-time jobs, and social simulation, he will also need to unravel the mysteries of the TV World alongside fan-favorite Teddie.

Atelier is one of the longest-running JRPG series, with a fanbase more dedicated than a football club. While the overall narrative remains anthological across the franchise, Atelier is organized into distinct subseries. Following the Secret subseries featuring Atelier Ryza, we have the Memories subseries, which kicked off with Atelier Yumia and now continues with Atelier Karia: The Night Kingdom & the Guide of Memories.

Atelier Karia continues the adventure in Aladiss, this time venturing into the underground world of Noctrem. This entry puts players in control of Karia, an amnesiac alchemist. Combat builds upon the real-time foundation established in Yumia, with Karia introducing a strategic layer that lets players pause combat to execute command skills or parry incoming enemy attacks. I won't say playing Atelier Yumia beforehand is strictly mandatory, but if you want full context, it might be the ideal route.

It's no exaggeration to say that Eternal Anima is one of my most anticipated JRPGs of 2027. The game was unveiled during a Nintendo Direct, and while I initially fell in love with its charming pixel art, it first looked like just another solid indie release. That changed once I looked into the talent behind the project, including industry veterans like Kazushige Nojima, a legendary Final Fantasy writer, and director Katsuhisa Higuchi, who worked as a battle programmer on several Final Fantasy titles and directed Monster Hunter Stories 2.

Early previews for Eternal Anima are singing high praise, spotlighting its refreshing turn-based combat, its time-travel mechanics (a narrative trope I never get tired of), and how seamlessly both elements interlock. The story follows Wade alongside an eclectic party gathered from different timelines. Combat features a mechanic called Future Sight that previews enemy moves, showing who they plan to target and the exact damage output, which elevates tactical planning and should be a joy to experiment with. I will be sitting in the front row waiting for Eternal Anima.

If you loved Final Fantasy Resonance, then it's time for more JRPG love, Jobs, and pixel art.

Final Fantasy VII Revelation needs no introduction and stands as one of the world's most anticipated JRPGs of 2027. We can wait for The Game Awards to hand out the Most Anticipated Game trophy, and if Final Fantasy VII Revelation doesn't take it home, feel free to call me out in the comments. Serving as the conclusion to the Final Fantasy VII remake trilogy, it appears to have refined everything that made its predecessors spectacular, pushing combat, exploration, and player freedom to new heights.

Narratively, it will conclude the main arc, though whether it introduces major plot twists remains to be seen. We do know the Weapons are present and look more imposing than ever. Combat introduces two new playable party members, Vincent and Cid, along with the new FITs system, which acts like a Job framework to give each character a broader tactical arsenal. As for traversal, with the Highwind at our disposal, Square Enix stated that world exploration is completely open, leaving route decisions entirely up to the player. Certain regions will have higher level thresholds, nudging players to tackle them later, but the overall scope looks immensely promising. Final Fantasy VII Revelation, the year is yours.

We now shift to games targeted for 2027 that lack specific day-and-date release slots, which leaves them open to potential schedule shifts. That said, if one developer rarely delays its titles, it is Nintendo. Because of that track record, when Nintendo announced Xenoblade Genesis for 2027, I knew with confidence I would be playing it next year.

Xenoblade Genesis marks the latest chapter in the Xenoblade saga. Whether it functions as a direct narrative sequel to the Chronicles arc remains unconfirmed, but it continues under the direction of Monolith Soft and series creator Tetsuya Takahashi. The reveal trailer omitted gameplay, focusing instead on narrative cutscenes that showcased story beats and main characters. The artistic direction initially drew mixed reactions from some fans for leaning closer to Fire Emblem than traditional Xenoblade, but I personally loved every bit of it, including the medieval aesthetic.

Knowing Monolith Soft, Xenoblade Genesis will feature a colossal world, an overhauled battle system, unorthodox progression mechanics, and endless features that will sink hundreds of hours into this new universe. Did I mention Yoko Shimomura is composing the soundtrack? That alone means it's a fantastic time to update my Spotify playlists.

In this new JRPG, players take control of Asch and Finé as they set out to prevent a world-ending catastrophe known as the Lightfall. Rev. NOiR. is an action RPG, but it features a tactical time-stop mechanic similar to Final Fantasy VII Remake, letting you pause combat to issue skill commands through a visually stylish menu. Deep gameplay details remain sparse for now, but as mentioned, I'm choosing to trust the project and will be ready on day one once Rev.Noir. locks in its official release date.

Here is another franchise that requires zero introduction. Pokémon Winds & Waves is being touted as the first truly high-fidelity graphical Pokémon title, which would certainly be a milestone achievement for Game Freak. Given its oceanic setting packed with islands and open seas, it has every tool required to expand the open-world framework of Pokémon Scarlet and Violet to unprecedented heights, complete with fresh traversal mechanics.

No specifics have been revealed regarding the battle mechanics. However, it will likely preserve the turn-based structure of its mainline predecessor while leaving real-time action gameplay to the Legends spinoff series. For anyone jumping into Pokémon Winds & Waves, three traditional elemental starter companions await: the puppy Pombon, the gecko Gecqua, and the bean chick Browt. I will almost certainly end up picking Gecqua.

I want to start by noting that, according to the developers and director Tetsuya Nomura himself, Kingdom Hearts IV will definitely launch in 2027 and won't face delays. Square Enix confirmed that internal development is progressing smoothly, setting up the long-awaited return of Sora's adventure in Quadratum, complete with new characters (along with returning faces for those who played the mobile entries) and a fresh selection of Disney worlds.

In terms of core design and gameplay structure, we can expect a polished take on the series' classic loop: Sora travels from world to world, adapts to local rules, meets regional protagonists, and aids them in their struggles. It is a charming formula, and I would not have it any other way. Officially, only the world of Coco has been formally revealed, though a leak stemming from Steam achievements unveiled several others. I won't share those details here to keep you safe from spoilers, but if you want to look them up, do so at your own risk.

From Eternal Sonata to Triangle Strategy, revisit seven underrated JRPGs that launched near major blockbusters and struggled for attention.
