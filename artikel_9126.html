engraved stainless steel and magnetic leather pieces compose wall-mounted geometric chess set

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

anastassiya leonova of somaforma reimagines chess as play forms, a wall-mounted stainless-steel board with magnetic leather pieces whose geometric cut-outs indicate their movement rules.

nastin leonova edited by christina vergopoulou November 19, 2026 4 min

Kazakhstan-based designer Anastassiya Leonova, founder of Almaty design studio somaforma, reimagines the traditional chess set as a wall-mounted object that combines functional design with a changing geometric composition. Named Play Forms, the project originated from Leonova’s experience of teaching her five-year-old son to play chess. Noticing that he struggled to remember how different pieces move, the designer began exploring whether their shapes could communicate the rules of the game. The resulting pieces are reduced to simple geometric forms, with their silhouettes and cut-outs referencing the directions in which they move across the board.

By translating movement into shape, Anastassiya Leonova aims to create a more intuitive visual language for chess. The designer also reconsiders the traditional format of the game. Rather than placing the board on a table, she develops a wall-mounted version that allows players to make moves at different times throughout the day. A game can unfold over several hours or days, while the arrangement of pieces creates an evolving composition on the wall. Measuring 60 by 60 centimetres, the board is made from engraved stainless steel, with magnetic-backed pieces crafted from natural leather. The reflective metal surface contrasts with the solid-colored geometric forms, giving the object a sculptural appearance.

Designed for serial production, Play Forms can be customized through different color combinations, allowing the pieces to complement a range of interiors. A special edition was developed for Freedom Holding Corp., featuring green and white pieces. The collection was photographed against the arid landscapes of the Boguty mountains near Almaty, where the board’s precise geometry contrasts with the irregular textures of the surrounding terrain. The photographs place the contemporary design object within the landscape of Kazakhstan, creating a visual connection between the project and its place of origin.
