These Prosciutto di Parma and Grana Padano Recipes Deserve a Spot on Your Holiday Table

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Preparing a memorable holiday meal doesn’t mean that things need to be complicated; simplicity can often be the best route to success. It’s easy to get hung up on overcomplicated techniques, expensive and hard-to-source ingredients, and combinations of flavors that sometimes require a leap of faith.When we cook for our friends and families, the dishes we return to time and again tend to have a few things in common: They make the most of what you have on hand, and they rely on the best ingredients possible. We’d much rather have a small amount of something exceptional than a large quantity of a substitute that just doesn’t measure up. Grana Padano PDO and Prosciutto di Parma PDO are two such ingredients, made according to generations-old traditions and held to strict production standards.Both are protected by the European Union’s Protected Designation of Origin (PDO), which guarantees that each is produced in its designated region of Italy using time-tested methods. Grana Padano is aged for at least nine months to develop its mellow, nutty flavor and granular texture, while Prosciutto di Parma is cured for a minimum of 400 days and made with just pork and sea salt. Their quality and versatility make them just as useful for everyday cooking as for a more celebratory meal.

For your next big gathering, these six recipes from our archive show just how much you can do with a few good ingredients.

Celery root adds an earthy sweetness to these mashed potatoes, which are whipped with butter, half-and-half, and Grana Padano until smooth and creamy. A generous amount of the aged Italian cheese gives the mash a salted, savory depth that makes it the perfect cozy side dish on an autumn table or as part of a holiday feast. Get the recipe >

Inspired by panisse, the famous Provençal chickpea fritters, these crisp polenta fries are enriched with butter and Grana Padano, then fried until golden and crunchy. The creamy roasted garlic dipping sauce gets a touch of heat from chipotle peppers and hot sauce, providing the perfect pairing for the rich, crispy polenta. Get the recipe >

Thinly sliced raw kale gets a bright, savory treatment in this simple salad. Grana Padano lends creamy depth, while fresh lemon juice and dried currants bring acidity and sweetness to balance the slightly bitter greens. Allowing the salad to rest briefly softens the kale and encourages the flavors to meld. Get the recipe >

Cloaked in a creamy buttermilk sauce brightened with lemon, this pasta gets a salty, savory note from thinly sliced Prosciutto di Parma folded in just before serving. Grana Padano lends a nutty finish, while poppy seeds cooked in butter bring an aromatic, toasty quality. Get the recipe >

Peas are gently simmered in a broth seasoned with olive oil, garlic, parsley, and small pieces of Prosciutto di Parma, allowing the ham to season the liquid as it cooks. A touch of sugar brings out the peas’ natural sweetness, while the prosciutto’s salty character balances the dish, making for a simple, deeply flavorful side. Get the recipe >

Garlicky sautéed escarole is layered with two kinds of Prosciutto di Parma: chopped pieces that cook with the greens, and thin strips that are fried until crisp, adding texture and crunch. Chile flakes and lemon juice round everything out, balancing the ham’s saltiness with heat and acidity. Get the recipe >
