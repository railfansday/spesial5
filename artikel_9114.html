Nina Kintsurashvili's Paintings of Yearning

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Georgian painter’s fragmented, dense compositions draw from her country’s rich history and complex present.

Copious research materials drawn from her country’s rich and complex history are integral to Georgian painter Nina Kintsurashvili. She doesn’t depict them — including ancient frescoes, Byzantine icons, earthenware vessels, books, or Soviet-era illustrations — but instead obliquely alludes to, channels, and transforms them in largely abstract yet partially figural paintings.

Her new paintings (all oil on linen from 2026) are luscious yet willfully scruffy, with paint often applied thickly and sometimes erased, and with brushstrokes ranging from vigorous and broad to wispy and spindly. In “Rifts,” creamy, sinewy, irregularly shaped forms spread horizontally from border to border. Underneath, multicolored vertical bands pull the picture plane downward, as if under gravity. There is motion here — pressure, tension — a shifting terrain. At the very bottom is something curious, which crops up often in these works: a grid, or rather a snippet of a grid. It could be the magnified warp and weft of the linen. It could be a section of fence.
