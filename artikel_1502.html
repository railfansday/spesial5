VAR a scourge on the Premier League as Forest denied by off-field meddling

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Almost £3.5bn was splurged on transfers this summer in a league supposedly described as the best in the world, so why does VAR continue to prove so ineffective and divisive?

Seven years after its introduction, it remains a scourge on the Premier League and here at the City Ground was another exhibit of maddening off-field meddling.

Tottenham Hotspur avoided a third successive defeat after the crucial intervention of VAR prevented Neco Williams from securing Oliver Glasner his first win as Nottingham Forest’s head coach.

The big moment occurred in the 65th minute. Morgan Gibbs-White’s chip over Antonin Kinsky should have been dealt with by Sandro Tonali and Jan Paul van Hecke but both players got themselves in a muddle and Williams bundled the ball in from almost on the goal-line.

Replays appeared to show the ball went in off Williams’ knee and he was protecting his face with his hands to avoid a stray boot. The VAR review ruled that Williams had handled the ball, yet in this billion-pound industry there was not one camera angle which proved entirely conclusive.

On this occasion the VAR official was Peter Bankes, recommending to referee Craig Pawson that the goal be disallowed. Williams said: “It’s a goal. VAR is there when there is a clear and obvious error and for me that is not clear and obvious. I’ve seen the images and the replays and at not one moment did I think I handballed it because I never did. I headed it in between my arms. I’m at a loss at that one, it’s disappointing. If you’re going to pause it, it’s going to look like it hit my arm but it didn’t hit my arm.”

Glasner and the other Forest players were also incensed, and once again supporters are questioning how critical moments in matches are being decided by officials sitting at Stockley Park. “I think the VAR can’t intervene when he is guessing,” said Glasner. “I told the referee after the game that I felt the goal should have stood.

“The VAR should say to stay with the onfield decision because it is about strengthening the referee. If it’s not 100 per cent clear, stick to the onfield decision.  We had a briefing before the season that the threshold for VAR intervention this year should be very high which is why this is wrong.”

While Tottenham were perhaps fortunate to pick up their first point, they are still waiting to score their first Premier League goal of the season. They did not manage a shot on target.

The grand £350m rebuild is proving a slow-burner and it is only Mykhailo Mudryk who will have cause to remember such a dull affair. Signed on loan from Chelsea on deadline day, Mudryk made his first appearance since November 2024 after being suspended by the Football Association following a positive test for performance-enhancing meldonium.

The Ukrainian forward was introduced with 16 minutes left, with his manager Roberto De Zerbi determined to reinvigorate his career.

Tottenham are still waiting for lift-off but De Zerbi remains convinced that his team will eventually click, particularly in attacking areas.

One of their summer arrivals, Omar Marmoush, wasted their best chance in the 16th minute by drilling a shot wide of the post. De Zerbi said: “In the last third we have to improve. We have to maybe keep more calm when we are very close to doing something important.

“If we improve in the last third I think we can score many goals because we have the players with big qualities. We could have scored three or four goals.

Considering we were only all working together on Thursday for the first time, this is the right spirit and the right way to move on.” On Mudryk, De Zerbi said: “He’s working to reach the best physical condition and he needs time.”

After the disallowed Williams goal, Forest were feeling further aggrieved soon after when Rodrigo Bentancur appeared to pull back substitute Igor Jesus in the penalty area. These are two teams clearly in period of transition and patience will be required from both sets of supporters.

Enzo Maresca believes streetwise Enzo Fernández will give Manchester City an extra winning edge – as he told his record £125 million signing to forget about the size of the fee.

City made the Argentina midfielder the joint most expensive purchase in Premier League history with their deadline day swoop from Chelsea.

Maresca has opted against naming Fernández among his captains’ leadership group, which features Ruben Dias, Erling Haaland, Marc Guéhi and Gianluigi Donnarumma.

But the Italian – who knows Fernández well from their time together at Stamford Bridge – is confident his fierce competitive instincts will bring an added dimension to his side.

“I don’t know if it’s streetwise but he knows how to win,” Maresca said, who is in line to hand Fernández his debut against Coventry on Saturday. “When you want to compete or win, it’s much more important to have players that already know how to do that than players who have never done it in the past.

“Enzo is not the only one. This squad has many players who know how to win titles and how to compete and this is what we are looking for.

“I know Enzo quite well and I know what he can bring to the team. He is a winner, [has] a winner’s mentality and knows how to win [after] the World Cup, Club World Cup [success].

“I just said he is a winner and when you belong to the squad and another winning player arrives, they are always happy.

“I can promise you that in the last four or five days, four or five players from the squad were asking if was coming or not because they wanted him to be with us. It’s very important that he is with us.”

Maresca said he planned to use Fernandez predominantly as a No 6 or No 8 depending on the game. “I see Enzo as a six, a little bit more deep, or [an eight] a little higher arriving in the box,” he said.

We have Mike McGrath reporting from the Etihad and John Percy at the City Ground and I will endeavour to keep you up to date with developments at the other three grounds via various methods. Forest vs Spurs seems the most consequential of the fixtures, not because either manager is in any danger but because now the window has closed it should give us a clearer picture of how Roberto De Zerbi plans to marshal his surfeit of wingers into a coherent attack that can break their goalscoring duck and how he proposes to organise a back four that have shipped five goals.

Fulham and Palace have come in for a lot of criticism regarding their recruitment and while I can understand the frustration of the former’s fans for doing so much business with Real Madrid, I think Gonzalo García is a proper player and will shine over the course of the season.

Palace, I think, are paying the price for the January splurge on Brennan Johnson and Jorgen Strand Larsen, not just the inflated fees but the sizeable wages on players who contributed almost nothing.
