Torus raised $500 million to manufacture container-sized AI computing units with batteries

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Series C round, led by Magnetar, will fund production of Foundry units that range from 1 to 20 megawatts in size

Torus raised $500 million in a Series C round to build a product about the size of a shipping container that pairs battery storage with AI computing power, according to Bloomberg. The Salt Lake City-based energy-storage startup said it will use the funding to build its Foundry product.

Magnetar led the round, with participation from ICONIQ Capital, Origin Ventures and Trinity Capital, according to Bloomberg. Hydra Host, a firm focused on AI infrastructure, counts itself as one of Torus’s customers, according to the startup.

Torus founder and CEO Nate Walkingshaw told Bloomberg the containers can be placed on-site at businesses or plants whose local grid has unused electrical headroom. That can head off infrastructure upgrades that would strain customers' utility bills, he said. Walkingshaw said the units are designed for AI inference work, come in sizes from one to 20 megawatts, and can be installed within four months.

"The Foundry product is our version of a distributed data-center approach to move the chips to the power that's already there," he said, according to Bloomberg.

U.S. data-center developers are scrambling to secure sufficient electricity for power-hungry AI workloads, according to Bloomberg. Developers planning huge campuses whose power demand rivals that of an entire city could face closer scrutiny and extended schedules in states such as Texas and Ohio. Democrats who ran on tougher curbs for data centers took the governorships in those states in last week’s midterm elections, Bloomberg said.

Torus has so far sold modular battery systems that let businesses and factories control their energy consumption and cut electricity costs. Walkingshaw said the changing political climate gives an advantage to firms able to deliver computing capacity while keeping power rates flat and avoiding extra neighborhood noise or pollution.
