Elon Musk may have bought control of the post-Trump Republican Party … but it cost him $30 billion of his net worth

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Elon Musk’s nearly half-a-billion-dollar bet on Republican midterm victories is a day late and many dollars short, but whether the world’s richest person’s political investments were all for nought remains to be seen. The tech billionaire donated at least $474 million to Republican candidates ahead of the 2026 midterm elections, Fortune’s Sasha Rogelberg reports.

Yet Musk had little to show for it: The Republicans were trounced in Congress.

But the jury is still out on whether he has lost influence among Republicans, or if he’s laying the groundwork for the 2028 election. In fact, Musk could very much influence the future of the party.

“He basically gets to steer a political party,” Virginia Commonwealth University political science professor Alex Keena said. “This is one of the biggest influences of money. It’s not just about who wins. It’s about setting the agenda and what types of issues we talk about.”

One of the quirks of calculating GDP is the fact that imports are negative for growth, simply because the money is being sent abroad (in exchange for goods) and thus contributes to someone else’s GDP. So much money is being spent on tech equipment in the AI boom that it’s actually hurting U.S. GDP, according to ING’s James Knightley.

“With the US running a trade deficit of $64 billion in September in tech products alone, out of an overall trade deficit of $105 billion, we need to remember that this aspect actually subtracts from growth. Instead, it is a boon to the likes of Korea and Taiwan,” which supply the products, Knightley writes.

“Tech products that are destined for data centres around the country have not merely gone exponential, but almost vertical.”

“This is a net negative for U.S. growth as money is flowing out of the country to the likes of Korea and Taiwan, from which U.S. imports are up 35% and 60% respectively,” he noted. “Only three years ago, the U.S. was a net exporter of tech products!”

U.S. S&P 500 futures were marginally up this morning after the index rose strongly yesterday on the midterm election results. Europe followed suit in early trading.

But the “blue tsunami” had the opposite effect in Asia today as markets in Korea and Japan closed sharply down. Traders led a selloff in chipmakers like SK Hynix and Samsung on the assumption that Democratic restrictions on new AI data centers in the U.S. might reduce demand for computing power.

This chart explains a lot about the recent selloff in tech stocks, particularly those linked to AI. It shows Anthropic’s revenues plotted against its operating expenses through Q3 2026. The good news is that its net losses (green) are getting smaller, once you discount the noncash charge for the changing value of convertible loan notes the company has issued. But the bad news is that Anthropic is clearly having difficulty keeping its opex (red) lower than its revenues (blue). It’s early days for the company, of course. But until those green bars rise above zero, its pre-IPO narrative remains tainted by the fact that the company does not yet have a profitable business model.

The percentage of the total addressable market currently claimed by OpenAI and Anthropic, per an estimate from Brett Winton at ARK Invest.

If the companies can increase the productivity of their corporate clients over the next 10 years, they could generate $40 trillion in revenue, Winton estimates.

You wouldn’t know it from all the complaining they do, but the average American is doing pretty well, according to these charts from Ben Carlson at Ritholtz Wealth Management. The data is based on the U.S. Federal Reserve’s triennial Survey of Consumer Finances.

“It’s hard to believe real incomes were unchanged from 1989 to 2013,” Carlson writes. But “after adjusting for inflation, incomes are up almost 30% from the 2013 lows.”

The median net worth chart is even more dramatic, given that the Great Financial Crisis culled almost 60% from stocks and 30% off housing prices.

SpaceX CEO Elon Musk made nearly half a billion dollars in campaign donations to the Republican Party in this election cycle, according to The Financial Times, and none of his bets paid off. In addition, SpaceX and Tesla stock declined by 7.6% and 5.7%, respectively, or about negative $30 billion, during overnight trading after election day. Of course, those are paper losses. Stocks can go up as well as down. But mostly they are going down: SpaceX lost another 3.8% yesterday and Tesla was down by 3.34%.
