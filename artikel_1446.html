Man City survive tunnel clash and Porto fury thanks to Haaland double

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Erling Haaland revealed on the eve of this clash how he loves chopping firewood in his spare time – and his goals set alight Manchester City’s Champions League campaign on a fractious evening in Porto.

So much for the new group stage of the competition taking time to warm up. This clash was a hot-blooded, bitter contest that spilt over in the tunnel at half-time, with players getting separated and tempers rising.

And City were not easing themselves back into European action either, they went full throttle at the start of a tough set of group games. With Enzo Maresca, it looks like it will be all-out attack to get the best out of this team.

They can look nervous at the back, particularly with the ball at Gianluigi Donnarumma’s feet, but have attacking weapons to hurt any team in this elite competition. This was a performance that their rivals will take note of.

“It was a complete performance on and off the ball for 95 minutes,” was Maresca’s assessment.

City will certainly scare the traditional heavyweights with Haaland leading their attack and taking an axe to Porto’s defence like he does with logs in his back garden. It was his 58th and 59th goal in 59 games in the Champions League, with his latest an expertly directed header off a post and a calm side-footed finish.

There is an argument that Pep Guardiola could have achieved more in the Champions League, given City’s dominance in domestic football. They have been knocked out by Real Madrid in the last three seasons but this is a new City, different personnel and even a new look.

They wore their black and yellow away kit, inspired by “Manchester’s iconic worker bee” according to Puma, and grafted their way to victory here. Non-stop running going forward, and scrapping with Porto, too.

The Portuguese champions were not happy with Marc Guéhi’s challenge on Gabri Veiga and claimed an elbow was used, with the row carrying on in the tunnel at half-time with security guards separating the players. Rúben Dias also appeared to nudge a Porto official on his way to the dressing room.

“I didn’t see anything but anyway, you can ask how many times you see me or my bench stand up? Zero, for 95 minutes. How many times do you see their bench? So...” Maresca added.

Only Dias and Haaland remain from the starting line-up from the Champions League triumph three years ago, so this is essentially a new team with a new manager.

Phil Foden, on as a sub in that game, started at Estádio do Dragão after his horse Wreck It Ronnie defeated Najidi Storm, which started the Goodwood race with 1-33 odds and is now the shortest-priced loser in British racing history. The rest of the team are new to City’s challenge.

That includes Ayyoub Bouaddi, 18, who was part of a £320m midfield refresh following the departure of Rodri, who was named among the Ballon d’Or nominees a few hours before kick-off to highlight the experience and class City have lost.

Bouaddi, who cost £81.4m from Lille, made a good start in his full debut. The Moroccan teenager is neat on the ball and gets around the pitch, with the next step being able to dominate games like his predecessor.

“In his last game he lost two balls and stayed calm and was back in our box,” Maresca said. “This shows how good he is because any player after two [poor] passes panics, but he is just 18. Erling, Rúben, Mateo [Kovacic], they can manage these kind of games better, but you don’t expect it from a guy of 18. I didn’t see him panic.”

By Bouaddi’s side in midfield was Enzo Fernández, who sought to drive forward into space when City counter-attacked. He was among the players who, along with Dias, were reminded of their Benfica history by the home fans.

Diogo Costa tends to be linked with a move to the Premier League every summer, but has been a one-club man for his career so far. But he showed why clubs are looking at him with his first-half saves to deny Haaland and Rayan Cherki.

Porto’s legendary goalkeeper, Helton, was in the press box at half-time and would have been impressed with the saves, although he was not in his seat when Haaland scored the first goal, which came 94 seconds after the restart.

City had worked the ball around the penalty area, with Antoine Semenyo drawing out Costa before laying off to Fernández. Haaland’s header from the cross had such precision that the goalkeeper did not dive as it angled in off a post.

Still, Porto had their chances with Alberto Costa heading wide from a corner and William Gomes charging down a Donnarumma kick – with City’s most nervous moments coming when they passed around their back line.

But Haaland sealed the win as the game ticked into stoppage time, finding the bottom corner from Rayan Aït-Nouri’s cutback.

“It was an amazing game against the champions of Portugal. We knew it would be a difficult fight, man against man, they are a top team,” Haaland said. “They play amazing football. All the pressure, we have to deliver on the big stage and that’s what we did. We controlled the whole first half, created chances.”

For Maresca, the victory was also a challenge against Porto’s aggression, with the Italian coach adding: “I was very pleased. We know in this kind of game when you come here it’s always difficult or tricky. The environment, the way they grew up, it’s normal, but I think we dealt with that very good.”

Enzo Maresca takes charge of his first Champions League game in charge of Manchester City as they have travelled to Portugal to take on Porto at the Estadio do Dragao. These sides last met during the Champions League group stages during the 2020/21 season. Manchester City won 3-1 at home before the two sides drew 0-0 in this stadium.

Manchester City were busy late on in the transfer window, bringing in both Iliman Ndiaye and Enzo Fernandez as they spent nearly £200m on deadline day alone. They come into tonight’s game off the back of three straight Premier League victories to kick off the Maresca era. Maresca has limited Champions League experience as a manager, which came with Chelsea last season. Manchester City have won their opening game of a Champions League campaign in six of the last seven seasons but this will be the first time since 2015/16 that Manchester City embark on a Champions League campaign without Pep Guardiola at the helm.

“It is a hard place to come [Porto],” said Maresca. “They have started really well. In the last 14/15 games in Europe they lost just one, that shows a difficult place to come, but we will try our best and try to win the game. It is a very, very good team. They have different players, but in terms of the team it is very clear in the way they play. They are aggressive without the ball and on the ball, they have a clear style. But we will try our best and try to win the game. The ambition is the same; try to be ourselves and to win games and then we will see.”

Porto, managed by Francesco Farioli, have won every league game so far this season and already sit four points clear at the summit of Liga Portugal. Farioli takes charge of his first game as a manger in the Champions League and the club make their return to the elite European competition after two seasons away.

“We are going to face one of the big favourites to win the competition,” Farioli said. “It will be a game that will demand that we be at our best, and we know that whenever we step onto the Dragão pitch, something special can happen. I am happy to be in the Champions League for the first time at 37 years old. This will be my first game, but we approach every game with the same attention to detail and the same focus. It is easy for the players to be motivated tomorrow [tonight]. But the key will be our strategy and our ability to execute it, individually and collectively.”

Porto were in the Europa League last season, making it to the quarter-finals, where they were knocked out by Nottingham Forest 2-1 on aggregate. The omens would not appear to be on Porto’s side tonight; they have only won two of their last 16 games against English opposition in major European competition.
