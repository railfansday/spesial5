Man City dismantled by PSG in breathless Champions League battle

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

When Manchester City play like this what a loss their football will be to the Premier League – and the Champions League – if they are heavily sanctioned later this season.

In what turned out to be a match for the ages, a night of brilliant, unbridled attack, we were treated to the ultimate game of two halves as the back-to-back European champions Paris St-Germain took City apart for 45 minutes. And then were left holding on.

City will hope this is a sign of what is to come in their Premier League fight. But with a different result. After suffering a resounding defeat at the hands of the Premier League’s independent commission, with 114 of the 115 charges against them proven, they maintain their innocence and are mounting an appeal.

Could they make a comeback? It would be incredible and highly improbable if they did so but, out on the pitch, and 3-0 down after a first half in which they were in danger of being humiliated by PSG, they created enough opportunities to save themselves.

The data backed that. City’s expected goals (xG) was 2.57 while PSG’s was 1.83. City had 27 shots to 15 and had more possession and more passes. Enzo Maresca made a triple substitution to devastating effect, especially with the introduction of new signings Ayyoub Bouaddi and Iliman Ndiaye.

In the end they clawed back two goals, both scored by Erling Haaland, who now has 61 in 60 games in this competition – seventh outright in the all-time list, before Ruud van Nistelrooy. And 37 of those for City, overtaking Sergio Agüero’s club record.

It could have been a hat-trick. It could even have been four as Haaland, taking the captain’s armband as Rúben Dias was withdrawn during the interval, clipped the top of the crossbar with a header and then was denied by goalkeeper Matvei Safonov as the striker slid in.

“Even with 3-0 in the first half, with the amount of chances we created in the second half, we deserved more. Luis [Enrique, PSG’s coach] said at the end of the game that they were quite lucky,” Maresca claimed before being asked whether he would relish facing PSG again, later in this competition. “My feeling is we are there. We are close,” he added.

Did City deserve a draw? No one was predicting that at half-time as they were suffering as comprehensive a defeat as was inflicted by the commission. Accused of “sham” contracts to inflate income, this was a sham of a performance, made all the more emphatic because of how brilliant PSG, and their exceptional 21-year-old winger Désiré Doué, were.

There is a certain irony that it was PSG who inflicted this defeat, ending City’s run of eight wins from eight in all competitions under Maresca.

It was a meeting of two clubs empowered more than any other by the riches of Gulf states: Abu Dhabi versus Qatar. Both have also had their run-ins over money. Both were sanctioned, but escaped full bans by Uefa for breaching financial fair play rules.

The question, also, was: would the eighth meeting of these clubs in the Champions League in the last 11 seasons be the last for some time?

So we should savour it and we did. That included Pep Guardiola on his first return to the Etihad – in a sign of solidarity – after quitting at the end of last season. Not that he savoured much of what he saw as PSG dominated City to such an extent that their fans adopted the “Poznan” celebration and the home supporters were silenced.

City could have gone in front before they conceded with the erratic Safonov – who was Gianluigi Donnarumma’s understudy and has become his unlikely replacement – punching a corner against his own crossbar before Antoine Semenyo shot the rebound over.

Then PSG ran all over City. They broke far too easily and scored far too easily. Not once, but twice, to go 2-0 up.
