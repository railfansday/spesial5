10 Ocarina of Time Remake Moments That Made Me Feel Like a Kid Again

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Covering the video games industry since 2017, with experience in news, articles, and reviews. Currently a writer at DualShockers and gaming magazines.

Growing up with The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time means carrying a very special set of memories with you. I first played this adventure as a kid, when the idea of growing up, taking responsibility, and dealing with the more bittersweet aspects of life (just like Link) felt very different from what it does today.

Back then, I was simply a child exploring Hyrule, discovering secrets, and wondering what would happen next. Now, coming back to this world as an adult, I find myself looking at everything through a completely different lens (of Truth, I suppose!).

But despite seeing this adventure with more mature eyes, there were moments when I completely forgot about everything around me, and, for a few seconds, I wasn't a grown-up playing a remake on a modern console anymore. I was that same kid sitting on the floor of my bedroom, playing Ocarina of Time on a CRT television and getting lost in a world that felt magical. So, let's get a little nostalgic today and revisit the 10 remake moments that made me feel like a kid again.

I dare you not to shed a few tears when you hear the first notes of The Legend of Zelda's iconic main theme – it is that very moment when you finally realize this remake is real, and you're about to embark on a journey that has meant so much to you for so many years.

There's something incredibly powerful about hearing a familiar melody return with a fresh presentation. Suddenly, all those childhood memories come rushing back, and you remember exactly why you fell in love with this series in the first place.

Thankfully, the remake also brings enough improvements and new touches to make the experience feel fresh. It's a wonderful balance between revisiting something familiar and getting excited about discovering it all over again. And we're only at the title screen!

Some games have opening sequences that are simply too good to get old, no matter how many times you replay them. And I feel exactly that way about the introductory hours of Ocarina of Time. Running around Kokiri Forest, learning about this world through the eyes of its young inhabitants, and slowly figuring out what we're supposed to do is still a delight.

There's so much to love about this section: getting those cryptic instructions from Navi, finding the Kokiri Sword and Hylian Shield, and finally making our way into the Great Deku Tree. It does such a wonderful job of introducing the world and making us feel like we're part of this little community before the adventure truly begins.

Honestly, just thinking about it makes me want to grab a bag of snacks, sit on my favorite childhood beanbag, and spend another afternoon exploring the forest. Some things never change, do they?

I think we can all agree that this is a canon event for every Ocarina of Time player: leaving Kokiri Forest and immediately sprinting toward Hyrule Castle, desperately trying to reach it before nightfall. Otherwise, you'll find yourself surrounded by those creepy Stalchildren, and nobody wants that on their first trip through Hyrule Field!

Thankfully, that particular rush isn't quite the same in the remake, giving us a much calmer first experience with the enormous landscape. And while I love that frantic moment from the original, I have to admit that I really needed those few extra seconds to stop and appreciate the view.

Seeing Hyrule Field with this new presentation, taking in the scenery, and realizing just how far the world stretches ahead of us was surprisingly emotional. For once, I wasn't in a hurry to reach the castle. I just wanted to stand there for a little while and enjoy being back.

Some of the original's most charming quirks from Zelda: Ocarina of Time didn't make the journey.

Using the ocarina as a gameplay mechanic has always been one of my favorite things about the original Ocarina of Time. There's something special about learning a melody, remembering its notes, and realizing that you can use music to interact with the world around you.

Still, I had my doubts about whether the remake could recreate that same feeling – and, well, it did! Besides, there’s a new feature that lets us sing or even play an actual musical instrument to trigger a song makes the experience feel even more personal.

It's such a magical way to interact with one of the most iconic elements of the adventure, turning something we usually do with buttons into an actual musical experience, making every familiar melody feel a little more special.

That first moment when Link pulls the Master Sword from its pedestal is unforgettable. After spending so much time as a child, we're suddenly transported seven years into the future, and everything we thought we knew about Hyrule has changed.

But the real shock comes when we walk out of the Temple of Time and see what has happened to Castle Town. The once-bustling streets are now in ruins, the atmosphere is completely different, and the familiar kingdom has become a much darker place.

And seeing all of this with the remake's new visuals gave me chills all over again. For a moment, I felt the rush of running to Kakariko Village to check if everyone was still there – since it literally hit me like the first time.

One of the most enjoyable moments of playing as Adult Link in the original game is finally reclaiming Lon Lon Ranch from the terrible Ingo. After all that trouble, getting reunited with Epona, now fully grown, feels like such a rewarding moment. Playing Epona's Song, challenging Ingo to a race, and finally winning back the ranch is an experience I could never get tired of.

And the remake makes this moment just as magical as I remember it! I won't even tell you how many tears I shed during this sequence, especially when Epona makes that iconic jump over the fence, right above Ingo's head, to escape the ranch. That little moment brought the biggest smile to my face.

Having a horse in a video game was such a cool concept back then, and somehow, it still feels just as cool today. There's something incredibly freeing about riding across Hyrule with Epona by your side, and I don't think I'll ever get tired of it.

Nintendo has expanded one of the original Zelda: Ocarina of Time's most heartbreaking side stories.

The symbolism and challenge behind fighting Dark Link in the middle of the Water Temple already make this encounter memorable. You're facing a mysterious version of yourself in what is the most challenging dungeon in the entire game. But the remake somehow managed to make this already iconic fight even more impressive.

And if you've faced him yourself, you probably know exactly what I'm talking about! Dark Link can actually dodge one of your attacks by jumping onto your Master Sword and balancing himself on the blade. It only lasts a few seconds, but it's such an incredible detail that I couldn't help but stare in disbelief the first time it happened.

It's one of those moments when you're simultaneously nervous and amazed by what you're seeing. You can almost feel the tension through the controller as you try to figure out how to deal with this little menace.

I love pointing out how The Legend of Zelda titles occasionally pretend to be horror experiences, and Ocarina of Time has some particularly memorable examples. The Bottom of the Well is easily one of them, throwing Link into a disturbing underground area filled with fake walls and some of the most unsettling enemies in the game.

And let's not forget the ReDeads and Dead Hand! Seriously, who thought putting those things in a game for children was a good idea? I still remember how uncomfortable I felt exploring this place as a kid, never quite knowing what might be waiting around the next corner.

Returning to the Well with improved visuals didn't make things any less terrifying, either. If anything, seeing all those creepy details in greater clarity made the experience even worse. I was just as scared as I was all those years ago!

Eiji Aonuma and the development team for the Ocarina of Time remake discussed how one of the original's infamous dungeons is being improved.

Boss fights are one of the most memorable parts of any Zelda adventure, and Kotake and Koume are easily among my personal favorites. The twin witches offer a genuinely fun battle, but what makes them so special is how much personality they bring to the encounter. They're not just dangerous enemies, but also absolutely hilarious!

Their constant bickering, especially when they start arguing over which one of them is younger, never fails to make me laugh. It's such a silly little interaction, but it gives the two characters so much charm and makes the entire encounter even more memorable.

And the remake takes things a step further with dedicated cutscenes and voice acting, making the sisters even more entertaining. Their performances are spot on, and I found myself grinning like an idiot every time they started arguing.

Reaching the climax of Ocarina of Time is already an emotional experience, but climbing Ganon's Tower is one of those moments that stays with you forever. And I'll never forget ascending that final staircase before confronting Ganondorf, with the intense organ music gradually growing louder as we approach the villain.

But here's the brilliant detail: Ganondorf is actually the one playing that music! It's such a simple touch, but one that made players lose their minds back in the day, especially talking about a time when games weren't always experimenting with environmental storytelling in this way.

The remake recreates the moment beautifully, preserving all the tension and impact while giving us an even more impressive look at the scene. And then there's Ganondorf himself, and seeing his updated design in all its glory only makes the encounter more intimidating! It's easily one of my favorite interpretations of the King of Evil.

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time is one of the most beloved games of all time, but these remakes might be just as meaningful to fans.
