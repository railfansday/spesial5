All That Glitters: Jewelry Highlights from Vogue’s Best Dressed List

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

When it comes to Best Dressed, it's not all about the threads, the baubles deserve their due, too. Scanning Vogue's illustrious 2026 list, we noticed that several of our honorees have a particular penchant for accessorizing, and an equally impressive command of the historical reference. Take Margot Robbie, who borrowed Elizabeth Taylor's Taj Mahal diamond for the Los Angeles premiere of Wuthering Heights, while Céline Dion revisited her own fashion history during her Paris residency, commissioning a new version of the Chanel Comète necklace she wore to the 1997 Oscars.

And the stories don't end there. From Colman Domingo's exuberant brooches to Zoë Kravitz's decidedly nonchalant approach to wearing enormous diamonds, this year's Best Dressed honorees know that jewelry can be as revealing of personal style as the clothes themselves. Whether it's a sentimental keepsake, a historical curiosity, or a spectacular feat of craftsmanship, these are the jewels that deserve a closer look.

For the Los Angeles premiere of Wuthering Heights in January, Margot Robbie paired her custom Schiaparelli gown with a piece of Hollywood history: Elizabeth Taylor's Taj Mahal diamond. The heart-shaped stone, suspended from a Cartier necklace, was a gift from Richard Burton to Taylor for her 40th birthday in 1972. With its associations of grand romance and one of Hollywood's most famously tempestuous love affairs, it was an inspired choice for a film about Catherine and Heathcliff. And Robbie's jewelry references didn't end there. At the London premiere, she wore a reproduction of Charlotte Brontë's mourning bracelet, whose original contained hair believed to belong to her sisters Emily and Anne. Clearly, someone had done their literary homework.

Céline Dion has never been one to shy away from a spectacular jewel, but her choices during her triumphant return to the stage in Paris came with more than a little sentiment. For her opening performance, she wore Boucheron's The Address necklace, a black-lacquer-and-diamond creation featuring a 10.01-carat emerald-cut diamond. The connection was deeply personal: her late husband, René Angélil, always wore Boucheron fragrance, and Dion said the necklace made her feel as though he were with her.

Then, in October, Dion revisited a defining moment in her own fashion history. In 1997, she became the first artist to perform twice at a single Oscars ceremony, doing so in a plunging silver Chanel gown by Karl Lagerfeld and a celestial Comète necklace. Nearly three decades later, Matthieu Blazy reimagined the gown for her Paris residency, complete with a new version of the necklace. Inspired by Gabrielle Chanel's original 1932 high-jewelry design, the piece featured 1,080 diamonds totaling more than 67 carats, set in platinum and white gold. Some things really are worth repeating.

Zendaya and longtime stylist Law Roach have made something of a specialty of dressing for the part, and their jewelry choices are no exception. There was the Bulgari Serpenti necklace, complete with a 93.83-carat emerald, at the 2021 Venice premiere of Dune, and the spectacular Chopard diamond bib worn with a sculptural Schiaparelli breastplate for The Odyssey this year. (Let’s not forget those controversial ancient Iranian gold medallion earrings that got people talking.) But perhaps her most intriguing accessory appeared during the Spider-Man: Brand New Day press tour in Shanghai: a diamond-set spider brooch with a yellow sapphire at its center, pinned to an archival Atelier Versace gown. The reference was obvious enough, but the provenance proved rather more complicated when two jewelry houses claimed credit for the jewel. The brooch was eventually traced to David Webb, though precisely how it ended up on Zendaya’s hip remains a mystery. A tangled web, indeed!

Few men have done more for the brooch in recent years than Colman Domingo. At the 2026 Emmys, he pinned Boucheron's Green Garden brooch, an extravagant arrangement of green tourmalines, diamonds, and green lacquer, to the fuchsia sash of his custom Valentino ensemble by Alessandro Michele. The piece comes from Boucheron's Like a Queen collection, inspired by a double-clip brooch given to Princess Elizabeth for her 18th birthday. Domingo's appreciation for jewelry history runs deep: at the 2025 Oscars, he wore a Boucheron ribbon brooch dating to 1956, while his 2025 Met Gala look featured the house's dramatic rock-crystal-and-diamond Iceberg necklace. Evidently, he sees no reason to leave the family jewels to the ladies.

Zoë Kravitz has a particular talent for making extraordinary jewelry look like something she just threw on. Case in point: the 20-carat Jessica McCormack diamond pendant she wore on a simple black cord for an appearance on Late Night with Seth Meyers last summer, paired with a cropped Saint Laurent vest, low-slung pinstripe trousers, and a bandana. As the jeweler's first-ever ambassador, Kravitz has made a convincing case for wearing serious diamonds with decidedly casual ease. At the 2026 Met Gala, she took a more surrealist approach, pairing her black lace Saint Laurent gown with McCormack's bespoke Medallion Cry Baby earrings and matching ring. Inspired by the imagery of Man Ray and Salvador Dalí, the earrings featured diamond tears suspended from golden eyes.

Leave it to Cole Escola to turn a piece of antique jewelry into a theatrical history lesson. At the 2025 Tony Awards, the Oh, Mary! playwright and star wore a custom ice-blue Wiederhoeft gown inspired by the costume Bernadette Peters wore to accept her Tony for Annie Get Your Gun in 1999. But the real deep cut was around Escola's neck: an antique pendant containing a portrait of Laura Keene, the actress and theater manager who was starring in Our American Cousin at Ford's Theatre on the night Abraham Lincoln was assassinated. The reference was particularly apt for the creator of a comedy about Mary Todd Lincoln, and hardly a casual choice. Escola has even spoken of visiting Keene's grave since Oh, Mary! opened.

Chase Sui Wonders has developed a fondness for Tiffany & Co.'s feathered friends. At the 2026 Venice Film Festival, she wore Jean Schlumberger's iconic Bird on a Rock brooch, featuring a diamond of more than 13 carats and a pink sapphire, pinned to a simple ribbon around her neck. It was an inventive way to wear a design that dates back to 1965, and considerably more interesting than an ordinary diamond necklace. At the Actor Awards earlier in the year, she had opted for another interpretation of the house's avian motif: a Bird on a Rock necklace set with a 12-carat rubellite, accompanied by diamond-and-rubellite earrings totaling more than 33 carats.
