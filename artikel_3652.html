What Happened to Sonia Exelby? Woman Killed by Man She Met in Fetish Chatroom

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

A British piano tutor’s shocking death at the hands of a self-described “alpha sadist” she met in an online fetish chatroom has ended with a Florida jury delivering a unanimous verdict, two consecutive life sentences and gut-wrenching statements from her family in court.

Dwain Hall was found guilty on November 20, 2026, of kidnapping and murdering Sonia Exelby in October 2025. A jury of 12 deliberated for roughly an hour before telling Judge Timothy McCourt they’d reached their decision, concluding that the murder was premeditated. Hall was sentenced immediately to two consecutive life sentences in prison.

The case drew international attention after Exelby — a music tutor from Portsmouth, England — flew across the Atlantic to meet a man she had only known online. What followed was a horrifying sequence of events that prosecutors described as a sadistic exploitation of a vulnerable woman struggling with severe mental illness.

Here’s everything to know about Sonia Exelby’s death, the investigation and the trial that ended with Hall behind bars for life.

Exelby flew from London to Gainesville, Florida, on October 10, 2025, where Hall picked her up from the airport. The pair then traveled to an Airbnb in Reddick, Florida, according to court documents obtained by Us.

When Exelby failed to board her return flight to England on October 13, 2025, authorities in the United Kingdom considered her endangered and missing. U.K. officials called on the Florida Department of Law Enforcement to help investigate her disappearance.

As the search unfolded, investigators turned up disturbing evidence on Exelby’s computer. According to Hall’s arrest affidavit, the materials indicated “she was suicidal and traveling to the United States to be possibly sexually abused, tortured, and murdered.”

On October 17, 2025, Exelby’s remains were discovered buried in a shallow grave in Marion County. Hall, who lived in Ocala, was arrested on murder and kidnapping charges about a month later, on November 17, 2025. He later pleaded not guilty. Inside Dwain Hall’s Online Connection to Sonia Exelby Hall admitted to investigators that he and Exelby had met on a fetish website and had been communicating for roughly two years before she traveled to Florida. According to the affidavit, he said the two “discussed bondage, suicide, and her desire to have someone kill her.”

Hall’s profile on the website — which has since been taken down in the U.S. — described him as a “sadist,” according to The Times. One of the sites where they connected, motherless.com, allowed anonymous users to upload disturbing images of women and children being tortured, raped and killed. The website is now defunct.

Hall also told police that Exelby had promised to pay him $4,000 to kill her, framing the arrangement as a consensual contract. But evidence presented at trial painted a very different picture of what happened inside that Airbnb.

While staying at the rental, Exelby sent frantic messages to a friend over Discord that suggested she was reconsidering the deadly arrangement — and that she was afraid.

“I’m sorry he keeps taking my phone he doesn’t trust me with it,” she wrote in messages included in the affidavit. “He made it clear there was no way out unless I shoot him. I was questioning it last night. I can’t kill anyone.”

She continued, “I’m so so scared I’m so broken and in so much pain all I can do is lay here and doing what [he] wants makes him respect me enough not to do the things I really hate. Everything but some are.”

A video Hall recorded of Exelby inside the Airbnb on October 11, 2025, showed bruises and abrasions on her body, according to the affidavit. The next morning, the Airbnb’s owner received a notification that Exelby had checked out of her stay — days before her body was recovered.

“Hall knew Exelby suffered from severe mental illness and was suicidal,” the affidavit states. “He claimed that he wanted to help Exelby and did not want her to die but his actions show differently. Exelby was in a foreign country and extremely vulnerable.”

Hall’s trial began in Marion County on Tuesday, November 17, 2026. Assistant State Attorney Amy Berndt told jurors that Hall had targeted Exelby “because of her mental illness and he exploited her for his own sadistic pleasure,” per The Independent.

“And when he finally got her alone, thousands of miles away from home, completely incapable of making any rational decisions, he stabbed her to death and buried her body,” Berndt continued.

Prosecutors argued that Hall didn’t try to save a struggling woman — he preyed on her. After just about an hour of deliberation, the jury agreed, returning a unanimous guilty verdict and finding the murder was premeditated. Hall, who was 53 at the time of his conviction, was sentenced on the spot to two consecutive life sentences.

Eight of Exelby’s family members were in the courtroom to hear Hall’s fate, and four of her siblings delivered statements before his sentence was handed down.

“Dwain, you pretended to care for Sonia,” Barry Wilton said in court, per the Daily Mail. “But you didn’t at all. You used her for your sick fantasy. You had the opportunity to save her. Instead, you took her from us. May your actions weight heavily on your mind for the rest of your pitiful life.”

Luke Wilton praised the Florida officials who handled the case and the strangers who supported the family during the painful proceedings.

“This year has been terrifying for the family. We were terrified coming here. What we found, however, is decency and care. People met here were stunning, seeing how the state of Florida fought for us, seeing all these people came here together in harmony for us,” he said.

Luke added, “Thank you to the jury for their patience and attentiveness, and to the prosecutors who left nothing to chance, and staff who looked after us for kindness, humanity and dedication.”

Ahead of the trial, Exelby’s sister Gemma Isaj created a GoFundMe to help the family cover travel costs to Florida. On the page, Isaj remembered her sister as “a beautiful, gentle and incredibly kind soul” who “struggled with her mental health.”

“Her struggles were only one part of her story,” Isaj wrote. “Sonia was so much more than the difficulties she faced. She was a loving sister, aunt, daughter, family member and friend.”
