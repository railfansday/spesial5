Mayday At 33,000 Feet: Leaky Faucet Floods Frontier Airlines Airbus A321neo Cabin

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Last week, a Frontier Airlines Airbus A321neo flying from Denver International Airport (DEN) to Detroit Metropolitan Wayne County Airport (DTW) had to divert to Chicago O'Hare International Airport (ORD) after a leaky faucet flooded the floor of the passenger cabin. The flight declared 'mayday' to air traffic control while cruising at 33,000 feet over eastern Iowa, and subsequently landed safely at Chicago O'Hare.

While there were no injuries as a result of the incident and the subsequent diversion and landing, the potentially serious nature of the leak meant that the situation had to be treated as a safety issue as opposed to just a mess that needed clearing up. This is because of the damage that water can cause to a jet's electronics. The aircraft, which is just two months old, has since re-entered commercial service.

Frontier Airlines flight F9-3566 is a daily scheduled flight from Denver to Detroit that typically takes between two and two-and-a-half hours to complete. On November 11, it took off at 11:02 AM, but, while cruising over Iowa at 33,000 feet, things took an eventful turn when a faucet in the forward lavatory of the A321neo operating the flight (N688FR) sprang a leak. The crew subsequently declared 'mayday.'

The flight subsequently descended through 24,000 feet as it made its way towards its requested diversion airport, Chicago O'Hare. According to View From The Wing, the pilots told ATC that they were "worried about electronics," with CBS reporting that the leak was so severe that the water spilled out of the bathroom and flooded the cabin, as seen above. Frontier told ABC7 Chicago in a statement that:

"The faucet in the forward lavatory sprung a leak, which caused water to flow along part of the aircraft floor."

The risks of water coming into contact with electronics are well known in all walks of life, but, in the world of commercial aviation, a situation such as this poses particular danger. This is because, on airliners like the Airbus A321neo, many of the jet's avionics and wiring systems are located below the floor of the main passenger cabin. As such, Wednesday's incident could have compromised them.

Aviation regulators are also all too aware of this threat, with the FAA having issued a relevant airworthiness directive, numbered as 2026-02-10. This replaced AD 2024-01-01, and it was issued after undetected leaks from Boeing 787 faucet modules seeped below the cabin floor and into the electronics bays. While this involves a different aircraft type to the Airbus A321neo, it highlights the relevant dangers.

It is also particularly noteworthy that N688FR, the Frontier Airlines Airbus A321neo involved in the diversion on November 11, is nearly brand-new. According to ch-aviation, it first flew just under three months ago, on August 21, and was delivered just over two months ago, on September 3. Frontier is an all-Airbus operator, with six A320-200s, 62 A320neos, 21 A321-200s, and 70 A321neos in its fleet today.

Two flight attendants were taken to hospital as the jet remains grounded and multiple flights were canceled.

After the flight landed in Chicago, a replacement A321neo registered as N655FR was dispatched to take the passengers of F9-3566 to Detroit. The Aviation Herald notes that their eventual arrival in the 'Motor City' took place around 4.5 hours behind schedule, just after 8:00 PM local time. As for N688FR, the first A321neo, the jet was grounded for the rest of the day before re-entering service on November 12.

This is the latest in a string of cabin incidents involving Frontier Airlines in recent months. As detailed in our coverage linked above, multiple flight attendants fell ill on one of the carrier's A321s in August, while, more recently, passengers rushed the exit door and clashed with crew after being kept on board with no air conditioning after a jet bridge failure. Ultimately, Wednesday's incident was a precautionary diversion with no injuries, and the aircraft has since re-entered regular service.
