Giving Thanks When America Feels Like The Last Days Of Narnia

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

This Thanksgiving, I keep thinking about the last days of Narnia. Not because I believe the world is ending, but because C.S. Lewis understood how easily faith can be exploited by those seeking power…and how difficult it becomes to see clearly when we are frightened or angry.

In The Last Battle, an ape named Shift dresses a donkey in a lion’s skin and persuades the Narnians that Aslan has returned. Shift then speaks for him, demanding obedience and using their love for Aslan to advance his own interests.

The deception works because the Narnians want to be faithful. They have difficulty believing that someone invoking Aslan could be asking them to betray everything Aslan represents.

I find that painfully familiar when I watch Christians excuse conduct in Donald Trump that they would rightly condemn in almost anyone else. Once defending a leader becomes a religious obligation, we have given him something that never belonged to him.

The lion’s skin does not make the donkey Aslan. Invoking Christianity does not make a political demand Christian.

That warning applies to me too. Seeing another person’s hypocrisy does not excuse my own contempt. Being right about a political failure does not make every word I write about it righteous.

I can become so occupied with what is wrong that I stop noticing what God has given me. I can begin to enjoy indignation. That is a dangerous habit for anyone, particularly someone who writes for a living.

Scripture takes ingratitude seriously. In Romans 1:21, Paul describes people who knew God but neither honored Him as God nor gave thanks. Ingratitude accompanies their descent into darkened thinking and idolatry. And in Ephesians 5:3–4, Paul places thanksgiving where corrupt speech ought to be displaced. Gratitude is part of how we resist sin.

Giving thanks reminds me that I am a recipient of mercy. I have no grounds to treat another person as beyond its reach.

I remain grateful to be an American. I will continue to criticize our government when I believe it deserves criticism, and I will continue to pray for those who govern us, including President Trump.

But my allegiance belongs to the Kingdom of Heaven and to Jesus Christ, the King of Kings. No party gets to borrow that allegiance. No president gets to claim it.

That gives me freedom to acknowledge something good done by someone I oppose and something wrong done by someone I supported. It also means an election cannot deliver everything I hope for or take away everything that matters.

Paul tells Christians to give thanks in all circumstances (1 Thessalonians 5:18). I understand that to include circumstances I want to change. Gratitude does not require pretending that injustice is good or that incompetence is harmless.

Today, I am thankful for my wife and children, for the church that has sustained me, my loving and supportive family and friends, and for the extraordinary privilege of traveling through this world and sharing it with you. Most of all, I am thankful that my hope rests with a King whose character does not require excuses.

Lewis understood that Shift did not have the last word. I need to remember that too.
