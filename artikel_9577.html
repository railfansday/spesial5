The Only 5 Metrics a Pre-Seed AI Product Needs

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Posted on Nov 25 • Originally published at okaihub.com

A pre-seed AI product does not have a measurement problem. It has a focus problem: founders install analytics, build dashboards, and then make the same decisions they would have made anyway because none of the numbers change what to do next. Five metrics do change decisions. The rest are decoration at this stage.

These are the five, why each one, and what to do when it moves.

The share of new signups who complete the action that indicates they got value — not just signed up, but did the thing. This is the single most important early metric because it separates a distribution problem from a product problem.

Do people who activated come back? Retention at week two is early enough to measure quickly and late enough to mean something. A product people do not return to has no business model regardless of how many signups it collects.

Not how much — from where. Segment by source, and because several platforms and AI assistants strip UTM parameters, use referrer as the reliable method.

The metric most pre-seed AI products ignore and most need. If your product calls a model on every use, serving a user costs real money, and a growing free base can be a growing loss you have not looked at.

For an AI product this is not a finance-team concern deferred until scale. A generous free tier with per-use cost can be losing money from the first hundred users, and the loss grows exactly as fast as your traction looks good.

The least dashboard-like metric and the one that matters most early. How many real conversations did you have with users or potential users this week? At pre-seed, learning speed is the constraint, and learning comes from conversations.

That is the whole review. It takes fifteen minutes, and every number in it points at a specific action rather than a feeling about how the week went.

A metric is worth tracking if it changes what you do. At pre-seed, five do. The dashboard with forty tiles changes nothing — it just makes the week feel measured.
