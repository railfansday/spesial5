Joe Baena may be the son of Arnold Schwarzenegger, but he is certainly forging his own path in the competitive bodybuilding business. The 29-year-old recently made his debut at the INBA PNBA World Cup in El Segundo, CA, reaching an impressive fifth place on the natural stage. And, for anyone who is aspiring to compete in the future, Baena chronicled his first pro outing for an insightful glimpse.

“Each photo has a great story of what it is like to be a competitor at a bodybuilding show,” posted Baena for his 895,000 Instagram followers, providing all the backstage goings on during the World Cup on November 7.

Earlier this year, the young upstart dominated in the Men’s Physique category of the NPC Classic Physique and Open, during his amateur debut, and then went to earn his pro card at the INBA Iron Gladiator event a week later on April 4.

Sharing his feelings as he arrived for the World Cup, Baena said, “Tan on, nerves high, and just trying to mentally prepare for the day ahead. Excited to see the carbs doing their work and that the abs are still there after two meals.”

Sharing an image of the changing room, Baena explained that before each competitor gets under the spotlight, they like to watch the other athletes first. “Seeing the guys in the other classes is always very exciting,” he wrote. “As you can see new things in their posing routines and styles. Everyone brings their individual personalities to the stage.”

Half an hour before his own routine, Baena began to carb up. “About 20 to 30 minutes before stepping on the stage, I’m getting my last light carbs in to fill up the muscles and look as pumped as possible,” he shared. “A couple rice cakes and a little honey drizzle does just the trick.”

Then comes “The Pump Up” to get the blood flowing into the muscles. “There’s no gym or equipment provided for most bodybuilding competitions, so you have to bring your own resistance bands or get some help from another competitor backstage,” explained Baena. “The camaraderie was amazing and everyone was just so excited to be there.”

For this detailed prepper, being specific about which muscles to pump is highly important. “You gotta make sure you pump up the right muscles, especially the ones that are lacking or need the most volume to present,” he shared. “For me, I know my arms aren’t the biggest, so I always put a little extra effort into pumping up the biceps and triceps.”

“The first thing when you step out on stage is you all lineup and basically square up next to each other,” said the competitor. “Presenting yourself in the best way possible is important along with standing out from the other competitors. After you’ve all lined up and done the mandatory poses it’s time for the pose down! You all get as close to the judges as possible and flex off together. Standing next to each other, hitting all your favorite poses and trying to present yourself as the largest, most ripped and best-looking competitor out there. Occasionally going pose for pose or just doing your own thing, you want to attract the judge’s attention as much as possible.”

The posing routine is the “last but definitely not least” aspect of the competition, wrote Baena. “The posing routine is my favorite part of the whole process. The artistry aspect of bodybuilding is what I think makes the sport so great. Building your body up to be statue-like and choreographing a routine that emulates and shows the aesthetic and beauty of the body.”

Having flexed for all that he was worth at the World Cup, Baena explained that with 11 competitors in his category, “It was extremely nerve-racking, standing there waiting for my name to get called out. My goal going into the show was to get into the top five so when I heard my name get called at fifth place it was a huge relief. I achieved what I signed out for and could finally think forward about getting a burger. Of course, the dream is to win the show and be the number one overall, but it all comes in due time and with the right amount of work. This was only my first pro show, and I couldn’t be more proud of myself. This is my first year competing with my first show being the end of March, the second show been the week after, and this being only my third show. A lot of the guys I competed with have been competing for many years now, so it is great motivation to be able to stand up and stack up next to them.”

Finally, Baena indulges in a well-earned meal. But what did he eat? “The Apple Pan,” he revealed. “Definitely one of the best burgers in LA. Had to celebrate at the Apple Pan for my first meal post competition. I ordered two burgers and two slices of pie. Somehow, even after eating all that I still had room for more.”

Glowing after “The Champion’s Pie,” Baena said, “I’m a huge pie lover and having been on prep during many weddings, my birthday, and other celebrations (where) I had to skip on many desserts, this pie tasted incredible, but I will say achieving a goal you set out for is a lot sweeter than any cheat meal.”

And that’s just as well, because soon begins the prep for Baena’s next competition.
