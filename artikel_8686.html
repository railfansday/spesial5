2027 FSA Limits – Health Is $3,500 per Worker, Dependent Care $7,500 per Household

Before you elect a flexible spending account (FSA) for 2027, check which limits you share with a spouse. A health FSA’s $3,500 cap applies to each worker, so you can each elect it in full. A dependent care FSA, for child care, has one $7,500 cap per household, so working spouses split it.

Mixing them up costs a two-earner couple either way. Spouses who think they share one health FSA limit can set aside half of what they’re allowed, and spouses who each elect the full dependent care amount owe income tax on the excess.

The IRS’s 2027 inflation adjustments put the health FSA limit at $3,500, though your plan can set a lower one. IRS Notice 2012-40 applies the limit employee by employee, so each spouse can elect the full amount, even at the same employer, and someone with two unrelated employers can elect it at each job.

A dependent care FSA works the other way. The IRS’s fringe benefits guide puts the limit at $7,500 a year, or $3,750 each for spouses who file separate tax returns, up from $5,000 and $2,500 before 2026. A married couple filing jointly gets one limit for both spouses’ accounts combined, and it can’t top the lower earner’s pay from work. Both figures are written into the tax code rather than adjusted for inflation, so neither changes for 2027.

Check your plan’s carryover rules before you elect. If it allows a carryover, up to $700 of unspent 2027 money can roll into the next plan year without counting against that year’s limit. A plan can allow less or none, and any unspent money beyond that is lost unless you spend it before the deadline. How much to elect is a separate decision, best based on last year’s medical bills.

Say you and your spouse both work full time, with both kinds of FSA at plans that allow the IRS’s limits. You can each elect the full $3,500 for health care, but your two dependent care elections can’t top $7,500 together, such as $4,500 at one job and $3,000 at the other. Electing the full $7,500 at both jobs would make half of that dependent care money taxable income.

Keeping the total within the cap is up to the two of you, so agree on how to divide the $7,500 before either of you enrolls.
