GB Boxing back 'Night of Solidarity' event for Grenfell Tower tragedy

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The 'A Night of Solidarity' project recognises the actions of local community groups in the aftermath of the Grenfell Tower fire; A night of amateur boxing at Kensington Town Hall raised over £60,000 with a North Kensington team overcoming London Fire Brigade Boxing Club 7-4

GB Boxing backed an event at Kensington Town Hall in support of the ‘A Night of Solidarity’ project, which raised more than £60,000 for community groups in areas affected by the Grenfell fire tragedy and the Fire Fighters charity.

'A Night of Solidarity' was created to recognise the actions of the local community in the aftermath of the Grenfell Tower fire in June 2017, when boxing and sport clubs united with the Fire Fighters charity to create safe spaces for young people and begin the process of restoring hope to a community affected by tragedy.

At last Friday's event a North Kensington select amateur boxing team overcame a team from the London Fire Brigade Boxing Club 7-4 in front of a sold-out crowd.

The charity boxing night in Kensington is one element of the project, which included a reception in the House of Lords in September and a visit to the GB Boxing gym in Sheffield where some of the young boxers connected to clubs and community projects in north Kensington will get to train with Great Britain's Olympic boxing hopefuls.

'A Night of Solidarity' was conceived by Lord Andrew Roe of West Wickham, a former Commissioner of the London Fire Brigade, who was on duty the night of the Grenfell Tower fire.

He said: "We've been thinking about this a long time so to see it all come together and see the boxers perform like they did, with their dignity, their strength and their courage and to see the way the community has come to support us, has made it a fantastic night.

"I have spoken to a lot of the boxers and they're buzzing because they've been treated like professionals. They have met some of their stars. They have met boxers that are going to go to the next Olympics, so they've felt like superstars, which is how they should do.

"It just shows you the power of boxing, from grassroots to the highest level."

Olympic medallist Lewis Richardson was supporting the event. He said: "These events have a powerful, positive impact for the community. It's a pleasure to be a part of it and representing GB Boxing, which does fantastic work for elite athletes, but it all starts at grassroots level, so it's important we give something back.

"These are memories that these young adults will have for life and it's great seeing that the other GB boxers and me can be here. Hopefully it will have a positive impact and be an inspiration for them."
