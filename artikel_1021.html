Emery endures heaviest European defeat as Villa manager to old foes Barcelona

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

While there can be little doubt about Unai Emery’s status as an elite manager in the modern game, matches at the Nou Camp continue to be the stuff of nightmares.

His record at Barcelona now reads 15 defeats from 16 matches and this was another difficult evening with Aston Villa unable to banish the demons for the man who has transformed their club.

It was Emery’s 55th birthday and with the Prince of Wales and Prince George both in attendance, Villa fans were hopeful of another memorable European night. But from the moment midfielder João Gomes handed Barcelona their opening goal, it was always going to be an uphill struggle.

Emery has suffered many uncomfortable occasions at this grand old stadium, none more so than the notorious “La Remontada” of 2017 when he last competed here in the Champions League with Paris St-Germain.

This latest visit would become his heaviest defeat in Europe as Villa manager. With three wins from the opening four matches, they still have a fine chance of progressing to the knockout stage. Yet, aside from the mistakes, there will be a sense that Villa could have capitalised more against a team in the midst of a defensive injury crisis. They did have a number of chances in the first half but were left to rue their lack of ruthlessness.

At the heart of Barcelona’s victory was Lamine Yamal, their outstanding jewel from the academy. The 19-year-old became the highest-scoring teenager in Champions League history, eclipsing the record of 13 he previously shared with Kylian Mbappé, with the opening goal.

Yamal was orchestrating everything from the pace of the match to the volume of the supporters, at one point imploring them to raise the noise in the first half with both arms aloft.

There was also another impressive performance from England international Anthony Gordon and Raphinha, who dedicated his two goals to the memory of Jesus de Miguel, the Tenerife forward, who died on Tuesday.

With that tragedy fresh in the minds and the Nou Camp under construction, it was a peculiar atmosphere at times.

The defeat marked the end of Villa’s 100 per cent record in the competition, with a tough test away at Galatasaray up next before they host PSG in December. They remain high up in the table and have often displayed a propensity to bounce back quickly from defeats.

Emery said: “We are in a process and this was a very good test against a team like Barcelona. We are not at the level we want to achieve but there are a lot of experiences for us to take. Overall, we are competing fantastically in the Champions League and there is still work to do.

“Over the process we’ve had here, we have made mistakes but then overcame them. We accept this defeat but take a lot of information from it.”

Nine years have passed since Emery’s last visit in this competition and time has not proved a great healer.

Then in charge of PSG, Emery witnessed his team surrender a 4-0 first-leg lead to lose 6-5 on aggregate. Any matches where he is involved at the Nou Camp will revive memories of that remarkable comeback. He must have feared a repeat of more pain when Yamal, making his 37th Champions League appearance, rewarded Barcelona’s early pressure.

Villa’s defending needed to be as close to perfect as possible, but they were exposed after a poor error of judgment from Gomes. Under pressure from Barcelona’s attackers, Gomes panicked and played a blind pass into the path of Yamal, who bent his left-footed shot into the corner.

That mistake could have threatened to damage confidence but Villa did respond. Matty Cash’s half-volley from inside the penalty area was only a few inches wide, while Johan Manzambi side-footed wide of the goal after a quick break down the right from Nicolas Jackson.

With Andreas Christensen, Eric García, Xavi Espart and Jules Koundé all absent here, Barcelona’s defence did appear vulnerable. Jackson was targeting the flanks and having his more dangerous moments challenging their high line. Emery paced his technical area, imploring his players to remain focused.

Yet Barcelona’s forward three were always a threat and they increased their lead eight minutes into the second half. Raphinha was given too much room to turn in the penalty area and his shot took a heavy deflection off the heel of Pau Torres to find the bottom corner. Emery was furious, appearing to raise his fist in the air at another poor goal conceded.

Barcelona added a third goal with Raphinha converting from close range after a sublime pass from João Cancelo.

Raphinha dedicated his goals to De Miguel, who died on Tuesday after sustaining serious injuries during a match the previous night. He said: “Tonight I don’t want to talk about football. I want to send a lot of strength to Jesus de Miguel’s family.

“May he rest in peace. In the end, this can happen to anyone. The truth is that it’s very tough. When we saw the news this morning, it was complicated. I’m incredibly shocked and sad. In the end, you go to play a match, and the same thing could happen to you. From myself, from the club, we want to send a lot of strength to his family.”

Villa did continue to fight for a consolation, with substitute Leon Goretzka heading against the post and their 3,000 travelling fans continued to sing in the rain after the final whistle.

This was the first encounter between the two clubs since the European Super Cup Final in January 1983, and yet another reminder of the upward trajectory under Emery.

Aston Villa have made the trip over to Spain to take on Barcelona in the Champions League on matchday four. These teams last met all the way back in 1983, when they met in the European Super Cup. Barcelona won the first leg 1-0 at home before Aston Villa claimed a 3-0 victory at Villa Park, meaning Villa progressed 3-1 on aggregate.

Aston Villa have made the perfect start to their Champions League campaign, winning all three games, beating Club Brugge, Fenerbahce and Viking, scoring 12 goals in the process. Unai Emery’s side drew 1-1 at home against Fulham in the league on Saturday. Emery, who celebrates his 55th birthday today, has a very dismal record against Barcelona, taking just three wins from 30 matches. His last Champions League game at the Nou Camp was infamous as his PSG side lost 6-1 to go out in the last 16 back in 2017 despite winning the first leg of that tie 4-0.

“In 1982, Villa won the European Cup, and we like to think about playing in this competition again and trying to repeat that success,” Emery said. “Barcelona are favourites for what they have done in the last ten years; now they need to prove why they are favourites.

“[Barcelona] have [Lamine] Yamal. He is a special player. You can see in him as a player who could end up being the best. He needs a few things but he could end up being a great player. Rodri gives personality to the team. They have [Anthony] Gordon giving them speed and hard work. They have Pedri or Fermin [Lopez] as offensive midfielders.”

Barcelona started strongly in the Champions League, winning their first two games, but lost 4-2 at defending champions PSG last time out. They warmed up for this match with an emphatic 6-1 win over Alaves in La Liga on Saturday. That victory gives them a two-point lead at the summit of La Liga.

“We know that at this level, anything can happen,” Barcelona boss Hansi Flick said. “Against Real Madrid, the result could have been different. These are special matches, but we are staying confident. It is normal to focus more on our last two defeats, but I have confidence in my team, which showed it again on Saturday. Now, we are focused on the Champions League, with the goal of finishing in the top eight. For me, he [Unai Emery] is one of the best coaches in the world and he has reaped extraordinary successes. His team’s style of play is clear and very well organised. Just look at how they execute set pieces; it is something impressive. Their confidence in themselves is unmatched. It will be a tough match against a great team and a great coach.”
