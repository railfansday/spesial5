Darren Bent's beach ball bedlam, Nani's controversial quick-thinking and Peter Enckelman's calamitous mishap: Our experts pick the strangest goals they have ever seen after Bruno Fernandes' 'divot strike' in Lisbon

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Published: 15:03 EST, 26 November 2026 | Updated: 15:03 EST, 26 November 2026

Bruno Fernandes and Manchester United were the beneficiaries of a bizarre goal in the Champions League on Wednesday night, as the captain's long-range effort bounced up off the turf and deceived the goalkeeper to help earn Mauricio Pochettino a 2-2 draw with Sporting Lisbon in his second game in charge.

Football has certainly seen its fair share of quirky, bizarre and downright bonkers goals over the years, and Fernandes' effort in Lisbon is certainly one to add to the collection.

Here, Daily Mail Sport's reporters look back at some of the strangest goals they can remember, from outrageous goalkeeping blunders to freakish deflections and moments of sheer misfortune.

Darren Bent (Sunderland 1-0 Liverpool) - October 17, 2009

Forget bouncing in off a divot, try the ball flying into the net off a beach ball. Darren Bent’s winner against Liverpool at the Stadium of Light back in 2009 has got to be the strangest.

A Liverpool fan lobbed a gigantic red beach ball on to the pitch (in the middle of October) which was initially removed by a steward only for the wind to blow it back.

What made it even funnier was that when Bent’s shot struck it, Liverpool keeper Pepe Reina tried to save the inflatable, which now resides in the National Football Museum, instead of the actual ball that flew past him.

Thank heavens there was no VAR back then to spoil the day.

Pepe Reina had no chance after Darren Bent's strike ricocheted off a beach ball into the goal

Peter Enckelman (Birmingham vs Aston Villa) - September 16, 2002

Obviously the mind immediately goes to balloons and players ghosting behind goalkeepers. But my favourite happened on September 16, 2002 between Birmingham City and Aston Villa.

Olof Mellberg took a throw-in. He threw it back to Peter Enckelman. The ball bounced under Enckelman’s foot and rolled into the Villa goal. Referee David Elleray reckoned he must have touched it with his studs.

It was all made worse for Enckelman with a Birmingham fan hopping out of the crowd to goad him to his face. I must have been nine years old and remember watching all this live.

Can’t imagine my parents told me what that hand gesture the fan was doing in front of Enckelman meant.

Peter Enckelman scored a disastrous own goal after the ball bounced underneath his foot

Mo Diame (Brighton vs Newcastle) - February 28, 2017

Brighton half cleared a corner and Christian Atsu returned a scuffed volley, which was helped on by the toe of Daryl Murphy, before Mo Diame planted his foot in the ground and allowed the ball to bounce off the back of his heel, looping up and over all inside the goalmouth and into the top corner.

I was there, and I have revisited my match report.

'Diame had been stood still for the majority of the previous 81 minutes,' I wrote. 'Finally, it was a tactic which served him well.'

I also refer to the goal as fluky and hilarious. It is now the stuff of legend on Tyneside, for it helped Newcastle to win promotion as champions. Indeed, without it, Brighton would have finished top and Newcastle second.

Mo Diame scored one of the craziest goals in Championship history against Brighton in 2017

Tony Popovic (Portsmouth vs Crystal Palace) - September 11, 2004

Some goals stay with you because of the sublime, others because of the ridiculous. And I doubt I'll ever see a stranger goal live than Tony Popovic's flying backheel into his own net in 2004.

With Portsmouth protecting a 2-1 lead late on against Crystal Palace, nerves were jangling around Fratton Park until Popovic's moment of spectacular misfortune.

Pompey's Steve Stone floated a hopeful cross into the Palace box, prompting Popovic to stretch out a leg in an attempt to clear. Instead, he somehow managed to flick the ball off his boot, onto the post and into the net, producing a finish any top striker would be proud of.

Portsmouth couldn't believe their luck when Tony Popovic flicked the ball into in his own net

Nani (Manchester United vs Tottenham) - October 30, 2010

This one has always stuck with me, partly for the sheer audacity of Nani and partly because it's the sort of goal that simply wouldn't escape VAR scrutiny today.

Back when a trip to Manchester United was still a fearsome prospect, Spurs were a goal down and chasing a late equaliser when Nani was brought down in the penalty area and appeared to handle the ball.

Crucially, though, Mark Clattenburg had not blown his whistle. Tottenham goalkeeper Heurelho Gomes, assuming a free-kick had been awarded, placed the ball on the ground.

Still gesticulating, Nani got up, realised the ball was still live, and coolly passed it into the back of the net. Cue bedlam in the Stretford End.

Nani celebrates his controversial goal against Spurs, to the fury of goalkeeper Heurelho Gomes

Zlatan Ibrahimovic (Sweden vs England) - November 14, 2012

It's not weird in the category of a divot or a beach ball but the most outlandish goal I've ever seen live was Zlatan Ibrahimovic's 30-yard overhead kick for Sweden against England at the Friends Arena in Stockholm in November 2012.

A long ball came over the top of the England defence, Joe Hart ran out and headed it clear and Ibrahimovic launched himself into the air, hooking the ball in a majestic arc over England's disbelieving goalkeeper. Great goals can be strange, too.

Zlatan Ibrahimovic's acrobatic bicycle kick against England was both strange and sensational

Stan Collymore (Blackburn vs Liverpool) - February 24, 1996

As soon as I watched Fernandes' speculative long-range strike bobble past Sporting Lisbon goalkeeper Kaique Pereira, my mind immediately cast back to Tim Flowers being embarrassed by Stan Collymore at Ewood Park in 1996.

Not that it was the Blackburn goalkeeper's fault, of course. Collymore's feeble effort from distance in the 11th minute trickled along the turf before hitting a rogue divot and bouncing over Flowers' head into the net.

Collymore, who cracked a smile but toned down his celebrations, turned away in disbelief as Flowers stared down at the pitch with his hands on his hips.

Liverpool went on to win the game 3-2 and Flowers described it as 'one of those ridiculous situations'.

Stan Collymore scored one of the strangest goals when his effort bobbled over Tim Flowers

Gary Neville (England vs Croatia) - October 11, 2006

Strange bobbles take me back to Gary Neville’s own goal in Zagreb. Early in the doomed England reign of Steve McClaren.

Neville rolled a simple back pass towards Paul Robinson who took a mighty swing at fresh air with his right boot and looked aghast as it trundled past him and into the net for Croatia's second in a 2-0 win. The Spurs keeper stamped furiously at the offending divot as the Maksimir Stadium erupted.

Adding another layer to the comic value were pitchside LED ad screens flashing up images of Sacha Baron Cohen’s new Borat film and one of his lame catchphrases: 'I like you'.

It was a bad omen at the start of the failed Euro 2008 campaign and the beginning of the end for both Robinson and Neville in England colours.

Gary Neville's own goal against Croatia came after Paul Robinson totally missed the ball

Stefan Kiessling (Hoffenheim v Leverkusen) - October 18, 2013

Legendary Bayer Leverkusen forward Stefan Kiessling has got to be one of the only players to score with a shot off target.

While away to Hoffenheim, a corner was swung in towards Leverkusen striker Kiessling, who, at first glance, simply headed home smartly.

But on closer inspection we can see that the ball was destined for the side netting and an inconspicuous hole in the side of the net let the ball slip in.

Even Kiessling himself had his head in his hands thinking he’d missed before turning around to see his teammates were celebrating.

Leverkusen's Stefan Kiessling must be one of the only players to score with a shot off target
