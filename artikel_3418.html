Star Wars Sequel Trilogy Could Have Been Completely Different As Disney Reportedly Scrapped George Lucas' Plans

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Disney's Star Wars prequel trilogy could have gone in a completely different direction.

Written by Wall Street Journal reporter Robbie Whelan, The House of Mouse: Bob Iger and the War of Succession at Disney discusses Bob Iger’s leadership and his decisions, including the acquisitions of Pixar, Marvel, Lucasfilm, and 21st Century Fox, as well as his return as CEO following Bob Chapek’s departure.

The new book has offered more insight into why George Lucas’ original plans for the Star Wars sequel trilogy never made it to the big screen. Although Disney bought Lucasfilm in 2012, Iger reportedly ensured that Disney was under no legal obligation to follow through on every project or idea the company had.

In a recent interview on The Town podcast, Whelan discussed the complicated relationship between Disney and the Star Wars franchise. The journalist spoke about what were reportedly Lucas’ original storylines for the sequel trilogy movies, as well as Iger’s alleged reaction to them and creative decisions.

Whelan’s book claims that Iger wanted to purchase Lucas’ massive franchise but also ensure that the acquisition agreement did not require the studio to use his ideas, as he reportedly did not like the creator's ideas. A source who was involved in the negotiations claimed that Iger disliked the original storylines so much that he had no intention of adapting them at all.

Belloni: Lucas actually had treatments for three Star Wars movies that he wanted Disney to make after the acquisition. You write this in the book: ‘After reading the plot outlines, Iger decided Disney had to buy them, but he made sure to write into the contract that Disney had no legal obligation to actually make the movies. Another person who was involved in the negotiation recalls that Iger hated the treatments and said privately, ‘We are not making these movies, How bad were they?

Whelan: Imagine you’re an executive who’s never written a movie, never directed a movie, and George Lucas sends you a package of scripts, and you look at these and say, ‘This is terrible. We’re never making this.’

So, instead of going with Lucas' undisclosed plans, Disney ultimately went in a new direction. The industry giant launched its sequel trilogy with 2015’s Star Wars: The Force Awakens. Directed by J.J. Abrams, the film became a massive commercial success, earning over $2 billion at the box office worldwide.

However, despite the success of Disney's first Star Wars film, the next two installments were less well-received. Rian Johnson’s Star Wars: The Last Jedi got overall positive reviews from critics, but fans weren't entirely happy with it. Abrams’ Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker received more mixed reviews across the board. Both movies also earned less at the box office than the first film.

While it is impossible to know how Lucas’ sequel trilogy would have performed, Whelan’s claims provide more context surrounding Disney’s creative decisions involving Star Wars. It should also be noted that neither Disney nor Iger have confirmed the allegations in The House of Mouse: Bob Iger and the War of Succession at Disney, so anything written in the book should be taken with a grain of salt and not as fact.

The House of Mouse: Bob Iger and the War of Succession at Disney will be available on December 1, 2026.
