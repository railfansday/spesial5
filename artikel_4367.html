10 Soulslikes Where You’re Actually the Villain

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Shane is a Contributor for DualShockers writing guides and other materials. He has a degree in Game Design and Criticism.

​Of all the genres in gaming, there isn’t one that stays as morally ambiguous as soulslikes. While the genre has grown to encompass a lot of different kinds of games, that question of morality remains the same.

Players are often a bit hesitant to become the villain of the story, but offering up that potential makes for a more difficult choice. Whether a player will embrace evil or remain on the path of good always makes for a good time.

This list consists of games that could be categorized as soulslike even if their main genre is a bit different. It does contain spoilers for certain endings, so you may want to read carefully to avoid anything you’d rather not know.

If you're ready to root for the bad guy, then check out this list of shooters where you're actually the villain!

Hellpoint is an interesting soulslike game that sees players take on the role of a Spawn as they face off against all kinds of cosmic horrors. While trapped on a space station, players will make their way through boss encounters while making choices to determine their alignment.

You don’t have to play as the villain in Hellpoint, but you do have the option to align yourself with the Prodigal Spawn and perform a dark ritual. It is morally grey, but those choices do matter and can make players into the villain of their story.

In a genre that typically sticks to being morally grey, Death’s Gambit: Afterlife makes things a bit harder by making you an agent of death. In this Metroidvania soulslike, players will go up against immortals as they attempt to snuff out immortality altogether.

This one is tough because your motivations aren’t necessarily evil, but the results you achieve certainly make you a bit of a villain. By destroying immortality, you’ll be killing anything and everything connected to it, regardless of alignment.

You wouldn't typically imagine a game about giant robots digging so deep into the ambiguity of who a mercenary decides to fight for, but Armored Core VI does just that. The game is more of an action game than a soulslike, but with FromSoftware at the wheel, it still feels like one.

Playing as C4-621, you have a choice in who you help, and depending on your choice, a lot of things can happen. Despite the choice of endings, you do spend a lot of time fighting and killing the residents of Rubicon 3.

If you're tired of being the good guy, then check out this list of JRPGs where you play as the anti-hero!

FromSoftware really enjoys giving players a choice in how they affect the world as a whole, and most of the time it's not for the better. Playing as an Ashen One, players will have to face off against the mighty Lords of Cinder and decide the fate of the world.

There is still that freedom of choice, but players can choose to simply burn the world themselves. This certainly makes you a villain to some, but it is still a choice, so it’s up to players if they become a villain.

As a Hunter in Bloodborne, your only purpose is to kill the monsters that plague the world and use their power to make yourself stronger. You may not start very villainous, but in the end you may become the very thing you set out to destroy.

In one of the endings, players can turn into a Great One, an otherworldly eldritch being that doesn’t follow our sense of morality. It could be up for debate whether this makes players the true villain, but it does seem a bit villainous to leave behind your humanity.

Elden Ring was a sensational game that expanded the soulslike genre into the open-world genre and gave players more freedom than ever before. With that freedom came plenty of choice, and those choices could end in disaster.

By taking the Frenzied Flame, players will cause a chain reaction of events that ends the game with the world on fire. The world may be a bit problematic, but causing mass destruction and burning the world to a crisp seems very villainous.

While playing as the protagonist, Wolf, players are given choices, some of which have long-standing consequences. The game isn’t a traditional soulslike, but with the difficulty and mechanics behind the game, it certainly feels like one.

Wolf is given a choice towards the back half of the game where he can either follow Iron Code or save Kuro. Following the code results in Wolf fully becoming a demon and killing many of the people he knows and cares about.

With interesting characterization and backstories, these are the JRPGs Where the Villain Feels More Compelling Than the Hero.

The premise of the game seems to frame your character as the hero, but in true FromSoftware fashion, you can become the villain. Featuring a morality tracker, the more you kill innocents and do vile deeds, the closer you become to being Pure Black.

At the end of the game, players can decide to simply betray their allies up to that point, killing them and thrusting the world into a new age of darkness. Whether you were the villain throughout the game or not, at the end there’s no debating it.

Vampires are pretty much always the bad guys, although when it comes to Dr. Jonathan Reid, players can decide what happens. Whether he succumbs to the monster within or maintains some semblance of humanity is in the hands of players.

Whatever path the player takes determines what happens to London and its people. Leaning into the destructive nature of vampires leaves the city in complete collapse and causes the epidemic to continue unfettered.

Final Fantasy games as a whole have always been a bit out there in terms of story, but unlike the other games on this list, you are literally the villain. Playing as Jack along with the other Strangers as you attempt to stop Chaos from destroying the world.

By the end of the story, there’s only one option for our heroes, and that’s for Jack to embrace the Chaos and become the villain of the story. This sends him back in time and sets him up as Garland, the villain of the first Final Fantasy game.
