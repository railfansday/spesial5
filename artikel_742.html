Gerry Griffith, Music Executive Who Discovered Whitney Houston, Dies at 87

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

As the vice president of A&R at Arista Records, he brought Houston to Clive Davis and worked with other stars like Aretha Franklin and Carlos Santana.

Gerry Griffith, the music executive who discovered Whitney Houston while serving as vice president of A&R at Arista Records, died Nov. 5 after a five-year battle with dementia, his family announced. He was 87.

Born in Chicago and an Army veteran, Griffith first saw Houston perform in New York, and he brought her to the attention of Clive Davis, who signed her to a record deal in 1983 before she’d rise to global superstardom with generational hits including “I Wanna Dance With Somebody (Who Loves Me),” “I Will Always Love You” and “Greatest Love of All.” Related Stories TV Michael John Roberts, Animator and 'BoJack Horseman' and 'TripTank' Director, Dies at 48 TV Dan Hicks, NBC Sports Play-by-Play Legend, Dies at 64

While Houston’s discovery would be Griffith’s career-defining achievement, he also worked with a collection of superstars at Arista including Aretha Franklin, Dionne Warwick and Carlos Santana, helping guide the guitarist during his late-career resurgence that included Supernatural and the 1999 album’s major hit “Smooth.”

Houston’s estate posted a brief message this week across Houston’s social media pages to honor the executive. “We mourn the passing of Gerry Griffith, the Arista Records music executive who first heard Whitney and brought her to the attention of Clive Davis,” Houston’s estate wrote. “May he rest in peace.”

Griffith also worked on the soundtracks for films including Pretty Woman and The Bodyguard, the latter of which launched “I Will Always Love You.” After Arista, he lead A&R at Manhattan Records, where, most notably, he worked with Richard Marx.

“Gerry never got the accolades he so deserved for discovering Whitney Houston, but we knew the Real Deal,” Brenda Andrews, a former senior vp of Rondor Music International, said in a statement. “He gave me two hits out of the box with her, “I Wanna Dance With Somebody” and “How Will I Know.” He knew HIT SONGS! All of us will miss him. May he rest peacefully with all of our brothers and sisters that have made their place in Heaven.”

As music manager and publisher Deirdre O’Hara wrote: “Early in my music publishing career, I was very fortunate to connect with Gerry Griffith. One of my proudest moments was urging Gerry to see Whitney Houston … and the rest is history.”

Griffith is survived by his two daughters and two sons.
