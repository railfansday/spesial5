Molly Meldrum, Australian Music Broadcaster and ‘Countdown’ Host, Dies at 83

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

"Affectionately dubbed 'The Guru,' Molly was the heartbeat, the champion and the spirit of Australian music," his family said in a statement.

Ian “Molly” Meldrum, the Australian broadcaster, journalist and record producer whose weekly television show made stars of international and local acts alike, died Monday (Nov. 23) at Epworth Hospital in Richmond, Melbourne. His family said he died peacefully after a long period of ill health. “Affectionately dubbed ‘The Guru,’ Molly was the heartbeat, the champion and the spirit of Australian music,” they said in a statement. “He shaped not only the sound of a nation but also the dreams of generations who grew up with his voice guiding them.” He is survived by his long-term partner, Yan Wongngam. From 1974 to 1987, Meldrum presented Countdown, the ABC’s Sunday-evening music program, which he co-created with executive producer Michael Shrimpton and producer-director Robbie Weekes. Across 563 episodes, it became appointment viewing for young Australians, and — years before MTV — a showcase that leaned heavily on music video.

The Australian Recording Industry Association said Monday that Meldrum had spent six decades convincing a country raised on British and American records that its own artists could stand alongside the greats.

That reach extended well beyond Australia. ABBA broke there on the strength of clips Meldrum played after their 1974 Eurovision win, scoring six No. 1 singles within two years, with “Fernando” spending 14 weeks atop the chart. “Molly started playing our videos down in Australia and it seemed as though the rest of the world just woke up,” Björn Ulvaeus later said. Madonna landed her first hit single anywhere in April 1984, when “Holiday” aired on the show. Blondie, Cyndi Lauper, Meat Loaf, John Mellencamp and Boz Scaggs all found early Australian audiences the same way.

At home, his endorsement moved records. When Meldrum told viewers to “do yourself a favour” on a Sunday night, ARIA said record stores braced for a hectic Monday. Countdown turned Skyhooks and Sherbet into national names and gave early platforms to AC/DC, INXS, Olivia Newton-John, Divinyls, Icehouse and a teenage Kylie Minogue. Elton John, a longtime friend, has called Meldrum the best thing that ever happened to Australian music.

Meldrum came to television from print and from the studio. He was writing for the pop weekly Go-Set by 1966 and produced his first No. 1 record in 1969, Russell Morris‘ “The Real Thing.” He went on to cut top 10 hits for Ronnie Burns, Colleen Hewett, Supernaut and the Ferrets, and founded Melodian Records in 1988.

His on-air persona — a cowboy hat, a stammering delivery, an unmistakable enthusiasm — became national shorthand. One of its defining moments came in 1977, when a flustered Meldrum told the then-Prince of Wales he had seen “your mum” in an open carriage in London.

After Countdown ended, Meldrum presented a music segment on the variety show Hey Hey It’s Saturday until 1999. He was made a Member of the Order of Australia in 1986, received ARIA’s Special Achievement Award in 1993, entered the Logie Awards Hall of Fame in 2012 and was inducted into the ARIA Hall of Fame alongside Countdown in 2014 — the first inductees who were not recording artists.

He survived a near-fatal fall from a ladder at his Melbourne home in 2011 that left him in intensive care, and his health declined in later years.

ARIA chief executive Annabelle Herd led the industry tributes. “All of us at ARIA are heartbroken,” she said. “It is impossible to imagine what Australian music would look like without Molly’s long and storied career. He shaped the music taste of a generation of Australians and gave so much of himself for the good of Australian artists, for which our whole industry is eternally grateful.”

Herd noted that Meldrum had used his Hall of Fame induction speech to urge viewers to get behind Australian acts. “It is up to all of us to keep fighting the good fight in his honour,” she said. “Our thoughts are with Molly’s family and loved ones, and with every artist who got their start because he believed in them.”

Singer Kate Ceberano was among the first artists to post. “They don’t make em like that anymore,” she wrote. “My heart is aching today at the loss.”
