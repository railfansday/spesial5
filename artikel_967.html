Erasmus runs risk of breaching World Rugby ban with watchalong

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

South Africa head coach Rassie Erasmus held an online watchalong during the Ireland vs Springboks match in the Nations Championship while serving a suspension from World Rugby; part of Erasmus' ban covers "direct or indirect contact with team management on matchdays"

South Africa head coach Rassie Erasmus went ahead with an online watchalong during Ireland's victory over his team in the Nations Championship on Saturday.

Erasmus is serving a six-match suspension handed down by World Rugby following extraordinary claims, questioning whether "match-fixing" and "manipulation" was occurring in rugby after his side's defeat to France in Paris last week.

The 54-year-old was suspended for "public criticism of match officials, undermining the integrity of individuals or the sport, and fuelling online abuse".

Erasmus organised a free watchalong for anyone to sign up and view him react to the Test match as he watched it unfold from his Dublin hotel.

After a brief welcome, Erasmus kept himself on mute during both halves of the match, where he could be seen clapping at times, gazing intently at a TV, apparently shouting into what presumably was a phone and thumping his table in frustration when Ireland won 20-17.

But he did come off mute at half-time to outline what he thought would be under discussion in the break. He said: "We've not been playing perfect.

"If we step it up I think we'll be in this match. I think they've been really clinical but I'd have been much more worried if we'd played perfect and we're not currently playing perfect and we shouldn't just play to get one bonus point.

"I think will be the gist of what's happening at half-time."

"The suspension applies with immediate effect, applies to international matches, and includes any matchday involvement including coaching, direct or indirect contact with team management or players on matchdays; entering the stadium, coaches' box, playing enclosure, technical area, approaching and/or communicating with match officials on matchday; engaging in any 'feedback' process with match officials; and engaging in media duties during the suspension."

Regarding the "direct or indirect contact with team management or players on matchdays" element of his suspension, Sky Sports sought guidance on the potential for one of South Africa's coaching staff or management set-up signing up, listening to and watching the reactions and comments of their suspended head coach during the contest.

While this specific scenario is not covered by the guidelines, Sky Sports understands Erasmus' plan could be considered a breach by rugby's governing body.

There was, though, a short delay to the broadcast feed Erasmus was using.

South Africa Rugby released a statement requesting a full misconduct hearing in an immediate response to the sanction, but it is understood formal confirmation of an intention to appeal is yet to be received at World Rugby.

Erasmus has now been suspended three times in five years for these sorts of incidents.

During the 2021 British and Irish Lions series in South Africa, after the Springboks lost the first Test, Erasmus broadcast an hour-long critique of Australian referee Nic Berry, and was eventually handed a two-month ban from all rugby activity and a 10-month matchday ban.

In 2022 he received a two-Test ban after sarcastic social media posts following back-to-back South Africa defeats to Ireland and France. A video critical of referee Wayne Barnes led to the official revealing he and his family had received death threats.

On Wednesday, SA Rugby released a statement "condemning the online abuse of officials" following confirmation of Erasmus' ban.
