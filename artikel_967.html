V8 engines return confirmed in bid to 'reconnect with DNA' of F1

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Multiple Formula 1 drivers had backed for V8 engines to return to the sport; FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem has confirmed V8 engines will return from 2030 after a 17-year absence; watch the Las Vegas GP on Sky Sports F1, with qualifying at 4am on Saturday and the race at 4am on Sunday

Formula 1 will return to V8 engines from 2030 in a move FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem has described as "an opportunity to reconnect with the DNA" of the sport.

Earlier this week, Ben Sulayem teased the possibility of V8 engines ahead of the Las Vegas Grand Prix, live on Sky Sports F1, with the sound of a screaming F1 car with a caption: "You spoke, we listened. V8s coming soon."

On Friday, the FIA announced a technical framework had been unanimously agreed between the FIA, Formula One Management (FOM), the teams and Formula 1 power unit manufacturers to move to a 2.1-litre turbocharged V8 power unit.

The agreement means the more popular V8s will make a comeback in 2030, 17 years after the sport abandoned them in favour of "road relevant" engines, a decision already backed by several drivers.

The current power unit, which is around 53 per cent electrically powered, has been criticised by some drivers this year as it has led to an unusual style of driving and racing at some tracks, with the drivers sometimes unable to push flat out in a traditional manner.

"This agreement marks a significant milestone in shaping the future of Formula 1," said Ben Sulayem.

"From the outset I have been clear in my ambition to deliver a sport that is more competitive and more exciting for our fans, while remaining at the forefront of innovation and sustainability.

"The return of V8 engines represents an opportunity to reconnect with the DNA of this Championship, bringing back the sound, emotion and pure racing that is loved worldwide. Our vision is clear, lighter, simpler and more affordable cars that put driver skill at the heart of the competition."

Max Verstappen has been one of the most vocal critics of the new 2026 power units and warned the long straights in Las Vegas this weekend will be "painful" to drive on as the cars will effectively slow down before the braking zone.

Verstappen feels the FIA have listened to the drivers and realised the 2026 regulations are "not what you want" and, if he was in charge, would get rid of the turbo aspect completely and effectively have a fully normally aspirated engine.

"In some tracks then you're not allowed to race because it will be too loud because if it's up to me I would take the turbo off," he explained.

"I would get rid, maybe you can use it as a push to pass, but not too much of the battery side. But then on some tracks, you're not allowed to race, so fuel consumption goes up a lot without a turbo, so you need a bigger tank if you don't want to do refuelling in the races, so I understand that you want to have a turbo.

"If it's fully up to me, of course you go back to like a V10 or whatever. That sounds even better, but I know that not something realistic. This [news] is good. This is for sure in the right direction."

Lewis Hamilton, Fernando Alonso, Nico Hulkenberg Sergio Perez and Valtteri Bottas are the only drivers who drove in the previous V8 era which ended in 2013.

Most of the grid have driven V8-powered F1 cars in demonstration of show runs at fan events and Mercedes driver George Russell says it's important for a hybrid element to remain so the cars can have over 1,000 brake horsepower.

"When I look at the old videos of the old engines, they give me goosebumps," said Russell.

"It's about just that high-pitched squeal that I'm missing from this era. The purists will say take away all electrical power, but I think when you look at how a lot of these cars have evolved in recent years, and the power output from some of these electrical motors these days, Formula 1 is going to continue to be the fastest race cars on the planet, you need to have it, that's just the reality.

"You need to have some sort of hybrid, because the power you get, you can't really produce that just solely on internal combustion."
