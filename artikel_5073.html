Zcash's NU7 Upgrade Goes Live, Recycling 60% of Fees to Fund Future Block Rewards

"NU7 has successfully activated on mainnet. A new era of Zcash is here," the project's official account tweeted.

"This is a huge milestone for Shielded Labs," Jason McGee of Shielded Labs, the Zcash development organization behind the mechanism, tweeted ahead of the activation.

The NSM tackles a problem Zcash inherited from Bitcoin: whether, as block subsidies decline with each halving, transaction fees alone will be enough to pay miners to secure the network. Under the mechanism, 60% of transaction fees will be removed from circulation and gradually reissued through future block rewards, while preserving the 21 million ZEC supply cap. Reissuance will begin in 2031, with the recycled funds spread along a smooth curve on top of the existing halving schedule.

The NSM was Shielded Labs' first project and builds on an idea that developer Nate Wilcox proposed years ago, originally called the Zcash Posterity Fund, McGee said. A separate proposal under consideration would require Zcash Community Grants to contribute 50,000 ZEC from its treasury to the NSM.

NU7 also limits how much private transaction activity can fit into each block, reducing the data wallets must scan to find their payments, according to the upgrade specification.

Shielded Labs' original design would have replaced Zcash's halvings with a smooth issuance curve. Community panels supported that change, but coinholders did not, so an alternative design that kept the halvings was developed instead.

"We still think the original design was simpler and better, but we respect the process that led us here," McGee wrote. "It should be difficult to change the protocol, and the process should resist capture by any single organization, group, or influential individual."

The upgrade arrives as Zcash's community debates the future of Zcash Community Grants (ZCG) and its share of the Dev Fund, the slice of block rewards that pays for development.

The five organizations that coordinate Zcash's coinholder voting, Project Tachyon, Shielded Labs, Valar Group, the Zcash Foundation and ZODL, have set out an expedited timeline for proposals and polling, saying "tensions were high" and that a drawn-out debate risked "deepening divisions within the Zcash community."

Proposals are due by November 8, with polls for coinholders and community panels running from November 16 to November 30. If rough consensus emerges, the groups will support a further network upgrade, NU 7.1, to implement it.
