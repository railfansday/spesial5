To Grow Up, First Grow Down

Our culture likes easy measurements of maturity, such as chronological age, number of achievements, financial stability, and the ability to adapt to conventional norms for having a job and a place to live. However, a person can meet these criteria for growing up and still have little or no psychological maturity. The word maturity comes from the Latin word matures, meaning “ripen.” We can understand ripen as meaning “moving beyond early stages of development.” But before we can ripen, we may need to grow down.

When we grow down, we confront early attitudes and beliefs that strengthen neither our relationship with ourselves nor our relationship with life. Here are a number of these attitudes and beliefs.

Growing down is a process of letting go or dying, where denial and certain attachments are challenged and gradually let go. It often takes multiple acts of letting go before an attachment is released. Let’s look at how the growing down process can translate into ripening or growing up.

Maturity can so often be such a vague concept that we don’t know what it is or whether we can actually influence it. Everyone seems to want to “grow up” into a flourishing adulthood, yet it can help to take “growing down” seriously because it shows us what we need to let go of and get ready for. It's not much different from clearing land of rocks and debris in order to prepare for planting and growing. The goal is to take some of the mystique out of becoming an adult. We owe it to ourselves and to others to attend first to some of our more primitive attitudes before we find ourselves merely pretending we are adults.
