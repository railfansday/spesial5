Why Kissing Matters More Than You Think During Sex

In today’s sexual landscape, we are getting a lot of information about how to supercharge our sex lives. There are Instagram ads for vibrators and other accessories, shelves of toys at our local drugstore, and no end of supplements marketed to anyone whose libido might be waning. But the data tells us that actually our strongest—but most undervalued—predictor of satisfying sex is just good old-fashioned kissing. And this is as true for people hooking up with new partners as it is for those in long-term relationships.

So why has kissing fallen out of fashion?

Researcher Lisa Wade (2017) found that in hook-up culture, people tend to follow the “no kissing during sex” rule to preserve boundaries, ostensibly keeping sex casual and emotionally safe. But that strategy for avoiding feeling too close to a casual partner can have an unintended consequence. Eschewing kissing can, as my own students report, leave them feeling empty afterward. For long-term partners, kissing can be bypassed out of time constraints and a misguided attempt at sexual efficiency. When you are trying to squeeze sex in around busy jobs or parenting, it can be tempting to “jump to the main event.” Meanwhile, both groups can be influenced by porn that emphasizes novel activities like choking over “vanilla” activities like kissing. But the research shows that forgoing kissing to keep sex casual or efficient skips the activity that most reliably predicts it being good, and it’s women who pay the price.

When researchers Busby et al. (2021) set out to quantify the impact of kissing on relationship and sexual satisfaction in long-term relationships, they found that both specific kissing (during the most recent sexual experience) and global kissing (during the last year) were positively associated with their subjects’ perspective on the quality of their sex lives and their overall relationship satisfaction. For women, kissing during sex was positively associated with their ability to orgasm, sexual frequency, and indirectly with sexual satisfaction. Because they were more likely to orgasm, this positive reinforcement made them want to have sex more often, creating a virtuous feedback loop. These findings suggest that kissing shouldn’t be thought of as superfluous, but rather reclassified as foundational to relationship maintenance and a couple’s sexual repertoire.

While both sexes associate more kissing with a stronger relationship, the orgasm benefits definitely skew toward women. Kissing signals emotional closeness, slows the pace of the encounter, and extends foreplay, all of which raise arousal and make orgasm more likely for women.

This is why Wade’s research into hooking up with an understanding that kissing is off the table sets women at a disadvantage. When researcher Frederick and his team (2017) asked a cross-sample of people if their last sexual encounter led to orgasm, unsurprisingly, 65 percent of heterosexual women answered affirmatively compared to 95 percent of heterosexual men. This is called the orgasm gap. Kissing can help close that gap even for new partners.

In addition to incorporating specific kissing back into sex, pay attention to global kissing. The above studies are correlational, but this study isn’t: Kory Floyd and colleagues (2009) assigned married and cohabiting couples to kiss more for six weeks. Compared with controls, the kissing group had lower stress, lower cholesterol, and higher relationship satisfaction. In a classic “fake it till you make it” framework, starting with increasing kissing can lead to a stronger relationship, rather than waiting for events to unfold the other way around.

New research from Christopher Watkins and Milena Rota (2026) found that fantasy and sexual imagination inform the perception of a good kiss rather than technique. And even more important than technique is duration. The Gottmans claim that as long as a kiss lasts at least six seconds, the brain releases oxytocin, which is the bonding hormone that enables all of the above (Brittle, 2014).

So, in our overscheduled, overstimulating world, think about coming back to basics. If your sex life has gone flat, kissing is the easiest place to start because it’s free, you can’t run out, its batteries won’t go dead, and best of all, it brings both people into the moment. That's what great sex is all about.
