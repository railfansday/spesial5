Inside the Bureau of Customs compound at Manila Harbor, a substantial collection of confiscated exotic vehicles is awaiting public auction. Authorities seized the fleet from operators of illegal offshore gambling networks who attempted to import the vehicles into the Philippines without paying the required duties. The most prominent models currently held in the government yard include a 2021 McLaren Senna and a rare Ferrari LaFerrari hybrid hypercar.

While we frequently see domestic police seize cars from local street racing syndicates, the financial scale of the Manila impound lot is distinctly different. The Philippine government imposes a 100 percent luxury tax on European automotive imports, which prompted the illicit operators to bypass customs entirely. With the gambling networks now shut down by authorities, the abandoned high-performance vehicles are slated for auction to recover the lost tax revenue.

According to a video from the amadeusiom YouTube channel, the auction inventory features several high-value European models. Beyond the McLaren Senna, which carries an estimated auction floor price of $1.01 million, the impound lot holds a 2022 Ferrari SF90 Stradale, a Rolls-Royce Cullinan, a Bentley Continental GT, and a Rolls-Royce Phantom. Because the vehicles have been parked outdoors in a humid, tropical climate, several models will require significant cosmetic rehabilitation before returning to the road.

Stateside, exotic vehicles occasionally cross the auction block after federal agents seize assets from high-profile financial fugitives. However, the Philippine customs bureau faces a unique challenge in officially valuing its rarest capture: the LaFerrari. Because the limited-production hybrid is exceptionally scarce on the global market, officials are struggling to establish a realistic reserve price that accurately incorporates the mandatory 100 percent import tax, leaving the Ferrari in a temporary regulatory holding pattern.

While domestic buyers frequently monitor government surplus websites hoping to score seized cars cheap at public sales, the upcoming Manila auction operates under strict financial parameters. The government’s mandatory reserve prices must account for local market values and the heavy import taxation rate. Consequently, the asking prices remain high by international standards, with a 2022 Rolls-Royce Phantom carrying a floor price of over $380,000 despite its current storage conditions.

Despite the high financial barriers to entry, the Bureau of Customs is proceeding with the sealed-bid public auction to convert these assets into usable government funds. However, the state has historically taken a much harsher stance on smuggling. In previous years, the Philippine government made global headlines by publicly crushing seized exotics with bulldozers as a strict warning to tax evaders. While this specific hypercar fleet was ultimately spared the crusher to benefit local taxpayers, the logistics of selling million-dollar vehicles directly from a port highlight the complex realities of international customs enforcement.
