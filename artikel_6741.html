Taylor! Lainey! Ella! Kacey! Megan! Backstage at the CMA Awards

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

“Little Lainey would be losin’ her mind if she knew she was about to be hosting the 60th Annual CMA Awards,” Lainey Wilson said ahead of country music’s biggest night at Nashville’s Bridgestone Arena. Indeed, when Vogue caught up with the chart-topper in a quiet moment leading up to the show, she was incredulous about landing the gig.

“I’m still that little girl from a town of 200 people in Louisiana who just grew up dreamin’ about being a small part of this world of country music,” Wilson said in her signature twang. The feeling of going from the middle of nowhere to the center of the ceremony, and in such a milestone year, is nothing short of surreal, she added. Not only was it the show’s six-decade anniversary and a mega year for the genre, but the night was also set to include a tribute to the late Dolly Parton—and the first CMA performance in 13 years from a certain recently wed crossover superstar.

“I’m just excited to celebrate the ones before, the ones now, and the ones comin’,” Wilson said. “I think the biggest thing for me is being present and being right where my feet are. It’s tryin’ to find that balance.”

On the day of the show, Downtown Nashville was a flurry of activity, fans, hot chicken, and road closures as the capital of country music prepared to step into the national spotlight. The recent FAA ground stop gave some out-of-towners agita, but the delay clouds thankfully parted in time. Music was ringing out from every bar and restaurant on Broadway, most of the time in the form of Langley’s ubiquitous “Choosin’ Texas.” The country queen of 2026 was also nominated for almost every award imaginable. It’s to the point where if Langley miraculously cloned herself, they’d be nominated for Best Duo, too.

“This isn’t a fad: everyone you know can sing along to every word of ‘Choosin’ Texas,’” Brittany Schaffer, the incoming Country Music Association CEO, told Vogue. Just hours earlier, Schaffer bolted out of her hotel at 6 a.m. to help give out the first award of the day live on Good Morning America. (Naturally, “Choosin’ Texas” won for Best Music Video.) Schaffer was also busy contending with the Taylor of it all, as her CMA comeback was a closely held secret.

“A small group of us knew for quite a bit of time in advance of the announcement,” Schaffer revealed with a wink. “Obviously, with this being an anniversary, she is such an important part of country music history, so we certainly started that conversation with her early on. We are just thrilled that she was so excited to join.”

While most major red carpets are hectic affairs, the vibe at Music City Center was decidedly chiller—even when an Oscar de la Renta-clad Swift arrived. A hush fell over the space as phones lifted to capture the moment, even that of the lighting technician. Elsewhere, VIPs tucked into fried green tomato sandwiches and sipped whiskey, seemingly unfazed.

“I had to bring my sister, Leslie,” actor Glen Powell, who’s set to play a country music star in next year’s The Comeback King, told Vogue. “ To share this together…I mean, we’ve been country fans our entire lives. We did these long road trips to the ranch, listening to country music the whole way.” A “starstruck” Leslie chimed in: “Two of our childhood idols, Faith Hill and Tim McGraw, are sitting next to us.”

As the Tennessee sun began to set, a series of Sprinter vans whisked people from the carpet to the arena for a 7 p.m. start time. Backstage, a trophy engraver was waiting to etch the names of future winners into history. Inside the arena, the crowd milled about as a booming voice on the loudspeaker urged, “In respect to Dolly, please take your seats.” It proved to be a subtle teaser, as a tribute featuring Wilson and Vince Gill singing “I Will Always Love You” was about to open the show. Later, Kacey Musgraves and Miranda Lambert lightened the mood with their honky-tonk ode “Horses and Divorces,” from Musgraves’s recent album Middle of Nowhere.

From her dressing room, Musgraves, resplendent in a custom suit by the Los Angeles-based western-wear designer RoseCut, took a breather on the couch. “I think people would be shocked to know how boring my pre-show ritual actually is,” she told Vogue. “It’s pretty chill. I don’t shut myself in a hotel room all day. I try to move and connect with the heartbeat of wherever I’m at.” Alas, a vocal doctor did warn once that she should have “no chocolate, no caffeine, or no booze before a performance. “I’m like, ‘Have you ever been on stage without booze,’” she recalled with a laugh. “So pre-show, the band and I usually do a tequila shot.”

According to Musgraves, a night like tonight is all about reconnecting. “So many of us here spend so much of our time out on the road. It’s really a reunion of sorts, getting to see a lot of people and friends that you haven’t seen in so long.”

One of those friends is Megan Moroney, whom Musgraves was happily sharing a dressing room with. Alongside excitement over her two nominations at the CMAs, Moroney is buzzing about her two recently announced Grammy nods. “It’s been such a crazy year,” Moroney told Vogue as the ceremony blared on a TV in the background. “On the morning of the nominations, my manager came into my bedroom at like 6:30 a.m. and said, ‘We need to turn on the Grammy announcement because I just have a good feeling.’ And I was like, ‘Are you insane?!’”

The show itself played out as expected. Ella Langley cleaned up, making history by winning everything from Entertainer of the Year to Female Vocalist of the Year and Album of the Year, breaking a CMA record for most wins. Even the country stalwarts were impressed. “A highlight for me was hugging Miranda Lambert,” Lady A’s Hillary Scott told Vogue of Langley’s collaborator. “What’s funny is we toured with her and Kenny Chesney twenty years ago, early on in our careers.” Her bandmate, Dave Haywood, added: “Longevity was the goal when we started. So to be here after all of these years is a dream realized for us.”

As the show wrapped up and fans departed the arena, stars grabbed snacks to munch on as they got ready to head back out on the road—including a lollipop-sucking Michael Bublé. “What I love about country music is this,” he mused to Vogue, “when you take away all of the production, all of the lights, and all of the magic tricks, you are left with good souls.”
