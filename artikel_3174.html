Nasdaq-100 Correction: 2 Proven Winners That Can Buck Historic Volatility

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Two non-tech stocks are the perfect buys amid a 15% correction in the Nasdaq-100.

Although the artificial intelligence (AI)-driven bull market over the last four years has sent Wall Street's major stock indexes to several record highs, the last few weeks have served as a reminder that stocks can, indeed, go down. Last week, on Nov. 5, the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC +2.74%) logged the largest single-session point decline in its storied history (1,422 points), while the Nasdaq-100 reached a nearly 15% correction in a month.

While a retracement has arguably been long overdue for high-flying tech stocks, several bargains abound within the Nasdaq-100 for patient investors. Two proven winners from outside the tech sector, Intuitive Surgical (ISRG +1.71%) and O'Reilly Automotive (ORLY -0.11%), can buck this historic volatility.

Though you can certainly find cheaper healthcare stocks, based on the price-to-earnings ratio, you'd struggle mightily to find a healthcare company with a sustained double-digit growth rate or with as large a moat as robotic-assisted surgical systems developer Intuitive Surgical.

Intuitive holds a veritable monopoly in robotic-assisted surgical systems, with more than 12,000 of its da Vinci systems installed worldwide. The relatively high cost of these systems, coupled with a lack of competition, often locks hospitals and surgical centers in as long-term customers.

What's arguably even more important than its virtual monopoly status is its revenue mix. In the early 2000s, Intuitive generated the bulk of its revenue from selling its high-priced, but mediocre-margin systems. As time has passed, higher-margin sales channels, including accessories sold with each procedure and system servicing, have grown into a majority of net sales. In short, this is a recipe for Intuitive's profits to grow even faster than its sales.

Innovation matters, too. In addition to developing new generations of the da Vinci surgical system, Intuitive debuted its minimally invasive lung biopsy system (Ion endoluminal system) in 2019. Ion procedures jumped by 31% in the latest quarter.

Intuitive Surgical stock may not be cheap, but there are several reasons it can maintain a healthy premium.

Another proven winner within the Nasdaq-100 that can buck this recent volatility is auto parts retailer O'Reilly Automotive.

Macro trends certainly favor O'Reilly. According to a May 2025 report from S&P Global Mobility (now Mobility Global (MBGL +1.12%)), the average age of vehicles in the U.S. rose to a record 12.8 years, up from an average of 11.1 years in 2012. Though newer vehicles are built to last longer, the aging of America's vehicles suggests drivers and mechanics will be relying on companies like O'Reilly Automotive to keep them in tip-top shape.

O'Reilly's success is also a function of its hub-and-spoke distribution model. The company's 32 distribution centers provide overnight access to more than 156,000 stock-keeping units (SKUs). Nearly 400 larger hub stores can also provide up to 115,000 SKUs on a same-day basis.

But the one factor that's really set O'Reilly Automotive apart, at least from an investment standpoint, is its top-notch share repurchase program. Since the inception of its buyback program in January 2011, O'Reilly has spent just over $31 billion to retire 1.5 billion outstanding shares (OS). The company has reduced its OS by 62% in less than 16 years, meaningfully boosting its earnings per share in the process.

Tech-driven volatility doesn't change the investment thesis for O'Reilly Automotive one bit.
