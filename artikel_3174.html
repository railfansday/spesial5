Blackwell rack found at Alice Huang-linked Shanghai site, Bloomberg reports

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Loan filings place a GB300 rack at a Shanghai data centre tied to Megaspeed’s founder. Shipping Nvidia Blackwell chips to China has always needed a US licence.

Two Chinese companies installed Nvidia’s restricted Blackwell chips at a Shanghai data centre linked to Alice Huang, Bloomberg reported on Friday. Reporters Andy Lin, Mackenzie Hawkins and Kari Soo Lindberg found the evidence in loan documents that companies submitted to Chinese regulators.

The filings show for the first time that Blackwell chips reached the site, the Yangtze River Delta Project, Bloomberg said. Shipping Blackwell to China has always required a US licence.

Shanghai Shuoyao pledged a GB300 NVL72 rack, one of Nvidia’s most powerful products, to secure a 96 million yuan ($14 million) loan from Shanghai Innovation Bank. The rack’s serial number appeared in documents submitted in November.

Bloomberg ran that number through a public warranty database. It showed that Taiwan’s Gigabyte built the rack. Gigabyte assembles Nvidia chips into servers and racks, and it declined to say who it first sold the hardware to, citing company policy.

The company “conducts all business in compliance with applicable laws and regulations,” a Gigabyte spokesperson told Bloomberg in an emailed statement.

A second company, Shanghai Kezhan, described a GB200 server, which also uses Blackwell chips, at the same site. It did so when it took a 90 million yuan loan in late 2025 from a leasing firm that a Shanghai government agency owns. Kezhan also listed 32 servers with H200 chips, an earlier restricted Nvidia model.

How the GB300 rack reached China, or the site, is unclear. Such gear often passes through several hands, and sellers often first ship smuggled products to places without restrictions, Bloomberg said.

Alice Huang founded Megaspeed in 2023. The Singapore company became Nvidia’s largest customer in Southeast Asia and is under US investigation over suspected chip smuggling. The US blacklisted Megaspeed this week, and then Alibaba’s Singapore unit.

Shuoyao is active at the Shanghai site and overlaps significantly with Megaspeed, according to Bloomberg. This month it confirmed it is one of Megaspeed’s service providers.

Shuoyao described the partnership as “limited to ordinary commercial cooperation”.

Megaspeed used a rendering of the Yangtze River Delta Project in 2024 to show investors its largest data centre, according to Bloomberg. Huang also has ties to several companies that invested in the project. NeXPower Japan, which the Chinese outlet Caixin said Huang controls, owns 83.3% of Kezhan.

Bloomberg said it has no evidence that Megaspeed itself sent Nvidia hardware to the site. Megaspeed has denied wrongdoing in the US probe, and neither Megaspeed nor Nvidia answered Bloomberg’s questions about the project.

Local media have said the site could house up to 800,000 servers. That would put it on a par with the facilities OpenAI uses. The filings cover only hardware pledged for loans, so they show a small part of what is there, Bloomberg said. They also show that some Chinese lenders, including ones with government ties, lent money against restricted chips.

The report lands days after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, amid scrutiny of how its chips reach China. President Donald Trump has said he does not want Blackwell chips going to China. Huang once said a GB300 rack, which weighs more than 1,500 kilograms, was too big to smuggle.
