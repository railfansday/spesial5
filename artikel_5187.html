How to Swap Bitcoin to Ethereum Without a Bridge

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Home » Cryptopedia » How to Swap Bitcoin to Ethereum Without a Bridge

The process, requirements, fees, and risks involved in swapping Bitcoin for Ethereum without using a bridge.

You can swap Bitcoin to Ethereum without a bridge by using a native cross-chain swap protocol such as THORChain. The process takes six steps: open a compatible swap interface, connect your Bitcoin wallet, enter the amount and your Ethereum receiving address, review the quote, sign the transaction, and wait for native ETH to arrive in your own wallet. No wrapped token is created, no account is needed and no custodian holds your funds. You send real BTC on the Bitcoin network and receive real ETH on the Ethereum network.

A bridge moves value between chains by locking your BTC and issuing a wrapped version on Ethereum. That wrapped token is a claim on BTC held by a custodian or bridge contract, so its value depends on that backing staying secure for as long as you hold it.

A native swap skips the wrapping. The protocol holds liquidity on each connected chain and pays out the real asset you ask for. Once the swap settles, you hold genuine ETH in your own wallet with no outstanding claim on anyone.

You do not need ETH in advance. The Ethereum network fee for delivering your ETH is deducted from the swap output.

The protocol is accessed through an interface. The official web interface is THORChain Swap, and the protocol is also integrated into a range of wallets and swap apps, including Vultisig and Keystore, with hardware wallets such as Ledger supported through compatible interfaces. Every interface routes to the same underlying liquidity pools.

Type the interface address directly or use a saved bookmark rather than following links from ads or messages, as copycat swap sites are a common phishing method.

Connect the wallet that holds your BTC. Connecting lets the interface read your balance and prepare the transaction, but it does not give the interface control of your funds. You approve every transaction yourself, and your private keys never leave your wallet.

The official interface also offers a walletless option, where you send BTC to a deposit address generated for your swap instead of connecting a wallet.

Select BTC as the asset to sell and ETH on Ethereum as the asset to buy, then enter the amount. Paste the Ethereum address where you want to receive the ETH. This must be an address you control on the Ethereum network.

The interface shows the expected ETH output and the costs involved. These typically include the Bitcoin network fee for sending your BTC, a liquidity fee that rises with trade size relative to pool depth, and an outbound fee that covers the Ethereum transaction delivering your ETH.

Behind the scenes, a BTC to ETH swap passes through two pools, BTC to RUNE and then RUNE to ETH, with RUNE acting as the settlement asset. The quote already accounts for both legs. Swaps are ordered by price impact within each block, which is designed to resist sandwich attacks and other front-running.

For large amounts, check whether the interface offers streaming swaps, which split a trade into smaller pieces across multiple blocks to reduce price impact.

When you confirm, your wallet asks you to sign a standard Bitcoin transaction. It sends your BTC to the network’s inbound vault with a short memo attached, which tells the network what to swap into and where to send it. The interface writes the memo for you.

These vaults are controlled jointly by the network’s validators, called THORNodes, using threshold signatures, so no single operator holds the keys. Each THORNode bonds RUNE as collateral and can be slashed for malicious behavior.

The network’s validators observe your BTC deposit once it has enough Bitcoin confirmations, execute the swap and send native ETH from the network’s Ethereum vault to your address. Total time depends mainly on how quickly the Bitcoin network confirms your transaction. Most interfaces include a tracker that shows each stage of the swap.

The swap is complete when native ETH appears at your Ethereum address. You can confirm it in your wallet or by looking up your address on an Ethereum block explorer. Compare the amount received against the quoted output; small differences come from price movement between quoting and execution.

If a swap cannot be completed, for example because the price moved beyond your slippage limit, the protocol refunds the BTC to your sending address, minus network fees.

Native swaps remove the depeg risk and ongoing custody risk that come with holding a wrapped token. They do not remove every risk. While a swap is in progress, funds pass through the protocol’s vaults, so you rely on the protocol’s code, its validator set and its economic security model during that window. As with all DeFi, there is smart contract and protocol risk. Start with a small test swap if you are new to the process.
