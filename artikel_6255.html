Every Airline Flying From The US To Australia & The Routes Served In 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

The Global Guy & Kevin Hackert | Shutterstock, Simple Flying

Nonstop travel between the US and Australia remains one of the most important long-haul markets across the Pacific, connecting major US gateways with Australia's largest cities and several destinations beyond its traditional east coast hubs. In 2026, airlines are operating an extensive selection of services, with the US gateways of Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), San Francisco International Airport (SFO), and Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport (DFW) providing the majority of connections.

The market features a combination of established nonstop routes, competitive services, and newer connections designed to give travelers greater flexibility. There are four main airlines providing nonstop links between the two countries, with a total of 8,820 flights scheduled between them in 2026, according to the latest data from ch-aviation. This equates to a total of 2,610,162 seats, and more than 18 billion available seat miles across the various airline and route combinations. These totals illustrate the scale of the market, while individual routes reveal how airlines distribute capacity according to demand, network strategy, and the importance of their respective US gateways.

American Airlines serves Australia through a focused network linking Los Angeles International Airport and Dallas/Fort Worth International with Sydney Airport (SYD) and Brisbane Airport (BNE). The airline's scheduled services for 2026 include 365 flights between Los Angeles and Sydney, and 135 between Dallas/Fort Worth and Brisbane. The oneworld carrier also schedules 13 flights between Los Angeles and Brisbane, giving it a presence across several important transpacific city pairs rather than relying on a single gateway. Sydney Kingsford Smith Airport (SYD) is the most prominent Australian destination in American Airlines' published network, with the Los Angeles service offering a major link between Southern California and Australia's largest international aviation market.

The Dallas/Fort Worth to Brisbane route provides another important option, particularly for travelers originating in the central and eastern US who can connect through American Airlines' major Texas hub. These services complement the airline's broader domestic network, allowing passengers from numerous US cities to travel to Australia without necessarily beginning their journeys on the West Coast. The airline's schedule also demonstrates the different scales of its individual routes, with Los Angeles to Sydney recording 110,960 seats and Dallas/Fort Worth to Brisbane offering 35,031. Delta Air Lines operates an extensive selection of services connecting Los Angeles with Sydney, Brisbane, and Melbourne. The schedule shows 418 flights between Los Angeles and Sydney and 182 between Los Angeles and Melbourne. The SkyTeam carrier's 65 flights between Los Angeles and Brisbane add another option for passengers traveling between the US and Australia's east coast. Los Angeles to Sydney is Delta Air Lines' largest Australian route, accounting for 114,981 seats and 860,862,747 available seat miles. The Los Angeles to Melbourne service adds 50,050 seats, creating a substantial second connection to Australia. These routes give Delta Air Lines a significant presence in the transpacific market, while its Los Angeles connections allow passengers from other parts of the US to access its long-haul services.

Delta Air Lines' network emphasizes established destinations with strong demand for business and leisure travel. Sydney provides access to Australia's largest metropolitan area, while Melbourne attracts visitors interested in its cultural attractions, sporting events, and business districts. Although the airline's Brisbane operation is smaller than its Sydney and Melbourne services, it broadens the available choices for travelers heading to Queensland. Together, these routes demonstrate how Delta Air Lines combines its US network with demand across Australia's principal international gateways.

The carrier's operation include 353 flights between Los Angeles and Sydney, 357 between Los Angeles and Melbourne, and 336 between Los Angeles and Brisbane. Qantas also operates nonstop services between San Francisco and Sydney, demonstrating that its US network extends beyond its principal Los Angeles gateway. The carrier's Los Angeles to Melbourne route offers 105,666 seats, while Los Angeles to Brisbane offers 78,740 seats. Qantas' Los Angeles to Sydney service provides 170,458 seats, making it one of the most substantial individual routes between the two countries. The airline's San Francisco to Sydney operation adds another important connection for passengers traveling from Northern California, reducing the need to travel through Los Angeles before beginning a transpacific journey. Los Angeles to Sydney stands out as one of the defining routes in the US to Australia market, with American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, Qantas, and United Airlines all represented. Their individual schedules show 365, 418, 353, and 362 flights, respectively, giving a combined total of 1,498 flights across these four carriers in 2026. Delta Air Lines records the highest individual flight count, although all four airlines contribute substantial capacity. The route's importance reflects the strong connections between Southern California and Australia's largest city, supported by tourism, business travel, education, and visiting friends and relatives.

Los Angeles is also a major connecting hub for passengers arriving from elsewhere in the US, while Sydney offers onward connections to destinations throughout Australia and the wider region. The combination makes the route attractive to both travelers beginning their journeys in either city and those connecting through the respective airline networks. Competition between four major carriers gives passengers a broader choice of schedules, loyalty programs, onboard products, and connecting itineraries.

A total of 110,960 seats are due to be operated by American Airlines throughout the year, with 114,981 by Delta Air Lines, 170,458 by Qantas, and 93,034 by United Airlines on their respective Los Angeles to Sydney services. These figures should be read alongside the flight counts, since aircraft capacity and scheduling patterns can influence the number of seats available. The four airlines operate a mix of Boeing 787-9, Airbus A350-900, and Airbus A380 aircraft on this route, and collectively, the services underline the route's central position in transpacific aviation.

United Airlines' San Francisco to Adelaide connection is a noteworthy addition to the range of routes linking the US with Australia, as Adelaide has traditionally received less direct transpacific service than Sydney or Melbourne. The carrier will operate a total of 44 flights and 11,308 seats on this route throughout 2026. Although considerably smaller than the established Los Angeles - Sydney operation, the service offers a direct link to South Australia rather than requiring passengers to arrive at another Australian city first. Adelaide provides access to a different side of Australia, with attractions including its central market, nearby wine regions, coastal scenery, and cultural festivals. The city is also an important gateway for visitors exploring South Australia, including the Barossa Valley and the Fleurieu Peninsula. For US travelers, a nonstop connection can also reduce the need for an additional domestic flight within Australia, although recent reports suggest the services are far from full, leading to a reduction in flights for the rest of 2026.

This route broadens United Airlines' Australian network by adding another destination beyond the country's largest eastern cities. Its relatively limited capacity suggests a smaller operation than the airline's established Sydney and Melbourne services, but the connection provides another option for travelers with business, family, or leisure interests in South Australia. The route's longer-term significance will depend on passenger demand, operating frequency, and how effectively United Airlines connects Adelaide with its wider US network.
