‘Not Served In Exit Rows’: United Airlines Passenger Refused A Drink, Gets 5,000 Miles

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

An exit-row passenger was flying to Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport (SJU). While on the flight, the passenger asked for an alcoholic drink and was refused service. The flight attendant said that such beverages are not served to exit-row passengers.

After other Reddit users told the passenger that the flight attendant was not following Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) policy, they filed a complaint with the airline. United apologized and added 5,000 miles to the passenger's MileagePlus account.

In the Reddit post titled "No adult drinks in exit row," the passenger explains that they were flying to SJU for the evening before their birthday. To celebrate their vacation, the passenger tried to purchase an alcoholic drink on the United flight and was disappointed to be denied service. The passenger initially thought that the flight attendant was correct, writing, "bad time to learn that alcohol is not served in exit rows."

The passenger then gave three updates to the many others who commented. The first said that they had made a complaint. The second explained that being drunk wasn't why they were refused, as the drink they ordered would have been the first of the day.

"I get that drunk passengers are a menace," they wrote.

Finally, the passenger said they received 5,000 United MileagePlus miles.

Simple Flying contacted United, but it said it will not comment on the matter.

The compensation comes because the flight attendant broke United's alcohol sales policy. Alcoholic drinks are not sold to those under 21 years old or those who are already intoxicated. Furthermore, the FAA has no ban on serving alcohol in exit rows. As a result, there is no reason that the passenger should have been denied service.

There are rules about exit row travel that frequent flyers will know. Passengers in these rows must be at least 15 years old, physically mobile, and able to read and understand safety briefings. They also must be able to respond to oral instructions in English or the crew's operational language. Finally, passengers in the exit row can't have other responsibilities that would conflict with them helping in an emergency. Examples include that they are traveling with small children or service animals that are caring for another passenger.

In addition to being able to help in an emergency, they must also be willing to do so. In the US, this must be confirmed verbally.

Delta and other carriers must start pre-departure service earlier so galley items are stowed prior to aircraft movement.

"With 5k miles, nothing. Just keep earning more," wrote one Reddit user on a different thread, underlining that the miles given to the passenger impacted by the denial of service is an apology and a gesture rather than true compensation.

Having the 5,000 miles contribute toward a larger spend is not the only way the passenger could benefit from the apology. They could use MileagePlus Merchandise Awards to shop for electronics, household items, and jewelry, although their options would be limited to just 5,000 miles. The only item currently available in the 3,000 to 5,000-mile range is a Gaiam Sidekick Waist Pack, costing 4,800 miles. This pack has a retail price of around $20. Other rewards, like hotels, car hire, and cruises, will also be out of reach with so few miles.

However, some ways to use small amounts of miles are no longer available. For example, United formerly had a program called MagsforMiles, where passengers could use their miles to take out magazine subscriptions. This program has now been shut down.
