Why Aberdeen's new gem Marshall will keep sparkling if Frick persists with diamond formation

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 13:00 EST, 25 November 2026 | Updated: 13:13 EST, 25 November 2026

Nicky Devlin has predicted that Findlay Marshall will continue to sparkle if he’s played at the top of Aberdeen’s diamond formation.

On the back of netting his first senior goal for the Dons from the bench against Kilmarnock, new boss Mario Frick deployed the 20-year-old from the start against Rangers in a central attacking role.

In what proved to be a man of the match display, Marshall set up Brad Lyons’ opener then claimed a goal of his own with a sweet left-foot finish.

And with Frick seemingly intent on sticking with the blueprint which caused Killie and Rangers problems, defender Devlin believes his younger team-mate, who enjoyed a successful loan spell at Arbroath last season, will only go from strength to strength.

‘Even at Arbroath, you saw how much of a goal threat he is,’ said Devlin. ‘He makes really good runs into the box and off both feet he’s a really good finisher.

‘He can definitely play other roles, especially in the formation that the manager’s been playing just now. But I think you get the best out of him when you let him attack the box, let him get into the box to score goals.

‘For me, he was the best player on the park. Especially for someone so young, to go there... he’ll probably hate me for saying it, but I could tell he was maybe a wee bit nervous because he told me about three or four times that it’s the first time he’d ever been to Ibrox.

Findlay Marshall in the thick of the action against Rangers in this week's 3-2 loss for Dons

‘So, I knew he was thinking about it. But then he goes and delivers a performance like that.

‘His finishing, especially off his left foot, is unbelievable. He could easily have got us a draw. He was really unlucky with an effort right at the end.

‘He’s grown so much, especially since I came to the club at first. He’s an example for everyone at our club in terms of youth players. He’s gone and done what the club wanted him to do. He’s had two or three different loans where he’s gone away, played well, scored goals, came back, had to wait his opportunity.

‘In the last two games that he’s come in, he’s scored two goals and he’s been brilliant.’

Marshall’s first goal for the Dons at Rugby Park was another crisp left-foot finish which came when he decided not to play in a team-mate.

Devlin feels that the better decision-making he’s now showing is a sign that his game is starting to click into place.

‘He’s a younger boy who just wants to learn all the time,’ he explained. ‘He mentioned after the game on Saturday that he had a chance like that in the cup and he chose the other option. He tried to play in maybe the more senior player just because he thought that was the right thing to do. He took on board that he should have maybe gone himself.

‘It was an unbelievable finish. That’s what he brings to the team. That’s what he can bring to us. And hopefully he can keep doing that.’

Marshall is only 20 but was Aberdeen's star man in the match at Ibrox on Tuesday night

Born in Stonehaven and part of the Dons’ set-up since 2017, Marshall is the new poster boy for the club.

At a time when the opportunities being afforded to young Scots is a hot topic, Devlin feels that Aberdeen have much to feel optimistic about on that score.

‘I think we’ve got plenty here that I think can definitely step up and come in,’ he explained.

‘You see towards the end of the game, we’re chasing a goal with 20 minutes to go and the manager turns to a 15-year-old (Andrew Fasanya). So, there’s players there that can come through.

‘There’s a few boys out on loan now that are doing excellent. The two Alfies at Inverness (Bavidge and Stewart) and Dylan Lobban has done brilliant at Dunfermline.

‘So, there’s definitely players there that can step up for us. Hopefully, they’ll get their opportunity through time. But I’m pretty sure the manager’s trying to assess everyone in the squad just now.’
