Odegaard winner spares officials from bizarre VAR row

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

An ultimately routine Arsenal win but, even by the standards of VAR, an utterly bizarre refereeing controversy. Mikel Arteta’s instant simmering fury at being denied a first-half penalty may also have turned into something rather more explosive had Martin Odegaard not later sent the champions six points clear with a ninth win in 10 Premier League games.

The big talking point arose with inadvertent theatre from referee Farai Hallam when Kai Havertz, who was clearly in an offside position, was equally clearly pulled down by Hull City defender Nobel Mendy inside the penalty area. Which came first? The intervention of VAR suggested the latter. But Hallam duly went to check the pitch-side monitor before then announcing the former.

“After review, there is a holding offence by Blue No 32 on Red No 29,” the referee declared. Cue cheers from the home Arsenal crowd. “However…,” he went on. “Red No 29 is in an offside position, so the final decision will be a free-kick for offside to Hull City.”

REF: "After review, there is a holding offence by Blue No.32 on Red No.29. However, Red No.29 is in an offside position, so the final decision will be a free-kick for offside to Hull City".

It all sounded so decisive that most inside the Emirates instinctively thought the outcome reasonable. But then came a closer look at the rules. And Law 11.2 certainly did seem to support Arteta’s protestations: “In situations where a player in an offside position is moving towards the ball with the intention of playing the ball and is fouled before playing or attempting to play the ball, or challenging an opponent for the ball, the foul is penalised as it has occurred before the offside offence.”

Arteta clearly saw no value after the match in aiming much criticism at Hallam following a fourth 1-0 win in 10 league games, but did still emphasise his surprise at the decision. “What do you think?” he asked one reporter. “I thought offside, but the law says that doesn’t matter,” replied the journalist. “So not offside then,” said Arteta, who stressed that he needs to understand “what is overruling what in the decision-making” after a Premier League statement simply said that Havertz was deemed offside “in the build-up”.

At the very best, it all leaves further confusion and again opens refereeing body Pro Ref to the charge of inconsistency. Fabian Schär, for example, was awarded a penalty for Newcastle against Bournemouth in 2024, despite being in an offside position when his shirt was grabbed by Adam Smith.

Conversely, Dango Ouattara had a penalty overturned for Brentford against Leeds last season after going down under pressure from Gabriel Gudmundsson before being deemed offside. The crucial point seems to be whether the player has actually challenged for the ball at the time of the infringement and, in this case, the answer was surely “no”.

Premier League title triumphs, however, are often forged on forgettable wins in frustrating circumstances and this was ultimately a classic of the genre. With Noni Madueke continuing to step up superbly in the absence of Bukayo Saka, Arsenal’s combination of attacking persistence and defensive resilience was ultimately rewarded by Odegaard’s scrappy 72nd-minute shot. For young players, it was also every bit as educational as the VAR controversy might be for emerging officials.

Madueke’s low shot forced a save by Hull goalkeeper Konstantinos Tzolakis, but Odegaard was somehow first to the rebound, despite being the wrong side of the penalty spot and five yards behind Hull defender John Egan when the ball was struck.

“The way he scores, not the most beautiful, but the most effective,” said Arteta, who said that his team had sometimes tried too hard for a “perfect goal” and should instead be happy with something “more pragmatic”.

It was a well-judged assessment as Arsenal head into the international break with creative deficiencies, but still almost flawless in the cold hard currency of results. Three wins in three days, including at Anfield and in Prague, also underlines a certain champions mentality.

“I need to praise the players,” said Arteta. “It’s been very very demanding; very short time to recover with all the travel. The mentality is incredible. It’s still going to be super difficult. We can improve in a number of areas.”

When it was then put to him that Arsenal have been offside 35 times this season – with Havertz responsible for 15 of those – Arteta acknowledged that “timing” was one element. But, with Havertz targeted by some frustrated fans, he stressed that responsibility was always shared for those split-second moments by the passer and receiver.

He also singled out Madueke, again easily Arsenal’s most creative player, following his surprise omission from Thomas Tuchel’s England squad. “He went away, started to train, he came back and it was a different Noni,” said Arteta, of his reaction to also being out of the Arsenal team earlier in the season. “You could sense that he meant business and he wasn’t going to accept the situation and, since then, I think he’s been incredible.”

Mikel Arteta has said Arsenal are “looking after” Declan Rice more now than in any previous campaign and has rejected suggestions that the England midfielder is being overplayed.

Rice played through nerve pain in his hamstring in the second half of last season, when he made an extraordinary 69 appearances for club and country.

The 27-year-old has played at least 60 matches in each of the past four campaigns, prompting concerns among Arsenal supporters that he is at risk of burnout or injury.

Arteta kept Rice on the field for the full 90 minutes of Arsenal’s midweek victory over Slavia Prague but insisted the club is working harder than ever to manage the midfielder’s workload.

So far this season, Rice has been left out of Arsenal’s two Carabao Cup matches and also withdrew from the previous international break in September.

Asked if he was concerned about Rice’s fitness, Arteta said: “Concerns? Which is the only player, the only player in this squad, that for two games in the Carabao Cup has been left out of the squad? At home with two days off? It’s not true [that Rice is being overplayed].

“We are really looking after him more than any other season. The way he is training and all the love [he is being shown]. That’s it.

“Here are the demands of this football club and a player like him. Not only him, because there are many others that have even less time. That’s it. That has to be the mindset. If you start to put other things in your brain, [it will create] damage. Erase them, get them in the bin and just focus on what you have to do.”

Newly-promoted Hull City stunned Arsenal at the Emirates thanks to goals from Geovanni and Daniel Cousin in September 2008, but you will find long odds on them repeating the feat this afternoon.
