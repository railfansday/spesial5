Is It Too Late to Buy the Vanguard Growth ETF? Here's What History Says.

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Vanguard Growth ETF (VUG -1.46%) has unquestionably been one of the market's best performers over the past several years. But some investors might look at recent returns and wonder if it's too late to buy.

Even though the fund is currently about 9% below its recent high, its 21.2% average annual total return over the past three years and 17.4% average over the past decade will have some people feeling like they missed out on the bull market.

History suggests that buying after big rallies isn't necessarily a bad idea. The challenge around buying the fund right now, however, has nothing to do with performance.

A lot of people think that buying stocks at all-time highs means that there's no real value opportunity. In reality, however, stocks are usually near all-time highs because economic conditions are good and company performance has been solid. That's the kind of thing that can keep pushing prices higher, even if they're at highs right now.

A JPMorgan Chase (JPM +0.98%) report from 2024 found that forward-looking returns for the S&P 500 were actually slightly better when investing at all-time highs than they were when investing at any other time. That's not to say that you should push all in when stock prices are at all-time highs. But buying at highs historically hasn't posed the risks many investors think it does. A follow-up report in 2025 found no significant advantage or disadvantage to investing at all-time highs compared to any other day.

The Vanguard Growth ETF has roughly 130 to 140 stocks, but it's far from diversified. It currently has 72% of its portfolio in tech stocks. 63% of the portfolio is in just the top 10 holdings, with Nvidia (NVDA -2.01%), Microsoft (MSFT +0.17%), and Apple (AAPL +0.09%) accounting for more than half of that.

It's not necessarily the fund's fault. After all, it's just tracking a rules-based index, and the tech sector is where the growth is coming from right now. However, the concentration makes it vulnerable to a sharp pullback like the one we've seen over the past few weeks.

The practical path is to keep everything automatic. If you're making contributions monthly, keep making them. Don't try to time the market or sell because you think the bottom is going to fall out. If you're a long-term investor, your focus should be on wealth creation, not finding the optimal entry point.

You can also pair it with a total market fund, a value fund, or one with international stocks. These have different compositions that can help offset some of the more severe downside risks.

Either way, don't judge a fund based on its past performance. Look at what it can do for your future.
