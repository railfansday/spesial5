Intel vs. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing: Which Tech Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Intel is betting big on external foundry customers, while Taiwan Semiconductor's margins sit above 50%. Which chipmaker deserves your capital heading into the next cycle?

By Erin Kennedy – Updated Nov 18, 2026 at 8:08AM EST

The chip industry is undergoing a massive shift as artificial intelligence and local manufacturing take center stage. Choosing between Intel (INTC -2.72%) and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM -0.58%) requires weighing a turnaround story against a market leader.

Intel focuses on designing its own processors while opening its factories to external customers. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing serves as a pure-play foundry, producing high-end chips for nearly every major player among semiconductor stocks. Both companies are central to the global tech supply chain but offer very different risk and reward profiles.

Intel designs and manufactures a wide range of processors, accelerators, and software solutions for the modern computing world. Under its latest strategy, the company is combining its internal chip design with a massive expansion of its foundry services to manufacture chips for other businesses. Its customer base is diverse, including distributors, original equipment manufacturers, and cloud service providers across sectors like healthcare, finance, and telecommunications.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 26, 2026, revenue reached approximately $59.9 billion, representing revenue growth of nearly 12.1% compared to the previous period. However, the company faced significant headwinds, reporting a net loss of roughly $12 billion during this time frame. This result highlights the high costs associated with its ongoing efforts to regain a leading position in the semiconductor market.

As of Intel's December 2025 balance sheet, the company carries a debt-to-equity ratio of nearly 0.4, which compares total debt to shareholder equity. The current ratio is approximately 2, indicating the ability to cover short-term financial obligations with assets that can be converted to cash within a year. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 27, 2025, free cash flow was approximately negative $4.9 billion. Note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 25.1% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation, since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing operates as a pure-play foundry, focusing exclusively on manufacturing semiconductors designed by its customers. It serves the most demanding segments of the market, including smartphones and high-performance artificial intelligence applications, from its base in Taiwan and facilities worldwide. By acting as a manufacturer for others, the company avoids competing directly with its clients while maintaining a critical role in the global tech supply chain.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $150.2 billion, an increase of approximately 36.1%. This growth translated into net income of close to $76.8 billion, resulting in a robust net margin of roughly 51.1%. These figures demonstrate the strong demand for the advanced manufacturing nodes that the company provides to its diverse customer base.

As of TSM's December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio was roughly 2.5, reflecting a healthy cushion for meeting short-term liabilities. The company maintains a conservative debt-to-equity ratio of nearly 0.2, showing that its total debt is low relative to its equity. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was approximately $30.1 billion, providing substantial capital to reinvest in the next generation of chip technology.

Intel faces intense competition from companies like NVIDIA (NVDA -0.76%) and AMD (AMD -0.46%), as well as hyperscalers who are increasingly designing their own custom silicon. The company is also navigating legal challenges, including shareholder lawsuits filed in November 2026 alleging concealment of manufacturing and inventory issues. High capital intensity and the operational risks of its turnaround strategy remain significant hurdles as it builds out new leading-edge factories while relying on sole-source suppliers like ASML (ASML -0.72%) for critical tools.

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing is exposed to risks associated with the high concentration of its manufacturing facilities in a single geographic region. This concentration creates sensitivity to geopolitical tensions and potential supply chain disruptions that could impact its global operations. Additionally, the company must continually invest billions in new technology nodes to stay ahead of rivals, which entails significant capital risk if demand for high-end chips from major customers like Apple (AAPL +0.74%) or NVIDIA fluctuates.

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing looks cheaper on a forward P/E basis, while Intel has a lower P/S ratio based on sales over the past 12 months.

A large part of the reason Intel reported that $12 billion loss is because it's in the midst of a turnaround. It's pivoting toward providing chip manufacturing services for third parties. In order to do so, the chipmaker is having to shell out a lot of money to build fabrication plants. Intel even suspended its dividend a few years ago in pursuit of this new strategy. That spending will likely continue, as turning around a ship of Intel's size is neither a fast nor an easy endeavor.

Intel's stock is miles behind Taiwan Semiconductor's, and worse yet, it hasn't even kept pace with the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.38%), trailing it by over 180 percentage points.

Taiwan Semiconductor dominates the foundry market and maintains a strong net margin despite operating in a capital-intensive business. It also pays a modest dividend.

I think there are too many unknowns tied to Intel's strategic transformation. Taiwan Semiconductor looks like a better buy for 2027 and beyond.
