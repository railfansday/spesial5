Nvidia Stock Is Down 32%. Is It a No-Brainer Buy at $163 or a Value Trap?

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Nvidia's 32% plunge has dramatically reduced its valuation, but serious questions about AI spending and leadership remain.

Nvidia (NVDA -2.44%) has gone from one of Wall Street's hottest artificial intelligence stocks to one of its biggest sources of anxiety. Shares of the chipmaker have plunged roughly 30% from their October peak amid concerns about AI spending and the unexpected departure of longtime CEO Jensen Huang. That sell-off has pushed Nvidia's valuation down to a surprisingly low level.

But is this a rare chance to buy an AI powerhouse at a bargain price, or a warning that its best days are behind it?

Two factors have weighed on Nvidia stock over the past month. The market has started growing increasingly concerned about the pace of the AI build-out. Several data center developers have seen their funding costs surge due to elevated debt levels, raising concerns they'll need to slow capital spending. Meanwhile, technology giants from Microsoft to Alphabet reported capital spending plans that fell short of forecasts due to AI chip delays. Microsoft reduced its capex forecast from $175 billion to $170 billion, while Alphabet maintained its $195 billion to $205 billion guidance range. These and other factors caused concerns that demand for the chips Nvidia develops might be lower than expected.

The real shocker came last week when Nvidia's co-founder and CEO, Jensen Huang, resigned amid reports of personal conduct and potential national security concerns. The company named its CFO, Colette Kress, as interim CEO until the board finds a permanent CEO. In a press release announcing the CEO transition, Nvidia noted that "Jensen's contributions to Nvidia are profound." Suffice it to say, he won't be easy to replace.

This combination of concerns about AI-related spending and an unexpected leadership transition has created significant uncertainty about its future.

The recent turmoil comes during a period of unprecedented growth. Nvidia's revenue skyrocketed 106% to $96 billion during its fiscal 2027 second quarter (reported in late August), with data center revenue growing a blistering 117% to $89 billion. Meanwhile, its net income surged 126% to nearly $60 billion, or $2.46 per share. The company generated $21.3 billion in free cash flow during the quarter and returned $26 billion to shareholders through dividends and repurchases. It ended the period with $99.4 billion in cash against $33.4 billion in debt.

Nvidia expects to continue growing briskly. It's guiding for about $108 billion of revenue in its fiscal third quarter, which it will report this week.

With its stock price tumbling, Nvidia now trades at about 17.5x its forward earnings estimate of $9.33 per share for this year. That's down from nearly 40x earlier this year, due to surging earnings and a falling stock price. It now trades at a discount to the broader market, as the S&P 500 trades at 18.8x forward earnings and the Nasdaq-100 at around 20.7x. Meanwhile, it's an even cheaper 10.3x the analysts' consensus earnings estimate for 2028 of $15.85 per share.

Nvidia is no longer trading at a valuation that demands an endlessly expanding earnings multiple. Instead, the crucial question is whether its earnings forecasts can survive a potential slowdown in the AI investment cycle.

My base case assumes 12.8% compound annual earnings-per-share growth over the next five years, with Nvidia's valuation multiple set at around its current level of 18x. That would generate more than 13% annualized total returns, growing a $1,000 investment into nearly $1,900. Meanwhile, my bull case assumes even faster earnings growth (21.8% annualized) and multiple expansion. Finally, my bear case assumes no earnings growth due to Nvidia's growing competition and continued contraction in the valuation multiple.

The base case return is pretty compelling. Nvidia doesn't need to grow its earnings at breakneck speed to deliver attractive returns from its current valuation. However, the bear case showcases the risks. If AI growth slows or competition intensifies, Nvidia could deliver underwhelming returns.

Nvidia's stock has become much more attractive following its steep sell-off. If the company can sustain even a fraction of its recent earnings growth, shares could deliver double-digit annualized returns over the next five years. However, the uncertainty surrounding AI spending and Nvidia's leadership transition makes it difficult to call the stock a no-brainer buy just yet. I'd rather wait to see what the company has to say when it reports earnings later this week before buying. While investors might miss some upside if shares experience a post-earnings pop, they could also continue to drop if Nvidia disappoints, potentially offering an even better entry point.
