Is Anthropic Stock a Buy Before Its Dec. 15 Share Unlock?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Index funds might buy the Claude maker's stock once in December. The scheduled share releases run well into 2027.

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 27, 2026 at 4:37PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›

Anthropic (ANT +10.98%) closed below its $83 initial public offering (IPO) price for the first time on Tuesday, Nov. 24. On Wednesday, Nov. 25, it traded as low as $73.85 and closed at $82.79.

FTSE Russell has said Anthropic will join the Russell 1000 after the close on Friday, Dec. 11, and the Nasdaq-100 might follow at the open on Monday, Dec. 21. In between, on Tuesday, Dec. 15, up to 303 million shares become sellable in the next scheduled release under Anthropic's lockup agreements (deals that ban existing holders from selling for fixed periods).

Could the index buying offset that new supply?

Dec. 15 isn't the first chance for insiders to sell. Under Anthropic's lockup terms, employees, consultants, and some officers and directors could sell up to 315 million shares starting the first day of trading.

The Dec. 15 release covers a group of existing investors, with up to 303 million shares becoming sellable then, another 303 million on Jan. 5, 353 million on Jan. 19, and 353 million more on Feb. 2. All told, up to around 1.3 billion shares might become sellable over seven weeks -- more than the 1.1 billion or so sold in the IPO. At Wednesday's close, the Dec. 15 release alone would be worth around $25 billion.

Then the releases grow. Up to 1.7 billion shares become sellable just after Anthropic posts full-year 2026 results, on a date it hasn't set, and up to 2.4 billion more follow in the next 10 weeks or so. The largest single release, up to 3.0 billion shares, comes after the first-quarter 2027 report or by mid-May 2027 at the latest.

Sure, not all eligible shares will be sold. But each date offers early holders a new chance to sell, with the growth stock already under what IPO buyers paid.

Anthropic's Nasdaq-100 entry is still a projection. Under Nasdaq's rules, an IPO whose full market value ranks among the index's 40 largest members normally joins after 15 trading days. But Anthropic's seventh trading day, Monday, Nov. 30, is also the ranking date for the index's yearly reshuffle, which Nasdaq is set to announce after the close on Dec. 11. That would put Anthropic in the reshuffle, effective at the open on Dec. 21.

Even then, the index wouldn't weight Anthropic at its $1.1 trillion valuation. Nasdaq leaves unlisted shares, like Anthropic's nonvoting Class N stock, out of its weighting. And for a company with under a third of its shares trading freely, it counts up to three times the free-floating shares. If it counts just the shares sold in the IPO, Anthropic would be weighted as if it were worth around $280 billion.

Nasdaq's own example of a hypothetical $1 trillion IPO with 6% of its shares floated came to about $6 billion of buying, assuming funds tracking the index had $600 billion. Anthropic's capped value is over 50% bigger than the example's $180 billion, so on the same assumptions I'd put its fund buying around $9 billion, well below the value of the Dec. 15 release.

Index funds must hold a stock when its addition takes effect. So Russell 1000 funds would probably buy at the close on Dec. 11, and Nasdaq-100 funds like the Invesco QQQ Trust at the close before Dec. 21. Then, their buying would mostly track fund inflows and any gain in Anthropic's index weight at a quarterly review.

The next big releases, up to about 4.1 billion shares in all, are linked to Anthropic's full-year 2026 results, which will include a holiday quarter. In its prospectus, the AI company said usage tends to drop around holidays, and total customer spend fell about 20% in the two weeks ending Jan. 2, 2026, compared to the two weeks before.

Its last holiday quarter also had the slowest sequential growth in 2025. Revenue for the fourth quarter climbed 48% over the previous quarter, after a 76% gain a quarter earlier. Growth was already down to 47% by the third quarter of 2026, and the company expects seasonal effects to get more noticeable as its growth rate falls.

In the end, I don't think the index buying can soak up much of that supply. The funds buy once and may finish by Dec. 21, but the releases run through February and grow after the full-year 2026 report. For a buyer around the $83 IPO price, I'd rather watch how the stock holds up through the January releases first. And, of course, I'd also keep an eye on the underlying business, looking to see if it can live up to its wild valuation.
