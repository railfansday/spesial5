Is Nvidia Stock's Trading at 10x Forward Earnings the Bargain of the Century or Dead Money Hiding in Plain Sight?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Nvidia trades at a valuation similar to that of companies with far less impressive growth.

If you weren't sure how valuable Jensen Huang was to Nvidia (NVDA -0.86%), then simply look at the stock over the past month. Nvidia's stock has fallen nearly 30% in the past month (as of the morning of Nov. 16). Huang admitted marital infidelity in a letter to the company on Nov. 8, and his resignation was announced Nov. 12.

This is an enormous move for a stock with a market cap of nearly $3.9 trillion, even after the decline. Huang co-founded Nvidia 33 years ago, and the company now reportedly controls 90% of the general-purpose graphics processing unit (GPU) market, which powers the technology many believe will completely transform the world as we know it.

Nvidia stock currently trades at roughly 10 times its projected fiscal year 2028 earnings (fiscal 2028 ends in January 2029). Is the stock the bargain of the century or dead money hiding in plain sight?

Whatever you think about Huang, who resigned after extramarital affairs came to light, including with one woman who may have been a Chinese spy, there's no denying that Huang was a strong CEO.

He built Nvidia into the juggernaut it is today and has become the voice for the entire AI industry. That said, no person is bigger than a company like Nvidia. Even without Huang, Nvidia still has a dominant product and the team that made it what it is today.

A big reason I think the stock is struggling is that Huang's ouster is not simply about an extramarital affair. It's also about national security, the quality of Nvidia's chip sales, and their end location.

Specifically, Huang's relationship with Alice Huang (no relation) — the controlling shareholder of a Singaporean data center company called MegaSpeed — has raised concerns that Jensen Huang may have been compromised and that Nvidia chips may have entered China illegally.

Former President Joe Biden's administration had only allowed Nvidia to sell older-generation chips to Chinese companies, due to national security concerns. In April 2025, the Trump administration told Nvidia it would need to obtain export licenses to sell chips to China. Since then, Nvidia has said there have been virtually no chip sales to China.

While many details remain unknown, reporting by media outlets, including The Information, has found that a network developed by Alice Huang allegedly helped funnel billions of dollars' worth of Nvidia chips to Chinese companies.

This will raise many questions for investors, and activist short-seller Culper Research estimated in May that a fifth of Nvidia's fiscal year 2026 sales may have illegally ended up in China. (Short-sellers make money when a stock they hold falls in value.)

If this proves true and the Trump administration or Congress can impose even tougher export restrictions, could Nvidia's revenue and earnings be revised downward? Or will it need to spend more for better compliance?

There could also be government investigations that lead to regulatory fines or other punitive measures.

Don't get me wrong, Nvidia stock at 10x fiscal 2028 projected earnings looks incredibly attractive. In Nvidia's fiscal 2027 second quarter, the company grew revenue by 106%; grew earnings by 128%; and posted a 75% gross margin. Companies that do that often see their stocks trade at earnings multiples that are multiples higher.

But one of the big questions at Nvidia is whether the company has been over-earning and whether all of these circular financing agreements it engages in reflect its true financial health.

Unfortunately, none of these questions have an easy answer and will require significant research that may be beyond the reach of the typical retail investor.

On the bullish side, there is still a very good chance Nvidia will continue to perform well and remain the dominant chip player. For all these reasons, I think investors can begin nibbling on Nvidia stock. But I don't think it's time to go all in just yet.

More information about all of this will certainly emerge in the coming weeks and months. Whether it's good or bad for the stock remains the big question mark.
