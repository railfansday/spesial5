The Met Opera is using YouTube to look for a new generation of fans

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Timothee Chalamet may think that no one cares about opera, but one of the most prominent opera houses in the U.S. is betting that it can find new fans for a 430-year-old art form — it just needs to look in the right place. New York’s Metropolitan Opera is delivering a YouTube live stream that will provide open access to a performance of the new work Lincoln in the Bardo.

The Lincoln in the Bardo opera, which is based on a bestselling novel by George Saunders, had its world premiere at the Met Opera last month. Now, as the 143-year-old opera company looks to expand its fan base, it is hosting a Lincoln in the Bardo live stream on its official YouTube channel. The broadcast is set to go live on November 14 and will remain available until the 18th. Beginning on November 20, the Lincoln in the Bardo stream will transition to paid formats.

The YouTube stream isn’t just a play for new fans — it’s also a fundraiser for the host company. The Met Opera is tying its free broadcast to its Make the Met Forever fundraising campaign, which aims to bring in resources that can support Met programming through 2030. As part of the stream, viewers will be encouraged to make donations via chat.

“With the final performances of our run completely sold out, we decided to share Lincoln in the Bardo for free with our global audience,” set Meta Opera General Manager Peter Gelb in a statement provided to OperaWire. “Opera is being transformed by powerful new works like Lincoln in the Bardo, and we hope our efforts will be rewarded with donations to our Make the Met Forever fundraising campaign.”

If the Met is looking to present dramatic fare that can get younger generations excited about the medium, then Lincoln in the Bardo is an excellent choice. Saunders’ novel won the Booker Prize, and Dua Lipa‘s Service95 venture chronicled the operatic adaptation through a mini documentary. The Met Opera will stream the doc during the YouTube broadcast’s intermission.

There are several barriers that make it difficult to market opera to today’s young people, and the medium’s elitist airs present one of those roadblocks. By making Lincoln in the Bardo available to all, the Met is showing that its performances can have broad appeal — and that promoting opera doesn’t have to be as difficult as getting your son back from beyond the grave.
