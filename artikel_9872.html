Cleverbridge admits attackers stole customers' credit card and CVV numbers. Yep, you read that right

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Payments platform hasn't disclosed how the data was captured or the scale of the breach

Software e-commerce platform Cleverbridge has admitted that hackers stole customers' card numbers and CVV security codes, raising questions about how the attackers captured data that payment security rules prohibit retaining after authorization.

The Germany-headquartered company, which handles software sales and subscriptions on behalf of other businesses, detected unauthorized activity on its platform on November 4. In an incident update, Cleverbridge said it had cut off the intruders' access and launched an investigation that later established payment card information had been copied from its systems.

The public notice doesn't spell out exactly what was stolen, but an email sent to affected customers, seen by The Register, fills in some rather important blanks.

"Based on our investigation to date, the information involved includes payment card details you used to make a purchase through Cleverbridge, notably your card number, expiry date, card security code (CVV/CVC), and contact details," the company told customers.

Email addresses, billing addresses, and phone numbers were also among the information potentially exposed, although Cleverbridge reckons account passwords escaped the breach.

More concerning are the stolen CVV codes. PCI DSS rules prohibit retaining card verification codes after authorization, even in encrypted form.

Cleverbridge hasn't explained how the attackers obtained the codes, whether they were retained after transactions, or whether the criminals intercepted them during payment processing. The company didn't respond to The Register's questions about the breach.

Cleverbridge handles the business of selling software online, from taking payments to managing subscriptions, and its client roster includes some familiar names in enterprise IT, such as Red Hat, SUSE, Tenable, Veeam, Jamf, Parallels, and Plesk.

That doesn't mean customers of all those companies had their card details stolen. Cleverbridge hasn't named the vendors affected by the intrusion, nor has it disclosed how many people were caught up in the breach or how long the hackers were poking around its systems.

In its notification to customers, the company said it had contacted data protection authorities, payment card schemes, and its payment partners, and was working with a specialist police cybercrime unit. It began notifying affected individuals on November 20, more than two weeks after discovering the intrusion.

Affected customers are being offered 24 months of free credit monitoring and fraud assistance. That may help detect identity misuse, but it won't stop a crook armed with their card number, expiration date, and CVV from attempting a fraudulent purchase.

Two years of credit monitoring is a nice gesture, but customers whose card numbers and CVVs are now in criminal hands might prefer a new bit of plastic. ®
