Tethered to stone: how rock and water availability shaped early desert occupation

Emanuele Cancellieri is at the Interdisciplinary Center for Archaeology and the Evolution of Human Behaviour (ICArEHB), Universidade do Algarve, 8005-139 Faro, Portugal.

Climate and freshwater availability strongly shape expectations about past human dispersal and occupation across regions that are now deserts. Writing in Nature, Dellmour et al.1 examine this idea by studying the Rub’ al Khali (which translates to the Empty Quarter), an almost uninhabited sand desert in the southern Arabian Peninsula.
