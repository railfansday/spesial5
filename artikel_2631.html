Player Grades: Cavs vs. Grizzlies – Cleveland loses back-and-forth affair

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Cleveland Cavaliers dropped Wednesday's offensive showdown 124-121 to the Memphis Grizzlies.

Grades are based on our usual expectations for each player.

34 points, 3 rebounds, 3 assists, 1 steal

Mitchell has been on a heater as of late.

It appears the momentum generated from Monday's matchup against the Chicago Bulls carried over as Mitchell was once again scoring at will. The Cavaliers found themselves in a constant back-and-forth, and Mitchell was the counterpunch Cleveland needed more times than not.

Nights like these are perfect responses for why the Cavaliers still can rely on Mitchell.

As of late, it feels like Harden is looking to be more of a distributor than a scorer. He was not hunting for scoring opportunities, and while it is nice to see Harden not force his way out of the slump, it is discouraging to see that this is the role he needs now.

Some might deem this as the right transition for Harden as he enters the late stage of his career. In terms of the Cavaliers' postseason outlook, it doesn't really make the roster that much better.

Allen had a tough task against the height of the Grizzlies. He was either matching up against Zach Edey or Cameron Boozer for stretches, and their height really put Allen to the test throughout the game.

It felt like at times that Allen was getting a lot of physicality and not being rewarded by the whistle. He responded to these challenges well, still finding ways to score despite not getting the calls. A great performance from Allen, where toughness was a key factor.

Proctor had his most forgettable performance in a minute against Memphis. Being named a starter again was deserved with the string of games that Proctor has put together. However, not every game is going to trend upwards; games like this will happen from time to time.

It was nice to see that even when Proctor is not firing away, he stays level-headed and does not become impatient with the lack of opportunities.

Finally, Mario has got the nod to be in the starting five. It only took injuries to nearly the entire Cavaliers roster, but we finally got there.

Hezonja continues to be a force when out on the floor. Whether it's his physicality or ability to knock down shots from the perimeter, Hezonja impresses me at every turn. Nights like these will only make the cries for his minutes to ramp up louder.

There is no one else on the Cavaliers roster who really can match the unique traits that Hezonja provides. At times, it feels like the opponents do not really have an idea of how to match him either.

Merrill looked like a man who was not out for the past few games. All he did was put his head down to get to the basket and fight for offensive boards.

While Merrill was not lighting up the scoreboard, he did show a lot of hustle and grit. While that will not please many, I was fine with this outing from a guy who was shelved for a bit.

Then, he missed two free throws that cost the Cavs the game.

Grade: C+ before free-throw misses, D+ after

14 points, 2 rebounds, 2 assists, 1 steal

Thomas is so consistent offensively. It is insane that the Cavaliers' second-round pick continues to contribute to the offense in such valuable stretches.

The shot is legit. Thomas's ability to shoot from almost anywhere, whether on the move or catch-and-shoot, allows him to fit very easily into any of the Cavaliers' five-man lineups.

Quality minutes from Bryant, however, with the lineups and athletic size of Memphis, it wasn't a great use of his skill set.

Omier is just a hustler. There is no second of this game where his effort will be questioned. On a team where it feels like there will be lapses, he will be a reliable hustler and a reminder of the effort needed to win games.
