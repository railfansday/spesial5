RJ Luis suing SEC and Greg Sankey after LSU basketball eligibility fight

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The saga that's engulfed LSU, the Tigers' men's basketball program and the SEC has taken yet another turn.

And, to the surprise of no one, it involves lawyers.

RJ Luis, the one-time LSU men's basketball player whose eligibility case has been a point of contention for weeks with the SEC, has filed a lawsuit against the SEC and commissioner Greg Sankey seeking damages caused by the conference's efforts that prompted LSU's decision not to play him following a previously awarded court injunction, acccording to a motion and other documents provided by one of Luis' attorneys to The Daily Advertiser, a part of the USA TODAY Network.

It's unclear how much Luis is seeking in damages.

Luis, who had previously signed NBA contracts with the Utah Jazz and Boston Celtics, was granted two separate injunctions in Louisiana courts, the first on Aug. 3 and another Nov. 13, as he sought eligibility for the 2026-27 college basketball season. Luis committed to play for Tigers coach Will Wade in May.

While Luis was cleared to play by the judicial system, he faced an additional obstacle with the SEC, which in August passed a rule barring member institutions from having athletes compete for them who had previously declared for the NFL, NBA or WNBA drafts.

Following the Nov. 13 injunction, the SEC issued a statement saying it would abide by the court's order and not punish LSU for putting Luis on its roster, but the league mentioned nothing about the Tigers actually playing Luis. On Nov. 18, however, Luis played 15 minutes, scoring 11 points and pulling down nine rebounds, in his team's 79-67 victory against Louisiana Monroe.

One day later, LSU president Wade Rousseau released a statement saying he instructed Wade not to play Luis, who has since been removed from the LSU roster and hasn't played in the team's two most recent games.

That didn't mark the end of the escalating tensions between LSU and the league of which it's a founding member. The SEC's chancellors and presidents are scheduled to hold a vote on Dec. 3 over whether to expel the Tigers from the conference. A two-thirds vote from the 15 non-LSU members is required for the action to pass.

A 6-foot-7 guard/forward, Luis averaged 18.2 points and 7.2 rebounds per game for St. John's during the 2024-25 season, which culminated with him being named Big East player of the year and earning All-American honors. He declared for the 2025 NBA Draft, but was not selected by a team.
