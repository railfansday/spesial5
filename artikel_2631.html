Ahmed Hassanein wants to balance discipline, relentlessness in NFL debut

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The stage is set for fan favorite Ahmed Hassanein to make his NFL debut for the Detroit Lions this Sunday against the Tampa Bay Buccaneers.

Coach Dan Campbell indicated on Wednesday that Hassanein, the team's sixth-round pick in 2025, will get his first action after starter Aidan Hutchinson suffered a torn ACL last week against the Patriots.

Hassanein has become revered among the fanbase for his intense and tireless attitude, but he also did his part in showing he could be an asset on the field. After missing most of 2025 with a pectoral injury, he shifted positions to Detroit's "big end" role this year, and thrived both in training camp and the preseason. That culminated with 2.0 sacks and five quarterback hits in three games—including a sack against Joe Burrow and the Bengals' first-team offense.

Yet Hassanein has been a healthy scratch all season. Detroit has been happy with the depth along the defensive line, and for good reason. The Lions have been able to generate pressure with their front four, and Hassanein had been thriving in his role of scout team defensive end, preparing Detroit's offensive line for each and every kind of pass rusher they've faced.

"When you get him scout team, he wants to do exactly what the opponent does," Campbell said. "'Tell me how this guy plays. What are you looking for? What do you need?' He asks (Penei) Sewell all the time. And then he says, 'OK, that's what it is.' Then he goes to win. He's trying to win and he works our guys and he's making himself better. He makes us better."

Sunday, that hard work behind the scenes will finally pay off for Hassanein's first NFL action. And to no one's surprise, he's thrilled about it.

"I'm ready. It's been a long time coming, and I feel good about this," Hassanein said. "I'm going to go out there, all for God, all for the team, and all for my family."

Hassanein admits the wait hasn't been easy. With an intense competitor like him, it's not easy to spend game days watching. But instead of wallowing, he's been putting that competitive spirit to use in practice, knowing that this organization still very much believes in him.

"I kept preparing. I got to go against Penei Sewell every day. I get to get better," Hassanein said. "I get to watch other d-ends, see what they do, get to add to my game, see what I'm weak on, what I need to work on. And, man, eventually, I keep going up every week, and I keep making plays."

Making an NFL debut would be an extremely emotional moment for anyone. And for someone like Hassanein—who literally broke down in tears after his first preseason sack—you better believe Sunday will be a huge day for him. But he knows he can't let the emotion get the better of him. He needs to bring the intensity, but not let it allow him to lose focus of what's most important: playing within his role.

"I have to hold my gap, I have to do my job," Hassanein said. "I have other people relying on me, and I have to execute it every single time. Not, 'Oh, I'm gonna do my own thing for one play or two plays or three plays.' That's not right. All you have to do is just: What's the call? What's your alignment? What's your assignment? And go accomplish it, and go just destroy the guy in front of you."
