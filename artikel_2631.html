Falcons Draft 2027: Way-Too-Early NFL Mock Draft 3.0

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Happy Thanksgiving, Falcons fans and welcome to the third iteration of what may become a weekly occurrence for the Atlanta Falcons, now that they're at the bottom of the NFC South. The Panthers, mercifully, have gained a two-game lead over everyone else in the division. With teams like the Minnesota Vikings, Detroit Lions, and even the Cleveland Browns coming up, it'll take some real adjustments to get the Falcons back to the top. So, without further ado, here's another Way-Too-Early, yet somehow right-on-time, 2027 NFL mock draft for the Falcons.

Hopefully, you find a pick in here to be thankful for!

Round 6, Pick 218 (Compensatory Pick – David Onyemata)

Round 6, Pick 223 (Compensatory Pick – Tyler Allgeier)

The secondary for the Falcons has been one of the main points of contention this season, as teams have been able to bully the outside cornerbacks pretty consistently. Ashton Hampton would immediately bring physicality to the outside and has a mix of speed and agility that would allow him to keep up with smaller receivers as well.

In today's day and age, nobody likes to spend a Day 2 pick on a quarterback because the thinking is, "If you believe he's the guy, you should have just picked him in the first round." However, with a guy like Trinidad Chambliss, who seems to have the athletic traits and composure to be a first-round pick but just doesn't have the size to hold up at the next level, a Day 2 bet on him feels right.

There's no telling how good Chambliss could be or whether the opportunity cost of drafting him would be worth it, but if he pans out, that'll make life for the Falcons over the next few years a whole lot easier.

Melvin Siani has stepped up tremendously in the absence of Trevor Goosby, who suffered a season-ending torn patellar tendon. Siani's ability to play both tackle spots makes him a perfect addition to the Falcons, who may need help at both positions in the coming years given the uncertainty surrounding Jake Matthews and Jawaan Taylor.

Losing Jessie Bates hurts, but being able to restart with a safety like Ty Benefield and make him a foundational part of your defense would help make up for it. Benefield is a versatile chess piece that Jeff Ulbrich would have a fun time deploying throughout the defense. I don't expect him to still be available at this spot by draft time, but for now, this would be an absolute steal.

After a disappointing 2026 season, Omarion Miller will likely see his stock drop in the upcoming draft. But make no mistake: Miller is a talented receiver who would add some much-needed stability to the Falcons' receiver room. Right now, the Falcons don't have a receiver who can consistently win one-on-one matchups on the outside besides Drake London. Miller would add another route runner who can beat a cornerback one-on-one and get open near the sidelines or downfield. As a true X-receiver, Miller could also allow the Falcons to utilize London and tight end Kyle Pitts in more creative ways out of the slot.

The tight end room will need an infusion of youth heading into next season, and Patrick Overmyer feels like a good addition. With a breakout season at Houston that includes over 650 yards and eight touchdowns, Overmyer has proven to be one of the better receiving threats at the position in this draft.

With Brian Robinson probably on his way out, the Falcons will need another running back to back up Bijan Robinson. Waymond Jordan is a big back out of USC with stellar vision, short-area quickness, and elusiveness, along with the patience to allow holes to develop in front of him.

Justin Evans gives me the feeling of a center who will find his way onto the field more often than not during his rookie season. Evans is a nasty run blocker who has plenty to work on in terms of pass protection, but he has the attitude and physicality to hold his own. If Callahan gets his hands on him, Evans could have a productive career in the NFL, potentially with the Falcons.
