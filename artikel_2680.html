Liberty runs over Mountain View to reach Open Division semifinals

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

No matter what, Liberty finds a way to win. Injuries at quarterback have forced coach Colin Thomas and his staff to be creative.

Two weeks ago, in the regular-season finale, it was senior wide receiver Braxton Huynh reverting to his youth football position and switching to quarterback with starter Jason Haines out with a broken collarbone and backup Kenny Raymond nursing a knee injury. When Huynh was injured in the game, Raymond was cleared at halftime and pushed through to help secure a 21-14 win.

This time, it was relying entirely on the running game with a heavy dose of the Wild Cat offense. No. 1 Peoria Liberty, behind five rushing touchdowns, took down No. 9 visiting Mesa Mountain View 38-24 in front of a standing-room-only crowd in the Open Division quarterfinals on Nov. 20.

Senior running back Jayden Sanchez led the way with four touchdowns, including two going for 81 and 50 yards.

"He's been a three-varsity guy for us, always been a really good player," Thomas said. "He stuck with it too and just kept getting better and better. So proud of him. Really good player.

"Obviously we had to make some adjustments there, but resilient kids, right? (Two weeks ago) was that. This week, Mountain View did a great job, a lot of credit to them. Then offensively, we had to get some scores to separate in the second half. We ran the ball really well and did that.

This was a close game all the way until late in the third quarter, when Liberty began to pull away.

After Mountain View tied the game at 17-17 on a four-yard run from junior quarterback Brayden Merwin with 2:39 left in the third, putting together a 17-play scoring drive that took nearly 10 minutes, Liberty knew it had to respond immediately.

Three plays later, Sanchez took a handoff, bounced off a defender and sprinted down the right sideline for a 50-yard touchdown to make it 24-17 with 1:51 left in the third.

"That's what we had to have," Thomas said. "Because our defense was gassed. Tremendous drive by them. So it was great to get momentum. The momentum kind of shifted off that."

Sanchez said the offense was trying to lift up the defense on the sideline as Mountain View nearly took up the entire third quarter with its long drive.

"I got you mentality, you know?," Sanchez said. "We see their heads down, it's like, I got ya. I mean, we knew they were gonna have our backs right after."

The next drive, Liberty pounced on a bad snap on a Mountain View punt and got the ball back at the Toros' 20-yard line. Junior running back Chase Ochoa punched it from 13 yards out to extend the lead to 31-17 with 9:50 left to play.

Following a turnover on downs from Mountain View on the ensuing drive, Sanchez zig-zagged his way to an 81-yard touchdown to deliver the knockout punch and push the lead to 38-17 with 2:27 left.

Raymond, who is still working his way back from the knee injury and is at about 80% right now, was grateful to have the run game firing on all cylinders. Sanchez's first two touchdowns of the game came on a direct snap out of the Wild Cat, which Liberty turned to in the red zone for the game.

"It's a blessing, because our O-line does a really good job at practicing everything and just making sure that we get the ball in the end zone," said Raymond.

Next up in the Open semifinals is No. 5 Chandler Basha, who rallied from a 35-0 deficit to beat No. 4 Peoria Centennial 36-35. Basha is the only team to beat Liberty this season, 30-22 on Sept. 18.

We didn't execute some things when we needed to, missed tackles, the small things. The things that that team does in the moments when it's tough, that's where we gotta get to."
