Kirk Cousins gives Vikings what they expected. That's enough – for now

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Even in a historically unique position for a quarterback, Kirk Cousins was exactly what the Minnesota Vikings literally bargained for.

In stepping in for Sunday's game against the Green Bay Packers after the Vikings acquired him Tuesday in a trade with the Las Vegas Raiders, the 15-year veteran became the first passer since at least 1950 to start for two different teams in consecutive weeks. Cousins, however, was no mystery box.

The 38-year-old isn't the same player he was when he departed the organization in 2024. But his value proposition as a pocket passer who can get through his progressions has long been evident, even if whatever team taking him on has had to endure a few hold-your-breath decisions in each game.

For a Vikings team that looked liable to waste a load-bearing defense, that familiar package – for all of its imperfections – was one the team would readily embrace.

Cousins' homecoming in a 30-10 win over the Packers fittingly featured few surprises, all in a mostly ideal fashion for a team both new and old to the quarterback.

And in completing 21 of 31 passes for 207 yards and two touchdowns, the signal-caller provided Minnesota with what it so sorely needed at midseason: a sense of timing and the possibility of sporadic escape from its problems.

The starkest contrast Cousins drew from Kyler Murray and Carson Wentz could be what he offered the offense once it fell in a hole.

Two of Cousins' best plays came on third-down strikes. On third-and-23 in the first quarter, he found Myles Price on a deep crosser, which the receiver then took into the end zone for a 38-yard score. That marked the longest third-down conversion throughout the NFL this season.

Cousins again delivered in another tough spot two drives later, when he lofted a third-and-15 pass down the left sideline that Tai Felton hauled in for a 24-yard gain. Aaron Jones plowed into the end zone for a 1-yard touchdown on the following play to put the Vikings up 17-0.

That kind of breakthrough might have felt transcendent to an attack accustomed to getting stuck in the mud. The Vikings entered the game tied for 30th in third-down conversion rate at 33.33%. On Sunday, they converted eight of 16 tries, including those two exceedingly difficult looks.

That's all part of the renewed sense of possibility with Cousins at the helm. Minnesota didn't pile up yards, but it played complementary football on a day when it won the turnover battle 4-1 and its defense generated eight sacks. And the offense kept churning in the fourth quarter, stringing together two long fourth-quarter scoring drives spanning more than 10 minutes to salt the game away.

"There's gonna be a lot of talk about offense and my addition here, but it's the defense," Cousins said in his postgame interview with Fox. "Our defense just played lights-out and kept giving us the ball back. I thought our staff did a great job of putting together a plan to allow me to be comfortable, and my teammates made plays and took it off my shoulders."

Cousins also quickly rekindled his connection with Justin Jefferson. The two-time All-Pro receiver notched a season-high nine catches for 74 receiving yards, making a particular dent on out-breaking routes.

Perhaps the most important element of Cousins' debut was the confidence he seemed to bring out of the rest of the operation. With the quarterback bringing back a sense of rhythm to the offense that had long been missing since his departure, the Vikings reclaimed access to the middle of the field. And coach Kevin O'Connell also did some of his best work in 2026 as a play-caller, showing particular creativity on the misdirection that left tight Gavin Bartholomew wide open for a 20-yard touchdown in the fourth quarter.

If Cousins is a known entity, so is his penchant for making the occasional cringeworthy decision.

That tendency bubbled to the surface again Sunday, as he heaved a regrettable interception to Packers safety Xavier McKinney toward the end of the first half, negating another possible scoring opportunity. He teetered on the verge of an even more calamitous decision earlier in the second quarter. Reviews showed Cousins' knee was down before he let go of a pass that would go on to be tipped and secured by edge rusher Lukas Van Ness.

And for all the progress the passing game displayed, the running game continued to get mostly middling results, netting 129 yards on 31 carries.

Cousins looked plenty comfortable in a game that conferred him plenty of advantages. Yet in a four-game losing streak with the Raiders, he had no answers for defenses that sent relentless waves of blitzes and man coverage. Packers defensive coordinator Jonathan Gannon has ratcheted up his aggressiveness this season, but he didn't blitz on the two critical third-and-long conversions.

Can Cousins overcome his limitations against the NFC's elite? His initial showing is highly encouraging for the team's chances of navigating the NFC North, which is far and away the league's best division. But Minnesota has to have grander ambitions than just winning O'Connell's first playoff game. Cousins will have to figure out ways to counter the pressure of teams like the Seattle Seahawks, who can pick their spots in throwing extra rushers at the quarterback. And two weeks after his three-interception meltdown against the San Francisco 49ers, he'll get another chance at cracking Raheem Morris' defense in next Sunday's prime-time game in Mexico.

Still, after the Vikings broke out for their second-highest scoring output of the season – and biggest since the opener – it's easy to see why a team that so badly wanted to settle its quarterback question is finally feeling at ease. Even prior to the game, Ian Rapoport reported that the Vikings were interested in having Cousins back for 2027.
