Rich Eisen Reflects on His Return to ESPN and Planting More Flags For The Future

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Last year, Rich Eisen received a phone call he never imagined could happen. For the first time since 2003, the famed ESPN SportsCenter anchor returned to the place where his sports media career began its national rise. Twenty-two years after leaving to become the first personality on the NFL Network, Eisen returned with his own sports radio brand as The Rich Eisen Show joined ESPN Radio, with additional distribution on ESPN+ and Disney+.

Now, more than a year later, Eisen finds himself enjoying the opportunities his return to ESPN has provided. He has gained additional distribution, new teammates and opportunities to create original programming. Ultimately, Eisen says there is simply nothing like being back home with the worldwide leader in sports.

"I enjoy delivering the program into the ESPN, Disney+ sphere of influence and distribution. There is nothing like the ESPN platform. I experienced it from 1996-2003 as a host. But never thought my career would morph into host/small business owner," said Eisen. "After taking my show over in 2019 and building it, getting the opportunity to have that be the vehicle that returns me to ESPN, only to then have ESPN acquire my broadcasting rights from NFL Network. That's stuff you can't make up."

Eisen now serves in a hybrid role that combines elements of his previous ESPN experience with his current business ventures. He remains an on-air talent, hosting several programs on NFL Network throughout the calendar year, while also serving as the CEO and founder of Rich Eisen Productions.

The Rich Eisen Show remains Eisen's property. He still owns and produces the program, maintaining complete autonomy over its content. That was one of several promises ESPN made in its efforts to bring Eisen back home to the worldwide leader in sports.

"Everything I was promised I received in the first year, including support from ESPN digital and social platforms. Good Karma Sports selling my show and opening partnerships. Basically, fire hosing the show. They've done everything they say they would," noted Eisen. "It's been an awesome feeling."

Over the past year, Eisen has also had several opportunities to expand his presence under the ESPN umbrella. In April, he returned to ESPN's headquarters in Bristol, Connecticut, to host SportsCenter for the first time in more than two decades. He also worked with ESPN's archives on a podcast series that tapped into the nostalgia surrounding former SportsCenter anchors.

Most recently, ESPN announced that Eisen's radio program would expand its reach on Disney+. For the first time, The Rich Eisen Show would become available to international audiences through Disney+, with distribution in select countries across Europe, the Middle East and Africa (EMEA).

"I'm a professional flag planter. I was the first guy to be on the set of the NFL Network with a microphone. Everything NFL Network did growing up, any time we did something for the first time, I was thankfully handed the reins to lead it on air," said Eisen. "I planted flags for my show with DirecTV. Then I planted flags all over the place when I owned it. So, when ESPN wants to put my show and expand it on Disney+, great. I don't think about it as I have a lot of responsibilities. It's just what I'm used to doing."

For Eisen, expanding into international markets made complete sense. During his travels covering and calling NFL games overseas for NFL Network, he saw firsthand the interest and passion international audiences have for American football.

"I've seen the audience in Frankfurt, Munich, and London with my own two eyes," explained Eisen. "When we do a deal with my show with Disney and ESPN, they have worldwide rights. It makes sense for us to inquire about their expanding strategy, to see if we could get worldwide rights. To the credit of everyone at Disney+ and ESPN, they're up for it. Let's see how it works."

Calling NFL games overseas proved to be a rewarding experience for Eisen. He enjoyed more than the opportunity to broadcast the games themselves. He also embraced the atmosphere surrounding the events, from hosting The Rich Eisen Show on location to occasionally taking a Saturday stroll through a local beer garden.

However, 2026 brought a change. With ESPN overseeing NFL Network as part of its acquisition agreement with the NFL, Eisen's time calling the league's international series came to an end. He acknowledges that the assignment differed significantly from his typical responsibilities, but he admits he misses the experience.

"For the last year, ESPN and Disney provided our show a studio overseas. It was fun, we brought some of the staff. But, I got three kids at home and my role with NFL Network too," said Eisen. "But yes, I do miss it. One time in a week off between Frankfurt games, I went to Berlin and did a bike tour of the city because my wife insisted on getting out of the hotel when I was on my own. But, what a great experience overall."

Above all, Eisen says he loves radio. He appreciates the opportunity to connect with people in real time and share conversations about their common passion for sports. He also recognizes how fortunate he is that the hard work of his entire team continues to earn support from the audience.

That connection gives Eisen a different sense of satisfaction from growing his radio program than he gets from anything he accomplishes on television.

"I'm growing a business. I have people that depend on me, and I depend on them. Having an outlet, I love having that and tracking our success on our platform," said Eisen. "On the television side of things, we're always looking for new ways to break down the fourth wall and connect with the fan and meet them where they are. Radio is the literal sense of meeting the fans where they are because they can connect with you from anywhere in real time."

Another way Eisen has strengthened that connection is through a podcast that tugs at the heartstrings of longtime SportsCenter viewers. For years, he wanted to develop a series that explored the stories behind the scenes and revisited memorable moments with the voices who once shared the desk on ESPN's flagship highlights program.

After noticing the growing popularity of nostalgia-driven podcasts, Eisen pitched the concept to ESPN President Jimmy Pitaro during discussions about his return to the network.

"With my show coming back into the orbit of ESPN, I thought this was the perfect opportunity to execute what I always been thinking about," said Eisen. "Jimmy [Pitaro] loved the idea, and we were off to the races."

With access to ESPN's SportsCenter archives, Eisen and his coordinating producer, Michael Hoskyns, went to work. Eisen reached out to former SportsCenter colleagues to discuss memorable moments from the past, while Hoskyns navigated the archives, identified footage and helped conceptualize the final product.

In June, This Was SportsCenter launched with six episodes featuring notable ESPN anchors from the past. The response was overwhelming, and Eisen is already planning a second season.

"I have a few things in the hopper for next year, including a book. I've already reached out to a bunch of my old school colleagues that I saved for a potential second season. Either because they were unable to do season one, or wanted to see the popularity of season one," explained Eisen. "Doing it in person is so much better than not. There's nothing like a face-to-face chat."

Eisen credits much of the series' appeal to how dramatically SportsCenter has changed over the years. Even today, fans approach him with memories of staying up late at night or beginning their mornings with Eisen and his longtime partner, Stuart Scott.

"SportsCenter, the way it used to be is different than the way it is today. That also applies to so much of sports television," said Eisen. "I hear stories from fans who remember me and Stuart Scott all the time. So, doing This Was SportsCenter, or occasionally popping on SportsCenter today, it's a lane that I'm pleased to have. With my radio show and all the distribution we have now, I can ask someone if they remember when I had hair and life was different for me and you. I'm thrilled ESPN allows me to occupy that lane."

This February, ESPN will broadcast its first Super Bowl in network history. The event will likely represent the largest and most consequential broadcast in the network's history. Eisen hinted that his show will have a presence throughout Super Bowl week and on game day, particularly with his home studio based in Los Angeles.

"ESPN is the greatest platform there is in sports and entertainment. I can only imagine it will be a Super Bowlpalooza," noted Eisen. "I look forward to being a part of it. My show will be connected and we're creating our own environment in my studio here in Los Angeles like we did for the last Super Bowl here. We have some other plans about the show that I imagine will be announced really soon. But I'm excited about it, and I'll be a part of the Super Bowlpalooza. Whether it's my show or NFL Network, or presumably both. I'm really excited about it."

For Eisen, returning to ESPN wasn't about revisiting the past or trying to recapture what he once had. It was about finding a new way to build on everything that came before.

From launching NFL Network to growing his own radio show, creating original programming and expanding into international markets, Eisen has spent his career embracing opportunities that once seemed unimaginable.

Now, with a Super Bowl on the horizon, the NFL Combine and Draft approaching, and new projects taking shape, his second act with ESPN is still being written. And if there's one thing Eisen has learned throughout his career, it's that there are always more flags to plant.
