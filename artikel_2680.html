Opinion: It’s separation week for the Seahawks and the NFL

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Happy Thanksgiving to all, and to all, holy catfish, do we have some amazing football this week! Not only do we have four days of NFL action starting today, but we have some absolutely epic matchups while we're stuffing our faces. Not only are there some incredible matchups (ignore the Sunday morning matchups), but many of these matchups will bring clarity to the hierarchy of contenders as this season heads into the final six weeks.

On the appetizers level, Cleveland should absolutely be rocking on Sunday Night Football, as they look to bury the Raiders dim playoff hopes, and take a stranglehold on a playoff spot of their own. In case you didn't hear the spectacular news, Mike McCarthy gets the last laugh, benching Aaron Rodgers and potentially ending one of the weirdest QB/coach relationships in NFL history. Instead, we'll watch the Broncos try to keep pace in the AFC West race, while we're forced to go through the Mason Rudolph experience.

The last of our appetizers will be on Monday Night Football, as the Panthers look to put the pathetic NFC South to bed, as a road win in Tampa could have Carolina actually getting above .500 and locking up a second straight division title. From a Seahawks perspective, there's a path where Seattle may find themselves traveling to Charlotte for wildcard weekend, so even if Joe Buck and Troy Aikman are miserable throughout, this game could play a pivotal role in the Seahawks season.

Now, onto the main courses. Wednesday night we have the Green Bay Packers coming to Los Angeles to take on the Puka-less Rams. Returning to the Packers is superstar pass rusher Micah Parsons. I thought that the Packers looked like a Super Bowl contender at points last year, with Parsons. If he could improve the Green Bay defense even marginally, then the Pack become much more of a threat to stealing a playoff spot from Seattle.

While the Rams continue to be wounded, they're still 6-4 and seemed to morphed into a ground and pound team that is getting better each week defensively. This isn't a loser goes home game, but the loser falls behind a game behind the winner, but also loses the tiebreaker. I may regret what I'm about to say, but I want the Rams to win. The Seahawks need to keep winning, and they control their destiny with two matchups still to come against the Rams, whereas they have no control over what the Packers will do here in the second half.

We may have arguably the greatest duo of games in Thanksgiving history as we'll see potential conference championship previews in both the NFC and AFC. In the 10am window, we have the titans of the North meeting in Detroit as the Lions take on the Bears. Seattle fans have seen what the Bears are capable of, while the Lions continue to win despite continuous stupid injuries. I really think this is arguably the best and biggest matchup of the week.

The Lions are so difficult at home, so I wouldn't be surprised if they win and take the lead in the division race. If the Bears win, that'd be their second statement win on the road, and the NFC could really start to feel like a two team race with the 49ers. With the Bears already defeating the Seahawks, the rooting guide calls for hoping Chicago wins.

The third and final game is another duel between Josh Allen and Patrick Mahomes. Who would've guessed before the season started that Patrick Mahomes would be the one playing at an MVP level, while Josh Allen has been far from his superstar standard, but that has not kept Buffalo from their best 10 game start since 90's super Buffalo years. With Cincinnati and Jacksonville taking brutal losses on Monday night, the winner of this game will become the favorite for the #1 seed in the AFC. I can already hear the Switfies and league offices assembling their army for one last Super Bowl run.

Speaking of Super Bowl runs, there may be no better chance than now for the San Francisco 49ers to finally get over the hump. Another dominating win in a different country, Kyle Shanahan's squad looks like the class of the NFL as Brock Purdy has ascended to legit MVP frontrunner and Fred Warner has returned to being arguably the best defensive player on the planet. They look really tough, but that doesn't mean they're not vulnerable in the biggest game of the week for the Seahawks, and arguably Seattle's biggest game of the season.

The Seahawks are rested, in better shape health wise, and we may be seeing the healthiest version of San Darnold, paired with a running game and defense that seems to be flexing its muscle more and more each week. The 49ers are coming off a weeklong period that saw them win a shootout against the Cowboys, prepare for the Mexico City altitude in Colorado Springs, and then beat the snot out of the Vikings in Azteca. If there's ever a moment where rest and desperation meet a tired team that is sitting pretty with its and comfortable in its current standings, this is it.

There's no dancing around it, this is the defining separation game for the Seahawks. A win with either quarterback likely settles what debate is left between Sam Darnold and Drew Lock, and you could then conceivably make a real case that Seattle, despite a worse record, was the better team in their two matchups vs San Francisco. They'd jump ahead of either the Rams or Packers for the final wildcard spot, and they'd be nipping at the heels of the NFC North contenders for that coveted #5 seed which is the NFC South door all wildcard teams would prefer.

At 7-4, with wins over the Chiefs and 49ers in the past five weeks, I think Seahawks fans should feel good about dreaming big again, even if the path to Super Bowl 61 means a month-long road trip. A loss on Sunday, and reality really begins to set in. Seattle would then fall into the position of nearly needing to win out, just to get into the playoffs as a No. 7 seed. I'm sure a loss would create more questions about the quarterback position, and having been swept in three games by the Bears and 49ers, you have to wonder about the future of this team's contention window.

Either way, we have an incredible week of football and should get some real clarity about the Seahawks, and the league after this weekend.
