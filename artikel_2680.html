Questions & Antlers: Early season impressions, featuring Nets Daily’s Anthony Puccio (Part 2)

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Last week, we introduced you to Nets Daily's Editor-in-Chief, Anthony Puccio, with the premise that he, as a long-time Brooklyn Nets fan and writer, knows what we're going through this season following our beloved Milwaukee Bucks. In response, you came screaming off a high pick and roll, turned the corner—drawing the strongside defender—and hit him seams up in the corner with your questions. As you'll see below, he didn't let you down with the finish. Nylon.

stoneAge: Did you expect the rebuild to take so long and how far away are the Nets from being a playoff team again?

AP: I expected it to take long, but I figured they would have moved some assets to speed this thing up. I was wrong. We're looking at a Nets team currently two-three years away from playoffs—and that's being generous.

stoneAge: Which of the Nets' current players are part of their future core and what do the Nets lack the most to reach the next level?

AP: Mikel Brown Jr., Egor Dëmin, and Nolan Traore. The Nets lack a lot of things, particularly size in the front court. This isn't new. They lack athleticism. They lack three-point shooters. And they lack basic fundamentals required to build on good habits for a sustainable culture.

Mitchell Maurer: How do you navigate a landscape where so many people in the same geographic area are head-over-heels for the Knicks? A title is (as we've seen around here…) a surefire way to attract bandwagon fans, who are as vocal as they are anonymous 'round these Internet parts. The Nets are not a "nothing" franchise but they clearly don't carry the same weight (broadly speaking) as their cross-town counterparts. It's not a fair paradigm, to be considered an "afterthought" and I'm curious if there's similarities with how Bucks fans may have felt back in the heyday of the Packers and/or Brewers (who also just won a title!)

AP: Nets fans don't want to admit it, but there's absolutely an inferiority. Before the Knicks' chip, the one thing Nets fans had on Knicks fans was a better on-court product since 2000. Now we don't even have that! Put it this way: if you tell someone you're a Nets fan in NY, depending on who you tell, they might say, "Who?" Or "Why?"

Terrapin Revisited: What was it like to adjust expectations when the Nets went from title contenders to "Well, what exactly are we now?" after their big trades? Any recommendations for Bucks fans on how to adapt?

AP: There's no way of sugar-coating it—it feels like a dead end. You adapt when you simply learn to accept this is the new reality, and you never know when it might return. My recommendation: lean on the community to talk. It's difficult without picks to look forward to. Expect the worst, hope the best. Don't turn on each other though. (Editor's note: Sage advice—and one of the things I love about Brew Hoop).

Terrapin Revisited: What have you enjoyed most about the Nets during this rebuilding era? In other words, what can fans focus on as far as the bright side of such teams?

AP: Watching people in the organization make a name for themselves, hustling and shining in the shadows of the down years. Ideally it a player, but it can be any personnel. College basketball, too.

Terrapin Revisited: Do you have any insights on what to expect from veterans on the team at this time? A number of Bucks' vets are disappointing this year, I'm not sure how much that is to be expected when the stakes they are playing for are a lot lower vs. how much that provides some useful insights on the vets. What have you seen from different vets on the Nets?

AP: Right now I see two vets on the Nets who want to get their shots up and head out. It feels like they embrace the leadership role until the losses actually pile up.

Terrapin Revisited: What recommendations can you make on how to watch the development of young players on teams like the Nets and Bucks? What might we watch for—and what might we need to set aside—in order to more effectively watch at present, and appreciate what we have?

AP: It's all a matter of expectations and perspective. Seriously. Watch for where guys say they're working on their games.

Pono: Based on the Nets' rebuilding experience, how should the Bucks navigate developing young players with potential and very young players with potential? For context, last season Ryan Rollins emerged as the Bucks' top young player but this season the full-on rebuild approach has altered his role and, early on, stunted his growth.

AP: This is where the G League needs to become a necessity. Get those very young players burn instead of rotting on bench, trying to force their games. Let them have some time dominating at the lower level, or learning, getting stronger, and ready for when the time comes.

BarryDort: What do you think of the new NBA draft lottery? It seems like it puts more bad teams at the mercy of the ping pong balls. Unless you can somehow get a top five pick, it's really hard to find difference makers as you know from the Nets' last three drafts.

AP: Honestly, it doesn't feel like a long term solution to stop tanking.

Nate O.: With six first-round picks the last two drafts and 11 through 2033, it feels like the Nets are a great example of how to stack assets. I'm curious if you feel like this is a position of strength to be operating from, and only good things can unfold, or are there any other moves you wish the Nets had made or concerns you have moving forward?

AP: Concerns. They have eight players on rookie contracts, and getting them all necessary burn to develop has been extremely difficult. Taking five picks in one round felt a little silly at the time, and it's simply a handful for the coaching staff.

Southern Marxist: The Bucks front office has had to totally shift gears from spending any unused asset in an effort to get Giannis a second championship to accumulating and developing talent. Based on your observations of the Nets front office, what advice would you give the Bucks front office?

AP: Don't wait too long and be as transparent as possible. Fans know things aren't easy right now; I think they'd prefer to know what the direction is instead of paying for a product that's headed nowhere—or looks that way. The Nets keep their fans in the dark related to a lot of things here, and it comes across as disrespectful to paying customers.

I can't tell the experts how to do their job but at least communicate with fans. It's such a dramatic dropoff for the base of the brand—the face of it to—are gone. Reassure people that you can get back and how.

And just like that, our second instalment of Questions & Antlers for the season is in the books. A massive shoutout to Anthony for sharing his wisdom on the Nets and what their rebuilding experience might mean for our Bucks fandom.
