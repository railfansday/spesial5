Voters want to 'move on' from the president, former Trump official claims as reason for GOP's midterm defeat

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Former Kansas Republican Sen. Sam Brownback tells Fox News Digital that voters rejected President Trump himself, not his policies, citing inflation and gas prices.

Former Kansas Republican Sen. Sam Brownback, who served as an ambassador during Donald Trump's first term, blamed the president's personal leadership style for Republicans' midterm losses.

He claims the outcome of the 2026 elections — where Democrats' sweeping victories in traditional red strongholds helped them reclaim majorities in the House and Senate — was a sign of voters rejecting Trump more than rebuking his policies.

"I think the thing we're missing is that it was really targeted at President Trump. I don't think it was targeted at the policies so much," Brownback told Fox News Digital in an interview.

"Pride comes before a fall and a haughty spirit before a stumble."

The former Kansas governor argued that Republicans should not write off the midterm results as the typical losses suffered by a president’s party through history during their second term.

Brownback, who represented Kansas in the U.S. Senate from 1996 to 2011 before serving as governor from 2011 to 2018, said economic concerns, including inflation and gas prices, played a role in the election results.

He suggested voters' frustrations extended beyond the economy to Trump's approach to governing.

DEMS SEIZE ON MIDTERM ROUT TO DEMAND ICE REFORM, BUT POLLS REVEAL A TWIST IN WHO PRIORITIZED IMMIGRATION

The Fox News Voter Poll found that 89% of voters considered the cost of living a crucial or major factor in their congressional vote, compared with 74% who said the same about the war in Iran.

The findings suggest broader economic frustrations were also a major concern among voters, although the two issues may overlap and the polling does not establish which ultimately drove Republican defections.

The former Kansas senator and Trump ambassador urged Republicans to embrace humility and kindness ahead of 2028. (Sarah Silbiger/Getty Images)

"People just saying, 'I'm ready to move on past President Trump,'" Brownback said, adding voters still appreciated the administration's efforts to secure the border but viewed immigration enforcement as too aggressive.

The former ambassador-at-large for international religious freedom also urged Republicans to reconsider how they communicate with voters, emphasizing the importance of humility and kindness.

"You just can't be proud. You've got to come forward humbly," Brownback said. "And I think you have to do that with the American public."

Brownback called for a more measured response to voters' concerns ahead of 2028.

"We hear you, American public. We're adjusting to the message that we heard," he said.

"America is great because it's good. If it ever loses its goodness, it will lose its greatness," he added. "There has to be, as I keep doubling back to, a humility and a kindness about it as well."

Both Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio have been floated as potential contenders for the 2028 Republican presidential nomination, with Vance saying he plans to decide whether to launch a White House bid in the spring.
