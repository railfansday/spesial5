Finding Unexpected Joy In Music And Hospitality From Los Angeles To Malibu

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

One of the beautiful things about traveling around the world is the chance to experience hospitality in different ways in different countries. I’ll never forget the hospitality I experienced In Iran or in Ukraine, but sometimes we don’t have to travel far to experience it, as I recently found out in Los Angeles.

I listen to music during the day as I work on this blog and on my other work. Some people require absolute silence to work, but I like music, specially Christian hymns. I primarily use YouTube and of course the algorithm gets to know you. There’s no music I like more than a powerful organ playing a beloved hymn. Lately, the algorithm has been suggesting a very gifted organist named Lauma Akmene, a Latvian-born musician who is the organist, conductor, and music director at St. Peter’s Episcopal Church in Salisbury, Maryland.

As I listened to her music all morning, one of her YouTube shorts noted that she would be performing a classical concert in Los Angeles, which immediately piqued my interest.

I obtained a ticket and found the concert would be held at a place called Stan’s Musical Parlor, which was actually a beautiful 1926 home in LA’s posh Lafayette Square neighborhood. Not knowing quite what to expect, I showed up and was warmly greeted by Stanley Harris himself, who I came to find out has opened his house since 1986 to showcase musical talents. Dr. Harris is a retired psychiatrist and was a longtime clinician and faculty member at the University of Southern California.

Stan has a Johannus-BachMen-Hauptwerk organ (including Hauptwerk virtual pipe organs) in his living room. Essentially, this digital organ can be set to emulate sounds from many of famous pipe organs around the world.

The concert began at 8:00 pm and was simply fabulous, with Lauma playing several classic and neo-classic works.

One of the guests mentioned that he was hosting Lauma the following evening at his home in Malibu, and invited me to attend. I had to be in Palm Springs for work that day, but I drove from Palms Springs all the way to Malibu via Pacific Coast Highway (took about three hours!) and made it just in time for the concert, another fabulous evening of food, fellowship, and great music.

Already, I’ve made friends through these two events, who I am staying in touch with, and will go to more of these concerts. Community is beautiful thing, as is hospitality, and all in my backyard, one of the many reasons I love Los Angeles and Southern California so much.

Truth be told, I almost did not go to either event. I was tired and probably could have used a couple extra hours of sleep. But I’m so glad I did go and I’m thankful to YouTube for suggesting Lauma and making these two possible in the first place.

Lauma is such a gifted organist. If you like classical music and especially sacred music, you can enjoy her work here.
