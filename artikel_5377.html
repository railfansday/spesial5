Your Business Is Losing Money on AI You Don’t Control — Here’s Why.

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

You're viewing the US website. Looking for the Site edition?

Every silent AI update breaks your workflow, and you pay your provider an 85% margin to fix what it broke.

Opinions expressed by Entrepreneur contributors are their own.

As a big believer in a domesticated superintelligence, I can’t pass up the Cheeky Pint podcast where OpenAI CEO suggested it’s a terrible idea to keep processing your personal data on your personal computers because his company’s data centers could process your data more efficiently. He walked the remark back after immediate backlash.It’s hard to recognize, yet your business likely lives in that world of centralization already. When using inference via API, you often rent access to a black box that shifts its behavior, pricing and reasoning effort without your knowledge or consent.

Ask yourself: how many times has your team asked for more budget due to inconsistent behavior from GPT or Claude? In Harvard Data Science Review, Stanford and UC Berkeley researchers Lingjiao Chen, Matei Zaharia and James Zou tested identical prompts on GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 across snapshots three months apart. GPT-4’s prime-number accuracy fell from 84.0% in March 2023 to 51.1% in June, while its chain-of-thought boost collapsed from 24.4% to -0.1%.

Everyday workflows suffered similar drops. On a happy-number task, GPT-4’s accuracy fell from 83.6% to 35.2%, while directly executable code plummeted from 52.0% to 10.0% because extra formatting broke pipelines. Instruction-following on answer extraction collapsed from 99.5% to 0.5% under the same model name and API.

A 2024 Carnegie Mellon and HKUST study by Wanqin Ma, Chenyang Yang and Christian Kästner found that 58.8% of prompt and model combinations lost accuracy after an API update, and 70.2% of those drops exceeded 5 percentage points. Across updates, 10.9% of individual predictions regressed and 63.8% of those failures occurred at maximum confidence, breaking working predictions 87.9% of the time even when overall accuracy rose.

Researchers Emanuele La Malfa and colleagues at Oxford and the Alan Turing Institute warned in a 2025 AAAI paper — first published in Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research — that language-model providers deprecate models on short schedules, collect prompts under commercial licenses and alter serving layers while keeping your pinned model ID unchanged. The end customer takes the hit and is forced to reinvest in adapting to another model that’s not smarter but cheaper to run.

Anthropic is currently defending against a consolidated consumer class action in California federal court (In re Claude Service Degradation Litigation, No. 3:26-cv-07699-RFL) alleging that the company wrongfully degraded its paid Claude Pro ($20/mo), Max 5x ($100/mo), and Max 20x ($200/mo) tiers between March 4 and May 6, 2026. In April 2026, the problem became so obvious that Anthropic had to publish an anti-crisis investigation into Claude Code complaints where it pointed to three changes they intentionally degraded without touching the model. They lowered default reasoning effort from high to medium, introduced a bug that discarded earlier reasoning after idle sessions, and added a brevity instruction that hurt coding output.

In September 2025, Anthropic acknowledged three infrastructure bugs that corrupted responses and misrouted traffic. One routing error grew from 0.8% of requests to 16% of Sonnet 4 traffic and hit roughly 30% of active Claude Code users. Customers paid full price for degraded service, and transparency arrived only after complaints.

The financial incentives behind this opacity are enormous. OpenAI closed a funding round at an $852 billion valuation and discussed a round above $1.2 trillion, while Anthropic prepared for a public listing above $2 trillion alongside roughly $895 billion in non-cancellable compute commitments. High switching costs and forced migrations serve those valuations because every broken workflow requires more API calls to test, more compute for quality assurance and more tokens to rewrite system prompts so your business is not sued by your customers one day for a change in your product you don’t even control.

In 1970, Nobel laureate George Akerlof showed in The Quarterly Journal of Economics how quality uncertainty creates a “market for lemons.” A 2026 CHI study by Alexander Erlei and colleagues at Göttingen, Cagliari, TU Delft and Columbia proved that this trap hits AI buyers. Without disclosure in a high-lemon market, 92.2% of delegations went to low-quality AI systems, whereas partial accuracy disclosure in a low-lemon market cut lemon delegations to 5.5% and raised earnings by 36.4 coins.

Regulators will not fix this soon. While Serena Booth argued at ICML 2025 that consumer protection laws apply to AI, a 2026 FAccT study by Abeba Birhane, William Agnew and colleagues found 249 instances across 100 news articles where AI firms used 27 capture mechanisms to fight oversight. Meanwhile, a 2026 report by Miles Brundage and 47 co-authors noted that frontier AI gets less independent scrutiny than consumer goods, with baseline AAL-1 audits costing $300,000 to $600,000 and AAL-2 reviews exceeding $1 million.
