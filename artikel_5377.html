Guild’s Nora Guerra on helping baby boomers buy and rehab homes with reverse mortgages

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Guild Mortgage’s Nora Guerra said lenders underserve older, equity-rich buyers despite NAR data showing boomers are 42% of buyers and 55% of sellers. She pointed to $15.34 trillion in 62-plus housing wealth and rising use cases for reverse mortgage for purchase transactions.  AI Summary

Baby boomers represent 42% of homebuyers and 55% of sellers, according to the most recent data from the National Association of Realtors (NAR), yet the mortgage industry has yet to fully account for their presence.

That was the message shared last week by Nora Guerra, senior vice president of community lending solutions at Guild Mortgage, during the National Reverse Mortgage Lenders Association (NRMLA)’s Annual Meeting & Expo in San Antonio.

Guerra — who previously worked at Freddie Mac, where she studied how homebuyers change by age group — told the audience that lenders have spent years chasing younger consumers while overlooking older, equity-rich groups who are moving frequently and are in need of help.

“This 55-plus buyer, it’s a main demographic buyer. Everybody has been so focused on the millennial — the older millennial, the younger millennial — and now the Gen Zer,” she said.

Before the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, boomers were largely staying put and had few incentives to move, Guerra said. But that changed quickly as single-family home price appreciation skyrocketed from 3.2% in 2019 to 11.3% in 2021 and 11.9% in 2022, according to HousingWire Data.

That contributed heavily to home equity accumulation, with homeowners 62 and older now holding $15.34 trillion in housing wealth, according to NRMLA and RiskSpan. Additionally, the pandemic caused a rupture in many families’ living situations. “They were sheltered in place. They couldn’t see their loved ones, their grandchildren and their children, and it shook them very hard,” Guerra said.

Each of these groups are likely to need financing to accomplish their goals, she said. Reverse mortgages may offer a more cost-effective solution since they don’t require a monthly payment, and they’re often a fit for applicants who’ve been turned down for other types of mortgage credit.

Reverse mortgage for purchase programs, in particular, can fulfill their needs. These products allow seniors to sell their current home and use the equity to buy a similar or more expensive property without monthly payments or the need to pay in cash. “They really don’t know what options are out there, so they feel no one wants to finance me,” Guerra remarked.

According to NAR research cited by Guerra, senior homebuyers frequently utilize mortgages, but the trend wanes as the buyer grows older. The data shows that among 61- to 70-year-olds, 61% financed their purchase during the year ending in June 2025. The percentage dropped to 54% among 71- to 79-year-olds, and to 48% among those 80 and older.

Guerra said mortgage professionals spend too much time discussing problems and not enough time on finding or developing solutions. This can be particularly unhelpful when working with house-rich, cash-poor clients who have significant equity but little in the way of savings or retirement funds.

With these types of needs-based borrowers, reverse mortgage professionals should be asking about the cash they’re looking to access for both everyday living expenses and unexpected expenses. Develop a cash-flow plan based on what they have in their retirement accounts and other investments.

Whether a client is considering a federally insured Home Equity Conversion Mortgage or a proprietary reverse mortgage, counselors certified by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) are a key resource to tap into. Guerra said she works with more than 200 counselors nationwide who “help educate and hold the hand of your parent, and tell them all about this program and how it works.”

Originators should ask direct questions about the client’s preferences to pay for their next home, identify any assets they don’t want to touch for that purpose, and offer comparisons of what the financial results will look like with a reverse mortgage versus a cash deal.

Family members often fear there will be nothing left for them when their parent or grandparent dies. But planning ahead of time can help heirs to sell the home and keep any proceeds, or to repay the reverse mortgage and continue living there. Some lenders offer private-label solutions for these exact situations.

Guerra also pointed to NAR data showing that 14% of recent home purchases were tied to multigenerational living situations. These clients often need their family involved when making decisions about reverse mortgages. Adult children want to protect their parents’ assets, so originators should include relatives early in the process.

“Mom and dad haven’t bought a home in the last 10 or 15 years due to lack of inventory. Grandma and grandpa haven’t bought a home in the last 15 to 20 years due to the same reason,” Guerra told the audience. “Multigenerational living is what we’re seeing on these 1003 (loan applications) — lots of grandparents cosigning for their millennial and Gen Z grandchildren if they can afford it, but also lots of gift letters.”
