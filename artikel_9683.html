Cyber Insurers See New Divide: AI Defense 'Haves' &amp; 'Have Nots'

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Dark Reading Confidential Episode 23: Cyber insurers share their view of the AI-driven threat landscape and how mounting challenges are putting pressure on both sides of the policy, leaving the current model unsustainable.

Cyber insurance providers are approaching 2027 facing a fundamental challenge. Claims rose 40% in 2024 and another 20% in 2025, even as premiums have stayed largely flat. This trend is something both Coalition and Travelers say isn't sustainable; and at the center of that shift is AI, which is reshaping the threat landscape not by inventing new attack types, but by making existing ones — ransomware, business email compromise, and phishing — dramatically easier to execute at scale.

In this episode of Dark Reading Confidential, Dark Reading media and projects director Becky Bracken and Dark Reading senior news director Rob Wright sit down with Lauren Winchester, vice president of cyber-risk services at Travelers; and Daniel Woods, head of emerging risk at Coalition, to unpack what the data actually shows. The conversation moves past the headline-grabbing surge in disclosed vulnerabilities to a more nuanced picture: exploitation is rising, but not as sharply as disclosure volume, while AI-powered phishing kits are bring sold SaaS-style to criminals with little technical skill, driving a measurable spike in BEC and ransomware activity.

Related:How Southeastern University Got a Handle on Its Data

The two insurers also agree on where the real exposure lies: not necessarily with large enterprises, which have the resources to track threat intelligence and patch quickly, but with small and mid-sized businesses that often lack an in-house IT team altogether. That awareness gap, they argue, is where insurers now have to step in — not just to underwrite risk, but to actively help policyholders defend their organizations against it.
