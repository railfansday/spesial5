Patrick Schwarzenegger Says This Small Detail Makes All The Difference In A Great Martini (Exclusive)

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Three years with one of vodka's oldest families will apparently teach you a few things.

Patrick Schwarzenegger knows a thing or two about a good martini. After three years working with Ketel One and spending time with the family behind the vodka, the White Lotus actor has learned that some people take their cocktails very seriously.

"I'm not that severe," Schwarzenegger told me, although he's developed a few preferences of his own. His biggest? "A frosted glass. A cold glass I think makes all the difference."

I caught up with Schwarzenegger in Schiedam, Netherlands, during a Ketel One-hosted visit to the historic Nolet Distillery, where the family has been making spirits since 1691. The trip was actually his idea. After hearing so much about the distillery from the Nolet family, Schwarzenegger wanted to see everything firsthand, even getting his hands dirty by shoveling coal into the distilling equipment.

According to Schwarzenegger, family members have gotten so particular about their drinks that they'll check where a bar stores its vermouth, olives, and white wine before ordering. If certain ingredients have been sitting out rather than refrigerated, they might opt for a drier martini.

Bob Nolet sees that same attention to detail in the way people order cocktails today. "Today's martini drinkers know what they want," he told Delish, whether that's a classic dry martini, something dirty, or an espresso version.

Of course, that hasn't stopped bartenders from seeing just how far they can stretch the definition of a martini. Schwarzenegger told me he tried The Corner Store’s viral sour cream-and-onion version largely because he'd heard so much about it, something he deemed “really good.”

The Tomatini was another story. He appreciated how convincingly it captured tomato flavor, but the novelty only went so far. "I think that I could have that for a few sips, maybe one max, but then I'm tapped out of that," he said.

His more dependable order is an espresso martini, something the Nolets know particularly well. Schwarzenegger revealed that the family even arranged for espresso machines to be sent to his wedding in Idaho, giving guests a way to enjoy his favorite cocktail during the celebration.

For Carl Nolet Jr., the connection goes deeper than a shared appreciation for espresso martinis. "Patrick comes from a family that is equally committed to their craft in the entertainment space," he told Delish. "You can see that commitment through his career and how he brings his legacy into a new generation."

Carl also credits Schwarzenegger with offering a younger perspective on cocktail culture, particularly how drinking has become part of the way people socialize, host, and celebrate.

For someone who knows his way around a cocktail, Schwarzenegger doesn't actually drink all that often. Between filming, fitness, and work, he's made a rule to keep alcohol to weekends. He's also taken an interest in the growing demand for better-for-you options, pointing to canned cocktails with less sugar and real fruit juice as examples of how people's drinking habits are changing.

That curiosity extends well beyond the bar. With his involvement in the protein snack company MOSH, Schwarzenegger has become the kind of person who considers wandering supermarket aisles a perfectly good Friday night activity. One of his first stops after arriving in the Netherlands was a grocery store, and he loves comparing what's available in different places, from Los Angeles to wife Abby Champion's home state of Alabama.

His Instagram algorithm isn't helping matters, either. Food, drinks, random syrups—if an ad catches his attention, there's a decent chance it'll end up on his doorstep. "I buy everything," he admitted.

Interestingly, all that enthusiasm for trying new things doesn't necessarily translate to elaborate dinners. He and Champion love exploring restaurants together, but they're equally happy cooking at home or keeping things simple. She's a fan of Chick-fil-A and In-N-Out, while he's perfectly content with a plate of chicken and rice.

After talking martinis with Schwarzenegger and the Nolets, I might start paying a little more attention to what happens behind the bar. I'll leave the vermouth inspections to the professionals, but keeping the rest of my martini on ice until I'm ready for a fresh, frosty glass? That's a level of service I could get used to.
