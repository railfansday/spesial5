Burnham urges caution on scrapping 3pm TV blackout

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The blackout originated in the 1960s when Burnley chairman Bob Lord encouraged the EFL to oppose television coverage of 15:00 Saturday matches

Andy Burnham has urged caution on lifting the Saturday 3pm TV blackout in the UK, saying the health of the football pyramid should come first.

The rule was introduced in the 1960s to encourage fans to attend lower league games and prevents matches from being shown on UK television between 2:45pm and 5:15pm on Saturdays.

The blackout, which is enforced by Article 48 of the Uefa Statutes and is imposed by the English and Scottish football associations, comes into effect when 50% of fixtures in the top two divisions are scheduled to kick off at 3pm.

The Premier League is in the middle of a record Â£6.7bn domestic TV deal for Sky and TNT to show up to 270 live games a season.

But there could be room for change with the 3pm rule in the upcoming broadcast cycle, as the rights deals for the Premier League, EFL and SPFL all end in 2028-29.

The prime minister said any decision to remove the ban would require careful thought about the consequences.

"I would urge a bit of caution [on the blackout], because it does protect the income of lower-league clubs," Burnham said.

"If you have effectively wall-to-wall Premier League football at any time, it takes away perhaps the incentive to go and watch the football league or non-league.

"So I think it has to be considered carefully. I'm not going to take the pure consumer view here. I'll take the health-of-football approach to this particular issue."

Only the FA Cup final, which traditionally kicks off at 3pm, is allowed to be broadcast during the blackout window.

In October, the SPFL said it is "flexible about the number of games" that will form part of the TV new deal, which BBC Sport understands includes matches shown on a Saturday afternoon.

Premier League chief executive Richard Masters has previously said a decision about whether the blackout stays in place would need to be made in the future.

But Burnham added: "I would first of all want to protect the football pyramid before I agree to saying 'let's have even more live football'".

"I think it's about protecting the experience with the paying supporter, protecting the football pyramid. Those issues matter to me, first of all."
