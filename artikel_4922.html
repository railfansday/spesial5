Want Headphones Built for Real Workouts? These 9 Survived Runs, Jumps, and Gym-Bag Chaos

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Headphones that can handle as much as you can.

Listening to a hyped-up playlist can make all the difference in your workout. As a fitness writer and longtime distance runner, I’ve seen firsthand how the right headphones can make or break a workout. And I’ve worked my way through a range of workout headphones before I found the ones I don’t want to be without.

I used to constantly fiddle with headphones that didn’t fit right, which is a real motivational buzzkill, and once spent 10 minutes trying to hunt down an earbud that popped out mid-run. (I never recovered it.) I’ve also discovered that some headphones are better for certain types of workouts over others. Case in point: My go-to headphones for strength training at the noisy gym are different from what I reach for when it comes to HIIT and running.

No matter the style and fit you prefer, elements like sweat resistance, battery life, sound quality, and adjustability are crucial, too. And, while I love a good bargain, I’ve learned that you can’t just grab any random pair of cheap headphones or earbuds and expect them to do the job well. After multiple rounds of testing, these are the best wireless workout headphones to consider.

The Apple AirPods Pro have always been a reliable pair of headphones for me, and the latest third generation is even better. Whether I’m biking or struggling through heavy reps, it delivers a premium experience, including impressive sound and a comfortable fit. And the latest edition further proves their God-tier status: heart rate, step, and calorie tracking.

It has a sensor that deciphers your pulse through your ear canals, which then calculates calories burned based on heart rate. I like to keep tabs on my heart rate during high-intensity cardio, so it’s a great alternative to wearing a fitness tracker. Just make sure the fit is right to get an accurate reading (four silicone ear cup sizes are available).

It also has motion sensors that can track your steps. All data transfers to the Apple Health app, so you need to own an iPhone or iPad. Luckily, I recently converted (sorry, Android).

When I’m grinding hard at the gym, I want to hear nothing around me except my hype house music. The active noise cancellation drowns out any grunters, loud gym music, TVs, and general unnecessary background noise. I feel like it’s just me and my music, so that I can laser-focus on the task at hand.

But when I need to maintain awareness during outdoor runs, I turn on transparency mode to hear oncoming traffic and people nearby. I also like that it automatically lowers my music when it detects me talking to someone. It has an IP57 rating, meaning it’s sweat and dust-resistant, and can be submerged in a meter of water for up to 30 minutes in case of any accidental puddle drops (been there). As someone who sweats a lot, each bud repels moisture very well and doesn’t fall out of my ears when jumping or running. Plus, sweat never clogs my ears—something I experienced with regular AirPods. Battery life could be better for all-day wear, but it’s good for workouts (assuming you’re not working out all day). I hit the gym after work, and the power is always drained after an eight-hour day. On the flip side, it’s easy to juice up in 15 minutes with the charging case (it offers up to 30 hours of battery life per charge). To fully restore the battery, make sure to charge overnight. The Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones are an absolute treasure. I own a lot of headphones, but the sound quality always keeps me coming back for more. The over-ear design completely covers my ears, providing A-grade noise cancellation at prime-time hours.

The sound is immersive with balanced, deep bass that doesn’t feel piercing—an issue with lower-quality headphones I tested. I blast my tunes at full volume when getting in the zone, and I never feel uncomfortable.

They look bulky, but looks can be deceiving. They never budge, even when speed walking at an incline or doing fast-paced plyometric drills. I’ve worn it during box jumps and ice skaters, and it stayed put.

Just note it’s not water resistant for intense cardio like spinning and running, where you’ll work up a sweat—although the plush padding rimming the headband and ear cups can easily be wiped clean.

Like the Apple AirPods Pro 3, I have to charge it daily in the included case, so make sure to keep the cable on hand for quick power-ups.

If you really just can’t with the possibility of your earbuds falling out, but still want a compact option, Dr. Dre delivered just what you need: the Beats Powerbeats Pro 2. It has hooks that seamlessly glide over the ears, ensuring a proper fit that stays put. And compared to the previous iteration that launched six years ago, it’s now smaller and lighter, and shares many of the same features as the AirPods Pro 3 (Apple does own Beats, after all), including active noise cancellation, transparency mode, and heart rate monitoring through the Beats app.

I’ve put it to the test during long runs, sprints, low-impact biking, and strength training. The verdict? It performs as sleekly as it looks. The sound is crisp and rich, while the bass is heavy without being overbearing.The sweat resistance is a notch below AirPods with an IPX4 rating, meaning it can withstand light rain but can’t be submerged. I’ve gotten caught in the rain one too many times while wearing these—yet, the sound quality is still the same as it was straight out of the box. And battery life is actually slightly better than AirPods, offering up to 45 hours with the charging case.However, this pair feels more uncomfortable than the Apple AirPods Pro 3. The material feels a little too hard for my liking. If you plan on walking or running for hours, the best overall is the way to go.

The last thing you want is uncomfortable headphones when in the midst of PRing your next barbell squat. Working out is tough enough, so having a pair that’s übersoft will take away some of the pain. Meet the Sony WH-1000XM6—a splurge that’s worth the investment.

Thick foam on the ear cups feels like lying on a pillow, while it also seals sound better for superior noise cancellation. The wide headband is plush and fits securely onto the head without being too tight. MH senior gear and commerce editor Ryan Brower throws it in his gym bag whenever he has strength training on deck. He also appreciates the adjustable smooth slider for a custom fit, calling it a fluid mechanism.

The headphones use 12 microphones to isolate and cancel out sound to deliver an immersive listening experience. Vocals and instruments sound balanced and rich, with Brower calling the sound quality superb. Battery life can run for up to 30 hours, while just three minutes of charging can provide three hours—perfect if you’re in a rush to hit the gym.

Brower’s only gripes are the lack of sweat resistance for high-intensity activities, like running or spinning, and that it feels a little bulky. So, he reserves it for less strenuous pursuits.

If you’re into an over-ear design in headphones, you know they can get hot and sweaty fast. But the Skullcandy Crusher 540 headphones feel surprisingly breathable on—for an over-ear headphone. The ear cups are made from a lightweight material that feels breezy against your ears, minimizing extra sweating while you work out.

These headphones also have an impressive battery life of 40 hours, which is only second on our list to the Powerbeats Pro 2. Skullcandy clearly designed these headphones for convenience, with plenty of buttons within easy reach. As a result, you can quickly turn the volume up or down without missing a beat. If active noise cancellation is a must for you, scroll up—while the sound quality on these headphones is excellent, they won’t help you fully tune out the world.

The Skullcandy Crusher 540 headphones don’t have an official IPX rating, but they have a special sweat- and water-resistant nano-coating to protect your gear. (The ear cup pads are also removable for easy cleaning.) A big draw of these headphones is the strong bass, which comes in handy during tough workouts. As someone who loses nearly everything, I appreciate the built-in Tile technology to help you find your headphones with the Tile app if you misplace them.

I first tested the Bose Open Ultra Earbuds last fall, and they’ve been my favorite pair for distance running ever since. These earbuds aren’t going anywhere thanks to a design that’s similar to a clip-on earring. Just gently hook these on your ears and move on. The style is perfect for people like me who have been burned in the past by an earbud falling out and vanishing forever.

As an early morning runner who regularly crosses streets, I appreciate the open-ear audio these earbuds deliver. I can hear my music perfectly, but still can detect things like chirping birds to give me a solid awareness of my surroundings. You can adjust the volume, skip songs, and flip between immersive and stereo sound with a tap on your earbuds, making it easy to switch things up mid-run.

A heads up: If you’ve never used an earbud style like this in the past, you may need to give your ears a little time to build up to wearing them. My ears were a little sore when I first started wearing the Open Ultra Earbuds, but I don’t even feel them when they’re on now. These earbuds are also IPX4 rated for water resistance, so they do just fine with light splashes or rain—just make sure you don't wear these to swim laps.

The earbuds have a seven-hour battery life, which is lower than several others on this list. But I’ve never had an issue with them dying on me. I just pop them in their charging case when they’re not in use.

Some people are going to struggle to get earbuds to fit, no matter what they do. If you have smaller ears and you’re over flipping through different ear tips in hopes something will fit, the Beats Powerbeats Fit are a solid choice. These earbuds actually fit inside your ears like a hearing aid with the help of flexible wingtips that really lock them in there.

These earbuds are small, but they deliver the top-tier sound quality you’ve come to expect from Beats. That includes the options to flip between active noise cancellation and transparency modes, so you can use these to tune out loud gym-goers or to be aware of your surroundings during outdoor workouts.

These earbuds pair seamlessly with Apple and Android phones, making them an equal opportunity headphone. They also have an IPX4 rating for sweat and splash resistance, so you can wear them in the rain, but not to swim laps. While the Powerbeats Fit comes with four different tips, your ears will still need a little time to get used to these before wearing them on, say, a 10-miler.

The earbuds do kind of jam in there, and it can take some time to get used to the feel of the wingtips. But if you’re looking for a discreet pair of headphones that aren’t going anywhere, you can stop your search here.

If you want secure earbuds that aren’t an investment, the Anker Soundcore Sport X20 fits the bill. It costs a third of the Beats Powerbeats Pro 2 and shares the same ear-hook design that doesn’t budge when pushing your limits during high-impact workouts. The sound is shockingly premium given the $80 price tag. If I were doing a blind test, I’d think it costs at least $200.

The strong noise cancellation blocks out every peep imaginable at the gym. From loud gym music to weights slamming, I can’t hear a thing except my jams. But when I don’t want to be super locked in, I switch to “normal” mode so I can listen to background noise. This mode is handy when running or walking at night. And when things get too wet during intense grinding sessions, the IP67 sweat-resistance saves me. Although I haven’t tested it while swimming, it can withstand water submersion beyond one meter for about 30 minutes.

The silicone ear hooks are soft, flexible, and extremely comfortable. I find myself reaching for it outside of the gym and often forget it’s even on my ears, which I’m not mad about—it makes a great cool-girl accessory. The only downside of wearing it for too long is that the battery will drain after about eight hours. Luckily, the charging case has two days' worth of battery life—so no excuses to skip a workout.

The Shokz OpenRun Pro 2 are my go-to for long-distance running because of the wrap-around and open-ear design that stays in place. It’s no surprise it’s a TikTok-viral favorite among runners and our editors.

The sweat-resistance rating is one notch below our best overall, with an IP55 score, meaning it can withstand intense rain during rough outdoor conditions. Moisture from sweat is never an issue since the design hangs over the ear and doesn’t go in the canal. It even alerts me with a vibrating beep if moisture is present in the port while charging.

If blocking out noise is your priority, you should consider other options, as this pair doesn’t offer noise cancellation. I don’t love that I can’t be fully engaged in my music, but that’s the trade-off for running safely. Unlike other headphones I tested in this guide, the sound emits outside of the eardrum using bone conduction technology, which is when noise is transmitted through vibrations in the skull to reach the ear. I hear every sound, from people talking to traffic, so I give it a perfect score for maintaining awareness. I deem it an essential pair if you train early in the mornings or at night. Just note it’s not going to be the same listening experience as the Apple AirPods Pro 3, for example.

The battery life is nowhere near the capacity of the Apple AirPods Pro 3, with only about 12 hours of playtime, so I find I have to charge it weekly for an hour. The flip side is that it only takes 10 minutes to get two hours of battery life if I quickly need it before a run.

Full disclosure: They come in two sizes, but tend to run large, which is why I went with the mini size. I ordered the standard size first and had to return them (small head probs).

If you plan on running, spinning, or doing other high-intensity training, it’s best to have headphones that can withstand sweat. They should have an IP rating, which is a protection score for how well electronics can resist water. “IPX4 or higher is perfect for working out,” says Joe Smith, an audio specialist at Music Critic. All options on this list have at least an IPX4 rating.

But our best budget option, the Anker Soundcore Sport X20, has an even better score of IP67, meaning it can handle being submerged in just over one meter of water for 30 minutes. If headphones aren’t made to withstand sweat, it’s best to reserve those for lower-intensity workouts.

Having your headphones die during a half-marathon is not the vibe (been there). Using a pair with good battery life is crucial to ensuring the tunes are always going strong during a long workout. With select pairs, it can be dependent on whether or not select features are in use.

For example, the total playtime for the new Apple AirPods Pro 3 from a single charge (with case) is up to 10 hours with active noise cancellation turned off, but only up to eight hours with it turned on. Carl Prouty, a tech expert at Abt, recommends snagging a model that charges fast in case you forget to plug them in the night before.

Noise cancellation is a function that utilizes a microphone hidden inside the earbud or earcup to analyze frequencies that emit opposite sound waves, thus helping to reduce background noise. Blocking out surrounding noise is great for staying focused and immersed, but it’s not the safest feature in desolate areas like quiet trails, busy streets, or at night.

I prefer an open-ear style to maintain awareness during outdoor runs. If you’re not a fan of that design, other options on this list allow you to turn noise cancellation on and off. “If you still want to be able to hear what's going on around you while also listening to music, look for a model with a good ambient and pass-through mode,” says Prouty.

You have a lot to choose from in a workout headphone, and each has its pros and cons. Earbuds rest inside your ears to deliver sound without bulk. These tend to fit comfortably, but may pop out if you don’t get the fit right. Ear hook earbuds add an extra layer—a hook that goes over your ears—to help combat this issue. There are also open-style headphones that hook over your ears and around the back of your head to keep them in place.

A newer category of these is open earbuds that clip over your ear like a clip-on earring, while nestling into your ear like an earbud. Finally, over-ear headphones have soft cups that fit over your ear for a classic look. While these usually do a good job staying in place, they may feel hotter during intense workouts than other designs.

As a distance runner, I rely on good music to keep me hyped up and entertained on long runs. I’ve tested dozens of pairs of wireless headphones for working out over the past decade and learned what features make pairs stand out. Only workout headphones with good sweat resistance, battery life, noise cancellation, and sound transparency made the final cut.

Because everyone has different needs in a workout headphone, I also leaned on experiences from Women’s Health editors, along with Men’s Health senior gear and reviews editor Ryan Brower. Each pair was used in different environments during cardio and strength training sessions to see how they performed when put to the test during workouts. Those included 5- to 10-mile runs, indoor cycling workouts, HIIT sessions, and more general lifting at the gym.
