Volkswagen’s new electric Jetta offers 285 miles of range for under $12K

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Volkswagen is offering its Chinese-market Jetta M6 electric sedan with nearly 290 miles of CLTC-rated range for less than $12,000 (USD), and if that’s not what “der people’s wagen” should be all about, I don’t know what is.

Launched as a joint venture between FAW and Volkswagen Group, the Jetta brand’s first battery electric car is finally here – and it is a genuinely accessible, capable, and kinda decent-looking family sedan in the same way VW’s original Type I was when it was first developed in the 1940s.

The new Jetta M6 was launched earlier this in three variants: the 465 Pro, 555 Max and 555 Ultra; each priced at 79,800 yuan (~$11,800), 89,800 yuan and 99,800 yuan, respectively, with the model numbers corresponding to the car’s estimated CLTC range in kilometers (which maths out around ~285 and 345 miles, if you’re curious).

Built on FAW’s Jiesi architecture, the M6 measures 4,806 mm long and 1,868 mm wide, putting it right in that sedan sweet spot between the Civic/Corolla and Accord/Camry crews, and within a few mm this way and that of the Tesla Model 3, so it should have plenty of room for young professionals and families – but the M6′ real selling point is its tech.

Inside, the Jetta M6 offers an 8.88″ driver display along with a big 15.6″ central touchscreen that integrates Chinese AI assistants Doubao and DeepSeek and ships with Apple CarPlay baked in. Advanced ADAS with, “intelligent cruise assistance, automatic emergency braking, and automated parking” is standard.

The entry-level “465 Pro” version of the Jetta M6 comes equipped with 42.9 kWh LFP battery and a 113 kW (~150 hp) electric motor driving the front wheels, a combination the company says is good for a 8.3-second sprint to 100 km/h (62 mph). Stepping up to the 555 versions gets you a larger 51.9 kWh battery and more powerful 145 kW (~195 hp) motor that cuts that sprint to 6.8 seconds.

Both the 42.9 and 51.0 kWh batteries are lithium iron phosphate deals supplied by CALB, with charging from 20% to 80% taking about 30 minutes.

There are parts of the US where a car like this would be perfect -- it's got enough range for the daily commute and short weekend day trips. Not for everyone, but for enough people that it would be a big deal.

Would those features and performance numbers be enough to get you to look at something like this in the US, even at twice the price? It seems to me that North America’s VW dealers need all the help they can get, and a volume leader like this with a “back to basics” ad campaign that plays on a different kind of nostalgia – nostalgia for a time when people could afford a Jetta, maybe? – could be a winner.
