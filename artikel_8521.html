Babystep:

What is your concern about TIPS? They have the advantage in that they are inflation protected, which is the biggest threat to nominal treasuries.
David,

Thanks. That is a good question.

I am not really concerned about inflation in this context. My concern is that nominal Treasuries and TIPS ultimately share the same underlying U.S. government risk.

For example, imagine that in 2036 the U.S. is facing a severe debt or interest-cost problem and the government changes the CPI methodology or otherwise changes how inflation adjustments are calculated. TIPS would still depend on the government's rules and ability to honor those obligations.

So I am thinking less about nominal Treasuries versus TIPS, and more about whether I want 100% of my fixed-income safety allocation tied to the U.S. government. I am considering whether it might make sense to diversify between U.S. Treasuries (some nominal and some TIPS) and something like BNDX or another high-quality non-U.S. bond option.

I hope that explains my rationale a little better.

Also, if you have not seen my other rationale for why I do not think TIPS are needed until closer to retirement, please take a look here. Vanguard's approach also seems broadly consistent with this.
