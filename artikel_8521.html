I know she mentioned one of the funds (no individual stocks) she is in a while back and it has a load, etc.. so that likely is where the biggest red flag is, but not where I want to start helping. She has about $120k in that according to her.
you've been given some good ideas with what to do with cash, if she doesn't want to invest it. But you should also look at the overall allocation because maybe her portfolio won't keep up with inflation if most of it is in cash. For instance, you didn't say how the $120k is invested in her IRA with her advisor, but even if we assume it's all in stocks (probably shouldn't be, but more on that in a sec) then she still only has 16% in stocks ($120k in IRA in stocks and $600k in cash ($700 - $100k you used to pay off the mortgage).

And that's IF she has 100% in stocks in her IRA. If it's less, she has very little in stocks and is going to get a low rate of return, one that might not keep pace with inflation. You didn't say how much income she has with SS and pension but let's say she's in the 22% (my mom is and she has pension + SS) then even if she's earning 4% (which is better than 1.7% so good for you to do that) she could lose 25% between fed and state (my State's income tax is 3.07%) which means the after tax return would be 3% which is lower than inflation (this year). Which is why we hold some amount in stocks, usually 30% (at least that's how/why target date funds are designed). With TIPS, Bernstein and McQuarrie, showed in a recent article it's not as necessary to take risk but anyway, that's standard advice for a conservative portfolio for many seniors (investor know thyself).

So for starters I think you DO need to know how the IRA is invested with the advisor for a few reasons:

1. you want to know what her overall allocation is and you can't if you don't know how IRA is invested

2. you want to know what the ONGOING fees are (sure he or the fund company took 5.75% likely to put her in a load fund) but there's a possibility of high ongoing fees that dwarf that one time upfront load. My mom had a friend who was touting in 2013 a high yield muni bond fund he put his money into. He thought he was being smart because 10 year bonds in 2013 were only earning around 2.35%. Never mind the fact that he paid a 5.75% load to get into the fund (he likely wasn't aware of), was in an undiversified bond fund (munis ONLY), was taking risk with bonds (high yield is "junk bonds") but he was also paying 1.5% expense ratio year after year (also unaware) for a bond fund. So you just don't know what's going on.

Let me put it this way: You can't just try to improve her cash but let her get raked over the coals on her IRA. It doesn't make sense from a holistic approach. You're not wanting to push due to getting push back either from her or the advisor and I'm sure you'll find your moment to address it, but I think it does need to be addressed. She's likely losing a lot to fees and likely losing much more than she's losing in a low yielding bank account. My two cents.

3. If the IRA is all stock it's tax inefficient. She should have her IRA in bonds and in a taxable brokerage you could swap some cash for stocks to end up with the same AA overall. You don't know how her IRA is allocated. It could be all bonds and then she has 0% stocks. You don't know what you don't know.

let us know how it goes.
