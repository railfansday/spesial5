Michael Burry Shorted Nvidia Stock. Is He About to Make Another Fortune?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Burry got famous for successfully shorting the mortgage market ahead of the 2008 mortgage crisis.

Michael Burry gained popular fame after author Michael Lewis chronicled his prescient shorting of the mortgage market ahead of the subprime mortgage crisis in his book The Big Short, which later was made into a movie of the same name.

Burry made hundreds of millions for himself and his investors thanks to that bet. Some years after closing the hedge fund he ran during the 2000s, he opened a new one called Scion Asset Management, which he closed down in 2025.

Michael Burry. Image source: Getty Images.

Now, he’s only investing his own money, so he's no longer required to report his positions quarterly to the Securities and Exchange Commission. But he still offers detailed trade info and his personal musings through his Substack and on X. He has been outspoken against the artificial intelligence (AI) trade, and he has specifically cited Nvidia (NVDA -0.86%) many times, likening how it operates to the mafia.

He has held a short position in Nvidia for some time, and with that stock down sharply since peaking in early October, he could be making another fortune.

Burry has been critical of Nvidia's circular financing deals for years.

The chipmaker's take is that there are many small companies that don't have the money to buy its top-of-the-line graphics processing units (GPUs), the foundational hardware for AI development, so it provides them with financing so that they can have a place at the table. It predominantly does this in two ways: It acts as a backstop for third-party financing, and it invests in some clients, providing money in exchange for equity stakes in those smaller companies. The most well-known of these arrangements is probably with CoreWeave (CRWV +0.01%), in which Nvidia has invested more than $2 billion.

Burry noted that the U.S. Department of Justice was already investigating Nvidia for several actions, including its acquisition of AI development platform Hugging Face. He has likened Nvidia's business model to a "protection racket," alleging that it forced clients to buy its products and abused its dominant position in the AI chip space.

Burry first disclosed a short position in Nvidia through public filings for Scion Asset Management last year, just before he shut the hedge fund down. Since then, he has bought put options on Nvidia due in December, but closed out that position in September. According to his Substack, he still has a short position in Nvidia. In the meantime, he has turned to shorting other AI stocks and exchange-traded funds.

Nvidia stock is down 32% since it hit a high on Oct. 6, and it's very possible that Burry is making a fortune right now.

The lesson for investors is to consider all risks before deciding on a position, or the size of a position, even if it's in a popular stock.
