Knicks Bulletin: ‘I was raised by Wolves’

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

I thought yesterday's game was over by the end of the third quarter…

…only for these fools to nearly drop it. Good thing, they did not.

Here's a bunch of quotes coming off Minnesota as the boys return home once and for all.

On finishing the road trip with a victory over Minnesota:

"Great to finish off a road trip, getting a win in a pretty tough environment by a well-coached team. It'll make the plane ride a little bit better going home."

On the Knicks' fourth-quarter offensive collapse against the Timberwolves:

"Our execution the first three quarters was at a pretty high level against this team. And then you give them credit. Fourth quarter, they did a phenomenal job defensively, kind of bogged us down. The ball and the pace offensively in the frontcourt wasn't popping like it was the first three quarters."

On the Knicks winning the battle for loose balls despite struggling offensively in the fourth quarter:

"The thing that saved us was winning the 50-50 ballgame. We were 13-for-14 in that area, and that was huge for us because we had trouble scoring in the fourth."

On beating his former team as an NBA champion with the Knicks:

"I was raised by Wolves, but it was Knicks in 5 at the end."

On why the Knicks don't need to win games convincingly:

"For 'Dancing with the Stars.' For us, at the end of the day, as long as we win, this team is just trying to stack the number on the left side."

On the importance of winning games in different ways:

"If you wanna win a championship, you gotta be ready to win in any way possible, because you never know what the game will be."

On his uncertain contract situation and remaining focused on his responsibilities to the Knicks:

"I got a job to do, at the end of the day. I gotta come here and put that jersey on and do what's best for my brothers and the guys they have in my locker room. So, I'm just here to do my job."

On refusing to let his contract situation distract him from performing:

"I gotta put that jersey on and put the best performance out there for my teammates. So, that's all I really think about."

On his emotional attachment to Minnesota:

"I still have so much love for this place. It doesn't go away. I think it's actually grown as I've been away. I think that, even for me and my dad, we were just driving around the city and just going to places. We went to eat at a spot we love to go here. It always hits home. This is the last place that I got to play basketball with my mom. I got to see the kids be young before now. Jolani, my niece, who was in my rookie of the year photo actually in here, she was a baby, and now she's in high school. It's a different part of life, but it's a part of life that I really enjoy."

On returning to Minnesota as an NBA champion:

"It's always great to be home and to be back here. Nine years of my life, some amazing years, amazing moments. Yeah, it's special to be back. Obviously, it's bittersweet to come back with a championship because I wanted to win it here so bad for the city, for Minneapolis, for Minnesota, for all the people who haven't seen an NBA championship here. But it's great to be able to come back and to be able to see friends, people I call family here."

On the release of the new Grand Theft Auto game and his excitement to play it:

"I haven't played the game yet, but I've heard it's a hell of a game. So shout-out to Rockstar [Games] and 2K and Take-Two [Interactive]; they did a good job with that one. It's fun, man. The world is taken over by 'GTA,' as it rightfully should be."

On his Grand Theft Alvarado nickname and his history with the video game:

"I was a big fan of it before my name. It was just a cool name that I had, so it makes it even better when I play the new one. Got the name for it; now, play the game."

On how much his life has changed since GTA V dropped:

"Thirteen years ago, I wouldn't even be talking to you, I promise you that. It's a long journey, fun journey, one hell of a ride. Still with it."

On the Knicks nearly blowing their lead against Minnesota:

"It was hectic. We gotta get better at finishing a game like that. But it's a win, 3-1 on the road trip. We'll take it."

On the criticism Karl-Anthony Towns faced during his Timberwolves years:

"Particularly when he started his playoff journey, how much flak he had gotten."

On Towns leading the Wolves to the Western Conference Finals:

"We don't do it without him in all facets of the game, whether it be his defense in the Phoenix series, the only guy who held us in the game in Game 7 in Denver when we had nothing else going until we could find it in the second half. Those are the little moments that spring to mind. There are many others along the way. And he kind of grew into that and then was able to build on that with an experienced team where he fit into in New York."

On watching Towns succeed in the Knicks' championship run:

"It was fun to see him at the heart of it, too. Especially as they were kind of tweaking their offense going along and all of his skills came to the surface through that."

On the similarities between Towns and LaMelo Ball:

"They play with a lot of expression to their game and people interpret that or they don't like that. But it doesn't mean that A) they're not super talented players, of course, and B) they can't play winning basketball. Sometimes it's as much of the environment that people might be judging them unfairly through, if that makes sense.

"They both are really selfless in the way that they play. But maybe some of their expressions and the way that they carry themselves on the court draws a lot of attention and they get judged unfairly for that, too. They're actually incredibly selfless. They both play with a lot of joy.

"They're incredible people. Just great teammates, fun to be around, always bring the same energy every day. Very coachable."
