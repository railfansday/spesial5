Nvidia vs. SpaceX: The AI Revolution Is Big Enough for Both. Here's the Better Buy.

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Nvidia and Space Exploration Technologies are two companies at the heart of the artificial intelligence trade.

By Bram Berkowitz – Nov 23, 2026 at 4:32PM EST

Few, if any, forces will reshape the economy in the coming years like artificial intelligence. Companies are spending hundreds of billions of dollars on the chips, data centers, and the models that power it.

Two very different businesses sit near the center of that build-out. Nvidia (NVDA +31.48%) designs the semiconductors that power and train most AI systems. Meanwhile, Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +4.35%) is working to become one of the key hyperscalers providing AI compute capacity, not to mention its plan to be a key pioneer in the space economy.

Both stocks have slumped from their highs, and Wall Street remains divided on their respective trajectories. While the AI revolution is big enough for both of these stocks, let's take a look at which is the better buy.

SpaceX runs three businesses. Its space segment leverages fully reusable rockets for space exploration, but it will also serve as the backbone of many of SpaceX's future ambitions.

But SpaceX's fastest-growing business, and the one with the most financial potential, is its Super Intelligence (SI) division. SI houses the social platform X, Grok intelligence, the company's data centers, and a future terafab facility.

SpaceX appears poised to eventually join Amazon, Microsoft, and Alphabet as a hyperscaler, powering the AI revolution. SpaceX reported 1.7 gigawatts (GW) of computing capacity last quarter and expects to reach 2.5-3 GW by year-end.

SpaceX founder and CEO Elon Musk also said on the company's recent earnings call that he's highly confident the company will bring an additional 10 GW of compute online in 2027 and floated a much larger figure as a possibility. Adding 10 GW of compute in 2027 would be an extraordinary achievement.

However, one could imagine that this kind of build-out will be extremely capital-intensive. SpaceX expects to spend $35 billion to $40 billion on capital expenditures in the fourth quarter alone.

SpaceX stock is not cheap, trading around 50x Wall Street's projected adjusted earnings per share. Analysts also expect revenue to jump from about $49 billion this year to $181 billion in 2027, nearly quadrupling. According to S&P Global Market Intelligence, Wall Street consensus estimates also project adjusted negative free cash flow of roughly $162 billion in 2027.

Meanwhile, SpaceX's longer-term ambitions, such as large-scale orbital data centers, depend on its fully reusable super-heavy-lift rocket, Starship, becoming operational and making regular trips into space.

No company has made a more critical contribution to the AI trade than Nvidia. Its chips serve as the lifeblood of AI models. Not only are Nvidia chips viewed as the best for general-purpose computing, but Nvidia's CUDA programming platform has been used by the majority of developers for nearly two decades.

CUDA can only be used with Nvidia hardware. This makes switching or developing programs on other platforms more difficult and creates a strong moat for the company.

Nvidia also continues to put up stellar results. Last quarter's revenue more than doubled to $115.8 billion, while the company's gross margin held near 75%. Management also guided for 90% annual revenue growth to $130 billion in the current quarter, topping analyst estimates.

However, the stock has been incredibly volatile since the scandal surrounding Jensen Huang came to light. Huang resigned on Nov. 12 following reports of multiple alleged extramarital affairs, including one reportedly with a woman who may have been a Chinese spy and who allegedly set up a network that illegally diverted Nvidia chips to China.

On Nov. 23, Nvidia's board of directors surprisingly reinstated Huang as CEO. A big question that remains is how much of Nvidia's prior revenue, if any, may have been illegally diverted to China. What could this mean for future revenue projections if regulators crack down on such sales, as they already have begun to do?

Lawmakers have opened investigations, and Nvidia plans to finish its own independent investigation regarding whether Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort.

While Huang's reinstatement triggered a furious rebound in Nvidia stock, it's still down 12% this year. There are still risks, depending on what new information comes to light or what investigations find.

But Nvidia still makes an industry-leading product in high demand among customers in the hottest industry on the planet right now. Even after the recent rally, the stock still trades at slightly over 10 times its projected adjusted fiscal 2028 sales.

While both SpaceX and Nvidia carry risks, and I wouldn't necessarily get overly aggressive on either, Nvidia is the better buy right now due to a valuation that has likely priced in a significant portion of the risk. SpaceX has tremendous potential, but I suspect it will struggle to balance growth with the need to continue raising significant capital.
