The Home Office underspent its Prevent counter-terror scheme budget by nearly a third last year, despite the number of referrals reaching record levels, the public spending watchdog has said.

The National Audit Office (NAO) said the Home Office underspent its £23.4 million Prevent budget by 28% for 2025-26, continuing a six-year pattern in which it underspent by an average of £5.6 million (17%) a year in real term, PA reported.

The government-led, multi-agency programme aims to stop individuals becoming terrorists. It has faced significant criticism after it failed to intervene in the case of Southport attacker Axel Rudakubana, who had been referred three times.

Demand for Prevent has grown significantly with referrals increasing year on year since 2020, and reaching a record 10,293 in the year ending September 2025.

Officials believe the referral spike might be down to more awareness of Prevent after the Southport Attack in July 2024.

Ministers will face a legal challenge over plans to convert a Ministry of Defence site near the Oxfordshire village of Piddington into accommodation for more than 1,200 detained asylum seekers.

It emerged on Thursday that Angela Rayner, the housing and communities secretary, has approved full planning permission, a decision welcomed by the Home Office, who want to end the use of asylum hotels.

Tim McNally, the local parish council chair, told BBC Radio 4’s Today Programme there would be “many actions” by villagers, and that ministers should “absolutely” be braced for a legal challenge, which would need to be lodged within the next six weeks.

Ministers have faced opposition over the plans from villagers, who even made a symbolic attempt to declare the village independent from the rest of the country after a referendum of its 350 residents. Asked how the village would respond, McNally said: “There’s going to be many actions, you will see. We will disclose that as we move forward.”

Asked if ministers should be braced for a legal challenge, he said: “Absolutely, absolutely.”

There will be a “growing pressure” for parliament to scrutinise EU legislation under new deals on food and emissions to be sealed ahead of the UK’s summit with the bloc next week, a Lords’ committee has warned.

Prime minister Andy Burnham is due to meet EU leaders in Brussels next week and sign agreements that he says could raise GDP by 0.4%. This includes a sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) deal on food and farming, as well as one on emissions trading and another for a youth mobility scheme.

A deal is also expected to be struck on a health security agreement to help address shortages of drugs such as antibiotics and ADHD medication, which have got worse since Brexit, according to a draft text seen by the I newspaper.

It will mark a significant shift in the Britain’s post-Brexit relationship with the EU as they will require dynamic alignment, the process of matching laws to the bloc’s evolving legislation, the House of Lords European Affairs Committee said in a new report.

The committee of peers says this will mean parliament needs to set up a “robust, consistent and adequately resourced cross-departmental system” to manage the UK’s dynamic alignment commitments. See the published report here.
