13 Clever Details I Saw at the 2026 Kips Bay Decorator Show House in Dallas

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Check out the first ever home gym in Kips Bay Show House history.

The seventh annual Kips Bay Decorator Show House in Dallas returns to 2999 Turtle Creek Boulevard, but don’t expect it to look anything like it did in 2025. A new group of the country’s leading designers has taken on the 25,000-square-foot French Renaissance estate, transforming each room to showcase bold ideas, highlight exceptional craftsmanship, and reveal the upcoming trends we can expect to see in the world of interior design.

Visit the show house and see each of the 22 designers’ rooms, from the first dedicated home gym in Kips Bay history by Dallas-based Platinum Gym Design, to a small but mighty European-inspired kitchen by Stephenson Home, to a 1970s-inspired primary bathroom fit with a glass brick shower and curtained-off water closet by Nuance Interiors. With sponsors like Benjamin Moore, The Shade Store, The Rug Company, and more, the vibrant and personality-filled spaces come as no surprise.

As always, proceeds from the show house go to the Kips Bay Boys & Girls Club, which provides educational, enrichment, and developmental programs to thousands of young people, as well as Dallas-based nonprofits Dwell with Dignity and The Crystal Charity Ball.

To see this celebration of exceptional design, you can purchase tickets here and visit the house from Saturday, November 14th to Sunday, December 6th. However, if you can’t make it to the Lone Star State in time, check out some of our favorite moments from the event below.

Kelsi Lamberth of Nuance Interiors pulled inspiration from the 1970s for her primary bathroom, separating the shower from the main prep space using a curved glass brick wall. The sculptural aspects can be found throughout in this groovy space.

In the primary bedroom, Troy Rivington of Rivington Marx Interiors cocooned the room in a dark floral wallpaper and accessorized with other moody botanicals throughout. He was inspired by the romance of 1940s Cairo, dressing the room up like it belonged to a well-traveled collector.

As the first ever dedicated home gym at the Kips Bay Show House, Peter Hoffman of Platinum Gym Design chose a refined, luxurious setup. He named the room Tanren after the Japanese art of hammering and refining metal, and used walnut in both the equipment and the ceiling slats, stainless steel frames, and brown leather accents to complete the exercise space.

In this bespoke bathroom, Russ Peters and Bill Cates of Peters Cates Design lined the wall behind the Studio McGee x Kohler bathtub with a rich merlot tile by RIAD Tile. It’s a beautiful statement and a fabulous way to bring more texture and dimension to the room.

In this tucked away library space, filled with collected finds, designers Christina Garcia Lysaught and Grayson Knight of Layered Dimensions finished off this dark space with a stunning statement mirror. Around the perimeter are resin busts of wildlife, like owls and deer, bringing in a bejeweled touch to the room.

The small but mighty kitchen, designed by Katie Stephenson of Stephenson Home, is crowned with a crackled-gold blooming wallpaper, bringing a warm balance to the rest of the room. It’s covered in birds, butterflies, and blossoms, and it wraps the cook space in a golden glow, reminding all who enter that this room should be as beautiful as it is functional.

The citron paint color wrapping the walls in Jen Bienvenu’s entrance hall is undoubtedly enchanting and serves as the perfect backdrop for her “After the Hunt” theme, but it’s the lampshades made from old silk saris that tie everything together for us. They provide such a magical warmth and really capture the essence of a collector’s treasure room.

Step through the citron entry and enter the round room, designed in collaboration by Helen Bergin Interiors and Lichten Architects. The walls are softened by long curtains from The Shade Store, which create beautiful juxtaposition with the curved, sculptural silver media console by Asher Gillman. The actual turntable is flush to the surface, giving it a seamless look while it emits wonderful sound.

In the Withdrawing Room by Jen Stevens of Fonde Interiors, plaster botanicals by artist Amanda Norman come to life as they crawl from the mantel along the chimney breast, escaping to the ceiling. The vines were handmade by Norman and capture the essence of a garden left undisturbed, showing that this formal-at-first-glance room has a wilder side.

The rosy palette in designer Jen Dean’s—founder of Jede Interiors—grand bathroom is topped off with a hand-painted floral mural that covers the ceiling. The rose-gold shine reflects the natural light in the room and give it more depth, while the natural motifs spread across the rest of the space.

Inside this observatory bedroom by sisters Amy Mobley and Katie Norrid of City Hill Design, the Verellen bed is raised up from the ground on a custom platform, emphasizing the importance of the furniture’s role in this space meant for relaxation and rest. It’s wrapped in a striped silk carpet that beckons your touch with a chartreuse mohair sofa at the foot.

Colorful and collected, this vibrant library by designer Betsy Wentz is not visually quiet. The maximalist space is covered in pattern-mixed Schumacher from ceiling to floor, and the designer pumped up the volume by opting for this triangular table in this tucked away corner.

In designer Amy Switzer’s textural bar lounge—filled with plush chairs, unique artwork, large tassel accessories, and beaded cigarettes—it’s the stone details that really make the room sing. Onyx cornices crown the windows, blue agate inlays decorate the floor, and a banquette which curves around a custom stone and wood table bring a natural coolness to the room that only stone can bring.
