These Are the Home Trends Gaining Ground for 2027, According to Zillow

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

A new analysis of millions of for-sale listings reveals the features sellers are increasingly highlighting.

Above: A color-drenched den on Long Island, New York, designed by General Assembly.

Homes are getting smarter, but sellers are also advertising places to unplug. Zillow’s 2027 Home Trends Report finds growing mentions of both AI and tech-free spaces, along with color-drenched rooms, home offices, and “catios.”

The report tracks year-over-year changes in mentions of hundreds of features across millions of for-sale listings. The results reflect how sellers and agents are marketing homes—not a survey of buyers or evidence that a particular feature will help a property sell. Still, they offer a look at the amenities and design details increasingly making their way into listing descriptions. Here are the main takeaways.

Mentions of AI are up 119 percent, the largest increase in this year’s report. Zillow points to AI-powered appliances among the features sellers are advertising, while mentions of app-controlled home systems rose 34 percent.

Spaces to unplug are also appearing 34 percent more often. The report describes an interest in both technology that simplifies daily tasks and designated places to take a break from it.

“Digital detox is the new luxury,” says Amanda Pendleton, Zillow’s home trends expert, in a statement. “Homes are getting smarter, but buyers also want tech-free zones where nothing needs charging. Sellers and their real estate agents are now highlighting spaces to unplug.”

Color-drenched spaces—rooms where the same color extends across the walls, ceiling, and trim—are up 84 percent in listing mentions, making them one of the report’s biggest increases. Zillow sees the approach as part of a move toward bolder, more polished interiors. Exterior finishes are gaining ground, too: painted brick is up 27 percent, and board-and-batten siding rose 24 percent.

Color drenching takes a shade beyond the walls and onto the ceiling, trim, and other architectural details. The approach has even spawned a bathroom variation: tile drenching, which extends the same tile across surfaces for a continuous look.

The language used to describe these homes is changing alongside the features. Mentions of “elevated” rose 71 percent, while “cozy” fell 22 percent, “eclectic” dropped 19 percent, and “boho” declined 13 percent. Cozy remains one of the most common words in listings, but it’s appearing less frequently than it did a year ago.

Kitchen language is moving in the other direction. Mentions of “chef’s kitchens” fell 21 percent, and “gourmet kitchens” declined 15 percent.

Mentions of multigenerational living jumped 58 percent. Zillow connects the increase to homes accommodating the “sandwich generation,” or people caring for children and older family members.

Features that can serve different ages and needs are also appearing more frequently. Grab bars are up 18 percent, wider doorways are up 13 percent, and nurseries rose 9 percent. The report places these changes alongside the growing emphasis on flexible rooms and spaces with more than one use.

The home spa is changing. Mentions of spa-inspired bathrooms rose 60 percent, and cold plunge pools increased 44 percent. Jacuzzis and jetted tubs, meanwhile, each fell about 9 percent.

Spaces for exercise are also gaining ground. Home gyms are up 18 percent, while golf simulators rose 29 percent. Together, the findings cover both workout spaces and amenities marketed around rest and recovery, rather than a single version of the home wellness room.

Cats get a category of their own this year. “Catios”—screened-in outdoor spaces that let indoor cats spend time outside—are appearing in 32 percent more listings. A place to get some air, watch the birds, and keep an eye on the neighbors—the cat has a wish list, too.
