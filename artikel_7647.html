Injunction Against Repeating Defamatory Statements (Including Ones Calling Woman "Witch" and "Satan Servant") + $35M Damages

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

In Life Mastery Network LLC v. Haygarth, by Judge Jill Otake (D. Haw.), Life Mastery and Liane Wilson sued Paula Haygarth, Byron Horvath, and Stefanie Vogt-Horvath for various torts. Haygarth et al. ("Counterclaimants") then counterclaimed against Life Mastery and Wilson ("Counterclaim-Defendants") for defamation and false light.

Specifically, the jury found the following statements to be defamatory, some of which were posted on Wilson's Instagram account, and some of which, as indicated, appeared on her website, www.exposingfamilyabuse.com ("Exposing Family Abuse website"):

The jury returned verdicts on August 13, 2026, awarding Byron and Stefanie $1.6 million each. The jury also awarded Paula $1.6 million for the statements found to be defamatory at trial, and awarded an additional $500,000 in damages for the statements the Court found defamatory per se at the summary judgment stage. Counterclaimants were each awarded $10 million in punitive damages. In total, the jury awarded Counterclaimants $35.3 million in damages.

As I mentioned, yesterday the court issued an injunction barring plaintiffs "from repeating the statements already found to be defamatory, and are directed to remove (or direct removal of) the statements already found to be defamatory from the Exposing Family Abuse website." The court noted that such anti-libel injunctions are generally legally permissible (see this article for more details) with regard to specific statements that have been found to be false and defamatory in a final judgment:

In this case, Counterclaimants seek an order enjoining Counterclaim-Defendants from repeating statements that have already found to be defamatory. Such an injunction is narrowly tailored to statements already found to be unprotected by the First Amendment, and thus does not constitute an unlawful prior restraint….

Although the public has a substantial interest in protecting freedom of expression, it also has an interest in protecting individuals against reputational injury caused by defamatory falsehoods. Here, an injunction prohibiting repetition of statements already determined defamatory protects individuals against reputational injury and leaves Counterclaim-Defendants free to engage in other protected speech….

And the court declined to certify to the Hawaii Supreme Court the question whether Hawaii law authorizes such injunctions, concluding that it was sufficiently likely that Hawaii law actually does so authorize:

The Ninth Circuit has provided that certification to a state supreme court is a process that is reserved for state law questions that present significant issues, "including those with important public policy ramifications," and that have not yet been resolved by the state courts…. Certification should be taken only after careful consideration and should not be done so lightly.

Here, the Court finds certification to the Hawai'i Supreme Court to be unnecessary to resolve the present motion. The relief requested concerns either repetitions or removals of speech already found to be defamatory—a category of speech not even Counterclaim-Defendants say is protected under the Hawai'i constitution. Furthermore, the Court agrees with Counterclaimants that the effort to certify such a question would unnecessarily delay entry of final judgment in the matter.

Joseph Adams, Laura Moritz, and Louise Ing (Dentons US LLP) represent the Countclaimants.
