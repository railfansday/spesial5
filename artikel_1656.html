Pellegatti: Calvelli, Amorim, Krösche – why it may have been ‘better to lose against Cagliari’

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Carlo Pellegatti has painted a positive picture regarding AC Milan’s developments, one that puts last season’s collapse into perspective.

Rewinding to the end of May, it felt like Milan’s entire world have caved in. A 2-1 defeat at home to Cagliari – a game that they led in early on – in the final round of the season condemned the Rossoneri to a fifth-placed finish, meaning they missed out on Champions League football again.

This is after Massimiliano Allegri had the team in a title fight going into the Spring, but ultimately the decline was near-vertical towards the end of the campaign. He paid the price for this, as did the CEO Giorgio Furlani, sporting director Igli Tare and chief scout Geoffrey Moncada.

In his latest column for MilanNews, Carlo Pellegatti has put forward the notion that the defeat to the Sardinians might turn out to be a blessing. Why? Because the reset that followed on a sporting and managerial level seems to already be taking a positive shape.

“Krosche and delight! It’s a somewhat obvious joke, but it captures the expectations of AC Milan fans. When will he arrive? November 27th, as I’ve been told, or in the first ten days of December, or even – I really hope not – January 1st?” he began.

“The difference in dates for the announcement of his new role is by no means insignificant, because the January transfer window becomes a crucial turning point for Milan’s fortunes, both today and in the future.

“The sooner the Head of Football sits down at Casa Milan, the better he can weave his web to strengthen the team in the winter transfer window. This should have been the job of a year ago, but was not accomplished by a shortsighted management team that never understood the difference between costs and investments.

“It’s also a shame that Igli Tare is a person who respects roles and is attentive to internal balances. He had to, and this is his great regret, bypass those who managed Milan, to speak with Gerry Cardinale and explain the importance of the arrival of a striker and a defender, who would have allowed them to be in the Champions League today.

“I’m using a very bold paradox, one I’ve never admitted. If failing to qualify for the Champions League was the reason that pushed Gerry Cardinale to fire the key figures of the three worst and most disastrous years in Milan’s history, then, given the exciting future prospects, it would have been better to lose against Cagliari, which avoided prolonging the agony of our dear old AC Milan. A painful sacrifice that, I hope, will be repaid with success in the years to come.

“With a Cardinale who was too absent and distant, guilty, by his own admission, of having delegated the management of his club to unsuitable people, but who now becomes the protagonist of this new way of operating, reminiscent of the good old days.

“I obviously feel sorry for Tare and Allegri, who, despite having their responsibilities, were victims of a confusing, chaotic, and divisive situation, and through no fault of their own.

“Returning to Krosche, I hope he quickly finds his feet in this Milan adventure that awaits him. I continue to believe that a highly experienced and highly skilled Italian operative, to work alongside the German director, could and can be of help to him and Milan, given the obvious difficulties of integrating into a world that is completely different from the German one.

“Gerry Cardinale and his chief assistant, Massimo Calvelli, are convinced of the wisdom of their choice, after meeting several times with Markus Krosche. I think it’s crucial that he’s not only a skilled market operator, but also a valid on-field contact for Ruben Amorim.

“The Portuguese will then be able to discuss potential choices and decisions with his Head of Football. In short, it was a position in the organisational chart that was culpably neglected, with the results that were seen, until Gerry Cardinale himself imposed Igli Tare, who, in any case, did not have an easy job.

“I mentioned Massimo Calvelli, who, in the media, always appears to be somewhat out of the spotlight. Certainly not in operational terms, because, from what I know, he was the absolute protagonist and architect of transactions that brought approximately €825m into Milan’s coffers.

“He was certainly a skilled architect of the return of Adidas as a shirt sponsor, but also a key figure in the acquisition of Generali, which will give its name to the new stadium, this time with the collaboration, it is said, of Paolo Scaroni.

“Therefore, he is a valuable executive who works behind the scenes, but with excellent results in terms of economic and financial aspects and also for the club’s image.”
