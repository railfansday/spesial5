The American manager with a maths degree making history in La Liga

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Pellegrino Matarazzo became manager of La Liga side Real Sociedad in December 2025

From kicking a ball around in the parks of New Jersey to lifting the Copa del Rey with Real Sociedad, Pellegrino Matarazzo's journey to managing the La Liga side has been anything but conventional.

He studied applied mathematics at Columbia University, played football in Germany's lower divisions and eventually became the first American manager in La Liga history when he joined the San Sebastian club in December 2025.

It is a journey that began in New Jersey, where Matarazzo grew up in an Italian-American family with football woven into its identity.

"My love for the game came from my family, from my dad, my uncles, the entire family with the Italian background loving the game," he explains.

With no MLS at the time, the family's footballing allegiances lay across the Atlantic, particularly in southern Italy, and Napoli became the team they gravitated towards.

"I spent my whole childhood playing football," Pellegrino told BBC Sport. "Whether that's for school teams, club teams, or just with the family on Sundays in the park.

"I continued to play through college, and then I realised that my curiosity was too big to not take that step overseas and that's what led me to where I am now."

Yet his academic path initially suggested a very different future.

Though Matarazzo graduated with a degree in applied mathematics, he insists it was never his great passion.

"It was just something I was good at," he says.

Instead, he decided to pursue his playing ambitions in Germany.

"I was always very ambitious but I never managed to play professional football above the third division at the time in Germany," he said.

"So there came a time where I needed to decide what to do next. Do I go back to the States and use my degree or do I remain in football?

An opportunity to obtain his coaching licences at a German club ultimately decided his future.

Pellegrino worked under Germany boss, Julian Nagelsmann, at Hoffenheim before being handed his first senior managerial role at Stuttgart.

There, he guided the club to promotion and established them in the Bundesliga. He later returned to Hoffenheim as manager, helping them avoid relegation before securing Europa League qualification.

In December 2025, was appointed Real Sociedad head coach.

"If I open doors, if I do something special for US managers down the line, then that's fantastic," he said.

"But I'm just doing my thing at the moment, looking to make progress in my own career and get better every day at what I do."

Matarazzo arrived at a difficult moment for Real Sociedad, who were struggling at the bottom of La Liga, but he believes understanding the people and the region was central to establishing his team's identity.

"The most important step was to really create the style of football and identity that fit both the people of Gipuzkoa [the province where San Sebastian is based] and the team," he explains.

"I really love the Basque culture. There are people that are very hardworking, intelligent, at the same time have the ability to remain humble and grounded despite success."

"Those are values that I also carry within me, which made it an easy transition."

He has also been working on his language skills, although admits the Basque language is a big challenge.

"Spanish is going well. It took me a bit of time to start learning, because I didn't have time to waste when I first moved, but speaking Italian certainly helps."

On the pitch, meanwhile, one player has been particularly important in establishing the culture he wants.

Captain Mikel Oyarzabal, who played a starring role in Spain's 2026 World Cup triumph, has many of the characteristics associated with the people of Gipuzkoa.

"He embodies that ability to stay grounded despite success, to work hard," Matarazzo says.

"He leads by example. He's not an emotional leader on the pitch, but he leads through clear communication and doing the work."

It is a relationship that makes the manager's job considerably easier.

And together they have already experienced something special.

Matarazzo arrived at Sociedad with an ambition to win a trophy, and Sociedad's Copa del Rey triumph in April delivered a landmark moment for the club.

It was their first trophy in five years, following their 2021 triumph in the same competition, which had been celebrated without fans in attendance because of Covid restrictions.

This time, players and supporters could finally celebrate the occasion together.

"It was an amazing dynamic that started in January and that we just exponentially increased to the explosion in Seville to win the title," Matarazzo recalls.

"It was an unbelievable moment for everyone."

Now, with European football offering another stage on which to demonstrate Sociedad's ambitions, Matarazzo is determined that success should not become a reason to stand still.

"We always want more and it's always a maximum possibility. We want more than what we had last year and we want to do well in La Liga, in the cup, in the Europa League and in the Super Cup."

They were beaten 2-1 by Crystal Palace on Thursday in the Europa League at Selhurst Park after taking the lead through Mamadou Sarr, but then having Jon Marin sent off.

But Sociedad sit fifth in La Liga, 14 points behind league leaders Barcelona.

So what would the young boy from New Jersey make of it all?

"I think my younger self would be very curious as to what comes next, especially because it's never linear. My wife, my son and I have also sacrificed a lot.

"But I also think he would be very proud."
