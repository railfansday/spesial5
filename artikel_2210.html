West Ham Had No Choice But to End the Nuno Era – Here’s Why

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

“Stale.” It’s a simple word, but one that perfectly sums up the final months of Nuno Espírito Santo’s reign at West Ham United.

The football had become predictable, uninspiring and, all too often, difficult to watch. What arrived with optimism and expectation slowly descended into frustration and stagnation, leaving us supporters increasingly detached from a side that looked incapable of building momentum.

A win rate of just 35.8%, made up of 19 victories, 19 defeats and 15 draws, is hardly the return expected from a manager reportedly earning a basic £4.5 million per year. Add in performance-related bonuses that could have taken that figure beyond £5.5 million and the value for money argument becomes even harder to defend.

Much has already been written about Nuno’s career, his close relationship with football super-agent Jorge Mendes and his previous managerial successes and failures. However, the issues at West Ham appeared to run far deeper than results alone.

Reports of dressing-room unrest became increasingly difficult to ignore. The reaction from several players on social media following the club’s announcement of Nuno’s departure painted a picture that suggested not everyone was disappointed to see the back of the Portuguese coach.

Whether fair or not, perceptions matter, and by the time the board made its decision, it appeared they had little room left to manoeuvre.

Frequent tactical changes, constant rotation and regular shifts in formation left players struggling to develop understanding and rhythm. Too often the team looked unsure of its identity. One week brought a back three, the next a back four. Players found themselves moved in and out of the side without any obvious continuity.

The consequence was a team that never truly settled.

That is not to absolve the players of responsibility. Several underperformed throughout the campaign, regardless of the system being deployed. Ultimately, however, modern football remains a results business and the head coach is invariably the man held accountable when things go wrong.

Rightly or wrongly, that responsibility now sits firmly in the past.

The former Bournemouth and Burnley manager arrives with a clear brief, reignite confidence, restore belief and turn one of the Championship’s most talented squads into a genuine automatic promotion contender.

The challenge is significant, but so is the opportunity.

Tonight’s fixture against Cardiff City comes too early for Parker to have much influence, with the interim coaching team of Potts, Robson, Noble and Prenderville overseeing proceedings. However, it offers the first step in what supporters hope will be a fresh start.

The Nuno Espírito Santo chapter closes with disappointment, underachievement and a lingering sense of what might have been. Whether the blame lies with the manager, the players, or both, the reality is simple: The project failed to deliver.

Now comes the hard part. West Ham’s season is still alive, but the margin for error has all but disappeared. If promotion is to be achieved, the talent within this squad must finally translate into performances on the pitch.

The Nuno era ended exactly as it had become, Stale.
