Have Yourself A Chrissy Little Christmas

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Chrissy Teigen is trading a picture-perfect holiday for homemade ornaments, flour-dusted counters, and a verrry full house.

Christmas for Chrissy Teigen, cookbook author and founder of the food and bakeware brand Cravings by Chrissy Teigen, means getting the whole crew together: her husband, award-winning artist and producer John Legend; their four kids; three dogs; a bearded dragon; and a parrot. There’s a lot going on under one roof.But Chrissy handles chaos with ease. These days, she’s more focused on making memories with Luna, 10, Miles, 8, Esti, 3, and Wren, 3, than on making everything perfect.Much of that joy happens in the kitchen, where Chrissy has built a career writing three best-selling cookbooks and launching a line of baking mixes in unexpected flavors, like snickerdoodle with miso-honey sugar.The family travels often, but Chrissy is looking forward to staying put this winter. It’ll give her more space to embrace the spontaneity she’s craving this year—the kind you can find only when you’re home for the holidays.

Chrissy Teigen: Oh, Halloween. We have a big kids’ Halloween party here at the house. We pick a theme and run with it. This year we picked Coraline.

We love crafting so much. I wanted to stay home for the holidays this year because I need all my children to start hand-making their ornaments and putting them on the tree. I’ve entered this time in my life where I want Christmas to be completely whimsical and over the top. I want to have a cute, colorful, chaotic Christmas this year. I want to thread the popcorn onto the string and have the most chaotic kids’ Christmas tree ever. And I can’t wait for that.

Cooking, of course. Scalloped potatoes in my house are very, very holiday. And my mom comes to town. She lives in Thailand, so we love getting the whole family together. And there’s a lot of football. John’s family loves it, and there’s usually a big Ohio State game around Thanksgiving that they all go crazy for. They go just as nuts for the Christmas game, too. It’s very much the whole family on the floor of my house, watching everything together, and it’s beautiful.

It will be filled with so much joy, but it’s going to be an emotional one for me, of course. To have that big yellow chair that my dad loved so much, empty...I don’t exactly know what to expect from me emotionally, but I can imagine it will be all the things that make the holidays so complex: sad and wonderful and special all at the same time.We will bake, with love, the potatoes Dad loved so, so much. We will clean the greens the way Granny (on John’s side) told us to. I’m sure our eyes will well up. It’ll be a testament to how much they meant to us all.

It is, but that recipe has always haunted me. My mom is impossible to get a recipe from. I think a lot of moms do this on purpose: You ask them to share a recipe, and something’s missing, or they leave out one little thing.I’ve checked in about this one so many times. She’s made it twice a year my whole life, and every time I ask about a detail—whether she uses MSG or when she adds the pepper—I get a different answer. So I’m not even sure I have the right recipe in my cookbook. Whenever she makes it, something is different. You could call it a mom’s touch, but I think she’s leaving out an important ingredient because she wants hers to be the best.

We do Tyler Florence’s beef Wellington every year. It looks impressive, but it’s quite simple, and it’s never gone wrong for me. When I cut into it, the color is beautiful, and it’s juicy. Over the years, I’ve learned how to keep the pastry crisp, even on the bottom where the meat juices collect. It’s a flawless recipe, and the peppercorn sauce is amazing. It pleases everyone, and the kids can cut shapes out of the puff pastry. For sides, we have the scalloped potatoes, green beans, and carrots.

My girls are always in the kitchen with me—I’m not allowed to cook without them. I love it, even though it slows things down. Esti is kind of the boss of the house right now, and she gets nervous that if she goes upstairs to shower, we’ll finish the recipe without her. We make sure she’s back in time to cook the recipe from beginning to end. I expect Esti and Luna will do most of the cooking for Christmas dinner.

No, they’re more likely to spill things. The living room is always covered in spills. I often put my drink on the floor because the table is too far away, and the boys knock it over with their feet.

Miles is our “won’t eat anything” guy. He’s eight now, and he’s been picky since he started eating solid food. He mostly likes chicken nuggets, fries, hamburgers, and pizza. Esti’s tastes change quickly. She might want apples with every dinner, then decide she doesn’t like them—and be like, “I never liked apples. Are you crazy?”Wren is our tank. He’ll eat anything. He gets almost catatonic when he’s eating, so he forgets it could make him sick.Luna knows how to make foods taste better. If something has a burnt corner, for example, she’ll cut it off or find another way to improve it. I think that’s really special.

I remember not liking raw tomatoes or avocados when I was young, even though I love them now, so I’m not too worried about how the kids’ tastes will change. They’re kids, and they’re told what to do all day. Food can be one way they speak up and feel like their opinions matter. I get that—it must be hard to be told what to do from morning to night. As long as they’re full and not waking up hungry, I’m fine.

Christmas morning is an all-day experience for us. Once my kids open a present, they want to play with it, so they take their time instead of rushing through the gifts. I used to have so many rules and run around stressing about the wrapping and recycling. Now I just relax and let the day unfold.

I love prepping for Christmas the night before, especially when it’s a secret, and I’m in charge. I’ll make hot cocoa bombs. In the morning, the kids just have to pour hot milk over them. And we’ll have pancakes, bacon, and Mrs. Butterworth’s syrup, get a sugar rush, and then hopefully have like a nice midday crash together.

Luna has always wanted to be in on the secret. I worried that, as my oldest, she might feel like she was missing out on the Christmas magic, but she loves helping make it special for her younger siblings. Last year, I asked if she wanted to stay up all night with me, and she loved that more than anything.She and Esti also love making stockings for the dogs, the bearded dragon, and now the parrot. We had lots of animals growing up, and I remember my mom taking me to PetSmart to pick out little gifts for our bird. I made sure every beating heart in the house got celebrated, and now the kids love doing that, too.

The bearded dragon toy industry is small. Last year we got little inflatable pools, and there are dried insects, as gross as they are. And a little Santa hat. He’s happy with that. I used to make bird toys with my dad for our parrot, so getting things for Audrey reminds me of that time with him.

Without a doubt, and I’m putting Luna in charge. Carrot cake is our family’s favorite. It was my wedding cake, and it’s John’s family’s favorite, too. There’s so much warmth in it: It has spices and real carrots, and it’s sticky, decadent, and delicious. It’s our holiday pleaser.In addition to our carrot cake kit, I hope we can one day offer a kit with lots of elements for people to decorate a cake and have fun with. The holidays are all about slowing down and getting your hands into something, and it doesn’t hurt if you can eat it after, too.

No, but I loved baking mixes, especially confetti cake mix. My dad had a big sweet tooth, and baking mixes and TV dinners were important to me. My mom’s food seemed too spicy or daunting when I was a kid, so my dad and I bonded over Hungry-Man dinners, Kid Cuisine, and Hot Pockets. Homemade food was mostly for the holidays.I think that’s why grocery stores still excite me. I take my kids shopping and love seeing what’s new—and finding foods that taste just like I remember, like frozen Salisbury steak. Those were the foods I grew up on, and I wanted to recreate them at home for my Cravings cookbooks. I’ve never looked down on those foods. They’ve stood the test of time for a reason. They’re nostalgic and wonderful.
