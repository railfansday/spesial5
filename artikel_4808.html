Here’s what happened in crypto today

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Need to know what happened in crypto today? Here is the latest news on daily trends and events impacting Bitcoin price, blockchain, DeFi, Web3 and crypto regulation.

Today in crypto, tokenized stocks linked to GTA VI publisher left Nvidia in the rearview mirror. Binance said it secured a $6.5 billion worldwide freezing order to help recover the stolen assets and broke down the full extent of the cryptocurrencies stolen in the attack.

Tokenized shares of Grand Theft Auto VI publisher Take-Two Interactive recorded nearly three times the trading volume of tokenized Nvidia shares on decentralized exchanges (DEXs) as the blockbuster began its global rollout.

Trading volume for tokenized Take-Two (TTWO) shares surged 578% to $249.5 million on Wednesday, up from $36.8 million a day earlier, according to Token Terminal data. Tokenized Nvidia (NVDA) recorded $88.1 million in DEX volume despite ranking first by market cap at $399.4 million.

The surge made TTWO the most-traded tokenized stock tracked by Token Terminal that day, accounting for roughly 42% of the $590.7 million in DEX volume.

Tokenized stocks are blockchain-based assets that track the value of publicly traded shares, allowing investors to gain exposure through crypto trading platforms. Their onchain trading volumes do not represent transactions in the underlying shares on traditional stock exchanges.

Take-Two shares on the Nasdaq rose 6.2% to close Wednesday at $216.61, according to Yahoo Finance. Shares gave back some of those gains Thursday, falling 4% to $208 in premarket trading as of 7:02 a.m. ET.

GTA VI, which Citi analyst Jason Bazinet estimated cost about $1.4 billion to make, ends a 13-year wait for a sequel to GTA V. PlayStation and Microsoft’s Xbox both said it was the most preordered game in the history of their respective stores.

Hackers behind the record $6.3 billion Binance hack stole a total of 192 cryptocurrencies across 11 blockchain networks, new court documents reveal.

The figures come from an asset schedule submitted by Nest Clearing and Custody Limited, which operated the affected Binance wallets, and included in a Wednesday Abu Dhabi court order shared with Cointelegraph.

Tether’s USDt (USDT) accounted for the largest share of stolen value at $2.50 billion, followed by $1.42 billion in Bitcoin (BTC) and $826 million in Circle’s USDC (USDC). Together, the three assets made up about 76% of the total value of misappropriated tokens.

While earlier reports identified many of the tokens stolen in the attack, the latest court documents show a full breakdown of all crypto assets stolen in the attack, including the quantities and what they were worth at the time of the theft.

Binance said it has secured a $6.5 billion worldwide freezing injunction from the Abu Dhabi Global Market Courts as it seeks to recover assets stolen in its record Oct. 29 hack.

The court orders target unidentified perpetrators of the attack and are intended to help issuers, exchanges and other partners freeze and return stolen assets, the company said in a statement shared with Cointelegraph on Wednesday.

The orders can be served via blockchain, according to M. Kendall Day of Gibson Dunn, which represents Binance. This allows legal notices to be delivered to wallet addresses rather than through traditional channels. Day said market participants would also be notified of updates to a “living list” of 26,000 wallet addresses identified as holding stolen assets.

Binance said some businesses require formal court orders before they can release, transfer or return frozen funds. The injunction’s $6.5 billion amount is not a tally of recovered assets, and the statement did not disclose how much had so far been returned under the orders.

Binance also said it obtained orders requiring stolen assets to be handed over. Separate interim orders cover assets frozen by certain named parties against whom no wrongdoing is alleged.
