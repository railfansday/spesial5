Team lose title amid losing streak after fans unfurl champions banner

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Opening up a 12-point lead with four rounds remaining and needing just one draw to lift the first trophy in their history seemed like the perfect script for Chengdu Rongcheng.

The ending, however, went down as one of the greatest collapses ever seen in a domestic league.

After losing all four of their final Chinese Super League matches, the club watched Dalian Yingbo surge ahead on the final day and claim the trophy after an improbable turnaround.

The collapse became one of football's biggest choke jobs, as Chengdu needed just a single point from the 12 still available.

To make matters worse, there was a familiar feel to the story: in the 2025 season, the team had also led with six rounds remaining, but managed just one win in the run-in and finished third.

"We are in a state of complete despair. There is nothing more to say," coach John Aloisi lamented immediately after the decisive defeat.

Optimism in the stands was so high that, weeks before the competition ended, fans were already displaying a banner celebrating Chengdu as national champions, according to The Athletic, part of The New York Times. Their confidence was justified by the team's form: top since the opening round, they spent almost the entire season leading the table.

Over nine months of competition, the team spent just over an hour off the top spot—enough, however, to let the trophy slip away at the finish. Dalian, the eventual champions, only took first place 15 minutes into their final match.

That early dominance had been built on 11 wins in the first 12 rounds. Buoyed by those results, Australian coach John Aloisi earned the nickname "Master Aloisi" and appeared on course to lead the club, founded in 2018, to their first national title.

The first signs of strain emerged in the second half of the year. In July, the team went seven games without a win, but recovered in time to enter the final stretch with what seemed an unassailable cushion.

Their first mathematical chance to secure the title came with five rounds remaining, but Chengdu could only manage a goalless draw against Henan. A roughly month-long break in the schedule then preceded the disastrous run that would turn their dream into a nightmare.

The club lost all four of their final matches. In one, they conceded Tianjin Jinmen Tiger's winner in the first minute of second-half stoppage time. Then, in the penultimate round, in the head-to-head clash with Dalian, Chengdu missed a penalty and had a 90th-minute equalizer ruled out by VAR.

It all came down to the final day. Around 4,000 supporters made the journey of more than 1,600 kilometers to Shandong to support the team against Shandong Taishan, knowing that, once again, a draw would be enough. But the hosts were 2–0 up after just 23 minutes. Chengdu threatened a comeback, twice reducing the deficit and having an effort cleared off the line in the closing moments, but fell to a 3–2 defeat.

Meanwhile, Dalian needed to beat Tianjin to complete the turnaround in the standings. On a snow-covered pitch, the match was level at one point in the second half—a result that would have handed Chengdu the title despite their defeat. Two second-half goals, however, sealed the win and the unexpected title.

The cruelest blow came from the tiebreaking rules: the two teams finished the season level on points. Despite boasting a far superior goal difference (+15 to +5), Chengdu lost the trophy on head-to-head results, having been beaten by Dalian in both of their league meetings.

The failure triggered an immediate crisis behind the scenes. Fans protested outside the team's hotel after the final game, forcing security staff to intervene as the players left.

Around 40 minutes after the final whistle, the club's board published an open letter asking for understanding over the lost title, but the gesture failed to quell the anger. Coach John Aloisi was sacked, while president Wang Lei and general manager Wu Bo resigned.

Beyond the emotional toll, Chengdu also dealt with key absences in the decisive rounds.

Suspended captain Wei Shihao missed the final three games, while Brazilian striker Felipe Silva, the team's leading scorer, sat out two of the last four matches.

The squad also includes three other Brazilian players: midfielders Matheus Jussa and Rômulo, and forward Wellington Silva.
