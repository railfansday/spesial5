Raiders have 7 weeks to build foundation for Fernando Mendoza

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

If the Raiders wanted to ease Fernando Mendoza into his new job, they have a peculiar way of showing it.

First came the Seahawks, who turned his starting debut into a chess match with consequences. Six times, those consequences involved Mendoza being dropped to the ground with the ball still in his hands.

Next up are the Broncos, rested from their bye and waiting in Denver. After that, the Browns in Cleveland.

It is difficult to imagine a much more treacherous introduction to professional quarterbacking. Three defenses capable of making experienced quarterbacks uncomfortable, with the last two getting the rookie in their own buildings.

Fernando Mendoza #15 and Cody White #82 of the Las Vegas Raiders warm up at Allegiant Stadium in Las Vegas on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. The Seahawks won 21-13. Getty Images

The schedule will eventually offer something more forgiving. For now, Mendoza is getting an education that requires an ice bath afterward.

And somewhere down the road, he might appreciate it.

The disguised coverages, the pressure arriving from unexpected places, the difference between what a defense shows before the snap and what it becomes afterward. Every uncomfortable experience gives him something to recognize the next time he sees it.

But there is another education taking place, and the Raiders need to pay just as much attention to it.

John Spytek, Tom Brady and Klint Kubiak are learning what they have to put around their young quarterback.

That makes Sunday's game against Denver, and the six remaining afterward, an evaluation of everyone involved in making this work.

Mendoza has responsibilities. He has to process information faster, understand when to abandon a play and get the ball out before a manageable situation becomes a drive-killing sack.

He accepted those responsibilities after the Seattle game. Good for him.

The entire building needs to do the same.

The Raiders rushed for 65 yards on 24 attempts against the Seahawks. Brock Bowers hurt his ankle on the opening series, and an offense already short on dependable answers had even fewer.

Las Vegas Raiders General Manager John Spytek arrives at Allegiant Stadium for a game against the Seattle Seahawks in Las Vegas on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. Getty Images

Those are organizational problems with consequences for the quarterback.

Start with the interior offensive line. Mendoza needs room to step into throws and a running game that makes second down feel like an opportunity. The Raiders have to determine which players can provide that consistently and where upgrades are necessary.

Every failed block deserves examination, including whether the problem was talent, technique or communication. Those distinctions will matter when Spytek starts deciding how to spend another offseason.

Then there is the receiver the Raiders still need.

A premier talent alongside Bowers would change how defenses prepare for Las Vegas. Give Mendoza someone who threatens downfield, commands attention and wins when the coverage is good, and the entire offense benefits.

Bowers gets more favorable opportunities. The running game faces different defensive decisions. Mendoza gets a receiver he can trust when the play requires someone to make something happen.

The Raiders need that kind of two-headed problem for opponents.

They also need to understand that supporting a quarterback extends to the possessions when he is standing on the sideline.

A defense that cannot consistently stop the run gives the opposing offense control of the game. It keeps Mendoza waiting, limits his opportunities and increases the pressure to produce when he finally gets the ball back.

The interior defensive line needs more star power and ruggedness. Someone has to disrupt the blocking, hold the point of attack and make an offense reconsider running directly at the Raiders.

Maxx Crosby needs a reliable complementary pass rusher, too. The attention he commands should create opportunities that another defender regularly turns into sacks, hurried throws and stalled drives.

The secondary has promising young players whose development deserves investment and patience. But young defensive backs also benefit when the quarterback has to throw on schedule and the defense can anticipate passing situations.

Fox Sports broadcast analyst Tom Brady looks on before a game between the Dallas Cowboys and San Francisco 49ers in Arlington, Texas, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

Better run defense creates longer third downs. Better pressure helps coverage. More stops give the offense additional chances and a little breathing room.

These next games will help reveal which pieces belong and which must be added.

The Raiders selected Mendoza because they believe he can become a great quarterback. His first starts offer the beginnings of that picture, with every adjustment and mistake adding detail.

They also expose the work required around him.

Sunday is another opportunity for Mendoza to show what he learned.

It is another opportunity for the Raiders to figure out what they must do about what they are learning.
