FIA to introduce full-time F1 stewards

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Formula 1's governing body, the FIA, has revealed full-time stewards will be used for the first time from next year; watch the Las Vegas GP weekend on Sky Sports F1, with Friday's practice sessions at 12.30am and 4pm

The FIA has announced it will introduce full-time Formula 1 stewards from 2027 with the aim of having "greater consistency in decision-making".

The use of full-time stewards has been a consistent talking point in F1 as different stewards, who adjudicate all racing and technical matters on a race weekend, are used at each event over the season.

There has been an argument that this can lead to inconsistencies in decision-making when it comes to imposing penalties, or the severity of a penalty.

But the FIA announced on Thursday, ahead of this weekend's Las Vegas Grand Prix, they will begin to recruit full-time stewards with a phased introduction from next year with the aim of establishing 12-15 full-time stewards over the coming years.

Currently, there are four stewards drawn from a large FIA pool of officials and one of those is a former racing driver. The new structure will continue to use the knowledge of driver stewards at each event and the FIA say full-time stewards will "allow for deeper implementation of best practice and greater consistency in decision-making".

The news comes a month after the opening of the FIA Remote Operations Centre at Biggin Hill in the UK, following a reorganisation of Race Control in 2022 after the controversial 2021 season which saw Max Verstappen overtake Lewis Hamilton on the last lap of the season-ending Abu Dhabi Grand Prix.

"This latest major investment in FIA officiating reflects our dedication to delivering the highest standards of decision-making, fairness and integrity in motorsport," said FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem.

"The recruitment of full-time stewards is a significant step in our longstanding mission of building a dedicated officiating ecosystem in the FIA Formula One World Championship, it is also an investment in the future of our sport.

"Following the milestone opening of the new FIA Remote Operations Centre and strengthening of officiating development through initiatives such as the FIA High Performance Programme, this development will help to build greater expertise and continuity within our stewarding structure, strengthening our ability to review and improve regulations, support the development of future officials and ensure the highest standards of consistent officiating across all our championships."
