Stellantis Exiting the WEC and Formula E to Focus on New Hi-Po Cars

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Rather than spend millions on factory-backed teams, Stellantis will focus on new-car development and customer-racing support.

Stellantis is preparing its exit from the World Endurance Championship (WEC) and Formula E, but the automaker isn’t giving up on motorsports. In fact, the decision is meant to funnel more energy into making motorsports-focused cars for customers.

The Peugeot 9X8 hypercar is Stellantis’s only factory-backed car in the WEC; that partnership will end at the end of the year. The automaker has a bit more skin in the game in Formula E, where Citroën and Opel each hold two spots on the 24-car grid this season. Both teams will exit Formula E at the end of the season in summer 2027.

By ending its FIA World Championship partnerships, Stellantis says it’ll be able to focus on building motorsports-capable cars for enthusiasts. Employees who currently work in the WEC and Formula E divisions will be retained and reassigned to other areas focused on performance-vehicle development and customer racing. Those reassigned employees will likely pivot to work on high-performance Alfa Romeo and Maserati models, as well as new motorsports-inspired variants for Peugeot, Lancia, and Opel.

Stellantis says it’ll continue to build on its engineering and competition knowledge in customer racing programs—especially rally racing, where several of its brands have deep rally roots. Dismantling factory-backed teams likely frees up the automaker to invest more in smaller collaborations with privateer teams, much like its partnership with PH Sport on the Lancia Corse HF in the World Rally Championship.

The restructuring aligns with Stellantis’s FaSTLAne 2030 plan, which aims to launch more than 100 new and refreshed vehicles by 2030. Reassigning motorsports employees to other areas will undoubtedly help streamline the development process for enthusiast-focused cars to meet the automaker’s ambitious goals.

Car and Driver reached out to Stellantis to find out how this might affect its involvement in other major motorsports events in North America. After all, Ram recently returned to the NASCAR Truck Series for the 2026 season, and Dodge recently announced plans to return to the NASCAR Cup Series for the 2027 season with two Charger-based race cars. A spokesperson provided the following statement: “There are no changes to Stellantis North America motorsports programs.”
