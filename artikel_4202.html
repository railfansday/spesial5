Here Are Our Tips For Getting Starting In FM 27

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Getting started in Football Manager can be tricky, especially with the wave of tutorials to navigate, so here are our tips to help you get started.

Getting into Football Manager 27 is a daunting task at the best of times. When starting a new game, you're met with a barrage of tutorials that are in place to teach you the basics of the game, but the reality is that you often have to figure things out as you go along. To help with this process, we've compiled a list of five beginner tips to get you started, make that overwhelming first hour of a save feel a

Before you even get into a Football Manager save, you're going to have to select the countries that you want to be part of in. This determines how many players are in the database at the start of the save and, more importantly, your PC's estimated performance in the game.

This will, of course, depend on the power of the PC or laptop you're playing on, but you rarely want to drop below "efficient" when it comes to game speed. This ensures that everything still runs relatively quickly, but there will be room for improvement. You can also add and remove leagues during a save, if you want to make an adjustment after starting a game.

It's been a long, hard road back to success for Sports Interactive, but FM 27 is the game I hoped it would be and so much more.

While your manager profile is somewhere you can be a little expressive, it also has an impact on your save. Whether you're playing it the realistic way, or want to enter the game as a managing prodigy, your decisions will have an impact on how your save will go.

For instance, selecting to start the game with no coaching badges will mean there are leagues in the world you simply aren't allowed to manage in until you earn them.

Elsewhere, your tactical focuses determine which attributes get a starting boost and what your board may expect of you early on. So, if you select that you're an expert in fast, attacking football, but then try to deploy slow, defensive tactics, you may find your team takes longer to adapt and grow into their roles.

It doesn't matter if you're brand new to the Football Manager franchise or a returning veteran; the introductions for each aspect of running a club are incredibly useful, and all of them are tailored to the club you currently manage. It's a great way to learn the ins and outs of tactical setups, team dynamics, training, scouting, set pieces, and more, while also understanding how each one applies to your specific club.

These rundowns give you an idea of your star players, those who may want to leave the club, the ongoing injury situation, your top transfer targets, and so much more. It's tempting to just blast through these introductions, feeling as though you can just learn as you go, but it will save you future frustrations if you pay attention to them at the very beginning of your save.

Identifying your best players should be a priority for every save. Yes, even before you decide on a tactic, because your best 11 (one per position) may create interesting dilemmas you can solve with tactical workarounds. If you try to stick to the same tactic for each save, you're fighting an uphill battle, especially if you have to try to sign players to fit the system every time.

By taking a close look at the club's best 11 players, you can figure out whether a one- or two-striker system is best; it will also tell you whether playing with wingers is possible and may reveal that playing five at the back is the way to go. Following the framework already in place when you join a club makes the first season much easier to approach, and you can gradually make changes over time, so you're more likely to see success.

FM 26 was a troubled game in many ways, but the move to Football Manager 27 won't force you to upgrade your gaming PC or laptop.

Your staff are the lifeblood of your club, but it's easy to overlook the staff situation when you start a new save; it should actually be a priority from the get-go. Checking your staff before you hit continue for the first time will help you identify any gaps you need to fill as soon as possible.

Thanks to the handy comparison tool, you can also see how your staffing totals compare with the rest of the league, so you know whether you're leading the way in quality or lagging behind. While replacing staff isn't cheap, it's a job well worth doing if you notice that your club requires big improvements to compete with the rest of the league.

You can then start scouting replacements via the recruitment screen, or, if you have a vacancy, post a job advert and see who applies for your open positions.

Now, this is going to look very different depending on which club you're taking over, but the first thing you should do on the financial front is adjust your budgets to suit your planned approach to transfers. If you're taking over a lower-league team that's tight on cash, you want to make sure you maximize your wage budget.

One thing that a lot of players don't realize, however, is that you can reduce your scouting budget directly into your transfer budget, and then you can make further adjustments if needed. This is especially useful for non-league teams in England, who may have up to £20,000 in scouting budget, but it's complete overkill, and you're better off feeding it into your transfer kitty to start making moves.

For richer clubs, you want to identify any major contract negotiations that will soon be required and, if so, make sure you have the wages available to action them.

The reason it's best to try to adjust your budgets ahead of time is that FM 27 can sometimes lock your budgets in place inexplicably during negotiations, which can cost you valuable transfers. So while you can't predict everything in advance, making rough estimates could save you headaches later.

At every opportunity, you should be talking to your players. This tip stretches beyond just the opening hour of the game, but it's something that not enough of us do, and it can be extremely helpful in the long run.

If players are performing poorly or well, be it in training or matches, speak to them about this performance and either praise or criticize it. This won't always be easy, as players with certain personality traits may surprise you with their response, but it will do more good than harm in most cases, and it can keep spirits up and your team motivated if you're constantly talking to them.

You should also be making the most of team meetings. Whether your recent form is poor or great, talk to the team and make sure you're checking in regularly. This is more important than ever with the introduction of Tactical Effectiveness, and your relationship with your players now plays a role in how well they pick up the tactics you implement. A stronger relationship can help them adjust to changes much faster and be more amenable to playing positions and roles they're not natural in.

The final tip is to scour your club for potential roadblocks or issues and deal with them as soon as possible. Unhappy players, financial issues, any agreed promises, fan distrust- whatever it may be- identify it and come up with a resolution as soon as possible.

The most likely problems you'll encounter relate to player happiness, and you can either force these players out of the club to prevent them from becoming toxic and impacting the rest of the squad, or reintegrate them to make them happy again.

This is going to be a more prevalent task if you're joining a club mid-way through a season, but some clubs have issues right from day one in FM 27, and it's your goal to make sure these problems don't get any worse.
