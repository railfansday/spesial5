We put the Saatva Classic, the DreamCloud Classic Hybrid, and the Helix Midnight Luxe through the same set of standardised lab tests. We also analysed each mattress in terms of value for money. Here's how each mattress stacks up:

Good pressure relief in a mattress combines two things: cushioning at the touch points, to reduce pressure build-up, and support for your spine to prevent strain or 'hammocking'. In other words, it's the 'aaah' feeling you get when you climb into a mattress that's just right for you. The Saatva, DreamCloud and Helix scored well in this area.

The Saatva earned a 5 out of 5 pressure relief test score for all sleep positions. (We tested the Luxury Firm Saatva Classic but it's available in three feels: Plush Soft, Luxury Firm and Firm). The surface contours lightly to your body, but springs back instantly to deliver constant and responsive pressure relief.

The Helix scored a 4.5 out of 5 score in the same test, with sink-in foams cushioning our shoulders and hips. While it's the best mattress for side sleepers, it didn't score full marks like the Saatva because it wasn't as responsive – the foams took longer to bounce back, and some of our testers found the Helix harder to move around on.

The DreamCloud Classic scored a 4 out of 5 in our pressure relief test, which is still an excellent score. It's lower than the Saatva and Helix because because contouring isn't quite as effective, and the materials took longer than the Helix to bounce back. There was also less lumbar support, but still impressive for a mid-range bed-in-a-box.

If you're waking up most nights because you're too hot or too cold, you need a matter with better temperature regulation. The better ones we've tested all use a breathable construction to stop stuffy heat from pooling in the mattress, leading to overheating.

The Saatva Classic scored 5 out of 5 in our temperature regulation test, ranking just underneath our best cooling mattress of the year the Bear Elite Hybrid. With our tester lying on the bed, the Saatva's temperature crept up by just 6.3°F (compared to 7.3°F from the DreamCloud and 9°F on the Helix.) The Bear had a faster heat dissipation rate, shedding over eight degrees of heat in a minute compared to a 3°F shed from the Saatva.

At 1.12°F the DreamCloud had a faster dissipation rate than the Saatva Classic (1.26°F per minute), but it onboarded more heat: the DreamCloud had a Maximum Temperature Increase of 7.3°F, compared to 6.3°F on the Saatva and 9°F on the Helix. Subjective feedback from our testers was also mixed from the DreamCloud, with three out of five saying it felt warm to sleep on.

A mattress with good motion isolation can eat up movement from a restless partner, meaning you stand a better chance of sleeping peacefully through the night. But motion isolation is something even the best hybrid mattresses struggle with, as by design the layers of springs and coils add far more bounce to the bed than what you'd get from a memory foam mattress.

We could see this 'bounce' during testing for all three mattresses. The Helix was best at absorbing initial force (just... it's impact rate of 7.4 is below the 7.8 and 7.9 of the Saatva and DreamCloud, respectively), as that thick, soft top eats up force.

But the Saatva Classic has the fastest decay rate at 1.703 — in under two seconds, movement came to a stop.

Strong edge support means you can lie on the side of your mattress without sliding off (a big deal for bed sharers). It also makes getting in and out of bed more comfortable, as you have a sturdy base to push against. We use a 25kg weight to assess the edge support on all mattresses we test.

The Saatva Classic has the best edge support of any bed we've come across. Foam railings deliver extra support to the sides, meaning our weight hardly sank (it showed 1" less sink at the foot of the bed than in the equivalent position on the Helix or DreamCloud).

Yes, you can sit and lie comfortable on both the DreamCloud Classic and the Helix Midnight Luxe... but you can park a truck on the edge of the Saatva.

Alongside our sleep lab, we test all mattresses at home for at least three weeks. The Saatva Classic, DreamCloud Classic Hybrid and Helix Midnight Luxe have all gone through extensive at-home testing over the past year.

During our Saatva Classic mattress review, our lead tester (and editor) described the Classic as having "the best pressure relief of any mattress we’ve reviewed so far."

She noted that you lie on top of the surface while the materials contour to your body, a feel one member of our testing team called "weightless". Add on full-body support and a "gloriously cool-touch surface," and you have one of the best mattresses for back pain you can buy online.

In comparison, our the DreamCloud Classic Hybrid mattress review team found it lacked a little lumbar support. However, our lead tester felt the bed "cushioned" her shoulders while "elevating" her spine, creating full-body comfort when side sleeping.

This had a direct impact on her sleep quality; "I woke less in the night because I wasn’t tossing and turning as much," she explained. Lightweight back sleepers should also feel supported, although heavier members of our team noticed a slight "hamocking" effect.

Made for side sleepers, our Helix Midnight Luxe mattress review team felt it lived up to its promise with "incredible pressure relief to the shoulders and hips."

It's also highly supportive for back sleepers, which is why our lead tester described it as the only mattress her and her husband have ever agreed on. The plush pillow-top delivers a "hug" that some find stifling — but others described as "cloud-like."
