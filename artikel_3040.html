Dick’s Sporting Goods Raised Its Full-Year Earnings Forecast as Foot Locker Returned to Positive Comparable Sales. Here's What Investors Need to Know.

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The retailer was an investor darling on Tuesday after reporting third-quarter earnings.

By Eric Volkman – Nov 24, 2026 at 5:31PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Dick's Sporting Goods ›

A turnaround at a major operating unit, estimates-beating numbers, and a raise in profitability guidance powered Dick's Sporting Goods (DKS +14.59%) stock to a double-digit gain on Tuesday. The specialty retailer's third quarter not only featured better-than-expected financials but also growth from the struggling brand it acquired last year.

Here's a look at how Dick's did overall and whether investors can expect further gains from the veteran athletic-gear purveyor.

Dick's net sales in the quarter were $5.13 billion, 23% higher year over year. The caveat to this is the mentioned brand, Foot Locker, which the company acquired in 2025. That acquisition closed in early September of that year; as a result, only 8 weeks of Foot Locker's results were consolidated into Dick's third-quarter 2025 accounting -- hence the big difference between last third quarter's sales and this one.

Stripping Foot Locker out of the equation, the company's comparable sales rose by 2.6% in the quarter. Dick's said this was largely due to a rise in average ticket (i.e., average customer spending per transaction), offset by a slight decline in the number of transactions. On a pro forma basis, Foot Locker posted a 0.5% improvement. On a consolidated, pro forma basis for the company as a whole, comparable sales were 1.8% higher.

Turning to profitability, non-GAAP (adjusted) net income came in at $167.5 million, or $1.94 per share. That was down from $180.6 million in the year-ago quarter.

Despite the bottom-line slide, Dick's easily beat the consensus analyst estimates. Prognosticators tracking the company were modeling net sales of $5.08 billion, and adjusted earnings per share (EPS) of merely $1.42.

While those key fundamentals headed in an encouraging direction, it was Foot Locker's performance that really won the race for Dick's. That 0.5% increase in the brand's "comps" is quite an achievement -- it's the first time since the acquisition that it's been in the black.

Previously, many investors had considered it to be a laggard. The unit didn't seem like a potential winner at all, with its 3.6% decrease in pro forma comps only one quarter ago.

It still has some distance to run. In the conference call discussing the third-quarter results, management stated that Foot Locker performed particularly well in its core U.S. operating unit, which delivered comps of 4.1%. But the overall number was dragged down by sluggish performance in the Europe, Middle East, and Africa (EMEA) super-region. The brand was also burdened by high inventory levels, which required discounting to reduce.

Another negative for Foot Locker is Nike's (NKE +5.89%) reset of its storied Jordan sneaker brand, which is aging and suffers from oversupply. Like any major brand overhaul, this will take some time -- Nike flagged fiscal 2029 for Jordan to return to growth. Nike footwear is a major draw within Foot Locker's product selection.

Given that, Dick's maintained its guidance for full-year Foot Locker sales and comps. Specifically, it's expecting the unit to contribute $7.4 billion to $7.5 billion to the top line, with comparable sales expected to be flat to 2% down. Its operating loss should range from $40 million to $80 million.

But the core Dick's business should compensate for this. Management raised the low end of the unit's net sales forecast from $14.5 billion to $14.6 billion, maintaining the high end of $14.7 billion. Ditto for comps, now expected to be 3.3% to 4% (previously 2.5% at the bottom end).

All in all, the company is expecting a prosperous year. With the boosts to the Dick's unit estimates, the company's overall guidance is higher. The new floor for overall net sales is $22 billion (formerly $21.9 billion), with the maximum holding steady at $22.2 billion. Better, adjusted EPS is now anticipated to land at $11.45 to $12.30, up notably from the preceding forecast of $11 to $12.

So, can we consider Dick's stock a buy candidate? I think much will depend on Foot Locker, which, given its size, has a significant impact on its parent's results. Despite investor excitement over the brand’s positive third-quarter comps figure, it still needs to improve sufficiently to become a regular, meaningful contributor to overall company growth. It has to work through its inventory issues and develop strategies to grow during the "Jordan gap."

That said, Dick's stock offers a high-yield dividend (currently paying 3.3%) that appears quite sustainable, and its core business is performing well. I also like the rather modest forward P/E of under 13. To me, these factors make the shares attractive, but more for those with a relatively long-term investment horizon.
