Hyundai IONIQ 5 caught with a new high-tech interior and big display screen

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Hyundai appears to be testing a new high-tech setup in the IONIQ 5. A prototype was spotted with the door wide open, exposing a large display and a layout that looks very different from the current model.

Is the IONIQ 5 next in line for an overhaul? Another mysterious IONIQ 5 test mule was recently caught in a parking garage in South Korea, revealing what’s rumored to be Hyundai’s software-defined vehicle (SDV).

Hyundai is betting heavily on software, AI, autonomous driving, robotics, and more as it expands from a traditional auto manufacturer to a “software-centric mobility leader.”

At the heart of it all is Hyundai Motor Group’s new Pleos Connect, an advanced in-vehicle infotainment and software platform.

Built on Android Automotive OS, Hyundai’s new setup features a larger, tablet-style infotainment screen with apps, split-screen views, and more. It’s similar to using a smartphone with an easier-to-use, customizable layout.

The Grandeur was the first to launch with Hyundai’s Pleos in Korea in May, followed by the IONIQ 3 in Europe. In the US, the new Tucson will be the first to go on sale with Pleos in early 2027.

Is the IONIQ 5 next in line? Hyundai aims to launch its first SDV as early as next year, and the IONIQ 5 appears to be a contender.

An IONIQ 5 test car was captured again (shown below) by @JHS_spyshot_official in a Korean parking garage, but this time the door was wide open, offering a clear view of the new setup.

A post shared by JHS (@jhs_spyshot_official)

Hyundai appeared to be testing automated parking features, and as you can see, the setup is considerably different from the current IONIQ 5.

A large infotainment display sits at the center that appears to be running Hyundai Pleos, instead of the current dual 12.3″ screens. Meanwhile, the steering wheel and dash both appear to be completely redesigned.

Although it’s hard to see, previous spy images show the IONIQ 5 SDV test car with no driver display, with all information expected to move to the large center infotainment screen.

By 2030, Hyundai Motor Group plans to equip around 20 million Hyundai, Kia, and Genesis vehicles with the new Pleos setup.

The system is the “first tangible step” toward an SDV (Software-Defined Vehicle) architecture, Hyundai said, which will unlock new in-car features, autonomous driving capabilities, and personalized driving experiences.

Over the summer, an IONIQ 6 prototype was spotted near Hyundai’s R&D hub in Europe with a similar high-tech setup.

With Hyundai planning to launch its first SDV as early as next year, we should learn more soon.
