13 Cooking Gifts for the Chef on Your List

Snow Peak Titanium Sporks, Mugs, and Cookware

Food is fuel—the gas in our tanks that lets us get outside and play. But for dedicated foodies, cooking is about way more than calories. It’s about the experience, and sharing that experience with friends and family. Below, you’ll find 13 gifts that any chef will love, whether they’re an ultralight backpacker, a backyard BBQ master, or a cold coffee connoisseur. Bon appétit!

The 10,000-BTU Zempire Stealth Jet Twin isn’t the most powerful camp stove we’ve ever tested, but it is the slimmest two-burner we’ve ever tested, making it a sweet gift for car campers who value trunk space and camp cuisine alike. Easy simmer controls and built-in igniters round this pick out as a major upgrade for campers still lugging WWII-era camp stoves that weigh as much as small sedans.

Both a firepit and a grill, Snow Peak’s Takibi is designed to be the centerpiece of camp life. While it’s a delight just to watch flames flicker in the portable stainless steel pit, the well-crafted, Japanese-made Takibi is an excellent charcoal grill. Pair it with Snow Peak camp cooking accessories to help your loved one build out the ultimate live-fire cooking setup.

We love the versatility of the Switch. It comes with an integrated pot for fast boiling à la Jetboil, but remove the pot and the prongs unfurl like those on a classic canister stove to accommodate bigger pots and pans. For backpackers who like to cook—but appreciate the convenience of dehydrated meals from time to time—the Switch is a nifty, thoughtful gift.

When backpackers want to cut down on pack weight, they often opt for titanium cookware and accessories, as the metal offers an unbeatable strength-to-weight ratio. No one does titanium quite like Snow Peak. Smaller pieces, like Snow Peak’s iconic sporks, are a backpacker’s dream stocking stuffer, while drinkware and cooksets are destined to become prized possessions.

The compact and efficient Campstove 2+ can boil one liter of water in four and a half minutes, so it has morning coffee covered (you can also cook on it with some add-on components).

This isn’t your usual camping stove, though. It runs on biofuel—either pellets you can purchase or sticks you find. And this stove has multiple uses: It can also charge your devices via an integrated USB port as well as function as a lamp with its included attachment.

Rare is the pan that can handle an open flame, an induction cooktop, and a piping-hot pizza oven. Crafted from a single piece of wrought iron, the non-toxic Solidteknics Iron Frypan is lighter than its cast-iron counterparts and features a long, slender, grabbable handle, making flipping flapjacks or searing steaks over a campfire a lot less strenuous. Adaptable, durable, and dependable, this pan is ready for adventurous weekends at camp and cozy weeknights at home.

Quince’s Hinoki Cutting Boards make ditching the microplastic-shedding chopping blocks of yesteryear a genuine pleasure. A Japanese cypress renowned for its almost citrusy fragrance, Hinoki is a soft, forgiving wood that will protect your precious knife blade. The wood is also naturally lightweight, making it easy to pack these boards into your camp kitchen bin.

Socks are always a slam-dunk stocking stuffer. But for foodies who grew up watching James Beard winner and Cooking Channel alum Emeril Lagasse say “Bam!” as he sprinkled seasoning into Creole and Cajun concoctions, these Stance socks earn the holiday equivalent of a Michelin star. Fun, hilarious, nostalgic, and comfortable to boot, these cotton crew socks are sure to earn a grin every time they’re dug out of the sock drawer. What more can we say but… BAM!

You might be wondering what the hell is Outside doing writing about a KitchenAid Tilt-Head Stand Mixer? It’s a fair question. The truth is that we’re big fans of baked goods around here. Whether we’re exploring via skintrack or singletrack, a well-timed lemon bar or squished chocolate chip muffin can make all the difference. And besides, the forest-green colorway and the natural woodgrain bowl make this mixer quite a thoughtful gift for the nature-loving baker, no? It’s on our holiday wish list, at least!

Being landlocked is no longer a problem for seafood-loving gourmands. Hog Island Oyster Company will ship boxes of Hog Island shellfish, accoutrements, and more nationwide, on ice, directly to your doorstep. If you’re looking for a gift that can hang out under a Christmas tree for a week, the California-based brand also sells a savory seasoning trio, subscription boxes, and gift cards.

Whether we’re cooking at home or at camp, the first ingredient we reach for is usually olive oil. The Mediterranean Dish has some of the best we’ve tried, with high-quality olive oils sourced from Greece, Italy, Spain, and California. A sampler set lets you gift multiple options—and the brand’s spice selection isn’t bad either.

Some people say their love language is touch. Others say acts of service. For some, it’s caffeine, delivered shortly after waking, preferably in bed. If a caffeine addict is on your list, Equator Coffee, a longtime California roaster, offers curated coffee subscriptions that speak that love language fluently. Oh—and its instant coffee is an epic stocking stuffer for backpackers who can’t hit the trail without some jet fuel.

There are a million gizmos and gadgets out there for the espresso fanatics, the drip disciples, the press-and-plunge crowd. But what about the nitro lovers? Behold: the Cumulus Coffee Machine. An all-in-one robot for chilled coffee lovers that brings barista-grade bevies into the comfort of your own home, the machine has three settings that allow you to convert Cumulus pods into iced espresso, crisp cold brew, and creamy nitro. The nitro setting is particularly rad, as the Cumulus sources nitrogen from ambient air—no nitrogen cartridges required. And there’s no ice required, either, as the machine self-chills. This is a dream gift for any cold brew connoisseur, but especially those who order nitro on the regular.
