The tiny camera tech creator Austin Evans uses for almost everything

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

After 17 years of making tech videos online, Austin Evans has used his fair share of complicated camera gear. These days, he'd rather keep things simple.

When Mashable asked Evans what creator essentials he keeps close at VidCon 2026, the longtime YouTuber started with the obvious answer for someone who's made a career obsessing over gadgets: not one phone, but two. Then there's the tiny camera that has become one of his most versatile pieces of gear — a far cry from the enormous, expensive cameras his team once relied on.

Here's what Austin Evans keeps in his bag.

"The phone's got to be number one," Evans said. Although in his case, "phone" is technically plural.

Evans uses an iPhone primarily for shooting video, and the convenience means he can respond almost immediately when a story breaks.

"There was so much news," he said of that day's tech cycle. "I was walking into the parking garage, and I'm shooting Shorts on my phone and just sending them off to my editor."

For everything that isn't primarily video, Evans also carries a Samsung Galaxy Z Fold. His reasoning is straightforward: He likes having the bigger display inside.

Together, the two devices let him shoot, work, and keep up with an industry where news can break while he's literally walking through a parking garage.

Then there's the piece of creator gear Evans really wanted to talk about: DJI's tiny Pocket cameras. "Those cameras have been so fantastic, especially if you're on your own," he said.

They're small, stabilized, can track a subject, and can easily move between vertical and horizontal shooting — all qualities Evans thinks make them particularly useful for solo creators. His own Pocket camera has certainly been put through its paces.

"The amount of times we've had that thing strapped with a suction cup to the front of a car driving on a racetrack," he said.

When it's not speeding around a track, he has another job for it. "I plug it into my desk. It's my webcam." Evans paused. "I feel like it's a giant ad. It's not."

More than any individual gadget, Evans' creator essential is really a philosophy: The easier the tech makes it to start filming, the better.

His team once used a RED camera that he remembers as "absurdly expensive and hot and heavy" — exactly the sort of equipment designed with a full film crew in mind.

Today's smaller cameras may not replace every piece of professional equipment, but that's not really the point. "You can get legitimately really good stuff that is not crazy expensive and, most importantly, feels very much designed for a solo creator," Evans said.

"Just pull it out of your bag, press three buttons, pair a mic, and you've got fantastic-looking results."

After 17 years of creator tech, sometimes progress looks like carrying less of it.
