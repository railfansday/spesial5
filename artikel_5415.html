‘Work on what people think is important.’ U.S. Steel’s CEO has advice for Democrats after the midterms: Focus on affordability, not court fights

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

U.S. Steel CEO David Burritt wants to see Democrats use their congressional sweep to focus on what voters have been asking for: improving the cost of living and affordability, not spending the next two years fighting Trump in court.

Speaking at the Fortune 500 Innovation Forum, Burritt said Democrats have an opportunity to respond to the economic concerns that have plagued the American electorate and drove voters to the polls, warning that a focus on political battles would undermine the party’s chances in the next election.

He added that Democrats could find themselves in a difficult political position in the 2028 elections if they fail to address voters’ priorities. The midterm results shouldn’t be interpreted as an endorsement of the Democratic Party, but instead a warning for both parties that voters are dissatisfied, he said in a panel discussion with Oklahoma Governor Kevin Stitt and Fortune Executive Editor Lee Clifford.

“They were sending a message to everybody: work on what people think is important. Let’s make things affordable,” Burritt said.

The Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress in the November midterm elections, a sweeping reversal of the political fortunes for Republicans and a rebuke to President Donald Trump.

Democratic candidates repeatedly made affordability the centerpiece of their campaigns, arguing for cheaper groceries, healthcare, housing and everyday necessities.

Housing and food accounted for 46.9% of average expenditures in 2025 as housing expenditures climbed 5.8% and food spending increased 4.6%, according to the Census Bureau’s Consumer Expenditure Survey released in October.

A CBS News analysis of the election found that 89% of voters considered the cost of living a major or crucial factor in their congressional vote.

But Trump was also a factor as well, with many voters treating the election as a referendum on him. According to the Associated Press’s Voter Poll, 39% to 60% of voters across age demographics used their vote to “show opposition” to Trump—compared to only 7% to 22% voting to show support.

The poll also showed that 33% of the electorate viewed “the state of democracy” as its most important issue.

The Democratic Party has signaled plans to investigate a range of issues, including Trump’s business interests, his administration’s immigration policies, the Justice Department’s actions, and the administration’s handling of the war in Iran. Congressional committees will gain the ability to issue subpoenas, compel testimony and demand documents once the new Congress convenes in January.

But for Burritt, the danger is that Democrats will mistake their electoral victory for a mandate to pursue a political agenda rather than address the economic concerns that helped deliver their majority.

Stitt offered a similar warning, saying Republicans had taken “a beating” in the midterms but that Democrats shouldn’t interpret the win as an endorsement of every policy on their platform.

“Don’t go out and have this pendulum swing, don’t go start attacking the other side,” Stitt said. “I think the American people get turned off by that.”

Joshua Hong is a News Fellow at Fortune covering data, AI, cybersecurity, energy, and retail.
