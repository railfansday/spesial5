Will the AI stock sell-off give us sub-7% mortgage rates before the holidays?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Mortgage rates can move below 7% if the 10-year yield drops toward 4.85% and mortgage spreads improve toward 2%. Purchase applications fell 3% week over week and 18% year over year as volatility remains elevated.  AI Summary

Sub-7% mortgage rates are back on the holiday menu! Just when it looked like we had no chance for a happy holiday, AI stocks are still taking a beating and pushing money into bonds. Oil prices almost went below $80 last week too. We have three holidays left before 2027 kicks into gear, so the question now is: Can we get the 10-year yield and mortgage spreads low enough to get lower mortgage rates?

If all this market drama happened four weeks ago, I believe the 10-year yield would be under 4.85% today, but this is happening amid a rapid-fire, two-week effort by the Federal Reserve to give forward-guidance about more rate hikes coming in the system. The key level of 5.15% for the 10-year yield held last week, and with the AI sell-off and oil prices down to $83 again, we have a shot to get sub-7% mortgage rates again, but that will require the 10-year yield to get to 4.85% and head lower — and for mortgage spreads to improve back down toward 2%.Mortgage rates have had a difficult time lately with the inflation data and the Fed’s hawkish tone. In addition, we still don’t have a resolution to the Iran conflict, and when President Trump talks about bigger tariffs with our partners, it makes it harder for yields to go down. We also have to be mindful that inflation is rising while the unemployment rate is falling; if those two things change, it will be better for rates. For now, let’s focus on getting rates lower before the holidays. What can get us there?1. Stocks selling off almost got us to 4.85% again; we got as low as 4.92% Friday. The problem is the speed of the decline doesn’t help mortgage spreads. However, if we see more market selling, that can be one positive variable.2. Any positive news on the Iran conflict or limiting the trade war.3. The hope that the Democrats will resolve the conflict and trade war after Jan. 3 might get bond traders and oil traders thinking differently.We don’t have much time before the end-of-year holidays, but the above will help. However, the reverse is also true: stocks rallying, the conflict getting worse or more negative trade war headlines can lead yields and rates higher.

Mortgage spreads have recently gotten worse, which is a big variable in why rates got as high as 7.60% recently, but they’re still close to multiyear lows. We need less volatility in the bond markets, and with time, the spreads can improve again. Since September, the markets have been a bit too wild, rising and falling, and the spreads’ job is to reduce mortgage rate volatility. A big key for mortgage rates to get below 7% is getting the spreads back to 2% and under.

Historically, mortgage spreads have ranged from 1.60% to 1.80%. Last week, spreads rose to 2.18% from 2.15% a week earlier.

Purchase application data, which looks out 30-90 days, has softened as mortgage rates rose above 6.64% and are now above 7%. Rates rose aggressively during inflation week, so this data line did get hit, down 3% week-to-week and down 18% year-over-year. Again, the year-over-year comps here are rough, as mortgage rates were a lot lower last year. Earlier in the year, all we had was positive year-over-year, but rates reached the range where housing data slows down, and the comps have been very difficult.

Housing inventory had a slight decline last week and is in its seasonal decline for 2026, as the peak in inventory already happened. Inventory growth did pick up when mortgage rates headed toward 7.60%; before that, inventory growth was slow this year, and we even had some weeks when inventory was negative year-over-year. Until mid-June it was hard for the comps to show growth this year and now they’re much easier to show growth as housing demand was heading toward a nine-month high last year this time.

New listings have been in their seasonal decline since June, and nothing abnormal with this data line has happened in 2026; in fact, it’s been the best year of new listings since early 2022. One concern always with higher rates is that sellers will decide not to put their homes on the market; that has not happened this year, which is a positive sign for housing.In a normal environment, new listings range from 80,000 to 100,000 per week during peak periods. Typically, about one-third of homes see price reductions before they sell, reflecting the housing market’s dynamic nature. Overall, price-cut percentages this year have been lower than last year. This held true until rates moved above 6.64% and then above 7%. At that point, demand fell, and the year-over-year price-cut percentage rose 1%, and last week the year-over-year price-cut gap was 2% higher than last year when demand was picking up with lower rates.In my 2026 home-price forecast, I called for a national decline of -0.62% for the year.
