AI import boom pushed U.S. goods trade deficit to widest since March 2025

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Capital goods imports rose 8.5% to $171.4 billion in October as the AI buildout continued, Census data show

The U.S. Census Bureau reported on Friday that the goods trade deficit widened 10.7% to $149.5 billion in October. The gap grew $14.4 billion from $135.1 billion in September as imports outpaced exports.

Goods imports rose $21.1 billion to $365.3 billion. Goods exports rose $6.7 billion to $215.8 billion. The data are adjusted for seasonality but not for price changes.

Imports of capital goods rose 8.5% to $171.4 billion, the Census Bureau said. Industrial supplies imports rose 6.0%, and consumer goods imports rose 2.6%. On the export side, capital goods shipments rose 3.4% and automotive vehicle exports rose 5.7%.

An AI infrastructure buildout kept sucking foreign goods into the country, according to Reuters. Capital goods imports hit a record, and the deficit has now widened four months in a row to its largest since March 2025, according to Bloomberg. Bloomberg said the capital goods category covers computers and accessories along with semiconductors and telecommunications gear.

The October figure exceeded forecasts. Economists in a Reuters survey were looking for a $133 billion shortfall, according to Reuters. The median estimate from economists whom Bloomberg canvassed came to $136 billion, according to Bloomberg.

The government said Wednesday that trade took 3.21 percentage points off GDP growth for the third quarter. The economy expanded 4.3% on an annualized basis in the quarter, following growth of 2.2% between April and June.

The Census Bureau's report also covered inventories. Retail inventories rose 0.8% to $893.2 billion in October, and wholesale inventories rose 0.9% to $1.02 trillion.

The latest figures follow a September report in which goods imports set a record. Capital goods imports reached $158 billion that month as companies expanding AI capacity bought more computers and accessories. The August goods deficit had also been the largest monthly gap since March 2025.

A fuller set of October trade data will arrive Dec. 8, according to Bloomberg, adding the services balance and goods numbers adjusted for inflation.
