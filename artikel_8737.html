Trying to Stay Present During Your Family Gathering? Take a Holiday Walk.

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The last two months of the year are crammed with holiday traditions. Some are sweet and nostalgic, others revolve around food and drink, and most require some amount of socializing.

But there’s one unsung custom that deserves a moment of praise, or at least a little quiet appreciation. It combines some of the simplest mindfulness practices, gets you outdoors, and helps ensure all of the other traditions can unfold a touch more seamlessly.

This walk takes on different forms depending on your specific circumstances. It might provide some one-on-one time with a friend or relative you don’t see often. (You can make it the stereotypical “cousin walk” of your youth.) Maybe it’s an opportunity to walk any and all dogs also hanging out in the house. The holiday walk could also, appealingly, be a solo endeavor, a chance to step away from any obligations or unwelcome conversations and catch your breath.

Sometimes it’s an actual hike or a long, slow meandering down a nearby trail. Most of the time, though, it’s a simple stroll around the block.

At its core, the holiday walk is about escaping the revelry for a beat of peace and quiet. It’s a change of pace that lets you return to your specific gathering with more appreciation and less stress and social overwhelm. And it can be one of the most gratifying ways to cultivate presence…even if it means needing to excuse yourself for a short while.

It’s also a chance to hone a number of other mindfulness practices that you can take back with you to the celebration and can make your seasonal experience a more pleasant one.

Walking alone is proven to reduce feelings of stress and anxiety while boosting mental and physical health. Try including one or all of the following to make your amble even more intentional.

Getting any amount of fresh air is always a win, but adding some purpose to your inhalations and exhalations through breathing exercises can help you get the most out of your holiday walk. Try simply lengthening your exhalations, matching the beat of your inhalations and exhalations (and the spaces in between) with box breathing, or letting it all out with lion’s breath.

If you’re struggling to access feelings of gratitude (for whatever reason) this season, try turning your appreciation toward your body. Pay attention to the way your feet, legs, and core carry you; how your lungs want to breathe deeply; and how cool it is that you have this vehicle that lets you move through the world.

Another means of cultivating micro-gratitude, glimmers are just about noticing beauty and joy around you. Fallen leaves (both their color and the way they crunch underfoot), passing clouds, someone’s laugh, a cool rock, notes from a familiar song that are audible from a passing car…glimmers are everywhere. When you get back to your gathering, notice them there, too.
