Former Buzzcocks Drummer Phil Barker Dead at 65

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Phil Barker, drummer for British punk rock band the Buzzcocks from 1992 through 2006, died on Nov. 21 at age 65 on the Isle of Wight, England. His death following a battle with an unspecified form of cancer was announced by the band on Nov. 26.

“We’re saddened by the news of the passing of our longtime drummer Phil Barker,” the band wrote on their website. “He died peacefully in his sleep on Saturday, November 21, 2026 on the Isle of Wight, after a five-year battle with cancer. He is survived by Deb, his wife of 26 years.”

They continued, “Phil joined the band alongside bassist and childhood friend Tony Barber in 1992, backing Pete Shelley and Steve Diggle. He was integral to our success throughout that era, and left the band in early 2006. Just good memories. He will be missed.”

The Buzzcocks were founded in 1976 by Pete Shelley and Howard Devoto, who left the band sthe following year. The group released three albums before disbanding in 1981. They reformed in 1989 and released their fourth album, Trade Test Transmissions, in 1993. It was during this era that Barker joined the lineup as drummer alongside bassist Tony Barber. He collaborated on five albums with the group prior to Shelley’s death in 2018.

Diggle paid tribute to Barker on social media, writing, “I’m remembering the good times Phil and you were a great Drummer. Thinking of you Debbie and family. During his career, Barker also recorded with Slaughter Joe and The Modern Folk Quintet, occasionally working under the alias Phil Phuture. During the 1980s, he played with Lack of Knowledge. More recently, he performed with Crass vocalist Steve Ignorant in Stratford Mercenaries and punk band Dirt. The Buzzcocks have continued releasing music and touring since Shelley’s death. Barker left the group in 2006.

Local record shop Triple A Records paid tribute to Barker on Facebook. “We’re very sad to hear our brilliant friend Philip Barker has passed away,” they wrote. “Phil drummed on our favourite Buzzcocks records and was a keen and generous supporter of the shop. We’ll be playing some of his music today as we think about his cheery visits. RIP mate x.”
