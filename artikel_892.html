De Zerbi compares Williams-Barnett to Lamine Yamal - who is Spurs teen?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Tottenham teenager Luca Williams-Barnett starred in Spurs' 2-0 home win over Ipswich on his first start for club; the 18-year-old scored the opener and helped set up Dominic Solanke's goal; Spurs boss Roberto De Zerbi hails "special" talent and compares him to Barcelona's Lamine Yamal

Tottenham boss Roberto De Zerbi was not surprised by a dazzling full-debut display from “special” talent Luca Williams-Barnett and compared the 18-year-old to Barcelona’s Lamine Yamal after Saturday’s 2-0 win over Ipswich.

De Zerbi turned to the highly rated Spurs academy graduate after two bright cameos and he delivered, scoring on his first Premier League start after 15 minutes before a mazy run set up Dominic Solanke at the start of the second half.

After Williams-Barnett made his senior debut at the age of 16 last season, the clamour for the teenager to play has only increased during a difficult last 12 months, and De Zerbi decided 10 days ago to include him in the starting line-up against Ipswich.

During a chat on Thursday with the skilful England U19 international, De Zerbi asked Williams-Barnett to play with freedom and work hard without the ball "like Raphinha, like [Lamine] Yamal" before he agreed when asked if the teenager could reach similar heights.

After De Zerbi revealed Williams-Barnett prepared for the match on the team bus by watching YouTube clips of Ronaldinho, Eden Hazard and Neymar, he said: "Luca is a special player.

"I'm not surprised because his talent is bigger than you think. He played very well. He's lucky because he's playing in a top club with top team-mates.

"I decided to play today with Luca from 10 days and in my plan, it was [James] Maddison and Luca very close to speak the same language, to play together and the experience of Maddison I thought could be important.

"The behaviour of Maddison could help him to play with freedom. And Thursday, I spoke with Luca and I asked him two things. Just two things. To play like he plays on the park with his friends, with the same freedom.

"And to play and to work hard without the ball, like [Ousmane] Dembele, like Raphinha, like [Lamine] Yamal, because football is going in a different way and the first defenders are the attackers.

"Even if they are Ballon d'Or winners, or any way the players with talent, they have to work on the pitch."

Asked if Williams-Barnett can reach the level of Lamine Yamal, De Zerbi said: "Yes, I think so.

"And one of the best things in my career… I've seen today in the bus from the training ground to the stadium, because he was very close with me, while we were coming in the stadium, he was watching on YouTube Ronaldinho, Hazard, Neymar.

"I thought he was a special guy, because if one hour and thirty minutes before you are debuting in the Premier League, you are watching Ronaldinho, Hazard, Neymar, all these types of talent. The football is magic, no?"

Williams-Barnett joined Tottenham as a 10-year-old from Luton, his home town, and has been in the Spurs academy since.

There has been plenty of hype around the winger, whose talent was noticeable when he won the U18 Premier League goal of the season award for a long-range stunner against Leicester.

The teenager then began featuring in Spurs' first-team squads, but after being named on the bench for a handful of games in the 2024/25 season, Williams-Barnett was finally handed his debut in September 2025 by then-boss Thomas Frank as he came on as a substitute in the Carabao Cup third-round home win over Doncaster.

Eight days later, Williams-Barnett signed his first professional contract with Spurs at the age of 17.

His appearance against Doncaster proved to be his only first-team action last season as he hit a hat-trick against Leicester for Tottenham U21s before netting five goals in a win over Slavia Prague for Spurs' academy team in a UEFA Youth League game.

He was deservedly rewarded by De Zerbi for a fine youth season by playing a part in Tottenham's pre-season plans as he showed glimpses of his skill and quality during some run-outs.

But he has finally made his first-team breakthrough this season, and he should have had an assist on his Premier League debut in last month's win over Crystal Palace after he came on as a substitute to superbly tee up Mohammed Kudus, who somehow missed to add a third in the 2-0 win (watch below).

Ahead of the defeat to Leeds, De Zerbi admitted Williams-Barnett was ready for a full debut, and he considered it for the win at Chelsea last month, but he was again a late second-half substitute in the loss at Elland Road.

But substitute appearances are set to no longer be an option after his stunning full debut against Ipswich.

On the international stage, Williams-Barnett has represented England at all levels between U15s and U19s to date.

In September, he came on for Arsenal sensation Max Dowman to make his England U19 debut in a 1-0 win over the Republic of Ireland.

Tottenham and Arsenal have two of England's brightest prospects, and the pair could play major parts in north London derbies in the years to come.

Williams-Barnett was denied an assist on his full debut despite his fine work to set up Solanke's goal.

Williams-Barnett terrorised the Ipswich defence from his left-wing position to tee up the striker with a low cross for a simple 51st-minute finish.

But Ipswich goalkeeper Christian Walton's diversion into Solanke's path meant Williams-Barnett was not officially credited with the assist.

It meant Williams-Barnett, at the age of 18 years and 51 days, just missed out on becoming the second-youngest player to both score and assist in a Premier League game (Michael Owen in December 1997 at 17y 364d).

Solanke, however, hopes the club will appeal the assist decision.

He told Sky Sports: "No, you have to give that to him! It's just a tiny touch [from Walton]. Hopefully the club can sort that out."

He added on Williams-Barnett's display: "Luca was magnificent today.

"We see him in training every day. We know what a player he is. He's such a talent, honestly, and to see him come in and do that in his first Premier League start is crazy.

"We'll be celebrating with him today, but he can bring so much to us."
