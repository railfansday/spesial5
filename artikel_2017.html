It was the story of the summer, Bruno Guimarães moving from Newcastle United to Arsenal.

It was a saga that went on for weeks and weeks.

For Newcastle United fans, this Bruno Guimarães to Arsenal saga felt very similar to the previous summer’s Alexander Isak to Liverpool one.

With the summer 2026 episode of transfer window torture, so much of it was played out in the media, claim and counter claim.

Now on the day that Bruno Guimarães returns with Arsenal to St James’ Park, there has been a media exclusive on ‘What really happened with Arsenal’s ‘multiple bids’?’

Luke Edwards at The Telegraph giving this insight into what happened, stating: ‘It was the saga of the summer, with constant reports and claims from so-called experts that Arsenal made multiple bids for Guimarães that were rejected by Newcastle. The truth can be revealed now.’

In his exclusive, Luke Edwards gives the following step by step detail of what happened with the Bruno Guimarães ‘multiple bids’ from Arsenal saga…

‘In July, Newcastle were shown a written offer from Arsenal by an intermediary. It was official looking, complete with Arsenal branding and said they would be willing to pay £45m for Newcastle’s captain.’

‘It was this document that was used to justify the constant stream of reports saying a bid had been made. Newcastle, though, were bemused. Not only was the fee offered derisory, but when they asked Arsenal to confirm the bid, they were greeted with silence for weeks. As a result, Newcastle dismissed it and maintained their “no bids” stance.’

‘It is believed by some well-placed sources that the document had been acquired to pacify Guimarães, who was angry about the lack of progress being made. His representatives wanted to prove to him that Arsenal’s interest was genuine and they were working hard on his behalf. However, it was the key part of a tactic to feed into media pressure on Newcastle to sell.’

‘There were no direct conversations between the clubs until the end of July. Even then, Arsenal continued to discuss numbers around the £50m mark, adding performance-related add-ons which Newcastle told them were unacceptable.’

‘At the start of August, with Guimarães’s return to pre-season training looming and growing concern he could stay away, Newcastle issued an ultimatum. They would accept an offer of £75m paid in full, with no clauses. If Arsenal were not willing to pay it, Guimarães would not be sold. A deal was struck.’

This update fits in very much with what I have thought all along.

Whatever people want to believe about Bruno Guimarães, to me it was Arsenal to blame for most of this mess that blighted Newcastle United across summer 2026. If they had done things professionally from the start, it could and would have been a transfer that progressed very differently, rather than an embarrassing mess.

Instead of trying to use their friends in the media to force Newcastle United to sell on the cheap, Arsenal should have done things the correct way and made a formal approach to United at the very start. A transfer fee could have then been negotiated and the weeks and weeks of nonsense avoided.
