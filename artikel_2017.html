Mauricio Pochettino to decide Diogo Dalot fate after 'unfair' Man United twist - Club adviser

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Manchester United right-back Diogo Dalot has become an "unfair" scapegoat at the club in recent months.

That is according to former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who does not believe that the Portuguese has deserved to be singled out.

Dalot has faced huge criticism from the supporters in recent weeks, and was even loudly jeered by the Old Trafford crowd when he was substituted during the recent draw with Aston Villa.

Nevertheless, Wyness does not expect the Red Devils to be rushing into any hasty decisions regarding the defender's long-term future.

Dalot is out of contract in the summer of 2028, but like several of their first-team stars, United have the option to extend that by a further year.

Michael Carrick opted to persist with the 27-year-old in recent weeks, but with his dismissal now confirmed, the goalposts may have moved for Dalot.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that Mauricio Pochettino has to keep picking Dalot, and cannot afford to damage his confidence by dropping him.

In a long-term sense, the Scottish businessman believes that it will be down to the incoming boss to decide whether Dalot is good enough to be part of the squad in the future.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained that Dalot needs an arm around his shoulder right now.

"Look, if Carrick was still there, I'd be saying, look, you know, carry on picking him; don't hide him," he said.

"It's really unfair that the club have picked a scapegoat like this, because there are much bigger issues than Diogo Dalot in terms of what United have got going on. And he's being played to a certain degree out of position.

"It's wrong when I see a scapegoat being picked like that, and I say the manager should be supporting a player, but it's difficult, though, if the manager is being changed.

"They'll make that decision in due course once the Pochettino team come in, which looks pretty much certain they will do."

Wyness previously told Football Insider earlier this week that Pochettino will only provide a "short-term" solution for United's issues.

The Argentine has already agreed to take the reins at Old Trafford, and could potentially be confirmed as their new boss by the end of the week.

However, Wyness believes that while Pochettino might improve things short-term, it will need more than another change of manager to get the club back on track.

The boardroom expert believes that significant change at executive level remains a must next summer if United are to steady the ship long-term.
