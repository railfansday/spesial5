Man Utd make worst start in 40 years after being hauled back by 10-man Spurs

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Manchester United fans were never going to pass up the opportunity to aim a volley of abuse in the direction of their bitter rivals.

By the end of another deeply unconvincing performance, though, they were more preoccupied by the problems afflicting their own team than the cheating scandal engulfing Manchester City.

Against City at Old Trafford last month, United lost to a team who were down to 10 men after just 23 minutes.

This time around they could not hold on to a lead against a Tottenham Hotspur side also navigating a numerical disadvantage following Rodrigo Bentancur’s sending off.

The sum total of it all is that United have now made their worst start to a season for 40 years, which is saying something given the traumas of the post-Ferguson era, and perhaps more disconcertingly, look to all intents and purposes like a team going backwards.

The bounce they enjoyed under Michael Carrick last season en route to Champions League qualification is over and, after just six points from as many games, it is hard to shake the feeling that it is going to be anything but a serious battle to finish in the top four or five.

The supporters did not hide their disgruntlement. There were boos around the half hour mark after an insipid opening when Spurs did everything but score and a slow, sluggish, dishevelled United almost looked impervious to their troubles.

Carrick had called for his players to show some “fire” coming into this match, but instead watched them ambling around like it was all a terrible inconvenience. The ease with which Spurs ran around them was alarming. There were more jeers at the interval and again at full time.

Carrick admitted that “hurt” although not as much you suspect as the sight of players feigning interest and then throwing away three points when they had finally summoned some fight to lead courtesy of Bryan Mbeumo’s goal.

Bryan Mbeumo slots it home to give Manchester United the lead against Spurs! 🎯 https://t.co/7yKnpyhfUe

It was a lovely finish after good work by the largely anonymous Matheus Cunha, and when Bentancur was sent off five minutes later after butting Luke Shaw, it should have been the cue to see out the game. To fail to do so against a side as toothless as Tottenham made it all the worse. Leading late on against Everton last month, they also failed to get over the line. It cannot keep happening.

Equally, United cannot play like they did in the first half here when they face Atlético Madrid in the Spanish capital on Tuesday night or at Elland Road against bitter rivals Leeds five days later and expect to get anything from either game. They are not the sort of matches Carrick would want with his team in this shape, but they need to find some inspiration from somewhere.

Carlos Baleba coming on around the hour mark and making a difference on his debut will at least offer Carrick some grounds for encouragement. The £65m midfield signing from Brighton offers some energy and urgency and looks a more natural bedfellow for Kobbie Mainoo than Youri Tielemans, who was woeful against Spurs and should have come off earlier.

In truth, Tielemans was not alone. Throughout the team there was a disconnect and unease and Carrick admitted it became contagious.

“We just couldn’t find a way to get ourselves into the game, whether that was with the ball or without the ball,” the United manager said. “We were a little bit slow with the decision-making, didn’t quite see the passing areas, the space. You don’t want it to happen, but sometimes it can and it probably spread through the team a little bit too much.”

It does not help that Bruno Fernandes is being hampered by an injury he has been carrying for weeks or that Carrick has no natural centre-forward to call upon with Benjamin Sesko again sidelined by the recurrence of a worrying shin problem. Amad Diallo did return here after a lengthy lay-off of his own, but did not look match fit.

In defence, Lisandro Martínez did enough to put off Dominic Solanke, who fired over from Sávio’s cross in the second half, but this was otherwise another poor showing from the Argentine centre-back, one of several positions where United need upgrades.

United needed shaking badly, but when Carrick turned to his bench he called upon Joshua Zirkzee, a fitful forward the club wanted to sell this summer, and right-back Diogo Dalot in addition to Baleba.

Marcus Rashford cannot have been fit given he was only introduced in the 95th minute and perhaps the same was true of the unused Mason Mount. As for £50m summer signing Andrey Santos, this was another league game when the Brazilian midfielder did not get a look in.

United fans had taunted Spurs when a goal and a man up. “You’re going down with the City,” they crowed, a nod to the possibility of City being expelled or relegated from the Premier League after being found guilty of breaching more than 100 financial rules.

United fans had also revived their Stretford End “ticker” after reaching the conclusion that none of City’s titles under Abu Dhabi ownership had been won fairly so, in their eyes, it is now 50 years since their rivals last won anything legitimately. City, of course, continue to maintain their innocence and have lodged an appeal.

But United fans were unable to crow for too long once Solanke levelled.

It had been similar early in the game when the home fans had trained their sights on City. “You cheating b--------, you know what you are”, they sang lustily.

But as the match wore on, those chants began to quieten as the crowd, taking their lead from the team, fell decidedly flat.

Carrick is going to have to find a spark from somewhere and quickly.

Speaking to BBC Match of the Day, Carrick said: “It was a mixed game. We found it tough to start with. For a number of reasons, we were not quite there. You could see it was a challenging time.

“But the lads grew into the game, in the second half I thought we were better in everything. To get the game as we wanted it, in such a good position - we should see it through. We have to manage those situations. That’s two late goals which have hurt us, and things look very different. That’s the reality.”

When asked about the boos at half-time and full-time, he replied: “It’s not nice, it hurts a lot because I care about this club. But I understand the disappointment, the reality is what it is, we need to get back to winning games.”

When asked about Solanke, he replied: “He is a good player for Tottenham. There for not too many strikers better than Solanke.
