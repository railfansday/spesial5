AI Bubble Keeps Bursting: SoftBank Crashes 15% as Asia’s Chip Giants Follow Nvidia Lower

The AI bubble took another hit today, November 25, as SoftBank Group shares crashed 14.8% in Tokyo to ¥2,696.5. It was one of the ten worst days for the stock since 2000, and the second drop of nearly 15% in four trading sessions.

The selling spread across Asia’s AI supply chain. SK Hynix fell 11%, Samsung Electronics 9.9%, Advantest 12.9% and Tokyo Electron 12.5%. The Nikkei 225 lost 2.8%.

It followed another rough night on Wall Street. On Tuesday, Nvidia fell 5.9%, AMD 8.9% and CoreWeave 11.9%. Anthropic, the newest AI stock, closed at $81.91, below its $83 IPO price.

SoftBank is now down 69% from its June record. So is the AI bubble bursting, and how far could it go?

SoftBank is one of the biggest bets on AI in the stock market. It owns most of Arm, the chip designer, and has poured billions into OpenAI and AI data centers. When AI stocks fall, SoftBank tends to fall harder.

Wednesday’s drop fits that pattern. The stock opened at ¥2,849.5, already down 10%, and hit ¥2,652 before a small bounce. The day before, Nvidia gave back a quarter of Monday’s 31% rebound on Jensen Huang’s return as CEO, and Anthropic broke its IPO price.

To measure the AI bubble’s deflation, we indexed the big AI names to October 6, the day the Nasdaq set its record close.

Measured from each stock’s own record high, the damage is deeper. CoreWeave is down 77%, Oracle 67% and SK Hynix 65%. Samsung and Tokyo Electron have both halved.

Crypto has not escaped. Bitcoin has tracked AI stocks lower since October. That suggests the same investors are cutting risk across both markets.

Nvidia trades around the clock on Hyperliquid as a perpetual futures contract, a futures contract with no expiry date. On Wednesday morning, xyz:NVDA was at $148.64, about 4% below Tuesday’s close and below its 50-day exponential moving average (EMA), an average that gives recent prices more weight, at $183.40.

The AI bubble has not popped in one go. It is leaking, one sell-off at a time. For now, the levels to watch are SoftBank’s ¥2,652 low in Tokyo and Nvidia’s $144 support.
