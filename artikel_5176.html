Ethereum unveils quantum-safe accounts, with a costly catch

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Ethereum researchers propose quantum-resistant accounts by 2027, with higher transaction costs until scaling upgrades arrive.

Ethereum research group Ethlabs has proposed giving users a route to quantum-resistant accounts before Ethereum can process those transactions at full scale.

Its Nov. 11 plan would let users adopt the protection earlier, but their quantum-enabled transactions would cost more than ordinary transactions because they take up more space.

The proposal pqai27 targets readiness for quantum-resistant accounts and consensus in 2027. Full-capacity support is a stretch goal for that year, and the target remains a proposal.

At “pqai-ready,” Ethereum would have post-quantum consensus and every user would have a post-quantum route for their account. At “pqai-scaled,” it would support full-capacity quantum-enabled transactions and blobs, the data objects used by rollups.

The first stage would allow users to migrate to post-quantum accounts and transact with a larger footprint. Signature aggregation and post-quantum data availability would provide the later path to full scale.

Ethlabs proposes an optional fallback to calldata from quantum-enabled transactions, carrying their data in regular blocks before post-quantum blobs are available.

Ethlabs places an orderly user migration, including tooling and wallet support, outside the network upgrade schedule. Protocol readiness would leave practical work for wallets and users before adoption is complete.

Draft EIP-8141, known as Frame Transactions, provides part of the proposed foundation. It would let account code define how transactions are validated and how gas is paid, creating a native route away from today's elliptic-curve signatures and supporting key rotation.

Ethlabs’ plan pairs Frames with the SPHINCS post-quantum signature scheme and other account proposals. Upgrading consensus remains a separate, substantial task, with the choice of design unresolved.

Ethereum.org’s Hegotá roadmap, last updated Oct. 16, lists Frames as scheduled for inclusion in that upgrade, which remains in planning with an unconfirmed date. That earlier listing is separate from Ethlabs’ new proposal for broader quantum readiness in 2027.

Ethlabs says it will withdraw its Quick Slots proposal for faster slots from Hegotá and redirect its resources toward pqai27.

For users considering the proposed account route, the practical milestones are wallet migration support and the cost of quantum-enabled transactions, followed by the aggregation and data upgrades needed to use them at full scale.
