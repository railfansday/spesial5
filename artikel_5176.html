'Pretty Low Demand': Dogecoin Co-Founder Explains $2.55 Billion Crypto Market Crash

Dogecoin co-founder Billy Markus, known on X as Shibetoshi Nakamoto, reacted to the recent market decline, which saw over $2.56 billion in liquidations. He hinted at weak demand as a possible reason behind the massive sell-off.

Markus earlier wrote in a post that "Black Friday came early." This came as the broader financial markets dropped, not just crypto: tech stocks, gold, copper, and Asian equities all fell.

Markus’s post garnered attention from the Dogecoin community and the wider crypto community. A Dogecoin community member responded: “Am I retarded for thinking crypto is about to go up?”

@alphadoge777 well it always goes up and down 🤣 but the market has been looking pretty low demand for awhile now

"Well, it always goes up and down," Markus said, pointing to crypto's inherent volatility and adding that the market had been experiencing "pretty low demand for a while now."

His comments come as the crypto market continues to crash, highlighting concerns about the lack of buying interest needed to prop up prices.

Markus did not point to a specific catalyst for the decline, but his remarks highlight an important ingredient for a bullish comeback in the market: demand.

The crypto market selloff deepened on Saturday, with Bitcoin hitting its lowest level since September 2024 and extending a brutal run for risk assets. Dogecoin was trading in the red, down 8.13% in the last 24 hours to $0.528 and down 16% weekly. Dogecoin fell to $0.05 earlier on Saturday, a low not seen since June 2022.

Data from Coinglass at press time show that $2.56 billion was liquidated in crypto positions over the past 24 hours; longs accounted for 89.2% of this total, at $2.28 billion, while short liquidations came in at $276,040.

Analysts pointed to rising real, or inflation-adjusted, U.S. Treasury yields as a key driver of the ongoing sell-off, which extends a bear market that began in October of last year.

Crypto investors are searching for the next positive catalyst after U.S. crypto regulation — a longtime market driver — fizzled in 2026. The broader crypto market has dropped since the Clarity Act faced a setback in September.
