Trump scores key win over House progressive accused of assaulting ICE agent

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Rep. LaMonica McIver confronted DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin during a heated House hearing on Thursday.

The Trump administration scored another win in court Thursday over a House progressive who was accused of assaulting federal immigration agents at a facility in New Jersey in 2025.

Rep. LaMonica McIver, D-N.J., was denied her request that the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 3rd Circuit reconsider its August 2026 decision that would have her face at least two of the three charges related to a confrontation between Democrats and Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) at Delaney Hall in Newark, New Jersey.

McIver previously tried to invoke the Constitution's Speech or Debate Clause in order to evade the charges, but the federal court's three-judge panel denied that motion in a 2-1 decision earlier this year. Thursday's order upholds that decision.

"Donald Trump continues to send warning shots to Democrats in Congress and in all levels of power — but their case against me right now, in broad daylight, goes well beyond a warning. Because of a weaponized Department of Justice and because I dared to do my job and conduct oversight of ICE, I am facing 17 years in prison," McIver said in a statement. "Now, due to the disappointing decision by the Third Circuit, we are all one step closer to a world in which the President of the United States can try to imprison people simply because he does not like what they stand for."

McIver called the result "disappointing" and said she and her lawyers "are looking at every possible option for what comes next."

An unannounced visit to the Delaney Hall detention center by a group of Democratic officials devolved into chaos in May 2025 that culminated in a physical standoff between federal lawmakers and ICE agents and the arrest of Newark Mayor Ras Baraka. McIver was charged with three counts, including assaulting and impeding federal officers.

McIver initially argued that her actions fell under her constitutionally protected duties as a member of Congress and, therefore, she could not be prosecuted.

Rep. LaMonica McIver, D-N.J., exits the grounds at Delaney Hall ICE detention center on May 9, 2025, in Newark, N.J. (Angelina Katsanis/AP)

But a 2-1 decision issued in August 2026 by Biden-appointed Judge Cindy Chung and Trump-appointed Judge Stephanos Bibas stated that McIver's argument of legislative immunity would not stand for two of the three counts, while the third count was sent back to a lower court for further review.

Court documents say McIver allegedly "slammed her forearm" into a federal officer trying to arrest Baraka. On that count, the federal appellate court agreed with a lower court decision that her "alleged intervention into the Mayor’s questionable arrest had no cognizable connection to any legislative function" protected by the Constitution.

The second count alleged that McIver used both of her forearms to "strike [an officer] as she returned inside of the secured area of Delaney Hall."

Newark's Delaney Hall, being used by ICE, has seen a significant amount of controversy over the last two years. (Michael Nigro/Getty Images)

The two judges wrote, "Like the acts charged in Count One, the acts of pushing past and forcibly striking someone are unambiguously non-legislative: they are not integral to the legislative process."

Judge Thomas Ambro wrote in dissent, "When an Executive Branch official prevents a Member from entering a DHS facility, she is denied effective access to an oversight inspection."

The case could make it all the way to the Supreme Court if McIver continues fighting against the charges.
