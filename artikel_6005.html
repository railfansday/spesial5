I'm not one to tell you to rush into applying for a credit card just because there's a limited-time offer. After all, there's virtually always an increased bonus available somewhere.

But when a card I've personally used and recommended for years adds hundreds of dollars in extra value to its welcome offer, that's something I want to make sure you don't accidentally miss out on.

And that's exactly what's happening with the Capital One Venture Rewards Credit Card.

Right now, the card is offering 75,000 bonus miles after you spend $4,000 on purchases in the first three months, plus a limited-time $300 Capital One Travel stays credit to use on eligible hotel and vacation rental bookings during your first cardholder year.

That's at least $1,050 in potential travel value if you redeem the miles at 1 cent each toward eligible travel purchases and use the full $300 credit. And with just a low $95 annual fee, it's a compelling offer on a card that earns two miles per dollar on everyday purchases.

But here's the catch: Capital One has confirmed that this limited-time offer is ending soon.

Apply now for a limited-time offer on the Capital One Venture Rewards Credit Card: Earn more than $1,000 in travel bonuses with a limited-time $300 Capital One Travel stays credit, plus earn 75,000 bonus miles once you spend $4,000 on purchases within the first 3 months of account opening.

Here's why I think this is an offer worth considering before it disappears.

The Venture Rewards Card has maintained its $95 annual fee since its introduction, even as its benefits and welcome offers have evolved.

And right now, its welcome offer is among the highest-value publicly available offers we've seen on the card in recent years.

Redeeming the 75,000 bonus miles at 1 cent each toward eligible travel purchases would give you $750 in value. Add the limited-time $300 Capital One Travel stays credit, and you're looking at up to $1,050 in combined travel value. Talk about an excellent return for a $95 annual fee.

And if you're willing to transfer those miles to airline and hotel partners, TPG's November 2026 valuations put the miles at approximately $1,388 — bringing the total potential value, including the $300 credit, to up to $1,688.

But since Capital One has already told us this offer is ending soon, I'd expect that your days to get the $300 Capital One Travel stays credit as part of the bonus are very limited, so i'd suggest to act soon to take advantage of the bonus.

One of my unwritten rules of earning points and miles is to never settle for earning just 1 point or mile per dollar spent. Sure, it's better than earning nothing, but why not get more out of your everyday purchases?

That's one of the reasons I've recommended the Capital One Venture Rewards Card for years. It earns at least 2 miles per dollar on every purchase, without having to keep track of rotating bonus categories or juggle multiple cards.

Not to mention, most other cards in its price range can't beat that earning rate.

TPG values Capital One miles at 1.85 cents each, meaning that earning rate represents a potential 3.7% return toward travel when you maximize the value of your miles through transfer partners.

That's an excellent return for a card with a $95 annual fee, especially if you don't want to have to juggle multiple cards to maximize every purchase.

Another reason I've long liked the Venture Rewards card is that it offers both straightforward and more advanced ways to redeem miles.

You can keep things on easy mode and simply redeem them at 1 cent each to cover eligible travel purchases made with the card. That means you can book flights, hotels, rental cars, train tickets, or other qualifying travel purchases exactly how and where you want and then use your miles to offset those charges.

Since miles are worth 1 cent each when redeemed that way, a $600 travel charge put on the card would take 60,000 miles to offset. I've redeemed Capital One miles this way over the years, and I appreciate having that flexibility.

But when you want to stretch your miles further, transferring them to Capital One's airline and hotel partners (and periodic transfer bonuses) can unlock some excellent redemption opportunities.

Award availability varies, and taxes and fees may apply. But having both redemption options is what makes Capital One miles so appealing, whether you're new to points and miles or have been collecting them for years.

I'm a huge advocate for finding ways to spend less time waiting in airport security lines. That's why I think Global Entry and TSA PreCheck are two of the most useful travel programs to consider.

Global Entry applications are $120 and include TSA PreCheck benefits. And this $95-per-year card will cover that application fee once every four years, which is just another way it outperforms its annual-fee weight class.

Here's a tip: If you already have Global Entry or TSA PreCheck, you can use the card's application fee credit for a family member or frequent travel companion by charging their application fee to your card.

If you're looking for a travel rewards card with a low annual fee can comfortably meet the $4,000 minimum spending requirement in three months and are eligible for the welcome bonus, I'd strongly consider the Capital One Venture while this limited-time offer is still available.

I especially like it for travelers who want to earn transferable miles without having to manage multiple bonus categories.

On the other hand, if you are unlikely to use the $300 Capital One Travel stays credit or would need to spend more than normal to earn the bonus, this offer may not be the right fit.

The current welcome offer makes an already compelling card even more appealing, particularly if you're hoping to take 2027 trips with points and miles.
