sanrivatti ONE takes the forward lean of motorcycle racing into hypercar design

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

inspired by the physical immediacy of motorcycle riding, the apex position moves the driver forward in the cockpit, challenging conventional assumptions about visibility, control, and the experience of speed.

Sanrivatti ONE, the debut hypercar of the company, is developed around a driving architecture that places the driver in a forward-leaning, head-forward position. The vehicle brings together an experimental approach to cockpit ergonomics and a lightweight, naturally aspirated performance platform.

Τhe company’s proprietary Apex Position moves the driver forward and upright compared with the reclined position found in most hypercars. Inspired by the physical immediacy of riding a high-performance motorcycle, the arrangement aims to create a closer relationship between the driver’s body, the vehicle, and the circuit. According to Sanrivatti, the position provides a more open field of vision, a heightened perception of acceleration and cornering, and a more precise understanding of the car’s placement on track.

The Dutch automotive company introduces the Apex Position and influences the relationship between the driver, cockpit, steering system, pedals, and surrounding bodywork. The intention is to make driving a more physical experience, where the driver feels more directly connected to the movements of the vehicle.

Sanrivatti says that the position provides 3.2 times more of the circuit in view at Corner Three at Silverstone, compared with a selection of ten hypercars and track cars. The company also reports a vertical field of view of 28 degrees, against 15.5 degrees for its benchmark hypercar.

The approach has also informed the exterior proportions of the ONE. Its low, elongated body features a sweeping upper line, a single-piece canopy, and pronounced through-body air tunnels. Sanrivatti cites the peregrine falcon in a high-speed dive and the precision of modern fighter aircraft as references for the design. The silhouette appears shaped around the driver’s forward position, with the cockpit integrated into a body that emphasizes directional movement and aerodynamic expression.

Sanrivatti targets an 800 hp output from a naturally aspirated 5.5-liter Judd V10, paired with a six-speed Hewland sequential gearbox and rear-wheel drive. A carbon-fiber monocoque forms the structural foundation, while the targeted dry weight is 1,100 kg.

The company also lists a target torque of 700 Nm and a projected maximum speed of 330 km/h. All these figures remain provisional and subject to development, testing, and validation.

The choice of a naturally aspirated V10 and sequential transmission points toward a driving experience centered on direct mechanical feedback. The ONE proposes an interpretation of contemporary performance that places physical engagement and driver perception at the forefront.
