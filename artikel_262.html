Raising the stakes for enterprise networks with AI modernization

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Understanding how NaaS architectures offer a flexible solution to enterprise network modernization efforts.

Enterprise networks are under severe strain. As companies rush to deploy artificial intelligence, the technology is quickly becoming the biggest source of demand on the network. Now, agentic AI is adding to the load. Tasks conducted by AI agents generate an estimated 450% more network traffic than the same tasks done by human workers.

As AI inference shifts from hyperscale training clouds—the huge centralized data centers where raw AI models ingest vast datasets and learn—to real-time, latency-sensitive applications, the need for on-premises or near-premises infrastructure grows. As a result, the gap between enterprise networks that are ready to support AI and those that are not is widening. As few as half of enterprises believe their current networks can handle AI’s bandwidth and latency demands, according to a recent study.

This report explores how treating network modernization as an on-going strategy rather than a one-off project can help organizations adapt in the face of disruption and uncertainty.

AI may force companies to shift from a cloud-first approach to routing workloads. Training AI requires server-to-server data center architectures, while inference calls for low-latency connectivity close to users. Enterprises may need to fundamentally reconsider their network architecture to meet these requirements.

AI inference is pushing infrastructure back on premises. Inference typically requires real-time processing and is sensitive to latency, making hosting such capabilities on distant hyperscale clouds neither economical nor practical. Inconsistent latency can lead to AI model failures, driving up queue wait times, saturating GPUs loads, and causing bottlenecks in data flows. Large enterprises may need on-premises or near-premises inference, which reverses years of cloud-first consolidation and forces new approaches to data center architecture, connectivity, and physical infrastructure.

AI workloads are stressing physical network capacity in unexpected ways. The traffic increase from agentic AI, combined with inference moving on-premises, is exposing bandwidth bottlenecks in legacy underlay infrastructure.

Network modernization is an ongoing project that requires a holistic approach, allowing companies to adapt with ease. A combination of strong architectural governance, reusable design templates, and retained internal expertise supports organizations in adapting to disruptive events without rebuilding their infrastructure from scratch each time. Such events could include mergers and acquisitions, changing vendors, or technology upgrades.

NaaS and AI-driven observability can improve efficiency and reduce complexity in network operations. Network-as-a-service (NaaS) and AI-driven observability can offer companies benefits like faster, more flexible provisioning and lower costs.

Data sovereignty and sovereign AI will create infrastructure challenges for global enterprises. Regulators worldwide are passing laws requiring data and AI processing to remain within national borders. This conflicts with how multinational companies currently operate, requiring the build-out of sovereign-capable infrastructure for each geography, which will require significant new investment and complicate operations.
