Florida Man Sentenced for Stealing Millions of Dollars from Lenders Using Sham Cosmetic Surgery Clinics and Stolen Identities

Today, A Wellington, Florida, man was sentenced today to 84 months in prison for using sham cosmetic surgery clinics and the stolen identities of more than 100 people to defraud medical lenders of approximately $2.3 million, and for fraudulently seeking COVID-19 disaster relief loans.

“Jacquez Tullis built fake medical clinics using stolen identities, including those of practicing doctors and deceased individuals, and used them to steal millions from lenders,” said Assistant Attorney General Colin M. McDonald of the Justice Department’s National Fraud Enforcement Division. “When the pandemic hit, Tullis then tried to exploit taxpayer-funded relief meant for struggling small businesses. Today’s sentence makes clear that the Fraud Division will hold accountable those who abuse the public’s trust, whether they steal from private lenders or from the American people.”

“The defendant’s actions represent a serious breach of trust, exploiting the identities of innocent individuals and manipulating financial institutions for personal gain,” said Special Agent in Charge Brett D. Skiles of FBI Miami. “Fraud schemes of this magnitude undermine the integrity of our financial system and inflict real harm on victims whose identities are stolen and misused. The FBI remains committed to protecting the American public, holding accountable those who exploit others for financial gain, and pursuing those who believe they can hide behind elaborate schemes.”

According to court documents and admissions in his plea, from approximately April 2018 through February 2024, Jaquez Tullis, 42, and his co-conspirators created six sham medical clinics that claimed to offer cosmetic surgery and other medical services, but provided no real care.  The conspirators registered the clinics with the Florida Department of State using stolen personal identifying information of practicing doctors and others without their knowledge or consent.  In some cases, they listed those doctors as clinic owners on applications to become approved merchants with six medical lenders.  Those lenders, two FDIC-insured banks and four financial technology companies financed out-of-pocket procedures not covered by insurance.

Once the clinics were approved, the conspirators submitted fraudulent credit card applications to the lenders in the names of more than 100 individuals, seeking approximately $3.1 million.  The applications falsely represented that the funds were for cosmetic surgery and other services the clinics would provide.  In fact, the individuals did not know about or authorize the applications and did not need or want any treatment. According to death records, some had died before the dates they supposedly received care.

Separately, from April through August 2020, Tullis submitted at least nine fraudulent applications on behalf of certain businesses to the U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) for Economic Injury Disaster Loan (EIDL) loans, a federal stimulus program authorized as part of the Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security (CARES) Act.  Tullis obtained funds under EIDL program by submitting false and fraudulent loan applications that collectively sought $696,860 in EIDL funds. The loan applications falsely represented, among other things, the business’ number of employees and revenue.

Tullis pleaded guilty on July 21, 2026, to conspiracy to commit bank fraud and aggravated identity theft.

U.S. District Judge Rodolfo A. Ruiz, II, of the Southern District of Florida sentenced Jacquez Dion Tullis, to 60 months on the bank fraud conspiracy count and a consecutive 24 months on the aggravated identity theft count, for a total of 84 months in prison, to be followed by 5 years of supervised release. Tullis was also ordered to pay $2,309,859.44 in restitution, $200 in special assessment, and forfeiture money judgement of $2,309,859.44
