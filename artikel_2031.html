Post-match Blackburn Rovers footage emerges involving David Bentley & Brad Friedel

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

There were a few modern-day Blackburn Rovers legends in attendance at Turf Moor on Sunday, as the spoils were shared in the Cotton Mills derby.

Burnley led twice in the game through goals from Largie Ramazani and Jaydon Banel, but were pegged back both times by Andri Gudjohnsen and Todd Cantwell from the penalty spot.

Neither side were able to find a winner, though, meaning that the Clarets' winless league run at home surpasses 400 days and 23 games, equaling the English record set by Sheffield Wednesday last season.

It also means that Blackburn are still without a win over their East Lancashire rivals since March 2010, and despite how disjointed this past week must have been, given the news that Tony Mowbray had to step back from his role as head coach for health reasons, some critics, and Burnley fans especially, felt this to be a missed opportunity for the away side.

That didn't stop Blackburn fans from having fun at a ground they rarely pick up points at, and former players were in attendance to enjoy the occasion with them.

David Bentley, who spent three seasons at Ewood Park between 2005 and 2008, during which he made the majority of his seven England caps, was in the away end and led chants in the concourse, and after the game, he linked up with former Premier League winner Collin Hendry and his ex-Blackburn teammate Brad Friedel.

Friedel, who is the new sporting director at League Two side Shrewsbury Town, was also part of those squads under Mark Hughes alongside Bentley. Blackburn finished sixth, tenth and seventh in those three seasons, making the semi-finals of the EFL Cup in 2006 and the FA Cup in 2007.

The American goalkeeper's time at Blackburn extended before Bentley's, too, and at Ewood Park, he helped get the side back into the Premier League in 2001 and won the EFL Cup in 2002.

It may have been close to 20 years since they both last played for Blackburn, but they're still iconic amongst the fanbase, and they had some fun with the Rovers fans as they were made to stay back after full-time.

Blackburn fans were joyous in that away end at Turf Moor on Sunday, even despite not picking up all three points, and were happy to see their former players in attendance, too.

Bentley, especially, was living the fans' dream in the Cotton Mills derby, both in the stands and on the pitch after the game, and Blackburn supporters enjoyed being with him during the game.

"Gotta love Bentley," one fan commented. "I want some of what he drinks every day! What a geezer 🤩," another said.

A third commented on the link-up with Friedel, reminding them of their last truly glorious era at Ewood Park, before this current spell in the EFL, saying, "This is the Blackburn of Mark Hughes. Missing them old days."

However, there were plenty of Burnley fans in the replies to the Blackburn tweet, too, reminding them that they hadn't won in the derby, and likely shouldn't be celebrating the point so hard, given how dire the Clarets have been so far this season.

"Congratulations on the draw lads 👏 👏," one Burnley fan replied. "Celebrating like they’ve won the league not just drawn with possibly the worst Burnley teams in 30 years," another said.

"Can’t wait to tell the grandkids about that famous 2-2 win at Burnley, even better than the 1-1 win last time," was also among the reactions from Clarets fans. Evidently, with just one league win all season, they don't think Blackburn should be getting too giddy about leaving Turf Moor with just a point.

Ultimately, Blackburn don't beat Burnley often, so when a decent result away from home can come about, they'll more than enjoy themselves, especially if some former players from a better period in the club's history are in attendance.

Also, Rovers avoided what could have been one of their most embarrassing defeats in the derby, handing their rivals only their second win of the season and their first win at home in 400 days.

Perhaps they'll look back on the game now and feel it's an opportunity missed to end a 16-year derby hoodoo, but at the time, it was a fine point, and those in the away end evidently enjoyed their day out; David Bentley, especially.
