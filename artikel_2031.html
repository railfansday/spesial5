Keith Wyness shares ‘key’ to Newcastle unlocking Matias Fernandez-Pardo

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Newcastle United must find a “settled role” for new forward Matias Fernandez-Pardo if they are to get the best out of him moving forward.

That is according to former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that Matthias Jaissle’s consistent team changes aren’t helping the big-money buy.

The Magpies shelled out £51million to sign Fernandez-Pardo from Lille in the latter stages of the summer transfer window, but he is still waiting for his first goal involvement in the Premier League.

Wyness does not expect the 21-year-old to be regretting his decision to move to St James’ Park just yet, but has acknowledged that stage will likely come if things don’t improve.

Fernandez-Pardo has started just four league games for his new club so far this season, and has been used in a variety of different positions.

The Belgium international has already played as a central striker, a winger and an attacking midfielder, and Wyness does not believe that is helping his cause.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that Fernandez-Pardo will start to become worried about his development if things haven’t changed for him by this time next year.

The starlet notched eight goals and six assists for Lille last term, but has struggled to contribute for Newcastle by any means since his move.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider’s Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained how the starlet needs to be given clarity over what is being asked of him.

“I don't think so, not regret. I think the problem they've got is there's no settled role for him. It's not so much him getting time or anything like that,” he said.

“There's just no specific role for him to settle into. I think any regret would come maybe a year from now in November, next November.

“I think it's way too early and I don't think he's really had a fair chance to get going. But that's part of this confusion we just mentioned, where there is no settled role for a player of this talent.

“So that's part of the negative side, but there are other positives as well. But still, I think he's somebody that, given time, will come good. He's the right age, but they've got to find a role for him. And that's the key thing.”

Former Tottenham and Everton scout Bryan King told Football Insider earlier this week that Jaissle’s methods are “not at all convincing” for the Newcastle squad at the moment.

The group are growing frustrated with the German already, despite him only taking the reins following Eddie Howe’s exit in the summer.

King believes that Newcastle have made a “mistake” by appointing the 38-year-old, who has managed in Saudi Arabia in recent years.

Jaissle is already under pressure after an inconsistent start to the season, and suggestions of players being confused by his methods.
