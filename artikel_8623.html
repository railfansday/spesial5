Mass monster Jay Cutler, who claimed the title of Mr. Olympia on four occasions between 2006 and 2010, was never afraid to step away from the barbell if there was an efficient way to sculpt his back. Recently, the bodybuilder revealed that he liked to begin each back session with cable reverse-grip pulldowns. Here’s why.

“This was always the first exercise I started my back routine with,” confirmed Cutler in a recent video for his 5.7 million IG followers.

Techniques used by Jay Cutler to string together 25 consecutive top 2 finishes.

Utilizing a supinated (underhand) grip, you’ll primarily tax the lats while also recruiting the biceps and mid back. For many, the supinated grip is more comfortable on the shoulders than the pronated (overhand) grip. So, seated at the cable station, you’ll replicate a similar movement with the upper body to a chin up as you lower the bar. This grip also allows for heavier loads, making it perfect for adding upper body mass.

Tip: Keep your torso still and pull with the elbows until the bar is lowered just under your chin. Don’t pull any further down, or the load will shift to your forearms and shoulders.

“When you do something, reverse grip, it’s going to give you more flexion in those lats. It’s going to allow you to pull a little deeper and engage certain fibers,” explained Cutler who, as a competitive bodybuilder, was all about lifting heavy weights to create a winning physique.

In his video, Cutler kept the distance between his hands a little over shoulder width. “Pull down, focus on 12 repetitions,” he added. The Olympia icon also explained that the reverse grip lat pulldown was essential to building the winged appearance of his lats. “So, when I was doing that front double bicep (pose), it really yanked those lats out,” shared the legend. “And created that great V-taper.”

For Jay Cutler, the reverse grip lat pulldown was key to building muscle thickness. To try it for yourself, avoid using momentum and concentrate on the muscle contraction instead, as you lower the bar. Then slowly allow the bar to return skywards for additional time under tension.
