976 triangular modules compose star-patterned timber canopy in switzerland

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

edyta augustynowicz and summum engineering construct stellar pavilion, a 19.8-by-19.8-meter folded timber canopy built from nearly 3,000 straight cnc-cut beams arranged into 976 triangular modules atop four branching columns.

Edyta A edited by christina vergopoulou November 21, 2026 3 min

Installed on the village square of Stein, Switzerland, the Stellar Pavilion is a 19.8-by-19.8-meter timber canopy designed by Edyta Augustynowicz in collaboration with Rotterdam-based structural engineering studio Summum Engineering and built by general contractor ERNE AG Holzbau. Positioned between the local school, town hall, and Saalbau event hall, the open-sided pavilion defines the public plaza and spans 11.3 meters between four tree-like timber columns to leave the central gathering area unobstructed. Topped by a translucent Polyned membrane that filters daylight, the folded timber lattice draws on two historical references: the neo-Gothic stellar vaults of Basel’s 1912 Heiliggeistkirche and Norwegian-American engineer Kolbjørn Saether’s 1961 research on compression-loaded structural membranes.

Augustynowicz and Summum Engineering unified both concepts in a parametric model where the roof surface rises continuously from the four supports along principal stress lines. The designer used alternating high and low radial lines to fold the canopy, stiffening the shell while channeling rainwater into the funnel-shaped columns for drainage. To rationalize fabrication, the curved lattice is composed of nearly 3,000 straight DUO timber members organized into 976 triangular modules, each combining two diagonal elements and one radial compression member. Arranged in a rotationally symmetric layout in which each module type repeats between 8 and 48 times, the straight beams were CNC-cut while the four branching columns were robotically milled. Carpenters pre-assembled the beams into triangles without custom jigs, combined them into wing-like segments in the workshop, and craned the sections into place on site, closing minor tolerances by hand using the natural flexibility of the wood.
