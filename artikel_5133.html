Bitcoin miners are shutting down machines, but mining just got even harder

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Bitcoin's mining difficulty jumped 7.61% despite the network recording its first annual hashrate contraction since China's 2021 mining ban.

Bitcoin's mining difficulty rose 7.61% on Nov. 27, intensifying financial pressure on miners already switching off machines.

Data from Mempool.space showed that the adjustment took effect at 05:17 UTC at block 977,760, raising mining difficulty from 134.82 trillion to 145.09 trillion.

Since expected mining rewards per unit of computing power move inversely to difficulty, the increase reduces what Bitcoin miners can expect to earn from their existing machines by 7.08%, assuming average transaction fees remain unchanged.

From a starting price of $60,000, Bitcoin would need to climb approximately 7.61% to $64,570 for miners to restore their previous gross dollar revenue.

Friday's adjustment reverses much of the relief miners received on Nov. 14, when mining difficulty fell 9.1%, and comes as deteriorating mining economics are already forcing operators to switch off machines.

Blockchain analytics firm Glassnode reported that Bitcoin's network hashrate had fallen 8% year over year, marking its first annual contraction since China's 2021 mining ban.

While regulatory intervention forced miners offline in 2021, Glassnode attributed the current contraction to falling Bitcoin prices and record-low hashprice, a measure of the revenue miners generate per unit of computing power.

Data from Luxor's Hashrate Index showed that hashprice fell to $29.36 per petahash per day on Nov. 23, even as Bitcoin's seven-day average hashrate recovered 7.8% to 1,033 exahashes per second.

That short-term recovery in computing power helped drive Friday's difficulty increase, further compressing revenue for operators already contending with historically weak mining economics.

With Bitcoin's block subsidy fixed at 3.125 BTC, higher transaction fees could help miners offset the decline in revenue.

However, Mempool.space data showed that transaction fees averaged just 0.0265 BTC per block across the 128 blocks mined in the 24 hours preceding Friday's adjustment, accounting for less than 1% of mining rewards.

At an unchanged Bitcoin price, average transaction fees would need to increase roughly tenfold to 0.2664 BTC per block to offset the difficulty increase.

An unusually long block interval on Nov. 26 illustrates the scale of that challenge.

Alex Thorn, Galaxy Digital's head of research, noted that Bitcoin went 98 minutes and 19 seconds without producing a block, its longest interval since July and its 69th longest since 2011.

When Foundry USA eventually mined block 977,698, it processed 3,723 transactions and collected 0.1562 BTC in transaction fees.

Despite being nearly six times the average recorded before Friday's difficulty adjustment, those fees fell approximately 41% short of what miners would now need to earn consistently per block to restore their previous revenue at an unchanged Bitcoin price.

With transaction fees contributing so little to mining revenue, operators remain heavily dependent on a Bitcoin price recovery. A prolonged period of depressed prices could accelerate machine shutdowns, extending the network's annual hashrate contraction ahead of its next difficulty adjustment.
