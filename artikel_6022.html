Accor's Asia development chief says Vietnam's fast-growing tourism island could win over price-conscious tour groups that now go to Thailand. It means Thailand needs to rethink how it sells itself. Accor has a stake in both outcomes: 85 hotels in Thailand, and a deal for more than 1,400 rooms on Phu Quoc.

Speaking in Bangkok on Tuesday, Accor's top developer for Asia had a blunt message for his Thai audience: a Vietnamese island is about to start taking your tourists.

"This is a warning to us here in Thailand. Competition is coming," Andrew Langdon, Accor's chief development officer for Asia, said at the NoVacancy Asia conference in Bangkok. "We need to have a look at how we market."

Langdon gave the example of a Russian tour group planning its next holiday. Next year, he said, that group could well pick Phu Quoc over Thailand "because it's going to be cheaper and just as easy to get to."

Accor's chief development officer for Asia, Andrew Langdon, used a Bangkok conference stage to tell Thai hoteliers that Vietnam's Phu Quoc island is coming for their visitors. Speaking at NoVacancy Asia, Langdon said a Russian tour group could pick Phu Quoc over Thailand next year "because it's going to be cheaper and just as easy to get to" — a direct hit on Thailand's fourth-largest source market, which sent 1.5 million travelers from January to early November 2026. The numbers support the framing: Phu Quoc drew 8.6 million visitors in the first nine months of 2026, up 30.5%, beating its full-year target a quarter early, with international arrivals up 60% and occupancy above 90% in January and February, against roughly 80% in Phuket. Savills forecasts about 11,000 high-end rooms arriving between 2027 and 2030, nearly doubling four- and five-star supply, and Horwath HTL expects discounting as that supply lands. Langdon singled out Sun Group, which he said has around 10,000 rooms on the island and has launched Sun PhuQuoc Airways, already flying Ahmedabad–Phu Quoc from Dec. 3. Thailand, meanwhile, is softening: international arrivals fell 4.1% to 24.4 million through mid-October and the government cut its 2026 forecast to 33 million from 36.7 million. Accor sits on both sides, with 85 hotels in Thailand and a July deal with Sun Group to add 5,300 rooms in Vietnam.
