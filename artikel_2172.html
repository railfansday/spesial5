Rangers youth prospect reveals personal ‘privilege’ of working with boyhood hero

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Rangers youngster Rydnn McGuire has opened up on the “privilege” of working alongside his former goalkeeping hero Allan McGregor at Ibrox.

McGregor returned to Rangers as a coach and is now helping develop the next generation of goalkeepers, including McGuire, who has spent time around the first-team set-up this season.

Speaking to Rangers club media ahead of Wednesday’s UEFA Youth League second leg against Dynamo Kyiv, McGuire revealed he grew up watching the former Scotland international.

He said: “Very good. It’s a privilege to come and work with him every day.

“He was probably my generation of Rangers goalie growing up, so I watched him from a young age, and he’s been really good to work with.”

McGuire has combined his involvement around the senior squad with appearances in the KDM Evolution Trophy and Youth League this season.

The young goalkeeper believes that mixture has been important for his development rather than simply training without getting competitive minutes.

He said: “I think it’s critical in what I need just now.

“To come down and play these games, and they’re high-level games, they are challenging games that I’m coming down and playing, so it’s getting experience from everything.”

McGuire was part of the Rangers youth side whose success last season earned them the chance to compete in Europe and was determined not to miss out after making the step up to the first-team environment.

He said: “It was the first thing I said to Greegsie when I went round. I don’t want all the hard work I did last year to go to waste and not getting to get to play in it, so I made sure my stance was that I wanted to play in these games.

“They’ve been really good, and they have let me play in these games, so a massive thanks to them for letting me do that.”

Attention now turns to Wednesday’s return against Dynamo Kyiv, with Rangers still in the tie due to the 3-2 scoreline, following two second-half goals in the first leg.

McGuire admits the Light Blues will have to produce an almost flawless performance if they are to progress.

He said: “The information we’re getting from Stevie and the staff, we believe we can go and pull it off. We probably need to be as close to perfect as we can on Wednesday.

“We can’t afford to slip up in any way; we need to be perfect in all senses to get the result we want.”

McGuire was impressed by the quality of the Ukrainian side in his first experience of European club football, highlighting their attacking threat in particular.

He added: “I think individual qualities in that team were outstanding. I think the striker and the winger were really good.

“I knew they’d be decent players, I knew they’d be a decent team, and they were, but a few top, top players that can hurt us on Wednesday.”
