ESPN reveals potential Michigan football coaches to replace Whittingham

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The hiring of Trev Alberts to be the new Michigan athletic director signals that there very well may be a change at the top of the football program. The timing of the hire, considering Warde Manuel steps down on New Year's Eve, and Alberts' aggressive nature indicates that change may be on the horizon.

So, will the Wolverines move on from Kyle Whittingham? Pete Thamel, Dan Wetzel, and Rece Davis debated the topic on the latest ESPN College GameDay podcast.

First, Thamel cautioned that it's not a sure thing that Whittingham is fired, noting some of the movement going on behind the scenes -- albeit before Alberts officially takes the job on December 1.

"I know Dan doesn't want to hear this, but there is still a chance Kyle Whittingham comes back," Thamel said. "Right now, behind the scenes, Michigan's aggressively searching for an offensive coordinator. They're obviously shopping for a quarterback like they're out there trying to put it together with him there."

"I think almost always it's a mistake to fire a guy, especially someone of Whittingham's credibility and resume after one year," Davis said. "This one, I could understand if they do it because it has gone so badly, so quickly.

"There's danger in that it could, I don't know if it could get a lot worse at Michigan, it's Michigan, but there's danger that it spirals on you and now, the pressure of do you get it right?"

So, if it's not Whittingham, who would the candidates be? Thamel starts with the obvious.

"I do think that Bob Chesney would be a great fit at Michigan," Thamel said. "He's a program builder. He's stability.

"He brings a level of charm, quite frankly, that's been missing there for quite a while. There's a charisma."

Chesney will actually be in Ann Arbor this weekend, as UCLA plays the Wolverines at The Big House. He's expected to sign a contract extension with the Bruins, and if all goes to plan, his team won't just be in the College Football Playoff, but there's a chance that his undefeated squad could be in it for some time, potentially precluding him from consideration, lest he leave early.

Thamel also points to the coach that Alberts currently has in College Station. Though he's struggled this year, he did make the College Football Playoff a year ago, and Alberts signed him to a contract extension based on his 2025 performance.

"A name that was not thought to be in the mix now that you sort of inherently have to put in the mix is Mike Elko, right?" Thamel said. "He has a good relationship with Trev Alberts, Midwest guy, a Penn grad, fits. He had a bad season, but pretty good track record."

Thamel continues, noting that SEC coaches could be considered, including Vanderbilt's Clark Lea, who's coming from the same school as Michigan's incoming president, Dr. C. Cybele Raver. But he also believes there is another SEC candidate who could fit the bill.

"Eli Drinkwitz, I think, fits Michigan's sensibilities," Thamel said. "I wouldn't rule him out as a candidate there who brings some familiarity with Trev from meetings and competition."

Regardless, if Michigan does move on from Whittingham, there will be no shortage of options. It will certainly be the top job that's open in football, much like it was last year, but unlike last year, it won't be a surprise that it's coming open.

"They're going to swing big, and they're going to get a good coach," Thamel said. "And look, they're paying $4 million just to buy Trev Alberts out of Texas A&M. They're going to pay him upwards of $4 million a year annually for the job."
