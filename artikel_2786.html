NFL Fantasy Football Week 12 2026 Start ‘Em Sit ‘Em: Running Back

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

NFL Fantasy Football Week 12 2026 Start 'Em Sit 'Em: Running Back appeared first on ClutchPoints. Add ClutchPoints as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

A breakout performance can transform an entire fantasy backfield overnight, but relying on that production to continue is often not straightforward. Jonah Coleman's sudden rise in Denver has provided managers with an unexpected starting option. However, the increasing competition for touches in Los Angeles and Minnesota has made it more challenging to evaluate several established running backs.

Week 12 is shaping up to be a test of opportunity rather than reputation. With fantasy playoff spots becoming harder to secure, identifying which players have genuinely earned larger roles and which ones are becoming dependent on unpredictable workloads could make all the difference when setting a lineup.

Jonah Coleman has quickly become a key player in the fantasy landscape over the past few weeks, significantly impacting Denver's backfield due to injuries that have opened up opportunities for the rookie. In a recent game against Las Vegas, Coleman delivered an impressive performance, rushing for 111 yards and scoring three touchdowns on 24 carries. He also contributed with four receptions for 18 yards. His strong showing played a crucial role in the Broncos' 38-24 victory, showcasing his ability to take on significant responsibilities.

Coleman has benefited from RJ Harvey being placed on injured reserve, while J.K. Dobbins has struggled to regain a meaningful role in the offense. Against the Raiders, Coleman out-touched Dobbins by a staggering 28-1, indicating how Denver currently views its backfield situation, so Pittsburgh will be a tough opponent, as they have faced challenges with defensive consistency throughout the season.

One major concern is Denver's short preparation time before their upcoming matchup on Friday. Given Coleman's heavy workload, the coaching staff might choose to spread out the carries more evenly, especially since he is still adjusting to the physical demands of professional football. Nonetheless, Coleman's combination of rushing opportunities, receiving involvement, and recent goal-line success makes him an attractive RB2 option with significant upside.

Chase Brown enters Week 12 after a disappointing performance against Washington, where he managed only 36 yards from scrimmage, despite Cincinnati remaining competitive throughout the game. His production has been inconsistent this season, but the Bengals continue to rely on him as a key contributor in both rushing and receiving scenarios. New Orleans presents an opportunity for improvement, having allowed 1,102 rushing yards to opposing running backs at an average of 4.4 yards per carry. The Saints have also struggled with defensive discipline during extended possessions, creating chances for opponents to establish effective rushing sequences.

Brown's role in the passing game adds to his appeal in PPR leagues, particularly when Cincinnati opts for shorter passes to counter defensive pressure. Additionally, a strong passing attack could prevent New Orleans from focusing solely on stopping the run, and while he remains susceptible to inconsistent rushing efficiency, his opportunities in this matchup appear significantly more promising than last week's disappointing results would indicate.

Bucky Irving has a highly favorable matchup for fantasy running backs this week, as he faces a Carolina defense that allows approximately 28.2 PPR points per game to the position. The Buccaneers running back has established himself as a reliable offensive contributor, combining efficient rushing with receiving production that enhances his fantasy value. In a recent game against Detroit, Irving carried the ball 18 times for 68 yards and a touchdown, while also adding three receptions for 32 yards, despite Tampa Bay's 31-21 loss. His ability to contribute in various situations should be valuable against Carolina, particularly because the Panthers have struggled to limit productive performances from running backs.

The Buccaneers will host the Panthers on Monday night, providing fantasy managers with a chance to take advantage of this favorable matchup in the final game of the week. His receiving skills offer an additional benefit if Tampa Bay finds itself trailing and needs to increase its passing volume. Overall, Irving should be considered a strong RB1 option, especially for managers looking for a dependable combination of rushing workload and receiving upside.

Other Starts: Omarion Hampton, Los Angeles Chargers (vs. NE); Breece Hall, New York Jets (@ MIA).

Hampton has rushed for 194 yards over his last two games, and his increasing involvement makes him an intriguing RB2 against New England. Hall faces a Miami defense allowing 25 PPR points per game to opposing running backs, offering a favorable opportunity for production despite New York's inconsistent offense.

Kyren Williams remains an important offensive contributor for the Rams, but his recent workload creates concerns ahead of Wednesday's matchup against Green Bay, as he has accumulated only 68 rushing yards on 27 carries over his last two appearances, averaging 2.5 yards per attempt while failing to score a touchdown. Blake Corum has become increasingly involved, creating uncertainty about how Los Angeles will distribute opportunities during important possessions, and Williams has also experienced discomfort in his toe, although he participated fully in Monday's practice and appears likely to play.

Green Bay's defense presents challenges, particularly if the Rams struggle to establish favorable rushing situations early. So, he should remain playable in deeper leagues, but managers with dependable alternatives should consider avoiding him until his workload becomes more predictable.

Kyle Monangai returned from injury against New Orleans but struggled to generate production, rushing eight times for 18 yards without contributing a reception. His limited involvement reflects the difficulties of maintaining consistent fantasy value while sharing responsibilities with D'Andre Swift. So, Detroit provides another challenging opponent, having allowed relatively modest production to opposing running backs throughout the season, and their offense can also force opponents into passing situations, potentially reducing opportunities for a secondary rushing contributor.

Monangai remains capable of providing useful production when Chicago establishes an effective rushing attack, but his uncertain workload makes him difficult to trust on Thanksgiving.

Rico Dowdle presents a challenging fantasy football decision because his opportunities rely heavily on Pittsburgh's backfield rotation. The veteran running back has shown his ability to contribute when given a significant amount of rushing attempts, but Jaylen Warren continues to handle the primary responsibilities. Facing Denver poses a difficult situation for a secondary running back like Dowdle, especially against a defense adept at disrupting possessions through effective pressure and disciplined tackling. Additionally, Dowdle's inconsistent involvement in the passing game limits his ability to offset modest rushing production in PPR (points per reception) formats.

While he remains an emergency FLEX option, the combination of uncertain touches and a tough matchup makes him a risky starter. Other players to consider sitting include David Montgomery of the Houston Texans (going up against Baltimore) and Jordan Mason of the Minnesota Vikings (facing Atlanta).

Montgomery rushed for 107 yards against Indianapolis, but the potential return of Woody Marks from his concussion could reduce Montgomery's opportunities.
