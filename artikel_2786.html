Stats Rundown: 4 numbers to know from the Mavericks 116-94 win over the Warriors

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Dallas Mavericks (8-7), fresh off a three-game losing streak, needed some home cooking on Wednesday night and got it in a big way. Dallas got a convincing 116-94 win over the Golden State Warriors on a night where the Mavericks celebrated the life of former head coach Don Nelson. This was a big win for a team that looked to be finding its way during a three-game winning streak, only to immediately appear listless in a follow-up three-game losing streak. The Mavericks looked locked in, hit the gas and never really let up in an effort that gets them over .500.

Cooper Flagg cracked the 30-point mark on a free throw early in the fourth quarter. That gave Flagg his 15th career 30-point outing, good for second place in league history for 30-point games recorded by a teenager. Flagg now trails LeBron James by five such games and has 33 days remaining as a teenager to match or exceed James' total.

If there is an object lesson as to how the 3-point shot influences the modern NBA game, this was it. The Mavericks drained 17 shots from deep to the Warriors' nine. The point differential almost exactly matched the point differential on the scoreboard. Beyond the comparison of made shots are the small adjacent details. The Warriors missed 29 longballs, connecting on just shy of 24% overall. It's difficult to overcome such a deficit on the makes, but giving up that many possessions to misses leads to points the other way.

Speaking of 3-point shots, Washington was in peak form, draining 5-of-7 on the night. His 71.4% shooting from deep is unrealistic in the long term, but if he could keep his off nights around 33% and pepper in some of these scorching hot nights, the Mavs will have a whole new dimension to their offense. If he can cement his consistency in this department, it's going to give the opponent fits.

The game was a clean sort of sloppy. Yes, that's an oxymoron of epic proportions, but speaks to the overall flow of the game. The turnovers were of the type where players just constantly jumped the passing lane and took off the other way. It lent itself to an overall flow of the game back-and-forth, but it was a lot! The Mavs had 19 turnovers and the Warriors nearly matched them with 17 of their own. 19 is simply far too high, and the Mavs are fortunate the Warriors couldn't capitalize on the miscues. In the end, the Warriors had 21 points off turnovers where Dallas had 18.
