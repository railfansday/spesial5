NFL Week 11 Winners and Losers: Steelers have another horrible loss, and it seems obvious how their season will end

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Pittsburgh Steelers are likely going to the playoffs. But they also appear very likely to be headed toward being one-and-done, if they do make the postseason.

The Steelers have been one-and-done in the playoffs six straight times and the way they're playing lately doesn't give much confidence that they can get out of the wild-card round this time either.

Losing to the Cincinnati Bengals is excusable, though it was troubling how badly they got blown out. But nobody should be losing to this season's Philadelphia Eagles. The Eagles, who had lost seven in a row, beat the Steelers 19-16. Philadelphia was led by Joe Milton III, their third quarterback of the season. Milton didn't play well, throwing for just 141 yards, but the Steelers still couldn't outscore the Eagles.

Not only was there the loss, but the entire DK Metcalf experience somehow continued to get worse. Metcalf was getting into it with Eagles cornerback Cooper DeJean throughout the game and then poked DeJean in the eye after a play in the third quarter. That dirty act got Metcalf ejected and the NFL will certainly look into a suspension. It's not like it matters given how little production Metcalf has given Pittsburgh this season and pretty much through his entire Steelers tenure, but it still had to be infuriating for Pittsburgh to be stuck with less than 10 points and then watch its supposed No. 1 receiver walking back to the locker room after being kicked out. It is turning out to be a disastrous trade for Pittsburgh.

"This is definitely a game we expected to win … we need him in those games," Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy told the media afterward. "That's not acceptable to get thrown out of a game."

It was a miserable day overall for the Steelers. They couldn't get the run game going, so they abandoned it. Their leading rusher was Aaron Rodgers, with 29 yards. Their top two backs, Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle, combined for 43 yards on 14 carries. Rodgers continues to fade as he creeps up on his 43rd birthday. He has been good for his age but expecting him to continue to play at an above-average pace all season was always wishful thinking.

Rodgers had 227 yards and a touchdown, but the touchdown came late with the Steelers trailing by 10 points. That was the only time Pittsburgh got in the end zone. The rest of the season might not be pretty, and it will come with more of Rodgers ranting against everyone around him.

The defense wasn't bad, but when it needed a stop on third-and-6 in the final minutes, Milton found DeVonta Smith for the game-clinching first down. Pittsburgh also gave up a 38-yard touchdown run to Saquon Barkley that was most of Philadelphia's offense on Sunday.

Steelers fans have to be wondering how they can get off the hamster wheel. Every season seems about the same. The Steelers pull off some unlikely wins, and their record is just good enough to get into the playoffs. Then they get to the postseason and get blown out. Their last five playoff losses have come by double digits.

The Steelers had a coaching change, going from Mike Tomlin to McCarthy, and nothing has really changed. They're probably good enough to make the playoffs in a weak AFC wild-card race. And there aren't any signs the last couple weeks that they're able to go on the road and upset a team like Jacksonville or Denver in the wild-card round or even keep the game close. And perhaps the Steelers, at 6-4, could fade in the wild-card race, considering their next three games are against Denver, Houston and Jacksonville.

It was another rough Sunday for the Steelers. Being a likely playoff team should be something to get excited about, but it's hard when the endgame now seems obvious.

Here are the rest of the winners and losers from Week 11 of the NFL season:

Josh Johnson: It didn't matter if the Arizona Cardinals beat the Kansas City Chiefs on Sunday. Johnson's story was a great one either way.

Johnson got the start for the Cardinals when Carson Beck's back injury kept him out of practice during the week. The 40-year-old made some history on his first snap, as the second-oldest non-specialist to play for the Cardinals, behind only Jim Thorpe, who played one game at age 41 for the Chicago Cardinals in 1928.

And Johnson, who has played for 15 teams, threw a touchdown in the first half, the 15th of his long and strange career. Johnson was reasonable, with 155 yards and that touchdown.

Predictably, the Cardinals couldn't keep up with the Chiefs. Kansas City got another big game from rookie Emmett Johnson, who had 105 yards rushing and 38 more receiving as Kenneth Walker III got another week off to nurse a groin injury. It was no surprise the Chiefs won 34-7. But even in the loss, Johnson playing and doing OK at 40 years old was fun to see.

Bills' defense: At some point Buffalo will face a tough offense. It has had a few weeks of playing against bad offenses with backup quarterbacks. But let's give the Bills credit for handling business.

Buffalo's defense did its job Sunday, holding down the Miami Dolphins led by quarterback Kyle McCord. The Bills won 26-9, allowing just 185 yards and no touchdowns.

The Bills' defense was not very good to start the season, bringing into question whether Buffalo was good enough on that side of the ball to win a Super Bowl. The Bills won't get to see quarterbacks like Joe Fagnano, Carson Wentz and McCord in the playoffs, so we don't know for sure if their defense has turned a corner. At least it's better than struggling against those quarterbacks.

Dak Prescott and CeeDee Lamb: Most of the focus for the Dallas Cowboys this season has been on their awful defense. That defense is a shame because it is wasting another good season of Prescott's prime.

Prescott was very good in a 51-30 win over the Tennessee Titans on Sunday, as he has been most of the season. Prescott threw for 325 yards and four touchdowns including one to Lamb, who had another big game. Lamb had 11 catches for 132 yards.

The Cowboys' defense might have been slightly better Sunday, with Mike Pettine taking over play-calling from Christian Parker. But it was the type of game the Cowboys are going to have to keep winning if they want to stay in the NFC East race, with the offense scoring enough to not make the defense's struggles matter. Prescott is capable of making it happen.

Tampa Bay Buccaneers: It's not like the Buccaneers were expected to win Sunday in Detroit. But maybe that's the problem.

The Buccaneers, like everyone else in the NFC South, seem incapable of beating a good opponent. Tampa Bay hung around for a while against Detroit, but the Lions eventually wore the Bucs out in a 31-21 win. Jahmyr Gibbs continued his fantastic season for the Lions with 118 total yards and two touchdowns.

Tampa Bay could still win the division because nobody else is running away with it. But at 3-7, it's not like the Buccaneers deserve a playoff spot.

Jesse Minter: Not all of the Baltimore Ravens' struggles this season are Minter's fault. But he's not having a great first season as head coach.

Minter's defense failed again Sunday. The Panthers put up 378 yards and won 35-33. Carolina drove at the end for a go-ahead field goal and Jacoby Brissett was picked off on a deflected pass. Brissett, however, wasn't the biggest problem. He had 276 yards and a touchdown. It's that nothing else was great for the Ravens, especially the defense. It was another blown lead for the Ravens, which has been a theme of their season.

"At the end of the day we had a lead, and we didn't hold it," Minter said. "So I'll take responsibility for that."

The Ravens are a perennial contender but that status is fading. Baltimore (4-7) lost its sixth in a row.

Minter might be a good coach over time, but there has to be skepticism after Baltimore's fade this season.

Kellen Moore: Could Moore be in trouble as New Orleans Saints head coach?

The Saints lost 21-13 to the Chicago Bears, and while that is a tough opponent, it's not like New Orleans has done much to impress this season. The Saints (3-7) haven't looked good all season. Tyler Shough hasn't progressed in his second season, though he's not getting a ton of help. The Saints' hot streak late last season, after a 1-8 start, seems like a mirage due to catching a very easy part of the schedule.

Team owner Gayle Benson had some startling comments earlier this season that seemed to be critical of Moore and the front office as well. She later said she endorsed Moore. But the losses are piling up.
