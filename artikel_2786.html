Jaguars vs Titans key matchups: Trevor to bounce back against Tennessee?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Jacksonville Jaguars have an opportunity to strengthen their grip on the AFC South when they welcome the Tennessee Titans to EverBank Stadium on Sunday. The two teams met just two weeks ago, with Jacksonville running out 40-23 winners in Nashville. However, Liam Coen's team will be looking to bounce back after a disappointing defeat to the New York Giants, while Tennessee arrives in Florida following a 51-30 humbling at the hands of the Dallas Cowboys.

There are several intriguing individual battles to watch, but these three could ultimately determine which team comes out on top.

Tony Pollard has been one of the few consistent bright spots in an otherwise difficult season for the Tennessee Titans. The veteran running back has rushed for 738 yards and six touchdowns this season, averaging 4.5 yards per carry, and has surpassed 100 rushing yards in three of his last four games. However, one of those four outings came against the Jacksonville Jaguars, who limited Pollard to just 33 rushing yards on nine attempts in their Week 10 meeting. Jacksonville's defensive front has been particularly effective against the run this season, allowing just 89.6 rushing yards per game, the fifth-best mark in the NFL. That ability to shut down opposing running games has been a significant factor in the Jaguars' defensive success, and they will be looking to replicate that performance on Sunday.

There is an important distinction to make, though. Although Jacksonville largely neutralized Pollard as a traditional runner in the first meeting, he still managed to contribute eight receptions for 66 yards and a touchdown. Tennessee will need to find ways to get the ball into his hands, particularly if its offensive line struggles to create running lanes between the tackles. If Pollard can become a genuine threat both on the ground and as a receiver, it will force Jacksonville to account for him in multiple areas and potentially create opportunities elsewhere. If the Jaguars can shut down his rushing production once again, they will put considerable pressure on Cam Ward to win the game through the air. Speaking of…

Cam Ward has shown signs of improvement during the second half of the season, and there is considerably more to like about his recent performances than his overall numbers might suggest. The rookie quarterback has thrown for 783 yards and six touchdowns across his last three games, with just one interception during that stretch. However, that interception came against Jacksonville, and the Jaguars will be confident that they can make life difficult for the young signal-caller once again.

Jacksonville has recorded 12 interceptions this season, the third-highest total in the NFL, while also leading the league in turnover differential at plus-eight. This is a defense that knows how to punish opposing quarterbacks for mistakes, and Ward will need to be particularly disciplined with his decision-making.

The challenge for Tennessee is finding the balance between protecting Ward and allowing him to be aggressive enough to challenge Jacksonville's defense. Simply asking him to play conservatively is unlikely to produce enough points against a Jaguars team averaging 28.7 points per game. Ward needs to take opportunities when they present themselves, particularly against a secondary that has shown it can be beaten.

However, forcing throws into coverage or attempting to manufacture big plays could quickly turn the game in Jacksonville's favor. With the Jaguars already demonstrating their ability to disrupt Ward in the previous meeting, Tennessee's quarterback will need to show that he has learned from that experience. If he can limit turnovers while still producing explosive plays, the Titans have a chance to make this considerably more competitive.

If there is one area of this matchup that should have Jacksonville licking its lips, it is the opportunity to attack Tennessee's pass defense. The Titans have been among the NFL's worst defensive units this season, allowing 285.9 passing yards per game and 29 points per contest. Their struggles were particularly evident in the first meeting between these teams, when Trevor Lawrence threw for 352 yards and three touchdowns in a convincing Jacksonville victory.

Lawrence has been one of the more productive quarterbacks in the league this season, recording 2,585 passing yards and 22 touchdowns through ten games. Coming off a frustrating performance against the Giants, where he threw two interceptions despite passing for 301 yards, he will be eager to get back on track against a defense that has struggled to contain opposing passing attacks.

The Titans cannot afford to simply sit back and allow Lawrence to pick them apart, but generating consistent pressure has been a challenge in its own right. Tennessee has recorded 21 sacks this season, and it will need its defensive front to disrupt Lawrence's rhythm without constantly exposing its secondary through additional blitzes.

Jacksonville's offensive line will also have something to prove after allowing considerable pressure against the Giants, so this is not necessarily a one-sided battle. If Tennessee can force Lawrence into uncomfortable situations and prevent him from finding his rhythm, it has a chance to keep the Jaguars' offense in check. However, if Lawrence receives time in the pocket, Tennessee could be in for another long afternoon. The Titans have already seen what happens when they allow him to dictate the game, and they desperately need a different result this time around.

Jacksonville has the advantage in all three matchups on paper, but the biggest question is whether Tennessee can find a way to make this a competitive contest. If Pollard can establish the run, Ward can protect the football and the Titans' defensive front can disrupt Lawrence, there is a path to an upset. Otherwise, the Jaguars have the firepower to make a statement in the AFC South and take another step towards the playoffs.
