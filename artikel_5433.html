Seattle fleet tracking company Zonar names Atlanta-based CEO, who says HQ is staying put

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

by Kurt Schlosser on Nov 17, 2026 at 7:30 amNovember 17, 2026 at 7:30 am

Zonar, the Seattle-based fleet tracking company, has a new CEO: Rob Consoli, a longtime tech sales executive who joined the company as chief revenue officer in January.

Consoli is based in Atlanta, but he told GeekWire that Seattle remains Zonar’s headquarters and he has “no plans to change that.” He said he visits the company’s office in downtown Seattle’s Exchange Building about twice a month.

Consoli succeeds Charles Kriete, who led GPS Trackit when the Florida-based company acquired Zonar from German auto supplier Continental in late 2024. Kriete ran the combined company, which is backed by private equity firm Inverness Graham, until September and is now a board advisor.

Consoli previously held revenue chief roles at legal software company Assembly Software, Skuid and Liaison Technologies, which OpenText acquired for $310 million in 2018. He also led North American sales at asset-tracking company I.D. Systems, and started his career as an aerospace engineer at General Dynamics.

Zonar sells GPS tracking hardware, cameras and software that fleet operators use to monitor vehicle location, fuel use and driver behavior, and says it has more than 400,000 active units installed.

Its biggest market is student transportation. Consoli said Zonar serves about half of the school bus market, and its customers also include trucking and delivery fleets such as US Foods, utilities and small businesses.

Zonar’s Seattle-area customers include the Kirkland Police Department, which in 2024 became the first agency to roll out Zonar’s mobile inspection app and GPS asset trackers in its patrol fleet after a pilot program. Officers use the app to complete a digital inspection of their patrol car before each shift, checking tire pressure, emergency equipment such as defibrillators and the rear enclosure used to transport suspects. Each inspection takes two to four minutes, according to the company.

Zonar has 409 employees after combining with GPS Trackit, including 70 in Seattle, more than in any other state. Consoli declined to share revenue figures but said Zonar has “more than doubled our profitability in the last 10 months,” helped by new pricing, a reorganized sales team, renegotiated hardware contracts and cost cuts.

Zonar has added 1,000 new customers so far this year, a spokesperson said. Sales to existing customers are running at 118% of the company’s 2026 plan, while new customer wins are at 97%.

“We’re a very attractive asset in this market right now,” Consoli said.

Zonar competes with larger players including Samsara, Motive and Geotab. Consoli said Zonar sets itself apart through customer service and flexibility. It offers Zonar One, a turnkey system that customers can install the day after ordering, and Zonar Flex, which is customized for large enterprise fleets.

He said those customers are “not going to get that level of tailoring and customization from any of the big players.”

Consoli also wants to lean further into AI. The goal is to send customers proactive alerts instead of making fleet managers dig through dashboards after the fact: a driver running stop signs, a truck off its route, or a school bus running late. Zonar already lets school districts notify parents through an app when a bus is delayed.

“Fleet operators today are really focused on outcomes,” Consoli said. “They want to know that compliance is handled, safety is improved.”

Founded in Seattle in 2001, Zonar was majority-owned by Continental from 2016 until the 2024 sale. The company cut 57 jobs in August 2024, when it had more than 300 employees, and made more layoffs after the acquisition.
