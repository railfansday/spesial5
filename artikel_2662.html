Duke Tobin sends out 'not comfortable' head coach Zac Taylor to explain Bengals' trade-deadline snafu with Eagles

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Zac Taylor almost certainly had little to nothing to do with the trade-deadline snafu on Tuesday in which the Cincinnati Bengals pulled out of an agreed-upon deal with the Philadelphia Eagles.

On Wednesday, the Bengals head coach took the podium to discuss the topic despite declaring that he's "not comfortable" doing so. He said he did so at the behest of director of player personnel and de facto general manager Duke Tobin.

Per Taylor, Tobin is "really apologetic." To be clear, that came from Taylor's mouth, not Tobin's.

"Obviously, we tried something yesterday," Taylor said. "Duke was very explicit with me when he came and spoke to me of how he wanted this portrayed. I'm not comfortable doing it, but I'm going to do what he asked.

"We did make an effort to kind of put some depth in there in place of Cashius [Howell]. As we got into it, obviously it was going to be a little more costly than we initially anticipated. He's really apologetic about it and wants to make clear: Philly was a great trade partner. It was on our end."

In case you missed it, the Bengals had an agreement in place with the Eagles prior to Tuesday's trade deadline to acquire edge rusher Arnold Ebiketie. The Bengals need depth at the position due to a wrist injury that placed Howell on injured reserve.

Per the agreed-upon deal, the Bengals would have sent a sixth-round pick to the Eagles and received Ebiketie in return. But it turns out the deal would have come at a significantly higher cost that the Bengals front office apparently didn't realize when it made the agreement.

Per the NFL's compensatory picks mechanisms, the Bengals would also have had to forfeit a third-round compensatory pick to acquire Ebiketie. So in essence, they would have parted with a third- and a sixth-round pick to acquire a backup pass rusher.

It's a price that's prohibitive and one that the Bengals reportedly didn't realize until Over the Cap's self-proclaimed "Compensatory Picks Guy™" Nick Korte pointed it out on social media.

Korte's accurate analysis picked up traction among traditional NFL media, and only then did the Bengals back out of the deal, having realized its true cost.

This was all reported Tuesday, and Taylor was left to confirm it with media on Wednesday.

Duke Tobin, not Zac Taylor, is tasked with overseeing Bengals transactions.

Again, this is not Taylor's job. He's a head football coach. He's not tasked with knowing the complex mechanics of NFL trades or the league's compensatory pick system, and he's not the one who pulled the trigger on the agreement with the Eagles.

Those responsibilities lie with the player personnel arm of Cincinnati's front office, which is run by Tobin. The mistake was an embarrassment for Tobin's department that played out across sports media Tuesday. On Wednesday, Tobin sent out Taylor to face the music.
