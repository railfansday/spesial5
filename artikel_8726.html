Taking a Second Job? Fix Your Withholding and Protect Your Main Job

Before you take a second job, head off the three ways it can backfire. Have your main employer withhold extra tax to cover the second paycheck, check that employer’s rules on outside work, and set hours and an end date that protect your sleep and your main job.

None of the three shows up in your first week. The tax bill comes due when you file your return, and a broken work rule or exhaustion can take months to cost you.

Each employer withholds tax as if its paycheck were your only income, unless your W-4, the form that sets your withholding, says otherwise. On your return, the two incomes are taxed as one, so you usually owe more on the second job’s pay than its employer took out. Owing $1,000 or more when you file can also bring an IRS underpayment penalty.

The other two put your main job, the bigger of your two paychecks, at risk.

To fix the tax, start the new job with a W-4 that has only your personal details and signature. After its first payday, run the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator, which needs a recent pay stub from each job. The extra withholding it finds goes on the W-4 for your higher-paying job, so give your main employer an updated one.

Protecting your main job starts before you apply. Read your employer’s handbook and any contract you signed. Some employers require approval for outside work, a conflict-of-interest rule can bar a job with a client or vendor, and a noncompete agreement can bar one with a competitor. When you interview, offer the same hours every week, such as weekends from 8 a.m. to 2 p.m., so the new shifts never overlap your main job.

Cap the hours so you still sleep at least seven hours a night, the minimum the CDC recommends for adults. Give the job a goal, too. Say a teacher works 12 weekend hours at a bike shop for $20 an hour and loses 30% to taxes. That leaves about $730 a month, enough to clear a $6,000 credit card balance at 22% interest in about nine months. Then the teacher quits.

The extra pay is worth it only while your main job stays safe, so put your end date on your calendar before the first shift.
