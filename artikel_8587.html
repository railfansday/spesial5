Whichever provider you pick, the tax rules are the same. If you itemize rather than take the standard deduction, you count a gift toward your deductions in the year you put it in, then recommend grants to charities over time. Giving stock that has risen in value, held more than a year, usually beats selling it and donating the cash, since the capital gains tax on the rise never comes due. The grants can’t get you anything in return, either. An IRS notice treats a grant that pays for your tickets to a charity’s event as a benefit to you, which brings a penalty tax on whoever recommended it.
