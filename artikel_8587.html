One of the highest-value redemptions in travel rewards

Picture this: you're settling into a lie-flat business class seat somewhere over the Atlantic, a glass of something cold in hand, knowing that in 48 hours you'll be waking up in Istanbul — and then, a few days later, continuing onward to Bangkok. Two bucket-list cities. One award booking. One points balance. This is what the Aeroplan stopover sweet spot feels like in practice. It's not a trick or a loophole — it's a deliberately designed feature of Air Canada's Aeroplan program that the vast majority of travelers simply don't know exists. And that gap between awareness and reality is exactly where the ChooseFI community finds its edge. Here's the math that should make your jaw drop. A business class itinerary from North America to Istanbul with a continuing flight onward to Bangkok — two long-haul segments, lie-flat seats, Star Alliance lounges at every connection — would cost somewhere between **$6,000 and $15,000 in cash**. That's a realistic retail range depending on season and how far out you book. Most people never consider it a possibility for a family vacation or a milestone trip because the sticker price puts it in a different universe. With Aeroplan, you're booking that same itinerary — the same seats, the same lounges, the same Turkish Airlines or Thai Airways product — for **75,000 Aeroplan points**. At the low end of the cash price range, that's already **8 cents per point**. At the high end, you're extracting **20 cents per point** from a currency that most people earned by putting groceries on a credit card. The pricing inefficiency here is structural. Aeroplan's partner award chart prices long-haul business class at a fixed rate regardless of cash fare fluctuations. When business class prices spike — around holidays, during peak travel seasons, during major events — your points cost stays exactly the same. You're insulated from the market. That's the FI mindset applied to travel: you've already paid for the trip in points, slowly accumulated through everyday spending, and now you're simply redeeming the stored value. The stopover itself is nearly free. Aeroplan charges just 5,000 extra miles to add a stopover city to a one-way award. That means your Istanbul layover becomes a multi-day stay — at zero additional points cost beyond that small add-on — and you haven't booked a second award at all. One booking. Two destinations. That's the entire premise, and once you see it, you can't unsee it.

The mechanism behind this sweet spot is elegantly simple once you understand it, but it requires knowing three things that most travelers don't: how Aeroplan's routing rules work, what a stopover actually is in the context of an award ticket, and why Star Alliance's global network makes this scalable to almost any destination pair you can imagine.

When Aeroplan relaunched its loyalty program in 2020, it did something remarkable: it eliminated fuel surcharges entirely and published a partner award chart with fixed pricing for flights on Star Alliance carriers. This is critical. When you book Turkish Airlines business class through Aeroplan, you pay the Aeroplan fixed chart rate — not what Turkish Airlines would charge in its own currency or miles program, and not the inflated cash price. That fixed chart is the engine of this redemption.

For long-haul international business class, Aeroplan's chart prices routing through multiple regions based on your origin and destination — not every intermediate stop. This is the aha moment. Aeroplan determines your award price based on where your journey starts and where it ends, and then allows you to add a stopover in between for just 5,000 additional miles. The stopover city is priced as a pass-through for the purpose of calculating your award zone, not as a second trip.

On a one-way Aeroplan award, you're permitted to add a free stopover — meaning you can stay in an intermediate city for any length of time before continuing to your final destination. That 5,000-mile add-on is essentially the cost of a full additional destination. Compare that to booking a second one-way award from Istanbul to Bangkok, which would cost you another 40,000–55,000 Aeroplan points in business class. The math is decisive.

The routing must be geographically sensible — you can't fly backwards across the globe — but Aeroplan's allowance for multi-segment itineraries through Star Alliance hubs gives you enormous flexibility. Istanbul (IST) is a Turkish Airlines hub. Bangkok (BKK) is served by Thai Airways and several Star Alliance carriers. Both are Star Alliance partners. Both are bookable through Aeroplan at fixed chart rates with no fuel surcharges.

Booking an Aeroplan stopover award in business class is genuinely one of the more complex redemptions in the travel rewards world, but it becomes manageable when you break it into distinct phases. Here's exactly how to do it.

Start by deciding on your two destinations. Aeroplan stopovers work best when your intermediate city is a major Star Alliance hub that lies geographically between your origin and final destination. Classic combinations include North America to Istanbul (Turkish Airlines hub) continuing to Southeast Asia, or North America to a European hub continuing to the Middle East or East Africa. Your stopover city should be somewhere you genuinely want to spend 2–5 days — this is a real trip, not just a layover.

This is the most important and most time-consuming step. You need to find saver-level business class availability on both segments independently before you book.

Once you've confirmed availability on both segments, go to aeroplan.com and use the multi-city search function. Enter your full itinerary — origin, stopover city with your intended layover dates, and final destination.

Once you've confirmed the award is bookable and are ready to complete the reservation:

Complete your booking on aeroplan.com or with a phone agent, confirm the stopover is properly reflected in your itinerary, and save every confirmation email. Verify your seats on each carrier's website immediately after booking.

A stopover is an intentional break in your journey lasting more than 24 hours at a connecting city. Most airlines treat stopovers as separate tickets, charging full price for each segment. Aeroplan is one of the few programs that allows a free stopover on round-trip awards, letting you explore a connecting city for days before continuing to your final destination. This is fundamentally different from a layover, which is a brief connection of a few hours at an airport. The stopover must be in a city served by a Star Alliance carrier on your routing, and the key is that Aeroplan prices the entire itinerary as one award, not two.

Let's build a real example so you can see exactly what this looks like on paper — and in the air.

The Route: Toronto (YYZ) → Istanbul (IST, 4-day stopover) → Bangkok (BKK) The Carrier: Turkish Airlines business class on both segments The Points Cost: 75,000 Aeroplan miles for the two segments + 5,000 miles for the stopover = 80,000 Aeroplan miles total Cash Equivalent: A comparable business class itinerary on this routing typically retails for $9,000–$12,000 in 2026, depending on when you search and how far in advance you book.

The CPP Calculation: At the midpoint cash value of $10,500 against 80,000 points, you're extracting 13.1 cents per Aeroplan mile — well inside the 8–20 cent range this sweet spot delivers, and roughly 10–13 times the value of redeeming Aeroplan miles for cash back.

The Turkish Airlines business class product on long-haul routes is consistently rated among the top business class experiences in the Star Alliance network. On the YYZ–IST segment — operated on a 787 or A330 — you'll have access to a lie-flat seat with direct aisle access, the Turkish Airlines Lounge in Istanbul (widely considered one of the best airport lounges in the world, full stop), and a meal service that reflects the airline's culinary reputation. The IST lounge alone — with its restaurant, spa, golf simulator, and sleeping areas — is worth lingering in even before your onward connection.

In Istanbul, your four days might include the Hagia Sophia at sunrise, a Bosphorus dinner cruise, and the Grand Bazaar before you head to the airport to continue your journey. You've paid nothing additional for this stay beyond the 5,000-mile stopover fee.

The IST–BKK segment puts you into Bangkok in the evening, ready for Thailand's extraordinary food, culture, and cost of living — which, for the FI-minded traveler, means your lodging and meals on the ground are remarkably affordable, stretching the value of your trip even further.

Total points cost for two lie-flat business class segments and a full European city stay: 80,000 miles, earned on everyday credit card spending. That's the entire proposition, and those numbers are real.

Book at exactly 355 days out. Aeroplan opens its booking window at 355 days before departure. The best business class availability on popular routes — especially Turkish Airlines long-haul — appears at this window and gets claimed quickly. Set a calendar reminder and be ready to search at the 355-day mark for your target travel dates.

Use Chase Ultimate Rewards as your primary earning currency for this redemption. The instant transfer speed to Aeroplan is genuinely differentiating. When you find award space and need to move quickly, Chase lets you transfer and book in the same session. Amex, Capital One, and Bilt all require 1–2 days, during which your availability can evaporate.

Search availability before you transfer points. Confirm space exists on every segment before a single point moves. There's no undo button on a transfer, and Aeroplan points sitting idle in your account are fine — points transferred to an account where you can't find availability are a headache.

Call Aeroplan for complex routings. The online multi-city tool is functional but can struggle with nuanced stopover configurations. Phone agents are experienced with these bookings and have the same access to inventory. The call is worth it for an $80,000-point booking.

Leverage the open-jaw rule too. Aeroplan also allows open-jaw routing — fly into one city, depart from another. Combined with the stopover, you can design a route that visits three geographic points on a single award: fly into Istanbul, take trains or ferries to Athens, and fly home from Athens. The award prices the same way.

Don't assume partner availability matches Aeroplan's search. Turkish Airlines and other Star Alliance partners don't always make every seat available through Aeroplan's online portal. If you see space on the carrier's own site but not on aeroplan.com, call — agents can often access inventory the website misses.

Don't book inflexible connecting flights. Long-haul itineraries with tight connections are a risk. Build in at least a 3-hour connection at hub airports to protect against irregular operations that could affect your award segments.

Don't earn Marriott Bonvoy points with this redemption in mind. The 3:1 transfer ratio to Aeroplan makes Marriott a poor choice for funding this specific award. Stick to Amex, Chase, Capital One, or Bilt at 1:1.

Don't book this for a time-sensitive trip. Award availability, transfer timelines, and multi-carrier coordination mean this requires planning runway. Build in at least 3–4 weeks of lead time beyond the actual booking date.

This redemption is purpose-built for the advanced travel rewards enthusiast in the ChooseFI community — someone who has already mastered the basics (earning transferable points, understanding award charts, booking straightforward partner awards) and is ready to unlock the next level of value.

The ideal profile: A couple or solo traveler planning a milestone trip — a significant anniversary, a sabbatical, a once-in-a-decade adventure — who wants to experience two destinations in business class without paying business class prices for either one. You're flexible on travel dates, comfortable spending time on the phone with an airline agent, and genuinely excited about the trip-planning process as part of the experience.

Minimum points balance: You'll need approximately 80,000 Aeroplan miles to complete this booking (75,000 for the award plus 5,000 for the stopover). That's achievable from a single premium credit card welcome bonus, a combination of two or three mid-tier welcome bonuses, or 12–18 months of everyday spending on a 2x transferable points card.

For the ChooseFI traveler who's been building transferable points for a year or two and wants to see what this system is truly capable of, this redemption is the answer.

This redemption is rated **Hard** — and that rating is honest. It earns that designation from three compounding challenges, not just one. **Challenge 1: Finding availability on multiple segments simultaneously.** Business class award space on popular long-haul routes is limited. Finding saver-level availability on one segment is manageable. Finding it on two connected segments, with dates that create a coherent stopover itinerary, requires persistence and flexibility. This is the primary barrier. **Challenge 2: Understanding Aeroplan's routing logic.** Aeroplan's stopover rules, multi-city booking interface, and the distinction between how partner awards are priced require more than surface-level familiarity with the program. A misunderstanding of how the stopover is added can result in a significantly higher points cost or a booking that doesn't reflect your intended itinerary. **Challenge 3: Coordinating the transfer timing.** With a 355-day booking window and fast-moving business class availability, the timing of your point transfers requires active management — particularly if you're not using Chase's instant transfer speed. ## Three Tips to Overcome These Challenges 1. **Start your availability research 60–90 days before the 355-day window opens.** Know your target route, your preferred stopover city, and your date range before you ever log into aeroplan.com. Pick a final destination and a stopover city on the same Star Alliance routing. Classic combos: Istanbul + Bangkok, Zurich + Tokyo, Vienna + Singapore. The stopover city must be a natural connection point on a Star Alliance route between your origin and destination.

Before transferring any points, verify that saver award space exists on both the outbound leg (origin to stopover city) and the continuation (stopover city to final destination). If either segment is unavailable, the booking will not work.

The Aeroplan stopover is one of the most powerful tools in the travel rewards playbook. By adding a free stopover to a round-trip business class award, you can explore two world-class cities for the points cost of visiting one. It requires planning, patience at the 355-day booking window, and comfort with Star Alliance routing rules, but the payoff is extraordinary: 10-15 cents per point in value on experiences that would cost thousands in cash. For the FI community member who has mastered basic award bookings and is ready to level up, this is the redemption that changes how you think about travel.
