Lotus’s New V-8 Ferrari Fighter Is Coming in 2028; Here’s the Engine That Will Power It

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Horse Powertrains has pulled the wraps off its first eight-cylinder powerplant, destined for the Lotus Type 135.

Back in May, Lotus revealed its Focus 2030 strategy for the future, in which the storied British racing and sports car brand aims to return to its roots by “protecting what makes Lotus, Lotus.” What makes Lotus Lotus is, of course, at least partially combustion-powered performance cars that follow Colin Chapman’s maxim to “simplify and add lightness.” As a thrilling nod in that direction, Lotus confirmed plans to launch its first supercar, the Type 135—which may become the new Esprit.

Now, we now know what will power it. On Tuesday, Horse Powertrain—a Geely subsidiary alongside Lotus—unveiled its new Horse W40 powerplant, which the company has confirmed the will power the Lotus Type 135. Horse also manufactures the new V-6 engine that will replace the supercharged Toyota V-6 in the Emira sports car.

The Horse W40 is a twin-turbocharged 4.0-liter V8 with a hot-vee configuration, designed for use in high-performance hybrid vehicle platforms. It weighs just 441 pounds, but it can produce an impressive peak output of over 670 horsepower and 590 pound-feet of torque all on its own. When paired with a hybrid component, Lotus said the Type 135 will be able to deliver more than 986 hp.

The Horse W40 uses a proprietary 8LDCT transmission. It’s an eight-speed transmission with a “sportscar-grade wet dual clutch.” Horse Powertrain claims a shift response time of less than 20 milliseconds, and the transmission can handle up to 774 pound-feet of torque.

Lotus has not yet provided full details about the Type 135—those will be announced before the end of 2026—but the company has confirmed it plans to launch the new machine in 2028. The Type 135 will be all-new, distinct from the Emira; Lotus expects to build the supercar in Europe, unlike the China-made Eletre and Emeya EVs, which may reduce the potential exposure to American tariffs. Lotus has revealed a teaser image of the new car, showing a dual exhaust and a large diffuser.

The Lotus Type 135 may not be the only application for this engine, either. Horse Powertrain says the engine’s low-end torque could be used for trucks and off-road vehicles, if you’ve been keeping a candle lit for Geely-owned Volvo to build a raucous but Scandinavian minimalist Mercedes G-Wagen competitor.
