Packers Film Room: The Tucky Sneak adds a wrinkle

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Since his second year in the league, tight end Tucker Kraft has been something of a battering ram in the sneak game. Since 2024, the Green Bay Packers have used Kraft as the ball carrier in 8 sneak attempts: 3 times on 4th & 1, 5 times on 3rd & 1. He has converted 7 of those attempts, for a Success Rate of 87.5%. The only time he has been stopped was on the 3rd & 1 play against the New England Patriots in Week 9 of this season. His longest gain came in the 2024 Wild Card game, picking up 8 yards on 3rd & 1.

So it would make sense for the Packers to have a variation or two in their bag. We've seen a couple of nice ones over the past couple of years from other teams; who could forget Mark Andrews ' 35-yard TD scamper on 4th & 1 off a spin?

So far, the Packers have kept it pretty vanilla. And why not? After all, an 87.5% Success Rate is exactly what you're looking for in that situation. Take your battering ram and shove him into the line until he gets at least a yard. There's not always a need to overcomplicate things.

However, you need the occasional curveball. You don't want the defense being able to load up in the middle of the line, but there are also explosives to be had if you can catch them out of position. That's what the Packers did in the 1st quarter this past weekend.

Down 3-0 with 8:52 remaining in the 1st quarter, the Packers were facing 3rd & 1 at the Vikings' 44-yard line. They come out in 11 personnel (1 RB, 1 TE, 3 WR), starting in an empty, condensed 3×2 look with Tucker Kraft [85] wide on the left. The Vikings have 4 men up on the line, with 3 linebackers set a few yards behind them.

Kraft shifts under center, and it creates motion on the Vikings side of the ball. Eric Wilson [55] comes up to the line and moves the DT to his side over and Andrew Van Ginkel [43] leaves his spot over Matthew Golden [0] to get up on the line. Ivan Pace Jr. [0] starts moving towards the interior of the line as well.

Everything is condensed to the middle, and the Packers are looking to play off that. At the snap, Kraft pitches back to Jordan Love [10], who rolls right. Chris Brooks [30] is shading out on the right side of the line. He's acting as a personal protector for Love on that side.

Golden slow-plays his release, then breaks on a corner route while Christian Watson [9] works as the trailer from the left.

This is supposed to go to Golden. If Love throws this directly after he gathers the ball, he likely has Golden for a nice gain. However, Isaiah Rodgers [2] is playing as the boundary on that side. He's initially held close to the line (in case of a Love scramble), but after Love is steered back inside by Van Ginkel, Rodgers breaks to the corner, taking away Golden. Love may think the throw to Golden would need to be behind him, and thus harder to corral.

So Love sets and fires to Watson in the middle of the field. Watson does not appear to ever truly be expecting this ball. He looks like he's slow-playing his release in the same way Golden does, but he never looks to hit a different gear or to break sharply and create a little more separation.

Love finds him all the same, and the Packers pick up 15 yards and the 1st down.

And if we're opening up the floor for other ideas off of this, I humbly offer up the Fake Sneak/TE Pop.
