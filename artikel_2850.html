Game Preview: Knicks at Raptors, November 17, 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

No, it's not a typo or mix-up by your favorite sports blog on the worldwide web. The Knicks are, in fact, still in Toronto. New York's basketball team hopes the peameal bacon sandwiches and poutine go down well and that they can make it 15 straight wins against the reeling Raptors on Tuesday. Sunday showed how quickly a game like this can go south in the north. The Knickerbockers needed a charismatic late-game comeback to pull out an overtime thriller.

If Mike Brown and his men are looking for a less stressful evening, they should take a lesson from Sunday's near loss. Toronto controlled the glass for most of the game and got what felt like a gazillion offensive rebounds. Everyone from Collin Murray-Boyles and Scottie Barnes to Alijah Martin and Allen Graves found something underneath the Knicks' hoop. Some of that is to be expected against one of the best offensive rebounding teams in the league, but New York still can't let the Dinos stomp all over them on the glass.

Toronto is still without Kawhi Leonard, their big-time offseason Aspiration (sorry, acquisition). "Nobody did any, you know, wrongdoing," as he once said. He's just injured right now, and rumor has it he can't even make it to his newest marketing gigs. Without Leonard, expect the Raptors to lean harder into their strengths. They'll crash the offensive glass, force turnovers, and muck up the game, giving their superior athleticism, their emotions, and maybe even the crowd a chance to take over. Scotiabank Arena is a hard place to play, so the Knicks would do well to keep the Canucks quiet.

Scottie Barnes, in particular, needs to be silenced. He returned Sunday after missing four games with a groin strain and dumped a whopping 30 points on the Knickerbockers, almost willing his Dino friends to victory. His late-game scoring barrage was another sign that the always-talented 25-year-old might have taken another step forward. The ultra-athletic, rangy jack-of-all-trades once lacked the killer instinct and game of a true frontline scorer. This season, "Scottie B" is averaging 24.2 points per game. Apparently, he was really upset about how his club fumbled the game away on Sunday, so expect another big effort out of OG's old friend.

Barnes is a load to deal with, and New York has plenty of problems of its own. Mikal Bridges and Josh Hart are struggling mightily, leaving fans to wonder whether a change to the starting lineup could be in the cards. Landry Shamet is doing his best to fire up those discussions with one spirited effort off the pine after another, though it looks like Mike Brown will stick with his starters for now.

Pick-and-roll defense and protecting the paint have been issues from time to time, but New York has held up pretty well on that side of the ball. Only two teams in the league hold opponents to a lower field goal percentage than the Bockers, who also rank in the top four in defensive rating. On offense, they're barely in the top half of the league, and shooting is a major reason why. The five worst teams by field goal percentage are Brooklyn (3-9), Utah (1-13), Charlotte (4-9), Milwaukee (1-13), and your very own Knicks (7-6). Yup, it's almost a miracle they're above .500 given how poorly they've shot.

Other construction sites include size on the wing, OG Anunoby's early-season inconsistency, and the backup center position. Andre Drummond played himself off the floor Sunday night, looking stuck in molasses against the pick-and-roll. James Wiseman saw meaningful minutes for only the second time this year. He held up decently for roughly 180 seconds, but Mike Brown still has plenty to worry about at backup center. It'll be interesting to see how he plays it Tuesday night.

Same goes for the Knicks in general. Facing the same team twice in a row is weird, especially in the same building, even when you've won 14 straight against them. On paper, New York matches up well with Toronto. The Raps don't like to switch on defense, they put a traditional center on Towns, and they don't have the shooters to punish the Knicks for packing the paint. OG is always highly motivated against his former club, and we all know what that means for opponents. After spending the first six and a half seasons of his NBA career north of the border, the former Raptor surely had a few restaurant tips up his sleeve Monday evening. For a second dessert, we'd find win No. 15 against Toronto quite tasty, however it comes about.
