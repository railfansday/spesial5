Lakers coach’s halftime message paid off vs Kings: ‘He lit me up’

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

With his team lacking the requisite toughness and energy during the first half of Friday's matchup against the Kings, Lakers coach JJ Redick delivered a different kind of halftime message.

And he did so in a way he hadn't done that much during the team's first 16 regular-season games, which they won 13 of.

Los Angeles Lakers coach JJ Redick had a passionate message to deliver to his team during halftime. AP Photo/Caroline Brehman

"I feel I did a decent job of regulating at halftime," Redick said. "They may have a differing opinion of that. Can't repeat every word I used. The main message was, 'We've got to be tougher, we've got to bring a little bit of energy.'"

The level of intensity that Redick brought to the locker room was nothing new for Luka Dončić after having played for Redick over 1 ½ years.

"I mean he yells, but I'm used to it," Dončić said.

But the Slovenian superstar felt that Redick's methods were necessary after the Lakers trailed by eight points to a one-win Kings team that had lost its previous seven games.

"Got us all fired up," Dončić said. "Sometimes you get to yell."

The Lakers responded to Redick's message, winning the second half 52-42 to beat the Kings 106-104 and avoid losing consecutive games for the first time this season.

The team's starting center, Walker Kessler, was among the players who took Redick's message to heart.

Kessler, who recorded a points-rebounds double-double in the previous six games and 10 of the previous 11, had just one rebound and four fouls to go with six points at halftime.

"He lit me up a little bit, rightfully so," Kessler said. "But it was constructive. He was like, 'I know you can do this. Do it.'"

Walker Kessler responded well to JJ Redick's halftime message. Getty Images

Kessler had team highs of nine points and six rebounds after halftime.

His biggest blemish in the final two quarters was missing a pair of late free throws that would've haunted the 7-foot-1 big man and the Lakers had Zach LaVine's buzzer-beating 3-point attempt converted.

But he also had one of the biggest highlights of the second half when he made his first 3-pointer of the season, a pull-up from the left corner to give the Lakers a one-point lead early in the fourth quarter.

"I wasn't playing [physically]," Kessler said of Redick's message to him. "I wasn't going up to dunk the ball. I was missing some more touches around the rim. And then defensively, being undisciplined. Jumping at stuff I don't need to jump at. He definitely lit into me. It was good. I needed it. That's what makes a good coach. Knowing when guys need that and when guys don't."

And it was clear the Lakers not only needed the message Redick delivered but also appreciated how it was delivered after not playing their best basketball over the last week to kick off the four-game homestand that ends Monday against the Jazz.

"At the core of everything that all of our coaches say, and JJ especially, they just speak to the truth of what's happening on the court," Matisse Thybulle said. "And if it needs to be said passionately, that's the message. That's how he needs to get it across, and that's what he does.

"But ultimately, JJ doesn't really mince his words, and he doesn't play games. He really gets to the point, and I think as players, we all really appreciate that. And like he understands, and we understand where he's coming from because he's been in our shoes as well."
