Review: GTA 6 (PS5) - The Greatest Open World Game Ever Made

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

What does eight years and an estimated $1 billion get you? Well, GTA 6.

In this long-anticipated crime sandbox, Rockstar has assembled the most technologically accomplished open world ever made, expanding on its groundbreaking work with Red Dead Redemption 2.

But crucially, with its dual protagonists Jason and Lucia, it’s grown up a bit.

This is the franchise at its most mature, shedding the cartoonish cultural caricatures of GTA 5 without losing the biting social commentary the series is known for.

The fictional state of Leonida is undoubtedly the star here; a dense and gigantic urban sandbox populated by an unfathomable array of art assets.

Whether it’s the easy-living coastal island of Key Lento or the mean streets of Crosstown, Rockstar’s artistry is utterly unprecedented.

Zoom in and you’ll see insects attracted to discarded food wrappers; a single convenience store has more unique art assets than entire districts in other open world games. This is remarkable stuff, delivered at such a scale it’s going to leave other publishers with their heads in their hands.

It feels alive, too; a casual visit to a gas station saw me berated by a cyclist for my wayward driving (I told him to chill out), while a brief pause on the side-walk ended up with my car getting hijacked by a masked criminal.

In over 150 hours of play, I’m still discovering random events that add depth and character to the open world. And even when the scripting is pushed to the background, the way every NPC feels designed, with distinctive personality and unique body types, makes this package an unfathomable feat.

But while Leonida is the standout, Jason and Lucia are the vehicles that guide you through it.

Just like GTA 5, each character has their own individual life, but the game is at its best when it brings them together.

I’ve never seen romance made into a mechanic quite like this, aside from perhaps the handholding in ICO, which is repurposed here. (Complete with DualSense haptic feedback when the characters touch.)

Jason is a bit of a beige blank slate, but Lucia brings genuine “girl power” to the story without pandering or feeling insincere.

In fact, in contrast to past GTAs, the many women in this instalment feel much more believable; the game frequently flips expectations on their head, without it ever coming across as forced.

A secret twist revealed in the opening moments adds gravity to the couple’s situation and becomes the driving force behind the story, which takes the pair from pillar to post, fulfilling the whims of a ragtag roster of ne’er-do-wells.

The campaign does fall into cliche territory at times, as the pair bounce from one bad idea to another in order to fulfil their ultimate ambition, and it’s something the characters seem to acknowledge. (“When are we going to get to call our own shots?” Jason wearily ponders at one point.)

But the mission design remains inventive throughout, regularly adding bespoke mechanics and interactions to keep things fresh.

In one early mission, Jason indulges his inner-PowerWash Simulator, as he rinses down a boat while other characters commentate key plot points. I won’t spoil the best moments, but needless to say there are missions here that will be remembered for a long time.

There’s also a slate of optional pastimes running concurrently to the main campaign which add genuine variety to the sandbox.

I was most enamoured by Jason’s home entertainment setup, which includes a distinctly PS4 coded console that can be powered up. I’ll leave you to discover his selection of fully playable games.

But beyond that, the ability to go hunting or even play a round of golf means you can lose yourself in this sandbox for hours at a time, and never really tire of the activities available to you.

Some of the action sequences remind me of all-time classics like Uncharted 3 – fist-pumping, set-piece heavy rollercoaster rides that push Rockstar’s engine to its limits, all within the confines of a meticulously designed open world.

Controller response is vastly superior to the studio’s previous games, with the cover shooting system feeling much more natural and the aiming mechanics buoyed by a pretty fun “bullet time” mechanic.

However, the game does fall into the trap of introducing heavily armoured bullet sponge enemies towards its conclusion, as it runs out of ways to make human-on-human encounters interesting.

That said, I really appreciated the agency GTA 6 gives you over how you approach each situation; non-vitals can be targeted to incapacitate enemies without murdering them, while it’s perfectly possible to sneak in and out of many areas without ever being detected.

This all builds a criminal profile which changes how the police and wider world will interact with you. Meanwhile, you can influence Jason and Lucia’s relationship itself, opting to keep them close or distant depending on how you want things to play out.

An early optional objective allows you to decorate Jason’s idyllic beach house in anticipation of Lucia’s arrival. Complete it in time and the couple will grow closer; ignore it and she’ll acknowledge you didn’t go the extra mile.

Other interactions are subtler: a kiss and cuddle on the balcony of the pair’s Vice City penthouse will strengthen their bond, but it’s up to you whether you want to invest in their relationship or not.

Multiple endings mean there are actual consequences to your actions, even if some decisions along the way feel more like an illusion than something truly meaningful.

The plot does begin to drag in its latter phases, and I do think Rockstar could have tightened up the roughly 60 hour storyline, which will likely take the average player around 80 hours to complete when accounting for the vast amount of side content as well.

(A 100% save could take anywhere up to 300 hours or more, depending on how much support you get from guides.)

As a crime sandbox, I also wanted to mention the ‘Wanted’ system, which has come on leaps and bounds.

A coordinated robbery can be accomplished without ever triggering a traditional car chase, as the game accounts for your outfit, vehicle, weapons, and much more.

Successfully scouting out your targets, and planning accordingly with a change of clothes and getaway car, can see you slip past the police undetected. Few moments in gaming are more satisfying than when you casually drive past a fleet of incoming cop cars, with a bag of successfully looted narcotics in your trunk.

Some players may respond negatively to the life sim elements here, which reward you for exercising and require you to occasionally put gas in your car or eat something.

But I think it adds to the immersive nature of the open world, and concessions have been made to the realism when it makes sense; a quick request to Scooter Bros, for example, will see your favourite car instantaneously delivered to your current location, so you don’t have to wait around.

The biggest downside to the outright awesome spectacle of the open world is that it runs at 30fps on the PS5 Pro, with no option to double the frame rate or tailor the presentation to taste, as has become standard in most other games.

Objectively this is disappointing, but given the sheer scale of Leonida I think it’s a necessary compromise.

It should be noted that despite all this, the frame pacing is solid and the game feels smooth and responsive as a result. I encountered very few (if any) instances of slowdown – even when flying over the map in a jet or speeding down highways being chased by rival gangs.

The audio presentation matches the high standards of the visuals, with different engine growls depending on how you’ve modified your vehicle. (Midnight Club fans may be excited to learn the racing missions here rival most dedicated franchises like Need for Speed alone.)

There are multiple fist-pumping audio cues, which leverage the title’s enormous licensed soundtrack to excellent effect. But credit must also go to composer duo Justice, who bring their distinct brand of disco-inspired electronica to the soundscape, elevating some of the best missions to impressive highs.

And I was blown away by how much texture the DualSense adds, with one of my favourite features being the feedback you feel when you pass cars at speed on the highway. The only thing I didn’t like was the use of adaptive triggers, which feel far too stiff and lack the nuance of first-party games.

GTA 6 is the deepest, densest, most accomplished open world since Red Dead Redemption 2.

Rockstar has set a new sandbox standard here, with a world that feels alive and populated with real people; Leonida is a place where it appears almost anything can happen at any time.

Crucially, despite the 30fps cap, the gameplay feels responsive and you have much more authorship over how you want to approach it. I enjoyed preparing for my robberies and feeling like I was rewarded for the legwork.

The audiovisual presentation is a new high for the industry, with a frankly flabbergasting array of art assets bringing it all to life.

But I think Jason and Lucia’s ragtag romance, which is central to everything in the game, also demonstrates an unexpected maturity – without completely removing the silly sense of downright dumb humour the series is known for.

Despite unprecedented expectations, GTA 6 delivers – and then some.
