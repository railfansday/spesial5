After finding recent versions of Apple’s iPads and HomePods much easier to disassemble for battery replacements or other repairs, iFixit assumed it would be the same story for Apple’s new HomeView hub that looks like a seven-inch tablet stuck to a smart speaker. The reality was much different and by the time they got the HomeView apart iFixit’s skilled techs had “ripped a connector, snapped a speaker in two, and put more force on the poor logic board than any of us were comfortable with.”

The tablet section of the HomeView proved to be the bigger challenge to disassemble as a result of some questionable decisions with Apple’s design. The glue used to secure the display in place “held on harder than on any Apple product we’ve opened recently,” iFixit says. And while Apple’s decision to turn the HomeView’s entire aluminum chassis into a heat sink was clever, the thermal bond between a heat shield and the device’s logic board made separating the two a “remarkably risky operation.” While these design decisions possibly streamlined assembly at a factory, the extensive use of glue makes servicing the HomeView incredibly difficult.

In an accompanying teardown video iFixit also reveals how much smaller the speaker drivers are in the wall-mounted version of the HomeView versus the tabletop option. Although both versions are priced at $299, the wall version costs a little more when you factor in the required mount that’s not included, leaving iFixit feeling that you’re getting less performance while spending more money.
