Goldman Sachs disclosed this week that it held about $1.28 billion in spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) on September 30. That is roughly double what its two main funds were worth three months earlier.

The filing, a 13F report that large investment managers send to the SEC every quarter, also shows how Goldman got there. It trimmed its stake in BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) and moved into Fidelity’s FBTC, which it raised more than 50-fold.

We converted Goldman’s fund shares into BTC. Its two main funds held about 14,100 BTC at the end of September, up 43% from about 9,900 BTC. BTC’s own 43% rise over the quarter did the rest of the doubling.

Since then, Bitcoin has fallen 22% to about $64,900, so the stakes in these filings are worth much less today.

Millennium Management, the hedge fund, reported about $1.21 billion. Both firms had cut these positions in the second quarter and rebuilt them in the third.

A move from one fund to another doesn’t have to be a bet on the price. Banks like Goldman also act as market makers and hedge options trades with ETF shares. Bitwise analyst Camran Khosravi has noted that 13F filings can make that routine trading look like a large bet on one fund, and that they leave out short positions.

Dollar values in 13F filings move with the price, and BTC rose from $58,625 on June 30 to $83,624 on September 30. To strip that out, we converted each bank’s share count into BTC using the funds’ September 30 prices.

Wells Fargo’s number comes with a caveat too. About 85% of its IBIT position sits in its two brokerage arms, which means it largely belongs to clients, not the bank.

The filings are a snapshot of September 30, when BTC traded near $83,600. Since then, U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs have had two heavy weeks of outflows in November, and bitcoin trades at $64,900 on Binance.

On the daily chart, bitcoin sits below its 50-day exponential moving average (EMA), an average that gives recent prices more weight, at $76,230, and its 200-day EMA at $75,870.

Goldman’s filing shows bigger bitcoin ETF positions, but much of the gain came from the price. The next 13F filings, due in mid-February, will show whether Wall Street banks kept buying through November’s drop.
