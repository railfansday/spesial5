Kraken Parent Payward Reports Quarterly Recovery Despite Weak Year-on-Year Profit

Kraken parent Payward has reported a sequential improvement in quarterly profitability even as its business remains smaller on several measures than it was a year earlier. The company posted $60 million in adjusted EBITDA for the third quarter of 2026, up from $23 million in the previous quarter, alongside an expansion of its platform beyond conventional cryptocurrency trading.

Those results point to a business trying to diversify while navigating a difficult trading environment. They also require attention to the company's chosen metrics: adjusted EBITDA and its newly emphasised Total Platform Margin are not the same thing as statutory net profit or ordinary gross revenue.

In its November 13 financial update, Payward reported Total Platform Margin, or TPM, of $487 million, compared with $436 million in the second quarter. That represented 12% sequential growth, although TPM was also down 12% from a year earlier. Adjusted EBITDA increased to $60 million from $23 million in Q2, while remaining substantially below the year-earlier result.

Payward has changed how it presents the business, replacing a previously highlighted adjusted-revenue measure with TPM. The company says this approach deducts costs passed on to customers, including certain staking rewards, to provide a better view of what the platform retains. Investors should compare periods on consistent definitions rather than treating a renamed headline metric as automatically equivalent to prior revenue figures.

Total platform transaction volume reached about $353 billion, up 22% quarter on quarter, though down 30% from Q3 2025. Funded accounts increased to around 7.1 million, reflecting an expanding account base even as overall trading conditions remained challenging. The combination suggests growth in the number of customers does not necessarily translate into a proportional increase in transaction activity every quarter.

The company also highlighted transaction-based, asset-based and access-based sources of platform margin. Together, asset and access activity represented slightly more than half of TPM, indicating an effort to rely less exclusively on trading fees. Whether those businesses remain resilient through another difficult market cycle will be more telling than one period of sequential recovery.

Payward's broader ambitions include banking, asset management, services for other businesses and AI-driven financial tools. Its statement describes plans to introduce an agent-oriented layer intended to let customers authorise software to perform actions within programmed risk limits. Such a system raises practical questions about consent, error handling and how a customer reverses an unwanted automated action.

NewsBTC previously covered Payward's proposed move into European banking through Saldo, which illustrates how the company is seeking a wider relationship with customers than a trading account alone. These plans should not be confused with proof that every announced product is already commercially available or licensed in every market.

Expanding into adjacent activities can smooth the effects of volatile crypto trading, but it can also introduce new costs and regulatory obligations. Payward's investment in Aave covered by NewsBTC is another example of the company's interest in market infrastructure beyond centralised exchange transactions.

The next quarterly update should show whether TPM growth continues without depending on a short-lived jump in volumes, and how much of the improvement reaches the bottom line. For now, the Q3 release offers a mixed but informative picture: recovery from Q2, weaker comparisons with last year and a company making a deliberate bet on diversification.
