4 Federal Agencies Are Now Monitoring US Air Traffic Control Through The Thanksgiving Rush

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Federal officials have formed the "Tiger Team" to monitor flight operations across the Thanksgiving holiday. The government hopes that it will prevent groundings during the busiest travel week of the year. The Tiger Team first met on November 16, and Travel Pulse reports that they will continue to meet once a week.

The group was formed in response to the four-hour nationwide ground stop on November 13. Millions of travelers across the country were stranded, and the stop would have been even more disastrous had it occurred during Thanksgiving.

The Tiger Team will include top officials from the FAA, the Department of Defense, the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, and the Department of Transportation (DOT). The White House has used the group in an attempt to persuade the traveling public that flights across the holiday will be safe and seamless. It comes during a year when fewer travelers are expected to fly because of expensive airfares caused by the ongoing crisis in the Middle East.

White House spokesperson Allison Schuster told the Daily Wire:

“President Trump and [Transportation] Secretary [Sean] Duffy have brought together the best and the brightest across the government to ensure what happened last Friday can never happen again. Work continues, and Americans can rest assured their country’s aviation is the best in the world.”

The ground stop earlier this November was the longest in US aviation since the 9/11 terror attacks. Combined with the knock-on disruption, over 17,000 flights had been canceled from the Friday of the stop to the Sunday immediately after it. American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, Southwest Airlines, and United Airlines all canceled over 1,000 flights on the day, accounting for between 26 and 34% of their schedules, with the three legacy airlines also delaying a further 800 flights each.

The ground stop had knock-on disruption that continued well beyond the weekend itself. Displaced aircraft and crews had to get to where they should be before service could resume without disruption. Delta extended its flexible rebooking policy in response and asked passengers to allow extra time when traveling.

Sean Duffy’s remarks put US airline merger speculation firmly back in play.

The Tiger Team's work isn't about preventing more ground stops. Instead, it will likely focus on ensuring that one such stop doesn't occur during this exceptionally busy period for air travel. Transportation Secretary Duffy has not ruled out the possibility of further ground stops while the US' air traffic control system continues to be updated. "We have to get off [the] old network, [the] old telecom," he told CBS News' Face the Nation.

Duffy has demanded a vast amount of additional funding from Congress in his efforts to make nationwide groundings a thing of the past. He has requested an additional $50.1 billion to modernize the US' air traffic control technology, in addition to the $12.5 billion granted to the DOT in 2025. That funding got the current overhaul project underway.

The Trump administration has placed the blame for the disruption firmly on the FAA's aging and inadequate technology. It says this needs to be replaced rapidly, while ruling out nefarious activity by state or non-state actors.
