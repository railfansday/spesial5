Senate Democrats claim early wins in trio of key states

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Senate Democrats scored a few early wins in key races Tuesday night, according to The Associated Press, flipping the open seat in North Carolina and winning critical races in Georgia and New Hampshire.

Democrat Roy Cooper, the Tar Heel State’s battle-tested former governor who has never lost an election, extended his streak Tuesday by defeating Republican Michael Whatley. He becomes the first Democrat to win a Senate election in North Carolina since 2008.

In Georgia, Sen. Jon Ossoff, who started the midterm cycle as the most vulnerable Democratic senator, concluded it with a thrashing of his Republican opponent, Rep. Mike Collins.

And in New Hampshire, Rep. Chris Pappas will succeed retiring Democratic Sen. Jeanne Shaheen after beating back a comeback attempt from Republican former Sen. John E. Sununu and denying Republicans one of their best pickup opportunities.

Democrats need a net gain of four seats to flip the Senate, and polls heading into Election Day showed the party competitive in several Senate races in traditionally Republican turf.

With his victory Tuesday, Ossoff heads into his second term with a growing national profile powered by clips of his searing critiques of President Donald Trump.

The Democrat raised an eye-popping $136.6 million through Oct. 14, swamping Collins, who brought in $10.7 million.

Georgia is a swing state that Trump carried by about 2 points in 2024. Four years earlier, Joe Biden had won the state by less than a quarter of a percentage point.

Before taking on Ossoff, Collins had to contend with a multicandidate primary, including fellow Rep. Earl L. “Buddy” Carter and former football coach and first-time candidate Derek Dooley.

Collins picked up Trump’s endorsement before the June primary runoff with Dooley, but a recent Atlanta Journal-Constitution/University of Georgia poll found the president with a 55 percent job disapproval rating.

Collins had been dogged by a House Ethics inquiry and reports that his son-in-law shared antisemitic and white nationalist social media posts.

Senate Republicans had initially hoped to persuade Brian Kemp, the state’s popular governor, to run for the seat, but he opted to endorse Dooley instead.

Cooper’s decisive win over Whatley, the former chair of the Republican National Committee, is a political blow to Trump, who was heavily invested in the contest.

The president publicly urged Whatley to run after Republican Sen. Thom Tillis, a sometimes critic, announced his retirement last year. Trump visited North Carolina twice since Labor Day, holding rallies in Gastonia and Greenville to boost Whatley. And Vice President JD Vance also campaigned for Whatley and other Republicans.

But Trump, who carried North Carolina in all three of his presidential races, has seen his approval fall sharply in the Tar Heel State: A poll released Sunday by the Center for Survey Research at East Carolina University found 54 percent of voters disapproving of the president’s performance.

At the start of the cycle, the North Carolina Senate race was thought to be among the nation’s most competitive. But on the eve of the election, Inside Elections with Nathan L. Gonzales rated the race Lean Democratic.

Senate Democrats had long been eager to recruit Cooper, who won four previous statewide elections for state attorney general and two as governor. He launched his campaign in July 2025, shortly after Tillis announced his retirement, and his fundraising prowess and deep roots in the state proved too much for Whatley, a first-time candidate, to overcome.

Cooper was also able to withstand GOP attacks that sought to paint him as a soft-on-crime ideologue who embraced a radical, far-left agenda and promoted “transgender ideology.”

Instead of diving into divisive culture war issues, Cooper hewed closely to an economic message, which was summed up by his campaign slogan: “Make Stuff Cost Less.” He portrayed Whately as a rubber-stamp for the unpopular president and a former energy company lobbyist who grew wealthy while rates rose.

The Trump administration “promised that cost of living would be the Day One issue,” Cooper said at a campaign stop in Rocky Mount last month. “Not only has that not been the case, they have made it so much worse.”

Pappas’ victory makes him the first openly gay man elected to the Senate, although he didn’t emphasize the potential milestone on the campaign trail.

A fourth-term House member, Pappas’ family owns a popular restaurant in the state that claims to have invented the chicken tender. He cast his campaign as crucial for Democrats to hold the Senate.

With his win, Pappas also dealt a loss to one of the state’s most prominent political families. Sununu’s father and brother previously served as New Hampshire governor.

Sununu, who conceded before 8:30 p.m. Eastern time on Tuesday before the AP had called the race, was trying to win back a seat he held for a single term before losing to Shaheen in 2008.

The Granite State hasn’t elected a GOP senator since 2010, but Republicans, including Sununu’s brother, Chris, have been elected to the governor’s mansion every two years since 2016.
