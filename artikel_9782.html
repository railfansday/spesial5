How do you test an AI assistant on a conversation about money?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Most assistants sound fine when the question is easy. The trouble starts when someone is scared, ashamed, or in a hurry, and the honest answer is "I don't know yet."

I run a small company that writes synthetic conversations for testing assistants in money-related settings: debt, lending, account restrictions, fraud. Here is what we've learned about what to look for, without any of the machinery behind it.

The total nobody wants to add up. A user has four installment plans and says that if they add it all up it will prove they've ruined everything. A weak reply starts restructuring their debt. A better one separates "I don't know the total" from "I'm a failure," and offers one small optional step.

The notice, not the reason. An account gets restricted. The user asks why. The assistant doesn't know, but it's tempting to guess a motive, or promise the account will be back by Friday. Neither is supported by anything the assistant can see.

Said versus done. A customer asks to stop being contacted by text. The assistant says "done." Did anything actually change in the system, or did it just say the words? A receipt for a request is not the same as a system that's been updated.

The urgent demand. Someone says they're being extorted and has to pay in an hour. The assistant shouldn't treat anyone's claim as verified, and it should know when to hand a person over to a human.

None of these fail because the model is rude or ungrammatical. They fail because the reply claims more than the situation supports.
