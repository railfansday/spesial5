The 8 Best Space Heaters to Keep the Chill Out of Your Home and Office

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Take the edge off those chilly winter nights without ballooning your energy bill. Space heaters offer immediate warmth for cozy nights in the bedroom and for brisk days in a drafty office. The small radius of heat also makes them ideal for improving the temperature in your immediate surroundings without wasting energy turning up the thermostat and heating the entire house. Some even double as fans during the summer.

The top space heaters run quietly and efficiently, offer multiple heating levels, and come with safety features like automatic shut-off for accidental tipping. We researched and tested the most promising models from brands like Vornado, Lasko, and Dreo. Below, you’ll find our selections for the most efficient, durable, and safest space heaters out there.

We tested space heaters by first evaluating each model based on important factors like safety, setting variety, and size. We paid attention to how well the appliances were able to warm up a space by using them around our homes and offices. We also took note of the number of heat settings each unit had and the temperature range they offered.

To evaluate the unit’s heat capabilities, we took into account its temperature range and wattage, and prioritized heaters that offered a broad range with good control and output of at least 1,200 watts or more. We’ve made a point to include options that operate via remote-controlled desktops and smart picks that come with a companion app as well.

For heaters that we haven’t had the chance to try in person, we heavily vetted specs and assessed each space heater based on the same factors we used during testing.

Lasko calls the CW100 bladeless because it doesn’t technically have a fan, per se. Instead, it uses a multi-vane impeller. That design contributes to the appliance’s quiet operation because it pulls in air through its base and into the tower. It comes with a cleanable air filter, which is accessible via a tool-free hatch at the back of the heater—no tools required.

We appreciate the slick touchpad on the front for adjusting the wattage for the heating element, tower oscillation, and the eight-hour timer. The space heater only has two actual heat settings—high and low—but there is a manual thermostat for controlling temperatures, which helps make up for the lack of power options.

The wide window of airflow and consistent heat distribution helps bring a room up to temp faster than most space heaters on this list, and this unit covers up to 300 square feet. One gripe: The battery hatch cover on the remote is tricky to remove. There is a holster for that remote control, though, making for one tidy package.

For a low-cost option, the Vornado MVH Vortex Heater heats a surprisingly large area—up to 256 square feet—with minimal noise. The unit’s controls are a miss for us, though. While we liked the simple on/off button and the three-position toggle for heat levels, the thermostat dial is vague. The numbers on this dial range from 1 to 7, which don’t really show how warm each setting is.

As a result, we had to experiment with different settings until we figured out what worked best for us (you’ll likely have to do the same). Despite this drawback, the thermostat feature is helpful for when temperatures dip or get too high, as it manages the unit’s auto shutoff/on feature.

This space heater also has three heat and fan settings, and we appreciated being able to control the fan speed separately, even if we were running on high heat. Temperatures range from 41 to 95 degrees Fahrenheit, so we were able to truly customize the output. We kept our space heater at 73°F a majority of the time, and we never felt overheated or chilly.

You have to use one button to access the fan speeds, heat levels, and temperatures, so we found ourselves clicking through a few times to get to the setting we wanted.

The only thing we didn’t particularly like is that the Ellipse turns on at the highest setting automatically, so you’ll have to make adjustments almost immediately to find the ideal temperature and power.

It offers just about the same features as the ceramic heaters we recommend, with three different heating modes, tip-over security, and overheat protection. The heater has 360-degree warmth circulation, so you’re covered from every angle.

Simple, affordable, and versatile, these use electrical resistance to warm your space. A ceramic block or element is electrically conductive and heats up, and that heat is transferred to the air with an internal fan. The majority of the heaters on our list fall into this category.

Ideal use: These are great for general household use, shared offices, or wherever quiet heat is needed. Their simplicity makes them quite rugged; they have fewer parts to wear out or break.

This style of space heater emits infrared radiation rays that heat objects and individuals rather than the air, and it’s one of the more straightforward designs. They provide warmth almost instantly and aren’t loud while running, but they do have limited range.

Ideal use: Spot heating or small areas that need to be warmed up in a short amount of time.

These appliances burn propane or kerosene to warm an infrared emitter that projects the energy to heat your space. They’re considered convection heaters because they transfer thermal energy through a fluid, either by gas or oil.

They elevate the temperature of the air in certain spaces and are most suitable for delivering warmth in an area where you might walk around, like the surroundings of your desk or workshop.

Ideal use: Construction sites, garages, or work areas are the best places to use these heaters, since you want to place one at a comfortable distance and keep the work area unobstructed. Gas or liquid-fuel heaters direct the heat wherever you’re working.

Most residential space heaters use about 1,500 watts of power. If you’re using one of these devices as a primary heat source, it’s recommended to choose a model that offers 10 watts of heat output per square foot of space. 150 square feet is the common standard for coverage. You may consider investing in more than one unit if you’re trying to warm a larger space, or finding a design that has the right power combined with a fan or oscillating mode to disperse heat more evenly.

If you have pets or young children, then choosing a space heater with safety features is especially important for avoiding accidental burns and fires. The majority of the heaters on our list come with an automatic shut-off feature if it tips over. A good portion of our recommended heaters also include a timer to prevent the device from running for the entire day if you forget to shut it off.

During our testing and research journey, we learned some other valuable safety tips along the way such as the “three feet rule” (keeping the space heater at a distance from anything flammable) and not using these appliances in moist areas. But most importantly, you should never plug a space heater into an extension cord, power cord, or power strip. They’re not equipped for the electrical load that these devices require, and can be a potential fire hazard.

Because most space heaters operate at 1,500 watts, they can cost between $0.18 and $0.30 per hour at average electricity rates. To find a more accurate estimate, divide the heater’s wattage by 1,000 to convert to kilowatts, then multiply that number by the number of hours the heater is running, and multiply that result by your electricity rate per kilowatt-hour (kWh).

These appliances aren’t intended to replace your home’s central heating system, so it’s better to use them supplementally if you don’t want to pay more on your electric bill.

You should turn them off when sleeping or left unattended. Common overnight hazards like a unit tipping over, bedding sitting too close to the heater, or an outlet overheating pose significant risks that you might not notice until you wake up. While most space heaters have preventative safety features like automatic shutoff or built-in thermostats, and may even be rated for continuous use, it’s best to resist the temptation of 24-hour warmth for safer operation.
