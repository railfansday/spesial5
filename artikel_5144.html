XRP Backer Counters SWIFT’s New Speed Report

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Home » Ripple (XRP) » XRP Backer Counters SWIFT’s New Speed Report

The preference for nearly-immediate settlement time windows unites a roster of banks in SWIFT’s chain trial.

A Swift report highlighting faster cross-border payments drew a pointed response from Digital Asset Investor, who replied that he prefers 3-4 seconds and pointed to XRP.

The exchange underscores the ongoing contrast between improvements in traditional interbank messaging and the settlement times associated with blockchain-based rails.

Swift’s Spotlight on Speed report, released this week, shows that 50% of payments on its network now reach the recipient institution within 40 seconds, with 75% arriving within 10 minutes.

The cooperative noted that these figures put the industry ahead of the G20’s target for one-hour end-to-end processing by 2027 in the interbank leg of the journey.

The same report emphasizes that the “last mile”, the period after a payment reaches the receiving bank and before it is credited to the beneficiary’s account, still accounts for about 80% of the average end-to-end journey time.

Across 40 receiving markets analyzed, median last-mile processing sat near one hour in roughly one-third of cases, with wide variation between cross-border markets.

The conglomerate has called for greater use of one-leg-out models that connect international payments to domestic instant systems to reduce those final delays.

Digital Asset Investor’s reply to the Swift post was brief: “I prefer 3-4 seconds. XRP.” The comment references the XRP Ledger’s typical ledger close time of roughly 3-5 seconds, which provides finality for on-ledger transfers.

In Ripple’s On-Demand Liquidity (ODL) model, XRP is used as a bridge asset so that currency conversion and settlement can occur without pre-funded nostro accounts.

End-to-end ODL flows that include exchange conversions have been described in the 3-5 second range for the core ledger step, though real-world completion including compliance and local payouts is often measured in seconds to a few minutes depending on the corridor.

The two claims are not measuring identical segments of a payment. Swift’s 40-second figure tracks time to the recipient institution, while the longer last-mile delays occur after that point.

XRP’s cited 3-4 second window refers primarily to ledger settlement of the bridge asset itself.

Traditional systems still handle the bulk of global bank-to-bank volume and benefit from established compliance and messaging standards; blockchain rails emphasize reduced pre-funding and faster finality on the asset transfer.

Whether the shorter settlement window on XRP produces a meaningfully better end-user experience continues to depend on how the on- and off-ramps, local banking rails, and compliance steps are integrated in any given corridor.
