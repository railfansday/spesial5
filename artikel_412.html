Bobby Moynihan on Reggie Dinkins, his SNL return, and You Can See Everything

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

On Mashable's Say More, hosts Kristy Puchko (Mashable's Entertainment Editor) and Mark Stetson (Senior Creative Producer) bring humor and their trusted insights to the biggest shows, films, digital trends, and cultural moments. From viral-worthy rants and passionate raves to smart recaps and first-look teasers, they cover what everyone is talking about. 2026 has been a banner year for Bobby Moynihan. On top of returning to Saturday Night Live as Drunk Uncle and voicing characters in Grand Theft Auto VI and Pixar's Hoppers, he also plays a daffy sidekick to Tracy Morgan in NBC's exceptional comedy The Fall and Rise of Reggie Dinkins.

Now in its second season, Reggie Dinkins follows a disgraced football player (Morgan) who invites a documentarian (Daniel Radcliffe) to stay with him and make a film that will rehabilitate his image. The growing relationship between the two oddly echoes the story at the heart of one of the year's buzziest titles: Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim's Elizabeth Holmes documentary, You Can See Everything.

"When I saw the trailer for You Can See Everything, I went, 'That's our show,'" Moynihan told Mashable Entertainment Reporter Belen Edwards while he visited our Say More studio.

The connection between the documentary and the sitcom was so clear that a scene parodying the infamous "why would I deceive you?" exchange wound up in an episode of Reggie Dinkins that was scheduled for later in the season. However, since the episode would have aired far later than the trailer's initial release, the show's team worried the reference would feel too dated and removed it. Morgan, Radcliffe, and Moynihan shot the parody as promo for Season 2 instead, so it still saw the light of day.

"If I am being one billion percent honest, I don't know if Tracy knows who Elizabeth Holmes is right now, even after we've done it," Moynihan said. "We showed up, and he was like, 'What's this?' and we held up cue cards, and he was like, 'Never mind, I got it.'"
