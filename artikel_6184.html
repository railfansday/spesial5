Allegiant Air Axed All Flights To 9 Airports In 16 Months: See Full List Now

One of America's ultra-low-cost carriers has cut services to a total of nine US airports in the last 16 months. Allegiant Air, based in Las Vegas, Nevada, has quietly trimmed operations to nine major airports, as the airline consolidates its point-to-point operations.

Allegiant offers low fares, providing a unique network of domestic flights across the country. Passengers pay extra fees for additional services such as checked baggage, seat selection, and meals onboard. Using data from Cirium, an online aviation analytics company, the airline overall has trimmed services to nine airports between January 2025 and May 2026.

In January of last year, Allegiant made the decision to suspend operations to Grand Junction (GJT) in Colorado and El Paso (ELP) in Texas. During Allegiant's tenure at Grand Junction, the budget airline operated a total of 2,877 departures, whereas at El Paso, the carrier provided a total of 1,392 departures.

In a report by KKCO 11 News, the airline's withdrawal from both of these airports comes as the airline continues to struggle with a fleet squeeze due to ongoing delays for new aircraft from Boeing. This has left Allegiant with fewer planes than expected to operate its network.

Allegiant's ultimate withdrawal from Grand Junction comes after 18 years of dedicated flights from the airport, and nine years of flying to and from El Paso.

Three additional US airports lost Allegiant Air flights in 2025, as the airline cut services to Norfolk (ORF), Minneapolis (MSP), and San Diego. These airports had a collective total of more than 8,000 Allegiant flights over the period for which routes were served.

Norfolk lost its Allegiant services in August 2025, providing a total of 2,776 low-cost departures from the Virginia airport. Minneapolis-St. Paul International Airport also saw its Allegiant services withdrawn in August 2025, almost a year before the airline announced its acquisition of Minneapolis-based airline Sun Country. Allegiant operated a total of 1,471 departures from MSP.

San Diego, in Southern California, had its Allegiant services terminated in September last year, with the airline having operated a total of 3,850 low-cost flights to the airport since it began operating to the airport in 2009.

Allegiant has dropped 20 Florida routes, but surprising passenger data reveals many were performing better than expected before cancellation.

At the beginning of this year, Allegiant made the decision to stop serving one of the country's busiest airports, Los Angeles (LAX). This came as the airline made the difficult decision to end its 17-year presence at the airport due to rising operating costs. Across its tenure at LAX, the airline recorded more than 21,000 departures.

Oakland (OAK), San Francisco's second airport, was the next to lose its Allegiant flights in May of this year. The airline cut operations at the airport as part of a wider network reduction. The airline had operated more than 9,800 departures from the airport during the period analyzed.

Palm Springs (PSP), a popular tourist destination in California, also lost its Allegiant services in May, the same month as Columbia (CAE) in South Carolina had its Allegiant budget flights terminated. These two airports combined recorded a total of 3,783 departures while they were served by the ULCC.

Allegiant Air primarily focuses on leisure travel, with nonstop flights from small and medium-sized cities to popular tourist destinations. The no-frills service provides cheap tickets; however, flight frequencies are often just a few times a week rather than multiple times a day.
