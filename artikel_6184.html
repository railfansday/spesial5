Flight Disruptions Ease; DOT Lays Blame for ATC Outage

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Air travel in the U.S. was returning to normal Monday, even as the backlog from Friday’s massive air traffic control outage remained evident.

Flights across the country were taking off regularly, and the long lines seen at airports such as Atlanta and New York-JFK over the weekend were easing. Some travelers are still waiting on rescheduled flights, however, and there will likely be several hundred flight cancellations and thousands of delays as the day goes on.

According to FlightAware, there were around 300 cancellations in the U.S. as of 9 a.m., which is already above average for an entire day of operations. There were about 1,270 delays.

Dallas/Fort Worth, Chicago O’Hare, Charlotte, North Carolina, and Reagan National reported the highest number of cancellations and delays Monday morning.

U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said over the weekend that operations should be close to normal by Monday evening.

According to the DOT and FAA, the outage was caused by a router installed during an upgrade of ATC communications systems.

The contractor that performed that upgrade, Duffy said Saturday, is L3Harris, a legacy technology provider for the FAA.

Either because of an installation mistake or a problem with the router itself, the router began “flooding” other routers in the system, the secretary said, leading to the outage.

The FAA has not made a final determination about the cause, but in either case, “we’re going to hold people accountable,” Duffy said.

“This was an FAA system that didn’t meet the moment,” he added. “A contractor didn’t meet the moment, or the equipment didn’t meet the moment.”

Duffy also noted that L3Harris actually owns the ATC communications network.

“I can’t go somewhere else, they own it,” he said.

Clearly frustrated with the situation, Duffy broadened his criticism to other private companies performing work for the DOT.

“Many of our contractors, many of our partners, they take our money and they provide really poor service to us,” he said. “Full stop. And we have demanded more.”

“L3Harris has been modernizing the communications infrastructure supporting the National Airspace System in close coordination with the FAA,” the company said in a statement released Saturday. “With safety and reliability as our highest priorities, L3Harris, together with its telco partners and the FAA, has completed more than 70% of the planned airspace communications conversion from legacy copper wires to a fiber optic system. We are supporting the FAA in its investigation to understand the conditions that led to the Nov. 13 ground stop of our nation’s air travel. L3Harris takes the nationwide ground stop and its impact on travelers, airlines, and air traffic operations seriously.”

The outage has also rekindled a months-long debate over funding for the DOT’s ongoing ATC modernization program. Duffy said Friday night that the FAA’s initial $30 billion target is no longer enough, and he now wants $50 billion.
