Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Michael Carrick stood perfectly still and raised his left arm, and his index finger, high in the air. Around him, chaos. As it had been all afternoon. Substitute Patrick Dorgu had just headed in a corner to rescue a point for Manchester United in the 96th minute and, maybe, save Carrick his job.

Going into added time and United were two goals behind. It seemed all was lost for them against Aston Villa and for their head coach, but how telling was the reaction, in the directors’ box, of chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox, who both jumped up in celebration? They were far more animated than Carrick and could not contain themselves.

Did their behaviour betray not just their excitement in the moment but their relief at not having to sack Carrick during this forthcoming international break? If so, they need to think again. They need to make a sober and rational decision based on evidence. And the evidence shows that Carrick must go. Because this kind of football is unsustainable. This kind of football does not work. This kind of football is stupid.

United are 18th in the Premier League. They have won one game in 10 in the league. They have conceded 15 goals in their last five matches in the league and Champions League. When they play Liverpool away, their next game, on November 22, it will be the first time they have done so from inside the relegation zone since March 1937.

They appear shockingly disorganised. Tactics seem to be an after-thought, their motivation is lacking until desperation kicks in. United conceded two goals within four minutes at the start of the game. And then two more within eight minutes of the second half kicking off.

The bottom line is this: can they afford not to make a change? If they do not, United could be accused of copping out and gambling with their status. So, Berrada and Wilcox must ask: what is Carrick doing in the dressing room and on the training ground? What messages are he and his assistant Steve Holland sending out? Are the players even listening?

At the end the fans sang Carrick’s name after United became the first team in the Premier League to come back twice from being two goals down. That also summed up the fog of confusion at a club who so obviously lack direction – from the top down – and need to get a grip of themselves. Carrick talked about having to “tighten up” the defending. But everything needs to be stronger. More taut. More competitive.

Chaos can be entertaining. Chaos can be fun. As an advert for the Premier League this was outstanding. United fans will be thrilled with the Fergie-time comeback. But chaos is also unsustainable. No one knows what to expect from United.

In the cold light of day Villa manager Unai Emery will have to ask himself whether he, too, got it wrong. His substitutions did not work, bizarrely taking off too much experience, such as his outstanding captain John McGinn, and he handed United a late initiative when it looked like all hope was lost.

But analyse the goals that United conceded. Any rational response would suggest a change should be made. Take the first two.
