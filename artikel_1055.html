Liverpool calamity hands Iraola his most chastening night yet

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Andoni Iraola looked up to the ceiling of Fenerbahce’s media auditorium as he tried to find the words to describe the madness he had just witnessed.

“Cheap” was his first conclusion, swiftly followed by “messy”; technically, Liverpool’s head coach was right but this was a moment when nobody would have found issue had he gone further. “Absurd” and “preposterous” would have been more accurate portrayals of this calamity.

Liverpool have won two European trophies in Istanbul, but they do not tend to enjoy visits to Turkey. Roy Hodgson, in August 2010, was the last Anfield manager to oversee a win here and there was an irony that the hero of that night in Trabzon – Dirk Kuyt – masterminded this downfall.

Kuyt, interim head coach, got a remarkable response out of a Fenerbahce team who had amassed 1,086 Premier League appearances between them and he was as giddy as the locals after Mason Greenwood, Archie Brown and substitute Vedat Muriqi scored to give the club a famous night.

But even Kuyt would have been the first to recognise the helping hand Fenerbahce were afforded. Liverpool were meek, naïve, and while the Turkish side danced in the rain on the Asian side of the city, Iraola was left to dissect the most chastening 90 minutes of his short Anfield career.

“It’s frustration because it’s a difficult place to come here and to play,” said Iraola. “During most of the game, we were thinking: ‘OK we can win this one.’ But obviously it hasn’t happened. We conceded, I think, messy goals, especially the first and the third one. The second one is a rebound.”

Yet it all started so smoothly. For all that Fenerbahce tried to put Liverpool out of their stride with a soundtrack of high-pitched whistling, Iraola’s side kept the ball well and they might have been ahead in the fifth minute but Victor Muñoz and Bradley Barcola, playing as a central striker, erred.

Another chance went begging when Joe Gomez fizzed a ball across the six-yard box that was screaming to be converted, but nobody could apply the finishing touch. When Trey Nyoni put Liverpool ahead, it seemed like all those local predictions of a resounding away win would come true.

Nyoni is a terrific player-in-the-making and the joy of seeing him wheel away, having become Liverpool’s second youngest Champions League scorer behind Trent Alexander-Arnold, was everything you want to see in football.

So for the landscape to change so dramatically – and so quickly – after Liverpool had taken the lead was staggering, and it all came about after Irfan Can Kahveci slid in and turned a cross from Mattéo Guendouzi on to the bar.

Those who remember Kuyt from his Anfield days 15 years ago will recall a player who never considered any cause to be lost and the Dutchman implored his team to attack the flanks with pace, even earning himself a yellow card at one point. Eventually, the pressure paid off.

Greenwood had felt aggrieved not to have been given a 23rd-minute penalty, after Kostas Tsimikas placed his hands on his back, but he did not have to wait long to make an impact, his opportunity coming after Dominik Szoboszlai conceded a cheap free-kick.

Szoboszlai is rapidly becoming an enigma. How can a player with the gift of being able to score goals from the heavens make the mistakes of a weekend amateur? There was no need for him to foul N’Golo Kanté but the error was compounded, Greenwood’s strike deflecting off Jérémy Jacquet.

Since Manchester United felt duty-bound to sell Greenwood in 2024 the former England forward has continued his career away from the Premier League. After leaving Marseille he has found a home in Istanbul where the fans have taken to him and they bellowed his name following the equaliser.

Alisson Becker, wrong-footed by Jacquet’s decision to break off from the wall and turn his body, was livid and rightly so. Liverpool had gone from sublime (in patches) to ridiculous and the passage of play up to the interval was hugely uncomfortable.

Would things settle down after the break? Not a bit of it. Liverpool, worryingly for Iraola, could not stamp any authority and though there was a brief respite when Cody Gakpo and Alexander Isak combined after being introduced as substitutes, the lead felt as sustainable as a snowball in a sauna.

Fenerbahce’s followers had made this a hot house and they levelled as quickly as they had done in the first period when Brown, who started his career at Derby before having spells in Switzerland and Belgium, stole in at the back post to turn in a flicked clearance that Gakpo got wrong.

A draw would have been fine but Liverpool contrived to throw it away completely. Alisson stayed stuck on his line as a high ball came in, Nyoni, Jacquet and Alexis Mac Allister were weak in the challenge and Muriqi nodded in to spark pandemonium.

Andoni Iraola says he will not waste any more time thinking about the penalty Liverpool were denied against Arsenal and won’t seek an explanation from Pro Ref. There was a significant fallout following the 1-0 loss at Anfield on Sunday with many judges believing Stuart Attwell, who was VAR, should have consulted match official Andy Madley to highlight an obvious pull by Jurrien Timber that prevented Alexander Isak heading a Bradley Barcola cross.

Liverpool’s head coach explained in the heat of the moment that he would tell his players to go down if they felt contact in the area and Cody Gakpo, the Netherlands international, explained last night in Istanbul it was a message the squad had taken on board.

“As a team, we play pretty fair – maybe we should dive some more like the gaffer said,” Gakpo, who also had claims to be awarded a penalty against Arsenal, explained. “On both occasions you could have given a penalty or a foul, but they decided not. We have to live with the decision. That’s it.”

Liverpool have made the long trip to Turkey to take on Fenerbahce on matchday four of the Champions League.

Liverpool enter this fixture having won all three Champions League matches thus far, beating Atletico Madrid, LASK and Villarreal to make it nine points from nine for Andoni Iraola’s side. Liverpool’s five-game unbeaten streak was ended by Arsenal in the league on Sunday. They return to the city where the club enjoyed one of their most memorable nights, beating AC Milan in the Champions League final 21 years ago.

“Istanbul is always going to be very special for Liverpool,” Iraola said. “It is difficult to find a more important night. Tomorrow [tonight] it won’t be easy, you talk about statistics, especially English teams playing here. They have very good players and a very good squad, they know how to play in this competition. We have to start strong in the game. It is very early in the season to establish comparisons. We want to continue how we have done in the Champions League, three games and nine points, it is a good chance to get to 12 points and then finish in the top eight. In addition to the long-term absentees Hugo Ekitike, Conor Bradley and Giovanni, both Ryan Gravenberch and Jeremie Frimpong have missed the trip to Turkey.

Fenerbahce have made a slow start to their Champions League campaign, taking just two points from their opening three fixtures. They have drawn twice against Roma and Slavia Prague at home whilst they suffered a heavy 6-0 loss at Aston Villa. Former Liverpool forward Dirk Kuyt has been appointed their interim coach following the resignation of Ismail Kartal following their recent defeat to arch rivals Galatasaray just over a week ago.

“It is a great opportunity for us to show what we can do,” Kuyt said. “It is also a great opportunity for me, a game between two teams that are very important to me. They come into tonight’s match having lost back-to-back league games since drawing against Slavia Prague on matchday three of the Champions League. They have plenty of former Premier League players in their ranks, including Ederson, N’Golo Kante and Romelu Lukaku.
