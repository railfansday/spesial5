AI helped hackers gain access at 10 unnamed organizations, researchers say

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

The November 12 investigation reports 25 operations against at least 19 organizations during August–September, with humans assigning targets and able to steer.

AI-assisted attacks accessed systems or retrieved data at 10 organizations, according to a November 12 investigation by Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42. The security research team tracked 25 operations against at least 19 organizations during August and September 2026, including semiconductor companies, defense and policy organizations, and civil-society groups.

Unit 42 said the attackers used a customized version of Cairn, an open-source framework for AI-driven penetration testing, paired with Zhipu GLM and DeepSeek models.

The reported outcomes went beyond reconnaissance. The researchers reported unauthorized access to a semiconductor company's production system and to more than one million member records at a US teachers' union.

Attackers also modified a live standards-ballot document and obtained nonpublic data at an industry standards organization, the researchers said.

Humans supplied targets and goals, sometimes adding intelligence gathered beforehand. The framework then planned, executed and recorded subsequent work.

Individual operations lasted from 20 minutes to more than 50 hours, Unit 42 reported, with human steering still possible.

Unit 42 concluded that AI increased the scale and reach of the campaign, not its technical sophistication.

The November 12 investigation adds quantified outcomes to earlier reporting on AI-assisted attacks. CryptoSlate's November 5 coverage of CERT-In's CRAFTY FALCON advisory already named Cairn among AI-assisted offensive-security tools. The reports describe different observed activity; shared tooling does not establish a link between their operators.

Unit 42 assessed with moderate confidence that the activity it tracked represented China-nexus intelligence collection, based on language, tooling and target selection.

For defenders, the report's practical warning is that familiar gaps can support prolonged, coordinated intrusion attempts. Unit 42 says identity hygiene, reducing exposed attack surfaces and software patching remain effective.
