Bitcoin options flash warning as downside premiums surge ahead of Fed minutes

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Short-term Bitcoin options recorded a sharp increase in bearish premiums, with contracts expiring immediately after the Fed announcement seeing the biggest jump.

Bitcoin's options market turned increasingly defensive on Nov. 18, with the premium on short-term downside protection rising sharply ahead of the Federal Reserve's October meeting minutes.

According to Pandabull's Deribit options data, the implied-volatility gap between Bitcoin puts and comparable calls expiring Nov. 19 widened to 8.92 points from 5.08 in the four hours ending at 10:36 UTC.

That 3.84-point increase outpaced the changes in contracts expiring later in November. For context, the Nov. 20 contracts rose 2.86 points to 8.06, while those expiring Nov. 27 rose just 1.25 points to 6.63.

Notably, the Nov. 19 contracts began the period with a lower downside volatility premium than the Nov. 27 contracts but finished above them.

Pandabull’s 10:36 UTC Nov. 18 snapshot compares Deribit Bitcoin 25-delta put IV minus call IV. The nearer expiries widened more over four hours. Over 24 hours Nov. 27 widened most, and Nov. 20 was unchanged at 0.00.

The reversal suggests that Bitcoin's options market was placing greater emphasis on immediate downside risks, with traders facing a major monetary policy announcement later in the day.

The shift occurred as Bitcoin traded around $64,200, struggling to recover from its recent decline toward $62,000.

The heightened caution comes ahead of the Federal Reserve's scheduled release of its October meeting minutes at 19:00 UTC on Nov. 18.

The minutes will offer further insight into the central bank's decision to maintain interest rates between 3.75% and 4% following a divided vote.

At the Oct. 28 meeting, policymakers voted 10–2 to keep rates unchanged, with two officials favoring a quarter-percentage-point increase. The disagreement puts the Fed's assessment of inflation and the direction of monetary policy in focus as Bitcoin investors weigh the possibility of interest rates remaining elevated.

Meanwhile, the expiration schedule makes the latest changes in options positioning particularly significant.

The Nov. 19 contracts expire just 13 hours after the minutes are released, while the Nov. 20 contracts expire 37 hours afterward. Those expiring Nov. 27 provide more than eight additional days of exposure.

The change also contrasts with the preceding 24-hour trend, when Nov. 27 options recorded a larger increase in put skew than either of the nearer expiries.

With Bitcoin already under pressure, the minutes could determine whether the recent surge in short-term defensive positioning persists. A more hawkish account of the Fed's discussions could reinforce demand for downside protection, while signs of a less restrictive policy outlook could ease that pressure.
