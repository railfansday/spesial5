Resident Evil Requiem’s Game Awards Nominee Is Ready for Resident Evil 10

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Here's hoping Angela Sant'Albano's Game Awards nom is the start of something

Angela Sant'Albano had never performed in a video game before Resident Evil Requiem. She'd never done motion capture, either. But after spending months physically throwing herself into the horror of Grace Ashcroft's story, she came away convinced that the medium was a perfect fit.

“I honestly couldn't have been luckier to be in Resident Evil,” Sant'Albano told Polygon a few weeks before her 2026 Game Awards nomination. “I still pinch myself.”

What surprised her most was how little she felt she had to change as a performer. Motion capture, she says, gave her the intimacy of television with the physical freedom of theater.

“Imagine you're doing theater in the round. So you have the audience 360 degrees around you, but then you have the intimacy of television and screen because you have a camera right here with you,” she says. “You can have an incredibly internal dialogue while also having the freedom of the body.”

And Requiem demanded plenty of body. The game's cutscenes were captured with the actors performing everything themselves, often in a darkened studio at London's Imaginarium Studios. Sant'Albano would run, fight, crawl, struggle, and generally work herself into a state of believable panic before scenes that required Grace to be gasping for breath.

“I would just run around, run around, do push ups, do jumping jacks, do whatever I could to keep my heart rate up,” she says. “Grace is a very anxious person. And yeah, sort of doing that when you kind of mimic anxiety in your body and mimic that breath, you then inevitably feel a bit anxious.”

Sometimes the production made the physical reality even more literal. In one early scene, Grace wakes up chained to a gurney. Sant'Albano was actually tilted around while lying upside down; when Grace gets dragged across the floor, Sant'Albano was dragged, too.

“I really believe that that is why the performances came across as so truthful,” she says, “because we put ourselves in a lot of those situations.”

That physical freedom extended to smaller choices, too. During rehearsals, Sant'Albano and the team were encouraged to push Grace's awkwardness and anxiety as far as they could, then pull it back. At one point, Sant'Albano decided there was one thing a terrified woman in her mid-20s would probably do more often.

“I just remember thinking like Grace is like a 25, 27-year-old girl. She's gonna swear,” she says. “How would Angela react in a lot of these situations? And I was like, she would say 'fuck.'”

Finding Grace's voice itself came from digging into everything that made the character uncomfortable in her own skin. Sant'Albano researched stuttering extensively — when it happens, what can make it worse — before building the character from her traumatic history, her relationship with her mother, and her sense that she didn't quite belong at the FBI.

“I sort of just dived into her backstory in order to find her,” she says. “And then through that, like her voice and her posture ... it all kind of came weirdly naturally.”

Now Sant'Albano hopes that work wasn't a one-off. Grace survives Requiem, and her actor sees plenty of unfinished business: her mother's mysterious work, her relationship with Emily, and her growing bond with Leon Kennedy.

“I do hope that Grace will come back,” she says. “I think her story is definitely not over.” But if Grace does return, don't expect her to go full Sarah Connor in Terminator 2.

“She's never gonna be Leon,” she says. “And I think that's what makes her special.”
