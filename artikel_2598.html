Week 11 game preview: Chicago Bears vs. New Orleans Saints

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Chicago Bears are back to business following the bye week.

To kick off the back half of their season, they host the New Orleans Saints. While the Saints haven't looked great in their past few games, this is the NFL, and no opponent should be taken lightly.

And Chicago shouldn't be resting on their laurels. In their last game, they needed a field goal at the end of regulation to win.

Plus, the end of their season is filled with playoff teams and their division opponents. They can't afford to trip up against the bad teams on the schedule.

Let's get into this matchup and see what it will take for the Bears to get a win against the New Orleans Saints Sunday.

Last week: 20-17 loss to the Carolina Panthers

Bears all-time record against: 16-18 (including 2-1 postseason)

Historical meetings: In week 9 of the 1991 season, the 5-2 Bears went to New Orleans to face the 7-0 Saints.

Saints QB Bobby Herbert hit Floyd Turner for a 65-yard score in the first quarter. The Bears added just two Kevin Butler field goals while the Saints tacked on a field goal of their own before halftime.

In the second half, Brad Muster rushed for Chicago's first touchdown of the day. The Saints struck back with Turner again, scoring on a pass from Herbert.

But Jim Harbaugh shook off what had been a bad day to that point and hit Wendell Davis for 27 yards and Neal Anderson for 6 yards, before finding Tom Waddle for a 12-yard score 59 seconds left.

The Bears won 20-17. Harbaugh was 5 of 22 for 61 yards, one TD and two interceptions. Three of his five completions were on the final drive of the game.

Last meeting: The Bears and Saints met in week seven last year.

Spencer Rattler started the game for New Orleans. On their first drive of the game, he was sacked and fumbled, setting up a Jake Moody field goal.

Neither team could get much going, as New Orleans punted, Caleb Williams was intercepted and the Saints missed a field goal. Following the miss, the Bears drove but had to settle for another field goal.

But then, Rattler threw a pick to Nashon Wright, setting up D'Andre Swift for a touchdown four plays later.

Kyle Monangai scored on the next Chicago drive and the Bears were in business.

Rattler rallied to finish the first half with a touchdown to Chris Olave, finding him for a 57-yard completion before hitting him for the 21-yard score.

Rattler again found Olave on the Saints' opening second-half drive for another touchdown.

But the Bears scored two more field goals and Rattler threw two more interceptions. The Bears won 26-14.

Injury report: The Saints listed nine players on their Wednesday injury report.

Offense: The Saints offense ranks 15th in points and 22nd in yards.

Their passing offense ranks 16th and their rushing offense ranks 27th.

Tyler Shough (66.8 cmp. pct./2,173 yds./15 TD/7 INT) is still developing, but has overall been solid and played well for New Orleans. He's led two fourth-quarter comebacks and had two game-winning drives. Lately though, he's been reluctant to throw downfield.

Kellen Moore's offense has Shough targeting Chris Olave (59 rec./764 yds./2 TD), Devaughn Vele (37/379/3) and TE Juwan Johnson (30/317/4). Alvin Kamara (28/160/2) remains a featured part of the offense. First-round pick WR Jordyn Tyson (15/110/0) could return from injury and brings high hopes with him. TE Noah Fant (12/96/3) is a red zone target.

On the ground, it's not been very pretty. Kendre Miller (57 att./159 yds./2 TD) is the leader in carries. Alvin Kamara (49/161/3) is the leader in touchdowns. Shough (40/187/2) is the leader in yards. Travis Etienne (38/142/0) just returned from injury.

As a team their 3.6 yards per attempt ranks 29th in the league and their 811 yards is 27th.

Defense: The Saints defense ranks 25th in points and 29th in yards allowed.

Their passing defense ranks 21st and their rushing defense ranks 27th.

While those numbers aren't impressive, they do have a solid pass rush.

DEs Carl Granderson (8.5 sk/10 QB hits/7 TFL/36 tkls) and Chase Young (8 sk/15 QB hits/5 TFL/19 tkls/4 PD/1 FF) are both in the top-10 for sacks on the year.

LBs Pete Werner (70 tkls/2 sk/2 PD/4 TFL/4 QB hits) and Kaden Eliss (56 tkls/10 QB hits/6 TFL/2 sk/2 PD) lead the team in tackles. Behind them, Justin Reid (46 tkls/2 TFL), Jonas Sanker (52 tkls/1 FR/4 PD/1 TFL) Julian Blackmon (43 tkl/2 PD/2 INT) and Martin Emerson (21 tkls/4 PD/2 INT) are making plays in the secondary.

Kool-Aid McKinstry (35 tkls/6 PD), Quincy Riley (21 tkls/5 PD) and Jonas Sanker (52 tkls/4 PD/1 FR) have all made plays at times, too.

Key matchups: The Bears have a lot of advantages here. The Saints' run defense should allow D'Andre Swift and Kyle Monangai to get going and set up those play-action shots.

Looking at the injury report, New Orleans could be without two key defenders in Werner and Emerson. Emerson had started three straight games after notching two interceptions against the Giants.

On the offensive side, the Saints could be without two OL in center Erik McCoy and RT Taliese Fuaga. Tyler Shough has shown regression in recent weeks, bailing on clean pockets or at the slightest sign of pressure.

The Bears can get him uncomfortable. If they can cover Chris Olave, the Saints don't have much else in terms of game-breakers.

The Saints rank 27th in third-down defense, allowing opponents to convert 45.1 percent. The Bears rank fourth in third-down offense.

New Orleans has allowed 30 rushing touchdowns, which is the third-most in the league.

The Saints are seventh in blitz percentage but 29th in pressure percentage. They have the 12th most sacks in the league, with 26.

The Saints are tied for the fourth-fewest takeaways on defense with nine.
