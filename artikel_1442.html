Olympiacos 2-4 AC Milan: Five things we learned – a breakout night and a glaring issue

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

AC Milan went into their Europa League fixture against Olympiacos in need of a win to get the ball rolling, and win they did.

The eventual 2-4 scoreline only tells part of the story, as Ruben Amorim’s side had to battle back from two goals down. Lorenzo Pirola’s header drew what was officially an own-goal from Mike Maignan, then Jota Silva doubled the lead after a slack pass from Koni De Winter was punished.

Then came the Omari Hutchinson show. The Englishman gave Milan hope with a wonderful solo goal, before turning provider for Alphadjo Cisse and Christian Pulisic to complete the turnaround. Diego Moreira’s late goal on the counter made sure of a very important three points.

So, the Diavolo return to Italy with smiles on their faces, cultivating the hopes of a play-off spot and keeping the top eight dream alive too. Ivan Stoev has picked out five major talking points from a dramatic night on the outskirts of Athens.

Omari Hutchinson has had mixed performances when given the opportunities since his arrival in the summer and the feeling is that he had plenty of work to do in order to justify the hefty optional price tag on his loan contract. Games like the one against Olympiacos are just the type of opportunities for the attacker to prove his abilities.

The Nottingham Forest loanee didn’t get off to the best start as Milan struggled in the opening 45 minutes with their opponents pressing very high and aggressively. This starved him of possession in a central role and he didn’t get the ball to his feet much, let alone with space to run at.

In the second half, however, Hutchinson moved to a wider role on the right flank and he immediately started causing trouble for the Greeks.

In the 56th minute it was an impressive solo run by the Englishman that started Milan’s comeback as the winger picked up the ball in his own half and carried it all the way to the opponent’s penalty box, before cutting onto his left foot and beating the keeper with a tidy finish in the far post.

Seven minutes later Hutchinson continued to cause trouble down that flank. This time around he found Cisse with a lovely cross and the youngster made no mistake to bury it and bring the scores level.

The former Arsenal academy man wasn’t finished yet though: in the 71st minute he provided yet another inviting cross into the penalty box which found Pulisic, who applied a lovely volleyed finish to make it 3-2.

Hutchinson then continued to be a menace in transition as he showed relentlessness even in the dying minutes. A corner was cleared and he picked up possession, somehow beat a man and ran with it, getting the ‘hockey assist’ for Moreira’s goal.

It was an absolutely brilliant second-half performance and if he finds continuity then that option to buy might just before more appealing, or at the very least a re-negotiation to keep him permanently. The issue is that the Olympiacos owner also owns Forest, and he saw that display first-hand.

The goals Milan conceded were quite preventable yet again. The feeling is definitely growing as January approaches that the back line needs a true leader in the middle of a defence that continues to be error-prone.

Mario Gila has looked like a bargain of a signing and he has been superb, but he is playing mostly as a right-back/outside centre-back. Strahinja Pavlovic remains a key player as well, but the same applies as with Gila just on the left side.

Koni De Winter is just not a man that you can trust to anchor the rearguard to a high level most weeks. He’s had some stellar performances – the game against Inter comes to mind – but he often follows up those performance with some shocking ones.

Matteo Gabbia is definitely better in terms of leadership and keeping things tidy, but we will need to wait and see if he features in Amorim’s long-term plans. He’s been injured for quite a while too, so a reacclimatisation period will be required.

Sankhoun Diawara also looked very shaky in Greece, but this in itself proves a point because the idea cannot have been for him to start as many games as he has. It just further signals that someone who can play down the middle and steady everything could be the difference this season and beyond.

Ardon Jashari got his first Milan goal at the weekend in the win over Frosinone. Then, the unfortunate injury to Luka Modric leading up to the Olympiacos game meant he was given the chance to prove himself, after a few shaky performances this season.

Excluding a few misplaces passes in the first half, the Swiss international was solid enough. There wasn’t anything flashy but also no errors leading to goal, which was a relief.  The stats say that he had 77 touches, completed 58/64 passes (91%), had 1 big chance created, 2 tackles, 7 recoveries and 1 clearance. He also won 2/2 aerial duels and 2 ground duels, committing just 1 foul.

Speaking of chances being seized, it’s worth giving a shoutout to Fikayo Tomori who came on for a struggling De Winter. Milan looked much more solid with the Englishman on the pitch as he showcased his athleticism but also his experienced reading of the game.

The professionalism shown by Tomori must be respected given the centre-back was excluded from the squad in the summer and put on the transfer market. He worked hard to prove that he deserves to stay and to play. Now, the next mission is to earn what currently seems like an unlikely renewal.

In the first half Milan were struggling with the high press and the limited space, so they had limited chances going forward. The second half was a completely different story especially with Yunus Musah and Pulisic coming on for Estupinan and Goncalo Ramos.

We have already mentioned Hutchinson ran the show, but Pulisic was also crucial as he scored an impressive volley which is hard to execute by any standard,  and he then goal the assist that made the final 4-2. He should’ve really scored another one, but we’ll give him a well-earned pass.

Moreira was also heavily in the mix and at times he was frustrating with his end product being lacking, though the direct nature of his pace and dribbling were a joy to watch again. Having a player who picks up the ball and says ‘Fine, I’ll do it’ is really refreshing. His defensive effort as a wing-back was not lacking either.

Cisse all in all had a quiet game yet found his name up in lights on the scoreboard for a 10th time this season with a tidy headed finish. The knack that he has for being in the right place at the right time is something that is so impressive in a 20-year-old. A pay rise could be on the way to fend off calls from England.

We have to give the coach his flowers too, don’t we. The first half wasn’t good but it was still better compared to some other European performances this season. The goal was very preventable, but the Rossoneri did well not to fold under the pressure Olympiacos were applying.

In the second half the coach made two changes which improved the team quite a bit, though one of them was forced as Ramos was injured. Amorim revealed after the game that he knew the Greek side couldn’t keep up their high press for 90 minutes, which would yield spaces to attack in the second half.

Shifting Hutchinson to the right was also the correct choice, as was taking off De Winter for Tomori, and also letting Cisse play as a centre-forward. Depending on how Ramos is, he might have to do the same in Cagliari.

Finally, it is worth mentioning the attitude was completely different as opposed to the game against Ferencvaros. The desire and belief that the game could be turned around offers some encouragement that this team cannot just get into the knockout stages, but can give anyone a game if they show up.
