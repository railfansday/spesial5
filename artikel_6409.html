Standing desks have become a go-to upgrade for anyone who works from home. Swapping your desk chair for a standing desk seems like an easy fix for a day spent slouched over a laptop, but standing still and upright for hours isn’t much healthier than sitting all day.

A 2024 study from the University of Sydney found that standing more didn’t lower the risk of heart disease, stroke, or heart failure, and it was linked to a higher risk of circulatory problems like varicose veins and deep vein thrombosis. To get the most out of your standing setup, you also need to sit for parts of the day.

If you work at a bar countertop or have a standing desk that can’t be lowered, a standard desk chair won’t work. The solution is an adjustable desk chair made for higher surfaces. It’s there when you need a break, and it’s easy to roll or slide out of the way when you’re ready to stand again.

To rest your legs and feet, you need a chair that reaches the right height, supports your feet when you’re seated, and gets out of the way when it’s time to stand.

Measure from the floor to the top of your desk at your usual standing height, then subtract 10 to 12 inches. That’s roughly the seat height you want, leaving room for your thighs under the desk. For a 42-inch desk, look for a seat that reaches 30 to 32 inches. Product listings usually give the seat height at its lowest and highest settings, and our picks top out anywhere from 29.5 inches to 35.8 inches.

At drafting height, your feet won’t reach the floor. Without something to rest them on, your legs dangle, and pressure builds up behind your thighs, which defeats the purpose of sitting down. Look for a foot ring or footrest, ideally one that adjusts, so your knees sit at a comfortable angle.

A backless stool keeps your core engaged and tucks under the desk when you aren't using it, so it's a good choice for small spaces. A chair with a backrest and lumbar support lets you fully rest, which matters more if you plan to sit for longer stretches. Armrests add comfort but can bump the edge of a high desk, so flip-up or removable arms are the most flexible.

Faux leather and vinyl are easy to wipe clean and usually cost less. Mesh breathes better if you run warm, and molded polypropylene or plastic seats are the lightest and easiest to move around. Check the weight capacity too. Our picks range from 250 to 400 pounds. And because you'll be moving the chair aside every time you stand, a lighter frame or smooth-rolling casters make the switch easier.
