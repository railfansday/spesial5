Guard dogs are watching over cattle in a Texas county with rare swift foxes and stopping all predator attacks

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Fewer than 250 swift foxes remain in Texas. Two dogs are now guarding cattle on the grassland to test whether they can keep coyotes away.

This summer, two Maremma guardian dogs named Duke and Dozer moved with a herd of cattle onto the Rita Blanca National Grasslands. The site lies in Dallam County, in the far northwest corner of the Texas Panhandle.

Their main job is to keep predators away from the cattle. Researchers at Texas A&M AgriLife want to know if the dogs can also protect the swift fox, a small native fox that coyotes often kill.

Fewer than 250 swift foxes are thought to live in Texas, according to Texas A&M AgriLife. The three-year project has no fox results yet. So far, though, the rancher taking part has not lost a calf to predators.

The swift fox (Vulpes velox) is about the size of a house cat. It weighs 4 to 7 pounds (1.8 to 3.2 kilograms), according to Texas Parks and Wildlife magazine, which makes it the smallest fox in the state.

The species once ranged from the Llano Estacado up into Alberta, Canada. It now holds about 40 percent of that range, the magazine reported in May 2026.

Dallam County is the only county in Texas currently known to have swift foxes. Dana Karelus is the state mammal specialist with the Texas Parks and Wildlife Department (TPWD). She said only 40 to 50 percent of the shortgrass prairie is left.

Bison herds and fires once kept that grass short. On a Kansas prairie, bison roughly doubled native plant diversity over 29 years.

In the Panhandle the grass now grows taller, and Texas Tech ecologist Clint Boal told the magazine that coyotes ambush foxes more easily once it passes 12 inches (30 centimeters).

Haley Sloan is a doctoral student in the Texas A&M Department of Rangeland, Wildlife and Fisheries Management. She said coyotes are a leading cause of death for swift foxes, using the taller plants as cover.

John Tomeček, an associate professor in the same department, co-leads the wider swift fox work with Texas Tech researchers. TPWD pays for it.

Tomeček told the magazine that Panhandle coyotes appear to kill swift foxes but rarely eat them, and that nobody knows why.

Wolves keep coyotes out of their ground in natural systems. In eastern North America, coyotes have adapted to landscapes without wolves.

Tomeček called a guardian dog “a wolf that works for you.” Swift foxes eat insects, plants and small rodents, so they pose no threat to livestock.

Gray foxes in North Carolina have a different way out. There, gray foxes use tree cover to live alongside coyotes because they can climb. Swift foxes have no trees on the prairie. They need short, open grass so they can see predators coming.

Guardian dogs are usually raised with sheep and goats. Bill Costanzo, the AgriLife Extension guardian dog specialist in San Angelo, had to change the training so the puppies stayed safe around much larger cattle.

The program’s monthly blog, The Guardian Way, followed the pups, which it describes as Maremma and all from one litter, through the winter in San Angelo.

According to the January post, in November 2025, Duke and Dozer went to visit adult dogs in the next pasture. Then they found a fallen gate and wandered over to pastures at Angelo State University.

The pair had first bonded with calves from Sul Ross State University. Early in 2026, staff traded those calves a few at a time for grown cows. Duke and Dozer’s cattle took longer to accept the new cows than the herd of their littermates, Delta and Diesel.

The handoff in early May went badly at first. The pups dug out of their pen on the day they reached the cooperating rancher. GPS trackers found them two days later on a neighboring ranch, according to the May 2026 post.

The rancher kept them penned until they got used to him, then let them out with the cattle again. By June, one dog stayed beside the herd each day while the other patrolled around the pens. In July they were due to move with the cattle to the Rita Blanca lease.

Costanzo said the team now has “a set of procedures to help guide the bonding process with guardian dogs and cattle.” He hopes to use guardian dogs with cattle on a larger scale.

Before the dogs arrived, Sloan and her colleagues put GPS collars on swift foxes and coyotes. The dogs wear GPS collars as well.

Those first tracks show how both species moved before the dogs came. Sloan’s idea is that the dogs stand in for a larger predator. Coyotes would then avoid the herd, and foxes could start using the ground they leave.

Over two years, the field team caught foxes, measured them and fitted the collars, the TPWD magazine reported. They took ear punches for genetic work. They also tested the foxes for parvo and canine distemper.

The wider project is testing prescribed fire, grazing patterns and artificial dens made from upside-down buckets. Game cameras watch the dens. A separate survey asks people in swift fox country what they think of the animal.

Guardian dogs are not trained to kill predators, according to a 2023 AgriLife report. They patrol, disrupt predators and chase them off. Killing coyotes has limits of its own, since coyote numbers bounce back quickly after removal efforts.

Sloan said success would help twice, with better odds for the foxes and fewer lost calves in calving season.

“I’m happy to report right now the dogs are doing great,” Sloan said. She added that the producer “hasn’t lost any calves to predation.”

AgriLife says it is too early to report findings. The dogs have spent only a few months on the lease. The release gives no numbers on fox survival, coyote movements or how many animals wear collars.

The test also rests on one herd and two dogs. The other two pups from the litter, Diesel and Delta, went to a ranch in Childress in April. The blog said no data would be collected on them because there was no funding.

Whether coyotes move away from the herd, and whether foxes move in, will be measured over the three years of the project.

For now, the two dogs that dug out of their first pen in May are staying with the cattle on the Rita Blanca, in the only Texas county known to have swift foxes.

The information was obtained from Texas A&M AgriLife Today.
