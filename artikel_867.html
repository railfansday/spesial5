Arsenal's struggles explained - and why they should have signed Putellas

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Digital journalist Laura Hunter returns for a second season with a WSL-dedicated column, analysing all the big talking points from across the weekend's action; this week's focus is on Arsenal's woeful form and why problems are likely to persist

Sky Sports columnist Laura Hunter analyses the big talking points from the sixth round of Women's Super League matches, bringing you closer to the key stories at the heart of the women's game.

Before the weekend, there had never existed a time where Arsenal, Chelsea and Manchester City all failed to win on the same matchday. A turning point, perhaps? The rest of the division will be quick to smell the whiff of opportunity.

Because while Arsenal languish down in sixth and both Chelsea and Man City prove that others besides each other can rid them of points, the Women's Super League appears increasingly more open. This could be a race for the ages.

Of course ambitions come in all shapes and sizes. To measure against standards, there is nothing wrong with where the top two finds themselves, albeit patchy performance levels at the weekend led both managers to speak of better balance.

Andree Jeglertz went a step further. He described City's 3-1 defeat to Spurs as a "wake-up call", the first time his side have been beaten at home in the league under him. And their first taste of back-to-back defeats since January 2025.

But in an analysis led on the paucity of performance and standing, Arsenal must take the brunt. They have hit a new low this term and trouble is brewing. Is Renee Slegers' time up?

For a side that won the Champions League just 18 months ago, Arsenal bare few hallmarks of elite. They are grossly underachieving against the calibre of talent within the squad and Slegers' course of correction, so far, has proved futile.

The Gunners have tumbled down the league and are well out of the running for a European spot. But if just qualifying for the Champions League again is all Arsenal's season has been reduced to, one has got to question exactly how far they have fallen and who is responsible for such a rapid decline.

Arsenal no longer have the authority on which their reputation was built. Opposition teams are not afraid to impose themselves at the Emirates, rather, they are invited to. Arsenal rank 11th in the home form table having scored as many goals as they have conceded (4).

On Sunday, frustration peaked. Arsenal were booed off by sections of their own support after being held to a sixth successive home draw, this time to West Ham. Fans have had enough of the impasse. And while Slegers has identified the issue - "We don't inspire enough. We don't bring enough energy" - she is yet to propose a solution.

Territorially, Arsenal are sitting deeper, with less urgency to commit numbers in forward areas. Their central players are having far less influence in possession and therefore connecting the thirds with passes that cut opposition teams open is proving difficult. The injury to Mariona Caldentey is consequential in that sense.

Of course Arsenal still have creators. Their build-up phase remains tidy - but that's part of the problem. Where are the risk takers? Who is the player that sees the picture before opponents can react? Arsenal's patterns are slow and safe, rooted in keeping the ball, not risking it.

And yet, collectively, the Londoners have completed more final third passes than any other side and generated the second-most touches in the opposition box. Why, then, are they only just averaging a goal a game? That tally is fewer than newly-promoted Crystal Palace and Birmingham.

Another problem is that their only reliable source of goals is Alessia Russo. She is carrying an ailing attack that is systematically failing. At the weekend their conversion rate at the Emirates plummeted to just 2.8 per cent. Four goals scored from 141 shots taken is an alarmingly poor return and places Slegers in a difficult position.

It's clear her side are tactically stale but individuals are also failing to live up to the Arsenal name. That combination is hard to hide from. Goals have dried up. Wins are scarcer still. And Arsenal's once vibrant fanbase - the envy of the league - has turned sour.

Attendances have dropped off majorly as a result and will continue to dwindle until something happens to break this doom cycle. Slegers cannot afford for it to get much worse.

One might journey back to the summer for the root cause of Arsenal's rot. They were active in the transfer window, doing some solid business to bolster depth, but one opportunity in particular was causing a major stir. Did Arsenal give Alexia Putellas enough consideration?

Supposedly the club's hierarchy explored the option of pursuing a deal for Putellas over the summer but nothing got off the ground. Europe's marquee signing joined London City instead. You need only look at their elevated position to understand her impact.

The Spaniard has upgraded Eder Maestre's outfit beyond recognition. She offers a level of control and creativity in games Arsenal can only dream of. They have a higher competitive ceiling because of it. And more obviously they have newly acquired status and presence; Arsenal's replacement among the league's elite.

Perhaps signing Putellas was never realistic given Slegers already had Caldentey, and her squad is governed by a stricter wage structure than London City operate. There are no guarantees La Reina would be interested in such a move anyway. However, the fact remains Arsenal were not bold or brave enough in the summer.

That misstep has never been more apparent than now.
