Packaging Innovation for Good

The European Union has declared an ultimatum against unsustainability: all consumer packaging placed on the EU market must be designed for recycling by 2030.  This mandate was announced by the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) and formally became law in all 27 EU member states on Aug. 12, 2026.  Since then, the mandate has placed restrictions on synthetic “forever chemicals” (PFAS) in food packaging as part of the “Design for Recycling” (DfR) framework, which will become the baseline by 2030.  It is significantly reshaping how companies design, import and sell products globally.

All packaged products must meet these regulatory changes, not just those manufactured in the EU.  Because many brands prefer to standardize packaging across different nations’ markets, it is probable that PPWR’s influence will reach well beyond Europe as brands change their designs to streamline operations.

Under PPWR, materials will be graded based on their recyclability performance rates through the DfR framework.  Grade A materials are __≥95% recyclable__and meet the highest circular life cycle standards.  Grade B materials are ≥80% recyclable and are still considered to be highly efficient.  Materials graded as C are ≥70% recyclable.  Any material rated below 70% is deemed unrecyclable and will be banned from all markets.

The new rules imply that paperboard is one of the best materials for long-term reuse, reducing and recycling because it combines a high recyclability rate and excellent compatibility with set content targets.  This paper-based material can be recycled 5-10 times into innovative packaging for consumer retailers and their customers.  After it is responsibly disposed of, the cycle starts again for this valuable material.  European waste systems reportedly recover 87% of paper and paperboard packaging for reuse.  This significantly outperforms plastic, which has just a 42% recovery rate.  Other fiber-based materials help companies adjust to the tightening restrictions and mandates on single-use plastic packaging while simultaneously supporting enduring goals made for resource efficiency and overall recyclability.

The first rising concern about single-use plastics began in the 2010s.  By 2022, the European Commission proposed PPWR to replace the outdated Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive (PPWD) of 1994.  By February 2025, PPWR went into full effect.  In the coming years, the development of secondary legislation will start.  By 2028, mandatory standardized labels will appear on consumer packaging and on all waste sorting receptacles. Later on in the early 2030s, all packaging in the EU, whether locally produced or imported, will officially meet DfR criteria.  Most plastic packaging and plastic parts will meet initial minimum recycled content requirements.  By the end of the decade, all packaging must meet even stricter criteria for recyclability to ensure an even more sustainable world.

The PPWR intends to set a binding packaging waste reduction target of 15% by 2040.  Brands that adapt to the DfR mandates from the start by adopting paperboard material and designing for a circular material lifecycle will be better prepared for decades to come.  Additionally, they will also gain an advantage in a competitive market where environmental sustainability is rapidly becoming the criterion.
