Denmark ratifies security deal with US and Greenland

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The deal provides the US with expanded military access, on top of what it already had based on a previous agreement. It comes after threats by US President Trump to take Greenland with force.

Denmark's parliament on Thursday ratified a security agreement with the US over Greenland, hoping to end tensions after US President Donald Trump had repeatedly demanded control over the autonomous Danish territory.

In the Danish 179-seat parliament, all 109 members present voted in favor of the text. Greenland's parliament already ratified the deal in early November.

US ambassador to Denmark, Kenneth Howery, welcomed the ratification, saying in a post on X: "The agreement is a tremendous accomplishment, one that modernizes our partnership for the security challenges of the future and bolsters the security of the United States, the Kingdom of Denmark, including Greenland, and all of NATO."

The trilateral security and military access agreement between Washington, Nuuk and Copenhagen allows for expanded US military presence on the mineral-rich and strategically located island in the northern Atlantic.

It also includes permission to establish a "Golden ‌Dome" ​missile defense system, while reaffirming Danish sovereignty and Greenlanders' right to self-determination.

The island is home to some 57,000 people and is strategically located along the shortest route from Europe to North America. It already is home to the northernmost US military base.

The deal was signed in September by Donald Trump, Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen and Greenlandic Prime Minister Jens-Frederik Nielsen but still needed ratification from lawmakers in Greenland and Denmark.

In January 2026, Trump renewed calls for Greenland to become part of the US, threatening military action and tariffs against European allies if they opposed him.

He argued that Denmark was not able to protect the autonomous Arctic territory from threats by China and Russia. His demands strained US relations with Denmark and alarmed European NATO allies.

Tensions somewhat eased in late January when Trump and NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte announced a "concept of a deal" that would expand US military access to Greenland. This then led to the agreement being signed in September.

Under a 1951 agreement, the US already has broad military access to Greenland. Denmark had repeatedly said it was open to expanding the US military presence on the island but rejected Trump's demand for full US control.
