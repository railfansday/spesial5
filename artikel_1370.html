Lionesses take step closer to World Cup but do not look like they can win it

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

England may have edged a step closer to booking their place at next summer’s Women’s World Cup but they do not yet look like a team who can win it.

This two-legged qualifying play-off with Greece was always going to be somewhat academic. Greece are ranked 58th in the world and have never qualified for a major tournament. But they could – and should – have taken the lead before England eventually broke the deadlock through Georgia Stanway just before half-time.

The goals flowed more freely in the second half, but Greece deservedly found the net themselves and are not yet out of the tie. There is every chance of the Lionesses winning the second leg in Leicester on Tuesday more comfortably, but not being able to put teams to the sword is exactly why they ended up in the play-offs in the first place.

The best teams are ruthless and unforgiving – and England did not display those traits here. They snatched at chances, and several players were guilty of firing wide. There were also defensive problems. Greece got in behind England’s back line too often and the goal they scored was far too easy for them.

“I think we made it [the game] a bit harder,” England manager Sarina Wiegman said. “Greece were very well prepared. They were very well organised and well disciplined. Of course I’m happy with the 3-1, I’m not happy with the one.

“We could have scored more goals, we should have. The final stage, the final pass needs to be better. I think we were a little bit in moments really good on the ball and in other moments not so good, and we could have made better decisions too. Lots of things to take from today.

“Sometimes we played really well, sometimes we were a bit sloppy, so sometimes we were a bit frustrated and I think that’s what you saw.”

Avoid defeat in the second leg and England will play the winners of Ukraine v Slovakia. Ukraine, whom the Lionesses beat 6-1 and 3-0 earlier in their campaign, have a 3-1 lead from their first leg. Perhaps the pedestrian nature of this game was partly because it felt inevitable England would win. They did enough to get over the line but still lacked a clinical edge.

It had been four months since they were thrashed 4-0 by Spain, a defeat which relegated them to the play-offs instead of automatic qualification. They had won every other game in their group before that capitulation, but with Spain topping the group on goal difference, England only had themselves to blame for not scoring more goals in their other fixtures. Breaking down ultra-defensive teams has long been an issue for this Wiegman side and they knew that was what they would be facing in this double-header against Greece.

It did not help that many of Wiegman’s players have been struggling for form in the Women’s Super League this season. Wiegman was also without the ever-reliable Lucy Bronze, who dropped out of the squad on Thursday because of injury. That meant Maya Le Tissier was handed a start at right-back while Laura Blindkilde Brown was preferred to Jess Park in the No 10 position.

Greece were far more open than many would have expected, with several gaps in their defence for England to exploit. They also created problems for themselves by insisting on playing out from the back.

England thought they had taken the lead in style when goalkeeper Fafailia Petaloti gave the ball away needlessly. Lauren James took advantage of Petaloti being off her line and produced a tremendous lob from 25 yards out. But the forward was offside when she picked up the ball and the goal was ruled out by VAR.

The excellent Kathryn Batte has run the rule over potential alternatives to Lucy Bronze.

The 35-year-old right-back is out injured, but Ella Toone was called up in her place, rather than a direct replacement.

“Bronze, who is two appearances away from reaching 150 caps for her country, has picked up an injury and returned to Chelsea for treatment,” Batte writes.

“The Lionesses will be missing one of their most reliable players for the matches on Friday and Tuesday, but it gives Sarina Wiegman an opportunity to look at potential replacements for the future.

“Bronze, who has been an integral member of the team throughout Wiegman’s reign, turns 35 later this month. While she will be expected to be the first-choice right-back should England qualify for next year’s World Cup in Brazil, the lack of a recognised back-up has been an ongoing problem.”

Sarina Wiegman revealed that while dropping Toone was a difficult decision, the midfielder’s reaction left a lasting impression on the coaching staff.

“When I gave her the call, of course that was a hard call, but she took that straight away,” Wiegman said. “Of course she was very disappointed, but the first thing she did was say: ‘OK, I’m going to get back, I’m going to do that.’”Wiegman was particularly pleased with how Toone handled herself both on the pitch and in front of the media before her late recall.

“That answer in the game, also the way she spoke about it, I thought that was really good,” the head coach added. “And then getting everything ready, I think that really impressed me. Well done, I would say.”

Welcome to Telegraph Sport’s live coverage of Greece vs England in the first leg of the 2027 Women’s World Cup play-offs.

The Lionesses kick off the two-legged tie tonight looking to secure a solid advantage ahead of Tuesday’s return tie at Leicester’s King Power Stadium. Although they finished level on points with group winners Spain, England’s goal difference sent them into the play-off path, setting up a first-ever senior meeting against a Greek side ranked 55 places below them in the FIFA rankings.

All eyes are on Sarina Wiegman’s selection choices following a turbulent week of squad news. The manager originally raised eyebrows last month by dropping Ella Toone and Chloe Kelly, stating bluntly that “the levels weren’t what I expect from them on the pitch.”

Toone quickly accepted the setback. Speaking after starting in Manchester United’s victory over Liverpool, the midfielder insisted: “It’s about me now. I’ve got to keep working hard and use it as fuel to come back even better.”

That opportunity arrived sooner than expected. An injury sustained by right-back Lucy Bronze - described by Wiegman as “really small” - forced the defender to return to Chelsea for assessment. Wiegman immediately turned to Toone, who flew directly to Greece from Spain, where she had been running her own self-directed training block during the international window.

With 69 caps and 24 international goals to her name - including crucial strikes in the Euro 2022 final and the 2023 World Cup semi-final - Toone brings proven big-match experience back into the setup at a critical moment.
