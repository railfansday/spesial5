‘Actively Makes The Game Worse’: Fans Are Furious With Pikmin 4′s Paid Nintendo Switch 2 ‘Upgrade’

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Last Thursday, Pikmin 4 finally received its Nintendo Switch 2 Edition upgrade, promised to add improved visuals, voice commands for the adorable Oatchi, and a new set of bonus levels in the added “Dandori Academy.” But rather than serve as an incentive for Pikmin lovers to return to the game over the weekend, it looks like it’s mostly just pissed off people who paid the $10 for what they say barely qualifies as an upgrade.

At the time I’m writing this, Pikmin 4 Nintendo Switch 2 Edition + Dandori Academy (to use its full, Christian name) has a 2.1 user score on Metacritic out of 265 ratings. While 265 isn’t a huge amount of user reviews, that is a remarkably low score for a game that has a base version sitting comfortably at 8.9. And the comments are full of the same, repeated complaints: framerates are still at 30 FPS, loading screens now take longer than they did in the original, and the new content is mostly a bunch of reused maps from the base game that can only be played at certain times.

“charging customers for almost no content nor technical enhancements (in fact, load times are even longer on NSW2 – itâs a disgrace!)” reads a 0/10 review from laurack.

“This is essentially a paid downgrade and calling this is a ‘lazy Switch 2 Edition’ or a ‘waste of money’ is an understatement for what is supposed to be an upgrade,” writes PrestonandTom in a 1/10 review. “This ‘upgrade’ of Pikmin 4 somehow manages to make the game run significantly worse with standard load times going from 6 seconds to 30+ seconds. The framerate went from an consistent 30 FPS to an inconsistent 30 FPS. Even despite all these problems, the new content isn’t that fun or engaging for the player by offering weak rewards for getting good dandori scores. The resolution is higher, but the tradeoff is not worth it and neither is the ‘new’ dandori academy content which mostly consists of old dandori levels that are already available in the base game.”

“While Pikmin 4 remains the undisputed peak of the franchise, this ‘Switch 2 Edition’ is a tough sell for returning veterans,” writes marcoskalil in a 7/10 review. “The bump in resolution and draw distance is welcome, but it ultimately highlights older textures that haven’t been properly upscaled for the new hardware. The bonus mode offers genuinely fun, meaningful content for long-time fans, yet it falls short of justifying the upgrade price. If you missed the original launch, this is undeniably the definitive way to play. However, if you have already conquered PNF-404 on the base Switch and is looking for more quality Olimar adventures, keep your Pikmin planted and hold out for Pikmin 5.”

“Very disaster edition game scam man!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!” wrote Lolitroppotsund in a succinct 0/10 review.

Now, not everyone is convinced the upgrade is horrible. Over on the Pikmin subreddit, longtime franchise fans are rolling their eyes a bit at the metascores, suggesting they’re “overdramatic” and that the release is in line with some of Nintendo’s other Switch 2 Edition upgrades. That said, not an awful lot of people are actively popping up in strong defense of the new edition, instead mostly just insisting it’s not as bad as the score suggest.

is it really that bad? byu/Papanataaa inPikmin

I’ve been holding off on playing Pikmin 4 for this update, but now I’m left questioning whether or not I ought to just save myself some money and buy the regular edition. The fans are right that it’s not the end of the world, and admittedly Nintendo didn’t promise much. But it is still a paid upgrade that, in at least one respect, makes the original game worse, and doesn’t seem to add much meaningful to balance that out. Pick it up at your own risk.
