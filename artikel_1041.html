What's still to play for in 2026 as F1 hits Vegas?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Both world championship battles may already be settled for this year, but other key questions are still be answered in the three rounds that remain; watch the Las Vegas Grand Prix weekend on Sky Sports F1, with qualifying at 4am on Saturday and the race at 4am on Sunday

The 2026 Formula 1 season has three races left to go with the Las Vegas Grand Prix opening the final triple header of the year this weekend.

For the first time since the final five rounds of 2023, both world championship battles are settled before the end of the season after Mercedes wrapped up the Constructors' Championship last time out in Brazil to go with Kimi Antonelli's winning of the drivers' crown the week before in Mexico.

But that doesn't mean there aren't other important battles to be won, and key questions to be answered, elsewhere through the field before the chequered flag falls on this season in Abu Dhabi on December 6...

While the battle to be world champion is the only one that truly matters for drivers each year, there is personal and competitive pride at stake in finishing as high up as possible in the standings, even if the drivers may not always talk this fact up.

While the new world champion holds a mighty and unassailable lead of 111 points, his rivals behind are more tightly packed together in the battle for the best-of-the-rest positions.

Just 18 points covers the drivers in second, third and fourth places in the championship, while second through to sixth are split by 33 points.

In a position that had looked highly unlikely at mid-season, it is Max Verstappen (279 points) who holds second place heading into the final three rounds thanks to a run of eight consecutive podium finishes that has included three race wins.

That kind of form will make the Dutchman hard to dislodge from what would be a sixth successive top-two championship finish stretching back to 2021.

But George Russell (262 points) and Lewis Hamilton (261 points) are close enough to still harbour hopes of each finishing second themselves.

For Russell, regaining the second place he has held for much of the year would at least provide a positive note to end a difficult season in team-mate Antonelli's wake, while Hamilton would be able to point to a four-place improvement on his 2025 campaign as showing firm signs of progress for both himself and Ferrari heading towards 2027.

One of Verstappen, Russell and Hamilton is most likely to finish second as it stands, but neither fifth-placed Charles Leclerc (249 points) nor sixth-placed Lando Norris (246 points), the winner of the last race in Brazil, can yet be definitely counted out of that battle with a maximum of 75 points left up for grabs.

In all this is also the sub-plot of which Ferrari driver finishes higher in the standings. Hamilton has been their lead championship runner since the Canadian Grand Prix in May, but Leclerc - who was comfortably the team's top scorer in 2025 - cut the gap to his team-mate to 12 points in Brazil.

While trophies are also on offer for the drivers who finish second and third in the standings, which will be presented at the FIA Awards in Shanghai in December, the battle for places between the 11 teams in the Constructors' Championship offers a more substantial prize.

It is the finishing order in the standings which determines teams' prize money. That means that every extra position gained, or place retained, equates directly to a team's money earned at the end of the season.

Ferrari are not yet officially secure in second but, with a buffer over third place of 95 points, that position could be confirmed in Las Vegas.

But the battle for third between Red Bull and McLaren will run potentially all the way to the chequered flag in Abu Dhabi.

At one point in late summer, outgoing champions McLaren had been looking likely to make a fist at chasing down Ferrari for second, but a troubled triple header across Azerbaijan, Malaysia and Singapore dropped them into the clutches of a resurgent Red Bull, who then overtook them for third at the United States Grand Prix.

They both scored 33 points last time out in Brazil and are separated by 17 points.

Elsewhere, and while not officially secure, Racing Bulls are on course for a best-ever finish of fifth with Red Bull's sister team holding a 45-point buffer over Alpine.

Alpine will almost certainly finish sixth, but seventh place is still up for grabs with just three points separating Haas and Audi.

Williams, Aston Martin and Cadillac all looked poised to stay in their current respective positions of ninth, 10th and 11th barring some unexpected big end-of-season results.

While 'momentum' certainly isn't as easy to measure as world championship placings, it's something all teams will be hoping to feel they carry into this winter off the back of the season's final three races.

That's particularly important this year as, unlike last winter, the rule changes afoot for 2027 are not revolutionary. There are changes to the both aerodynamic and engine regulations, which will see the power split shift to 58-42 in favour of the internal combustion engine, but they are not as substantial as the complete reset we saw at the start of 2026.

Late-season performance in 2026 has more chance of being maintained into 2027, while upgrades brought by teams across the final months of this season will have been with a view to development direction for next year too.

The fact that the season is finishing with a battle at the front in which the competitive order from race to race between Mercedes, Ferrari, Red Bull and McLaren is not predictable, certainly bodes well for next year.

Indeed, fresh from securing the team's first constructors' championship in five years, Mercedes boss Toto Wolff cautioned: "It looks like the most maybe competitive era that is about to start because the teams have become so professional in capability and resource that these kind of runaway seasons, I don't see them anymore."

Ferrari boss Frederic Vasseur sees it a similar way, saying of the competitive end to 2026: "I think it's good for the championship, good for the show.

"Probably it will be true next year and the following years with the convergence of performance."

New champions Mercedes have only won one of the last five races since introducing an aerodynamic upgrade at the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia which they had hoped would reassert their dominance from earlier in the season. It hasn't. The Silver Arrows are now set to introduce their long-awaited ADUO-allowed turbo upgrade in Vegas, although have played down the likely performance gain it will deliver.

Wolff added: "It's four teams that can win races and will be challenging for a world championship.

"And I have no doubt that it's going to be very difficult for us next year. So, for me, the 2027 season has started now."
