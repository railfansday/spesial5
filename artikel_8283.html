My Biggest Backpacking Gear Hits and Misses of 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

It’s time to make one of my favorite videos of the year, which is my roundup of all of my favorite and least favorite gear from the 2026 hiking season.

And this year, I put in hundreds of miles. I hiked the JMT, I hiked a large portion of the San Diego Trans County Trail, I did a large section of the Colorado Trail, so I definitely had the chance to put in plenty of miles, plenty of field time with these pieces of gear, and I’m excited to tell you guys what I loved and what I really did not like this season.

Everything from tents to backpacks to new apparel, new fleeces, new mid-layers, new base layers, new electronics, new sunglasses, all sorts of new stuff. And I have a lot of thoughts, some of them great, some of them not so great, but I’m excited to hear what you guys think, so let’s get into it.

The first piece of gear that I’m really excited to talk to you guys about today is what I’m currently wearing, and that is my Alpha 60 from SamBob.

Now, I have had experience with Alpha Direct layers in the past, but this is SamBob’s lightest Alpha Direct model, the Alpha 60. They also offer an Alpha 90, an Alpha 120.

But specifically when I picked up this layer, I was looking for a mid-season light mid-layer fleece that I wouldn’t overheat in, that I could still hike in, that was breathable, that was quick to dry, that was lightweight, that was versatile, that was comfortable enough that I could wear it during the day, at night when I went to bed, as a PJ layer, all of the above.

So ultimately, I was really happy with this piece of gear.

Alpha Direct is not the kind of thing that I tend to turn my nose at. I know that it is a controversial piece of gear just because it is a bit more on the premium side. It is not a cheap fleece by any means.

But for me, it is definitely light enough that it is worth the extra money for me. And this is actually the third piece of SamBob gear that I own. I am a SamBob diehard. I think they make great stuff.

And all of the normal pain points that you tend to see with Alpha Direct, where folks are complaining about the durability and the longevity, I have yet to have those issues.

Again, I have not brought an Alpha Direct layer on a full thru-hike yet. I’ve brought some Alpha Direct blends, and I’ll talk about that in a minute.

But largely, I’m not seeing any wear and tear, I’m not seeing any holes, it’s not overly stinky, and I think it’s fashionable enough that I can wear it in day-to-day life.

So I love this piece of gear. I take it on trail, I take it off trail.

So yeah, that’s all to say I am a huge fan of this Alpha Direct 60 from SamBob. It has not let me down. It is breathable, it is light, it is ultralight, it’s great.

Huge fan. It will continue to come out with me in the field on future trips.

Now let’s talk about a piece of technology that I am not crazy about.

I’ve complained about this piece of gear before, but it’s gonna get me complaining about it two years in a row because it once again failed me this year.

This is the Nitecore 10,000 mAh battery pack charger.

Now, there’s some obvious upsides to this piece of gear.

If you are the kind of person who is not vlogging and you just need your phone to navigate and take photos every now and then, this is probably a perfectly reasonable option for you.

But for me, I have had this battery pack not charge or lose a charge oddly fast several times on me, to the point where it put me in dangerous situations where I realized, oh my God, my battery didn’t charge last night, I’m out here for two more days, how am I going to use FarOut?

And then I would have to massively ration my battery power, which as somebody who vlogs for work is super inconvenient and kind of scary at times, especially as an often solo female hiker.

So the Nitecore has failed me enough times now that I’ve kind of become an Anker convert.

This right here is an Anker battery. They offer them in the 20,000s. It’s definitely a heavier option. This is their 10,000.

So yeah, when you compare them, you can see the obvious upsides of bringing the Nitecore.

But what I found is that when this one doesn’t charge or loses a charge oddly quickly, this one holds it.

So I think I’m reaching for ANKER more these days. But again, it’s quite possible that I’ve just had bad luck with Nitecore.

I know plenty of people that swear by them. I use their headlamp. I love Nitecore headlamps.

They make great stuff. But in this case, I’ve had this specific piece of equipment fail me enough times that I am no longer a Nitecore stan.

And if you don’t know what a stan is, it’s just a fan.

All right, this right here is the BearVault BV ONE.

This is a pretty flashy, pretty hot new item this year that a lot of folks were talking about. Very buzzy.

It is a convertible bear can that has the ability to both screw up to hold a maximum of four days’ food and to screw down to carry just one day’s food.

And this is really appealing because if you’re going to be carrying this super big, super bulky piece of gear, then you want to know that you can shrink it down into your backpack and it’ll fit comfortably, especially as those days go on.

There’s nothing worse than being out there for like four or five days. You barely have any food left in your bear can, but it’s still super heavy and still taking up so much room in your pack.

This piece of gear weighs just about two pounds. I really like how flexible it is. I can bring this out for a weekender.

I can bring this out for a longer trip. I don’t have to have, you know, every bear can in the BearVault lineup. I can just have this one for the most part and then maybe a BV500 for those much longer trips.

So yeah, shout out the BV ONE. Not really a lot to complain about beyond the fact that I’m a little dumb and sometimes I screw it the wrong way and then I’m like, ah, that’s annoying.

So seriously, not much to complain about.

It is important to note, though, that this piece of gear is not approved for every single area where a bear can is required.

While it is an approved piece of gear by the Interagency Grizzly Bear Committee as being a proven tool for keeping bears out of your food, it is not necessarily an approved piece of gear by areas like Kings Canyon and/or Sequoia National Park or Yosemite.

These areas have their own unique lists for what are approved bear food prevention methods, and this one is not on it yet.

So if you are planning to do something like the John Muir Trail, make sure you do your research and make sure that the bear can that you bring and carry with you the entire time is an approved bear can.

That is a totally hypothetical situation that I just presented to you.

Now let’s talk about a piece of gear that I both loved and hated this season.

This right here, the Mountain Hardwear Alakazam.

I wrote an entire review on the Alakazam right here on the The Trek — check it out.

I’m really excited to talk about this. This is a piece of gear that I used to love and now hate.

Now, if I was talking to me two years ago who carried a Katadyn for like 99% of the Appalachian Trail and I was saying, I hate this filter, she heard me saying that, she’d be like, girl, you’re crazy.

I brought this water filter out for four days on the Colorado Trail and by day two, it was completely clogged.

I ran some water through it that looked like it was likely some very silty water, but you know, nothing that a fresh water filter shouldn’t be able to tackle.

It was the primary water source in this area. Plenty of folks had used it.

But the major issue with this filter is that you can’t backflush it. So if it gets all clogged up, you’re kind of screwed. You can unscrew it and you can rinse it in the water and do your best to get all that gunk out of there.

But it sort of felt like the second I ran that silty water through, I was going to be dealing with the worst, most embarrassing water flow for the rest of the trip.

I was filtering water in front of my friend and he was like, yo, that is pitiful. That’s embarrassing.

So yeah, after that trip, I became a staunch Sawyer conversionist. I love a Sawyer now. I understand the appeal of having a backflushable water filter. It runs super fast. It’s like new every time I backflush it. I’m a huge fan.

Again, I think that the Katadyn BeFree served me well on the AT when I was not dealing with super silty water.

But out here, where you’re going to be getting some more glacial flows that you’re filtering from, it is super inconvenient to be out in the backcountry for two days and now your water filter is totally busted and the next two days that you’re on trail are miserable.

So I am no longer a fan of Katadyn, I am sorry to say. Shout out Sawyer.

Another piece of apparel that I’m really excited to talk about, and if you’ve already watched my other video where I talk about all the gear that I tested this year, then this isn’t a surprise to you.

But this right here is my Jolly Gear button down.

This was actually a collaboration with Sawyer.

I wore this for all 200 miles of my John Muir Trail thru-hike this summer, and this is by far one of my favorite pieces of gear that I tested this year.

And that’s really saying something because prior to trying it on for myself, I was a massive hater of the button down.

For me, I just never thought that it was a flattering layer just for my body type.

I understood why people liked them because you could button them down to get more ventilation if you needed it on a hot day, but for me, I just didn’t think that they were very flattering.

But ultimately, on the JMT, I was looking for a performance layer.

I wanted a piece of gear that would keep the sun off my arms, and this is a UPF of 30, which is great, so you get a little bit of sun protection.

I knew that mosquitoes were going to be a risk, so I really wanted something that had sleeves, but also sleeves that I could roll up no problem and still be comfortable.

I wanted a sun hood because I knew it was going to be super duper sunny out in the Sierra, and I wanted a ponytail hole because I like to wear my high pony, especially when I haven’t washed my hair in a few days, and I want to keep it off of my neck.

So yeah, there were a lot of reasons that I decided to test this piece of gear beyond the fact that I love both Jolly Gear and Sawyer as companies.

Now that is all to say that while I love this piece of gear and I think you’ll be hard-pressed to find someone who wears Jolly Gear button downs that isn’t just like a total fanatic for them, that I did have a couple of gripes.

And Jolly, if you’re watching this, this is probably a me problem and I’m sorry in advance, but this is a washed layer.

It is real dirty. It is real dirty. I washed this. I’ve washed this several times since I wore it on the JMT.

So I don’t know if it’s just this specific colorway or maybe I’m just filthier than the average hiker, but for me what I noticed is that this layer really held on to dirt. And it’s not like the stinkiest layer, but you know, it doesn’t smell the freshest.

So yeah, that’s all to say that chances are that if I were to run this through like a power washer or something or, I don’t know, use some sort of scrubbing apparatus to really get the dirt out of the sleeves, I could, but I don’t really feel a need to do that.

I mean, this is ultimately a hiking layer for me, so if it’s a little bit dirty, like who cares?

But for folks out there that are more concerned with looking crisp and clean and sharp when they’re out on the trail, and that might sound like an oxymoron, but I know there are some of you out there because I’ve seen you and you guys look fresh as hell every time I see you, then this ultimately might not be for you.

Or you might want to get a white Jolly Gear button down that you can bleach every time you get to the trail.

So that’s a really petty criticism, honestly, not a huge deal. This is an awesome piece of gear. I love, love, love, love my Jolly Gear. I already bought a new one when I was at PCT Trail Days. I cannot wait to bring that with me next season.

Huge fan of the Jolly Gears. Shout out Jolly Gear.

Another piece of gear. Maybe not a piece of gear, but I’m going to call this a piece of gear.

Another thing that I tried this year that I was so excited to have discovered was Blue Lizard sunscreen. And funny story, how I found this, it was on the JMT. Someone left it on the ground. So it must have fallen out of somebody’s pack. I could have sat there and waited to see if somebody came back for it. But as far as I knew, if I walked away from this, I would have been leaving trash on the ground. And I just so happened to be running out of sunscreen at that point in time. So in my opinion, I was left no choice but to pick this up.

So after I picked this up, as my hike continued and my sunburn started to go away and I was able to wear shorts again, I started putting on this Blue Lizard sunscreen and I did not burn again for the rest of the trail. This stuff is the real deal.

So if you have sensitive skin, you’re prone to burning, you don’t want to wear pants all day, you’re a big shorts-hiking girly like me, then I would consider Blue Lizard, especially if you’re worried about skin cancer, which if you are outside as much as everybody watching this is, you should probably be worried about skin cancer.

So yeah, protect your skin. This is a great option.

If you put it on your face, you’re going to look a little bit goofy. Who cares?

This is great sunscreen, high quality, sport, sweat-resistant, all that stuff. I’m not sponsored. I just am an evangelist for this stuff now.

Three more pieces of gear. Thanks for hanging in there.

And I promise you if you wait for the last one, you won’t be disappointed.

Now I would like to talk to you about the tent I used on the JMT, the Big Agnes String Ridge VST 1.5. Now this is Big Agnes’s very first trekking pole tent, which was particularly exciting and intriguing to me. So I was super stoked to bring this out onto the trails and give it a try.

Especially as somebody who hiked the AT in the Copper Spur. I loved my Copper Spur. I’m a huge fan of Big Agnes.

And some major upsides for this tent are that it has a huge vestibule, a huge mesh door, lots of ventilation. It is the condensation killer, which if you hike with a trekking pole tent, which is single-wall, you know how bad that condensation can be. Additionally, I love the colorway.

It is a well-constructed, seam-sealed tent. I was in this tent in an absolutely torrential downpour to the point where it was flooding under my tent. And I stayed dry as a bone. It was basically a swimming pool under my tent and I was completely dry.

It is well-constructed. I didn’t notice any sort of tears or anything. It is roomy. It is that 1.5 sizing.

So it would be very comfortable for one person and, say, a dog, but if you had to shove two people in there, you could.

A couple of complaints about this tent, and some of them I believe are universal, is with the trekking pole tents, as you probably know, it is a bit of a learning curve to get that perfect pitch.

So occasionally I would absolutely nail the pitch. It would be taut as steel and rain would bead right off it and I’d be so proud of myself and it’d be so comfortable.

And then occasionally I would have not so great a pitch. Maybe I was on granite and I wasn’t able to get my tent stakes into the ground effectively and I had to use rocks.

And occasionally I would put one trekking pole up on the front side and then that one would fall the second I got to the back to put up the second trekking pole. That was really annoying. So a little bit of a learning curve.

I think that with enough time anybody could master this tent.

So if you’re looking for a trekking pole tent and you’re a big fan of Big Agnes, you like the stuff that they make, you like that they’re based in Steamboat here in Colorado, I like that fact very much.

And say that maybe you’re not interested in the offset pitch of the Durston X-Mid or maybe you’ve tried the Zpacks, been there, done that, you’re looking for something new, I think that this is an awesome new option.

I’m excited to see more and more hikers using this tent. I like the String Ridge. It’s definitely going to come out with me on future hikes.

The other trekking pole tent that I use is the Gossamer Gear The One and I also love that tent, so I have a feeling that both of these will be making it into my rotation.

With the caveat being that I think that I would use this specific tent more if I was hiking more out east and it was easier to get non-rocky pitches.

That’s the Big Agnes String Ridge VST 1.5.

Shout out to NeoAir, Therm-a-Rest. Yeah, yeah, yeah. It’s dope.

All right, y’all. Last but not least, and the thing I am very, very excited to talk to you about today and overshare about, is the Backcountry Bidet. Now, this is a Backcountry Bidet. This is definitely new for me.

For context, I used to be the kind of person that would carry around 400 wet wipes and a billion toilet paper rolls and I would use them because I hated being dirty on trail.

And I would carry out like a pound of toilet paper and wet wipes and honestly, that was gross and way more unsanitary than if I had just used one of these.

You put it into a water bottle and you do your business and then you spray downwards on your behind and then you scrub, scrub, scrub with some sticks or rocks or your hand.

And then when you’re done with your business, you use soap and you wash the ever-living hell out of your hands to get them so, so, so, so clean and then you’re done. I am never going to go back after using the Backcountry Bidet.
