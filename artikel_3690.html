"It would end up appealing to no one": The Witcher 4 narrative leads aren't willing to strip away the series' "distinct edge" in the name of chasing a broader audience

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

In a recent interview with Korean outlet Inven, CD Projekt Red's narrative director Philipp Weber and vice president of story Marcin Blacha shared some words on The Witcher 4, focusing on their awareness that not only will it inevitably be different from The Witcher 3, but also that it needs to be in order to justify its existence. Simultaneously, there's a certain something to The Witcher as a series that they feel is difficult to make tangible, but is something they want to ensure they don't lose sight of.

Weber describes the core of a 'Witcher-like' story to be something that "explores the various facets of human nature — a game for adults", which he clarifies doesn't just mean violence or sex, but "treating the player as a mature adult, asking difficult questions without offering easy answers." While it has been quite a while since its predecessor, they still feel that the upcoming sequel must be true to the series it's part of and earn that numbered title: "If we were to strip away The Witcher's distinct edge just to reach a broader audience, it would end up appealing to no one."

Of course, the biggest change is that of the protagonist, from Geralt to Ciri. Blacha explains that "Ciri isn't calm and composed like Geralt, nor does she hide her emotions easily. She is far more hot-headed and tends to be emotional when making decisions." In terms of how this actually affects the narrative itself and the protagonist's place in it, he elaborates that "when playing as Geralt, players acted more like an arbiter judging how to resolve a situation. When playing as Ciri, you are much more deeply involved in the story through her personal perspective." It's an interesting notion, to state that the difference in role is that where Geralt was more of a fixer, Ciri actually approaches situations on an interpersonal level. Weber seemingly half-jokes that "we find ourselves in a position very similar to Ciri's. Just as Ciri must succeed Geralt, we must create a game that lives up to The Witcher 3."

As for how The Witcher 4 will play, moment to moment, Weber explains that some inspiration has been taken from Cyberpunk 2077 in regards to its open, player-led approach to customisation and, as a result, quests. "You could be a netrunner or a cyber ninja air-dashing into a scene with a double jump. We had to account for all those possibilities available to the player," he recalls. While I can't imagine Ciri will be air-dashing around Kovir, this statement does make me wonder how this approach can be applied to a game with such a specific existing protagonist, rather than the blank slate of Cyberpunk 2077's V. Recalling the side content of The Witcher 3 and its acclaim, he believes that people loved them "because we strived to offer something fresh" and "didn't create quests that merely waste the player's time", which is a principle that "remains unchanged today." Later, he condenses his feelings into the mantra that "A great quest can stem from a compelling story, an engaging character, or fantastic gameplay alone, but overall, there must be harmony."

Towards the end of the interview, Blacha jokes that "One of our greatest achievements is convincing people to finish their workday, buy our game, and then clock into another job as a character inside the game." It's quite a long and engaging read, so if you're invested in The Witcher IV and its narrative development, I do encourage you read the entire thing.
