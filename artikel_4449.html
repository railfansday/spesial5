Trump says he ‘protected’ Musk in first term after ‘a tough incident’ – what could it be?

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

President Trump says he “protected” Elon Musk during his first term, and that “you wouldn’t have Elon if it wasn’t for me.” He tied it to “a certain incident” that he called “a tough incident,” but he didn’t say what it was.

Musk had plenty of run-ins with federal regulators between 2017 and 2021. One of them could have ended his career as a public company CEO, and it’s our best guess.

Trump called in to CNBC’s Squawk on the Street on Monday. That same morning, Nvidia’s board voted to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO, and in its press release it said it had “received input from senior levels of the United States government” before concluding Huang posed no national security risk.

Trump argued that the US needs to “protect our geniuses.” Then he brought up Musk:

“You know, I protected Elon in my first term. Very big. Elon was protected. I talk about it, and I’m proud of Elon. I love Elon. I think he’s great. You know, 80% super genius, 20% he has other problems. But I tell him that. But Elon, I protected. You wouldn’t have Elon if it wasn’t for me. And, you know, a certain incident took place, which was, you know, a tough incident.”

It’s not the first time he has hinted at it. Last week, in a Fox News interview from Shenzhen, Trump said: “I helped Elon a lot in my first term. I helped him a lot. He was in big trouble.” Back in 2024, he told CNBC he had helped Musk “when I was president,” again without details.

On August 7, 2018, Musk tweeted that he was “considering taking Tesla private at $420. Funding secured.” It wasn’t. Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund had shown interest, but no deal or price had been agreed, and the take-private plan was dead within three weeks.

The SEC sued Musk for securities fraud on September 27, 2018. The agency didn’t only ask for a fine. It asked the court to bar Musk from serving as an officer or director of any public company. That would have meant no more Elon at Tesla, which lines up with “you wouldn’t have Elon if it wasn’t for me” better than anything else on this list.

And then it went away fast. Two days later, the SEC announced a settlement: Musk stayed CEO, gave up the chairman title for three years, and he and Tesla each paid $20 million, $40 million in total. Tesla added two independent directors and agreed to have a lawyer pre-approve Musk’s material tweets, but the SEC never fully enforced it. Musk didn’t admit or deny anything.

The SEC was run at the time by Jay Clayton, a Trump appointee.

There was a criminal side too. In September 2018, federal prosecutors in San Francisco opened a fraud investigation over the same tweet. It never led to charges, and the Justice Department never said publicly why.

The SEC came back in February 2019 and asked a judge to hold Musk in contempt after he tweeted Tesla production guidance without pre-approval. That one ended in April with an amended deal listing the topics he needed approval for, and no new penalty.

In May 2020, Musk reopened the Fremont factory in defiance of Alameda County’s COVID orders and tweeted: “If anyone is arrested, I ask that it only be me.” Trump tweeted the next day that California “should let Tesla & @elonmusk open the plant, NOW.” But the county was already working toward a reopening date of May 18, and a president doesn’t control county health orders.

There’s also the September 2018 Joe Rogan episode, where Musk smoked a joint on camera. NASA followed with a workplace safety review of SpaceX and Boeing, and SpaceX lives on federal contracts.

For context, Musk sat on Trump’s business advisory councils early in that first term, and quit in June 2017 when Trump pulled out of the Paris Agreement. That feels like a lifetime ago now that xAI runs its data centers on natural gas turbines.

Musk announced a take-private deal at a specific price that the other side never agreed to, and the stock jumped. It’s textbook securities fraud. The SEC asked to remove him from public company leadership, and two days later he got a $20 million fine, an insignificant amount of money for him, and lost a mostly insignificant title. For someone who’s now worth hundreds of billions, that’s a slap on the wrist.

The main remedy was supposed to be independent oversight. Tesla gave the chair to Robyn Denholm, who had been on the board since 2014, and the board has done what Musk wanted ever since: it defended his pay packages, approved new ones, and stayed quiet while the brand took damage. Denholm herself has sold tens of millions of dollars in TSLA along the way. The SEC got a governance fix on paper and nothing in practice.

Just imagine where Tesla would be if a product oriented CEO had taken over from Elon in early 2018. The company would have spent the last 6 years developing new models instead of betting the farm on autonomy. The Cybertruck almost certainly does not happen, at least not in its current form. Instead, they would have developed a more conventional pickup. We'd have gotten 2nd generation models of the X and S instead of them being canceled. Instead of a Cybercab, there would be a model 2 compact car people could buy. And there would likely be a Tesla van and a full sized SUV, possibly developed on the same underlying platform. Also, the SEC was trying to ban him from running public companies. At the time, SpaceX was still private. So, Elon could have still run SpaceX.

Trump likes to take credit, and he could be inflating his role here. But if the White House leaned on the SEC or the Justice Department in 2018 to help a CEO facing fraud allegations, that’s a serious problem, and someone should look into it.

The timing doesn’t help. He said it the same day he appears to have intervened to reinstate Huang as CEO of Nvidia.
