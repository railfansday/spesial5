Frog Closures Are the Unexpected Detail Defining Fall Jackets

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Every product on this page was chosen by a Harper's BAZAAR editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

It’s all in the details, from intricate knots to elaborate braiding

There are plenty of ways to make a statement with your outerwear, from an oversize silhouette to a high collar or bold color. But this fall, designers are turning their attention to a smaller detail: the fastening. Jackets featuring decorative knots, elaborate loops, and ornate toggles have emerged as one of the season’s biggest outerwear trends, offering an alternative to standard buttons and zippers.

In particular, frog fastenings are having a resurgence, bringing a fanciful finishing touch to everything from minimalist wool peacoats to tailored military-inspired jackets. Typically constructed from fabric or braided cord, the fasteners consist of a knotted button and a loop. Originally designed to secure garments, the closures have evolved into a more ornamental role with intricate shapes and floral-like designs.

While they may seem new for the season, frog-fastener jackets have a history that spans centuries. In Chinese dress, knotted closures known as pankou have long appeared on garments such as the qipao and tangzhuang. By the 18th and 19th centuries, frogging had also become a feature of European military uniforms, worn by cavalry regiments whose fitted jackets were adorned with braided cords.

Fashion has revisited the detail since then. Oscar de la Renta incorporated frog fastenings into his designs in the 1990s, while Valentino explored versions for its fall 2015 couture collection. More recently, for fall 2026, designers reinterpreted knotted closures: At Kallmeyer, black toggles punctuated a gilded jacquard jacket, while Kim Shui incorporated the detail into fur-trimmed blazers and opera jackets. Meanwhile, labels like Khaite and Ann Demeulemeester embraced military-inspired looks featuring ornamental braiding and embellished fronts.

Today’s store-rack versions range from collarless blazers with understated knots to dramatic outerwear designs featuring detailed cording. Brands like Róhe have introduced contemporary takes on frogging, while Hong Kong–based fashion label Sau Lee has reimagined traditional Tang-style jackets with modern silhouettes.

Rather than reinventing jackets entirely, designers are proving that something as minuscule as a fastening can make a familiar silhouette feel new again for the season. Ahead, shop our favorite takes on the look.
