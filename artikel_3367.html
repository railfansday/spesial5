Elizabeth Debicki Reveals What It Was Really Like Slapping Brad Pitt in ‘The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth’

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Like its predecessor, The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth is packed with talent, on and off-camera. With Quentin Tarantino penning the Once Upon a Time… in Hollywood sequel, but handing the directing reins over to David Fincher, there’s no doubt Brad Pitt’s return as the former eponymous stuntman is guaranteed to wow audiences when it hits theaters and IMAX before heading to Netflix next month.

In the movie, it’s been eight years since Booth went toe to toe with the Manson family. Now, he’s working as a bartender and a theater projectionist, but still putting his exceptional skills to work as a fixer for Hollywood’s elite. When he gets a call from Karen Crosby (Elizabeth Debicki), the talent agent of TV superstar Charlie Peters (Scott Caan), Booth is curious why a man known to perform his own stunts would need his aid. His instincts are spot-on, and Booth finds himself tangled up in a whole new mess, with a whole host of characters, played by Carla Gugino, Yahya Abdul-Mateen II, Timothy Olyphant, and more.

In this interview with Steve Weintraub, Debicki and Caan open up about their nerves leading up to production with filmmaking titans like Fincher and Tarantino. They also discuss Fincher’s genius direction, his precision over things like Caan’s montage, and Debicki shares what it was like filming the balcony scene where Karen slaps Cliff Booth. Don’t miss the full conversation in the video above, with a transcript provided below.

COLLIDER: You've both been in a number of projects, but I am curious, as an actor, when you land the Fincher role, and it's written by Q.T., how much are you in your head a little bit more in the days leading up to that first day of filming, thinking about everything and how you want to play the role, and the preparation that goes into it?

ELIZABETH DEBICKI: You know what? It's funny, I didn't think about this, obviously, while it was happening, but in retrospect, the really interesting thing about working with David is he is so wildly intelligent as a filmmaker and a human being that you don't think that much, and that's kind of his gift to you as an actor.

You have this impeccable script. It is a gift to you that you will soon get to dip into and figure out how to do it with these amazing actors, and then you have David, who already knows what he wants. He knows how he's going to get you there. And so actually, it's super freeing.

My memories of gearing up to do this, it's like, yes, we would be a little nervous every now and then if we had, like, a big monologue that day. But once you get one or two out of the way, it's like, then you're just having a good time. So, it's kind of a uniquely fun set, I think.

SCOTT CAAN: I agree. Once we're doing it, all that stuff went away. But certainly, it's not about the result. It's about, “I want to make them happy that they made the decision to cast me in this part,” you know? So, I definitely was nervous.

But I also think as actors, you know, I have a friend of mine who’s a fighter, and he said, “The one time I lost a fight and got beaten up really badly was when I wasn't nervous.” So I think it's good for us to have those feelings. I think if you go into something like, “I got this,” you're going to get knocked out. So, I definitely had those feelings, but I embrace those feelings, and I think that's part of what we do. And I think it's important to own your nervousness — and I definitely was… I don't think nervous is the right word, but anxious and wanted to do good, and wanted to make sure that they don't say, “We hired the wrong person.”

DEBICKI: Scott, they would never say that a million years.

One of the things that really makes the audience erupt is you getting to slap Brad Pitt, and I think one of the reasons it's so effective is the way David directs the scene. It's not just you outside of the balcony. There's a camera shot from inside, where Brad hits the glass. Can you talk about filming that? Scott, I loved the movie montage, if you will, about your career, and the way that you're in these different projects. Talk a little bit about filming that and giving all that backstory because it's all so effective.

DEBICKI: Well, the balcony scene is an extraordinary piece of writing. I think it's like an eight or 10-page scene, and it was always like this meal that we knew we were going to get to eat, like we were going to get there one day and shoot that scene. And it's unlike anything I've ever read or played, actually. The dynamic of these two people is so unique.

That second slap, I might be wrong, but my memory of this is we were set up inside shooting the scene that precedes it, and so we were already inside the apartment shooting the scene with Gus sleeping, when we wake him up, and I think that David being David and Erik [Messerschmidt] being Erik just sort of looked at it and went, “Oh, we’ve got to shoot it from inside because that's the cut that you want.” And so we did it.

I think we shot that first, and then we went outside the next day and started the scene. Something like that. But it's just genius. It's genius cinematography, and Brad [Pitt] is an incredible stuntman. He really is.

CAAN: I think with those things, and David, I've never worked with somebody that has ideas that are so specific. There are a couple of times in the movie I was doing a scene, and he would say, “This is my idea…” and I’d go, “Alright, I'm going to do it. I don't quite…” And then I see the movie, and I go, “Oh, now I see.” I've never worked for someone who has that many great ideas that when you see the movie, you go, “Oh, I see that one, I see that one, I see that one.”

Specifically, to answer your question about the documentary, the way that was done, as an actor, I just did what he told me to do. He was like, “Okay, for this, I'm going to need you to do this, and for this, I'm gonna need you to do this.” Like the scene with Timothy [Olyphant], obviously, and the scene in the parlor, those were acted scenes, but everything else was just in his brain. He and Quentin figured out what they wanted to do, and it was like, “Alright, hold the tennis racket. Flip your cigarette.” That was just me following what David wanted me to do.

DEBICKI: That scene with Timothy and you was so good. It's really one of my favorite parts of the film.

CAAN: I love that. That was fun. Timothy’s so good.

DEBICKI: It's so good. It's so funny and brilliant.

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth is now playing in IMAX for a limited time, before premiering on Netflix on December 23.
