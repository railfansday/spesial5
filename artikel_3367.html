‘The Rings of Power’ Made the Right Call Saying Goodbye to This Storyline in Season 3

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Editor's note: The below contains spoilers for The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power Season 3.

Truth be told, The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power has been pretty hit-or-miss over the past few years. Distinct departures from character and lore, poorly written dialogue, and odd pacing were struggles during the first season and carried over into the second as well. Fortunately, Season 3 has stuck more closely to J.R.R. Tolkien's works than ever, and part of that has to do with the consolidation and even eradication of certain storylines and characters. With four episodes premiering this week to kick off the third season, one thing has become abundantly clear: cutting out the Harfoots was absolutely the right move for the show.

We all love spending time in the Shire in The Lord of the Rings, and The Hobbit would not exist without Bilbo Baggins, but, without a doubt, the worst storyline in The Rings of Power's first two seasons was the traveling Harfoots. Nori (Markella Kavenagh) and Poppy (Megan Richards) were likable enough, but the whole thing felt like a discount Frodo and Sam story. While Hobbits are essential to the end of the Third Age of Middle-earth, their ancestors, the Harfoots, are nothing more than a blip in Tolkien's writings, and certainly not of grave importance in the Second Age. This is, in effect, why Bilbo, Frodo, Sam, and the others are so notable in The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings. For centuries, halflings were overlooked and undervalued, but Gandalf (played by Daniel Weyman here) sees something in them that Sauron (Charlie Vickers) would never expect.

The cast and creators break down the show's most personal storyline yet for Tolkien's most iconic wizard.

On The Rings of Power, the Harfoots always felt like an afterthought, like a good idea meant to echo what folks loved about The Lord of the Rings but without the traits that made them so endearing. In many respects, they felt out of place considering the events occurring around them, and unlike Tolkien's two seminal works where halflings were the main characters, the show had far too many threads to follow in order to do them justice. Any time they were on-screen, the entire tone of the program was altered — and not always in a fun, Tom Bombadil (Rory Kinnear) kind of way. Without them, The Rings of Power not only flows more smoothly, but also has the chance to finally expand on plotlines that were largely ignored or underdeveloped in previous seasons.

The truth is that The Lord of the Rings and The Hobbit will always be the best Hobbit stories. Part of that is because Tolkien invented Hobbits in the first place and knows them unlike any other, and part of that is that their most heroic stories were always meant to be about Frodo and Bilbo. Beyond that, Hobbit culture as we know it is more refined and cozy by the Third Age, which means that The Rings of Power never really had the chance to nostalgically take fans to the very place that most associate with the Hobbits. As a result, the Harfoots always felt shoehorned in rather than a natural part of the larger story the Prime Video series has been telling. It's one thing for The Rings of Power to create new characters to supplement the adventures of Second Age staples like Galadriel, Elendil (Lloyd Owen), and Durin IV (Owain Arthur), but it's another entirely to insert Gandalf and then pair him with halflings like a page out of The Fellowship of the Ring.

If you want more Hobbits, Harfoots, or other halflings (and don't forget the Stoors, too), the best thing you can do is read Tolkien's "Concerning Hobbits" chapter of The Lord of the Rings. The next best thing is to revisit the rest of the books or even the Peter Jackson films themselves. As for The Rings of Power, Season 3 has finally hit its stride with a more focused plot, more well-rounded characters, and every storyline building toward something meaningful as Sauron threatens to rule over Middle-earth.

New episodes of The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power Season 3 are available for streaming Wednesdays on Prime Video.
