garton group architecture lines tropical costa rican home casa black in stone and timber

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

set on costa rica's pacific coast, garton group architecture designs casa black, a linear residence defined by stone-lined corridors, continuous timber ceilings, and a pedestrian bridge.

Tom Garton edited by christina vergopoulou November 12, 2026 4 min

Sited in Playa Grande along Costa Rica’s Pacific coast, Casa Black is a private residence designed by Garton Group Architecture. Organized along an extended linear axis, the home mediates between communal gathering areas and secluded retreats, establishing continuous thresholds that connect domestic routines with the surrounding tropical landscape.

The spatial sequence begins at a shaded entryway that transitions into an elongated, stone-lined circulation corridor. Punctured by fenestration framing courtyard gardens and adjoining thresholds, the textured masonry wall registers changing patterns of natural light and material transitions. At the core of the plan, Garton Group Architecture Studio brings the living room, dining area, and kitchen together under an expansive, continuous timber ceiling. Custom millwork and integrated casework echo the warm wood grain overhead, while full-height sliding glass doors open the living volume to exterior terraces, a swimming pool, and gardens shielded by deep cantilevered roof overhangs.

The material palette combines board-formed concrete, native stone masonry, and finished timber, establishing a neutral architectural base articulated by the owners’ curated art collection, textiles, and glazed green ceramic bathroom tiles. Private quarters occupy distinct wings of the site; the primary suite is accessed via an exterior pedestrian bridge spanning a natural ravine, isolating the volume within the tree canopy. In the bathrooms, full-height glazing, integrated planters, and ceiling-suspended mirrors draw perimeter vegetation directly into the interior envelope.
