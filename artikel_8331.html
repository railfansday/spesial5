Private Banking Explained – A Bank’s Relationship Tier Usually Does the Job

You don’t need a private bank for a dedicated banker, fee waivers, or loan discounts. Banks offer many of those perks through relationship tiers that reward customers who keep more money with them. Join the tier your deposits and investments at one bank already reach, rather than moving cash there just to qualify.

Bank of America’s BofA Rewards program waives the monthly fees on your checking and savings accounts at its Preferred Plus tier, which starts when your combined balances at the bank and its Merrill brokerage average $30,000. Chase Private Client drops its $35 monthly fee when your linked Chase deposits and qualifying investments average $150,000.

A true private bank asks far more. Bank of America’s Private Bank, which helps with trust and estate planning and philanthropy, sets a $3 million investment minimum. In return, a private bank gives you one banker who coordinates your lending, investments, and estate planning, plus loans built around what you own, such as a credit line backed by your portfolio. You pay for the service, usually through a yearly fee based on the assets the bank manages, and its preferred rates on loans and deposits aren’t always the best you could find elsewhere.

Relationship tiers deliver parts of that package for far smaller balances. Chase Private Client assigns you a dedicated banker. Bank of America’s Preferred Plus takes $300 off its mortgage origination fee, the upfront charge for making the loan, and adds 25% to the rewards on eligible Bank of America credit cards.

Bank of America counts qualifying Merrill investment accounts toward its tiers, and Chase counts personal investments you hold through JPMorgan Chase toward its $150,000. A brokerage account you already keep with either bank can put you in a tier at no cost.

Moving cash to qualify is another matter. Say reaching Preferred Plus means moving $30,000 from a high-yield savings account paying 3% into Bank of America’s Advantage Plus Banking checking, which pays no interest. That costs $900 in interest a year, $756 more than the $144 a year the tier saves on that account’s $12 monthly fee. A $250 direct deposit each month also waives that fee. The $300 off the origination fee applies once, to a mortgage you get from Bank of America.

Add up what you already keep at one bank and its brokerage, and join the highest tier that total reaches.
