Europa League preview: Olympiacos vs. AC Milan – Team news, opposition insight, stats and prediction

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

AC Milan will make the trip to Greece with the hopes of turning a corner against Olympiacos after a rough start to their Europa League campaign.

It has been a tale of two competitions this season so far for Milan. In Serie A they are inside the top four and have won eight games out of 12, leading to suggestions of a Scudetto bid. However, in Europe they have lost three of four, and sit in the bottom eight.

It means that they’ll have to be perfect to even be in with a chance of a top eight finish – given you’ve needed at least 15 points in the last two seasons. Nevertheless, a point and gaining a bit of momentum across all competitions would still be appreciated.

Game date: Thursday, November 26 Kick-off time: 18:45 (CET) Venue: Karaiskakis Stadium Referee: Chris Kavanagh (VAR: Bankes)

As mentioned, things have been going quite well for Milan domestically. Sunday’s 5-1 victory against surprise package Frosinone was another example of the potential of this team, but there remains a necessity to find some consistency.

This is especially the case when it comes to the Europa League, in which the Rossoneri have been quire frankly awful. A 4-0 win against RB Salzburg in round two was the only glimmer of light, with the other three games being defeats to Benfica, Bournemouth and Ferencvaros, all without scoring.

As things stand right now, Milan would not even make the play-off spots as they sit outside the top 24. There is only one point in it and even eighth is only four points away, though the runway is getting shorter with only 12 points to play for.

The result of this is that Amorim is expected to name quite a strong side for the game, but we must start with the injury absences. The big doubt is Strahinja Pavlovic, who needs another check on a kidney stone problem despite playing 20 minutes off the bench against Frosinone.

Alexis Saelemaekers, Matteo Gabbia, Samuel Chukwueze, Pietro Terracciano and Ruben Loftus-Cheek have all missed the past few games due to various issues and will not be available on Thursday either.

On Tuesday, the news broke that Milan will be without Luka Modric, who felt a tweak in his right thigh during the last game. The Croatian will be given Thursday night to recover and potentially the Cagliari game too, with the hopes of having him back at the start of December. Then, Francesco Camarda and Davide Bartesaghi have already been ruled out.

The other bit of news is that Fikayo Tomori returned to training on Wednesday, which is a big boost given how thin the options are in defence, but it remains to be seen if the Englishman is deemed fit enough to play a part after his absence.

With the squad running quite thin, Amorim won’t have many options to rotate, nor does he have the luxury to with the standings.

Nevertheless, there will be one at the back, where Pervis Estupinan is expected to start. Mike Maignan starts behind Sankhoun Diawara and Koni De Winter, with Mario Gila at right-back again.

Modric’s injury means Ardon Jashari will be given a chance on Thursday, and it’s one he must take. Alongside him, he’ll be covered with reliability from Yunus Musah and Adrien Rabiot, which should help.

Finally, Diego Moreira and Christian Pulisic are expected to start on the flanks, with Goncalo Ramos through the middle.

Predicted Milan XI (4-3-3): Maignan; Gila, De Winter, Diawara, Estupinan; Musah, Jashari, Rabiot; Moreira, Ramos, Pulisic.

Predicted bench: Bouyer, Torriani, F. Terracciano, M. Cissé, Tomori, Perera, Comotto, Vos, Ossola, Hutchinson, Cisse, Menon

Injuries & suspensions: Saelemaekers, P. Terracciano, Gabbia, Chukwueze, Loftus-Cheek, Modric, Pavlovic, Camarda, Bartesaghi

Olympiacos came into the new season having had a mixed 2025-26 campaign under Jose Luis Mendilibar. They finished runners-up to city rivals AEK Athens in the Greek Super League and lost in the Greek Cup quarter-finals, but won the Super Cup and got to the knockout stage play-offs in the Champions League.

In the summer window, the Erythrolefkoi spent over €78m on signings. The headline additions were midfielder Gustavo Puerta from Racing Santander for €16m, centre-back David Carmo rom Nottingham Forest for €9m and striker Armando Gonzalez from Chivas for €8.6m.

They also spent sums between €5-7m on striker Marius Mouandilmadji, right-back Marcus Pedersen, winger Leon Bailey, left-back Nair Tiknizyan, winger Joel Roca and centre-back Zinedin Smajlovic.

The spending was almost totally offset by four big sales. Goalkeeper Konstantinos Tzolakis went to Hull City for €23.3m, winger Andre Luis headed to Kansas City for €15.5m, midfielder Christos Mouzakitis also joined Hill for €15, and full-back Costinha was bought by Brighton for €12.7m.

Mandilibar would only last until September 9 before being replaced by Imanol Alguacil as the new head coach. Faith had been waning since the 2-1 aggregate loss to NEC in the Champions League third qualifying round, and the change came after a 1-1 draw with Volos.

Olympiacos are currently second in the league table with 26 points from 11 games, via eight wins, two draws and one defeat. They have scored 22 goals and allowed only seven, trailing the current leaders Panathinaikos by four points. They are three ahead of AEK, who have a game in hand and a better goal difference.

In the Europa League, they have been exceptional so far. They beat Jagellonia Bialystok 2-1 at home, Marseille 1-0 away, Sparta Praha 1-0 at home and drew 0-0 away with Stade Rennais. That puts them sixth in the table, and within striking distance of top.

In terms of injury news, Olympiacos are expected to be without forwards Ayoub El Kaabi, Yusuf Yazici (who has a Europa League hat-trick against Milan, for Lille) and Chiquino. Midfielders Gustavo Puerta and Santiago Hezze are out, while Leon Bailey may be the latest to join the treatment room as per reports.

In defence, a few changes are expected, especially after conceding twice at the weekend. So, Ortega, Saliakas and Restos keep their spots. However, Carmo and Onyemaechi are expected to start.

Moving into midfield, the pivot won’t be touched, with Scipioni and former Bologna man Remo Freuler keeping their spots with Silva starting ahead of them.

It seems Leon Bailey may be given the nod on the right, despite the medical issues, whilst Fortounis will continue on the left of the attacking quartet behind Gonzalez.

Predicted Olympiacos XI vs AC Milan (4-2-3-1): Ortega; Saliakas, Retsos, Carmo, Onyemaechi; Freuler, Scipioni; Bailey, Silva, Fortounis; Gonzalez.

Predicted bench: Stournaras, Georgakopoulos, Smajlovic, Pirola, Toufakis, Pedersen, Martins, Yaremchuk, Tiknizyan, Bruno, Sa.

Injuries & suspensions: El Kaabi, Yazici, Chiquinho, Puerta, Hezze.

AC Milan and Olympiacos have faced each other four times. Two came in 1959-60 and the other two were in 2018-19, which means that the meetings were almost 60 years apart.

In the European Cup clashes in 1959, the Rossoneri prevailed 5-3 on aggregate after drawing 2-2 in Greece and then beating them 3-1 back at San Siro. Giancarlo Danova scored a hat-trick in the second leg.

The most recent encounters were eight years ago as mentioned. Patrick Cutrone (2) and Gonzalo Higuain fired Gennaro Gattuso’s side to a 3-1 home group stage win. Yet, in Piraeus the Diavolo lost 3-1 on the final matchday to drop to third and crash out.

➤ Olympiakos have won one of their four major European matches against Milan (D1 L2), winning the most recent meeting 3-1 in December 2018 in the UEFA Europa League. The Rossoneri have never won an away major European match in Greece in six attempts (D4 L2), failing to score in three of those matches.

➤ Olympiakos have lost just one of their last 13 UEFA Europa League matches (W8 D4), keeping nine clean sheets. In the group stages, they’re unbeaten in 11 games (W7 D4), conceding just twice.

➤ Milan have lost three of their four UEFA Europa League games this season (W1), including their last two without scoring. They’ve never lost three major European games in a row without finding the net. Olympiakos’ Jota Silva has had the most shots in the UEFA Europa League this season (17) – however, he is yet to score with any of those attempts, only landing two of them on target.

➤ Hosts Olympiakos have won each of their two previous Europa League contests at Stadio Georgios Karaiskákis this season. Olympiakos will be out to prolong a six-match home unbeaten streak in all competitions.

➤ Milan have one win and one loss on their travels in the competition this season in two contests. In their last six matches in all competitions, Olympiakos have won four and drawn two. They scored 17 and conceded five over that stretch, scoring first in four of the six contests. They had five first-half goals, while their opponents netted two times in the first 45 minutes.

➤ Milan have won four and lost two in their last six. They scored 14 and conceded nine in that stretch, netting first in four of the six. Most of their goals (10) came in the first half, while their opponents netted three times before the break.

➤ Armando González has caught the eye for Olympiakos in Europa League play. He is their top scorer with two goals, one of which has been the tone-setting first of the match.

➤ Alphadjo Cisse has been the leading attacking threat for Milan, scoring on three occasions in the current Europa League campaign, including one match-opening goal. He is tied for third in the competition’s scoring charts in 2026-27.

➤ The visitors have found it challenging to score in Europa League play recently, as they are scoreless in their last 198 minutes in the competition. They haven’t been on the scoresheet since a Gonçalo Ramos goal in their league phase win against Salzburg.

➤ Milan may face difficulty scoring yet again, as Olympiakos are in the midst of a shutout streak and haven’t allowed a goal in their last three Europa League matches, dating to a Kajetan Szmyt finish in their league phase win against Jagiellonia Białystok.

Starting in midfield, the obvious choice here is Ardon Jashari. This has been called his game by the Italian media and it’s tough to disagree. If he’s going to have a future with the club beyond this season, a strong start tomorrow is imperative.

The other obvious choice is Pervis Estupinan. The Ecuadorian is a late entry into the expected XI, despite reports linking him with a potential exit in January. Bartesaghi’s injury means he’ll be given a chance and maybe, just maybe, it’s not too late for him. Something of promise needs to be seen, though.

With Milan lacking Modric in their midfield, it could allow someone like Remo Freuler to take the game by his pace. He’s got plenty of European experience and with the Rossoneri likely to rely on Jashari to set the pace, he may take the edge.

Then, if Leon Bailey starts, he’ll be the big threat of the night. Facing Estupinan and Diawara, he’ll have the opportunity to face a duo that have no relation yet. If he catches them out, it could be a long night for the Diavolo.

It’s been a real case of Jekyll and Hyde for Milan in the Europa League, to the point where it’s tough to call which side we’ll see. Logically, after such a heavy win at the weekend, the visitors should be coming away with three points.

However, both teams aren’t brilliant defensively and it could become an open game as a result. Considering what’s at stake, though, the Rossoneri may just edge past the hosts, but it won’t be easy…
