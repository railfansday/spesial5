Fernando Mendoza’s tests not getting any easier. Broncos will show how much rookie learned

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Fernando Mendoza compared what Mike Macdonald did to him Sunday to playing chess.

He left out the part about the chessboard being set up inside an octagon.

The Seahawks' defensive wizard of a head coach challenged the Raiders rookie with shifting coverages and pressure packages, then let his defenders deliver the physical consequences. There were punches, a little taekwondo and enough swift kicks to leave Mendoza studying the board from the canvas.

Fernando Mendoza (15) of the Las Vegas Raiders warms up before a game at Allegiant Stadium in Las Vegas on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. Getty Images

Now comes Denver, where another accomplished defensive coach and a rested collection of pass rushers await his second NFL start.

Broncos defensive coordinator Vance Joseph has had the bye week to prepare for the Raiders. Macdonald just provided fresh film of what confused Mendoza, what slowed his decisions and what happened when an encouraging first half gave way to a miserable second.

Joseph will have his own ways of asking whether the rookie learned anything.

That is how this works. Put a weakness on film and the next opponent will make you prove you have addressed it. Fix that one and someone will start looking for another.

Mendoza understood as much after his debut, accepting responsibility for the sacks and acknowledging that Macdonald had fooled him. His willingness to own those mistakes was encouraging. Sunday offers the first opportunity to show what he has done about them.

Brock Bowers #89 and Fernando Mendoza #15 of the Las Vegas Raiders bump fists on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026, at Allegiant Stadium in Las Vegas. Getty Images

Mendoza completed 11 of 13 passes for 90 yards in the first half against Seattle, distributing the ball to eight receivers and scoring on a 10-yard quarterback draw. He looked comfortable enough to give the Raiders reason to believe they could win.

Then Seattle adjusted. Mendoza finished the second half 6-of-16 passing, and the Raiders struggled to sustain anything.

The challenge in Denver is recognizing those changes quickly enough to keep the offense moving. That means identifying pressure, trusting the appropriate answer and understanding when a play has reached its expiration date.

It also means getting considerably more help.

The Raiders rushed for 65 yards on 24 attempts against Seattle. Asking a rookie to keep solving difficult passing situations while the running game produces so little is an exhausting way to develop a quarterback.

Klint Kubiak has to find ways to create manageable downs, move Mendoza when the situation calls for it and give him throws he can make decisively. The offensive line must provide enough time for those decisions to matter.

Denver has won five straight games, and Nik Bonitto supplied a reminder of its ability to finish opponents with his late strip-sack against Carolina before the bye.

Mendoza is walking into another difficult classroom, with the crowd adding to the demands.

Someday, he might look back on these first two starts with a debt of gratitude. The lessons could help him recognize trouble sooner, play faster and become the quarterback the Raiders envisioned.

For now, he would probably appreciate learning a few of them without another black eye.

Bonitto remains one of the league's most dangerous rushers, even with his sack production trailing the pace of his recent seasons. His strip-sack against Carolina showed how quickly he can decide a game, and the bye provided time to recharge for the stretch run.

Miller must handle Bonitto's speed without becoming vulnerable to his counters. Mendoza can help by getting the ball out on time and maintaining his position in the pocket.

But the Raiders need their veteran left tackle to win enough individual battles to give their rookie a chance.

Stokes needs a response game after an uneven season took a conspicuous turn against Seattle.

Kubiak briefly pulled him after plays on which the coach said his effort fell short. The message was clear. A player the Raiders trust to handle difficult coverage assignments still has to finish every down.

Waddle can make any lapse expensive. His speed puts pressure on a cornerback's positioning, and his ability to create separation demands concentration throughout the route.

Stokes must stay disciplined when Bo Nix extends plays and keep competing if the ball goes elsewhere. The Raiders' pass rush also has to finish its work.

Against Denver's most dangerous receiver, coverage and effort will both be under scrutiny.

Mendoza looks more comfortable in his second start, gets the ball out faster and throws his first NFL touchdown pass. There will be enough improvement to give the Raiders something tangible to build on. But their inability to establish a running game keeps putting too much on the rookie, and the defense gives Denver too many opportunities to answer. Coming off the bye, the Broncos have the preparation time and pass rush to force mistakes. Mendoza takes a step forward. The Raiders still come up short.

That many Seahawks defenders pressured Mendoza in his first start, illustrating how much the Raiders must improve their protection before facing Denver's varied rush.
