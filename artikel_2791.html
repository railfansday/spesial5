Raiders owner Mark Davis hated the Madden movie

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The new Madden movie includes a brief, fleeting glance of a young Mark Davis. The depiction wasn’t flattering.

His assessment of the film is far less flattering.

The Raiders owner teed off on the Madden movie, in an interview with John Katsolimetes of the Las Vegas Review-Journal.

“My comment is, it’s full of turducken,” Davis said initially.

“It depends on whether you like turducken or not, and obviously, I don’t like turducken,” Davis said.

“I think it’s really it’s unfair to take people that have passed away and make caricatures out of them, and try to sell it as truth,” Davis said. “They start the movie with a statement saying that all of it’s not true, but some of it is, so it’s confusing what to believe.”

That’s not an uncommon device. “Based on a true story” means that plenty of liberties will be taken in an effort to make the story more compelling.

Mark Davis had one specific concern regarding the shading of the truth. He objected to his father’s reaction to the serious neck injury suffered by Patriots receiver Darryl Stingley, during a preseason game in 1978.

“So John goes over to the hospital afterwards, you know, and really looks after him,” Mark Davis said, “and there’s a scene where my dad is telling John, ‘You don’t need to go over there. You don’t need to take care of other teams’ players. You take care of our players.’

“That’s just so far from the truth. Anybody who has ever known him knows my dad always took care of people behind the scenes. If it was a player from another team had an issue that wasn’t being taken care of, my dad would quietly take care of that even if it was players on other teams. I’m serious. . . . It just it felt cheap to me to build up John’s image at the expense of my father’s.”

Davis praised Christian Bale for his portrayal of Al Davis.

“You can’t blame the script on him,” Mark Davis said. “He tried.”

Mark Davis also didn’t care for the characterization of the iconic John Madden by Nicolas Cage.

“You know, he at times did look like John, but he wasn’t John,” Davis said. “I mean, they made John to be a pussy, and John was the farthest thing from a pussy.”

The end result could be a positive. Mark Davis is now motivated to create something that properly and accurately depicts his father.

“I don’t know what this movie’s purpose was, to be honest,” Mark Davis said. “But you know, it’s making me more and more realize that I’m going to have to put together something on my dad. It could be a movie, it could be a characterization, but at least it’ll be truthful. . . .

“I think I need to do that, or at least let people know that I’m thinking about doing that, so that don’t jump in and think that they can do it first.”

The concerns expressed by Mark Davis regarding the script raise the question of whether he or the league or anyone bothered to review it before allowing the producers to use the Raiders name, colors, and logos in the filming of the football sequences. That’s a critical aspect when it comes to the overall credibility of a film about pro football.

If it looks real, it feels real. Unlike other films that didn’t secure the ability to use actual team names and logos (Any Given Sunday, The Replacements).

And that’s the irony of this. Davis and the league allowed the Raiders name and uniforms to be included in the film, giving it the impression of accuracy and authenticity. Which, in turn, makes the story seem accurate and authentic. Which makes it even harder to separate the truth from the fiction.

Here’s hoping Mark Davis comes up with a way to tell the true story of Al Davis. With no embellishment, fabrication, or Hollywood tricks aimed at making the story seem better than it was.

Because the story of Al Davis would be pretty damn compelling if it were indeed confined to the strict parameters of reality.
