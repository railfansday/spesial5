U.S. soldier taken into military custody over suspected spying for Russia

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

LONDON — The American military has taken a U.S. Army soldier into custody after he was arrested in the U.K. on suspicion of spying for Russia, British authorities said Tuesday evening.

Senior serviceman Lt. Col. Brandon Carroll Tyner was arrested in England last week as part of an investigation by Britain's Counter Terrorism Policing with assistance from U.S. authorities, including the military and the FBI.

He was assigned to the NATO military alliance's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps (ARRC) and stationed at its headquarters in the county of Gloucestershire, working in human resources.

ARRC has more than 400 permanent staff from more than 20 nations ready to deploy land forces where they are needed.

The U.S. Army confirmed Tyner's identity to CNBC on Wednesday.

Following his arrest, the 41-year-old was detained in London on suspicion of committing offenses under Sections 1 and 3 of the U.K.'s National Security Act.

Authorities did not share details of the case, but Section 1 offenses relate to obtaining or disclosing protected information, while Section 3 covers assisting a foreign intelligence service.

CNBC reached out to the Russian government for comment on the latest developments in the case. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told reporters on Nov. 11 that Moscow had "absolutely nothing to do with" the situation, following the soldier's arrest.

British prosecutors engaged with the U.K. Attorney General's Office and American partners after what U.K. officials called "an extensive investigation."

A decision was then made to pass the investigation to U.S. authorities, in line with international protocol.

FBI Director Kash Patel said in a statement on Tuesday that the service has "zero tolerance for anyone who betrays their oath to the United States or puts our national security at risk."

U.S. Brig. Gen. Sean Stinchon said in a statement that the case should serve as a "stark warning to any service member suspected of violating the oath we all take to protect and defend the Constitution."

"Whenever we suspect somebody of betraying the trust of their fellow soldiers and jeopardizing our collective defense, we will identify you, and we will ensure justice is served," he said.

"These are extremely serious allegations, and our collective response should send a strong message that we will act swiftly and robustly where we suspect this kind of espionage activity taking place," Laurence Taylor, head of the U.K.'s Counter Terrorism Policing, said as the updates on the case were announced.

"We will continue to support our U.S. colleagues as they take the matter forwards," he added.
