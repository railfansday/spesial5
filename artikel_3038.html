Berkshire Hathaway's Next Big AI Bet Could Be Hiding in Plain Sight

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Berkshire Hathaway is a conglomerate with notable exposure to two sectors benefiting from AI's growth.

By Reuben Gregg Brewer – Nov 22, 2026 at 7:15PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Berkshire Hathaway ›

Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -0.47%)(BRKB -0.36%) isn't exactly seen as an artificial intelligence (AI) play. But it may have more exposure to this emerging technology than you realize. In fact, the company's large utility and energy operations present opportunities for AI-related investment that are hiding in plain sight. Here's what you need to know and what Berkshire Hathaway could easily do to increase its AI bets.

Berkshire Hathaway is usually classified as a finance business, which isn't a bad place to put it. After all, it has large insurance operations. However, the company is really a massive conglomerate, with businesses including insurance, utilities, energy, manufacturing, housing, and retail, to provide just a short list and high-level view. In Greg Abel's first annual letter as CEO, he reported that the company had 51 non-insurance operating businesses. In a previous letter, former CEO Warren Buffett said the company owned 189 operating businesses. The definition of an operating business is probably responsible for the two different numbers, but either one you pick, the number is huge.

There is a lot hiding inside Berkshire Hathaway. But, notably, it breaks out a few large businesses, including railroads, utilities, and energy. The last two are increasingly tied to the artificial intelligence industry. The company's utility operations provide the electricity that powers AI. The energy business helps supply the natural gas that lets utilities, and increasingly AI companies, generate electricity.

To be fair, Berkshire Hathaway could easily afford to buy a technology business with its cash hoard of more than $370 billion. Only technology isn't really the type of business that Berkshire Hathaway tends to buy. Former CEO Warren Buffett long focused on acquiring boring businesses that were well run and attractively priced at the time of purchase. For example, trains, utilities, and pipelines. Greg Abel's acquisition of homebuilder Taylor Morrison Home for $8.5 billion suggests he's not going to suddenly shift directions.

It would be exciting if Berkshire Hathaway went out and bought a giant U.S. utility, such as NextEra Energy (NEE -0.41%), or a large pipeline business, such as Enterprise Products Partners (EPD -0.91%). While Greg Abel probably wouldn't target companies of that scale, the CEO could certainly afford to buy smaller players in either sector.

But that's not necessary. Both the utility and midstream sectors are capital intensive. Utilities own massive power plants that take years to build and require huge sums of money. Midstream companies are no different, with their pipelines, storage, refineries, and transportation assets. It isn't unusual for a utility or midstream company to have multi-billion-dollar capital investment plans extending as far as five years into the future.

What that means is Berkshire Hathaway could make very large capital investments within its existing operations. And that, in fact, is probably the most likely outcome. The cash on the company's balance sheet, meanwhile, means it could easily afford the required spending. Speaking to the utility and energy segment, Greg Abel noted at the start of 2026 that, "Infrastructure built for hyperscalers and data centers must be paid for by those customers and reflect the risks tied to step-changes in long-term demand." Increasingly, data center operators appear willing to do just that.

For example, Chevron (CVX +0.91%), a longtime holding in Berkshire Hathaway's stock portfolio, has agreed to build a power plant that will use Chevron-produced natural gas to supply dedicated power to data centers. Berkshire Hathaway could do something similar with relative ease.

Berkshire Hathaway is unlikely to start buying up AI technology businesses. That's just not what the company does. However, if you dig into the conglomerate's operations, you see that it still has material opportunity to invest in the AI sector. And while it would be exciting if Greg Abel augmented the company's utility and energy operations through new acquisitions, it is far more likely that Berkshire Hathaway will simply invest in its own operations to benefit from AI growth over time. That's the opportunity that is hiding in plain sight.
