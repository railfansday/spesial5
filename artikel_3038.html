Kraft Heinz vs. Coca-Cola: Here's the Stock to Buy

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Kraft Heinz's net loss and Coca-Cola's net margin tell very different stories, and the valuation gap may surprise you.

By Jack Delaney – Updated Nov 21, 2026 at 5:18PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Kraft Heinz ›

Should everyday investors favor a turnaround value play or a reliable beverage leader? This match-up explores whether Kraft Heinz (KHC +1.89%) or Coca-Cola (KO +0.94%) is the better buy for your portfolio heading into 2027.

Kraft Heinz focuses on household food staples and condiments, while Coca-Cola dominates the global non-alcoholic beverage market. Both companies provide essential products that often resist economic downturns, yet they follow very different growth paths and valuation models today. This comparison breaks down their latest financial results and potential risks to help you make an informed decision.

Kraft Heinz produces and markets a massive global portfolio of branded foods and condiments, ranging from its namesake ketchup to Philadelphia cream cheese. As one of the prominent consumer staples stocks, it sells its products in more than 40 countries through retail and foodservice channels. Its largest customer is Walmart (WMT +1.51%), which accounts for approximately 21% of net sales, and this customer concentration adds a layer of risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 26, 2026, revenue reached nearly $24.8 billion, representing a slight decline of approximately 1.5% compared to the prior period. The company reported a net loss of roughly $3.5 billion during this timeframe, and its net margin was approximately -14%. These results reflect a period of adjustment as the company manages its global portfolio and navigates changing retail demand.

Coca-Cola is a global beverage powerhouse that owns or licenses hundreds of brands across categories like sparkling soft drinks, sports drinks, and juices. The company utilizes a vast network of independent bottling partners, including Coca-Cola Europacific Partners (CCEP +0.72%) and Coca-Cola HBC (CCHBF +0.67%), to distribute its products in over 200 countries. It recently secured an exclusive pouring rights agreement with KFC U.S. starting in January 2027.

In the 12 months ended Oct. 2, 2026, revenue reached approximately $51.1 billion, which represents a healthy growth of roughly 7.1% over the prior year. The company delivered net income of $14.6 billion, resulting in a strong net margin of about 28.7%. This profitability highlights the strength of its brands and its ability to maintain pricing power in various global markets.

Kraft Heinz faces intense competition from both major branded rivals, such as PepsiCo (PEP +1.11%) and Nestlé (NSRGF +1.24%), and low-cost private-label products. The company must constantly adapt to rapidly changing consumer preferences and is dependent on key retail customers, who may reduce shelf space or pricing leverage. Additionally, supply chain volatility and potential future impairments of its intangible assets remain significant concerns for the business.

Coca-Cola is exposed to risks from unfavorable global economic and geopolitical conditions that can impact its international operations. The company also faces health-related concerns regarding sweetened beverages, which often lead to increased regulatory scrutiny and shifts in consumer habits. Furthermore, Coca-Cola depends heavily on independent bottling partners and must navigate challenges like water scarcity and maintaining its brand image in a competitive landscape against PepsiCo and Keurig Dr Pepper (KDP +1.25%).

Kraft Heinz currently looks cheaper based on its much lower forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio and price-to-sales (P/S) ratio, suggesting it is priced as a value stock compared to Coca-Cola.

Between the two, I favor Coca-Cola more for a simple reason: Kraft Heinz is executing a turnaround, while Coca-Cola is just executing.Looking at its recent performance for the third quarter of 2026, unit case volume grew 5%, organic revenue grew 7%, comparable earnings per share (EPS) grew 11%, and operating income grew 12%. Overall, that shows not only consumers buying more, but also Coca-Cola converting demand for its products into greater profits.

Also promising in those quarterly results was that volume growth wasn't tied to just one geographic area, giving Coca-Cola multiple markets and helping reduce reliance on just one market.

In addition, it's seeing success with its classic offerings, with Coca-Cola volume growing 5% in that quarter. But it's also seeing even larger volume growth with tweaks to its classic formulas, with Coca-Cola Zero Sugar volume growing 16%. To finish out the year, Coca-Cola's management expects 5% organic revenue growth and 11% comparable EPS growth.

While Kraft Heinz needs to show investors it can earn their confidence back, Coca-Cola has already earned the confidence. It just needs to keep executing from here.
