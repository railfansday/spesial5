This Under-$300 Aluminum Carry-On Is My Favorite Travel Upgrade For the Holiday Season

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

It looks luxe, rolls smoothly, and costs way less than a Rimowa.

THE MOST CONVENIENT travel companion is a backpack, and I envy those who can pack a trip’s worth of clothing into one. But I need a carry-on suitcase; I like to think of it as option packing instead of overpacking. For years I relied on my Filson Duffle—and still love it as a weekender—but found myself wanting a roller bag for hands-free travel and to get a little more organization.

Carry-on suitcases are quite bland though. They’re plastic boxes that require you to attach a keychain or bandana just to recognize your own. I considered Rimowa’s iconic carry-ons for a short while until I remembered my tax bracket and came back to reality (if you’re unfamiliar, they’re expensive). More palatable aluminum options from Away and Monos exist, but they miss the mark for me, be it style or features.

A lucky typo while searching Porter Yoshida bags led me to Portar luggage. The brand’s aluminum and polycarbonate/aluminum luggage is sleek, durable, and costs a fifth of a Rimowa cabin bag. I used the Portar Aluminum Carry-On Luggage all summer and it exceeded my expectations, and it’s now seeing me through holiday travel.

Portar’s carry-on is exactly what I was looking for lookswise—an unbranded aluminum suitcase with leather handles. Functionally, it surpassed expectations after trips to Michigan, Chicago, Houston, and Amsterdam.

The aluminum shell has held up to TSA's less-than-careful hands over the past couple of months. Aluminum luggage gathers minor dings and scuffs throughout travel—it’s part of the charm—but the black covers any marks I may have picked up, and I don’t notice any. I also like the rivets along the shell's border. It reminds me of old travel trunks I’ve seen in vintage stores.

The leather handles are not only a nice aesthetic touch, but they actually make handling the suitcase much easier. They’re substantial and padded, unlike the flimsy plastic strip you see on a lot of carry-on luggage. It makes hoisting it into the overhead cabin a bit more steady and secure.

The latches are also a huge change for me, as I’m used to hassling with zippers that snag or tear—I will never go back to zippered luggage again. Both the telescopic handle and wheels operate smoothly, so I glide gracefully to my gate.

Technically my fiancée’s Away carry-on has more space based on dimensions, but I swear I can efficiently pack more into my Portar. Maybe it’s the clever pocket configuration or the removable compression straps and divider, but coming back from Amsterdam, we were counting on my Portar to bring back Delft ceramics and other acquisitions. All of which fit despite my over (option!) packing.

My Portar will now accompany me home for the holidays. It will also travel to the Pitztal Valley in Austria, then on to Vienna the week after Thanksgiving. It’s a first-class carry-on in the cabin space above my economy seat. Air travel used to be luxurious, and people would dress up for the occasion of reaching 38,000 feet. The Portar carry-on is bringing back some of that charm and costs me less than $300.
