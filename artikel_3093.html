Breakfast News: Staying Calm in a Correction

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Where our analysts see opportunity in the AI sell-off.

When stocks fall this fast, it’s tempting to assume they’ll keep falling, and acting on that fear is often costly.

Three of our analysts offer a more measured view of the AI sell-off, each through their team’s philosophy. Yasser El-Shimy and Sanmeet Deo bring the Rule Breakers lens of patience with bold, long-term bets, while Matt Frankel brings Hidden Gems’ disciplined but flexible approach to putting cash to work. All three also share stocks on their radar right now.

Read on for how each of them is approaching the decline!

Yesterday felt like free fall: the Nasdaq Composite sank nearly 5%, erasing Wednesday’s post-election rally and putting the index on pace to close in correction territory, as Nvidia (NVDA -9.26%) and Micron (MU -17.46%) led a broad AI/chip sell-off.

As David Gardner, our Chief Rule Breaker, likes to say: “The stock market always goes down faster than it goes up, but it always goes up more than it goes down.” For Rule Breakers, the only term that counts is the long-term. Yes, political scrutiny may crimp AI capex growth. But a slower build-out likely extends the cycle rather than compressing it into three or four manic years — bullish, in my view, for patient investors willing to buy into the irrational selling likely over the coming days and weeks. This is a marathon, not a sprint.

One stock I’m eyeing after the sell-off is Shopify (SHOP -6.06%), whose price chart says something broke even though the business numbers say otherwise. Shopify fell about 8% after posting $119 billion in GMV, up 29%, revenue up 32% to $3.7 billion, and monthly recurring revenue up 19%, then slid further as today’s tech selloff dragged every highly-valued stock down with it. Buried under the carnage are signs of a resilient business that thrives in an AGI era: AI-driven traffic and orders to merchant storefronts tripled year over year, and Shopify is quietly becoming checkout infrastructure for the agentic web.

This is why Meta Platforms (META -3.96%) partnered with the Canadian company to power Muse AI’s e-commerce infrastructure. Of course, a stock near 70 times earnings leaves little room for error. But I’d call this a valuation reset, not a verdict on execution. I welcome further price declines on this business, because the last quarter makes me want to add more to my already sizable position.

It has been a rough few weeks in the stock market, especially when it comes to AI-related stocks like chipmakers and infrastructure names. The Nasdaq is now in correction territory, falling 10% from its October high, and in many cases the selling has little to do with how well the underlying businesses are doing, and is driven by fears related to the AI build-out and how it's being paid for.

Of course, nobody knows where the bottom is. It's entirely possible that stocks could fall further before things get better. But for long-term investors, paying lower prices means paying less for the future earnings of solid businesses, and there are some excellent businesses that trade for valuations we haven't seen in a while.

On Team Hidden Gems, we try to stay disciplined but flexible, and sell-offs like this one are when that approach pays off. We keep some dry powder on hand and watch our Potential Growth Indicator (PGI), which compares the cash sitting in money market accounts with the total value of U.S. stocks. When fearful investors retreat to cash, the PGI rises, and we get more eager to buy great businesses at lower prices.

One AI infrastructure play in particular I'm looking at is Comfort Systems (FIX -11.58%) which makes HVAC systems for commercial buildings, particularly data centers. Its backlog nearly doubled year-over-year in the third quarter, but the stock has fallen more than 50% from its peak and trades for less than 17 times forward earnings, compared with its five-year average of 26.

Another on my radar is Alphabet (GOOGL -2.20%)(GOOG -1.93%), which had its worst day in a long time after it reported recent earnings, even though Google Cloud's revenue more than doubled and overall revenue grew by nearly 25%. Concerns about Google's advertising results are fair and are worth watching, but at the current price, Alphabet trades for less than 15 times training earnings, far below other mega-cap tech companies.

It’s been an unsettling roller coaster in the stock market the past few days and your emotions may be running hot. This volatility is the price of admission to ride what we call investing. An important aspect of investing I have learned in my career is patience. If I lacked it, I missed out on some very big winners and was left with regret. Warren Buffet once said that “The stock market is designed to transfer money from the impatient to the patient.” Have you initiated that transfer or are you managing your emotions? A good first step would be to take a couple minutes to take long exhale breaths to calm the nervous system. Then, you trust the habits and mindsets you have built to make patience natural.

The Rule Breaker Investing recipe for patience starts before you buy: commit to holding at least three years, so inevitable drops don’t trigger fear. Put on the jersey and stick with companies you love, like fans cheering through a bad season. Expect a rollercoaster, but remember this ride ends on a mountaintop, with the market’s roughly 10% long-run return at your back. Ignore minute-by-minute wiggles. Be a winter soldier, not a sunshine patriot who flees at the first storm. And lean on structure over willpower: own plenty of stocks, size positions to your sleep number, buy in thirds, and review quarterly, not daily.

Owning plenty of stocks also means looking beyond AI. SharkNinja (SN -0.97%), which just reported earnings, is top of mind for me right now. It delivered excellent sales growth, surging net income, and expanding gross margins, and it sells products customers use daily that won’t be affected by AI, data centers, or capex spend. While it won’t completely soothe the red in our portfolios, it’s a reminder to make sure our portfolios consist of a diversified group of companies that reduce exposure to one hot sector.

And in the ultimate power move to face your fears, find a stock you have wanted to own for a while and just buy a few shares (nothing crazy) and let it ride. Now the impatient investor has transferred money to you. You’ll feel better that you were able to zig while the market zags.
