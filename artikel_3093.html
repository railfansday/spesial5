Bear Market Fears Are Back. History Says This Proven Strategy Can Protect Your Investments.

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

CNN's latest "Fear and Greed Index," which offers insight into the emotions driving the stock market, shows fear is currently driving investor sentiment. In the days leading up to the midterm elections, the metric was in the "extreme fear" range.

Although markets reacted positively to the midterm results, one elephant in the room is still fears of a potential bear market in the near future. Those fears have lingered much of this year, as investors worry about a potential AI bubble and ongoing geopolitical events, but they've intensified after the S&P 500 (^GSPC +1.63%) and Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC +2.16%) finished October in the negative.

Only time will tell when a bear market happens, but history has consistently taught one lesson: staying the course is almost always the best route for most investors.

Bear markets are defined as declines of at least 20% in major indexes from recent highs, and, for better or worse, they're inevitable. Knowing that, when approaching or in a bear market, the best thing investors with time on their side can do is to continue investing as usual.

Past results don't guarantee future performance, but there's no reason to think this won't continue in the long term. Abandoning investing during these bear markets could (and likely would) have meant missing out on valuable gains once the indexes rebounded.

It can be tempting to see all the noise in the stock market and try to time your investments accordingly, but that's one of the more counterproductive moves investors can make. Nobody can predict how the stock market will perform, regardless of whether you're an everyday investor or a billion-dollar Wall Street firm with seemingly unlimited resources. If we could, there would be a lot more millionaires floating around and a lot fewer people losing money.

To help fight the urge to time the market, the strategy I always encourage is dollar-cost averaging, which is essentially putting yourself on a set investing schedule and sticking to it regardless of what's happening in the market.

Say, for example, you have $400 to invest monthly in an S&P 500 ETF. You can choose to invest $100 weekly, $200 biweekly, $400 monthly, or whatever best fits your financial situation. More important than when is sticking to a set schedule. This helps remove a lot of the emotion from investing.

Again, nobody knows when a bear market will happen. It could be in the coming months, or years away. Instead of trying to predict it, stay the course with your investing and trust that, if anything, investing during a bear market is a way to grab great investments at much lower prices than before. It's one strategy that has stood the test of time.
