Kiwi pioneered a better way to find flights, but the economics of selling and servicing them proved harder.

In September 2019, Skift ran a story with the headline: “Kiwi.com CEO Wants to Build a Rival to Expedia.” Oliver Dlouhý, then 31, was about to speak at Skift Global Forum in New York. The company had just brought in General Atlantic as its majority investor.

Seven years later, Expedia has agreed to buy Kiwi.com for an undisclosed sum. The deal is expected to close in the first half of 2027, subject to customary closing conditions.

What Dlouhý and his co-founder Jozef Képesi built out of Brno, Czechia, was a remarkable entrepreneurial achievement. Kiwi developed some of the most inventive flight search products of the past 15 years.

Expedia has agreed to buy Kiwi.com for an undisclosed sum, with the deal expected to close in the first half of 2027 subject to customary closing conditions. Founded in Brno, Czechia, in 2012 as Skypicker, Kiwi pioneered virtual interlining — stitching separately ticketed flights from carriers that don't sell connections on one another's planes into single itineraries — along with map-based search and its NOMAD multi-city product. That model carried a servicing cost: when a traveler misses a self-connection, no airline is obliged to rebook them, so Kiwi built its Guarantee, priced the disruption risk, and ran the operations behind it. Skift's September analysis of flight search economics found Skyscanner, which refers travelers to booking partners, was substantially more profitable. Kiwi's revenue fell 20% from €284 million ($331 million) in 2023 to €227 million ($264 million) in 2025, which the company attributed to lower ticket prices; cuts to personnel and customer support spending lifted operating profit to about €15 million ($17.5 million), though it still recorded a net loss of roughly €600,000 ($700,000). After years of disputes — including a U.S. injunction barring it from scraping Southwest's website — Kiwi reached a commercial agreement with Ryanair in January 2024 and said by April 2025 it had more than 200 airline collaborations. Kiwi has said it intends to continue operating as a standalone brand.
