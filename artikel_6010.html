Deadlocked Florida election decided by coin toss

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Fox News' Griff Jenkins and Charlie Hurt report live from Cape Canaveral, Florida, on Election Day to speak directly with local voters at a diner. Voters share their thoughts on the state's gubernatorial race, economic concerns and key midterm issues.

Jennifer Lagner won the election for Atlantic Beach city commissioner in Florida via a coin toss after successive recounts left her tied with her opponent, according to the New York Post.

Lagner and Lisa Herrold each sat at 3,162 votes after Duval County's Supervisor of Elections Jerry Holland initiated both a manual and a machine recount.

The deadlock initiated the unique tiebreaker last week in which each candidate would have the chance to win two of three coin flips.

Under the city's election rules, each candidate had a chance to call the coin's landing. Herrold went first, calling heads, but the coin landed tails, giving Lagner a 1-0 lead.

Lagner went next, accurately predicting heads to win the contest, securing her election victory to become city commissioner.

Supervisor of Elections Jerry Holland holds up his challenge coin to the tail's side, to be used to determine the winner of the best two-out-of-three coin-flip tiebreaker conducted by the Duval County Supervisor of Elections on Friday, Nov. 13, 2026, in Jacksonville, Fla., to decide Atlantic Beach Commission Seat 4. Candidates Lisa Herrold and Jennifer Lagner were tied with 3,162 votes each. Herrold went first and guessed heads and the coin landed tails. Lagner guessed heads with the coin landing heads, winning the seat. (USA Today Network via Reuters Connect)

"This shows how great and free our country is," Lagner told a local news outlet.

"I am honored to have the opportunity to serve as your next City Commissioner for Seat 4," she wrote on Facebook. "A coin toss was certainly an unexpected and unforgettable way to bring an election to a close! Thank you to Jerry Holland and his entire team for their work throughout this election process."

Candidate Jennifer Lagner hugs fellow candidate Lisa Herrold during the best-two-out-of-three coin-flip tiebreaker conducted by the Duval County Supervisor of Elections on Friday, Nov. 13, 2026, in Jacksonville, Fla., to decide Atlantic Beach Commission Seat 4. (USA Today Network via Reuters Connect)

"Jen and I have been a part of this community for years ... It was meant to be ... We’re going to look back at this and realize how unbelievable this was," Herrold told the local outlet.

Supervisor Holland added, "It’s remarkable, really ... the meaning of that is every vote matters, you know, and one vote can make a difference."

Last Friday's coin flip wasn't the first time in Duval County history that an election tie was broken in an unconventional manner.

In the 2014 Neptune Beach City Council race, Rory Diamond and Richard Arthur broke their tie by choosing from a series of numbered ping pong balls. Arthur, who selected the ping pong ball with a higher number, won.

Lagner, Herrold and the Duval County's Supervisor of Elections did not immediately respond to Fox News Digital's request for further comment.
