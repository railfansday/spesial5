Before applying for a Southwest credit card, it's important to understand both Chase's general application restrictions and the rules specific to Southwest cards. These restrictions can affect whether you're approved for a card and whether you're eligible to earn its welcome bonus.

Since Southwest credit cards are issued by Chase, applicants are subject to the issuer's general application restrictions.

Here are a few key rules to keep in mind:

In addition to Chase's general restrictions, Southwest cards have their own rules that can affect your eligibility.

Personal Southwest cards: Chase generally restricts cardholders to one personal Southwest credit card at a time. You also generally won't qualify for another personal Southwest card if you've received a welcome bonus on any personal Southwest card within the past 24 months.

Business Southwest cards: The two Southwest business cards have separate eligibility restrictions. You generally can't qualify for a particular business card if you currently hold that same card or received its welcome bonus within the past 24 months. However, holding one Southwest business card doesn't automatically prevent you from qualifying for the other.

There's no publicly disclosed minimum credit score required to qualify for a Southwest credit card, but applicants generally have the best chances of approval with good to excellent credit — typically a FICO score of 670 or higher.

However, your credit score isn't the only factor Chase considers. Your income, existing debt, credit history and recent credit card applications can also affect your approval odds.

When planning your Southwest card applications, timing and sequence matter. Start by checking your 5/24 status since personal card eligibility depends on the number of new cards you've opened in the past 24 months.

Many travelers start with a business card because these don't trigger the personal card "family" restriction, keeping personal card options open for later.

Business cards also tend to offer strong welcome bonuses and make it easier to split business and personal expenses.

Due to Chase’s application restrictions on personal Southwest cards, it’s a good idea to wait for the highest welcome bonus available or when a Companion Pass is offered before applying.

But remember, Southwest recently announced that its credit card welcome bonuses will soon no longer qualify for earning a Companion Pass. So, if you're hoping to quickly earn a Companion Pass with the help of an eligible Southwest credit card offer, you'll need to apply and be approved for the card by Dec. 2.
