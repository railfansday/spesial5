Can Reddit monetise authenticity without killing it?

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Brands have spent two years seeding Reddit threads to fool chatbots. Now Reddit is turning those same threads into shopping pages.

For years, one of the easiest ways to find an honest opinion about a mattress, a moisturiser or a mechanical keyboard was to search for the product on Google and add one word: Reddit.

The habit became so common that an industry grew around exploiting it. Marketing agencies now sell services that plant fake first-person recommendations in Reddit threads, aimed less at the people reading them than at the AI models that use those discussions as sources.

This week, Reddit gave those conversations a direct route to a purchase.

Announced on November 10, Reddit Reviews uses AI to turn community discussions about roughly 10,000 products into structured review pages. They include summaries, counts of positive and negative opinions, common likes and dislikes, and links to retailers where product catalogues are available.

As we have reported, Reddit says it will not take a commission on purchases and that “businesses can’t pay to have products featured”. The feature is rolling out to US users on iOS, Android and the web over the coming weeks, ahead of the holiday shopping season.

The question is whether Reddit can turn its discussions into a shopping destination without changing the conversations that made people trust them in the first place.

The timing is complicated by the fact that brands have already been trying to influence what people say on Reddit. In August, Mia Sato reported for The Verge that skincare companies, record labels, weight-loss apps and marketing agencies were paying to seed discussions with posts designed to be picked up by large language models rather than persuade human readers.

Data compiled by Semrush for the investigation showed that Reddit was the most frequently cited domain by major AI tools, including ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, ahead of news publishers and Wikipedia.

The incentive is straightforward. If chatbots treat Reddit comments as genuine consumer recommendations, a fabricated first-person account can become an advertisement without looking like one. 404 Media documented services that automate this kind of activity.

Reddit classifies such manipulation as spam. According to the report, volunteer moderators carry out roughly half of takedowns, while around three-quarters of content removals happen automatically.

Reddit Reviews gives these campaigns another potential destination. Until now, a planted recommendation might have influenced an AI-generated answer on another platform. It can now feed directly into a product summary on Reddit itself, appear alongside purchasing information and influence a decision without the user ever leaving the site.

Reddit’s response relies on its platform rules, automated spam detection and volunteer moderators. The company controls the first two, but much of the work of keeping discussions credible falls to people who are not paid to do it.

Moderators of product-focused communities have already been dealing with the increase in promotional posts, using bans, filters and public threads to identify brands and agencies attempting to manipulate discussions, according to The Verge.

They have been doing this to protect their communities. It is less clear whether they will see protecting Reddit’s new commercial review pages as part of the same responsibility. Reddit has not announced any changes to how moderators are supported or involved in the process.

There is another problem that moderation alone cannot solve. People may change what they write when they know their comments could end up summarised beside a purchase link.

A useful Reddit discussion often includes disagreement, qualifications, personal circumstances and details that do not fit neatly into a product verdict. AI summaries remove much of that context to make opinions easier to compare. The result may be more convenient for shoppers, but it can also make the original discussions less useful.

The commercial motivation is clear. Reddit has been looking for ways to reduce its dependence on search traffic as Google’s AI features answer more queries without sending users to external websites.

The company is responding by building more reasons for people to visit Reddit directly. It is testing login requirements outside the US, plans to shut down its RSS feeds on November 13 and has been talking about making more of its existing recommendations and reviews visible to users.

On its second-quarter earnings call, chief executive Steve Huffman said Reddit wanted to surface more of this evergreen content. Chief operating officer Jen Wong also discussed shopping ads in search, as Search Engine Journal reported.

Reddit Reviews is the consumer-facing part of that strategy. The company began testing AI-generated product summaries in February, initially covering electronics, video games, and health and beauty products. Home and fashion categories followed in September.

Reddit also says its research shows that shoppers already use the platform to check recommendations from AI tools. The new feature gives them a reason to start their research on Reddit rather than visit only after receiving a recommendation elsewhere.

It is a reasonable response to the pressure on its traffic. But Reddit is asking the same conversations to serve two different purposes. Users come to the platform for opinions that feel independent of commercial interests.

Retailers and advertisers want those opinions organised into something that helps sell products. The first depends on open discussion, including disagreement and uncertainty. The second works best when those discussions can be reduced to clear recommendations.

Reddit says products cannot be promoted through paid placement in Reviews. That addresses one potential source of bias, but it does not solve the problem of brands trying to influence the discussions that feed the summaries.

The real test will come when a planted recommendation makes it through moderation and appears in a product review, or when users begin writing comments with the summary in mind. Reddit built its reputation on people sharing opinions without thinking about how those opinions might be packaged and sold.

Reviews changes that relationship. The company now has to show that making those conversations easier to shop from does not make them harder to trust.
