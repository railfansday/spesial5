Police arrest seven men after Man United's 4-0 thrashing by Liverpool at Anfield - with two suspected of illegal tragedy chanting

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Published: 03:43 EST, 24 November 2026 | Updated: 03:45 EST, 24 November 2026

Merseyside Police reportedly arrested seven supporters at last weekend's clash between Manchester United and Liverpool, with two on suspicion of tragedy chanting.

The bitter rivals met at Anfield for the first time in the Premier League season as Mauricio Pochettino kicked off his stint as Man United manager - under the most concerning circumstances.

But even before the visitors saw Leny Yoro sent off and four conceded without reply, this fixture has long been one of the most incendiary in the top flight.

Supporters arrested for suspected tragedy chanting are thought to be a 63-year-old man from Heywood, Greater Manchester, and a 57-year-old man from Wallasey, in Wirral, as per the Athletic.

Both clubs have waged independent battles against tragedy chanting, which sees fans taunt opponents by mocking major disasters, such as Hillsborough, the Heysel Stadium disaster, and the Munich air disaster.

Some tragedy chants are even aimed at the personal lives of players on the pitch, and in 2024, new guidance from the Crown Prosecution saw tragedy chanting upgraded to a criminal offence.

Seven people have been arrested following Man United's trip to face Liverpool at Anfield (police pictured breaking up a fight in the away end)

As well as tragedy chanting, a 47-year-old man from Swinton, Greater Manchester, was arrested on suspicion of possession of a class A drug.

Two men - a 49-year-old from Didsbury, South Manchester, and a 38-year-old from Denton, Greater Manchester - were arrested on suspicion of indecent and racialist chanting.

A further two, aged 30 and 23, were arrested during the final minutes of Sunday's clash as a skirmish broke out among the away supporters at Anfield.

But despite the enmity between the two teams, there were a greater number of arrests last month, when Man City visited Anfield for the first match in the wake of the revelation that the club had been found guilty of the vast majority of charges levelled at them for breaching the Premier League's financial regulations between 2009 and 2018.

That fixture saw 10 fans arrested, with five arrested on suspicion of tragedy chanting.

The match-up between the two sides became increasingly charged with the establishment of a rivalry at the top of the table under former managers Jurgen Klopp and Pep Guardiola.

In 2018, the Man City bus was attacked with bottles, bricks and flares ahead of a Champions League meeting between the two sides, with future fixtures in Liverpool at times seeing the players' bus route amended to avoid throngs of home fans.

In March 2025, Merseyside police requested an unorthodox kick-off of 3.45pm for the match, rather than the customary Sunday start time of 4.30pm, in a bid to reduce issues between the two sets of fans.
