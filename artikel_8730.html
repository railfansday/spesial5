Cheaper Concert & Theater Tickets – Buy in the Presale or the Final Days

To pay less for concert and theater tickets, buy early through artist and credit card presales, or late through theater lotteries, rush lines, and resale listings for shows that aren’t sold out. Before you buy resale, compare the all-in price across sites, which sellers now have to show up front.

Brokers using bots can buy many tickets the moment they go on sale, a 2018 Government Accountability Office report found, so fans often pay a reseller’s markup. In the events it sampled, fees averaged 27% of the ticket price on the original sale and 31% on resale.

The cheapest seats usually sell at the start of a sale or close to the show. A presale lets you buy at face value, the price before fees, ahead of the general public. In a show’s final days, theaters release discounted seats, and resellers with tickets to a show that isn’t sold out often cut prices.

Sign up for the artist’s presale the day the tour is announced, and see whether your credit card or phone carrier runs one too. When a tour uses Ticketmaster’s Face Value Exchange, fans who can’t go can resell there only at the price they paid, so check those listings first.

Most Broadway shows, and some tours, run a digital lottery, a drawing for discounted seats. Enter through the show’s website, Broadway Direct, or the TodayTix app. Say a hit show’s seats cost $250 and its lottery price is $49. Win a pair and you pay $98 instead of $500, in seats the box office picks. Discounted rush tickets go on sale the morning of the show, and some theaters sell standing room once a performance sells out. TKTS booths in New York and Philadelphia sell same-day seats for up to 50% off. TDF, the nonprofit that runs them, also sells a $42-a-year membership that lets those who qualify, such as retirees and people 30 and under, buy Broadway tickets for $53.50 to $62.50.

Compare resale prices for the same seats on Ticketmaster, StubHub, and SeatGeek. Since May 2025, a Federal Trade Commission rule has required every seller, resale sites among them, to show the total price, mandatory fees included, up front.

For a show that’s sure to sell out, the presale is your best shot at face value. For any other show, check resale again in its final days.
