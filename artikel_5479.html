Krispy Kreme raised its full-year revenue outlook as its turnaround gains traction

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The doughnut chain also cut its 2026 capital spending forecast to $40 million to $45 million from $50 million to $60 million

Krispy Kreme raised its full-year net revenue outlook to $1.35 billion to $1.40 billion from $1.25 billion to $1.35 billion on Thursday, as the company reported third-quarter results.

Third-quarter net revenue was $328.8 million, down 12.4% from a year earlier, reflecting the company's refranchising deals. Systemwide sales were $490.2 million, up 1.0% in constant currency. The GAAP net loss was $13.3 million, an improvement of $6.8 million.

The new revenue range is in line with the $1.38 billion Wall Street expected, according to Bloomberg. Third-quarter revenue also topped estimates.

Adjusted EBITDA was $35.3 million. Excluding a $9.3 million cybersecurity insurance recovery in the prior-year quarter, that was an increase of 12.7%, and the adjusted EBITDA margin widened by 240 basis points. Including the recovery, adjusted EBITDA fell 13.1%.

Year-to-date cash provided by operating activities was $18.7 million, up $29.8 million from the first three quarters of 2025. Free cash flow was negative $10 million, an improvement of $82 million. The net leverage ratio was 5.5x, down 1.2x from the end of fiscal 2025.

Franchised locations generated 42% of systemwide sales in the third quarter, compared with 25% in fiscal 2025. The company's goal is to reach 50% beginning in fiscal 2027.

Krispy Kreme brought 98 new doughnut shops online across the globe during the first nine months of the year, and franchisees run almost all of them. Capital expenditures fell 64.6% over the same period. After the quarter ended, the company entered a development agreement to open 120 shops in Pakistan over five years. That is its fourth international franchise development agreement of 2026.

Systemwide sales growth of 2% to 4% in constant currency, from $1.96 billion in 2025.

Adjusted EBITDA of $140 million to $150 million.

At least 100 new shops, nearly all expected to be franchised.

The company said the guidance includes completed refranchising transactions but no additional ones in 2026.

Chief Executive Officer Josh Charlesworth told Bloomberg that the increase is being driven by overseas markets, internet orders and U.S. sales through the company's fresh delivery channel. Krispy Kreme pulled out of its weaker fresh delivery spots roughly 12 months back and now concentrates on places that draw more foot traffic, Charlesworth said.

Demand remains resilient even though the industry is uneasy about weight-loss medications and shoppers are tightening their budgets, Charlesworth said. Customers are ordering more "double dozens" in search of value, he said.
