Promise Robotics raises C$40M for robot factories that build homes

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Canadian startup signed America's largest homebuilder last month and plans a Phoenix plant.

A Promise Robotics robot assembles a wall frame at one of the company’s Alberta factories.

Promise Robotics has raised a C$40M Series B led by White Star Capital to expand its robotic factories, which make structural home parts for builders who pay for output rather than plants. It has more than 2,500 homes under contract, a deal with D.R. Horton, and a first US plant planned for Phoenix.

Promise Robotics, a Canadian startup that uses robots to make parts for houses, has raised a C$40M Series B, it announced on Tuesday. White Star Capital led the round, with Horizons Ventures, Radical Ventures and Canadian pension fund PSP Investments joining.

Promise runs factories where robots make structural parts for homes from a builder’s existing designs. Builders pay for what the factory produces, instead of building plants of their own. The company calls this “Factory-as-a-Service”.

It has factories in Calgary and Nisku, in Alberta, and more than 2,500 homes under contract across three markets. Its first US plant, in Phoenix, is due to open in 2027.

The round follows a deal last month with D.R. Horton, America’s largest homebuilder by volume. Under the multi-year agreement, Promise will help produce more than 1,250 of its homes.

“Homebuilding can’t scale on trade labor alone, and builders shouldn’t have to become manufacturers to grow,” said CEO and co-founder Ramtin Attar.

Robots are moving into construction as housing shortages bite. Bedrock Robotics raised $600M in October for self-driving excavators already working on US sites. Berlin’s All3 raised $25M in April for legged robots that work on building sites.

Dutch startup Monumental is betting bricklaying robots can ease Europe’s housing crunch. Promise last raised a $15M Series A in 2023.
