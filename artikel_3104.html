Grupo Financiero Galicia: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Falling NPLs and a shrinking efficiency gap leave investors weighing whether a 15% return target by 2028 is realistic.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 27, 2026 at 3:25PM EST

On its third quarter of 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 25, 2026, Grupo Financiero Galicia (GGAL +5.33%) reported net income of 229 billion Argentine pesos. Falling inflation compressed asset yields faster than deposit costs and reduced short-term profitability across the financial sector. Long-term investors must evaluate how the bank transitions from inflation-driven investment gains to loan-driven operating earnings. Three things management said stand out.

Asset quality improved during the period through stricter credit underwriting and disciplined collection efforts. Nonperforming loans, or NPLs, dropped to 7.8% of total loans. Loan loss provisions fell 12% sequentially to 509 billion Argentine pesos.

Management reduced operating expenses by 10% sequentially and 36% year over year to 731 billion Argentine pesos. Integration with HSBC Argentina helped lower administrative costs. The group's efficiency ratio, which measures operating expenses relative to revenue, improved by more than 20 percentage points compared to the previous year.

Lower interest rates narrowed the financial margin, or net yield on earning assets, to 14.4%. Management aims to rebuild sustainable profitability through higher transaction volumes and fee growth, targeting a 15% return on equity, a measure of net income relative to shareholder equity, by 2028.

Macroeconomic stabilization continues to lower inflation rates. Management expects real loan growth between 5% and 10% next year, supported by recovering consumer demand and a lower cost of risk, which measures expected credit losses against total loans.

Capital strength remains high, with computable capital exceeding regulatory minimums by 4.8 trillion Argentine pesos. A diversified business model across banking, asset management, and insurance positions the bank to capture growth as credit markets normalize.
