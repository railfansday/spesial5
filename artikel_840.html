Overton rips through Australia as England storm to 2-1 series lead

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

England roll Australia for lowest T20I score at home with Mitch Marsh's men bundled out for 108 in 16.5 overs; Jamie Overton records career-best T20I figures of 5-12 after struggling in first two games at Melbourne and Carrara; tourists looking to wrap up series win in Sydney on Sunday

Jamie Overton claimed career-best T20I figures of 5-12 and was involved in seven wickets in total as England hammered Australia by seven wickets on DLS in Brisbane to take a 2-1 lead in the five-match T20 international series.

Seam-bowling all-rounder Overton had bagged eye-watering figures of 1-44 from three overs and 1-28 from two across England's defeat in Melbourne at the weekend and victory in Carrara on Tuesday, going at an economy rate of 14.

But he ripped through Australia at The Gabba as the hosts flapped against his cross-seamers and bounce, ending a game-altering spell with a double-wicket-maiden in the 13th over to help roll Mitch Marsh's men for 108, their lowest T20 score at home, in 16.5 overs.

A successful chase appeared a formality at halfway but a stumble to 49-3 around three rain delays, as Harry Brook (9) and Aneurin Donald (32 off 12) followed Phil Salt (5) in being dismissed by Riley Meredith (3-30), gave Australia a sliver of hope before Jordan Cox (33no off 14) - dropped on one by Nikhil Chaudhary - took England to a revised 93 in 8.5 overs, well inside the new 16-over limit.

Australia 108 all out in 16.5 overs (put in to bat by England): Sam Harper (26 off 14 balls), Jake Fraser-McGurk (22 off 11 balls); Jamie Overton (5-12), Liam Dawson (3-16), Sam Curran (1-17)

England 93-3 in 8.5 overs (revised target of 93 in 16 overs after rain delays): Jordan Cox (33no off 14), Aneurin Donald (32 off 13), Tom Banton (10no off 11); Riley Meredith (3-30)

Donald backed up his 85 from 32 balls on the Gold Coast three nights ago, with his six boundaries in Brisbane (five fours and a six) including three in a row off towering seamer Billy Stanlake, who was making his first T20 international appearance since 2019.

Player of the Match Overton earlier caught Marsh (11 off eight) and Sam Harper (26 off 14) at mid-on off spinner Liam Dawson (3-16), with Australia 55-2 when Harper holed out but then 92-7 as Overton took the next five wickets, starting with Jake Fraser-McGurk (22 off 11).

England will now aim to secure a series victory with a game to spare in Sydney on Sunday, ahead of the final match in Canberra next Wednesday, both of which start at 8.15am UK time.

Captain Brook said: "It would be a phenomenal effort to come over here against a strong Australia and win in their back yard. It would be nice to finish it off in Sydney but we've got to stay present, stay in the moment and see what happens."

The opening powerplay on Friday was a pretty even affair - Australia, who lost the toss, ending it on 64-2 as the wickets of Marsh and Harper were intermingled with a glut of boundaries, including three sixes for wicketkeeper-batter Harper.

However, it was all England in the middle overs as Overton had Fraser-McGurk and Chaudhary (19) pouched at long-on, Tim David (6) and Joel Davies (3) hacking bumpers into the leg-side and then Ben Dwarshuis (0) edging behind to wicketkeeper Cox.

Overton took a nasty blow on his non-bowling hand one ball before dismissing Chaudhary when the Australia batter lashed back towards him but he also inflicted pain, literally, on the hosts, clonking Chaudhary on the grille and David on the elbow.

Spin twins Dawson and Rashid (0-17) also limited Australia and Sam Curran (1-17) polished off the innings by cleaning up Stanlake (0), although Rashid did drop Chaudhary off Overton at backward point.

Salt ran out Mitchell Owen (17 off 13) from midwicket but the home player exacted a slice of revenge when he caught the England opener at cover, before Brook and Donald also perished to Meredith after the first of the three rain interruptions.

It was another headline-making day for Australia away from the on-field action, with it revealed that the Sandpapergate probe would include a look at the 2017-18 Ashes and that veteran off-spinner Nathan Lyon was set to be dropped for the Test series at home to New Zealand that starts in Perth on December 9.

Player of the Match, Jamie Overton (5-12 from four overs):

"The last couple of games I have been really disappointed with the way I bowled.

"With a few experienced players not here, it felt like an opportunity to step up and I hadn't done that yet, so it was nice to put in a performance today."

"I thought Overton was outstanding tonight. He has worked hard over last couple of weeks, put in little bit extra graft yesterday at training and reaped his rewards tonight.

"He was bowling it on a string and it felt you could set a different field and let them make the mistake."

"It was a very disappointing night and we have to own that. The Gabba is the sort of place where you can overplay a bit and some of our shot selection was probably not up to it.

"I said to this young group that I don't want them to fear making mistakes but tonight was probably a case of needing to be a bit more calculated in the conditions we were playing in.

"That was a belting wicket, as good as The Gabba can get. No excuses. We need to be better and luckily it is a five-match series.

"There's so much talent here. I'm still excited about it. It's probably the first series playing international cricket for some of them so there's still opportunities and we'll be up for Sydney."

All T20 internationals to start at 8.15am UK time
