Why Emile Heskey decided Bolton Wanderers would be his final club

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Former Premier League, Championship and England international forward, Emile Heskey, has revealed exactly why he opted to retire after a spell with Bolton Wanderers in the mid-2010s.

The 48-year-old turned out for some of English football's most historic clubs during a 22-year career, which included a high-profile switch from boyhood club, Leicester City, to Liverpool for £11m in the year 2000.

Heskey accumulated 62 England caps and netted seven goals during his esteemed career, as well as winning eight major honours.

Having racked up 516 appearances in the Premier League alongside 110 goals and 49 assists, Heskey then returned to Championship level for the first time in 18 years in December 2014 when he initially signed a short-term contract at the Toughsheet Community Stadium.

After leaving Liverpool in 2004, Heskey went on to feature for Birmingham City, Wigan Athletic and Aston Villa, as well as a two-season spell with Newcastle Jets of the Australian A-League between 2012 and 2014.

Returning to England in late 2014, it was confirmed the striker would sign an initial short-term contract with the Whites, who were managed by Heskey's former Leicester teammate, Neil Lennon, at the time.

During his final career stint, he was partnered with Icelandic striker, Eidur Gudjohnsen, who had returned to the North West for a second spell earlier in December 2014, with the latter providing a debut goal for Heskey against Blackburn Rovers on Boxing Day of 2014.

In total, Heskey made 48 appearances for Wanderers, but his only full season at the club and the last of his career ended in relegation to League One, before he became one of 13 players who departed upon the expiration of their contracts.

Heskey started just 15 Championship games in his final career season, scoring twice against Charlton Athletic and Brighton and Hove Albion, but had already surpassed his 38th birthday at the midpoint of the campaign.

There are several examples of one-time top-level strikers continuing to lead the line into their 40s, but speaking to current Burnley forward, Jamie Vardy, Heskey revealed how he knew it was time for him to call it quits on his playing days.

"I went to Australia for two years then came back to Bolton," he said.

"It was basically, in training, more or less, when you see young kids running past you, and you think, not 'I can't do this anymore', because I could've, and I was in good shape and I felt fine, but I didn't want people running past me and not being able to tackle and get back at them like my head was telling me I could do.

"I was like, 'Do I really want to keep going down the leagues to League One and League Two and continue there', just for what?," Heskey admitted.

"My boys were getting bigger, and I just decided that was enough for me. I got to 38 and probably could've played another one or two years, I think."

Vardy, 39, is known for his remarkable 200-goal exploits at Leicester, during which he won the Premier League, FA Cup, Community Shield and two Championship titles between 2012 and 2025.

However, he then admitted to Heskey that he has had similar conversations with ex-professionals who may have retired earlier in their 30s, with the Sheffield native still turning out on a regular basis in the second tier.

From Championship Golden Boot winners to cult heroes of years gone by, a number of familiar EFL names are still going strong in 2026.

There are shades between the final club stint of Heskey's career and his own, with Burnley currently in a dire predicament in the Championship after relegation from the Premier League, as they could emulate the unwanted feats of Luton Town and Leicester and become the third team to suffer successive relegations from the top-flight to League One in as many years.
