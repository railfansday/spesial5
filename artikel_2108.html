West Ham & Ipswich Town plot move for 'unbelievable talent' who Craig Bellamy likes

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

West Ham and Ipswich Town may have traded divisions at the end of the previous campaign, but the two sides are poised to be locked into the same transfer race for an "unbelievable talent" who Craig Bellamy already has "one eye" on following new developments.

One of Bellamy's fresh call-ups to the Welsh national team is earning admiring transfer glances from West Ham and Ipswich, with the latter bidding to return to the Premier League at the first attempt under the recently-appointed stewardship of Scott Parker after suffering an unprecedented relegation last season.

The Hammers have endured an unsettling reintroduction to life in the Championship for the first time since 2012, having headed into this month's ongoing international break positioned outside of the eight-team play-off picture with just six wins from their opening 15 encounters.

Wholesale changes have been undertaken at the London Stadium in recent times, and with Parker now getting his feet under the table upon returning to the club as Nuno Espirito Santo's replacement, the Championship promotion hopefuls are preparing to rival Ipswich in the race for an acquisition with significant potential long-term upside.

Joe Duncan's star is rising on loan at Southend United from Bristol City, which has led both the Hammers and Ipswich Town to shortlist the shot-stopper on their respective transfer radars ahead of a potential move.

That's according to a report from The Sun, who revealed on Monday afternoon that the two sides have decided to begin tracking Duncan's availability following his shock recent call-up to Craig Bellamy's senior Wales set-up for November's decisive UEFA Nations League double-header against Norway and Portugal.

Duncan, having caught the eye on loan with Southend in the National League so far this season, received the nod from Bellamy and made the matchday squad during the Red Dragons' 3-1 defeat away to Norway over the weekend.

It's now said that Duncan's pre-season displays against West Ham and Ipswich encouraged the pair to take up what is described as "an active long-term interest", which they are both ready to progress forward with in an effort to prize him away from the club.

Despite having not yet made a senior appearance at Ashton Gate after emerging through the Robins' academy, Duncan has gained plaudits for his displays out on loan with 13 appearances to date as the fifth-tier outfit's number-one shot-stopper under a former teammate of Bellamy's in fellow ex-Hammer Kieron Dyer.

The 20-year-old's growing reputation has only caught the attention of West Ham and Ipswich, who could now deal a blow to the West Country outfit by poaching the young goalkeeper on the cheap ahead of the expiration of his current contract next summer.

Duncan is a highly-rated prospect who the Robins will doubtless intend on retaining amid intensifying interest elsewhere, and under-fire boss Michael Skubala recently talked up the academy product's talent when discussing his first-ever call-up to the Welsh first-team fold.

Having previously gained international experience in Jamaica's youth ranks before committing to Wales, Duncan's recent feat collected praise from Skubala as the 44-year-old lauded the 'keeper's "unbelievable talent" and revealed that a big future at Ashton Gate beckons on the horizon.

Skubala, who may not even be in charge of the Robins for much longer, said of the West Ham and Ipswich target: "What a guy. I met him for a couple of weeks before he went out on loan.

"He’s got an unbelievable talent and I think you can see that.

"He is going to be an important part of this club and he has got an unbelievable future ahead of him if he keeps growing in the right way."

As for Bellamy - a previous teammate of Parker's in East London between 2007 and 2009, of course - made the unexpected decision to name Duncan in his most recent Welsh squad in a bid to solve his nation's long-term goalkeeping crisis.

Duncan, quite naturally, is not in contention to displace number-one choice Karl Darlow just yet, but with the Manchester United back-up now aged 36 ahead of 33-year-old deputy Danny Ward, the Robins prospect was promoted in favour of Everton reserve Tom King as Bellamy looks to lower the age bracket within his goalkeeping department by providing more exposure to young shot-stoppers on the fringes of the side.

Bellamy, helped by his relationship with the aforementioned Dyer at Southend, has been closely tracking Duncan's development, and is keeping "one eye" on exactly that. When quizzed on Duncan's inclusion, the Welsh manager explained: "It's a clear issue at this moment, of course it is, because of the age of our goalkeepers.

"At one stage a long time ago, we had the world's number one goalkeeper [Neville Southall], most incredible goalkeeper.

"But this period now, where's the next one? One of my best friends is the manager at Southend [Kieron Dyer], who gives me big updates. He's been over the moon with him [Duncan].

"When I've seen him, I've been really happy with him.

"The National League will test you, it will test your toughness, there's no protection for goalkeepers, so I think it's a brilliant part of his development.

"The more you see him and you see him live then it makes it clear if we think he's going to be ready in the next couple of years.

"He might not be ready right now but we always have to have one eye on the future."

As mentioned, the pursuing pair could have already received a boost in their respective efforts owing to Duncan's contractual circumstances, which could see the Welsh international depart as early as the conclusion of the current campaign if the Robins fail to successfully negotiate fresh terms.

As such, there's every chance that Duncan could leave on a free and, though his age means the Robins would be entitled to some form of compensation, it would likely pale in comparison to the 'keeper's actual market value.

It may just turn out that his future is decided by what sort of pathway he sees available at Ashton Gate, as with Premier League prospects and an inevitable pay rise sure to be on offer elsewhere, that may be the only real leverage at the disposal of City's key decision-makers.
