Mark Noble’s Return To West Ham Dugout Comes With A Catch

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The sight of Mark Noble sitting alongside Scott Parker in last night’s game has prompted plenty of debate among West Ham supporters.

For many, the question is simple. If Noble does not hold the UEFA coaching qualifications, how can he be part of the first-team setup and occupy a place on the bench?

The issue has become a hot topic among supporters, particularly as UEFA regulations require coaches working at various levels of the professional game to hold the appropriate licences or be actively working towards them. UEFA’s coaching pathway runs from C Licence through to Pro Licence, with increasing levels of qualification required for senior positions.

However, the reality is that not everyone seated in a technical area is necessarily registered as a coach.

Modern football clubs employ a wide variety of football staff whose responsibilities can overlap with the first team without them formally holding coaching positions. Player liaison officers, football advisers, technical assistants, performance specialists and football operations staff can all be involved on matchdays without being registered as coaches.

That distinction could prove crucial in understanding Noble’s position.

He brings invaluable experience and knowledge to the team. Few individuals understand the pressures and expectations of representing West Ham better than the club’s former captain. His influence around a dressing room, particularly during periods of transition, could be viewed as every bit as important as the contribution of a traditional coach.

Nevertheless, the situation raises an understandable question among fans. Yet as his role continues to evolve, supporters will remain curious about the precise nature of his responsibilities and whether formal coaching qualifications may eventually become part of his longer-term pathway in football.
