ConocoPhillips more than doubled its profit as oil prices climbed

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Adjusted earnings rose to $3.27 a share from $1.61 as the oil producer lifted its quarterly dividend 11%

ConocoPhillips $COP reported third-quarter earnings of $4.0 billion, or $3.34 a share, more than double the $1.7 billion, or $1.38 a share, it earned a year earlier.

Adjusted earnings, which exclude special items, were $3.9 billion, or $3.27 a share, compared with $2.0 billion, or $1.61 a share, in the third quarter of 2025. Special items included the reversal of a tax reserve.

The company said the increase came from higher prices, offset in part by lower volumes. Its average realized price was $61.40 per barrel of oil equivalent, up 32% from $46.44 a year earlier.

Adjusted earnings per share topped the $3.05 that analysts polled by FactSet expected, according to The Wall Street Journal. Revenue was $20.06 billion. That exceeded the $18.38 billion that analysts surveyed by Zacks Investment Research projected, according to The Associated Press.

Production totaled 2.302 million barrels of oil equivalent a day, which was 97,000 fewer barrels daily than in the third quarter of 2025. Excluding the effect of completed acquisitions and dispositions, production declined by 40,000 barrels daily, a 2% drop. Growth in the Lower 48 states was outweighed by the effect of the Middle East conflict on Qatar and by higher royalties at Surmont, the company said.

Cash from operations was $7.2 billion. ConocoPhillips spent $3.1 billion on capital expenditures and investments and received $1.6 billion from sales of noncore assets. It returned $3.4 billion to shareholders, made up of $2.4 billion in share repurchases and $1.0 billion in ordinary dividends. The company ended the quarter with $10.4 billion in cash and short-term investments.

ConocoPhillips increased the ordinary dividend it pays each quarter by 11%, to $0.93 a share. The higher payout covers the dividend for the fourth quarter, which will be paid on Dec. 1 to stockholders of record at the close of business on Nov. 16.

"We strengthened our balance sheet and continued progress toward our 45% return of CFO target for 2026, including increasing our ordinary dividend at a top-quartile S&P 500 growth rate," CEO Andy O'Brien said in a statement.

Looking to the fourth quarter, the company expects to produce between 2.32 million and 2.36 million barrels of oil equivalent daily. Full-year capital expenditure and adjusted operating cost guidance is unchanged. ConocoPhillips will hold a conference call at noon ET on Thursday to discuss the results.
