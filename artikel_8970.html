Miami Through the GTA VI Looking-Glass

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

A conversation with artists misael soto and Leo Castañeda, ANGA looks back on a year of activism at the Venice Biennale, and more.

Days before the Venice Biennale opened, the Golden Lion jury resigned amid calls to exclude Israel from the festival. Dozens of artists and pavilions took themselves out of the running in solidarity. Now, as the exhibition draws to a close, the Biennale is ready to present its visitors’ choice awards — a “poisoned chalice,” in the words of the Art Not Genocide Alliance (ANGA), that “few want to win.” ANGA reflects on a historic year of organizing against attempts to artwash Israel’s assault on Palestine in an opinion piece today.

As gamers everywhere pore over Grand Theft Auto VI, Senior Editor Valentina Di Liscia sits down with Miami-based artists misael soto and Leo Castañeda to make sense of the game’s many tensions and complexities, while Staff Writer Rhea Nayyar tracks down its various art references. In another form of uncanny valley, Associate Editor Phillip Pyle wanders through empty corporate offices in Midtown Manhattan, transformed by artists who dismantle the systems of technocracy from the inside out. More to start your week below.

—Lakshmi Rivera Amin, associate editor, books

Stripped of the silver and gold they wore in previous iterations of the Biennale, the new Lions have been thrown to the tourists; “Best Artist” and “Best National Pavilion” will be decided by a popularity contest. Why this surprising democratic turn? The expert jury initially appointed to give these honors resigned en masse in April of this year. These resignations were, in part, the result of the Art Not Genocide Alliance’s (ANGA) nearly three years of campaigning to exclude the Israel pavilion from the art world’s most important biennial. | Art Not Genocide Alliance
