Comcast vs. Walt Disney: Which Media Stock Is a Better Buy?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Comcast's cash machine and Disney's prized franchises pull in opposite directions, and the valuation gap between them is hard to ignore.

By Jack Delaney – Updated Nov 22, 2026 at 10:00PM EST

Is the future of media found in the cables under the street or the stories on the screen? Investors often choose between Comcast (CMCSA +0.16%) and Walt Disney (DIS -1.89%) for diversified exposure.

Comcast operates as a backbone of modern connectivity while diversifying into world-class entertainment and theme parks. Disney remains the gold standard for intellectual property, using its content library to fuel a massive ecosystem of streaming and travel. Comparing them helps clarify whether you prefer reliable utility-like cash flow or brand-driven growth.

Comcast provides broadband, wireless, and video services to millions of customers, largely under its Xfinity and Comcast Business brands. As one of the prominent media stocks, it also owns NBCUniversal, which produces television and film content and operates Universal theme parks. The company maintains critical partnerships with providers like Verizon and T-Mobile to support its expanding mobile phone service segment.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $123 billion, which represents a slight decrease of approximately 0.2% compared to the prior year. Net income for this period was roughly $11.3 billion.

Walt Disney focuses on its entertainment, sports, and experiences segments to drive global engagement. The company leverages iconic franchises through Disney+ and Hulu while managing a massive portfolio of theme parks and cruise lines. It also holds strategic stakes in other platforms, including a 70% interest in FuboTV (FUBO -11.94%) and a 37% interest in JioStar in India.

Comcast faces heavy competition from wireline and wireless broadband providers like Charter Communications (CHTR -3.34%). The continued rise of streaming services threatens the profitability of its linear network agreements and traditional video packages. Furthermore, technological shifts such as generative artificial intelligence and a $117.5 million data breach settlement represent significant operational hurdles.

Walt Disney has an intense rivalry for viewers and advertising dollars and competes with tech giants like Amazon (AMZN -4.10%) and Apple (AAPL -1.90%). Operational risks include the potential for labor strikes among park employees or entertainment guilds, as well as service blackouts during contract negotiations. Additionally, the company must manage the volatility of consumer spending on high-end vacations and cruise ship voyages.

Comcast trades at a significant discount based on its forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio, which compares the stock price to future earnings estimates, and its price-to-sales (P/S) ratio, which measures market value against sales over the past twelve months.

Deciding between Disney and Comcast really comes down to what you're looking to accomplish with your portfolio, as well as your risk tolerance. That said, between the two, I would lean toward owning Comcast over Disney.

One of the main reasons is that investors seem to be taking on less expectation risk with Comcast than with Disney. In the third quarter of 2026, Comcast generated $4.2 billion in free cash flow, giving it significant flexibility amid considerable uncertainty in the markets and the economy. While still impressive, Disney's free cash flow of $3 billion in its third quarter is noticeably less.

For Comcast, its broadband business has some issues, but its wireless segment is growing quickly and could offset weakness in that division if it continues to expand.

Disney may be a more exciting investment and has more stock price growth potential. Still, in this kind of market, I'd rather own the stock that generates more free cash flow and passes it on to its shareholders through a dividend that yields 5.3%.
