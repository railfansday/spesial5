Berkshire Hathaway Owns 22.6% of American Express. Is the Stock a Buy, Sell, or Hold After a 17% Drop in 2026?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Investors appear to be pricing in an economic headwind, but if they are, it hasn't materialized yet. And maybe it never will.

By James Brumley – Nov 23, 2026 at 9:45AM EST

10 stocks we like better than American Express ›

It may be one of Berkshire Hathaway's (BRKA +0.55%) (BRKB +0.59%) largest and longest-held holdings. But Berkshire's sizable stake in American Express (AXP +1.13%) certainly hasn't performed of late as you'd expect a Warren Buffett pick to perform.

As of the latest look, AXP shares are down 17% year to date. Now what? If you've been thinking about stepping into a long-term stake in this credit card name, this sell-off is a buying opportunity.

That doesn't necessarily mean American Express shares have already hit their ultimate bottom, to be clear. They could move even lower, dragged down by continued weakness from the overall market. That's what's happened so far this year. Investors appear to expect economic headwinds that will undermine its payments business.

If that were going to happen, however, it would have likely begun by now. It hasn't. Through the first three quarters of 2026, Amex's adjusted per-share earnings are up 14% on 10% revenue growth. And last quarter was just about as strong as the first two, with similar growth in the cards for the quarter currently underway.

Credit the bulk of its cardholder base, mostly. Amex tends to serve a more affluent consumer who is less impacted by economic headwinds than the average consumer. At some point in the foreseeable future, AXP shares should begin reflecting this cardholder resilience.

This might help in the meantime: Despite the stock's recent subpar performance, the analyst community remains undeterred. Their consensus price target of $366.39 is 20% above this ticker's current price. That's not a huge degree of upside, but for a slower-moving name from the financial sector, that's a fairly lofty starting target. Plus, you'll be plugging into a decent forward-looking dividend yield of 1.2% in the meantime.

Or if nothing else, consider this: Berkshire's relatively new CEO Greg Abel hasn't been afraid to let go of some of Buffett's handpicked holdings, some of which Buffett himself began shedding before stepping down as CEO at the end of last year. The fact that Berkshire's still holding on to this name speaks volumes.
