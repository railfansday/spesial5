Unauthorized GTA 6 PC port shut down by Rockstar Games parent company

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Don't try to port Grand Theft Auto VI to PC yourself — or you'll potentially find yourself in big legal trouble.

GTA 6 may finally be out on Xbox and PlayStation 5, but that doesn't help out the PC gaming faithful, who still have much longer to wait for their version of the game.

As a result, some impatient gamers have recently attempted to create PC ports of the game themselves. Notorious GTA 6 leaker, Cyberleek, also recently released an executable .exe file of the Xbox build of the game, which helped those efforts. Even without Cyberleek, these efforts were probably inevitable, as new AI tools have led to a surge in PS5 jailbreaks and unauthorized ports of classic video games.

However, Rockstar Games' parent company, Take-Two Interactive, clearly does not approve of these unofficial attempts to bring GTA 6 to PC ahead of schedule. On Monday, the company had the biggest GTA 6 PC port project, known as Leonida, taken down. So far, GTA 6 has generated billions of dollars, and the PC version could bring in billions more. And when it comes to protecting their IP, Take-Two and Rockstar aren't messing around.

GitHub, where the project was hosted, posted a Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) notice it received from Take-Two.

"We are the IP Enforcement Team for Take-Two Interactive Software, Inc., parent company of Rockstar Games," the notice reads. "We are writing to submit a takedown request regarding ItsNotRexed's GitHub repository and the 2900+ forks attached to it that infringes upon Take-Two's copyrighted works and facilitates copyright infringement of Rockstar Games title, Grand Theft Auto VI."

"On November 20, 2026, stolen game files from an Xbox Series build of Grand Theft Auto VI were publicly distributed," the takedown notice reads. "The following day, this Leonida repository opened with the purpose of running the Xbox Series version of GTA VI on PC. Its README requires Xbox Series game files as input."

According to Take-Two, Leonida attempts to get around the IP issues by instructing project contributors to "bring your own files."

"This is intentionally misleading," Take-Two explains, pointing out that the files in question are the unauthorized GTA 6 files leaked by Cyberleek.

As our colleagues at PCMag point out, this is just one of many unauthorized projects attempting to bring GTA 6 to PC before the official version comes out. (Disclosure: Mashable and PCMag are both owned by the same parent company, Ziff Davis.) Other projects have reportedly managed to get the game's opening scene running on PC. Another has an extremely degraded version of the game running on Mac.

According to the Leonida project admin, Take-Two also successfully had the Discord server for the GTA 6 porting project taken down as well, so it does seem like the company is going to ramp up its takedowns of any attempted GTA 6 ports.

GTA 6 for PC has no official release date. However, GameBeat recently reported that the PC version may be released in early 2027, well before most gamers expected.
