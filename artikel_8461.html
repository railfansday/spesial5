Katie “Raven” Gunvalson Sets New Overall Self-Supported FKT Reported on Pinhoti Trail

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Record-breaking news from the southeast this week. Although the attempt has yet to be verified, it appears the Pinhoti Trail will soon have a new self-supported Fastest Known Time (FKT).

On November 15, 2026, the Pinhoti Outdoor Center posted about the latest FKT. According to the post, Katie “Raven” Gunvalson covered the 350-mile Pinhoti Trail through Alabama and Georgia in 6 days, 14 hours, and 39 minutes. If verified, this attempt will establish a new overall self-supported record for the point-to-point route, which includes approximately 50,000 feet of elevation gain.

Pinhoti Outdoor Center’s Facebook post highlighting the pending record-setting FKT.

The previous women’s record was set in 2022 by Liz Derstine, with a time of 7 days, 18 hours, and 22 minutes. The previous men’s record, which also stood as the overall record, was set in 2020 by Willie Thacker, with a time of 6 days, 16 hours, and 7 minutes. Gunvalson’s 2026 effort shaved nearly two hours off Thacker’s record, establishing a new overall time to beat.

Gunvalson is no stranger to record-breaking efforts. She has set FKTs across the US, including the current female self-supported records on the Oregon Coast Trail and the Ouachita Trail. The Pinhoti Trail attempt would be her first overall record, establishing a new benchmark for all competitors.

FastestKnownTime.com, the official FKT website and record-verification platform, lists the Pinhoti Trail as 335 miles long. However, FarOut and other trail guides list its length at approximately 350 miles. Gunvalson’s tracking GPX file has yet to be published, so her total mileage has not been confirmed. New records can take several weeks to be verified and published on the website, so additional information should become available in the coming weeks.

According to FastestKnownTime.com’s guidelines, there are three distinct “styles” that indicate varying levels of outside assistance: unsupported, self-supported, and supported.

Unsupported means athletes receive no external assistance and must carry everything they need from start to finish. They may only collect water from natural sources.

Self-supported allows a little more flexibility, permitting athletes to accept assistance they can “manage or find along the way.” Self-supported athletes cannot have resources arranged specifically for them, but they can accept assistance available to other trail users, such as trail magic, hostels, and resupplying in town.

Supported efforts allow unlimited outside assistance, “as long as you are entirely self-powered.” For example, an athlete could receive food and water from a support crew but must continue moving under their own power.

Gunvalson’s Pinhoti attempt falls into the self-supported category. So far, there has been little coverage of the achievement, and details about her approach remain unclear. Did she resupply along the way? Did she stay overnight at a hostel? These logistical questions and others will likely be answered once the record is submitted and verified.

In addition to a GPX file, many athletes upload photos and commentary documenting their FKT attempts. In her previous records, Gunvalson has included daily mileage breakdowns, which readers may also be able to look forward to once this attempt is approved and published.

Photo of Katie “Raven” Gunvalson along the Pinhoti Trail. Photo from Pinhoti Trail Alliance’s Instagram.

The Pinhoti Trail was designated a National Recreation Trail in 1977. It spans approximately 350 miles from its southern terminus at Flagg Mountain in Alabama to its northern terminus in Georgia, where it connects with the Benton MacKaye Trail. Flagg Mountain is considered the southernmost peak of the Appalachian Mountain range and stands more than 1,000 feet above sea level.

The Pinhoti Trail is beloved by thru-hikers, section hikers, and day hikers alike. One of the most iconic thru-hikers in recent years even helped establish a hostel along the trail. M.J. “Nimblewill Nomad” Eberhart became the oldest person to thru-hike the Appalachian Trail in 2021 at age 83. His record was broken this year by 91-year-old Dale “Grey Beard” Sanders.

Nimblewill Nomad, along with friends Lloyd and Tammy Caperton, recently opened the donation-based, “no-frills” Southern Comfort Hiker Hostel in Weogufka, Alabama.

A handful of adventurous hikers also use the Pinhoti as a connector on a border-to-border route along the East Coast known as the East Coast Trail. This route links the Florida Trail, Pinhoti Trail, Benton MacKaye Trail, Appalachian Trail, and International Appalachian Trail.

Since 2021, hikers and trail enthusiasts have flocked to Alabama for Pinhoti Fest, a festival created to promote and celebrate the trail. The Pinhoti is continuing to gain attention and popularity, and Gunvalson’s potential FKT will bring even more awareness to this southern footpath.
