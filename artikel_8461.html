Gifting Your House Costs Your Family the Tax Break They’d Get by Inheriting It

Giving your house to a child or grandchild while you’re alive hands them your cost basis, the figure their capital gain is measured from, so selling it can cost them tax on years of growth. If its value has climbed, leave it to them instead, since an heir who sells owes tax only on growth after your death.

Signing the house over to family looks simple, until they sell.

Your cost basis is what you paid for the house plus improvements. Say a mother paid $90,000 for her house and gives it to her son, who sells it at today’s $490,000 value. He takes her $90,000 basis, so the sale leaves him a $400,000 taxable gain. If he owns and lives in it two of the five years before selling, the home sale tax exclusion shelters up to $250,000 of it ($500,000 on a joint return). She’d also file a gift tax return, but owe gift tax only after using up a lifetime exemption in the millions.

If he inherits it at that value instead, his basis steps up to $490,000, and he owes nothing on the sale.

So if your house has gained value, leave it at your death through a will, a living trust you can change, or a transfer-on-death deed where your state allows one. If it must change hands sooner, sell it at market value, lending the buyer the money through seller financing if needed, so their basis is the price paid. Or give only part of it, and the share you keep steps up at your death.
