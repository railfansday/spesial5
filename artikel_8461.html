Before you donate or toss old clothes, check whether a store you already shop will take them back. Many retailers pay in store credit, a discount, or points, mostly for their own brand in good shape. Since the reward is good only at that store, use the program where you’d shop anyway and donate the rest.

Americans generated about 17 million tons of textile waste in 2018, the latest year the EPA has measured, and landfills took 11.3 million tons of it, about two-thirds.

The stores that pay for old clothes split two ways. Those that take back only their own brand mostly pay by the piece, and more for a piece that cost more new, provided it’s in good shape.

Those that take other brands mostly give you a discount on your next purchase instead.

Denim shows how far apart two programs can be. A pair of Levi’s from the past 10 years earns up to a $5 gift card at Levi’s, or $25 off a full-price pair of jeans at Madewell, which takes any brand that’s at least 90% cotton. Whatever no program takes can go to a charity that accepts used clothing.
