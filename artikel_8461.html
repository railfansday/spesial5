Skip Fabric Softener & Dryer Sheets – Most Loads Don’t Need Them

Stop buying fabric softener and dryer sheets by habit. Softener can make towels less absorbent and leave a residue that traps odor in workout clothes, and most other laundry comes out fine without either. For static, dry your synthetic fabrics in their own load and take them out as soon as they’re dry.

A $13 jug of Downy covers 190 medium loads, and a $10 box of Bounce holds 240 sheets. A family that washes the roughly 300 loads a year ENERGY STAR says is average, using a medium dose and one sheet each time, spends about 11 cents a load, or about $33 a year.

Both work by coating fibers so they feel smoother and cling less, and that coating is the catch. Consumer Reports says liquid softener leaves a film that makes towels less absorbent and more flammable. Nike says softener leaves a residue on workout clothes that traps odor-causing bacteria and that sheets clog the fabric, so detergent has a harder time getting in. CHOICE, Australia’s consumer group, warns that softeners can also weaken the flame resistance of children’s sleepwear.

So check the care label before reaching for softener, and keep it away from towels, workout clothes, kids’ pajamas, and anything labeled flame resistant. Softener changes how fabric feels, not how clean it gets, so the rest of the wash can skip it too.

For static, the fix costs nothing. Whirlpool, the appliance maker, says synthetics such as polyester and nylon are especially prone to static and that over-drying makes it worse. So dry them in a load of their own on low heat or the dryer’s moisture-sensing setting.

Wool dryer balls, sold as a reusable swap for sheets, start at about $9 for a set of six, roughly a quarter of the $33 a year that softener and sheets cost. Whirlpool says wool balls help prevent static by absorbing moisture. But a 2016 test by Consumer NZ found no less static and no softer clothes with dryer balls. CHOICE’s 2024 test found that none of the balls it tried did much for drying time or energy use. Treat them as optional.

Use up the jug and box you have, then leave both off the shopping list and keep about $33 a year.
