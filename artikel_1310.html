USMNT Next Manager Odds: Pep Guardiola, Thierry Henry & Candidates to Replace Mauricio Pochettino

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

With Mauricio Pochettino on his way to the Premier League to take over Manchester United, the United States Men's National Team needs a new head coach. Pochettino was one of the biggest appointments in USMNT history, with the former Tottenham Hotspur, Paris Saint-Germain and Chelsea coach presiding over the team's fortunes from September 2024 until November 2026, steering them to the Round of 16 at the 2026 World Cup.

The Argentine followed in the footsteps of figures such as Gregg Berhalter, Bruce Arena, Jurgen Klinsmann and Bob Bradley, among others, and departs the scene having failed to deliver silverware, despite a win percentage of 61% during his tenure. Expectations are high among U.S. soccer fans, with many understandably excited by a crop of players which features talent such as Christian Pulisic, Tyler Adams and Malik Tillman, as well as exciting prospects like Cavan Sullivan.

So, who will step into the role being vacated by Pochettino? There are plenty of major names being linked with the job, including a number of European coaching luminaries as well as home-grown talent who have proven track records in MLS, one of the top 30 leagues in world football. Here, GIVEMESPORT takes a look at the favorites to be appointed next USMNT manager.

Including Landon Donovan, Clint Dempsey, and Christian Pulisic, the best USMNT players of all time have been ranked.

Antonio Conte is currently without a job after leaving Napoli following the conclusion of the 2025/26 season and is rated a 50/1 outsider for the role currently. The former Chelsea, Inter Milan, Juventus and Tottenham boss guided Napoli to the Serie A title in 2024/25 and has an impressive CV, featuring a Premier League title with Chelsea.

Highly regarded young English coach Eddie Howe is similarly rated to Conte in terms of his chances of being appointed the next USMNT manager. Howe was recently relieved of his duties as manager of Premier League team Newcastle United and has been touted as a potential future England boss.

It might be a point of contention among their neighbors to the north, but Jesse Marsch is in the mix at 30/1 to be the next USMNT manager. Marsch, who has managed clubs in Europe, has been Canada men's national team head coach since May 2024 and led them to a round-of-16 finish in the 2026 World Cup.

Didier Deschamps won the World Cup with France both as a player and a manager, so his pedigree means that he would be a major coup for the USMNT. However, the former Marseille and Juventus legend only recently stood down from his role as France manager after 14 years at the helm and may take some time to decide on his next move. Still, he is rated 30/1 to be the next USMNT manager.

One of the most successful England managers in history, Gareth Southgate is considered a 25/1 bet to be the next USMNT manager. Southgate was recently replaced as Three Lions boss by Thomas Tuchel, but he brought the curtain down on his tenure having transformed England into a force to be reckoned with. They were beaten finalists in the 2020 and 2024 European Championships and a perennial contender for World Cups under him.

Former Germany and Bayern Munich boss Julian Nagelsmann is widely considered to be one of the best young coaches in the world and he would represent an intriguing prospect for the USMNT. Despite failing to deliver success for Germany, Nagelsmann is a progressive coach who has been able to imprint his philosophy on a variety of groups. At 25/1, however, he remains an outside bet.

Roberto Martinez won the Nations League with Portugal in 2024/25 and came third in the 2018 World Cup with Belgium. The Spanish coach's grounding came in English football, where he led Wigan Athletic to an unlikely FA Cup triumph in 2013, when they defeated Manchester City in the final. Martinez's recent association with international football means he is well used to the terrain.

From Mikel Arteta to Xabi Alonso, football is home to some of the most exciting young managers.

Interestingly, one of Martinez's assistant coaches, Thierry Henry, is deemed as likely to get the role as him at 14/1. Henry worked as Belgium assistant manager on two different occasions, as well as managing the France Under-21 and Olympic teams, guiding the latter to a silver medal in 2024. The Arsenal icon, ranked ninth among the greatest strikers in football history, has strong associations with North American soccer, too, having played for the New York Red Bulls and managed Montreal Impact.

San Diego boss Mikey Varas has already sat in the big seat for the USMNT, albeit in an interim role, manging two games in 2024 prior to the appointment of Pochettino. He has a strong feel for what is required at that level, having previously served as assistant to Gregg Berhalter and knows the players well from his time as United States Under-20 manager, during which period he won the CONCACAF U-20 Championship.

Pellegrino Matarazzo is rated 6/1 to be the next USMNT boss and the New Jersey native would be a fascinating appointment given his recent success with Real Sociedad. Interestingly, Matarazzo is close to the thinking of another candidate, Julian Nagelsmann, with the pair working together at Hoffenheim.

Nashville SC head coach BJ Callaghan is considered the strong favorite to get the USMNT job after Pochettino's departure and, like Varas, he has previously served in the role, having been a successful interim boss in 2023. Under Callaghan, the Americans won the CONCACAF Nations League and he took his magic touch to Nashville as they won the US Open Cup in 2025 and the MLS Supporters' Shield in 2026.

Steve Cherundolo is not far behind Callaghan in the odds reckoning at 4/1 and he is already in the ecosystem considering he is the manager of the U.S. U-23 team. Cherundolo was a fantastic servant to his country as a player and cut his teeth in management with Los Angeles FC, winning the 2022 MLS Cup and the 2024 US Open Cup.

By far the biggest name in the mix for the next USMNT head coach is Pep Guardiola, who is currently available for work following his departure from Manchester City. One of the greatest managers in history, Guardiola has won practically every title there is to win in European club football and would be something of a dream candidate for U.S. Soccer as they seek to embiggen the sport in the country and, eventually, compete for world glory.
