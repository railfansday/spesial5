Aaron Rodgers, DK Metcalf can't fix Steelers' broken passing game after Eagles loss

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The Pittsburgh Steelers had another ugly day offensively and dropped a 19-9 decision to the Philadelphia Eagles on Nov. 22 at Lincoln Financial Field.

Top receiver DK Metcalf was ejected after multiple personal-foul penalties as the passing game struggled. Quarterback Aaron Rodgers finished the day with 30 completions on 40 attempts for 227 yards and a touchdown.

On the other side, the defense played fairly well, but not well enough to cope with the lack of scoring from the offense.

Here's USA TODAY Sports' report card on the action from a Pittsburgh perspective.

Rodgers simply isn't a threat to beat defenses downfield consistently right now. He was able to get some momentum going at times with the short and quick passes that have proven effective for the Steelers at a few different points this season. It's also true that his receivers need to be open consistently for him to hit some of the more explosive plays. They frequently are not. None of that changes the reality that the lack of a deeper passing game is a major problem for this offense right now, and a near 43-year-old quarterback needs to share responsibility for the failures with everyone else, whether his career is likely to end at the Pro Football Hall of Fame or not.

For the second game in a row, Jaylen Warren wasn't able to make practically anyone miss. The blocking wasn't great, but Warren frequently hasn't needed it to be to transcend and make a lot of explosive plays this season. With left guard Mason McCormick out, possibly for a while, the sledding isn't likely to get easier. The Steelers need more elusiveness from him and primary backup Rico Dowdle. Warren had 28 yards on nine carries. Dowdle had 15 on five touches.

Roman Wilson's return to the lineup didn't lead to much meaningful direct production, nor did it seem to make life easier for top receivers DK Metcalf and Germie Bernard. The former was frequently invisible, as he was in the first half of the loss to Cincinnati last week, and he got himself ejected with a pair of senseless personal-foul penalties. His numbers continue to track toward career lows and the conduct is only compounding the headache he's becoming. Bernard made some of those plays in the short and quick range, which was an encouraging sign after he was held off the scoresheet by the Bengals. Practice squad veteran Max Hurleman added his first NFL touchdown late. It wasn't a performance that engendered much confidence in the group as a whole, though.

Pat Freiermuth was more active than he has been recently as a safety valve for Rodgers, but the downfield success still was not there. Darnell Washington was also relatively quiet. As the receivers continue to struggle, you'd like to see more from these two. They're both playing on lengthy contract extensions to make more plays to jump-start this struggling offense.

There was no push ahead of Dowdle and Warren in the running game. In the passing game, Rodgers was scrambling to avoid pressure frequently and was sacked five times. Much of the momentum this group seemed to establish before the bye has dissipated. The loss of McCormick is certainly disruptive, so there's a temptation to give these guys some benefit of the doubt as they figure life out without him. The results need to be better even with that caveat, though, particularly in the running game. This offense will quickly become one dimensional if this group can't figure it out.

Saquon Barkley's 38-yard touchdown run was a game changer that this group was primarily responsible for. Cam Heyward and Co. did well to contain him otherwise - he had just 37 yards beyond that one big play - and were frequently in the backfield making plays. With the offense playing the way it was, the team just needed them to be better against a struggling offense like Philadelphia's.

Patrick Queen was all over the ball yet again. He's playing some of the best football of his life within DC Patrick Graham's defense right now. Alex Highsmith forced a fumble and generated two long-awaited sacks before an injury disrupted his day. T.J. Watt was once again mostly invisible, except while he was beaten badly on the back side of the long Barkley touchdown run.

Eagles quarterback Joe Milton had chances to make the Steelers pay but missed with some poorly timed overthrows into solid coverage. Beyond a 38-yard completion to Dallas Goedert, the Pittsburgh defensive backs avoided most of the explosive plays that cost them dearly against the Bengals. That was a positive step forward for this soft zone scheme.

McCarthy does not seem to have control over whatever is going on with Metcalf this season. For as much criticism as Mike Tomlin took for being too much of a "player's coach," it's fair to question what's going on in McCarthy's locker room when his top receiver not only can't produce, but also can't stay on the field because of his temper. The lack of downfield passing is also a reflection of his playcalling.
