Lakers win after playing bad basketball. Now comes real test

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

LOS ANGELES – Walker Kessler let out a sigh before taking his seat at the podium at Crypto.com Arena on Friday, Nov. 20.

As much of a cliche as that saying is, it still somehow manages to encapsulate the night – or the whole week, actually – almost perfectly as the Los Angeles Lakers struggled for the third night in a row against another subpar team at home.

It took a dramatic rally and a career night from Quentin Grimes to force overtime and survive the Charlotte Hornets with a win on Tuesday. They simply looked overpowered and had no answer for the Utah Jazz on the second night of a back-to-back, losing by 12 and giving the Jazz just their third win of the season. This time, they found themselves trailing the lowly Sacramento Kings by eight at the half after sleepwalking through the first two quarters.

Head coach JJ Redick took it upon himself to motivate his players. Nobody would repeat what exactly was said but whatever it was, the messaging was received as the Lakers came back to escape yet again with a 106-104 win over the Kings.

"I can't repeat every word I used," Redick said. "The main message was we gotta be tougher, we gotta bring a little bit of energy. Certain nights, whether it's the team, how we're playing, the atmosphere in the arena, you could just be a little flat, and I thought we just had to be tougher and pick up our energy.

The Lakers' big man struggled for most of the first half, picking up four fouls that limited him to just under 12 minutes through the first two quarters. In that time, he had made just three of his six shots -- all of which came under the basket -- lacking touch on multiple finger rolls and just one rebound. Kessler said Redick laid into him in the locker room at halftime. He wasn't being physical enough. He wasn't looking to dunk the ball and was missing some touch around the rim. Defensively, he was playing undisciplined, jumping at pump fakes.

"He lit me up a little bit, and rightfully so," Kessler said. "... I needed it."

The Lakers, and Kessler, responded. They outscored the Kings 52-42 in the second half behind nine points and six rebounds from Kessler, who finished the night with a total of 15 and seven and got his plus-minus up to +4 from the -2 he had at the half.

He shot four of five from the floor in the second half, none bigger than at the start of the fourth quarter.

Collin Sexton had the ball above the three-point line, slashed to his right into the paint and dished it to his former Jazz teammate standing in the corner. Kessler put it up.

"It was a very unorganized play," Redick said. "I'm not even sure why he was standing in the corner."

The ball went through the hoop for Kessler's first three-pointer of the season on his first attempt.

"It was open," Kessler said of his reasoning for taking the shot. "I'm confident in that shot. It was open, and Bull actually told me to space because he was about to slash. ... Shot it. Went in. I feel good. I should've made those free throws (at the end), but that's part of it. It was good."

But despite the win, the Lakers' offensive struggles this week continued. Dončić had another inconsistent night shooting 3-for-8 in the second half after putting up 17 points in the first half. The team collectively shot just 35.1% from three-point range. They turned the ball over 16 times and failed to capitalize on their own takeaways early on.

Nevertheless, the Lakers took home their 14th win of the year.

"Obviously we started out not good at all," Kessler said. Me personally, I didn't play well in the first half, but I like how we responded. There's sometimes a tendency to lay down and just completely die. So it was good to see the guys fight through another tough game."

It doesn't get easier from here. The Lakers struggled against the two worst teams in the Western Conference on Wednesday and Friday. They'll have a chance to close out their homestand with a rematch against Utah on Monday before embarking on a four-game road trip that includes stops against the Memphis Grizzlies (12-4), San Antonio Spurs (13-3) and Houston Rockets (12-5).

It's not uncommon for teams to fall into the trap of playing down to the level of their competition. In a league as talented as the NBA, that can lead to some odd stretches like the one the Lakers have been on over the past week. But the team's critics have been quick to point at a perceived "soft" schedule as the driving factor behind their red-hot start that has gotten them to the first seed in the West through 17 games. They'll use these last three as evidence to support their argument, too.

The next five, however, might give a little more insight into where the Lakers really stand among their peers at this point in the season.
