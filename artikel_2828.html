Hull City's Oli McBurnie opens up on emotional Scotland return under Pocognoli

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Striker Oli McBurnie has recalled the deeply personal moment he received the call from new Scotland manager Sébastien Pocognoli, confirming his international comeback after five years in exile.

The Hull City forward had nearly abandoned hopes of ending his international drought under previous head coach Steve Clarke, despite netting 19 goals for the Tigers, including the crucial Wembley goal that secured the club's Premier League promotion, having been overlooked for the World Cup.

McBurnie concluded his club commitments on Saturday following the Tigers' tight 1-0 defeat at Arsenal, with focus now shifting to two pivotal Nations League fixtures at home against North Macedonia on Friday night, before traveling to Switzerland on Monday evening.

"He (Pocognoli) FaceTimed me; he messaged me, asking if I was free," the City forward recalls in conversation with Hull Live. "I was just sat at home with the kids and the missus, and he FaceTimed me out of the blue and told me about his plans and what he'd been watching and what he'd seen in me and what he'd wanted his teams to be and them sort of thing.

"We had a good chat, and then he told me that I was going to be in. It was, it was hard for me to keep the smile off my face, and yeah, it was, it was a really nice moment.", reports Hull Live.

While we were away, I learnt a lot about him. The thing is, with internationals, they have so little time to implement their game plan. You have 25 lads coming from 25 different teams that all play a different way, and then you have a week of training. It's gonna take time, but yeah, I was really impressed with the manager and his ideas, and yeah, really impressed.

"My missus was in the room with the kids, so she heard it, so I didn't have to tell them because they heard it. I was trying to tell my little one; she didn't quite get it. She knows Hull as the Tigers cos she likes tigers, and Scotland has a lion on it, so the Lions. That's the way she knows. They came up, watched the games that were at Hampden.

"All the things now that you do is for your kids, so my kids have been able to see me represent my country. It was something I never thought they'd get to see. I've played however many times for Scotland before, and it was something that I never thought they'd get to see their old man playing for the country. To get to see their old man playing in the Premier League and playing for his country as well. It was nice for me and for them to see that."

McBurnie featured in all four of Scotland's Nations League fixtures during the revamped international window earlier this campaign, though he acknowledges feeling disappointed at failing to score and finally get off the mark after 20 caps and over 1,000 minutes on the pitch.

"Oh it was good, mate, it was really good. You know how long it's been for me, and how long it's been a goal for me to get back into the team, so I loved it," he continued. "I probably should have had a couple of goals, but the gaffer giving me the chance, I started all four games when I was up there in a little bit of a different position, but really enjoyed it being back with the lads.

"The skip (ex-City man Andy Robertson) had his 100th appearance while I was away with him as well, so seeing things like that and being back involved with the Scotland setup, it's a real proud one."

While McBurnie may have been a familiar returnee, he notes the influx of fresh faces in the squad helped ease the anxiety of coming back after a five year absence.

"I did feel like a little bit like a kid on the first day of school," the 30 year old admitted. "I knew half of the boys from the last time, but what helped was there was another set of new lads, like seven or eight, that had never been in there for the first time, so I was more experienced than them, even though I was still kind of one of the newbies. It was good.

"It was nervy for the first couple of days, but the gaffer was brilliant, and whatever happens now, I'll always be grateful to him for giving me that chance to represent my country."

Sergej Jakirovic lightheartedly suggested that McBurnie was frustrated at missing out on his golf during the international break, to which McBurnie fired back that a broken finger had already ruled out any such plans regardless.

"My finger has done me in anyway, so there's no golf for me at the minute, so it's kind of a bit easier. I haven't played golf for a while; maybe that's why I ain't scored so many. I haven't been playing my golf, but no, it's tough, and the weather's getting a bit worse now anyway, so it might be club's away for the winter now."
