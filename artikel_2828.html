Three former Falcons advance in Pro Football Hall of Fame voting

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The voting process for the Pro Football Hall of Fame's Class of 2027 is underway, as the Hall continues to narrow down its list of candidates ahead of February's NFL Honors.

On Tuesday, the Hall announced that 52 players have advanced to the next round of voting, with three former Atlanta Falcons among them, all of whom are deserving of a spot in Canton: Tommy Nobis, John Abraham and Billy "White Shoes" Johnson.

It's long overdue for "Mr. Falcon" to receive his gold jacket. The first-ever draft selection in franchise history, Nobis spent his entire 11-year NFL career with the Falcons from 1966-1976.

Nobis was named NFL Rookie of the Year in 1966, earned five trips to the Pro Bowl and was named to the NFL's 1960s All-Decade Team, despite entering the league midway through the decade. He led the Falcons in tackles in nine of his 11 seasons and was an inaugural member of the Falcons Ring of Honor in 2004. His No. 60 jersey is also retired by the franchise.

One of the best pass rushers in franchise history, Abraham spent seven seasons with the Falcons from 2006-2012, recording 68.5 sacks during his time in Atlanta, which remains an official franchise record.

Abraham finished his 15-year NFL career with 133.5 sacks, five Pro Bowl selections and two first-team All-Pro selections. "The Predator" recently received some well-deserved recognition from the franchise, as he was inducted into the Falcons Ring of Honor in October.

One of the most electrifying return specialists in NFL history, Johnson spent six seasons with the Falcons from 1982-1987. In 1983, Johnson earned his third career Pro Bowl selection and was named NFL Comeback Player of the Year. He also led the Falcons in receptions in both 1983 and 1985.

Perhaps most baffling is the fact that Johnson remains the only player named to the NFL's 75th Anniversary All-Time Team who has yet to be inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame. He was also named to the NFL's 100th Anniversary All-Time Team, as well as the 1970s and 1980s All-Decade Teams.

The list of 52 nominees will be narrowed down to 25 following the next round of voting, with the results expected to be announced in approximately two weeks. Hopefully, all three former Falcons will find themselves one step closer to football immortality in Canton, Ohio.
