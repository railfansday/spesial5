Dora Maar’s Paris Apartment Is For Sale, Picasso Wall Drawings and All

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

In this quiet, 17th-century Parisian apartment on rue de Savoie in the 6th arrondissement, strange-looking insects appear to be climbing up the middle of the living room wall near the chimney. But these 15 or so critters—spiders, ants, and cockroaches—are actually drawn and carefully protected by glass coverings.

That’s because they were made by none other than Pablo Picasso, once a regular visitor to this home, where his lover, the artist Dora Maar, lived from 1946 until she died in 1997. The abode, along with the Picasso drawings on the wall, is now for sale, listed for the lump sum of €2.85 million (about $3.1 million), reports Le Parisien. How’s that for a dinner party icebreaker? Picasso used to call Maar “ma puce,” which literally means “my flea” and is used as an endearing term in French for “my sweetheart.” It is believed Picasso’s drawings were a playful pun on his nickname for Maar, per the apartment’s current owner, identified only as Ida. “They give it a history,” she added.

All the wall artworks have been officially recognized by the Picasso Administration, which manages the artist’s estate, and oversees a committee that catalogs his works.

Many may know Maar (1907–97) as the famous figure Picasso depicted in many paintings over the course of their seven-year relationship, including his “Weeping Woman” series, and another portrait of her from 1943 that sold for $37 million in 2025, quadrupling its estimate. Yet Maar herself has been belatedly recognized for her own paintings and photographs that contributed to the Surrealist movement’s heyday in Paris from the 1920s to the 1940s.

The 1,600-square-foot apartment’s well-lit rooms, large windows, and high ceilings do fit an artistic sensibility, said the owner. With two bedrooms, an office, kitchen, and the famous Picasso-drawn living room, the Maar’s former home also boasts 13th-century petrified wooden beams from a former mansion that was converted into the apartment building.

Future owners of Maar’s apartment will also be able to hop over to the Louvre and the Centre Pompidou, right across the Seine River. Then again, if the urge to see art by a modern master strikes them, they won’t even need to leave their living room.
