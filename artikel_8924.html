Did a Trump Staffer Accidentally Reveal White House Plans to Revamp the Smithsonian?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Smithsonian Institution, which runs a network of nearly two dozen museums and research institutes throughout the United States, has been a target for president Donald Trump throughout his second term. The president and his staffers have criticized Smithsonian policies and challenged museum leaders, including hauling them to the Capitol to submit to openly hostile questioning from elected officials.

Now, nonprofit newsroom ProPublica has published a report saying that a man on a commuter train heading to Washington, D.C.—apparently a White House staffer—was seen working on a plan to drastically revamp the policies of Smithsonian Institution museums, including the Smithsonian National Museum of American History, in the nation’s capital, which has repeatedly come in for scrutiny. The evidence? Another commuter’s photos of the staffer’s laptop, snapped over the man’s shoulder as he worked on it in the last week of October.

The “Smithsonian Institution Renewal Plan” comprises 25 items. They range from questioning specific exhibits the institution has organized to proposing to rewrite educational materials for schoolchildren and reviewing financial arrangements.

One item deals with a proposed permanent exhibit, to be titled “The Riches of Our Western Civilization Inheritance” and another on “Great Families of American History.” Outside the institution, says the document, should stand a “Colossus Statue” of George Washington.

The plan also refers to new interpretative guidelines for exhibits and promises a review of the Smithsonian’s “strategy” for traveling exhibitions; it specifically names one, “The Bias Inside Us,” that explores implicit bias and toured the US from early 2021 through summer 2026.

Trump has called museums “the last bastion of ‘woke,’” and that buzzword appears in the planning document, which proposes a review of the Smithsonian’s financial grants to “woke third parties” such as the American Alliance of Museums. (A press representative for AAM did not immediately answer ARTnews’s email requesting comment; the organization released a statement with policy proposals for a Democratically dominated Congress the day after the midterm elections.)

Educational materials for school children also come in for review. The plan proposes overhauling the history museum’s online K-12 curriculum to “realign educational materials with patriotism, not ideology.” Out: a module on immigration called “Becoming US”; in: a “Founders-focused curriculum titled something like Architects of Liberty: The Heroic Journey of America’s Founders OR Profiles in Liberty: Washington and America’s Heroic Men of Courage.”

The staffer was also working on a sheet of paper that bore other ideas, such as “announce exciting cool exhibit on D-Day” and “no X rated exhibitions policy.” ProPublica indicates that the latter likely referred to an exhibit on gay history that included a piece of paraphernalia associated with sadomasochistic sex, which was cited in the July report and the hearings that followed.

According to the initials displayed on the staffer’s laptop, ProPublica has determined that the staffer is most likely Sam Bock, a 27-year-old associate policy adviser with the Domestic Policy Council, the White House office that produced a lengthy critique of the Smithsonian, published on July 4, that, among other allegations, accused history museum director Anthea Hartig of promoting “an ideological agenda contradictory to the museum’s founding purpose of fostering patriotism.”

The White House press office did not immediately respond to ARTnews’s request for information on the document or the staffer.
