Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO: what happens next

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

What happens when a CEO resigns, explained through Nvidia's Nov. 12 announcement: interim leadership, board statements, SEC disclosure rules, the successor search and the earnings call

A chief executive's resignation sets off a chain of board decisions, and many of them land within hours. The board has to put someone in charge of daily operations. It has to tell investors, employees and customers what changed. It has to start looking for a permanent leader. It also has to show that contracts, products and financial reporting will carry on as planned.

Nvidia $NVDA is now moving through that sequence. On Nov. 12, 2026, the company's board of directors said Jensen Huang had stepped down as president, chief executive officer and board member, effective immediately. The board named Colette Kress, Nvidia's executive vice president and chief financial officer, as interim CEO. She keeps the CFO role while she fills the top job. She will hold the interim post until the board names a permanent successor. The board said its search will consider both internal and external candidates.

The announcement left several questions open. Nvidia gave no reason for Huang's departure. It set no timeline for the search, named no candidates and disclosed no terms for Huang's exit, such as severance or equity. Other outlets have published reports about the circumstances, but those reports are unconfirmed. Nvidia did say it will release third-quarter fiscal 2027 results on Tuesday, Nov. 17, as scheduled, with its earnings call starting at 2 p.m. PT.

What Nvidia did disclose follows the standard steps of a leadership transition at a public company. That makes the case a practical way to understand what happens when a CEO resigns, from the first board statement to the first earnings report after the exit. The seven slides that follow cover the announcement itself, the choice of a sitting CFO as interim leader and the board's role. They also explain how a CEO search runs, what Securities and Exchange Commission disclosure rules require and how companies keep customers and product plans steady. Each slide separates what Nvidia has said from the general process that applies to U.S. public companies.

Nvidia $NVDA's board of directors announced on Nov. 12, 2026, that Jensen Huang had stepped down as president, chief executive officer and board member, effective immediately. The board delivered the news in a written press release.

The release quoted Harvey C. Jones and Colette Kress. It included no statement from Huang himself.

The announcement went out as a paid press release distributed by GLOBE NEWSWIRE and carried on Yahoo Finance. That makes it Nvidia's own account of the change. It contains what the company chose to disclose on that date and nothing more.

The board's wording set out how far the exit reaches. Some departing chief executives give up the CEO title but keep a board seat. Others move into an executive chair or adviser position. Nvidia's board said Huang left the board as well as the top executive job. The release does not say whether he will hold any advisory role. It also does not say whether he remains connected to the company in another way.

The timing differs from a planned handover. Many CEO transitions are announced weeks or months ahead. A set departure date leaves time for the outgoing and incoming leaders to overlap. An immediate effective date removes that overlap. A board in that position generally names interim leadership in the same announcement. Nvidia's release did so, naming Colette Kress, the company's executive vice president and chief financial officer, as interim CEO.

The release gave no reason for Huang's departure. It does not say whether the decision was his, the board's or a mutual one.

A written, public announcement also fits U.S. securities rules on fair disclosure. The Securities and Exchange Commission adopted Regulation Fair Disclosure in 2000. The rule bars public companies from sharing material nonpublic information with select analysts or investors without disclosing it publicly at the same time. A press release distributed over a newswire is one of the standard ways companies meet that requirement. A wire release reaches newsrooms, trading terminals and financial websites at about the same time. That limits the chance that one group of investors learns of a leadership change before everyone else.

Nvidia $NVDA's board named Colette Kress, the company's executive vice president and chief financial officer, as interim CEO after Jensen Huang's departure. Nvidia said she keeps the CFO job and will hold both roles until the board names a permanent successor.

The choice puts the company under an executive who already knows its finances in detail. Kress joined Nvidia in September 2013 and has served as CFO since then, according to the company. That is a run of more than 13 years in the same finance seat.

Her career before Nvidia was also in technology finance. Nvidia said she spent nearly 25 years in finance roles at technology companies before joining. Cisco $CSCO and Microsoft $MSFT were among those employers.

Harvey C. Jones, quoted in Nvidia's announcement, pointed to that familiarity. He said Kress has a deep understanding of the company's business, customers, operations and financial position.

An interim CEO fills a gap rather than setting a long-term direction. The role lets a board keep decisions moving while it runs a search. It also removes pressure to name a permanent leader overnight.

Picking an insider for the temporary post has a practical logic. An outside executive would first need time to learn the company's products, people and commitments. An existing officer can act from the first day.

A finance chief brings specific strengths to that job. The CFO works with every division on budgets and forecasts. The CFO also deals directly with investors, auditors and lenders. Those relationships matter when a company has to explain a sudden change at the top.

Holding both posts combines two sets of duties. A CFO oversees financial reporting, cash management and the close of each quarter. A CEO sets priorities, represents the company in public and manages the senior team. Those responsibilities are normally split between two executives.

The arrangement also concentrates accountability for disclosure. Under the Sarbanes-Oxley Act, a public company's principal executive officer and principal financial officer each certify its quarterly and annual reports. If Kress holds both posts when a report is filed, the same executive signs both certifications.

Keeping the CFO title also avoids a second vacancy. Had Kress left the finance office entirely, the board would have needed another temporary appointment there at the same moment.

Some questions remain open. Nvidia did not say whether Kress is a candidate for the permanent job. It said only that the board will consider internal and external candidates, and it gave no end date for the interim arrangement.

After a CEO leaves, the board of directors takes direct control of the transition. It names who runs the company in the meantime, decides how to find a permanent leader and tells investors that oversight is intact. Nvidia $NVDA's board took all three steps on Nov. 12, 2026.

The authority sits with the board for a structural reason. Under Delaware corporate law, which governs many large U.S. companies, a corporation's business is managed by or under the direction of its board of directors. Executives run daily operations. Directors hire the chief executive, set that person's pay and can remove them. When the top job opens, no one inside management has the power to fill it alone.

Nvidia's announcement came from the board rather than from management. The board said Jensen Huang had left his roles as president, CEO and board member, with immediate effect. Because he also held a director's seat, his exit changed the makeup of the board as well as the executive suite.

The board's first decision was who would lead in the interim. It chose Colette Kress, Nvidia's executive vice president and chief financial officer. She keeps the CFO role while she serves as interim CEO. The board's second decision was to open a search for a permanent chief executive.

The third task is less formal but carries weight with investors and employees: a public statement of confidence. Nvidia's release included remarks from Harvey C. Jones. Jones said Kress has a deep understanding of Nvidia's business, customers, operations and financial position. He said the leadership team has been instrumental in executing the company's strategy. He also said the board has full confidence in Kress and the team.

A statement like this has a specific function in a sudden exit. It shows shareholders that the directors have endorsed the interim leader by name. It also places the board's own credibility behind the executives who remain. It does not lay out what happens next in detail. It records the board's position at the moment of the change.

An open board seat is a separate matter from the open CEO job. Under Delaware law, the remaining directors can generally fill a board vacancy unless the company's charter or bylaws provide otherwise. Nvidia's announcement did not say whether or when the board would fill the seat Huang left. That detail would have to come from a later company statement or filing.

The board of directors runs a CEO search. It decides what the company needs in its next leader, then measures insiders and outside executives against that profile. Nvidia $NVDA's board said on Nov. 12, 2026, that it is starting a search for a permanent CEO and will consider both internal and external candidates.

The first step is usually about structure. Boards often hand the daily work to a small search committee of independent directors. That committee reports back to the full board, which still holds the final vote on who gets the job.

Next comes the job specification. Directors set out what the role will demand over the next several years. That includes the company's strategy, the markets it sells into, the scale of its operations and any skills missing from the current leadership team. The specification becomes the yardstick for every candidate, inside or outside the company.

Internal candidates bring existing knowledge of the products, customers and culture. Boards typically judge them on their track records and on direct conversations with directors. Some boards also use formal leadership assessments. A company with a written succession plan may already have a short list of executives it has been preparing for the top job.

External candidates widen the field. Boards often hire an executive search firm to build that list. Spencer Stuart, Heidrick & Struggles, Russell Reynolds Associates, Egon Zehnder and Korn Ferry are among the large firms that specialize in this work. A search firm approaches executives at other companies, screens them confidentially and presents a slate to the committee.

Finalists then meet directors over several rounds of interviews. The board checks references and runs background checks. It also negotiates pay, a task that usually involves the compensation committee. An outside hire may expect to be compensated for equity left behind at a previous employer.

An interim CEO can also be considered for the permanent role. The interim appointment does not make that person a candidate automatically. Nvidia has not said whether Colette Kress, its interim CEO and CFO, is under consideration.

The board has given no timeline for its search. It has also named no candidates and no search firm. Once directors settle on a choice, they vote, the company announces the appointment and the interim arrangement ends.

A U.S. public company whose chief executive resigns must report the departure to the Securities and Exchange Commission on Form 8-K, under Item 5.02, within four business days. The filing is posted to the SEC's EDGAR database, where anyone can read it.

Item 5.02 is the section of Form 8-K that covers changes among directors and top officers. It reaches beyond the CEO. The same duty applies when a president, principal financial officer, principal accounting officer, principal operating officer or any named executive officer retires, resigns or is terminated.

Director departures fall under the same item. The rules ask for more when a director leaves over a disagreement with the company about its operations, policies or practices. The same applies when a director is removed for cause. In either case, the company must describe the circumstances. If the director sent a letter describing the disagreement, the company must file it as an exhibit. The company must also give the director a copy of its disclosure and a chance to reply by letter. Any reply is filed as well.

New appointments carry their own disclosure. When a board names a new principal executive officer, including an interim one, the 8-K must state the person's name, position and start date. It must also summarize the appointee's business experience. Any family relationships with directors or officers must be disclosed, along with certain transactions between the appointee and the company.

Pay is a separate trigger. Material new or amended compensation arrangements for named executive officers generally must be reported under Item 5.02(e). Separation agreements can fall into this category. That makes an 8-K one place where severance or equity terms for a departing executive can first become public, if the company has agreed to any.

Regulation FD adds another layer. It bars a public company from sharing material nonpublic information with selected analysts or investors before telling the market at large. A chief executive's exit is generally treated as material information. That is one reason boards announce it broadly, through a press release and a regulatory filing, rather than through private calls.

Nvidia $NVDA's Nov. 12, 2026, press release does not mention a Form 8-K. Jensen Huang's exit as president, CEO and board member, and Colette Kress's appointment as interim CEO, are the kinds of events Item 5.02 is written to cover. Any related filing would appear on EDGAR under Nvidia's company listing.

A company keeps customers and product plans steady after a CEO exit by doing three things quickly. It says that operations will continue, names who is in charge and contacts key partners directly.

Nvidia $NVDA's Nov. 12, 2026, announcement of Jensen Huang's departure carried that kind of continuity message. In the statement, Harvey C. Jones said Nvidia expects its operations, customer commitments and product roadmap to continue without interruption. He said interim CEO Colette Kress has a deep understanding of the business, customers, operations and financial position. He also said the board has full confidence in Kress and the leadership team.

Kress, who also remains Nvidia's chief financial officer, addressed the company's outside audiences in her own remarks. She said she is committed to building on the foundation Huang created over the last 33 years. She said Nvidia's focus remains on customers, partners, developers and shareholders.

Each of those four groups has a different stake in a leadership change. Customers want to know that orders, delivery schedules and support agreements will hold. Partners want assurance that joint projects keep moving. Developers who build on a company's platforms look for signs that tools and software will keep getting support. Shareholders want to know who is accountable for results.

The legal structure of a corporation does much of the work. Supply agreements, customer contracts and licensing deals are made in the company's name, not the chief executive's. A CEO's departure does not, on its own, end those obligations.

Product work at a large technology company is spread across many people. Chip designs, software releases and manufacturing plans are run by engineering and operations teams, often on schedules set well in advance. An interim or permanent successor inherits those plans and can decide later whether to change them.

The outreach steps often run at the same time. Senior sales and account executives call major customers to repeat the company's message. Internal memos tell employees who they report to and what stays the same. Investor relations teams take questions from analysts and large shareholders.

Keeping the rest of the executive team in place is part of the same effort. Jones credited Nvidia's leadership team with being instrumental in executing the company's strategy. Public backing of the remaining executives signals support for the people who run daily operations.

The test comes in the months after the change. Customers and investors then see whether shipments, product launches and public commitments arrive as the company said they would.

Nvidia $NVDA said it will still release its third-quarter fiscal 2027 results on Tuesday, Nov. 17, as scheduled, despite Jensen Huang's departure. The call puts the company's leadership in front of analysts five days after the board announced the change.

The company said the results are due at about 1:20 p.m. PT. Its earnings call is set to begin at 2 p.m. PT. Keeping the original date means Nvidia's reporting calendar has not shifted along with its management.

Earnings calls at U.S. public companies follow a familiar pattern. Executives walk through revenue, profit and margins for the quarter. They then take questions from Wall Street analysts. Many companies also give a forecast for the quarter ahead.

The Securities and Exchange Commission's Regulation FD bars a public company from giving material information to select investors before releasing it to everyone. A webcast earnings call open to the public is one common way companies meet that rule. Any new detail executives share about the transition on such a call reaches all investors at once.

Colette Kress's dual role changes who answers which questions on this call. Nvidia named her interim CEO while she keeps her job as executive vice president and chief financial officer. CFOs typically present the financial detail on earnings calls. At Nvidia, the person who holds the finance job is now also the company's top executive. Analysts can direct questions about the quarter's numbers and about the leadership change to the same person.

Nvidia's fiscal year does not line up with the calendar year. That is why results released in November 2026 carry a fiscal 2027 label. The figures cover a quarter that closed before the board announced Huang's exit.

The forward-looking parts of the call are the ones most closely tied to the transition. Those include any outlook for the next quarter, any comment on the product roadmap and any update on the board's search for a permanent CEO. Nvidia has not said whether the call will address the search.

The stock had already moved before the call. A Yahoo Finance market widget showed Nvidia shares at 152.96, down 20.77, or 11.96%, when the page was captured. That reading was an intraday snapshot, not a closing price or a final measure of the market's response.
