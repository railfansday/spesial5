Week 12 Chicago Bears power rankings roundup

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The Chicago Bears moved to 8-2 with a pretty forgettable win over the New Orleans Saints. But it is quickly on to bigger and better things.

The team turns around to face the Detroit Lions on Thanksgiving Day.

This week is super compressed and not just the players, but also your bloggers. And this year is even worse because there is a game tomorrow already.

Heading into this crucial stretch as the season really starts boiling down to talks of division crowns and playoff seeding, let's see how the analysts rank the Bears.

And with the holiday looming, there is naturally a mostly thankful theme to this week's rankings by many outlets.

Reason(s) To Be Thankful: Despite some skepticism, the marriage of Ben Johnson and Caleb Williams has been as advertised (or hoped).

Three years ago, when the Bears drafted Williams, it felt like all of their quarterback troubles were going to wash away eventually. Year 1 under former head coach Matt Eberflus went about as expected, but landing the big fish in Johnson the following offseason was a franchise changer. Things didn't always look pretty last season, but both have worked to establish a winning culture. With Williams taking the next step in Year 3, it feels like the sky is the limit for this team if their defense keeps improving.

The Athletic – Chad Graff and Josh Kendall

Something to be thankful for: The Jeffery Simmons trade

Since being traded ahead of Week 8, Simmons has five sacks, which is second in the league in that span. He had three Sunday against the Saints, and it's not the only way he is contributing. He is making 1.7 splash plays per game since joining the Bears (which also ranks second in the league) thanks to 10 quarterback pressures and a run stuff. "My man crush continues," Mike Tomlin said on "Sunday Night Football." "This guy is an apex predator."

The Bears haven't trailed in five straight games, and that streak brings back memories of the greatest team in franchise history. The last time the Bears went five games without trailing was 1985, when they went the final three games of the regular season and the first two playoff games without trailing, according to Larry Mayer of ChicagoBears.com. The Bears briefly trailed the Patriots 3-0 in Super Bowl XX before winning 46-10.

While this version of the Bears is much different than the famous 1985 team, their pass rush is starting to emerge since Jeffery Simmons came aboard in a trade. On Sunday, they sacked the Saints' Tyler Shough six times (three times by Simmons) and had 24 pressures, which is the most for the team since 2018, according to Next Gen Stats. The defensive improvement will be tested on Thanksgiving in a massive NFC North game against the Lions.

2. Chicago Bears (3): They haven't trailed since QB Caleb Williams' Week 6 return from a hamstring injury. Could be a tough streak to maintain on Turkey Day in Motown, though.

5. Chicago Bears (5) Super Bowl odds: +850 (third)

The Bears should have played a lot better than they did in their 21-13 home win over the Saints, especially since New Orleans turned the ball over twice and had a nightmare performance on special teams. But Chicago's vaunted offense could never really get going, outside of two second-quarter touchdown drives. QB Caleb Williams was just OK. The running game was productive, though top back D'Andre Swift had only 51 yards. This marked the Bears' fifth straight win, but it honestly looked like they were enjoying the calm before the storm of their Thanksgiving showdown with the Lions in Detroit. They'll need a much better showing from their offense to have a chance then. This will be the game when we find out if the Bears are who we thought they were all along.

3. Chicago Bears (4) The offense hasn't been humming lately, but the defense has really taken a step forward. The Lions game will be a real test on Thanksgiving.

4. Bears (No. 5; 8-2): We'll learn a lot about this team in Detroit on Thursday.

Williams becomes eligible for his first extension after this season. He's the franchise quarterback the Bears searched decades to find, and pairing him with coach Ben Johnson for the foreseeable future is the priority. Chicago's front office has an incentive to get ahead of a rapidly inflating QB market, which will see the likes of C.J. Stroud and Lamar Jackson come to the table, along with others in Williams' draft class. Though Williams said in September that "money's not a motivating factor for me," his top-10 play on a contending team could lead to him earning around $70 million per year. Signing Williams early would allow the Bears to keep his 2027 cap hit low while prorating a massive signing bonus into future years. — Courtney Cronin

The last few weeks haven't been the Sunday cruise that the Bears produced earlier this season, but we've certainly come to understand just how valuable Jeffery Simmons is to their Super Bowl chase. The recently acquired defensive tackle terrorized the Saints in Sunday's win, recording three sacks and once again elevating the entire unit. There are still kinks to work out for the Bears — including attempting to locate their rushing attack — but they maintained pace in the NFC North race for another week.
