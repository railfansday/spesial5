Getting Defensive: Week 12 fantasy plays led by Vikings, Jaguars; top streaming defenses

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Team defense is an unpredictable position in fantasy. Bad weeks happen to the best of teams. Tomato cans turn a defensive score into a big week. It happens.

But when just about every high-end defense that actually plays lays an egg in a given week, that's another matter.

Entering Week 11, no defense had more fantasy points than the Minnesota Vikings. But Brian Flores' ultra-aggressive unit got their hats handed to them in Mexico City—Minnesota managed just one sack and no takeaways in a blowout loss to the San Francisco 49ers.

Not completely unexpected. But still…ouch.

Things didn't go any better for the team that entered the Week second in fantasy points. Like the Vikings, the Tampa Bay Buccaneers had a rough matchup in Week 11 in Detroit against the Lions. And like the Vikings, the Buccaneers faceplanted—a single sack and 31 points allowed.

The Jacksonville Jaguars were fourth in points among defenses after 10 weeks—before just one sack and a fumble recovery in a listless loss to the New York Giants. All three defenses finished outside the top-15. Two finished outside the top-20.

It's a reminder both that no position in fantasy is more matchup-dependent than defenses and that so many variables go into said position every week that even a favorable-looking matchup is no guarantee of success.

Because we needed something else to be anxious about with three weeks left in the fantasy regular season.

Last week's Mexico City mauling was unfortunate, but the Minnesota defense was going to give at some point this season—especially against a 49ers team that moves the ball and doesn't make mistakes. But the Vikings are still second in total defense, second against the pass, first in the league in points allowed, first in the league with 38 sacks, third in takeaways and (most importantly) first in fantasy points. Against a Falcone team allowing the fourth-most fantasy points per game to defenses, order will be restored to the universe.

Last week's uneven effort at Met Life Stadium aside, the Jaguars have played well defensively this season—Jacksonville ranks fifth in the league against the run, leads the AFC in scoring defense, lead the AFC in takeaways with 17 and still sit fourth at the position in fantasy points. Since the bye week, the Titans have admittedly played better offensively, and just a few weeks ago the Jags posted a modest stat line in Nashville. But this is a different team at EverBank Stadium—and an angry bunch coming off a loss.

Last week's ugly effort against the Lions in Motown put an end to a big-time hot streak by the Buccaneers. Over a three-week span from Week 7 to Week 9, the Buccaneers weren't just the highest-scoring fantasy defense—they were No. 1 by a massive margin. Tampa sits fourth in the league in total defense, second against the run and lead the NFL with 21 takeaways. This is a do-or-die week for the Buccaneers, and the Panthers sit just outside the top-10 in fantasy points per game allowed to team defenses this season.

This NFC West showdown could all but lock up the NFC West for the 49ers—a win puts them four clear of the Seahawks with two head-to-head wins over a Seattle team that has been inconsistent at times this season. The first meeting between these teams this season in Week 5 was a wild 32-29, but it was one that featured big plays on defense galore—seven turnovers, eight sacks and a fumble return for a touchdown. The San Fran defense had more fantasy points than any defense in the NFL this season.

That the Broncos are tied for the AFC's best record is at least a bit surprising—Denver's offense has been inconsistent, and while the defense has been solid it's not as good statistically as last year's squad. But thanks largely to the fifth-most sacks in the league the Broncos are a top-10 fantasy defense, and this week's matchup with the Steelers is a favorable one. The Steelers are struggling on offense right now, with just one game with 20 points scored over their last four.

The Bengals' five-game winning streak came to an end Monday night in Washington, and while the Bengals defense didn't play poorly in the game, outside of edge-rusher Boye Mafe's three-sack rampage, the team didn't play especially well, either. But thanks mostly to big plays, the Bengals remain a top-10 fantasy defense after 11 weeks, and this Sunday they play host to a Saints team giving up the 10th-most fantasy points per game to defenses and the most in the NFC over the past month.

For most of the 2026 season, the Giants have been of little interest to fantasy managers. But since the bye week, the first-place Giants are quietly sixth at the position in fantasy points after three sacks and a pair of interceptions in last week's win over the Jaguars. Now, the Giants get a dream matchup against the Colts. Indianapolis has allowed 28 sacks this season. Turned the ball over a league-high 20 times. And given up the most fantasy points per game to opposing defenses—before cutting Daniel Jones loose.

That the Browns come out of a Week 11 bye very much in the hunt in the AFC North is not a square that was on a lot of preseason prediction bingo cards, but here we are. The Browns are good but not great rushing the passer—27 sacks. Good but not great at taking the ball away—13 times for the season. And good but not great in fantasy points—15th for the season. But over the two weeks that Fernando Mendoza has started for the Raiders, Vegas is fourth in fantasy points per game allowed to defenses.

If you happen to come out of your food coma over the weekend and this game in on the TV, do yourself a favor and go back to sleep. The Patriots are falling apart. The Chargers have already fallen apart. But a matchup with the Chargers is hard to ignore—no team has surrendered more fantasy points per game to defenses this year, in part because only one team has given up more sacks this season than the Chargers and only two have turned the ball over more.

Don't look now, but the Commanders are very much in the NFC East hunt after Monday night's upset win over the Bengals. Washington's defense hasn't been great this season—the team ranks 22nd in the league in total defense has produced just one big fantasy effort all season long. But right now, the Cardinals are making every defense they face look like the '85 Bears—the Redbirds have scored just 16 points over their last three games and will be starting either rookie Carson Beck or Josh Johnson at quarterback.

To be perfectly clear, the Chargers have been a disaster this year pretty much across the board, and the defense bears some responsibility for the team's complete and utter collapse in 2026—the Bolts have put forth two decent efforts in 10 games, and the team sits among the bottom-five among fantasy defenses for the season. But the Patriots were allowing the seventh-most fantasy points per game to defenses this year with Drake Maye under center. Now Danny Devito is. OK, Tommy. Same difference.

At a juncture in the season where many fantasy managers just cannot afford to take another loss, there are maybe three defenses in the league this writer would roll the dice on in a bad matchup—and with all due respect, the Seahawks are not one of those defenses. Yes, Seattle's "Dark Side" defense ranks inside the top-10 in fantasy points at the position for the season, but it's just barely. And the Niners are well and truly a trash matchup—no team has surrendered fewer fantasy points per game to defenses this season.
