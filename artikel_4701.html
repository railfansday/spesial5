How to Deal With a Big Liar

Sure, it's common to encounter little lies that don't matter a whole lot in the grand scheme of things—like being told you look great in an outfit when you actually look like Bubble Bass from SpongeBob SquarePants. But it can be a different story when you run into a big liar—someone willing to completely fabricate things about you in order to discredit or take something away from you. One cannot lie. This can be quite a frustrating experience.

This can occur, for example, when someone without the proper credentials or track record is trying to get ahead in an area. If you are already well-established in that area, you may quickly become a target of liars using big lies to take your ideas, credit, or resources. This can even include liars accusing you of doing what they are trying to do themselves—namely taking something from them when they actually took it from you.

That's the opposite of a gas. It's gaslighting, which I've covered before for "A Funny Bone to Pick." It can leave you questioning reality, as in, "Hold on a second. Someone who has no experience in my area of expertise is somehow claiming that I took an idea from that person?"

And people who've already demonstrated that they have no qualms about lying may stop at nothing to get what they want. Such dangerous people may try to bury you in a flurry of fabrications. So what can you do?

The very first thing is to gain control of the situation and completely cut off any interactions with the liar that are not clearly documentable. Big liars will use such undocumented meetings to trap you and generate more fodder for more lies—like claiming you behaved in such-and-such manner or said such-and-such during the meeting. It can then become a you-said-liar-said situation. And guess who then has the upper hand if you aren't willing to lie but the liar certainly is?

And don't get trapped in meetings that have so-called witnesses who have been chosen and set up by the liar to go against you. You never know what control the liar may have over others—such as pictures of others in some kind of compromising position with fish—that may force the witnesses to lie, too. So, choose your witnesses carefully yourself and maybe record the meeting as a backup.

Documentation is so important because liars can be notoriously slippery—using lots of undocumented side conversations with others to spread falsehoods. Liars operate best in the dark—sort of like vampires. And what's the best way to combat vampires? Sunlight.

Yes, your goal should be to get the liar and the liar's actions out in the open as soon as possible for everyone to see. One way is to switch all your conversations to email while telling everyone clearly why you are doing so. In fact, try asking the liar direct questions by email. Anyone with nothing to hide should be able to answer such questions directly, right? Any hedging or reluctance can suggest otherwise.

This may seem like a lot of work, especially when no one else around you seems to care about the truth at the moment. Liars, by definition, typically don't play the long game—because they otherwise might see the long-term consequences of lying and how it may catch up to them eventually. Lying to them seems like an easy short-term fix. But if you play the long game and keep noting every lie, you never know when it may come in handy down the road.

Since liars frequently will use lies to pit other people against you, don't take on a liar by yourself. The sooner you document the lies and show them to others—including those in the legal profession—the better. If the liar somehow thinks that you are alone, isolated, and thus easy to bully, you may have to flex your network to the liar a bit.

It can help to expand the so-called blast radius of any of the liar's actions. When a liar drops a lie, bring others into the conversation. That will force the liar to lie to more and more people.

All the while, be mindful of any role switching that big liars may do—such as playing the victim when they are the ones actually attacking you. Liars can be great actors because they aren't hampered by inconvenient stuff like truth, evidence, and facts.

So, stay cool, and stick to the facts. Don't play their game and resort to vague, unsupported statements. Big liars are going to be better at that than you.

Throughout the downpour of lies, choose what you do and don't care to fight, practice self-care, and get support from your friends. The gaslighting can be tough and leave you wondering why others can't see what the big liar is doing and questioning humanity in general. Remember, though, that even if others do in fact see through the big liar, they may keep quiet because they don't want to get involved and may even fear the big liar themselves. Your good friends can provide healthy contrasts to such cowardly people.

Plus, ask yourself: Would you ever want to be the big liar? Such people have to rely on lying because they can't stand on their own merits. These are deep down insecure and broken people who have to keep maintaining increasingly tangled webs of lies. They've probably done the same lying to many other people, and at some point, it may all catch up to them.

Therefore, when it seems like the big liar is getting away with something, ask yourself specifically what. Ultimately, these big liars can't get away from themselves and the sad existence of having to rely on lying. There's no lying about that.
