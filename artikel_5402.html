Neighborhood Matters More Than the Home: Report

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Neighbours talk and eat around a table at a block party

As rising housing costs force buyers to make tough trade-offs, new research shows their flexibility has boundaries. Home shoppers may settle for less square footage or a fixer-upper, but they won’t sacrifice on feeling safe and secure in their neighborhood.

The research follows a previous study from Cotality which found 69% of buyers were willing to cut lifestyle costs to afford a home.

Millennials are more willing than Baby Boomers to pay extra for a sense of belonging (74% versus 58%). They are also more prepared to accept a less ideal property to secure it (66% versus 43%), suggesting that neighborhood fit can outweigh certain property preferences, the Cotality report shows.

Shared cultural and social identity plays a more limited role in neighborhood selection. Just 10% of buyers include similar social, cultural or lifestyle mindsets among their top three neighborhood considerations, while 7% select political or religious characteristics.

“For one buyer, belonging might mean knowing the neighbors. For another, it might mean feeling comfortable coming home and having the space to keep to themselves,” said Kevin Greene, senior vice president of Real Estate Solutions at Cotality. “An agent’s value lies in understanding what that means for each buyer, then helping them find reliable information to assess whether an area will meet their needs.”

The findings are particularly notable among U.S. respondents, Cotality writes. Nearly three-quarters (73%) would pay extra for a sense of belonging in their neighborhood–the highest proportion across the five markets surveyed–while 61% would accept a less ideal home to achieve it.

First impressions don’t always tell the whole story

While 73% of buyers believe they can quickly tell whether an area feels right by visiting, data shows that 65% of those respondents worry about discovering negative aspects after moving in. Personal impressions also carry considerable weight: 73% say what they see, hear or learn during a visit matters more than official local statistics. However, among this group, 51% would still welcome verified reports on noise and activity.

There is also a clear opportunity for agents to help bridge the information gap, according to the report. Over half of all respondents (51%) consider it appropriate for agents to share verified neighborhood reports. Only 11% want agents to avoid neighborhood discussions entirely and limit their guidance to property specifications and legal sales transactions.
