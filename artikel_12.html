Landmark Crimea bridge hit as Russia reports biggest Ukrainian drone attack of war

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Kerch bridge was built in 2018 and is one of President Putin's signature infrastructure projects (file photo)

Russia says it has recorded the biggest Ukrainian drone attack since the war began, in which a landmark bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea sustained minor damage and several oil refineries were hit.

The Russian military intercepted and destroyed 2,049 drones and four Flamingo long-distance missiles over 24 hours, officials said. Around 600 flew towards the capital alone, Moscow Mayor Sergey Sobyanin added.

Kyiv, meanwhile, came under a record 17 air raid alerts in a single day. At least seven people were killed by Russian strikes and attacks on energy infrastructure caused blackouts for 50,000 people.

The intense strikes come days after US, Ukrainian and Russian envoys held talks aimed at ending the war.

The war in Ukraine began with Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022.

The Moscow-installed head of Crimea, Sergei Aksyonov, confirmed that drones had struck the Crimean Bridge connecting Russia with the annexed peninsula, leading to traffic restrictions.

The structure - also known as Kerch Bridge - was built by Russia after it invaded and annexed the peninsula in 2014. It was inaugurated by Russian President Vladimir Putin to great fanfare in 2018 and is carefully protected with multiple layers of aerial and maritime defences.

Russian pro-war military commentators said on Telegram that rather than a direct attack, the bridge had suffered from the impact of drone warhead debris. Neither Ukraine's domestic security service nor its main military intelligence directorate has responded to a BBC request for comment.

Atesh, a partisan Ukrainian group operating inside Russian-held territory, said on social media that authorities in Crimea were "bracing for further strikes" on the Kerch Bridge and that they felt Moscow did not appreciate the seriousness of the situation.

Ukrainian drones also hit several energy facilities, including an oil refinery in Ilsky, in the southern Krasnodar region, where falling drone debris sparked a fire and injured six people, including children.

Last week, President Volodymyr Zelensky accused Russia of carrying out a concerted operation against Ukraine's bridges in order to "divide Ukraine... and make life as difficult as possible for people" on both sides of the Dnipro River which cuts across the country.

Despite the high-level diplomacy and visits by US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff to both Putin and Zelensky, peace remains a distant prospect.

Moscow is uncompromising on its demands for Ukraine to cede territory and give up efforts to become a Nato member.

The Kremlin has now also started to talk again about the need to rethink Europe's "security architecture", often intended by Moscow to mean a scaled-down Nato presence in countries close to Russia.

The apparent willingness of the US to engage in this conversation has set off alarm bells among Ukraine's European partners, which are concerned their continent's security could be redrawn by Washington and Moscow without their involvement.

A team of six - Britain, France, Germany, Italy, Poland and a representative of the EU institutions - will represent Europe in future talks with Russia, Ukraine and the US.

But the Kremlin continues to be dismissive of European input.

"We have also said more than once we need to understand... how much their position will help the peace process," said spokesman Dmitri Peskov.

EU representatives kept pushing for "unconditional support" for Ukraine and "enhanced pressure" on Russia, Peskov said, adding that "this sort of wording rather suggests their reluctance to facilitate the peace process".
