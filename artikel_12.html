Democrat Scott Colom wins Mississippi Senate race in surprise victory for party

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Democrat Scott Colom is the first Democratic senator elected in Mississippi since 1982

Democrat Scott Colom has won the US Senate race in Mississippi, delivering a shock victory for the party in a deeply conservative state that has elected Republicans for decades.

Colom's victory makes him the first black person from Mississippi to be elected to the US Senate in more than 140 years.

Only a few thousand votes separated Colom from his Republican opponent, the incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith.

The margin was so close that the race could not be called until more than a week after election day, as local officials counted every single ballot that had arrived before the deadline.

Colon won with 49.2% of the vote, while Hyde-Smith received 48.7% and an independent candidate got 2.1%, according to figures from CBS.

Hyde-Smith congratulated Colom in a video posted on X on Wednesday evening and wished him well.

"While this was not the outcome we worked for or hoped for, I respect the decision of Mississippi voters," she said.

The result means the Democratic majority in the Senate is now 55 seats, with a knife-edge race in Alaska still to be called.

It also wipes away a Republican winning streak in Mississippi that dates back to 1982.

The Mississippi contest was never expected to be this close, with Hyde-Smith running for re-election in a state that President Donald Trump won in the 2024 election by more than 22 points.

But Democrats have scored a series of major wins in these midterm elections as the party won control of both the House and the Senate.

It has won Senate seats in numerous Republican strongholds, including Texas, South Carolina and Kansas.

Polls suggest this was due in large part to voter frustrations with the economy and the Iran war under President Trump.

Colom appeared to a ride a national wave of resentment against Republicans, with the party suffering several defeats during the midterm election in typically conservative strongholds like Texas, Kansas and Iowa.

Voter backlash against Trump's Republican party has been driven in large part by economic woes, blamed on his administration's tariffs and war with Iran, according to exit polls.

Colom, a local prosecutor, used the national mood to his advantage, highlighting his opponent's support for Trump's tariffs and giving her the nickname "Cindy High Prices".

The Democrat campaigned aggressively, visiting all of Mississippi's 82 counties.

Hyde-Smith's campaign assailed Colom as a puppet of billionaire liberal donor George Soros, whose family has helped fund the Democrat's political campaigns.

The Republican was criticised for a lack of public appearances.
