For battling blemishes and signs of aging, there are few better at-home tools.

AS SOMEONE RAISED on slasher horror movies, I was always going to gravitate towards LED face masks. But while their creepy Jason Voorhees-at-a-rave look is what initially grabbed my attention, the skincare benefits are what sold me on the products. LED face masks combine the firming power of anti-aging products, the depuffing of eye cream, the energizing boost of moisturizers, and the breakout-fighting strength of acne washes—making them one of the most versatile grooming tools out there.

LED masks use specific wavelengths of light to penetrate the skin and trigger cellular changes, according to dermatologist Dr. Hannah Kopelman, MD. This can include stimulating collagen production to reduce fine lines and support firmer skin, as well as treating acne-causing bacteria and calming inflammation to promote a clearer complexion. Some devices offer all of these benefits, while others are laser-focused (get it?) on just one or two. The key is to know what each is capable of and what service you’re looking for.

Dermatologist Dr. Brian Toy, MD, warns that the market is flooded with LED face masks that vary greatly in quality and effectiveness, so it’s best to start your search as informed as possible. To temper the confusion, I went ahead and used myself, along with fellow Men’s Health writers and editors, as guinea pigs. This way, you’ll know what works and what is merely an overpriced Halloween mask.

When shopping for an LED face mask, choosing a device that’s FDA-cleared is essential, says Dr. Kopelman. This ensures the product meets fundamental safety standards for consumer use. To be clear, however, FDA-cleared is not the same as FDA-approved—the latter involves extensive clinical trials and rigorous testing. Currently, no LED face masks on the market have achieved FDA approval.

For now, stick with FDA-cleared options, which means the device is proven to be as safe and effective as another legally marketed product. You might also see terms like FDA-registered or FDA-listed, but these simply indicate that the manufacturer has notified the FDA of the product's existence—there’s no review or evaluation involved. Because of that uncertainty, you won’t see any of those on this list.

Based on this intel, I, along with other Men’s Health writers and editors, put several masks to the test, evaluating each device’s effectiveness, ease of use, and how comfortable it was to wear.

As many LED face masks do not offer immediate results, we used each one for at least one month to ensure enough time was given to gain any possible benefits. Following this extensive research and testing, I landed on these seven LED face masks as the best for men in 2026.

The dermatologists I spoke to for this story were clear on this: you should be cleaning your device after every use. If you don’t, bacteria, oil, and product residue can build up on the surface and transfer back onto your skin, causing irritation or breakouts, which may ironically be what you were hoping to minimize by using an LED mask in the first place.

Dr. Kopelman says that LED masks should always be used on freshly cleansed, dry skin to ensure the light penetrates as effectively as possible. After the session, apply your serums and moisturizers—doing it beforehand can block the light and reduce its benefits. A hydrating serum like hyaluronic acid or a peptide-rich cream works especially well to complement the collagen boost from the treatment, Dr. Kopelman says.

According to Dr. Toy, be sure to skip any products that might react to light. For example, because retinol and vitamin C serums are photosensitizing, they’re an absolute no-go before any LED treatment. Dr. Chacon adds that reflective products like heavy oils or sunscreens should also be avoided during use as they can scatter the light and disrupt the device’s effectiveness.
