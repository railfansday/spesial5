Gen Z Embraces Crypto While Most Americans Call It a 'Side Quest'

For many young Americans, crypto is coming before the 401(k).

Some 21% of Gen Z adults own cryptocurrency, compared with 16% who hold an employer-sponsored retirement plan like a 401(k), according to a survey released Thursday by SoFi Technologies.

That’s seven times the rate of baby boomers, just 3% of whom own crypto. Crypto was Gen Z’s most widely held investment in the survey, ahead of stocks, bonds ,and real estate.

The online survey of 2,064 U.S. adults was conducted by SoFi and YouGov from Oct. 15 to 28. SoFi, a nationally chartered bank that sells crypto alongside checking and investment accounts, developed the report with Culture Co-op.

Gen Z respondents described crypto less as a gamble than a strategy. They were nearly three times as likely as the general population to call it one of the best ways to build future wealth, 21% vs. 8%, and 61% said they see it as a financial advantage unique to their generation.

Only 20% of Gen Z crypto owners said they bought in because it “sounded interesting,” compared with 34% of crypto investors overall. Diversification (32%) and easier international payments (29%) ranked higher.

Part of the gap reflects age. Many respondents in their late teens and 20s don’t yet have access to an employer retirement plan.

Across the broader population, the picture is far cooler. Only 13% of Americans own crypto, and 62% are firm skeptics. Three in four say crypto feels intimidating to get into, and 77% agreed with the statement, “Cryptocurrency feels like a financial side quest that I don’t have the time or energy for.”

Even owners feel out of their depth: 82% of current crypto investors said they should know more about it than they do, and 11% admitted knowing little to nothing.

“Nobody feels the gaps in their financial education more sharply than someone who has just bought Bitcoin,” said Natalie Brunell, host of the Coin Stories podcast and a contributor to SoFi’s crypto newsletter.

The findings echo a recent survey conducted by Ipsos for the DeFi Education Fund, which found that 22% of Americans have ever owned or used crypto but only 13% used it in the past year.

SoFi argues that putting crypto in the same app as banking is the key to wider adoption. Half of respondents open to crypto said having their primary bank offer it would be a tipping point, and 63% of current investors said they’d trade more if they could do it at their bank.

SoFi has been building out that model, launching a tokenized deposit that pays 4.4% and moves like a stablecoin, as well as a bank-issued stablecoin running on Polygon’s payments infrastructure.

The survey also challenges the crypto “tech bro” stereotype. Black and Hispanic respondents own crypto at more than twice the rate of white respondents, 22% to 9%. Miami led the 10 cities surveyed at 20% ownership, while San Francisco matched the national average at 13%.
