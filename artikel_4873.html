A Fantastical Perch Above Toulon’s Old Port, Where Mermaids Meet Mediterranean Light

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Though perhaps not well known outside France, Vincent Darré is one of Paris’s most familiar figures—always on the scene, day and night. The impish designer rarely just enters a room; he bounces in, impeccably dressed, with an infectious laugh and beautiful manners. At home in the worlds of fashion, society, art, and design, he is welcome at every table, always ready with a clever remark, a kind word, and no shortage of creative energy.

From his early days in fashion, which included stints at Moschino and Ungaro and a close, yearslong collaboration with none other than Karl Lagerfeld, Darré has followed a singular path, turning his creative energies to furniture and interiors with the opening of his own studio, Maison Darré, in 2008. A pair of recently completed Paris hotels—L’Aventure, at the foot of the Arc de Triomphe, and Hôtel Le Magnifique, in the heart of Montparnasse—are propelling him onto the design scene in a bigger way than ever. His work has long encompassed scenography and ephemeral installations, among them crowd-pleasing turns at AD Intérieurs, the erstwhile annual showcase hosted by AD France.

In the formal salon, a pair of carved wood armchairs with inlaid stone backs have seat cushions of a Dedar fabric. Darré-designed objects include his Calder table, Minos rug, and Lyre mirror. He also created the trompe-l’oeil pilasters topped with 18th-century gilded wooden capitals. Late-19th-century French lantern; 1940s Italian ceramic dolphin table.

In the guest bedroom, Darré sits on an 18th-century gilded wood bench upholstered in velvet by Métaphores. A Darré-painted mural frames the 18th century Italian headboard upholstered in Tony Duquette’s Xanadu fabric for Jim Thompson. The bedcover and cushions are from Darré’s collection for Monoprix.

Darré was born in Paris, in the 14th, and went to school there as a boy. When his parents separated in the 1970s, his father—a sociologist—moved to the Luberon, and Vincent went with him; not long after, his mother, unwilling to live far from her son, also moved south, to Montpellier. “This allowed me to be Parisian, but also one who knew other places,” he says. It’s a telling detail: the South of France has informed his eye ever since, with its particular sense of light, water, and ease. Though he first went to school for fashion, his real love was drawing, as it had been since childhood. “Sometimes people are surprised,” he notes, “that a finished work of mine is almost always exactly like I had drawn it.”

After a series of Paris apartments over the years, Darré has recently acquired a getaway in Toulon, the Mediterranean port city. The attraction was immediate. He had been invited to preside over the jury of Design Parade, the summer design festival held in Toulon and Hyères, and the city seized him the moment he arrived. “Once I got to the airport, in 2017, I immediately felt I was home. Chez moi! I had never experienced that feeling before.” He kept an eye on the listings and, after a few false starts, found exactly what he wanted.

In the entrance, Darré’s Vase Soleil stands atop a 1940s Venetian console table against a wall decorated with trompe l’oeil caryatids.

A 19th-century rattan sofa upholstered in Jean Lurçat’s Sirenes fabric by Pierre Frey.

Overlooking the Toulon harbor, the primary bedroom’s office area features several Darré designs including his Orphée sconce, Singez-moi table lamp, the carved and lacquered wood desk, a Vertebré chair cushioned in a fabric by Pierre Le-Tan, and a Column stool.

Lion head mascarons flank the doorway into the primary bedroom. 1970s plaster Buddha lamps with Darré-painted shades stand atop 17th-century French consoles. A 1960s plaster seahorse is displayed in the fireplace.

The apartment occupies the top floor of a little three-story house overlooking the fishing port—a compact 860 square feet with stunning views of the sea. It was already lovely when he moved in, all white, with nothing architectural to be done: the perfect backdrop for Darré and his pencils and paints. He brought many of his favorite things—collections, objects, and furniture gathered over the years, added to since at the Drouot auction house and the little brocantes he loves to haunt. Furnishing was, he says, a gradual process, bringing in pieces little by little and slowly making the space his own over time.

Darré’s Crocodile bench stands in the primary bedroom. Two-Faced lamps with hand-painted shades atop Pas Gigogne tables flank the bed, and his Poisson bookcase is at left. The plaster chandelier is by Mathieu Challières, and the plaster unicorns are from a set designed by Jean Cocteau.

A metal figure given to Darré by Ines de la Fressange stands next to his Singez-moi lamp on the office area desk.

Darré’s Tabou shelf in the primary bedroom

Darré on his Column stool with a 19th-century gilt-wood mirror from a Paris flea market

Darré speaks often of his love for Toulon, and of Jean Cocteau, who adored this coast and painted frescoes nearby. “I like the sweetness of life here,” he says. “Nothing has really changed since the 1950s. It’s the Provence of another era.” Every little square has its fountain; everyone is kind. It’s a military port, but its douceur de vie is what holds him.

The apartment represents Vincent Darré to a T: not an unadorned surface in sight, a home full of memories, tributes, and joy. “This has been one of my dreams, to have a house on the water,” he says. “My place in Paris is exactly the same, though not on the water. But both are little suspended palaces. I create my own follies.” From the painted columns flanking the windows—among his very first purchases, which have followed him from home to home—to the mermaids and sea creatures and the tented bed, everything belongs to the fantastical world of Darré. As one of his teachers observed early on, you could recognize his hand from the very start.

A close-up of Darré’s ceramic Two-Faced lamp

The dining area features a red lacquer table and chairs with Darré’s Dolphin lamp in the corner.

That world is entirely his own. He draws on a lineage of great fantasists—Charles de Beistegui, Henri Samuel, Jean Cocteau, Christian Bérard, Tony Duquette—yet the result, for all its jumble, is rigorously coherent. His flourish is always present. As the actress Isabelle Adjani once told him, to enter one of his interiors is to step into one of his drawings. Which is exactly what he wants.

For all the bounce in his step, Darré is no one’s fool. He knows we live in difficult times, and likes the idea of adding a flourish of fantasy to reality. “I have my little world—a dream universe, but one you can live in.” He loves to dance, and even took tap-dancing lessons as a teenager, all the better to channel Fred Astaire or Gene Kelly. “Life is a film, and we are the actors,” he says. “That’s why I always dress the way I do.” Lights, camera, action—Darré is always ready.
