Eurostar Premier (First Class) Review: Better Than Flying, But Hardly A Luxury Experience

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

My first Eurostar Premier journey took me from Brussels to Amsterdam Schiphol Airport, where I connected to a KLM flight to Los Angeles. The train itself felt older than I expected, but the quiet carriage and productive journey made it a pleasant alternative to flying.

I sometimes enjoy trying a new travel experience without researching every detail beforehand. There is a certain irony in that, considering how much of this blog is devoted to helping others know what to expect. Eurostar was one of those experiences. I had long wanted to try it, and an award booking provided the opportunity.

I booked Brussels to Los Angeles through Air France/KLM Flying Blue for 60,000 miles in business class (plus $478.13 in taxes and fees). The itinerary included a Eurostar train from Brussels-Midi to Amsterdam Schiphol, followed by KLM World Business Class to Los Angeles. I could have booked a departure from Brussels Airport for the same mileage price, but chose the rail connection to try something different.

The cabin description was confusing. My ticket indicated business class, while KLM’s Air&Rail information referred to a “premium” experience for those connecting in business class. Eurostar’s actual classes are Standard, Plus, and Premier. At check-in, the agent confirmed I was booked into Premier.

Rather than use the actual Eurostar check-in desk, Brussels-Midi has an Air France-KLM ticketing office and rail check-in counter located in the rear of the station.

My train departed at 5:53 am and I showed up at 5:15 am to check-in…where I found a massive line and only one agent on duty. One passenger in the front of the line was traveling on SNCF to Paris but had a ticketing issue which the lone agent was trying to resolve. But time was ticking and the line grew longer.

Finally at 5:30 am I walked to the front of the line to ask for assistance, noting my train departed in 20 minutes. I did not mean to be the ugly American, but it was just what the agent needed to ask the frazzled traveler from Côte d’Ivoire (I noticed her passport) to step aside. Within a few minutes she had cleared the line and I was on my way upstairs to the rail platform.

There was a Eurostar lounge in Brussels, but it did not open until 6:00 am during my visit, so I could not use it…though I would have access to it had my train departed an hour later.  Not that I ended up having a lot of time to spare, but it woudld have been nice to have a cup of coffee there before the train departed.

Stepping onboard, I found my seat in Coach 11, the very last cabin designated as Premier.

Stepping onboard felt more like entering an older TGV first class carriage than the sleek, modern interior I had imagined. Old is, not necessarily a bad thing, but the cabin showed wear. My footrest was broken and the cabin just look tattered and well past its prime.

In the very rear of the cabin was a compartment with seating of four that remained empty throughout the journey.

The bigger advantage on this particular departure was how quiet it was: there was only one other passenger in my carriage and even as we neared Amsterdam there was never more than five in the cabin.

A nearly empty premium cabin is not something you can count in Premier, but it made this an especially pleasant setting to work ahead of a long flight in which I did not want to open my computer.

The lavatories in the Premier cabin were very typical European train toilets with metal toilets, a small sink, but fairly spacious.

We departed on time, and an attendant welcomed us and offered me breakfast menu shortly after pushback, along with a packaged moist towelette.

Sandwich IGP Bayonne ham, AOP Comté cheese, with dill picked cucumbers

Fruit Kampot pepper and verbena apples, gala melon, watermelon

Both were accompanied by yogurt, a croissant, juice, and coffee. I chose the fruit, though I would have liked both (and my request for both was politely declined).

It was a modest meal, but I enjoyed it. The fruit was good and the coffee was decent, as was the croissant.

I preferred this breakfast to the catering KLM serves on its short intra-European flights and the presentation was far nicer. It did not need to be elaborate to be satisfying at that hour and I had a second cup of coffee and some cold pressed berry juice prior to arrival at AMS.

The onboard internet worked well, allowing me to be productive during the ride. For this trip, I valued that more than a particularly luxurious cabin, though I was initially concerned that I would be capped at 500 MB of data.

Thankfully, even though I quickly exceeded the cap the internet continued working…

In-seat power was also available so I arrived in AMS with my devices fully charged.

The attendant’s welcome and breakfast service provided a pleasant start to the morning. Sure, it would have been nice to have the sandwich as well (I was still hungry and the cabin was empty), but she denied my request in a very kind way!

At one point I took a walk through the train to check out the Café Car which offers food and drinks for purchase.

Looking at the menu, I’d say I would have been fine with a falafel wrap and Lavazza coffee had I been booked in coach.

My destination was Schiphol Airport, not Amsterdam Centraa, whcih thankfully meant no backtracking a regional train to the airport.

Next came my KLM lounge visit and onward flight to Los Angeles.

For my itinerary, I was happy to have Premier included in the Flying Blue award.

Whether I would pay a substantial premium for it separately is another question and the answer is probably not. Eurostar lists extra-spacious seating and lounge access among Premier’s benefits, but I could not use the Brussels lounge on this departure.

It is also important to compare the correct route. Eurostar’s class descriptions distinguish between London services and continental routes. For example, the included meal in Premier on a London-route would have included a multi-course hot meal.

For a separate purchase, I would compare the actual fare difference and the benefits available for the particular trip (like the lounge), probably paying no more than €50 for the upgrade on this particular route.

Eurostar Premier was less polished than I expected, but I enjoyed the journey. The worn interior and broken footrest deserved attention, while breakfast and reliable internet helped make the ride worthwhile. For this Brussels–Schiphol connection, I was glad I chose the train. It gave me a quiet, productive start to the trip home and left me interested in trying Eurostar again.
