Treyarch Lays Off Call of Duty Zombies Mastermind Craig Houston After 20 Years

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Contributor at DualShockers, former Esports Insider DACH content lead, former Escapist Magazine Germany content lead, software engineering background.

Craig Houston, the writer behind much of Call of Duty’s Zombies storyline, has been laid off by Treyarch after around 20 years at the studio.

As the development of Black Ops 7 winds down, Treyarch has indicated that they'll make big changes to Zombies in their future Call of Duty games.

He shared the news himself on Friday via X and LinkedIn, and I have to say, it’s a rather graceful message considering the circumstances. Houston said he was proud and privileged to have worked alongside so many talented people, many of whom will remain friends for life, before thanking fans for their enthusiasm, support and kind words over the years. On X, he posted it alongside a video celebrating 30 years of Treyarch, featuring footage from many of the games he worked on, which makes the whole thing feel a little more bittersweet.

He didn’t go into the reasons, but it appears to be part of the huge Xbox restructuring announced earlier this year, which is set to affect 3,200 jobs. Treyarch sits under Activision Blizzard, which in turn is owned by Xbox, so it isn’t a huge surprise that the cuts have reached the studio. Still, seeing a name like his on the list stings, especially if you’ve spent any amount of time shooting zombies with your friends at 2 in the morning.

For those who don’t know him by name, and I suspect that’s quite a few people, Houston has been at Treyarch since 2006. His first Call of Duty there was Call of Duty 3, where he worked as a video editor, which is a pretty humble start for someone who would go on to shape one of the series’ biggest modes. From World at War in 2008 onwards, he was the main writer for Zombies, whilst also writing for Treyarch’s campaigns.

He is credited as a writer and designer on Black Ops 1 through 4, and later as part of the design and creative development teams on the likes of Cold War, Black Ops 6 and Black Ops 7. Interestingly, he isn’t credited on this year’s Modern Warfare 4.

Zombies obviously wasn’t a one-man show, with Jimmy Zielinski, Jason Blundell and Kevin Drew all being a big part of how the mode evolved. Houston’s writing is what a lot of fans point to when they talk about why they fell in love with the characters, though. He also wrote the jingles for the original perks introduced in Verrückt, which is one of my favourite details in all of this. If you’ve ever caught yourself singing along to a vending machine whilst a horde closes in on you, that one’s on him.

The response since Friday has been lovely to see. Former Treyarch developer Tony Flame replied to Houston’s post calling him a legend and saying it was an honour to work with him, adding that he created a truly special experience that will live on for millions of fans. Over on Reddit, one fan described him as a great guy, noting that you can tell how much he loves the story and characters he writes simply by how excited he gets when talking about them in promo material.

Many fans argue that Houston never quite got the recognition he deserved. In the wake of this announcement, many people admitted they hadn’t really been aware of him until after Black Ops 3, despite him having been there since the very beginning, and I think that’s true for a lot of us players. We tend to know the directors and the faces from the trailers, whilst the people writing the lines we end up quoting for years stay in the background. Zombies a mode spawned out of passion, and it’s hard to see someone fundamentally involved with that leave.

Truth be told, this is the part of the Xbox restructuring that I find hardest to get my head around. Twenty years of knowing a story inside out, including every timeline, every character and every twist, isn’t something a studio can simply hire back in. Of course, I don’t know the reasoning behind this particular decision, and Houston himself hasn’t said a bad word about Treyarch. It is, however, a reminder that these layoff numbers are made up of people, some of whom have been shaping the games we love for the last two decades.

Houston hasn’t said what he plans to do next. Whatever it ends up being, I hope it involves writing, and ideally a jingle or two.
