Kylie Cantrall Wants to Be Your Modern-Day Carrie Bradshaw

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

After all, the rising star has a single named after the Sex and the City character.

Last night at the Moxy Downtown Los Angeles, rising pop star Kylie Cantrall teased her next project. “It’s gaggy. It’s giving 21,” she joked onstage during a small, intimate performance. “I actually haven’t written any ballads so far. I’m healed.”

Cantrall, who is perhaps best known for portraying Red, daughter of the Queen of Hearts, in the Descendants film series, has had a banner year. The L.A. native just wrapped a nationwide tour, appeared on Fox’s The Masked Singer, is set to perform at iHeart’s Jingle Ball, and dropped her EP Valley Girl Problems. A standout on the release is “Carrie Bradshaw,” which examines the similarities between Cantrall and the iconic Sex and the City character, played by Sarah Jessica Parker. “I think my fashion sense has been influenced by Carrie,” Cantrall says. “Also, she’s just kind of a mess, but she keeps it together on the outside. That’s honestly how I feel about myself. The choices are questionable, sometimes. Choices in dating, questionable.”

At the show, Cantrall played four tracks, including “Carrie Bradshaw.” She took pictures with her fans, signed vinyls, and, for one fan, even signed their chest. The experience is a part of a larger effort to use music as a connector at the Moxy, which has also hosted Ayra Starr at its London property. The brand unites artists with their fans in private settings and gives travelers the opportunity to relax with a musical backdrop. “I’m so excited to be here at Moxy,” Cantrall told ELLE before taking the stage. “I love doing these little underplays and acoustic shows, because it’s a different side of myself that I feel like people don’t usually get to see.”

Ahead of her performance, Cantrall sat down with ELLE to discuss Valley Girl Problems, her acoustic set, and new music on the horizon.

Full production, dancers, props. It’s always very razzle-dazzle, Kylie the performer, but I feel like this is more Kylie just the singer and songwriter. I like getting to showcase that aspect.

Honestly, I get nervous for it all. I think I put so much thought and care into every show, so there’s always a pressure I put on myself. But I feel like once I get out there and see the cuties’ faces, it feels chill. I know so many of them. We have more of a friendship bond.

It’s everything. What’s really special about me and my fan base is that we’ve really grown up together, because a lot of them are my age now. They had maybe started watching me from YouTube or from my first Disney show when I was 13 and have stayed along with my journey. It’s crazy. Our bond is kind of familial at this point.

I think they’re quite different. It’s so different playing a character. Of course, with someone like Red, I’m very grateful I got to play a character that I felt like had a lot of me in it already. But, Kylie the musician, Kylie the artist, that’s completely, 100 percent raw me. I have so much freedom to take risks and experiment. With Valley Girl, I feel like that perfectly encapsulated the person I was a couple months ago when I wrote it. I’ve changed so much since then. I’m just excited for the next project that’s going to reflect who I am now as a 21-year-old. I want all of my bodies of work to reflect the person I am in whatever chapter that is. It’s like a time machine. It’s very powerful in that way.

No. I listen to it so much when I’m creating it, and obviously it’s just nonstop before it comes out. The minute it comes out, it feels like I have no control over it whatsoever. It’s not mine. It’s for everyone else. I have to have that kind of separation. If I am still listening to it after it’s been out, I’ll start to pick things apart and be like, “Oh, I wish I did that. Oh, I could have done this differently.” Then, you have people’s criticism or opinions also in your brain when you’re listening to it, which can taint the way you view it. I was so proud when I finished it. I knew how I felt once I submitted it to the label and was like, “Okay, this is the final product. Here you go.” I just want to remain in that place.

It’s always the vocal fry—holding the ends of my words for a long time. Definitely the way I talk. I guess hiking and matcha are also kind of a worldwide thing at this point, but for some reason growing up I always felt like that’s such a Valley thing. In the Valley, it’s either matcha cafes or Pilates studios and fruit carts on the corner, and that kind of encapsulates the Valley for me.

So excited. At the top of the year, I wrote in my little manifestation journal, I’m going to perform at Jingle Ball this holiday. It was before it was even a topic of conversation. I just threw it out there because I was like, I really want to do Jingle Ball just to be a part of a lineup. I’ve watched every single Jingle Ball since I was seven years old. The fact that I’ll get to share the stage with Katseye, Olivia [Rodrigo], the Jonas Brothers, Adéla, these are all artists that I look up to. I’m just excited to be in that orbit.

I was probably enjoying my days off for the first two days when I got back home from tour. Then, the third day I was like, “Okay, I’m ready to get back out.” I’m already thinking in my head what the new set list is going to be with the new songs that I’m recording at home right now and getting ready for the next album. My wheels are turning.

New York had a crazy energy. The New Yorkers know how to pull up and show some love. And, of course, L.A. I got to end the tour here, so there was something that felt very full circle about getting to come home and celebrate with my friends and family that all came out to the show, and a lot of my L.A. fans who are here tonight at the Moxy. It’s also just so fun anytime I get to do anything in L.A. It’s all my closest fans, and all my first pop-ups I had ever done were always here in L.A. A lot of my foundational fans that have been with me, they’re all here.

I’m just taking a lot of inspiration from my life right now, as a 21-year-old who’s going out and just kind of embracing this next chapter. It feels like I have all the freedom in the world. I’m just excited to kind of show people maybe a little more of a wilder side. A little more messy, a little less curated. Kylie, outside of the work I’ve done or the image, the narrative, whatever that may be. Here’s who I am right now. I like to have fun. I like to go out. I’m going through it all the time, but I get to pour it into my music, whether it’s just discovering my kind of identity in this era, dating, or all the things I’m learning about myself. It’s all going to be poured into this record.

I’m a Cancer. I’m very in touch with my emotions at all times, and music is my chance to fully just, without censoring anything, explore that.
