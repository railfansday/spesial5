How We’d Spec It: 2027 BMW 3-Series

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

You can now configure your ideal version of BMW’s new sports sedan, and that’s exactly what we did.

The BMW 3-series is the poster child for sports sedans, so it’s always exciting when a new generation debuts. The gas-powered Neue Klasse 3-series that can be configured on BMW’s consumer site is available with a 255-hp turbocharged 2.0 four-cylinder (330) or a 437-hp turbocharged 3.0-liter straight-six (M350). In rear-wheel-drive form, the 330 starts at $51,250, while the all-wheel-drive-only M350 starts at $67,250. Regardless of powertrain, both Bimmers offer a wide selection of paint colors, wheel designs, and stand-alone options. That level of personalization makes the new 2027 BMW 3-series a perfect candidate for our How We’d Spec It series.

I find BMW’s Neue Klasse era to be a bit overwhelming, so I kept things simple with my 3-series. Though the 437-hp six-cylinder M350 is tempting, the 255-hp four-cylinder 330 offers enough power for my needs, and I like that it’s available in a standard rear-wheel-drive configuration. Given that the new 3 is larger and heavier than ever, I’ll consider this to be the “superleggera” spec.

I’m a sucker for a green-over-brown combination, so I chose Eucalyptus Green Metallic paint for $650 and the Castanea reddish-brown faux-leather interior (free of charge). The available staggered summer tires with 20-inch wheels are a pretty good deal at $600, and I opted for the space-saver spare for $250. My total comes out to a reasonable $52,750, which is barely more than the average cost of a new car these days. –Joey Capparella

Like Joey, I went with the 330 model, only I went with the $2000 xDrive (AWD) system and the M Sport appearance package. I also painted it Ocean Wave Blue Metallic and threw on the two-tone 20-inch 1142i wheels. Here’s the point in the build that I got a little disappointed with BMW’s offerings. The only way to get genuine leather upholstery is if you go with a drab, all-black interior theme. Yuck. I settled for the faux-leather “Veganza” upholstery because I wanted the reddish-brown Castanea color. Next, I added the Comfort package, which is an easy yes at $1350. It includes a power trunk lid, a heated steering wheel, ventilated front seats, and remote start.

I had planned to skip the $1900 Technology package because, with the Neue Klasse’s new Panoramic Vision screen, I see no need for a head-up display. I also don’t care about light-up grille elements. But I was bummed to find out that the Technology package is the only way to get the Harman/Kardon stereo, so I begrudgingly added it to my shopping cart. All in, I’m just under $60,000—$59,900 to be precise—for a well-optioned 3-series that I think will be considered a “good spec” 10 years down the road. –Drew Dorian

I’m not sold on the Neue Klasse look. On the new 3-series, I mostly don’t like how the high beltline and smooth side panels create a thick-bodied design that is visually pretty boring. Also, this one’s not an EV, so can I have normal door handles, please? I do like what I’ve dubbed the “stretched-kidney” grille, and I like how they flow into the contoured hood. But enough about my personal styling preferences; my ideal 3-series—at least until the M3 arrives—is the 330, not because it costs less than the M350, but because it offers rear-wheel drive.

I also think it looks best in Black Sapphire Metallic ($650), and I deleted the rear badge to trick some people into thinking it’s not the base model. For $1200, the 20-inch M Trigon machine-faced wheels with staggered high-performance summer tires were an easy choice. The same goes for the $2000 M Sport package, M-tuned adaptive suspension ($550), and spare tire ($250 is worth the peace of mind of not getting stranded with an inflator kit).

Inside, I made the risky decision to pick the Digital White theme. I don’t have kids, but I’d definitely be enforcing a strict “No Flamin’ Hot Cheetos” rule. I also swapped the standard steering wheel for the $150 M-branded version that forgoes the strange top spoke, as if the weirdly angled center touchscreen wasn’t trippy enough. I’d pay $1300 for massaging and ventilated front seats alone, but the Comfort package also includes a heated steering wheel, a power trunk lid, and remote start. Score! I debated not spending $1900 on the Technology package, but it’s the only way to get the Harman/Kardon sound system, and I’m a sucker for a good stereo. The illuminated grille is a nice bonus, but the head-up display is pointless. Altogether, my RWD 330 totaled $59,300. –Eric Stafford

While I appreciate my colleagues’ restraint, I’m not going to pass on the 437 horsepower available in the M350 xDrive. I don’t really love any of the paint options available this year, but for $650, the Le Castellet Blue Metallic is a solid color. All of the wheel options are too busy for my taste, but the 20-inch M Trigon Bicolor 1141M wheels with the staggered high-performance tires suit my tastes the best.

The rich Castanea faux-leather interior similarly suited my fancy, and for an additional $0, what’s not to love? I skipped the M Technology package and the rear seat comfort features since the M350 comes pretty well appointed as is. Total, my spec comes to $69,100—certainly not cheap, but not horrible considering the performance. –Jack Fitzgerald
