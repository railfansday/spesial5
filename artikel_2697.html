Questions by position as Eagles return from 2026 bye week

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Questions by position as Eagles return from 2026 bye week originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

This has been a miserable Eagles season and there are still eight games left.

The Eagles (2-7) finally had a chance to regroup during their Week 10 bye week but remaining the basement of the NFC East. They're not technically out of the division race, although The Athletic gives them less than a 1% chance to make the playoffs.

But there are still plenty of reasons to watch the rest of the season even if it ends in early January. With that in mind, here's a question about each position group as the Eagles return from their bye week:

Quarterback: How much better will Joe Milton get?

The Eagles put Joe Milton in a tough spot against the Giants in Week 9. While it's understandable that they hoped his mobility would help behind a struggling offensive line, Milton arrived just three weeks earlier and was still learning the offense. Heck, after the loss Milton said he told his OL that he vowed to learn their verbiage up front so they could communicate better. Maybe we see that in the second half of the season. Even though the Eagles lost to the Giants, Milton did some good things. He showed off his strong arm and used his legs at time. It'll be interesting to see if he can prove to at least be a backup-level QB going into 2027.

Running back: Will a healthy Saquon still be elite?

It was a frustrating first half of the season for Saquon Barkley, who has dealt with a stinger, a hamstring injury and a back injury. He was limited to just 69 carries in seven games for 296 yards and 3 touchdowns. Over the two games before the bye, Barkley had 26 carries for just 52 yards and 2 touchdowns. So his high yards-per-carry average plummeted to 4.3. But at times this season, Barkley has looked elite. The Eagles made it clear that they weren't going to trade Saquon because they think he's going to be a big part of the team after this season. We'll see if he got healthier during the bye week and if he can get back to his elite status.

Receiver: Is Makai Lemon going to continue to improve?

The Eagles were without DeVonta Smith (hamstring) and Dontayvion Wicks (ankle) for the three games leading into the bye week. The Eagles went 0-for-3 in those games and clearly missed their top two wideouts. But the silver lining in their absences was that rookie receiver Makai Lemon had the best performances of his young career:

Week 9: 6 for 61Week 8: 6 for 57Week 7: 7 for 67, 1 TD

The Eagles drafted Lemon with the No. 20 overall pick and after a slow start, he has started to produce. The Eagles are obviously going to get Smith and Wicks back in the lineup but they have to find a balance too — because it's so important that Lemon continues to grow.

Tight end: Will Eli Stowers get a bigger role?

The Eagles held on to Dallas Goedert at the trade deadline so he's obviously going to be their TE1 the rest of this season. But they should make a concerted effort to see more of second-round pick Eli Stowers. Stowers missed the first six games of the season on IR before making his debut against the Cowboys. But in three games, Stowers has played a grand total of just 32 snaps.

Here's how the TE snaps have looked the last three games:

Dallas Goedert: 160Johnny Mundt: 37Eli Stowers: 32Grant Calcaterra: 27

Earlier in the season, Mundt was playing way more, so it is at least a good sign that the coaching staff has backed off his usage. And it's obvious that Stowers isn't a finished product, especially as a blocker, but the Eagles should at least give him more opportunities.

The other side question for the tight end position: Will Zach Ertz get a chance to break the record?

Ertz is stuck on the practice squad with 582 receptions as an Eagle, just seven behind Harold Carmichael (589), who holds the record. In this lost season, it would be a real shame if the Eagles can't get Ertz to the 53-man roster for a run at that record.

Offensive line: Can Markel Bell and Drew Kendall prove themselves?

The Eagles' offensive line has struggled this season and was at one point in a complete state of flux. But we know as long as Drew Kendall and Markel Bell are healthy, they're going to be the starters at center and right tackle for the rest of the season. Kendall is a second-year player and Bell is a rookie, so if they play well there's a good chance they'll be long-term starters. Kendall got into the lineup early this season and has probably been playing at a higher level than they expected from Cam Jurgens, who was eventually traded to Baltimore. But Kendall hasn't proven that he's a long-term solution. And Bell has definitely had some struggles since taking over for Lane Johnson, who abruptly retired earlier in the season. Bell (6-9, 346) has given up 5 sacks and 19 pressures this season, per PFF. He has had some shaky moments, but some good ones too.

Defensive tackle: How much will the Eagles miss Jordan Davis?

The Eagles made a huge trade a week ago, when they dealt Jordan Davis to the Jacksonville Jaguars for a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick. That was tremendous value for Davis, but they will miss him on the field and in the locker room. Without Davis, the Eagles will need someone to play more early downs and will need other players to take up his overall snaps. We might see more Byron Young and Ty Robinson down the stretch. It'll also be worth watching how Jalen Carter handles this move. Carter and Davis are close and have been playing together for many years, dating back to their days at Georgia.

And will Moro Ojomo now get an extension? The Eagles haven't given out in-season extensions in recent years but that's because GM Howie Roseman was worried about dividing a locker room of a potential Super Bowl contender. That is definitely not a concern this season. And the Eagles should work to keep Ojomo beyond this season.

Edge rusher: Will Jonathan Greenard heat up?

Not only did the Eagles give up picks to acquire Jonathan Greenard, they also gave him a four-year deal worth $98 million. Greenard's time with the Eagles got off to a rough start when he suffered a pec injury before training camp that kept him out all summer and the beginning of the season. When he was finally back on the field and healthy for the first time, he then suffered an ankle injury he has had trouble getting over. Greenard has played in just six games this year and has just one sack and three QB hits. But if he's able to have a strong second half, that would be good news because his contract dictates that he's very likely going to be on the team for at least next season.

Linebacker: Will Vic Fangio keep the rotation?

The Eagles held on to Zack Baun at the trade deadline so we know he is going to be one of their starters the rest of the season. The Eagles had gone to a rotation at the other spot between Jihaad Campbell and Jeremiah Trotter Jr. But because Campbell missed the last couple games before the bye, Trotter got all the snaps. While it seems like Trotter has simply outplayed Campbell this season, there's a really good chance the Eagles go back to that rotation. They used a first-round pick on Campbell last year and really need to see what they have in him. Campbell has been disappointing through the first year and a half of his NFL career but he plays a position that can take some time to develop and the Eagles shouldn't give up on him yet. While Howie Roseman will probably want to see his first-round pick succeed, we'll have to see if Vic Fangio keeps giving Campbell playing time.

Cornerback: Can Cooper DeJean turn his season around?

One of the strangest storylines of the 2026 season has been the regression of Cooper DeJean. This guy was a key starter in the Super Bowl season as a rookie and was an All-Pro in Year 2. But Year 3 has been a struggle for DeJean and it goes beyond the snaps where he's playing from safety depth. He has also struggled in his normal nickel cornerback spot, which was extremely surprising. While Quinyon Mitchell has been excellent again in Year 3, DeJean has dropped off. Both are eligible for contract extensions after this season and Mitchell seems like a no-brainer. He's one of the best outside cornerbacks in the NFL and even though he doesn't have a ton of interceptions, it has been easy to see how good he is. But it might be tougher to pay DeJean if he doesn't show improvement. There was already the issue of how to value nickel corners and now DeJean is struggling.

Safety: Is Drew Mukuba a long-term starter?

The Eagles used a second-round pick on Drew Mukuba last year and it's time to see if he's really going to be a long-term starter in Philly. There's no doubt that Mukuba has some play-making ability but his play has been inconsistent in coverage and has struggled as a tackler. Maybe it really hurt his play when the Eagles allowed Reed Blankenship to leave in free agency. But Mukuba was given a full-time starting job this season and hasn't been good enough to think he's going to be in that spot for the next several years.

Specialists: Will Braden Mann be team MVP?

It's never a good sign when the bright spot of a season is the punter, but Braden Mann has been awesome. The 28-year-old punter has already had 49 punts with an average of 47.5 yards and a net average of 41.3 yards per punt. He had that monster 86-yard punt before the bye. But the stat that stands out most is his punts inside the 20. Mann has already downed 22 punts inside the 20, which accounts for 44.9% of his punts this season. He has just two touchbacks.

Coaching: Will Nick Sirianni survive this season?

This season has been ugly. The Eagles won their first two games and have now lost seven straight. Sure, they lost Jalen Hurts for the season and have dealt with countless other injuries, so it has been a weird year. Maybe Jeffrey Lurie looks at the situation and gives Sirianni a mulligan. After all, Sirianni has been to the playoffs five times, to the Super Bowl twice and won it all to cap the 2024 season. That wasn't that long ago. But we have also seen Lurie fire a Super Bowl-winning head coach and explain that it wasn't because Doug Pederson deserved to get fired; he moved on in 2021 because he thought it was in the best interest of the franchise. So Lurie has to look at this situation and determine if the best thing for the franchise is to run it back with Sirianni in 2027. And if he does that, who are his coordinators? It's hard to imagine Sirianni getting fired before the end of the season, so those are all fair questions for January. In the interim, let's just see if the players continue to fight for Sirianni and his future.
