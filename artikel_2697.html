10 Bears Takes: An 8-2 start, but the true test is next on a short week

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Following Sunday afternoon's 21-13 victory, the Chicago Bears find themselves at 8-2 heading into a critical mid-season divisional matchup on Thanksgiving morning. For a second straight week, it was not pretty. Turnovers have continued to be an issue, and overall, they simply didn't look like a team that 11.5 points should have favored. There's no questioning this team's legitimacy as a contender, but with the heat about to turn up on their remaining seven games, better play must follow. With a short week ahead, we'll blast through our 10 Bears Takes before we turn the page to the Detroit Lions on Thanksgiving.

1. Following their 21-13 victory over the New Orleans Saints, the Bears are riding high at 8-2 with two critical tests ahead. How will things look on a short week after back-to-back uninspiring performances with first place on the line?

Coming into the game, the Bears closed as 11.5-point favorites. That line alone probably gave fans a sense of confidence, but to be fair, this has been a team that hasn't trailed in five weeks. That said, this was the second straight week it was clear the Bears were the better team, yet they still failed to pull away from an inferior opponent.

Some might see this mindset as nitpicky, and while that might be fair, this team has much higher expectations than simply a winning season and a playoff berth. When they traded for Jeffery Simmons (who we will talk about more in a few), they were signaling to the entire league (and fan base) that they believe they can win a Super Bowl now and in the near future. Now, don't get me wrong– no team will play consistently good football over a 17-game season. Looking around the league, there are plenty of very good teams that don't perform the way most expect a few times a year.

That said, at least for the Bears, they've had two straight games where they aren't playing well on offense, and more importantly, they are turning the ball over too much. The good news is that their defense is playing at a level where they can afford these growing pains while still winning games. That said, the offensive performances have dipped over the last few weeks, and that's something they'll need to figure out heading into the final seven games of the season.

For as hot as the Bears have been (five wins in a row), the Lions are even hotter. Not only have they won six straight, but they've outscored their opponents by a +76 margin. We'll dive more into the specific matchup later, but simply put, Chicago must play better football if they expect to come away with a win on Thursday afternoon. Both teams appeared relatively healthy coming out of Sunday's games, so the war of attrition might not be as big a factor as it will be for other teams playing on a short week.

Either way, the Bears must take a step forward and play cleaner football games over the next two weeks if they plan to win one (or both). The good news: Typically, the best teams in the league play at their highest level when the spotlight is the brightest.

2. The Bears have yet to trail in a game since Caleb Williams returned from his hamstring injury five games ago.

The last time the Bears had this type of streak? 1985.

No matter how you cut it, that's impressive. Williams, like the entire offense, wasn't at his best for a second straight game. Despite that, they did just enough to support a defense that keeps moving in the right direction. Admittedly, the offense hasn't looked quite the same over the last two weeks, and part of that has been the passing game. Williams' accuracy hasn't been quite as good, and the offensive line isn't holding up like they should in pass protection. Not having a consistent run game has been a notable issue, but even with all that in mind, they haven't trailed in a game in close to 50 days.

We all know what the offense is capable of, and maybe playing higher-level competition will bring out the best in them. At the beginning of the season, they were tasked with scoring 30-plus each game, or they might lose because the defense was playing so poorly. Week 5's blowout loss at Lambeau Field was a prime example of both units playing at their absolute worst. Since then, the defense has been playing at a Top 10 level, and even when the offense has failed to score 30 points, they have built big leads and held on to them.

Overall, the Bears haven't been playing their best football, but in some ways, they've been playing the best complementary brand of football we've seen all year. Winning in different ways will serve them well down the stretch, and unlike last year, they haven't had to conjure every ounce of good luck to finish off games. There's still plenty to improve upon over these last seven contests, but sitting at 8-2 thanks to a five-game winning streak is about as good a start that any team could hope to mount. Their streak of not trailing in games will be heavily tested on Thursday against a Lions team that has rarely trailed during their six-game winning streak.

3. Jeffery Simmons came up with a hat trick on Sunday. The last time a Bears defensive tackle had three or more sacks? It's been over a decade.

Before Sunday, the last time a Bears defensive tackle registered three or more sacks was when Jeremiah Ratliff did it on October 19th in 2014. To be exact, it's been 12 years, one month, and three days. Similar to many of the markers the Bears have been matching this season, it's been a while.

When general manager Ryan Poles traded two first-rounders (plus) for Simmons, the hope was that he would have enough of an impact to change the trajectory of their defense for years to come. Even in the most optimistic outlook, I'm not sure anyone in the organization could have foreseen the magnitude of impact the 29-year-old would have over his first three games with his new team.

Since coming over from Tennessee, Simmons has five sacks, six tackles for loss, and 14 total tackles. Not only is he producing in the box score, but his presence has allowed his linemates around him to rack up numbers. The number of double-teams Simmons has seen has opened things up, especially for the likes of Montez Sweat and Austin Booker on the edge. In the three games with Simmons in Chicago, the defense has 13 sacks and a much higher pressure rate, while allowing only four points on most of those disruptions. Not only that, but the defense is giving up an average of 18 points per game and just under 304 yards per game.

Opposing teams' rushing efficiency has dipped, and quarterbacks are seeing much more pressure. Simply put, it's proving the theory that all the Bears needed was a game-wrecker who would move everyone's role down a notch and allow them to benefit from it. Since Simmons' arrival in Week 8, the Bears have improved in EPA/dropback (-.24), pressure rate without blitzing (+2.6), sack rate (+1.8), and rushing success rate allowed (-6.7).

The secondary still leaves some to be desired, but the Bears are still well on pace to have the best third-down percentage in NFL history. Assuming they can get Kyler Gordon back at some point over the next few weeks, and maybe even Coby Bryant at some point in December, it's not hard to see a path to a true Top 7-10 defense. For now, we'll just enjoy the impact Simmons has made and how much that will help them in the next few years.

4. Even with Kyle Monangai back in action against the Saints, the running game's struggles persisted– why it might be time to look inward, namely at center Garrett Bradbury.

It was good to see Monangai back in the lineup, and although he didn't take a ton of snaps in his first game back in a month and a half, it was somewhat to be expected, especially with a short week ahead. That said, the overall efficiency of their running backs has continued to dip.

On Sunday, the team's top two running backs averaged just 3.29 yards per carry. Veteran Salvon Ahmed helped things out with 33 yards on six carries, but overall, their run game continues to leave a lot to be desired. In recent weeks, Williams has started to run more, which will help inflate those numbers, but the holes in the designed running game still aren't there.

Chicago has been well-known for running behind their excellent right side, and when they break runs outside in more outside/wide zone looks, they have found more success. The issue is that the middle of their offensive line has been getting blown up lately. While it's impossible to put all of that on one player, Bradbury's lack of athleticism, especially when pulling, has been extremely notable. At this point, nobody should expect Johnson to abandon ship with the plan of having a veteran center for his third-year quarterback. Still, it's impossible not to wonder what this group (and rushing attack) would look like with a stronger and more athletic center like Logan Jones.

If Johnson does stick with Bradbury, as should be expected, he has his work cut out for him to figure out this running game. At their best, this has always been a run-first unit. It's impossible to do that, especially when your top two running backs are averaging such a low number per carry against one of the league's worst rushing defenses. With a tough slate of games on the horizon starting on Thursday, the Bears will be in scramble mode trying to figure out how to get this running game going to support their quarterback. If there were one potentially major concern over the final seven games of the regular season, this would be it.

5. Sometimes the way your luck breaks is a part of the game. For Caleb Williams, his five interceptions over the last two games look worse on paper than they do in reality.

Since coming into the league in 2024, Williams has thrown the lowest percentage of interceptions in the league. In fact, he's had multiple 200-plus-attempt stretches without one. That's why these last two weeks have been very out of the norm for a quarterback who has never regularly turned the ball over.

Against Tampa Bay, his four-interception performance was the most he's ever had in a single game, at any level. Of those interceptions, one was a blatantly bad decision, two were poor throwaways that didn't get out of bounds, and one was sheer bad luck. His one interception against the Saints was, again, purely bad luck. He had Luther Burden III wide open for what would have likely been a big gain over the middle, but instead it was batted straight up in the air and fell directly into the arms of linebacker Danny Stutsman.

Williams' six interceptions through seven games is one-off his career high of seven set last season in 17 full games. He will likely set a new career high in that category, but ultimately, some of it has been sheer bad luck. My bigger concern is that when he has thrown interceptions this year, he tends to start playing more tentatively. Even on Sunday, once he threw his lone interception, most of his throws after were shorter attempts.

It's completely understandable for any quarterback to have second thoughts after throwing an interception, but for Williams, it has seemed a little more personal at times. Some of that is because he's never been a quarterback who has had them come in bunches, but with how much the run game has struggled over the last month and a half, his ability to take it in stride, move on, and be the same aggressive passer needs to remain.

Typically, once a quarterback gets out of a game without an interception, the waters calm. With the Lions and Jaguars on the schedule as the next two opponents, Williams will have to work extra hard to stay away from additional turnovers. As a team, they are not on the right side of the margin, and even after a two-takeaway game against the Saints, the Bears came out of the game as a net-even because of Williams' interception and Swift's fourth-quarter fumble. As a unit, the offense needs to be much better in this department down the stretch.

6. First-round rookie Dillon Thieneman has continued to flash on tape. Even with some rookie mistakes mixed in, he's quickly becoming one of the defense's more versatile pieces.

Every rookie is going to experience the ups-and-downs of a first year in the NFL, and Thieneman hasn't been the exception. That said, he's had a lot put on his plate, especially after the injury to Coby Bryant, and for the most part, he has looked the part. More importantly, defensive coordinator Dennis Allen has been using him all over the defense. He can play deep as the cover safety. He can play closer to the line of scrimmage to defend intermediate routes. Hell, he can even play at depth and still has enough speed to come down and fill gaps as a run defender.

Despite being a rookie, Allen has not shied away from filling up his plate. To a certain extent, that's what you'd expect from a first-rounder, regardless of position. The reality of most of those situations typically play out much differently, though.

What's even funnier is that Pro Football Focus has not graded Thieneman too highly, but Pro Football Network has him as a Top 5-7 player at his position, depending on the week. On Sunday, he had an impressive run fill coming down from his safety spot, and then a few plays later, he read Tyler Shough well and was in a great position for his second interception of the season.

It's worth noting that his running mate, Cam Lewis, has also played well since moving back to safety full time, but in comparison to what they expected to get from Bryant, especially in coverage, Thieneman has been heavily relied upon. That has shown up a few times on deep balls, especially with their cornerbacks struggling on most throws downfield.

Fourth-round rookie Malik Muhammad has played well when he's on the field, but on Sunday, it appeared that they were ramping him back up for a bigger workload on Thursday. Realistically, there's no clear-cut way to fix their cornerback room this season, but with a big role for Muhammad and a healthy Gordon, the hope is they can hold up a little better on deep balls. For now, we'll just continue to enjoy Thieneman's rookie season, and even if he doesn't get the credit he deserves in Year 1, I have little doubt that he isn't due for a long and successful career. The early returns from the rookie have been largely impressive.

7. The last time the Bears started 9-2 was 2006, which also happened to be their last Super Bowl appearance. With a win on Thanksgiving, they have a chance to repeat that performance.

Speaking of things they haven't done in a while: With one more win this season, this will be the first time that the Bears have had back-to-back winning seasons since 2005-2006. In many ways, all roads are pointing back to Lovie Smith's best season in Chicago, and another potential Super Bowl appearance.

Sometimes the way that expectations change can help shape a team's season. Last year, it was a pleasant surprise when the Bears went 11-5 and got into the playoffs. Although it would be a stretch to call 2025 a "down year" for the NFC North, it is, in comparison, to what we've seen so far this year. No matter what is happening inside their own division, Chicago has quickly proven that last season was not a fluke and that they are here to stay. As we saw in Detroit once Dan Campbell finally turned things around, it's much easier to sustain success when you have a young roster that keeps getting better.

Another key part of all this is the coaching staff, which starts with Johnson. The second-year head coach has completely flipped the culture within the building and established winning expectations in Year 1. It wasn't very long ago that Bears fans were tracking draft position heading into Thanksgiving, and now they are dreaming about a potential trip to the Super Bowl. Even in their not-so-great performances that still resulted in wins, the high level of coaching has been on display. I'd also argue that this is the most well-rounded team the Bears have fielded since the days of Mike Ditka. The offense is probably still the better unit, but the overall turnaround from the defense is nothing to overlook.Getting a win on Thursday will not be an easy task, but for the first time in a while, it's hard not to feel like the Bears can beat anyone, as long as they are playing their brand of football. Buckle in, Bears fans; the road to the playoffs is just heating up, and Thanksgiving will be their first true test in the second half of the season.

8. A look at the NFC's playoff picture heading into Week 12: More status quo with the bottom of the Wild Card mostly on bye weeks.

With all but two of the 16 teams at 10 games played with seven remaining, the NFC playoff picture has taken shape. In short, the NFC North and West are loaded with talent, while the East, and especially the South, will field a playoff team, even if they don't "deserve" it over another team that loses out in the Wild Card race.

Looking at the four divisions, it would be a complete shock if all three Wild Card winners don't come from a combination of the North and West. Heading into Week 12, all four teams in the North would be in the playoffs, but with plenty of divisional matchups remaining, there could be a situation where the process of elimination will take out one, if not two, of those teams. On the flip side, a few key divisional games remain in the West, including a pair of games over three weeks featuring the Rams and Seahawks.

In the East, there are two, maybe three potential contenders. The Giants came up with a statement win at home against the Jaguars, while the Cowboys convincingly took care of business against the lowly Titans. The Commanders will face the Bengals on Monday Night Football, and barring an upset, they'll be three games back and will also lose the tie-breaker due to divisional record. Finally, in the South, the Panthers have a two-game lead with seven games remaining, and with any consistency, they could run away with the division over the next few weeks. Many still believe in the Buccaneers, but after another disappointing loss to an NFC North opponent, their ship is taking in too much water to stay afloat.

With seven weeks remaining, here's how the NFC playoff picture looks heading into Week 12:

Detroit Lions (8-2) *Wins tie-breaker over Bears due to divisional record

Green Bay Packers (6-4) *Wins tie-breaker over Rams and Seahawks

On the outside looking in: Seattle Seahawks (6-4), Los Angeles Rams (6-4), Dallas Cowboys (5-6)

San Francisco's convincing win against the Vikings in Mexico City puts them in a great position to ride into the playoffs as the NFC's top seed. A trio of head-to-head matchups will help further shape the playoff picture in Week 12, including the Packers at Rams on Thanksgiving Eve, followed by the Bears at Lions on Thanksgiving. Finally, the 49ers will host the Seahawks in a critical game inside the West. The loser of the Packers-Rams game will be in a tough spot. Not only will they have lost the head-to-head tie-breaker, but they'd drop to one game over .500 with just six games remaining. The blow could be softened by a Seahawks loss. The winner of the Bears at Lions will yield a division leader in sole possession of first place for at least a week.

Looking at the East, the Cowboys appear to be the only real competition remaining for the Giants, but an upset on Monday night could drastically help the Commanders. Even with a nice win against the Steelers, it's hard to imagine the Eagles making a serious push with Joe Milton at quarterback. Finally, the South is the Panthers at .500, followed by the three others at 3-7. Although there are still multiple head-to-head matchups yet to be planned within the division, it's hard to imagine anyone catching the Panthers without a collapse from Carolina.

Overall, I would argue that there are 10 teams fighting for seven playoff spots. Seeding will work itself out over the next month-plus, but unless the Cowboys win out, it's hard to see them competing for anything more than an outside chance of winning the East. The heat is turning up,, and this is the time of year when contenders separate themselves from pretenders.

9. NFC North Lookaround: The Lions roll against the Tampa Bay Buccaneers, the Vikings were blown out in Mexico City in front of a national audience, and the Packers watched the gap between them and the division leaders grow during their Week 11 bye.

Did you think the NFC North was going to loosen up any time soon? Think again.

Week 11 brought three outside divisional matchups and, unfortunately for the Packers, the gap between them and first place grew (twofold). We'll start in Detroit, where the Lions allowed the Buccaneers to hang around until the fourth quarter. The 31-21 final score was strangely indicative of how the overall game went, but how each team got there was the odd part. Tampa Bay never led in the game, but had two separate opportunities to do so. One was early in the first quarter when they failed to convert on a 4th-and-1 from the Lions' 9. The second was an 80-yard touchdown run from Bucky Irving that was called back on a holding call that didn't impact the overall play at all. The Lions offense took a bit to get going, but in the end, they totaled just under 400 yards of offense and were 7-of-11 on third-down opportunities. Overall, the Lions looked like the better team, and their defense continues to look night and day compared to the first month of the season. So far, the losses of Jack Campbell and Aidan Hutchinson have not slowed this group down.

Unfortunately for the Vikings, things did not go as planned in Mexico City. What started off as a close game quickly got out of hand. The 49ers were the first team to hang 30-plus points on Minnesota's elite defense, and the Vikings offense struggled to move the ball for most of the night. Some will say that altitude played a big part in Sunday night's game, but outside of the first quarter, the two teams did not look like they belonged on the same field. The good news for the Vikings is that their final seven games are relatively favorable, outside of matchups against the Lions and Bears. After the loss to San Francisco, their chances of earning the No. 1 seed are slim, but they are still in a good position to not only make the playoffs, but contend for the division.

For the Packers, they were the last of the four teams in the North to "enjoy" their bye week. Not only did it come this late in the season, but with a "Thanksgiving Eve" game on the horizon, their bye week felt more like a Thursday-night-to-Sunday-day turnaround than a true 14-day bye week. Despite their 6-4 record, they appear to be getting healthy at the right time. Through a few early practices, it appears that Micah Parsons will make his season debut on Wednesday, and other key players will be back in action. Green Bay's margin for error is smaller than most teams, but make no mistake about it: They are far from out of the playoff race, even if the division is looking less and less likely.

10. Week 12 Look Ahead: The Detroit Lions on Thanksgiving with a pair of teams that are on a combined 11-game winning streak.

Without question, the Bears' biggest game of the season comes on a short week to kick off the three-game Thanksgiving slate. The Bears have been hot; the Lions have been hotter. The Bears' offense has been good; the Lions' offense has been better. Ultimately, no matter how each of their seasons started, they'll both head into Detroit at 8-2 and have a chance to take over sole possession of first place with six games remaining.

In many ways, the two teams have taken similar roads to getting to the same record. Both defenses were complete disasters at the start of the season, and each unit has drastically improved over the last six-plus weeks. Heading down the stretch, the Lions have a considerably easier schedule, but Thursday's matchup will be the first of two over five weeks. Heading into Week 12, the Lions are a perfect 2-0 inside the division and hold a 5-1 conference record. On the other side, the Bears are 0-2 within the division, but hold a similar conference record at 6-2.

This will be Ben Johnson's third time going against the Lions since taking his first-ever head coaching job, and he'll also be looking for his first victory against his former team. The two teams know each other well, and although it's a divisional game, the ties run deeper than that. For the Bears to have a real shot at the division, Thursday's game is almost a must-have. Each team is well-positioned for a playoff spot, but with Minnesota playing at a high level and the Packers in desperation mode, fielding a 3-3 record in the division must start in Week 12. This game is far from a death sentence for either team, but it's a game that is likely to go a long way in determining how the NFC North will pan out over the final two months of the season.
