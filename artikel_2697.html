Kyle Hamilton: Ravens are "disgracing" their "standards"

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Ravens wore all black on Monday night. It looked like they were going to a funeral.

The Ravens’ season feels over after losing their fifth in a row, following a 4-1 start. And while it’s easy to blame the free fall on the multiple injuries suffered by quarterback Lamar Jackson, new quarterback Jacoby Brissett managed to beat the Chargers with the Cardinals in Week 1. On Monday night, the Ravens were flat, lifeless, and uninspired in prime time against the previously 0-8 Chargers.

The end result was a second straight home loss in a standalone, nationally televised game.

“Right now, we are disgracing those standards, as leaders, as players and as a team,” safety Kyle Hamilton said after the game, via Jamison Hensley of ESPN. “Whatever flak we get is warranted. . . . All the talking that’s been going on is all just talk. If you don’t go out there and do what you’re supposed to do, stop the run, nobody really cares, and stuff like this just stops mattering.”

Hamilton understands that performances like this can have consequences.

“At some point, you have to just decide about your manhood and what you want out of your job,” Hamilton said. “This is our job. We can all get fired; that’s the reality of it. If we don’t do better, then we get fired.”

Hamilton has enhanced job security from his contract. Hamilton’s $17 million salary for 2027 is already fully guaranteed. And his $17 million salary for 2028 becomes fully guaranteed in early 2027.

Still, others who lack that type of financial certainty could be facing a one-way ticket out of Baltimore.

Compounding the latest loss is the fact that it came against Jim Harbaugh, the brother of longtime Ravens coach John Harbaugh and the mentor of current Ravens coach Jesse Minter. One of the explanations for the Chargers starting 0-8 was the departure of Minter, the Chargers’ defensive coordinator during Jim Harbaugh’s first two years in L.A. and his final two years at Michigan.

The embarrassing loss to the NFL’s last winless team creates an unfortunate bookend to John Harbaugh’s 18-year tenure in Baltimore. The year before he arrived, the Ravens started 4-2 before going on a losing streak that included losing to the league’s last winless team at home — the 0-13 Dolphins.

That got Brian Billick fired. And while Minter is a long way from being one and done, the Ravens need to stop the bleeding. Quickly.

The schedule isn’t favorable. They have a short-week trip to Charlotte and a visit to Houston before a Week 13 bye. The stretch run includes a home game against the Buccaneers followed by a four-game tour of the AFC North: at Steelers, Browns, at Bengals on New Year’s Eve, Steelers.

In John Harbaugh’s 18 seasons, the Ravens had fewer than eight wins only once (they were 5-11 in 2015). It’s going to be very hard to get to eight wins in 2026.

They may not get past five. Hell, they may not get to five.

Things can change quickly in the NFL. But the more the losses pile up, the harder it gets to dig out of the ever-deepening hole. For now, they’re fading into the rear-view mirror in a division with three teams that have six or more wins.

If the Ravens lose on Sunday, good luck finding the focus and the discipline during Thanksgiving week to be fully prepared for a meeting with the Texans that could be a de facto elimination game.

Even if the Ravens’ all-black uniforms was a fitting demonstration of the fact that, for 2026, they’re already dead.
