Week in Review: Spurs’ young core spreads the wealth as win streak extends to 12

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Welcome to Week in Review: a Monday feature that looks back at the week that was for the San Antonio Spurs, takes a look at the week ahead, and more. Enjoy!

Week 3: The Spurs extended their winning streak to 8 with three blowout wins — two expected ones at home against the Jazz and in Sacramento, plus a somewhat surprising one at Portland — sandwiched around an all-time classic against the Mavericks. The Spurs got out to a 19-point lead before blowing it in all-too-familiar fashion, but Victor Wembanyama channeled his inner Wilt Chamberlain to help gut out the one-point win with a historic 53-point, 24-rebound, 8-block effort.

Battling off some demons in what is often a challenging venue, the Spurs went into Phoenix and demoralized another middling opponent trying to find its footing. They countered a 32-point outing from Devin Booker with 40-11-8 showing from Wemby, who even had Suns fans cheering after it became clear that the Spurs had this one in the bag, and they decided to just enjoy the show.

It wasn't their prettiest showing, but the Spurs weathered an insanely hot start from the Bulls — who at one point in the second quarter were shooting 83% from both the field and three — before bouncing back and with a dominant second half to overcome a 22-point deficit for their 10th straight win.

123-85 win vs. Golden State Warriors (NBA Emirates Cup)

An anticipated match-up lost its luster after vintage Steph Curry was ruled out with an oblique tear, but the Spurs didn't let that get in their way. Motivated by being home after a four-game road trip, they put on a show for their home crowd by starting on a franchise-record 18-0 run and never looked back. Stephon Castle finally broke out of his offensive slump with 32 points and 6 assist. The Warriors were so demoralized that Draymond Green went back to his shtick of trying (but somehow failing) to get himself ejected, and Rookie of the Year candidate Yaxel Lendeborg admitted to being so "f***ed up mentally" after the game that he…cut his hair? (I get it after a series of bad games — Wemby and Keldon Johnson did it last January — but one game?)

This one had all the hallmarks of a trap game, and it almost worked: right back out on the road, altitude, Sunday matinee, 11-game winning streak vs. 11-game losing streak, and an opponent so shorthanded they have nothing to lose. For the second time this season, the Spurs went into Utah and had to work for the win. Mo Bamba gave them the Curry performance they missed the game before by going 6-6 from three (his first six attempts of the season!), but again, the Spurs weathered the storm and countered with a career-high 31 points from Dylan Harper. As weird as it is to say about a 1-13 team that they swept, I'm glad the Spurs are done with the Jazz for this season.

John Schuhmann, NBA.com — 1 (last week: 1)

OffRtg: 119.2 (3) DefRtg: 106.6 (3) NetRtg: +12.6 (2)

The Spurs were down 22 points in Chicago on Wednesday, and they trailed by three with a little more than three minutes left in Utah over the weekend. But their winning streak is now at 12 games, and they remain at the top of the Western Conference.

Victor Wembanyama played just 25 minutes in Phoenix last Monday. But in those 25 minutes, he had 40 points, 11 rebounds and eight blocks. With another 12 blocks (and only four fouls) over the last three games, he has 2.3 times as many blocks (60) as fouls (26), which would easily be the highest ratio for a player with at least 50 blocks in the 54 seasons that they've been counted, topping his own mark of 1.7 blocks per foul two seasons ago.

According to Synergy tracking, the Spurs now lead the league in both transition points per game (29.4) and transition points allowed per game (16.4). Over their last five games, they've outscored their opponents by 116 points (155-39) in transition.

Coming up: One other team ranks in the top six in both transition points and transition points allowed per game, and it's the Pistons, who the Spurs will host on Tuesday. After that, they'll face three teams – the Clippers, Pelicans and Kings – in the bottom four in the West.

Law Murray, The Athletic — 1 (last week: 1)

Make that three straight weeks of wins for the defending Western Conference champions. Harris, San Antonio's top offseason acquisition, still hasn't debuted. But the Spurs are the only team in the league that is top three on both offense and defense, and they won games with Devin Vassell and De'Aaron Fox missing at least one start apiece.

Kurt Helin, NBC Sports — 1 (last week: 2)

San Antonio has a net rating of +12.6, just off Oklahoma City's historic rating (+12.7) in 2024-25 season, when the Thunder won 68 games and the NBA title. The Spurs are looking to become the first team to lose in the NBA Finals and then return to that big stage since the Cavaliers in 2018 — and they are off to a fast start. San Antonio has a top-three offensive and defensive rating. A lot of that has been because of Victor Wembanyama's impressive start to the season, but the guard lineups — with De'Aaron Fox, Dylan Harper, Stephon Castle and Devin Vassell, in whatever combination — are thriving as well. Check them out for yourselves Tuesday night on Coast 2 Coast on NBC and Peacock, starting at 8 ET.

Brett Siegel, Clutch Points — 1 (last week: 2)

Four of the Spurs' last five wins have come by at least 15 points. The only single-digit wins in this span were against the Chicago Bulls and Utah Jazz. In that game against the Bulls, San Antonio trailed by 22 points before outscoring the Bulls 68-49 in the second half.

The Spurs have now won 12 straight games since losing back-to-back games to begin the 2026-27 season. Each of the last three times the Spurs won at least 12 straight games, they went on to win at least 61 games.

San Antonio ranks inside the top 3 in both offensive and defensive rating.

Tues. 11/17 vs. Detroit Pistons (7:00 PM CT)

Thurs. 11/19 vs. Los Angeles Clippers (7:00 PM CT)

Sat. 11/21 vs. New Orleans Pelicans (7:00 PM CT)

Prediction: 3-0 — After playing five of their last six games on the road, the Spurs finally get to stay home all week for what feels like an extended homestand (at least by this season's standards). They're still in search of their first "signature" win of the season and look like they could use a challenge, and Tuesday's match-up against the Pistons may provide just that (although, like the Portland and Golden State games, it's tainted by no Jalen Duren). After that, the Clippers and Pelicans shouldn't present much of a challenge and may not even provide them with a chance to go up against two disgruntled former Spurs, with Kawhi Leonard hightailing it back to Toronto and Dejounte Murray possibly sitting out after demanding a trade. (I get the feeling Pop's fatherly love for these two shielded them from a lot of bad press in SA.)
