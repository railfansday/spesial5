A.O.T 3 Final Preview: Possibly Shaping Up To Be One of the Best Anime Adaptation Video Games

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

This is DualShockers' preview of Attack on Titan 3, the penultimate entry in the AOT Video Game series developed by Omega Force and published by Koei Tecmo.

The Attack on Titan series by Hajime Isayama is genuinely one of the most important works for fans worldwide. The show has sparked a massive debate over its controversial ending, but putting that aside, you can’t argue with its overall impact: brilliant storytelling and action that fans will remember for ages.

DualShockers played A.O.T. 3 at a preview event, slashing Titans and humans alike, with the entirety of Attack on Titan's story being adapted.

And now, eight years after the last game in the franchise, Koei Tecmo’s Omega Force is back with Attack on Titan 3. The full game launches next month in December on the PS5, Xbox Series X and S, PC, and Nintendo Switch 2. At DualShockers, we had the opportunity to check out a final preview build, and I must say, as a veteran fan of both the series and the games, they’re truly going out swinging on this one.

One of the biggest changes in Attack on Titan 3 is how it approaches its storytelling. You see, back then there was this evident problem that Omega Force could only adapt parts of the story since the anime was still airing, which meant that you weren’t going to get the full package, or at least, where the manga had reached in contrast to the show.

With AOT3, it still remains true to the storytelling presentation of the prior entries. But rather than simply allowing you to relive the anime's biggest moments through the familiar cast, this game once again introduces an original character for you to create and customize.

Our OC character suffers a devastating loss after their previous squad is wiped out during a Titan encounter beyond the walls. So, after developing a fear of Titans from the incident, they're removed from active duty and assigned to instruct the 104th Cadet Corps. This gives you a chance to interact with familiar faces such as Eren, Armin, and Mikasa before eventually graduating from the instructor role and taking command of your own reconnaissance squad.

This approach is very promising because it gives Omega Force a chance to explore parts of the Attack on Titan universe that don't necessarily receive the same attention in the anime. You're not another member of Eren's generation experiencing everything alongside him. But instead, you're an experienced soldier watching these young cadets grow into the people who will eventually shape humanity's fate.

There are also original story events and character interactions designed to expand on the relationships between your character and the existing cast, which is a very nice gesture for us longtime fans if we want something new from this whole experience, especially for characters that didn’t get a ton of screen time in the main show or earlier seasons.

DualShockers spoke to A.O.T. 3 producer Hideo Suzuki about the new Titans, balancing grittiness with arcade action, and why there's no romance.

Of course, none of this hard work over the past 8 years would matter nearly as much without the one thing that makes AOT such an exciting property to adapt into a video game: its combat.

Fortunately, Omega Force hasn't simply brought back the same Omni-Directional Mobility (ODM) system from Attack on Titan 2 and called it a day. The studio has made several adjustments to how you can move around the battlefield, giving the familiar grappling mechanics far more flexibility while also adding new layers of strategy to Titan encounters.

Previously, much of the combat revolved around attaching your ODM anchors to a Titan, reeling yourself towards it, and timing your attack to strike its nape. While that foundation remains intact, AOT3 has generally put more emphasis on identifying weaknesses before committing to an attack. The gameplay rhythm now feels more like a hybrid between a strategic action game.

You can now target individual body parts, allowing you to damage limbs, interrupt attacks, and temporarily immobilize Titans before going for the finishing blow. This is especially important because these guys are much more aggressive, with new attack patterns that require you to watch their movements rather than blindly rush toward their weak spots.

Other noticeably uplifting additions include the maneuverability upgrades and defensive options. You can attach yourself to certain surfaces to reposition, use boost jumps to gain additional height, and even dive or slide along the ground to avoid incoming attacks, which is very useful when dealing with larger Titans during their one-on-one boss fight encounters where you have to stun them for the one-hit kill from their nape area.

As for defensive mechanics, you can use well-timed dodges and parries to defend against incoming attacks. Combat Arts also add another layer of progression, giving you access to powerful unlockable special attacks as you increase your military rank. It's an ambitious evolution of the combat from the prior entries, one that I’m very excited to experience to a greater extent with the full release.

The overall game’s flow looks extremely ambitious, and what I mean is that the game has three working gears in motion for the full package here: interacting with your squadmates at the base, the main story missions that progress the campaign, and your Exterior missions.

The latter is one of the highlights, as it lets you freely explore the world with your squad in tow to hunt down more Titans and fulfil different requests from NPCs you come across. It’s essentially a way to give you more gameplay freedom instead of railroading you from start to finish to showcase the anime's main story, and doing Exterior missions also lets you reap additional rewards and useful upgrade materials, which you definitely need and can’t skip since they let you enhance your equipment.

And speaking of gameplay freedom, you can put any doubts to rest about whether you can jump into this game without prior experience with how the first two games worked. There are a bunch of accessibility and control scheme options that let any player easily enjoy the story, but if you want total control, you can turn off any options that automate the combat mechanics, which I honestly prefer since you’re way more vulnerable to mistakes that way, and it makes the game more punishing.

Still, I can promise you that Attack on Titan 3 is genuinely worth having on your radar for the December releases. With how much I got to sample here, I can’t wait to see how more of the character interactions play out, how the talent points fully work with everything in place, along with how the noteworthy battles in the show play out here, and that includes the whole sections of Marley from Season 4 and beyond. It is a lot to look forward to on my side, but I’ll gladly march into battle head-on with my goth Scout Corps Captain.
