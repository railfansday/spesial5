GLOBAL MANUFACTURERS ACCELERATE STOCKPILING TO MITIGATE FUTURE INFLATION AND SHORTAGES: GEP GLOBAL SUPPLY CHAIN VOLATILITY INDEX

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

CLARK, N.J., Nov. 11, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- GEP Global Supply Chain Volatility Index, a leading indicator tracking demand conditions, shortages, transportation costs, inventories and backlogs, based on a monthly survey of 27,000 businesses, showed that manufacturers accelerated precautionary stockpiling in October, reflecting growing concerns about future inflation and supply disruptions.

The increase was particularly pronounced in North America, where the safety stockpiling indicator more than doubled from September, and Europe, where inventory building rebounded sharply.

This signals that manufacturers are purchasing additional materials to protect production against higher future costs and potential shortages, even as the pace of global purchasing growth moderates.

While this inventory buildup may support manufacturing activity in the near term, it also ties up working capital and creates a risk of weaker purchasing demand once companies have accumulated sufficient stock.

Meanwhile, global transportation costs increased for the second consecutive month, adding to already high inflationary pressures here.

Despite these concerns, global manufacturing activity remained resilient. Purchasing demand continued to grow, supported by sustained strength across Asia and improving European demand. However, overall global supply chain pressures eased in October as material and labor shortages declined.

"Manufacturers are increasingly concerned about what's ahead, particularly inflation and the risk of renewed supply disruptions," said John Piatek, Vice President, Consulting, GEP. "They're responding by building larger inventory buffers to protect production and manage future costs.  The concern is what happens next. This additional purchasing is supporting manufacturing activity today, but it also ties up working capital. Once inventories are built, purchasing could slow, creating a drag on manufacturing and the wider economy in 2027."
