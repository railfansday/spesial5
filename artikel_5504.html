Builder credit conditions tighten for 19th straight quarter

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

NAHB’s Q3 2026 AD&C Financing survey shows credit for land acquisition, development and construction tightened for the 19th consecutive quarter, mirroring Federal Reserve loan officer data. Contract rates moved only modestly and were offset by lower points, keeping effective borrowing costs elevated across most AD&C categories. Builders are increasingly using construction-to-permanent loans tied to end buyers, with 32% of respondents using them and, for those, 58% of homes financed this way. Persistent tight AD&C lending threatens to slow lot development and new-home supply, reinforcing structural housing shortages even as the mortgage market shows its own signs of stress.  AI Summary

Credit conditions for residential land acquisition, development and construction (AD&C) loans tightened again in the third quarter of 2026, extending a nearly five-year stretch of more restrictive lending for homebuilders, according to a National Association of Home Builders (NAHB) survey.

NAHB’s quarterly AD&C Financing survey showed a net easing index of -15.3 in Q3, meaning more builders and developers reported tighter credit than easier credit. It marks the 19th consecutive quarter of net tightening for AD&C borrowers, NAHB economist Paul Emrath wrote in an analysis published Nov. 13.

The findings closely track the Federal Reserve’s October survey of senior loan officers. NAHB reported that the Fed’s net easing index for residential AD&C loans came in at -15.5 in the third quarter. Aside from modest lender-reported easing in the second quarter of 2026, both lenders and builders have indicated that AD&C credit has been tightening steadily since early 2022. The Fed’s index has been in negative territory in 18 of the past 19 quarters.

For homebuilders and land developers, those readings underscore that bank appetite for risk in residential construction remains weak even as broader housing demand depends on new supply.

While credit availability continued to contract, AD&C financing costs showed only modest quarter-to-quarter movement in the third quarter, with no clear trend toward either higher or lower overall borrowing costs.

For builders, the message is that even where nominal rates tick lower, the all-in cost of capital for lots and vertical construction remains high by historical standards. Combined with tighter underwriting, that environment can slow lot pipeline development, constrain spec building and push more risk and cost back onto builders’ balance sheets.

NAHB’s third-quarter survey also highlighted increased use of construction-to-permanent, or one-time-close, loans tied directly to the end buyer.

Among survey respondents who built single-family homes in the third quarter, 32% said they financed at least some production with construction-to-permanent loans to the ultimate homebuyer. On average, 58% of the homes those respondents built were financed this way, NAHB reported.

Those structures can help builders reduce their direct exposure to AD&C credit by shifting some financing to retail mortgage channels earlier in the process. They can also appeal to buyers looking to lock both construction and permanent financing terms in a single transaction, an important hedge in a volatile rate environment.

NAHB noted that market stress in residential mortgages – including a recent drop in applications and rising delinquencies – has drawn significant attention. But the group argued that constrained AD&C financing is an underappreciated bottleneck for adding new supply, especially in affordable and entry-level segments.

Because AD&C loans finance land pipelines, infrastructure and vertical construction, persistently tight lending standards can limit lot development, delay projects and reduce the number of homes that reach the for-sale market. That dynamic can amplify the impact of low existing-home inventory and keep upward pressure on home prices and rents.

For builders, developers and their lenders, the survey results suggest that planning assumptions for 2027 should continue to factor in restricted credit availability, elevated effective borrowing costs and a need for diversified capital sources, including joint ventures, private capital and greater use of buyer-based construction-to-perm structures.
