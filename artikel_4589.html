Tesla Cybertruck Production in Texas Is Ending, Elon Musk Says, Truck Seems Likely to Be Killed Off

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The truck’s lead engineer is already referring to it in the past tense on X.

Tesla is “winding down” production of the Cybertruck in Texas “around the middle of next year” to make room for additional Cybercab production, CEO Elon Musk says. As with so many other Tesla lineup announcements, the decision was announced in a tweet shared overnight. The Cybertruck’s lead engineer followed up with an X post of his own referring to the vehicle in the past tense, suggesting that the pickup will be axed rather than see production moved elsewhere.

It marks a fitting end for what was meant to be a major volume product for Tesla, one that was intended to fight the Ford F-150 and Chevrolet Silverado the same way that the more successful Model 3 and Model Y have long fought internal combustion sedans and crossovers across the world. The unusually-styled Cybertruck never became anything close to that kind of rainmaker for Tesla, even after Tesla introduced a cheaper, more basic model last year. Those results made this outcome almost inevitable.

The Cybertruck was revealed in November of 2019, but not delivered to customers until November of 2023. When the truck did arrive, it failed to hit the lofty range and price benchmarks that Tesla announced on stage at its initial reveal. With production now set to end in the middle of 2027, the gap between the reveal and release could end up being longer than the amount of time Tesla actually sold its flagship pickup.

Cybertrucks were recalled seven times in 2024 alone and again throughout its life cycle, most notably for risks of unintended acceleration and entire pieces of bare metal bodywork that could be shed at speed. In 2025, one owner shared a video of that piece flying off his truck with Road & Track.

Without the Cybertruck out, the Roadster set for a claimed 2028 release, and the Model S and Model X canceled, Tesla’s only current products will soon be the Model 3 and Model Y. Both have been major hits for Tesla, and both have received recent facelifts, but they will have to carry the brand’s showrooms on their own until Tesla can actually find a way to deliver its promised Cybercab.
