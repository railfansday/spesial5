ZEC drops below $1,000 as profit-taking, ETF outflows and broader market weakness intensify selling pressure.

Open interest falls nearly 28% from its recent peak, signalling significant deleveraging across ZEC derivatives markets.

The $969 support remains crucial, while reclaiming the psychological $1,000 threshold could improve ZEC's recovery prospects.

With the ZEC price approaching a key support range, the key question is whether buyers can prevent a deeper correction.

Zcash’s latest decline follows a sharp rally that pushed the token to multi-month highs, with profit-taking, weakening institutional demand and broader market uncertainty adding to selling pressure. As the price climbed to highs above $1,600, profit-taking intensified, which led to the drop below $1,000. The token is trading near $985.70, with the 0.5 FIB level emerging as the immediate level to defend.

The daily RSI has declined significantly, suggesting the strengthening of the bearish trend, while the indicator has not yet confirmed oversold conditions. Besides, the OBV has also turned lower, indicating weakening buying participation alongside price. Therefore, ZEC remains vulnerable while trading below $1,000, with $969.45 acting as the immediate downside trigger to watch. A confirmed breakdown could expose $800,85, where a successful defence followed by a reclaim of $1,000 could allow a recovery towards $1,138.95.

Zcash’s derivatives market is showing signs of significant deleveraging as the token struggles to hold above $1,000. The four-hour open interest chart shows a sharp decline in outstanding futures positions alongside the falling ZEC price, suggesting that traders are reducing their exposure amid growing market uncertainty.

ZEC’s total open interest has dropped from a recent peak of approximately $3.1 billion to $2.23 billion, representing a decline of nearly 28%. The simultaneous fall in price and open interest indicates that existing positions are being closed, rather than confirming a substantial buildup of fresh short positions. This suggests that the latest correction is accompanied by a broader reduction in leveraged market exposure.

The ZEC liquidation heatmap shows a significantly larger concentration of cumulative short-liquidation leverage above the current price of approximately $988.70 than long-liquidation leverage below it. The most notable liquidity clusters are positioned around $1,007 to $1,055, with potential short-liquidation exposure continuing to build towards $1,100.

This positioning creates an interesting scenario for ZEC. If buyers manage to push the price above $1,000, a move towards these liquidation clusters could trigger forced short closures, potentially accelerating an upside recovery. However, if selling pressure persists and ZEC loses the $969 support, leveraged long positions could face further liquidation risks.

The liquidation map therefore highlights two important areas for traders: the $969 region as an immediate downside reference, and the range around $1,000 as a potential short-squeeze zone. These are estimated liquidation concentrations, not guaranteed price targets.

However, continued ETF withdrawals and broader market uncertainty could prolong the correction. Failure to reclaim $1,000 may reinforce bearish sentiment, leaving ZEC price vulnerable to further selling pressure in November.
