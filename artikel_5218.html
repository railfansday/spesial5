Bitcoin miners face a new profitability cliff as difficulty heads for an 8.5% jump

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Bitcoin miners face shrinking revenue and tighter electricity margins as a projected difficulty increase threatens less efficient machines.

Bitcoin miners’ expected dollar revenue for the same computing power fell about 6% from Nov. 16 to Nov. 20 in two readings of Luxor’s revenue index.

A difficulty cut had lifted expected Bitcoin earnings earlier in the month, but with the next adjustment now tracking higher, less efficient machines could soon need cheaper electricity to cover their power bills.

Simple Mining’s Nov. 20 reading of Luxor’s hash price index put gross mining revenue at $28.58 per petahash per second (PH/s) per day, with Luxor’s Nov. 16 roundup listing $30.45.

To estimate a machine’s gross daily revenue, multiply the hash price by the machine’s computing power in PH/s. One PH/s equals 1,000 terahashes per second.

The slide also appeared in CloudMineCrypto’s daily series, which estimates revenue from Bitcoin blocks. In its partial Nov. 20 reading, dollar hash price had fallen to about $28.90 from $30.30 per PH/s per day on Nov. 16, while the coin rate held near 0.00047 BTC.

The paired readings explain why a miner can earn roughly the same amount of Bitcoin for a day of work and still have fewer dollars to pay an electricity bill.

Bitcoin’s Nov. 14 adjustment lowered difficulty by 9.13%, from 148.36 trillion to 134.82 trillion. Holding the reward per block steady, the same amount of work could then earn about 10% more Bitcoin. Luxor’s Nov. 16 roundup found that the BTC hash price had risen from 0.00042763 a week earlier to 0.00046839 BTC per PH/s per day.

Over the same period, the dollar hash price fell from $31.95 to $30.45.

The expected coin payout depends on the block subsidy, transaction fees, and how hard it is to find a block. The dollar payout also depends on Bitcoin’s price, since fees accounted for only a small fraction of block rewards in both data sets.

Luxor reported a weekly average of 0.0229 BTC in fees per block on Nov. 16, and the partial Nov. 20 data imply about 0.022 BTC in fees on top of the 3.125 BTC subsidy.

As of Nov. 20, MineOrNot counted 1,008 blocks, halfway to the next adjustment. Blocks were arriving about every nine minutes and 14 seconds, compared with Bitcoin’s ten-minute target.

If that pace persists, MineOrNot estimated difficulty would rise 8.47% to about 146.24 trillion around Nov. 27.

If Bitcoin’s price, the subsidy, and average fees per block stay fixed, an 8.47% rise in difficulty would lower the $28.58 benchmark to about $26.35 per PH/s per day. A 10% increase would put it near $25.98 and bring difficulty almost back to its pre-cut level.

For a machine using 30 joules per terahash, the observed benchmark translates to a power-only break-even rate of 3.97 cents per kilowatt-hour.

With an 8.47% increase in difficulty, the same machine’s ceiling would fall to 3.66 cents. A machine using 13.5 joules per terahash can absorb higher electricity rates, with its ceiling falling from 8.82 cents to 8.13 cents under the same assumptions.
