Bitcoin bottom signals are becoming harder to ignore, but calling the market low now would be premature. Supply in loss has moved above 50%, the Bull-Bear Cycle Indicator has slipped below zero, and futures activity is flashing another warning.

The seven-day moving average of supply in loss reportedly reached a 140-day high, moving back above 50%. Analyst Gaah described the reading as a return to a historical bottom zone.

That matters because previous periods with a large share of supply underwater have coincided with market bottoms. Still, the indicator doesn’t confirm that prices have finished falling.

Analyst R3N highlighted another warning. The Bull-Bear Cycle Indicator has dropped to approximately -0.5, ending its brief positive stretch from late summer.

Previous cycle lows were considerably deeper: around -2.4 in 2015, -1.8 in 2018–2019 and -2.0 in 2022. The first-half 2026 low reached roughly -1.2.

The current reading is bearish, but it isn’t historical capitulation.

Analyst Nino reported the first futures overheating spike of 2026 on November 20. Price fell to approximately $58,819 while trading volume reached $82.97 billion.

Heavy volume alongside weak prices can indicate distribution or selling pressure. With BTC near its summer lows, a double bottom remains a risk, not a confirmed outcome.

These Bitcoin bottom signals describe mounting stress, but can not be seen as a guaranteed reversal signal yet. But, there’s a cautious signal because if a deeper move comes toward -1 on the cycle indicator could signal further weakness.
