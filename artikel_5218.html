TL;DR: BitGo reported roughly $44.1 million in third-quarter net income even as adjusted EBITDA remained negative at about $4.3 million. Assets on its platform rose to $59.9 billion on the company’s normalized measure. The headline revenue figure includes substantial direct costs, so it should not be treated like a conventional software sales number.

BitGo’s third-quarter results offer a useful case study in why crypto-company earnings need more than a glance at the revenue line. The custody and infrastructure provider reported a positive net result, growing platform assets and continued expansion of its customer base. Yet one measure of ongoing profitability remained below zero.

In its earnings exhibit filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, the company reported net income of roughly $44.1 million and adjusted EBITDA of negative $4.3 million for the third quarter of 2026. Those two figures tell different stories because they do not include the same items.

Reported total revenue was approximately $2.85 billion, compared with around $5.81 billion in the comparable year-earlier quarter. At first glance that looks like an extraordinary contraction for a custody business. But BitGo also reported about $2.81 billion in direct costs against the latest revenue total.

The large figures reflect the accounting presentation of transaction-related activity, rather than pure software or recurring subscription income. The spread between revenue and direct costs is therefore an important part of understanding the economics. Comparing headline gross revenue with that of a conventional enterprise-software company could mislead readers.

The company attributed positive GAAP net income in part to treasury-related appreciation, while its adjusted EBITDA measure was still negative. Investors should examine those adjustments rather than assuming accounting profit automatically equals positive cash generation from ordinary operations.

BitGo said normalized assets on platform rose 31.9% to $59.9 billion and normalized staked assets grew 41.1%. It also cited approximately 6,640 clients. Those metrics provide a view into demand for its services even as fluctuations in transaction-related revenue complicate the income statement.

The distinction matters to institutions selecting custodians. A provider’s commercial footprint can expand while costs, pricing or exceptional items cause a temporary mismatch between headline earnings and adjusted operating profit. Conversely, assets under service are not the same as assets BitGo owns or can freely use.

NewsBTC recently described BitGo’s role in custody arrangements for a Grayscale-related Hyperliquid product. We also covered plans for BitGo to provide access to a WisdomTree tokenized Treasury product. Both illustrate why custody businesses compete on institutional integrations as well as transactional volume.

The figures reinforce the idea that crypto infrastructure companies are not interchangeable with the tokens they support. A higher Bitcoin price may lift the value of assets in custody, while trading patterns, service mix, expenses and treasury changes can pull reported results in different directions.

Normalized platform assets also depend on the company’s definition of that measure, which readers should consult in the filing. It is not identical to customer deposits at a bank and should not be used as a proxy for BitGo’s own balance-sheet cash.

The useful follow-ups will be whether client and staking activity translate into better margins, how much of future earnings comes from recurring custody services, and whether adjusted operating profitability improves. Any forward-looking forecast should be separated from the quarter’s actual results.

BitGo has shown growth in assets handled and a positive bottom-line accounting result. But the negative adjusted EBITDA means the operating-quality story is still more complicated than a single profit headline might suggest.
