Golden Globes: ‘Spider-Man: Brand New Day’ Goes Comedy, ‘Dune 3’ Skips Box Office Achievement Category and More (EXCLUSIVE)

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The Golden Globes ballots are out, and some of the year’s biggest surprises are emerging from the official reminder lists sent to members.

Variety has exclusively obtained the official motion picture eligibility list for the 84th Golden Globes, which lays out every film submitted across seven categories along with the actors, directors, writers and composers each studio put forward. The document includes 88 titles for best motion picture (drama), 43 for best motion picture (musical or comedy), 24 animated features, 42 non-English-language films, 100 original scores, 51 original songs and 20 contenders for cinematic and box office achievement. It reveals some unexpected genre placements, ambitious campaign decisions and performers who could find themselves competing against their own work.

There’s the musical or comedy race, which Sony has turned into its own blockbuster lane. “Spider-Man: Brand New Day,” which has grossed more than $2 billion worldwide, files on the comedy side of the house with Tom Holland in lead actor. The rest of the ensemble, including Zendaya, Florence Pugh, Mark Ruffalo, Sadie Sink, Marisa Tomei, Jon Bernthal and Tramell Tillman, goes supporting. The studio’s “Jumanji: Open World” follows it in, with Dwayne Johnson, Jack Black and Kevin Hart in lead actor and Karen Gillan in lead actress.

Netflix’s “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth,” directed by David Fincher from a Quentin Tarantino script, joins both the comedy race and the cinematic and box office achievement race, with Brad Pitt in lead actor.

The cinematic and box office achievement category has 20 entries. Introduced in 2024, it recognizes films that have grossed $150 million, with at least $100 million grossed in the U.S., or have garnered “commensurate digital streaming viewership recognized by trusted industry sources.”

One notable omission is Denis Villeneuve’s upcoming sci-fi epic “Dune: Part Three,” which appears in Best Motion Picture (Drama) and Best Original Score but not in the box office race. Of course, its actors, including Timothée Chalamet, are in the mix for acting recognition. Even though the Warner Bros. epic is allowed, based on Globes rules, to use projected grosses, it’s opted out of the category, the same as its predecessor, “Dune: Part Two,” which didn’t submit in the category last time around. “Dune: Part Three” opens Dec. 18. For Netflix, “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” starts a two-week, IMAX-only run Wednesday before it streams Dec. 23, and it’s already on the box office list.

The rest of the box office field includes “Backrooms,” “The Devil Wears Prada 2,” “Disclosure Day,” “Focker In-Law,” “GOAT,” “Godzilla Minus Zero,” “Hoppers,” “The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping,” “Jumanji: Open World,” “Michael,” “Minions & Monsters,” “Moana,” “Obsession,” “The Odyssey,” “Spider-Man: Brand New Day,” “Star Wars: The Mandalorian and Grogu,” “The Super Mario Galaxy Movie,” “Toy Story 5” and A24’s documentary “You Can See Everything,” the only place it can compete on the ballot, likely making it the frontrunner to win the prize.

Animation isn’t just found at the kids’ table anymore.

Eight of the 24 animated submissions also turn up in a best picture category. Netflix’s “Ray Gunn,” from two-time Oscar winner Brad Bird, makes a surprise entry in the drama field alongside Leah Nelson’s “Tangles,” Laika’s “Wildwood” from director Travis Knight and Sony Pictures Classics’ “Iron Boy,” which doubles as a French entry in the non-English language field.

The musical or comedy side gets its own animated contingent with DreamWorks Animation’s “Forgotten Island,” Illumination’s “Minions & Monsters” and “The Super Mario Galaxy Movie.” One conspicuous no-show is Pixar’s “Toy Story 5.” The billion-dollar sequel seemed to have skipped the best motion picture (musical or comedy) entirely, competing in animated feature and cinematic and box-office achievement, in addition to Randy Newman’s score and Taylor Swift’s original song “I Knew It, I Knew You.” Interestingly, “Chiikawa the Movie: The Secret of the Mermaid Island,” a Japanese dark fantasy from Toho and director Kei Oikawa, makes the opposite call, appearing in non-English language, score and song but not in animated feature at all.

Several performers will be competing against themselves, and a few could do it in two genres at once.Zendaya has four titles in the running. “Dune: Part Three” and “The Drama” put her in both lead actress races, drama and comedy and “The Odyssey” and “Spider-Man: Brand New Day” put her in supporting.Robert Pattinson also has four, with “Primetime” in drama and “The Drama” in comedy for lead actor, plus “Dune: Part Three” and “The Odyssey” in supporting.

Sandra Hüller makes it a three-way tie at four, with “Rose” in lead actress and “Fatherland,” “Digger” and “Project Hail Mary” in supporting. Anne Hathaway has three: “Verity” in lead actress (drama), “The Devil Wears Prada 2” in lead actress (musical or comedy) and “The Odyssey” in supporting.

Penélope Cruz has three, with “Bunker” in lead actress and “La Bola Negra” and “The Invite” in supporting. Olivia Wilde has three performances, including “I Want Your Sex” and “The Invite” in lead actress (musical or comedy) and “Behemoth!” in supporting. She is also in the running for one of the six slots for best director for “The Invite.”

Brad Pitt is in lead actor twice, for “Heart of the Beast” in drama and “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” in musical or comedy. Andrew Garfield has two, with “The Uprising” in lead actor (drama) and “Artificial” in supporting. In addition, Javier Bardem, Mark Ruffalo, Stephen Graham, Willem Dafoe and Caitríona Balfe each have three titles on the list.

There are 131 directors on the reminder list, 23 of whom are women, or about 18%. They represent a range of categories: drama, including Emerald Fennell (“Wuthering Heights”), Siân Heder (“Being Heumann”), Kelly O’Sullivan (“Mouse,” co-directed with Alex Thompson), Beth de Araújo (“Josephine”), Georgia Oakley (“Sense and Sensibility”), Jennifer Peedom (“Tenzing”), Rachel Morrison (“Love of Your Life”) and Aleshea Harris (“Is God Is”); comedy, including Olivia Wilde (“The Invite”), Jane Schoenbrun (“Teenage Sex and Death at Camp Miasma”), Hayley Kiyoko (“Girls Like Girls”), Alicia MacDonald (“Finding Emily”) and Eleanor Wilson (“Wicker,” co-directed with Alex Huston Fischer); non-English language, including Ildikó Enyedi (“Silent Friend”), Marie Kreutzer (“Gentle Monster”), May el-Toukhy (“Woman Unknown”), Marine Atlan (“La Gradiva”), Myrsini Aristidou (“Hold Onto Me”) and Sandra Wollner (“Everytime”); and animation, including Leah Nelson (“Tangles”), Fawn Veerasunthorn (“Hexed,” co-directed with Jason Hand), Alyce Tzue (“Steps,” co-directed with John Ripa) and Phuong Mai Nguyen (“In Waves”).

Ballots are due Dec. 4. Nominations will be announced Dec. 7, and Nikki Glaser hosts the ceremony Jan. 10 from the Beverly Hilton on CBS and Paramount+.
