Burberry Retail Sales Grow 4% in Q2, Carried by China and the US

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Burberry’s comparable retail sales grew 4% on a constant currency basis in both the second quarter and first half of the fiscal year 2026/2027. For H1, Burberry’s group revenues grew 6% to €1.1 billion.

“Two years ago, we launched Burberry Forward [a plan to bring the brand back to where it was four years ago with a focus on outerwear and a diversified pricing structure]. Today, our brand is stronger, and our business is healthier,” said CEO Joshua Schulman on Thursday’s call. “We have delivered five consecutive quarters of growth and improved our quality of sales, which demonstrates that our strategy is working. This momentum stretches across product categories, channels, and geographies, and has translated into higher gross margin and increased profitability.”

Adjusted operating profit reached £62 million in H1, while gross margin rose to 68.3% — up 40 basis points from 67.9%.

Regionally, the Americas took the lion’s share of retail growth in Q2, up 11%, driven by local spending and activations, and healthy in-store conversion. Greater China followed, up 5% in Q2, a deceleration from Q1’s 9% climb. Burberry attributed this industry outperformance, in part, to the strong appeal of Burberry as a British heritage brand.

“We know that there’s been a lot of news about the challenging luxury market in China, but we’re being very deliberate, continuing to invest in our relationships with our customers in that part of the world,” said Schulman. “We understand that luxury customers around the world — particularly in China — are experiencing fatigue with exorbitant pricing, and in this context, our strategy of having enduring value and providing great value for money is resonating strongly with the Chinese customers.”

The Asia-Pacific region, excluding China, also grew in the quarter, up 3%, with South Korea growing 12%, driven by local demand. Japan was a weak point, down 1% in the quarter, attributed to softer tourism from mainland China. EMEIA also dropped 2%, due to softer tourism and traffic.

“It’s no secret that global tourism is down. So the local customer has been spending in their respective regions. We’re seeing globally that Middle Eastern, Chinese, European, and British customers are shopping at home,” said Schulman. “This is a trend we’ve been seeing all year long. There’s just less cross-border travel than there has been in the past.”

By category, womenswear led growth across retail and wholesale, growing 9%, followed by accessories (6%). Childrenswear and other also grew (5%), as well as menswear (3%).

The company didn’t share figures for outerwear, but reported double-digit retail growth in H1, led by the “rainwear” category. Lightweight gabardine designs and shorter styles performed through summer, while new cashmere and leather launches showed traction towards the end of Q2. Accessories also grew in Q2, and leather goods accelerated, boosted by strong performance in women’s handbags — particularly the vintage check and Cotswolds ranges.

Wholesale revenue grew 17% in H1. The company attributed this performance to the confidence of its partners, who are achieving high sell-throughs of Burberry products and continuing to invest in the brand’s designs. Licensing revenues dropped 5%. The brand opened seven stores and closed five, bringing the total to 412 directly operated stores as of September 2026.

The company also addressed its developing relationship with Frasers Group, which now has a 6% stake in the British brand. In October, Frasers Group had adjusted its stake in Burberry from 7.4%. This came after the growing retail group raised its position in the company from 6.3% earlier in the month, after upping it from 4.2% at the end of September. “We have a long-standing valued relationship with Frasers,” said Schulman. “They are an important wholesale customer for us in the United Kingdom, and now they are an investor. And just like we do with all of our investors, we will meet with Frasers Group in due course. In terms of their agenda, that would be a question for them.”
