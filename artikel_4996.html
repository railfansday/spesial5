Why Bitcoin surged toward $64,000 before pulling back as trading slows 54% into Thanksgiving

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Bitcoin recovered from a weekend low near $55,100 to approach $64,000 before retreating toward $62,500. Earlier Coinbase data showed trading volume falling 54% in a comparable morning hour.

Bitcoin traded near $61,200 early Monday, up 0.5% over 24 hours after falling to approximately $55,100 early Saturday. The recovery subsequently accelerated as U.S. markets reopened, carrying Bitcoin toward $64,000 before a volatile pullback toward $62,500. Wednesday's U.S. inflation report and fresh ETF flow data will provide further tests of the rebound.

Bitcoin had already reclaimed $61,000 on Sunday. Through Monday’s morning reading, about $4.65 billion changed hands in 24 hours across the spot markets CoinGlass tracks, where investors trade Bitcoin directly. That was about 24% less than in the previous 24 hours.

The Coinbase trading data showed a sharper slowdown in one comparable hour. About 333 bitcoin changed hands against the dollar from 7 to 8 a.m. ET Monday, compared with about 730 in the same hour Sunday. That is a decline of about 54%. Bitcoin ended the Monday hour at $61,253 on CryptoSlate's Bitcoin price page.

From 7 to 8 a.m. ET, 729.99 BTC traded Sunday and 333.38 BTC Monday in Coinbase Exchange’s dollar market.

Bitcoin extended its recovery during Monday's U.S. session, climbing from about $61,200 earlier in the day toward $64,000 after NVIDIA announced Jensen Huang's return, before retreating toward $62,500.

The rally briefly put Bitcoin approximately 16% above Saturday's low near $55,100. Even after the pullback, the cryptocurrency remained approximately 13% above that weekend trough.

The volatile price action followed an earlier slowdown in spot trading. Coinbase recorded approximately 333 BTC changing hands against the dollar during the comparable morning hour Monday, down 54% from Sunday's 730 BTC.

That comparison captures trading conditions before the subsequent rally. The later advance and reversal add a new development to the recovery, while leaving open whether trading activity strengthened as U.S. investors returned.

The next U.S. spot Bitcoin ETF flow figures will provide another measure of demand. Farside Investors' latest completed tally had shown $669 million in net outflows on Friday, before the weekend rebound.

Stocks were coming off a rough Friday. The S&P 500 fell 3.7% in that session, and futures tracking the index were up 2.1% early Monday before the U.S. stock market opened, according to Libella’s morning market update. Around 7 a.m. London time, the same report put Brent crude futures near $90 a barrel, gold futures up about 3% and the U.S. dollar index down about 0.2%.

A separate test comes from funds that hold Bitcoin and trade on stock exchanges. Farside Investors’ flow data showed $669.2 million in net outflows from U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs on Friday, the latest completed tally available Monday morning. The next full report will show whether investors continued withdrawing money from the funds or returned as Bitcoin recovered. A cryptocurrency can rise through the weekend while its U.S. exchange-listed funds are out of session, so the next tally will put a different group of market participants into view.

The Bureau of Economic Analysis has scheduled two reports for 8:30 a.m. ET Wednesday. One includes the October personal consumption expenditures price index, the Federal Reserve’s preferred inflation gauge. The other updates the estimate of third-quarter economic growth. In September, consumer prices measured by PCE rose 3.6% from a year earlier. Excluding food and energy, they rose 3.1%.

An inflation reading above expectations could keep interest-rate expectations elevated and make it harder for Bitcoin’s rebound to extend. A softer reading could give it room if Treasury yields ease. With the growth and inflation reports due together, yields and the dollar can help put Bitcoin’s first price move in context. The Fed’s Beige Book comes out at 2 p.m. ET, offering a look at business conditions across the country.

NYSE markets close Thursday for Thanksgiving, and Friday’s main stock session ends at 1 p.m. ET. Bitcoin exchanges can keep trading through the holiday. Spot Bitcoin ETFs listed on NYSE Arca skip Thursday’s regular session, so Bitcoin can move during a stretch when the funds’ main market is closed. For Arca, the gap runs from 4 p.m. ET Wednesday to 9:30 a.m. ET Friday. A Bitcoin price move during that stretch can carry through to the next regular session for fund holders. Friday’s shortened session leaves less regular trading time for the funds to respond to any holiday moves.

Heading into the Thanksgiving break, the question is whether Bitcoin can sustain its recovery after Monday's rally and pullback. A stronger recovery would combine price stability through Wednesday's economic reports with improving spot trading activity and renewed ETF demand. Further price weakness alongside continued ETF outflows would weaken the case for a sustained rebound.
