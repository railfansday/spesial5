SoftBank’s Son explored a $1tn SK Hynix buyout, The Information reports

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

SoftBank says a $1tn SK Hynix buyout is no longer under active consideration. SK Hynix says it knows nothing of it, and any foreign takeover would need Seoul’s approval.

Masayoshi Son, chairman and chief executive of SoftBank Group.

SoftBank chief executive Masayoshi Son recently approached Wall Street institutions about financing a possible buyout of SK Hynix worth about $1tn, The Information reported on Friday, citing people familiar with the matter. Valida Pau, Phoebe Liu, Qianer Liu and Juro Osawa wrote the story.

SoftBank told The Information that Son is no longer pursuing the idea.

“SoftBank explores many opportunities, and this one is no longer under active consideration,” a SoftBank spokesperson told The Information.

SK Hynix said in a statement that it had no knowledge of the matter described in the report, Reuters reported. The chipmaker added that Korean law requires prior government approval for any foreign acquisition of the company. TNW has not independently verified the report.

SK Hynix is now worth about 777 trillion won, or roughly $577.7bn, according to Seoul Economic Daily. That is well below the peak it reached about a month ago. The paper said the $1tn figure reflects that peak value plus a 40% premium for control.

The memory maker first joined the trillion-dollar club in May, on demand for the high-bandwidth memory (HBM) that sits next to AI chips. In October it reported a 632% profit jump and said the memory shortage would last until 2030.

The Information set out why the deal faced long odds. It is unclear how SoftBank would pay, the outlet reported, because its OpenAI bets have already stretched its balance sheet. SoftBank completed its $30bn OpenAI investment in October.

There is also no sign that the Wall Street firms would have lent the money. South Korea, like the US, tightly controls foreign takeovers of domestic technology. The Information judged it extremely unlikely that Seoul would let a Tokyo-based group lead a buyout of one of the country’s most important tech assets. Nor is there any sign that SK Group, which controls SK Hynix, wanted to sell.

Memory is the one part of the AI supply chain SoftBank has not secured, Seoul Economic Daily reported. The group is the largest shareholder in chip designer Arm and owns server chip maker Ampere Computing and data centre investor DigitalBridge. It also holds large stakes in Intel and OpenAI. SK Hynix is one of three suppliers of HBM for AI accelerators, alongside Samsung Electronics and Micron.

Son has paid for his biggest bets with borrowed money. He funded the OpenAI stake by pledging Arm shares as collateral, the paper reported. This month SoftBank swung to a $7bn loss after writing off $3.7bn of AI power equipment.
