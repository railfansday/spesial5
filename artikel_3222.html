Blackstone’s data centre trust buys 75% of 13 AWS buildings in $5bn deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

AWS keeps a 25% stake and will lease back all 13 buildings for at least 12 years. It is BXDC’s first deal, announced the same day as its third-quarter results.

Nick Pell, chief executive and president of Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust.

Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust (BXDC) is buying 75% of two joint ventures with Amazon Web Services (AWS). The deal is valued at about $5bn. It is the first acquisition for the Blackstone-managed trust, which listed in May.

The joint ventures will own 13 newly built powered shells on two AWS data centre campuses. BXDC announced the deal in a statement on Monday. Powered shells are buildings with power connections that the tenant fits out. The statement does not name the campuses.

AWS keeps the other 25% and will lease back all 13 buildings. The lease is triple net, so AWS pays the property taxes, insurance and upkeep. It runs for 12 years at first, and up to 32 years with extensions. The price reflects an initial capitalisation rate of 7%, the first year’s net income as a share of the price.

“The quality, basis and tenant profile of these assets make this an attractive match for the deployment of our IPO proceeds,” said Nick Pell, BXDC’s chief executive and president.

“Partnering with Blackstone allows us to continue expanding responsibly while keeping communities at the center of how we build,” said Prasad Kalyanaraman, vice president of AWS Infrastructure.

Pell joined Blackstone in May from Link Logistics, the warehouse operator Blackstone set up in 2019. He was its president and chief investment officer, Link Logistics said at the time.

The deal is expected to close in the fourth quarter, subject to closing conditions. Morgan Stanley, CBRE Capital Markets, Citi, Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan and Wells Fargo are financial advisers. Simpson Thacher & Bartlett is Blackstone’s legal adviser.

In May, Blackstone also took the majority position in a new TPU cloud venture with Google.

BXDC also reported its third-quarter results on Monday, in a separate statement. Net income was $15.6m, or $0.15 a share, up from $7.1m in the second quarter.

Funds from operations (FFO), the standard profit measure for property trusts, was $0.15 a share. Adjusted FFO was $0.17. The trust targets newly built data centres leased to large cloud companies on long contracts. An affiliate of Blackstone manages it.

“Importantly, it highlights our ability to source unique investment opportunities as well as the evolution of our investible universe, as the hyperscaler sale-leaseback channel doubles our total addressable market,” Pell said in the results statement.

The deal comes days after QTS, Blackstone’s data centre operator, cut a planned private debt sale. QTS is raising about $300m instead of roughly $800m. Menghan Xiao and Emily Graffeo reported it for Bloomberg on Friday, citing people with knowledge of the deal.

Some investors balked at the price, the people said. Others faced limits because QTS had already tapped the private placement market several times. Blackstone chose a smaller deal at its own price rather than pay much higher yields, according to Bloomberg. TNW has not independently verified the report.

The $300m includes a $100m seven-year private placement, priced at 2.45 percentage points over benchmarks. A $200m five-year bank loan is due to be priced in the coming weeks. QTS had planned to use the $800m to refinance five-year bank debt. That debt can now stay in place, Bloomberg reported.

Blackstone bought QTS in 2021 for about $10bn including debt. In July, QTS ended its Digital Gateway data centre project in Virginia.
