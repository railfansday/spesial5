Capita CEO leaves following pensions contract disaster

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Shares plunge around 70% as outsourcer cuts profit expectations and plans £190M cash call

Capita CEO Adolfo Hernandez has stepped down with immediate effect as the outsourcer warned of sharply lower profits next year, nearly a year after its troubled takeover of administration of the UK's Civil Service Pension Scheme (CSPS).

In a statement, Capita said CFO Pablo Andres had become CEO, with group financial controller Gemma Bate-Williams succeeding him as finance chief.

Capita now expects adjusted operating profit for 2027 to be £85 million to £95 million below previous expectations. That includes £15 million to £25 million in additional costs under a pensions recovery plan agreed with the Cabinet Office, alongside pressure from contract exits and other trading headwinds.

Shares fell around 70 percent following the announcement.

The company – which is also coming under fire for its £370 million contract with the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) – said it had made significant progress in its transformation and strategic simplification. It also plans to raise around £190 million through a share issue in the first half of 2027 to reduce borrowing and to "allow the business to move forward from a more stable foundation," subject to support from its financial creditors.

James Preece, principal analyst at UK tech research firm Megabuyte, said: "Slow motion car crash comes to mind. It has been a turnaround job for coming up to four years now, with long-suffering shareholders watching dozens of disposals and business exits fail to arrest a structural decline in both margins and growth.

"Today's update suggests that management's underlying forecast assumptions alongside other more transient factors were still too punchy, and the leverage situation now calls for action. The share price reaction suggests confidence in this being a one-and-done raise is limited," he said.

Capita took over administration of the scheme in December 2025 under a seven-year, £239 million contract, after which The Register exclusively revealed problems with its online systems. The vendor promised AI and automation, but the takeover brought a glitchy online portal, while some members struggled to receive pension payments. By January, it was clear the service was seriously failing, leaving some retired civil servants struggling to make ends meet.

Capita missed its deadline to restore normal service by the end of June. It subsequently told MPs that all but the most complex cases would return to normal service by September.

The pensions scheme serves around 1.7 million members. Media reports and MPs have described members feeling upset, frustrated, and ignored, with some waiting months for payments.

The government said it had withheld £10 million in payments over Capita's performance during the transition of the pensions service and was negotiating further penalties. It also appointed an auditor to assess the case for further penalties for Capita's performance on the contract.

Last month, the National Audit Office launched an investigation into the CSPS contract. Responding to the investigation, Capita said it "accepts that performance remains below the standards that scheme members and the Government rightly expect" and that fixing the problems was its top priority.

The Public and Commercial Services Union is campaigning to get the government to scrap a separate Capita contract, the Synergy shared services deal, which Capita values at £370 million. The deal covers HR, payroll, finance, and other back-office services across four government departments and nearly 100 arm's-length bodies. Rival bidder Sopra Steria, which owns incumbent provider SSCL, has also launched a legal challenge alleging Capita's bid was abnormally low. Capita and the DWP – which leads on the contract – have denied the allegations. ®
