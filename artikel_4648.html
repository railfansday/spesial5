Never Fight a Pig? Why Sometimes You Have To

Never fight with a pig. You'll just get dirty, and the pig likes it.

You've heard that line. It sounds high-minded, and it's a relief not to have to fight anyone. You keep your hands clean, you deny the pig its satisfaction, and you get to mutter "pig" as you walk away.

It's the same comfort as "When they go low, we go high" or "Love trumps hate." These claims soothe because they're conclusive, flattering, and easy. They take the pressure off engaging in conflict and flatter us as high, not low; loving, not hateful. We rarely kick the tires on such proud proclamations from a safe distance. We should, because they leak.

Never fight with a pig? Even a pig who harms your family, or sells your country for parts and pockets the profits? I say never say never. Sometimes you have to fight. You will get dirty, and the pig will like it, at least at first. The real question is how to make the fight less fun for the pig. Not how to strut off triumphant, but how to get a pig to stop being a pig and admit they're human.

Here's a poke at a definition. A pig blames all of their mistakes on others, and does so with the preening pretense of God-like authority. Their logic is circular: "Since I deem everything I do perfect, I'm the supreme authority, and so qualified to deem everything I do perfect." It's the same circularity as claiming a sacred text is the word of God because the text says so.

That isn't "playing God" in the sense of changing the world. It's playing God by pretending you're the authority on which changes are permissible. It's the sneer at moral relativism from someone who declares their own subjective morality the last word, so anyone who challenges them is the devil.

A pig acts like they can do no wrong and so feels free to do endless wrong. They like getting dirty because, in their minds, they can't get dirty. They're sadistic narcissists, elevated through theatrical contempt for rivals. When someone contemptible dirties them, that proves they're clean.

Pigs are self-winding. Praise proves they're perfect. Condemnation proves they're perfect too, because they've already decided that anyone who condemns them is contemptible. Pigs don't go low. They go low from on high, nasty to anyone who triggers them yet entitled to be nasty because they've declared themselves saints.

How do you yank them off their high horse without mounting it yourself? The best answer I have so far: Get comfortable being dirtied. Stop trying to win battles with them. Stop trying to get them to dismount and be nice. They're addicted to playing God, so they won't just quit.

Stop trying to win arguments, too, and stay off your own high horse. Skip the morality lectures and the fact-checking, because you can't beat them at that game. The only rule of their game is that anyone who challenges their authority is beneath contempt.

Instead, expose the game, over and over. Interpret their contempt as nothing more than the game. Point out that they'll say anything to pretend they're the authority, and when they respond as though they're the authority, thank them for proving you right.

I've been doing this online for a while, holding up a mirror to the game. I get thousands of responses, all true to formula. Nobody shares an opinion; they make pronouncements. Anyone who challenges their authority is dismissed from their supreme courtroom. I get called every insult in the book, over and over. It doesn't make me flinch. It's boring, a broken record preening as an unbroken one.

I never defend myself, as if appealing in their courtroom. That would be enabling. I never take the bait, because they don't mean it. Their output is inconsistent nonsense on stilts, so I don't feel dirtied. I keep my own counsel on whose counsel I keep, and it isn't theirs.

I've even come to enjoy it. It's fun tangling them up in their own nonsense. They always respond with a defensive pronouncement that they are never triggered, and I tease them right back about how defensive and triggered they sound. It's a taste of their own medicine.

That's all you can do, and all you need to do. You won't want to, but it's a civic duty.
