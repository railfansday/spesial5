Modigliani sculpture heads Phillips’s New York evening sale at $44.6m

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Modigliani’s Tête led Phillips's auction last night, selling for $44.6m

A striking limestone head by Amedeo Modigliani sold for $44.6m (with fees) at Phillips Modern and contemporary evening sale last night (17 November), leading a nearly sold-out auction totalling $85.75m ($104.4m with fees). That is an increase of 55% from the same sale last November, which topped out at $54.8m ($67.3m with fees).

Robert Manley, Phillips’ chairman and worldwide head of Modern and contemporary art, said in a statement that there had been strong bidding across Modern, post-war and contemporary art: “Nearly 90% of the works offered had never been previously sold at auction, with many of the sale’s works coming from esteemed private collections where they had been for decades. Tonight’s results are a powerful reminder that truly exceptional works continue to inspire extraordinary competition in the market.”

Sales were brisk in a bustling room, the pace in part due to the large number of works backed by third party guarantees. With just 22 lots on offer—the house withdrew Derek Fordjour’s Haberdasher, estimated at $350,000 to $550,000, at the last minute—14 carried a guarantee.

It took less than a minute and a half, for instance, for auctioneer Henry Highley to hammer Claude Monet’s La Maison dans les roses (1925) at $3.2m, matching the $4m high estimate with fees.

On the other hand, the Modigliani, also guaranteed, sparked a ten-minute three-way battle, escalating from the starting bid of $18m to $28m in just a few seconds before slowing to $100,000 increments. A phone bidder speaking with Phillips specialist India Phillips ultimately prevailed over clients on the line with specialists Jeremiah Evarts and Kevie Yang, placing the winning bid at $38.2m ($44.6m with fees) on an estimate of $28m to $35m.

The piece, dating from 1911 to 1912, is one of just 28 sculptures by the artist, and part of a larger series of limestone heads each titled Tête. The price, while impressive, failed to approach the $70.7m achieved by another work from the series at Sotheby’s New York in 2014, which remains the record for Modigliani sculptures. His painting record, $170.4m for the 1918 painting Nu couché—the sixth most expensive work ever to sell at auction—dates to the following year.

The evening’s second-biggest lot was David Hockney’s eight-panel oil painting David, Celia, Stephen and Ian, London 1982, which sold for $10m ($12.2m with fees) within its $10m to $15m estimate. The artist, who died in June at the age of 88, made the work in 1982. It reflects his experiments at the time with photographing a subject from slightly different, overlapping viewpoints to create Cubist-inspired collages, and marks Hockney’s return to painting after a period focused on photography.

The painting, which was sold by the Joel and Carole Bernstein family collection, also boasts an impressive museum pedigree, including appearances at the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, the Los Angeles County Museum of Art, and the National Portrait Gallery in London.

Rounding out the sale’s top three was Alexander Calder’s 1959 mobile Snow Flurry, May 14—one of the few works without a third-party guarantee, and being sold by the family of Brazilian art critic Mário Pedrosa. Yang caused some excitement when she raised her bid straight from $4.6m to $6m, a price no one else was willing to top. The total with fees was $7.5m, above a high estimate of $6m.

Helen Frankenthaler had the sale’s top result for a woman artist with Seabreeze (1980), which went for $4.5m (with fees). Frankenthaler followed that up a few lots later with Peacock Alley (1990) $2.7m (with fees), right in the middle of its $2m to $3m estimate.

The night’s only bought-in lot was Anselm Kiefer’s Melancholia (2005), which carried an estimate of $650,000 to $850,000, but bidding stalled out at just $500,000. The rust-coated painting features a polyhedral glass-and-iron sculpture mounted on the canvas.

And while Jackson Pollock’s 1946 abstraction Constellation did find a buyer, the work failed to meet its low estimate of $3.5m. Despite the current show of Pollock and his wife Lee Krasner uptown at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, and a provenance boasting the legendary dealer Peggy Guggenheim, the work hammered at just $2.6m ($3.3m with fees). Frank Stella’s Port Tampa City, from the estate of the late dealer Robert Mnuchin, also hammered below estimate, at $2.8m ($3.5m with fees) despite an estimate of $3m to $5m. The orange canvas, in the shape of an eight-point star quilt block, dates to 1963.

Nevertheless, both paintings made the sale’s top ten list, slotting in at number eight for Stella and nine for Pollock, just above the second Frankenthaler. Other noteworthy results included Clyfford Still’s PH-589 (1959). Expected to sell for $4m to $6m, it hammered for $4.2m ($5.3m with fees) to an online bidder from Tel Aviv.

Led by a once-scandalous Modigliani painting, the £296.3m London evening sale provided a bright spot for a market struggling with concerns of Brexit and overseas competition

The auction house’s bottom line benefitted from the strong performance of works from the collections of S.I. Newhouse and Paul G. Allen
