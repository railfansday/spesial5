Microsoft brings serverless PostgreSQL into Fabric to bridge transactions and analytics

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

HorizonDB preview follows rivals' moves, with analysts warning tighter integration could make an eventual exit harder

Microsoft has brought its HorizonDB PostgreSQL service into public preview in Fabric, aiming to bring transactional and analytical workloads closer together.

The corporation says applications can submit queries through a single PostgreSQL endpoint, with transactional and analytical work handled by the appropriate engines.

Analysts said the move follows similar efforts by Databricks and Snowflake to combine operational databases with analytics. But tighter integration with Microsoft's stack could make applications harder to move elsewhere.

Fabric HorizonDB joins SQL database and Cosmos DB in Microsoft's Fabric database portfolio. "By bringing operational databases, analytics, and AI together on a unified, governed platform, developers can build intelligent applications with less data movement and complexity. Fabric HorizonDB will also serve as the PostgreSQL backend for Fabric apps," Microsoft said.

Microsoft first unveiled Azure HorizonDB in preview in November 2025 and made it generally available last week. It competes with other cloud database services and distributed systems such as CockroachDB and YugabyteDB, both of which support PostgreSQL's wire protocol.

At the time, it said HorizonDB would offer "massive read scale without the need to replicate the database between instances."

Speaking to The Register last week, Shireesh Thota, veep of Azure databases and Fabric applications, said that bringing the system into Fabric – its platform for data lakes, data warehouses, and analytics launched in 2023 – would connect operational data in HorizonDB with analytical data in OneLake, Fabric's unified data lake.

"Once the data hits the OneLake storage, you can have analytical engines that take advantage of it. In HorizonDB, the PostgreSQL query engine can talk to the distributed query engine that we use in our Fabric data warehouse. We're fusing these query engines, thereby enabling the developers to submit a query that has pieces of the query that can work on OLTP and pieces of the query that can work on OLAP," he said.

"The application need not worry about two endpoints, or about consistency of data and all that complexity. They can just talk to HorizonDB as a Fabric endpoint, and they can submit all these queries. We are basically operationalizing in OneLake."

Operational data remains in HorizonDB's database storage, while mirroring maintains an analytical copy in OneLake, introduced in 2023, which replicates a snapshot of the external database to OneLake in Delta Parquet tables and keeps the replica synced in "near real time."

Thota said the process would not affect the transactional system. "Our core goal is to never impact OLTP workload while we are doing this, and we succeed [by] reading off the log as an additional replica to do this mirroring," he said.

But the overall plan of putting serverless PostgreSQL OLTP in an analytics platform, bringing it close to OLAP systems, is one some vendors are already executing to meet the demands of new AI agent-led workloads.

Adam Ronthal, research veep in Gartner's Data and Analytics Group, said: "The addition of Microsoft Fabric HorizonDB follows the 2025 acquisitions of PostgreSQL vendors by Databricks – Neon – and Snowflake – Crunchy. It delivers a high-performance, serverless operational RDBMS that is tightly coupled with Microsoft's lakehouse offering and well-suited to the ephemeral demands of AI agents.

"Competing directly with AWS Aurora and Google Cloud's AlloyDB, HorizonDB acts as a foundational serving layer for the next generation of AI- and analytics-infused applications. For organizations aligned with Azure and Fabric, it provides an industry-standard PostgreSQL foundation for AI development."

Andrew Snodgrass, research vice president of analyst company Directions on Microsoft, said the announcement of HorizonDB in Fabric was a "surprise," although "a watered-down version" compared to the version of the serverless database that became generally available on Azure last week. "It joins (document database) CosmosDB in Fabric and SQL database (based on the SQL Server engine) in Fabric. It highlights that Microsoft is still focused on making Fabric the data hub of their universe."

HorizonDB – both in Azure and Fabric – is a PostgreSQL service which Microsoft says has 100 percent compatibility with the open source system that has enjoyed a meteoric rise over the last decade. The support for PostgreSQL has led some to question the software giant's commitment to its own proprietary OLTP system, SQL Server.

Thota pointed out that the SQL database, based on the SQL Server engine, is already available in Fabric. Microsoft is also evaluating similar integration for that service, although he gave no timetable. "We are just beginning this journey, and most of the innovation is neutral to PostgreSQL and SQL. Some of it would be particular to how the individual query engine fuses with the analytical query engine. PostgreSQL does it slightly differently than SQL, but the idea is that most of the work is neutral. We are evaluating that work as well. I don't have timelines for that, but it is certainly in our thinking," he said.

Nonetheless, Holger Mueller, principal analyst at Constellation Research, said it was good to see Microsoft go all in on support of open source. "In general, it is an acknowledgement that they cannot win the battle for the SQL database with SQL Server – as it is as proprietary as the leader Oracle – and now they bank on the openness of PostgreSQL as another approach," he said.

However, using HorizonDB integrated into the Fabric/Azure stack may create so many dependencies with Microsoft's technology that lock-in to the vendor will remain a risk. "The primary concerns of open source – scale and support – have been eliminated by Microsoft's commitment and by now track record... the challenges are the embeddings of HorizonDB into the bigger Microsoft stack – which will make portability hard again. But every vendor wants and provides stickiness – clothed in ease of use and lower TCO," Mueller said. ®
