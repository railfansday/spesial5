Getting asked why you haven’t bought a house this Thanksgiving? Here’s what to tell your relatives

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

It’s bad enough you have to sleep on the pull-out couch for a few days, but then come the questions.

If the topic of homeownership—and, more pointedly, why you don’t yet own a home—comes up at Thanksgiving, here’s a stat to keep your pestering relatives at bay. A household today earning a median income would need to save for 10.5 years to have enough for a 10% down payment on a median-priced home—that’s more than three times longer than in the 1980s, according to a new report from Realtor.com.

While your relatives may bemoan the double-digit mortgage rates of the 1980s, homes on the market were relatively more affordable. That’s because the surge in home prices, and especially in recent years, has far outpaced the rise in wages, which is keeping many prospective homebuyers on the sidelines, according to the Austin-based real estate site.

“The reality is that today’s aspiring buyers face a different housing market than generations before,” Hannah Jones, senior economist at Realtor.com, said in a statement. And it’s not just that home prices have outpaced wage growth, homebuyers are also facing rising costs for insurance and homeowners association (HOA) fees that are stretching their budgets, she added. “The path to homeownership has become increasingly difficult.”

In the 1980s, the median-priced home was selling for roughly 3.2 times the typical annual household income at the time, according to Realtor.com. Today, shoppers are trying to squeeze into a market where homes are selling for 4.8 times the typical household income. It’s no wonder, then, that so many younger people are living at home for longer to save up for a down payment when a first-time homebuyer is now 40 years old.

WHERE HOUSING IS MORE, LESS AFFORDABLE

Depending on where you live, you might already have an arsenal of mind-blowing stats about your local housing market. In some major metro areas, saving enough money for a down payment for a median-priced home has become a herculean task unto itself.

People earning the median income could understandably feel locked out of the housing market in many areas. Among the 50 largest metro areas, the five where workers must save the longest for a 10% down payment on a median-priced home are: 

  • Los Angeles, California: 24.8 years
  • San Diego, California: 18.8 years
  • San Jose, California: 18.4 years
  • New York, New York: 17.0 years
  • Boston, Massachusetts: 15.7 years

On the flip side, saving up for a down payment in the following five metro areas could happen in the span of less than a decade for some households:

  • Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania: 7.2 years
  • Detroit, Michigan: 7.6 years
  • St. Louis, Missouri: 7.6 years
  • Buffalo, New York: 7.9 years
  • Cleveland, Ohio: 8.1 years

Of course, housing-related costs aren’t limited to saving up for the down payment and then budgeting for monthly mortgage payments. Homeowners today must also factor in the rising costs of things like insurance or HOA fees at a time when the price of, well, everything seems to be going up.

“For many younger Americans, the challenge isn’t a lack of willingness to save,” Jones said. “It’s that the financial goalposts have moved.”
