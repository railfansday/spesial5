Airline revenue is growing faster than profit. Revenue at the six largest U.S. airlines rose about 18% in the third quarter from a year earlier, but operating profit was roughly flat, and higher jet fuel prices are exposing which carriers can raise revenue fast enough to protect margins. Executives speaking across product, network, loyalty, and operations described a more granular set of decisions: IAG's Luis Gallego said strong North Atlantic demand lets the group offset its fuel bill, while Breeze's David Neeleman protects economics with low trip costs and routes with limited nonstop competition. Premium is being defined as a full-trip proposition rather than a seat — American's Nat Pieper said network, customer experience, premium revenue, and loyalty "all have to be working in concert," and Gallego said British Airways' heavier premium A380 configuration would not work across every IAG market. Capacity growth now depends on matching aircraft size to route economics, with Porter and Breeze targeting markets larger carriers have little reason to serve and Embraer's Arjan Meijer pointing to a widening gap between 76-seat regional jets and narrowbodies. Loyalty faces a harder test as AI makes fares and benefits easier to compare, and Air Canada's Nasr said loyalty is a multiplier off an operating base. Underneath all of it sits spending on reliability: Air Canada's largest-ever technology investment, LAX's $30 billion capital program, and about $10 billion planned at Paris Charles de Gaulle over eight years.
