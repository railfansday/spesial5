CEO Josh D’Amaro highlighted a Fortnite tie-in as a prime example of how Disney can connect theme parks to its digital business. The company's annual filing reveals a larger stake in Epic Games in fiscal 2026.

Disney’s stake in Fortnite studio Epic Games grew in fiscal 2026 as the company works to link digital play to real-world visits.

Language in Disney’s annual filing says its ownership in Epic Games increased from 8% to 10% during the fiscal year that ended early October, although it’s unclear what drove the increase and whether Disney made an additional investment.

The filing lists Disney’s Epic Games stake at $2.2 billion as of Oct. 3, up from $1.6 billion a year earlier. Its cash flow statement shows $548 million in net “acquisitions and purchase of investments” for the year, though it doesn’t break out how much, if any, went to Epic.

Disney did not respond to a request for comment in time for publication.

Disney's stake in Fortnite maker Epic Games rose from 8% to 10% in the fiscal year that ended in early October, according to language in its annual filing, with the holding valued at $2.2 billion as of Oct. 3, up from $1.6 billion a year earlier. The filing doesn't make clear what drove the increase or whether Disney put in more money; its cash flow statement shows $548 million in net acquisitions and purchases of investments for the year without breaking out Epic. Disney first disclosed a $1.5 billion equity stake in Epic in February 2024, citing the use of Epic's Unreal Engine in more than 15 park attractions, and since August, guests who ride Millennium Falcon: Smugglers Run can unlock a digital reward in Fortnite. CEO Josh D'Amaro said on Tuesday's earnings call that digital and physical experiences can reinforce one another and that there is "a lot more of this ahead." The bet is funded by a parks and cruise business that produced $11.2 billion of Disney's $19.7 billion in total segment operating income, up 12%, with theme park admissions revenue up 9% in fiscal 2026 and attendance up 3% in the most recent quarter while United Parks fell 4.7% and Six Flags was essentially flat.
