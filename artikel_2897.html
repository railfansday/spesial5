Chiefs, Kansas officials float polarizing PSL blueprint in new stadium documents | The Kansas City Star

The new documents, which the authority approved Wednesday, suggest that the team is at least considering selling personal seat licenses, or PSLs, a polarizing pay-to-buy system in which fans purchase licenses for seats in order to later buy season tickets.

Under the agreements released this week, the revenue generated from any PSL fees would help the Chiefs pay for construction of the new multibillion-dollar domed stadium and count toward the team's 40% commitment. The remaining 60% would come from public funds, including supercharged bonds from the state of Kansas.

The documents mark the first public revelation that the team is evaluating imposing the fees on fans to help pay for the new stadium — though in previous fan surveys, they have included questions to season-ticket holders about PSLs.

Studies: Blood test has potential to detect CTE in the living | ESPN

One study, published Thursday by researchers at Boston University and Banner Health, was based on a small sample of former American football players who donated blood while alive. After those players died, autopsies of their brains determined they had CTE. The researchers then identified a CTE marker by examining the levels of a specific protein in the blood samples.

Michael Alosco, the co-director of clinical research at Boston University's CTE Center and co-senior author of the study, called the test a "game changer" in the ability to diagnose the degenerative brain illness in the living.
