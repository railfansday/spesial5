From the injury bug to youth movements, here are notes on all 30 teams to help you in fantasy basketball in Week 5

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A quick fantasy basketball breakdown on all 30 teams after Week 5 of the 2026-27 NBA season. Here are five fantasy trends grabbing my attention coming out of Week 4, plus rapid-fire notes on every other team.

Warriors: Stephen Curry's oblique tear will keep him out at least two weeks, and the team is bracing for an absence closer to a month. Golden State got blown out 123-85 by the Spurs in the first game without him. De'Anthony Melton got the first crack at the starting spot and is the priority add. However, this team will lean more on Yaxel Lendeborg and Brandin Podziemski until Curry returns.

Jazz: Lauri Markkanen is out at least a month with a fractured shooting hand. Keyonte George (ankle) is back and should see hefty usage. Darryn Peterson just snapped a five-game streak of scoring 21+ points across Weeks 3 and 4. He continues to trend up at 4.5 assists per game in November with nearly a steal and a block.

Hornets: Brandon Miller returned and should immediately reclaim a heavy-usage role. Coby White has played six games under a 20-minute cap, but he's expected to ramp up soon — he's dropped at least 20 points in his last two outings. Don't expect to see Kon Knueppel for at least a month after aggravating his hamstring.

Grizzlies: Memphis is 9-4 without Ty Jerome because the kids keep delivering. Cedric Coward ranks 58th in points per game and 25th in 9-cat through Monday. Cam Spencer continues to thrive in a starting role, averaging 13 points, 4 boards, 10 assists and 1 steal per game with Jerome out. Don't drop him.

Timberwolves: Minnesota is surprisingly 7-3 without Anthony Edwards. Ayo Dosunmu is balling, putting up 20-5-3 on 53/42/96 shooting. You can't beat that efficiency. Meanwhile, Terrence Shannon Jr. just dropped a career-high 34 on Portland on Monday night. LaMelo Ball is in a shooting funk, but I'm buying low if a manager is rattled.

Pistons: Jalen Duren is out at least two more weeks. Daniss Jenkins' minutes are up over the last four games, averaging 36 minutes, which has led to a productive 20-4-4 line over that span. Keep holding, especially now that he's starting.

Cavaliers: With all five starters resting Saturday, Tyrese Proctor posted one of the most random triple-doubles of the season (24-11-10 ) against Dallas. I was reluctant to hold, but he started in Monday's win over the Bulls, with Evan Mobley (toe) out of the lineup. Mobley will be re-evaluated in 7-10 days. Meleek Thomas and Mario Hezonja are deep-league names to watch.

Magic: Franz Wagner is fifth in the NBA in assists and has strung together four straight games of at least 30 points across Weeks 3 and 4. Point Franz is crushing his ADP, and it'll be interesting to see what this looks like when Paolo Banchero returns.

Pacers: With Tyrese Haliburton still out, Andrew Nembhard's done a great job facilitating. His 7.6 assists per game are seventh-best in the NBA. Nembhard missed Sunday's clash with Philly, but the Pacers don't play again until Wednesday. A few days off should help him get back out there.

Heat: Did you know that Davion Mitchell is leading the league in assists? Let that sink in. Dude's been unreal to start the season. Bam Adebayo left Monday's win over Houston with hip tightness, though Miami doesn't expect a long-term issue. If he sits, Mitchell gets a bump.

Hawks:  Sell Jalen Johnson. He posted his first triple-double (27-11-10) of the season a couple of games ago, and looks back to normal following a three-game absence due to an ankle sprain. Since 2022-23 (excluding a low-usage rookie year), he's missed 28% of regular-season games, including a season-ending torn labrum in 2025.

Pelicans: Dejounte Murray wants out, and per Marc Stein, the Pelicans feel the same. But a deal isn't close with his age, Achilles history and nearly $33 million salary. He's away from the team, so cut him in shallow leagues. Jeremiah Fears' stock is way up.

Wizards: Trae Young went scoreless Sunday for the first time in his career as the Wizards' losing streak hit eight games on Monday. I don't think he'll keep shooting this bad, and at least he's still getting the assists and points. There's a case to sell high on AD, but I wouldn't yet. Seems like he's auditioning for a trade …

Raptors: There's a small window to buy Kawhi Leonard if you're willing to take on some risk. He's still very efficient and productive when he plays.

Suns: Sell Miles Bridges; he's not a top-35 player in 9-cat. In fact, the slide is already happening, as he was top-25 last week. But also, I'd drop Jalen Green — still 67% rostered, but the team is operating better with players like Collin Gillespie, Jordan Goodwin and Luke Kennard taking his minutes.

76ers: LeBron James passed Jason Kidd for third all-time in assists, then sat Monday while Joel Embiid dropped a season-high 37 to move Philly to 12-3. That's one of the best records in the NBA, and the Sixers have done it with James still not fully ramped up, per ESPN's Dave McMenamin. If this is the Sixers before LeBron hits full speed, the ceiling is scary. Now, they need to fortify that frontcourt and add some shooters by the deadline to really make a run for it.

Celtics: Boston is 11-2 after a 131-94 demolition of Dallas in which it led 42-9 after one quarter. Jayson Tatum is the engine, dishing out a career-best 7.1 assists per game. And he's still top-10 in scoring.

Spurs: San Antonio has won 12 straight. I like the Spurs schedule this week, so pick up guys like Julian Champagnie and Keldon Johnson. Hopefully De'Aaron Fox returns from his hip injury and we'll get to see Tobias Harris make his season debut at some point this week.

Lakers: The 11-2 Lakers keep winning shorthanded because Luka Dončić has been unconscious, gunning for his first MVP.  He's the top player in points leagues and second in 9-cat, averaging 39-7-8 with 49/40/87 shooting splits. Austin Reaves (heel bruise) and Walker Kessler (lower leg soreness) are both out Tuesday, but Reaves could return later this week. Quentin Grimes is a deep-league add with Reaves out.

Rockets: KD's taken his foot off the gas a bit, which has opened up Alperen Şengün's game. Şengün is in the top-10 in assists and over his last six games, he has averaged 23 points, 10 rebounds and 6 assists with 1.7 stocks, shooting 58% from the field.

Bucks: Brayden Burries and Ryan Rollins are still battling at point guard. Burries is starting, but he hasn't separated. I understand if you don't want to roster either of them right now. I still have Burries in a couple spots because, with the season going the way it is, why not give the keys to your first-round pick?

Bulls: Chicago lost prized rookie Caleb Wilson for 4-6 weeks with a fractured hand. Matas Buzelis' numbers are up pretty much across the board in the three games Wilson's been out. Coincidence? I think not. I still say sell high the next time Josh Giddey goes off.

Clippers: Brandon Ingram could debut before the road trip ends Nov. 25, and they need him.

Kings: At 1-12 with Doug Christie on the hot seat, rookie Darius Acuff Jr. keeps a massive usage role no matter who's coaching.

Knicks: The defending champs are 7-6, and Josh Hart (two straight scoreless games) admits he's "not in a good headspace." Mikal Bridges is still going through it, too.

Mavericks: It's a lot to ask of a teenager, but Cooper Flagg is making it happen. He's a top-10 player in points and 9-cat through Week 4.

Nets: The Day'Ron Sharpe panic should die down now. He's been a top-35 player in 9-cat over the past two weeks and was top-60 in High Score in Week 4. Let the good times roll.

Nuggets: Jamal Murray and Nikola Jokić are the first duo since 1997-98 to log 1,000 assists to each other. Nobody has figured out how to stop their dribble handoff and pick-and-roll yet.

Thunder: It takes a village and though SGA's efficiency is suffering, he's putting the team on his back. I'd try to buy some Ajay Mitchell stock.

Trail Blazers: Who would've thought the Blazers would be 9-4 right now? With Ja Morant out with a strained hamstring, Deni Avdija has been a first-round asset. He's elevated his efficiency and scoring to 25-6-6 with 51/40/75 shooting splits without Ja on the floor.
