Nets rocked by lowly Bulls in ugly night to forget

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

That's how the Nets described the 137-107 butt-kicking they took from the Bulls on Wednesday night in front of a sellout crowd of 17,548 at Barclays Center, humiliated on the home floor.

Not by Chicago. But by their own lack of fight. Of fortitude.

Patrick Williams shoots over Danny Wolf during the first half of the Nets' 137-107 blowout loss to the Bulls on Nov. 18, 2026, at Barclays Center. Corey Sipkin for the NY POST

"That was embarrassing. Been in the league for 13 years, and that's one of the worst games I've ever been a part of. And not because of the score … but it's how we did it. That wasn't cool. Nothing about it was cool," Julius Randle said. "We've got to find a way to figure out the root of it or whatever, but that's just unacceptable."

"It's just a pride, an effort, the competitive spirit, all of that, it was nonexistent."

The Nets (3-11) scored the first bucket and never led again, trailed by 37 at home, got shredded defensively and once again got punched in the face and never hit back.

They never even got up off the mat, allowing 53.5 percent shooting and 21-for-45 from deep.

"Very disappointed, and it was just starting with effort. We were not a competitive group, we were not a connected group, we were not a selfless group," Nets coach Jordi Fernández said. "So, I saw selfishness, I saw pointing fingers, and I saw guys that didn't always play hard."

The most galling part was after Monday's loss to the 76ers, they talked about having to punch first. Randle reiterated that at Wednesday morning's shootaround.

Brooklyn head coach Jordi Fernández reacts during the first half of the Nets' blowout loss to the Bulls. Corey Sipkin for the NY POST

But the Nets haven't been able to walk how they talk.

Asked if his message wasn't getting through, Fernández said, "I can try to say it in Spanish because the English didn't understand. So I'll keep trying and find whatever language works, but yes, the effort was not good."

In the past few days, the Nets have suffered one-sided beatings at the hands of both the Grizzlies and now the Bulls, routs that reflect poorly on their own rebuild and raise real questions about the big picture.

But Randle said this is less about players having to look to Fernández for messaging and more about having to look in the mirror.

Julius Randle drives past Jalen Smith (25) and rises up for a dunk during the Nets' blowout loss to the Bulls. Corey Sipkin for the NY POST

"Nah, as a man, you've gotta look yourself in the mirror, come in to work and figure out what matters. When winning is what matters, then we'll do the necessary things for that," Randle said before admitting, "but no, it's not there right now."

Egor Dëmin had 22 points, four assists and four rebounds, while Nolan Traore added 13 points and four assists. Mikel Brown Jr. scored a dozen of his 15 in the fourth quarter, with the game well out of hand.

But leading scorer Michael Porter Jr. was worse than invisible, too conspicuously poor to hide. He had just five points on 1-for-7 shooting and left the locker room before the media got inside.

"Everything was missing," Dëmin said. "That is not who we want to be, and we definitely got to be better than that."

The Nets gave up 25 to Matas Buzelis, and Josh Giddey (18 points, 14 rebounds, nine assists) just missed a triple-double. Ex-Net Nic Claxton added 14 off the bench.

It was another failure against a team they're measured against.

Memphis tanked last season to land Cam Boozer, whom the Nets had liked. The Grizzlies are now 11-4 after wins over Golden State, Portland and now Cleveland.

But at least getting blown out in Memphis was understandable. These Bulls are terrible, and played without rookie Caleb Wilson (hand injury).

The Nets came into Wednesday with an identical mark to Chicago and another chance to measure themselves against another rebuilding rival. And once again, they were found wanting.

"It's just unacceptable by us, by everybody, not just one individual, everybody," Brown said. "Stuff like that just can't happen, it's just unacceptable. That type of effort, that's not what the fans deserve. That is not what everybody bought their tickets to come watch this game deserve. That's just not acceptable."
