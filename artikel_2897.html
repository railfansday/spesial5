NFL news, live updates: Ravens name undrafted rookie Joe Fagnano starting QB; Cardinals RB Jeremiyah Love headed to IR with ankle injury; Lions LB Jack Campbell remains hospitalized with neck injury

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Week 8 of the 2026 NFL season is nearly a wrap, with just the Chicago Bears and Seattle Seahawks left to play on "Monday Night Football."

There was plenty of excitement on Sunday, including two games that went into overtime, but they unfortunately weren't without injuries. Baltimore Ravens quarterback Lamar Jackson made his return to the lineup after rehabbing an ankle injury, but that excitement was short-lived, as he suffered a thumb injury on his first pass attempt of the game. Per head coach Jesse Minter, he'll require surgery and a stint on the injured reserve list — a brutal blow.

Backup Tyler Huntley is also dealing with a turf toe injury, which forced Baltimore to resort to their emergency third quarterback, Joe Fagnano. He completed 10-of-21 passes for 91 yards and an interception in relief of Jackson and Huntley.

The Ravens are playing on a short week, as they're set to host the Jacksonville Jaguars on "Thursday Night Football" in Week 9, and Fagnano will officially be QB1 for that matchup. The 25-year-old, who played his college ball at Maine and then UConn, will be the second undrafted rookie to start under center for Baltimore in the Ravens' 32-year history, per ESPN's Jamison Hensley.

The Detroit Lions handed the Minnesota Vikings their first loss of the season, but lost a key playmaker on defense as All-Pro linebacker Jack Campbell suffered a scary-looking neck injury. He was immobilized on a backboard before being transported to the hospital as his teammates gathered around him in an emotional scene.

Follow along with Yahoo Sports for the latest NFL news, injury updates and more coming out of Week 8.

The Colts aren't purging their entire roster.

Per NFL Network, the Colts and defensive lineman Adetomiwa Adebawore have agreed to a $35 million contract extension with a $13 million roster bonus.

The deal adds $22.5 million in new money to his contract through the 2027 season, per the report. Adebawore was previously on track to became a free agent in the offseason at the conclusion of his four-year, $4.6 million rookie contract.

Adebawore, 25, has tied his career high with four sacks through eight games in addition to 19 tackles and eight quarterback hits.

The New Orleans Saints are on the kicker market.

The Saints announced on Tuesday that they have released veteran kicker Daniel Carlson after Carlson missed another field goal in a 16-10 Week 7 loss to the Pittsburgh Steelers.

Carlson missed a 52-yard attempt with 9:42 remaining that would have cut Pittsburgh's lead to 16-13. He finished the game hitting 1 of 2 field-goal attempts, dropping his season success rate on field goals to a career-low 66.7% (8 of 12).

The Ravens injury woes continue, as the team placed kicker Tyler Loop on the injured reserve list with a groin injury on Tuesday.

Loop has made 13 of 17 field goals attempted this season, having gone 5 for 5 on kicks from 50-plus yards. He missed one field goal in Week 8's loss to the Buffalo Bills from 34 yards.

The team signed kicker Jake Moody to the practice squad on Oct. 20, and he'll presumably step up in Loop's absence.

If officially elevated to the active roster, the Ravens will be Moody's fourth team over the past two seasons. He hopped between the San Francisco 49ers (who selected him in the third round of the 2023 NFL Draft), Chicago Bears and Washington Commanders last season, good on 19 of 23 attempts in 2025.

The Buffalo Bills have released safety C.J. Gardner-Johnson, NFL insider Jordan Schultz reported on Tuesday. The team then confirmed the move on social media.

Gardner-Johnson served a one-game suspension this past week after spitting on Las Vegas Raiders tight end in Week 6, an action that led to his ejection. The Bills then had a bye in Week 7, which led to him serving that suspension in Week 8.

Gardner-Johnson has been a bit of a journeyman in recent seasons, with the Bills being his sixth team in eight seasons. He started his career with the New Orleans Saints, who drafted him in the fourth round out of Florida. He's also had stints with the Philadelphia Eagles, Chicago Bears, Detroit Lions and Houston Texans.

He started in all six games of his games played for the Bills this past season. Damar Halmin got the start in Gardner Johnson's place while he served his suspension.

Baltimore Ravens head coach Jesse Minter told the media on Tuesday that the team would start undrafted rookie quarterback Joe Fagnano on Thursday against the Jacksonville Jaguars.

The announcement comes as starting quarterback Lamar Jackson is headed to injured reserve with a thumb injury and backup quarterback Tyler Huntley was diagnosed with turf toe.

Fagnano will be the second undrafted rookie to start under center for Baltimore in the Ravens' 32-year history, per ESPN's Jamison Hensley.

The rookie finished his collegiate career at UConn. He played in 24 games with the Huskies and ended his time with them having accumulated 5,252 passing yards and 48 touchdowns. Fagnano is less mobile than Jackson or Huntley and rushed for just 223 yards and 4 touchdowns on 93 carries at UConn.

Although Baltimore Ravens quarterback Joe Fagnano was spotted wearing a guard/brace on his right ankle Tuesday, as captured by The Athletic's Jeff Zrebiec, he was practicing, two days out from a "Thursday Night Football" game against the Jacksonville Jaguars.

Lamar Jackson (thumb) and Tyler Huntley (toe) have already been ruled out for the matchup. Jackson, of course, could be done for the season.

Huntley is navigating what's reportedly thought to be turf toe.

So Fagnano, a 25-year-old undrafted rookie who played his college ball at UConn and Maine, could make his first career NFL start this week, in primetime no less.

Fagnano was listed as a limited participant in the Ravens' walkthrough on Monday. Baltimore signed fellow QB Austin Reed to the practice squad that day.

Arizona Cardinals rookie running back Jeremiyah Love is headed to injured reserve with a right high-ankle sprain, NFL Network's Ian Rapoport reports.

The injury and IR designation will sideline Love for a minimum of four games. The Cardinals have upcoming games against the Seahawks, Rams, Chiefs and Commanders.

Love injured his ankle in Sunday's win over the Dallas Cowboys when his ankle got rolled underneath his body on a tackle. He was able to get up to walk to the sideline, but he did not return to the game as the Cardinals rallied from a 14-3 halftime deficit for a 33-30 overtime win.

Trade rumors have surrounded Arizona Cardinals defensive end Josh Sweat for a while now. NFL insider Jordan Schultz reported Tuesday that the belief around the league is that the 29-year-old Sweat would be open to a trade.

That said, per Schultz, Arizona's asking price is believed to be high, and the team has given the impression that it'd be OK holding onto the pass rusher.

Sweat is in his second season with the Cardinals, who signed him to a four-year contract in free agency last year, a deal reportedly worth $76.4 million, including $41 million guaranteed.

When Sweat arrived in Arizona, he was reuniting with then-Cardinals head coach Jonathan Gannon, who was previously Sweat's defensive coordinator with the Philadelphia Eagles. But the Cardinals moved on from Gannon after last season. That coaching change raised questions about Sweat's future in the desert.

During the 2025 season, Sweat piled up 12 sacks. He got off to a slow start this time around but has logged 19 total tackles and three sacks in his past five games.

Cardinals rookie Jeremiyah love seemingly can't catch a break on the injury front, with head coach Mike LaFleur telling reporters on Monday that the rookie has suffered another ankle injury.

Love missed multiple preseason games with a high right ankle sprain, and missed two games last month with a left ankle injury. However, LaFleur said that the injury Love sustained on Sunday was a new injury, not a re-aggravation of one of his previous knocks.

"This is new, so this is not the left ankle or the right ankle, it's a new portion of the ankle," LaFleur said.

Love exited Sunday's win over the Cowboys after his ankle got rolled under his body on a tackle in the first half. The rookie running back was able to walk to the sideline but slammed his helmet down, clearly frustrated, before heading into the locker room; he did not return to the game.

LaFleur said that he'll give reporters more information on Wednesday, adding that he wanted to get a chance to talk to Love first.

Garrett's played just one game for the Rams since the blockbuster offseason trade that sent him to Los Angeles from the Cleveland Browns. He underwent knee surgery after struggling to make an impact in the Rams' season-opening loss to the 49ers in Australia and has been sidelined since.

Garrett's planned return to practice doesn't mean he'll be ready to play Sunday against the Washington Commanders. But it's a positive sign for his return to the playing field.

His return to practice from injured reserve opens a 21-day window for Garrett to be elevated to the active roster or placed on season-ending injured reserve.

Arizona Cardinals head coach Mike LaFleur wasn't ready to commit to a starting quarterback on Sunday afternoon, even if his backup just led them to a wild overtime win against the Dallas Cowboys.

But just 24 hours after that victory, he made the move. Gardner Minshew is their starter moving forward.

LaFleur opted to bench Jacoby Brissett for Minshew on Sunday afternoon at AT&T Stadium, a decision that eventually led to their 33-30 overtime win against the Cowboys. LaFleur declined to commit to either quarterback when asked directly after the game, even though the expectation was that Minshew would remain in the role.

"I'm just excited about this win right now," he said, via ESPN.

Now, Minshew will get the start against the Seattle Seahawks on Sunday. Carson Beck will serve as his backup, and Brissett will be the team's third-string quarterback.
