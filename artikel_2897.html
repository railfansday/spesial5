Not a cold streak; the Utah Jazz are historically bad

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

When the buzzer sounded on San Antonio's 110-102 win in Salt Lake City, the reigning Western Conference champions tallied their 12th consecutive win, pushing their record to an NBA-leading 12-2. The Utah Jazz, losers of Sunday's Delta Center duel, likewise extended an active streak of 12 games. 12 losses in a row.

12 straight losses are tied for the second-longest losing streak in franchise history, but that's not even the worst part of this all.

This is only the second time the Jazz have started a season 1-13. The first time? The Jazz played their home games in New Orleans, Louisiana, and were playing their very first season as an NBA franchise. That 1974-75 team opened the season with a 1-16 record and fired head coach Scotty Robertson following their 14th loss.

This Utah Jazz team isn't just bad — they're historically bad.

Yes, this year's team has been playing at a deficit when it comes to injuries. They would have likely picked up a few more wins to this point were Jaren Jackson Jr., Jusuf Nurkic, and Lauri Markkanen not seated courtside, modeling the Polo Fall 2026 line at the end of the bench. Even so, Utah's foundation is riddled with cracks and is nowhere near the level of a playoff participant.

Undersized at the wing, Utah is consistently out-physicaled and outdone athletically against every team they've faced.

It's not all bad news, though. The Jazz's stars and veterans are all expected to return in a month or so, and they have already faced each of the Western Conference's top-six teams. After facing this slate in the early season, Utah's schedule will only get easier:

Even at full strength, Utah couldn't have reasonably expected to escape that gauntlet with a winning record.

The biggest issue facing the Jazz right now isn't injuries. It isn't inexperience. It isn't even personnel. The Jazz simply don't know how to win — they've never tried before.

It's a team that has grown accustomed to losing for the last four years, and those habits have reappeared night after night.

They took San Antonio and New Orleans into overtime, only to concede a 12-0 run in each of those additional five-minute periods. They fumbled away a 10-point third-quarter lead against Houston but clawed back to within one point at the 2:46 mark, only to relinquish a 2-8 run in crunch time. Down just one point against Memphis with one minute remaining, Utah failed to score again. Behind LA by one point with three minutes to go, the Jazz still couldn't seal the deal with JJJ and Markkanen active.

They've put themself in a position to win several times, but only once did they hold on to win.

How's this for glass-half-empty: Utah is one Dejounte Murray jumper away from being 0-14.

The Jazz front office continues to back Will Hardy as head coach, determined to support the man who patiently endured four years of non-competitive basketball to lead Utah back into the playoffs when the time is right. Just as Utah's roster is learning how to win in a competitive NBA, Hardy's baptism by fire has likewise left him a bit seared.

Now is not the time to take drastic measures — still 68 games to go in the season — but Utah's issues are buried much deeper than a string of bad luck.

Calvin Barrett is the Associate Editor for SLC Dunk and a Utah Jazz columnist for the Standard-Examiner. Originally from Springville, Utah, he currently writes from his desk in Japan.
