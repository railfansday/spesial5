10 Biggest Reveals From Final Fantasy 14's Tokyo Fanfest

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Stephanie Watel is a writer for DualShockers. She has 3 years of experience writing about video games, from news to lists to in-depth guides.

To be perfectly candid, Final Fantasy XIV’s Tokyo leg of the Fanfest circuit ahead of Evercold was a surreal and almost puzzling experience. Most of the expansion’s biggest surprises were already spoiled in both Anaheim and Berlin, while Tokyo essentially served as a clarity-and-elaboration session for much of what we already knew in a basic sense.

However, that isn’t to say that director Naoki (“Yoshi-P”) Yoshida’s keynote wasn’t full of impressively dense information for players to soak in. There was a lot to go around, to the point that Yoshi-P went almost 30 minutes past his allotted runtime for the presentation, on top of saying that it originally came out to almost four hours during rehearsal. That still doesn’t even include the “several” Producer Live Letter streams he also has planned leading up to Evercold’s launch to go over everything even further.

If anything, it’s a perfect metaphor for just how dramatically different Evercold is actually set to be, compared to all previous expansions in the game. Most of the game’s major content is getting a systemic overhaul, and those changes branch out even further, down to some of the smallest details. It’s almost on par with A Realm Reborn’s aptly named “rebirth” of the entire game over a decade ago, without having to shut the entire game down for nearly a year.

All that said, Tokyo Fanfest had plenty of information for players to take with them, and here are the biggest reveals from the entire keynote.

Evercold dropping in the dead of winter in January was poetically on point, and up through Berlin’s Fanfest we’ve known that Final Fantasy 14’s latest and most ambitious expansion yet was set to kick off a busy year for gamers (with the likes of Fable and Final Fantasy 7: Revelation also set for early Spring).

However, at the top of Yoshi-P’s keynote in Tokyo, he announced with regret that Evercold has gotten a three-week delay to late February. Now the expansion will debut on February 23, 2027, with a special Early Access period for those who pre-order starting on February 19.

As for why this is the case, Yoshi-P explained that, initially, Evercold would have been “barely” ready to go in time for their original January release deadline, which going by a three-week delay means that the expansion would have arrived at the tail end of month. However, due to “several issues” they encountered during development, Yoshi-P made the executive decision to impose the delay to ensure those problems are properly resolved before launch.

Despite this, the delay hasn’t impacted when players will get to preorder Evercold, and you can do so for all platforms starting November 4, 2026. Ultimately, given just how much is changing in the game systemically with Evercold, it’s just as well that this delay happened, and January did seem a bit ambitious already. Nonetheless, the rock isn’t getting kicked too far down the street, and the expansion will be in players’ hands soon enough.

On the heels of Evercold’s amended release date announcement, Yoshi-P pivoted straight to the grand reveal of the expansion’s second new combat job. Granted, there was strong speculation already after the many hints dropped at Berlin’s Fanfest that it revolved around something akin to a Hunter or a Falconer, and it was already confirmed that it would be a physical ranged DPS.

Finally, all the theory crafting was laid to rest with the reveal of Zephyr, courtesy of Visna posing as the job’s mascot. As shown in the active demonstration video, the player wields a crossbow weapon while flanked by a magical falcon familiar known as a Nephele. The video displayed various combat abilities, from basic attacks with the crossbow to more striking, magic-imbued ones that have your work together with your falcon to produce some spectacular moves.

What’s more, during a Q&A session on the second day of Tokyo’s Fanfest, Yoshi-P answered a fan’s question about being able to customize your bird companion. It turns out that you actually can thanks to a dual-dye system that allows you to recolor the bird when visiting a “certain NPC” in the game.

While FF14 players knew that changes to gear would be inevitable with Evercold’s new activity system, Yoshi-P surprised just about everyone during the keynote with sweeping alterations to raid gear in particular.

While Seasonal Gear is intended for “everyday” players with a simple optimization routine, Raid Gear comes with a few different new details. The first and most surprising change is that every single person in a raid group will now get their own personal chest drop after completing the duty, rather than having the group roll the dice on a single chest once a week. This will make raiding significantly more efficient (especially for premade groups or “statics”) in lieu of having to farm several runs just for the sake of gearing everyone up for the next raid.

Alongside that, players will also get to edit the “secondary attributes” of raid gear, allowing them to personally customize how their earned gear performs rather than just being stuck with preset attributes. Also, the item level of Raid Gear can be raised twice, and materia slots will unlock upon reaching that maximum item level.

Yoshi-P went into explicit detail in this section of the keynote to explain exactly how the gear upgrading process will work, and while it felt somewhat like a labyrinth of steps and stat numbers, it nonetheless looks like a promising move in the right direction for the game’s raid community in general.

The core that’s tying mostly everything together in Evercold’s revamped gameplay is the all-new Activity Level system. The Berlin keynote gave a soft introduction to this system with a basic explanation of weekly missions that will replace Final Fantasy 14’s traditional daily duties.

During the Tokyo keynote, Yoshi-P tied that back in with the introduction of the Activity Level system, which is how all players will obtain their Seasonal Gear throughout each “season” of the expansion’s patch cycle. As you complete Missions from the long list of available options, you’ll earn Activity Points that in turn raise your Activity Level. As that level goes up, you’ll gradually unlock rewards for your efforts, ranging from gil to emotes to mounts, and then so-called “gear categories”.

Unlocking these gear categories allows you to receive gear pieces of that category (e.g. - accessories) for your jobs. Furthermore, each category is shared across all jobs, so you don’t have to worry about your alt jobs missing out on those rewards. Redeeming those gear rewards is also done via the game’s new character UI dashboard.

Up to this point, the basic understanding that we have about our journey on the Fourth is that most of it will occur on the planet’s Fimm Himnar realmships, or the “Five Firmaments Left to Mankind”. These absolutely massive realmships each pertain to a different element and a certain sector of produced resources that can’t otherwise be obtained on the frozen surface.

Back in the Berlin Fanfest, Yoshi-P provided a detailed look at two of these realmships. One was Naglfar, the Realmship of Water where Visna resides. The other was Hringhorni, the Realmship of Fire where we now know that Bastion-wielding Baldr serves as its foreman.

This time around, in-depth looks were provided for two more of these realmships. One was the Realmship of Earth, Sessrymnir, and a walkthrough video gave insight into the ship’s agriculture and mining production areas. Meanwhile, the Realmship of Wind, Skidbladnir, was also shown off in all its glory. Not only is it where the ridiculously adorable Kottr live, but it’s where they work on the production of airships, the main method of transportation on the Fourth.

The fifth and final realmship remains a mystery, which is likely for the sake of story spoilers, and it also remains to be seen what other explorable areas there may be. After all, a traditional Final Fantasy XIV expansion normally has at least six major zones that players can freely navigate, and the five realmships leave at least one of those zones in limbo. The closest guess would be somewhere on the subzero surface of the Fourth, but players will have to wait and see in February.

Alliance Raids aren’t just getting a new collaboration with Neon Genesis Evangelion for Evercold, they’re also getting their own systemic overhaul for the new expansion and beyond. These changes, according to Yoshi-P, reflect a few overarching issues that the content has suffered from over past expansions.

These issues start with skewed runtimes for the game’s entire roster of Alliance Raids. Some are generally more “efficient” to run, while others (e.g. - Tower of Paradigm’s Reach) are notoriously long and thus considered not optimal for dailies, leveling, farming gear, what have you. This gaping divide in runtime has since led to a bias within the player community, and leaves certain Alliance Raids infamously difficult to queue for, much less actually complete.

However, Yoshi-P has sought out a solution in the form of subdividing Alliance Raids into two instanced chunks. The first chunk will consist of the overall raid’s first two bosses (with trash mobs in between), while the second chunk will be the final two bosses in the same fashion. After completing the first half and obtaining your rewards from it, you’ll then be able to queue separately at your own discretion for the second half, complete it, and earn separate rewards from that chunk.

This is to help alleviate the burden of farming endlessly for certain gear items over the course of a 30 minute to 1 hour raid instance, all while also being able to enjoy the content more feasibly with less of a time commitment. Furthermore, Yoshi-P hinted at not having to roll anymore for loot from these raids, with a promise to elaborate more on that closer to Evercold’s launch.

Finally, Alliance Raids get one more big update starting in Evercold in the form of a legitimate “Savage” mode. Influenced by the success of Variant and Criterion Dungeon content, this will be an 8-player mode, rather than the usual lineup of 24 WoLs, and will function more as a boss rush-style instance with two bosses from the original raid set at “Savage Turn 3”-level difficulty. This mode will kick off with Evercold’s Evangelion collaboration raid series.

While many of the game’s existing combat modes are getting their own unique changes, there’s also an entirely new type of combat content debuting during Evercold called the Gauntlet of Power.

As its name suggests, it’s a gauntlet-style instance that functions as a more casual (compared to Alliance Raid Savage, at least) boss rush for players to embark on at their leisure. That is to say, it’s a mode that can be easily accessed from almost anywhere via the game’s overhauled UI. It can be played either with a premade group or a random queue, in much the same fashion as other instanced duties in the game.

This boss rush consists of up to five “floors” for a light party group, and clearing each floor leads to rewards that weren’t yet revealed during the keynote. Players can vote whether or not to continue the gauntlet, which provides a flexibility that not many of the other types of battle content in the game have. Despite being simple in concept, it may very well help bridge a gap that’s been lingering in Final Fantasy’s 14 gameplay flow for several years.

What’s more, this mode will help keep things interesting by choosing from an assortment of 200 different existing bosses in the game. This assures that the mode will traditionally prevalent issue of content monotony as much as possible while maintaining a sense of liberty and gratification for the player to make the most of their time.

Another big surprise that Yoshi-P was surprisingly candid about during Tokyo Fanfest’s Q&A session is that Allagan Tomestones of Poetics, the currency that’s remained in the game the longest (aside from maybe MGP), is set to officially retire in Evercold.

Yoshi-P did say that he shouldn’t really be discussing it just yet, but did confirm the plan nonetheless. He did offer a brief explanation in that “there will be something that allows you to trade for gear for alt jobs, or past rewards; you don’t have to worry about it for now. It just won’t be Poetics anymore.”

For players who have been in Final Fantasy 14 for at least some amount of time, Allagan Tomestones has since become the fall-back currency for the game’s more niche purchases. Alternatively, you could redeem them for one of the game’s more recent variants of tomestone currencies. Beyond A Realm Reborn, Poetics lose much of their remaining purpose, and at this point they feel more like a souvenir that just collects dust from one expansion to the next.

However, on that note, with the game’s dramatic revamp to the Activity Points system and Seasonal Gear, it remains to be seen if other tomestone currencies, such as those we had in Dawntrail, will keep some sort of utility themselves. Yoshi-P hasn’t been abundantly clear on just how long tomestone currency will hang on for, and if it will perhaps deviate to some other set purpose in the game. Otherwise, Evercold may be the “poetic” farewell to tomestone currency across the board as we know it.

While not necessarily anything to do with the game’s many different content systems, the keynote did get a small update on the story front in the form of identity reveals for certain characters. Baldr, the one wielding Bastion gear in the trailer, was revealed as the foreman of the Hringhorni realmship. Meanwhile, an entirely new face appeared in the final version of the trailer that ironically looked very familiar to many fans.

The presentation revealed her as Lagertha, the first mate of the Naglfar realmship where Visna resides. However, beyond finding out her purpose on the ship, fans are moreso scratching their heads over her striking resemblance to Ysayle, a key character who tragically passed during the events of Heavensward and the Dragonsong War.

However, players with a sharp eye may have caught the glimpse of her during the final Dawntrail MSQ content in Patch 7.56, meaning that her appearance is no coincidence. The most likely explanation for her presence on the Fourth is that Lagertha is her reflection. As to how deeply her character will impact the story of Evercold, players will simply have to wait and look forward to it.

On another non-combat tangent, Final Fantasy 14’s ever-controversial housing system is getting more bells and whistles added to it with Evercold’s patch cycle. Those changes are spread across “three pillars”, as indicated in the presentation.

First off, as hinted over the past several months, all houses will get an “interior area size customization” feature. This means that, for example, you can customize the interior of your small house to have the layout of a large house, for a currently undisclosed gil fee. Not only that, the furnishing limit will flex according to the layout you choose, so there’s no need to worry about getting cut off on your desired decor.

Secondly, floating interior furnishings will no longer be something only achievable through unconventional PC glitching controls. Along with an all-new placement interface, players will be able to move furnishings more freely on the vertical axis on both console and PC platforms.

The third and final pillar consists of adding even more housing plots, though Yoshi-P noted that they’re still discussing how exactly they want to achieve that, whether it’s through more, smaller areas, “hidden” areas, et cetera. While more plots feels moreso like another bandaid to a system that itself needs an overhaul, it’s at least nice that they’re doing their best to improve what’s in place until that change hopefully comes one day.
