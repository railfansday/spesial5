How to change layers in WoW Forever? (After the layer size change)

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

If you want to play with friends in WoW: Forever, you have to go back to one of WoW's most dreaded concepts - layers and shards.

World of Warcraft: Forever skips realms and introduces gameplay rulesets instead, where you can pick your preferred playstyle and get whisked to an Azeroth populated with like-minded individuals. But things can get complicated if, say, you want to play with your friends or guildmates from across the world.

Plus, it’s not like Blizzard will squeeze everyone using your gameplay ruleset into the same world instance. This is where layers come in, and it’s similar to, but also very different from, its original iteration in Classic.

Others new to WoW might be wondering what exactly layers are, and how they might affect their WoW: Forever experience.

So we know MMOs have servers, and each WoW server has a realm that contains a version of Azeroth and the players inside it. Of course, we can’t expect Blizzard to spawn everyone at once, since we’d overcrowd the game world and break the experience.

When a location gets a high-enough population, WoW uses sharding to copy the area into a “shard” and transfers some players there. A shard is a perfectly working copy of a location, complete with NPCs and objects, except you can only see and interact with players of the same shard.

The idea is to manage overpopulation by keeping player counts at a certain number. This keeps lag manageable and ensures there are enough mobs and quest items for everyone in any given zone.

Since shards are zone-wide, you can see players “disappear” into a new zone because they’re crossing over to a new shard (called phasing). If you’re approaching a zone where all shards are at capacity, you’d hit a soft “loading” queue before you enter. Worst case, you get disconnected from the server, which can ruin your momentum.

WoW Retail still uses sharding. It sometimes implements cross-realm zones (CRZs), so low-population realms can share low-population zones.

We got to layering at the launch of WoW Classic in 2019, where it was presented as a bigger solution to server population. Sharding at the time just didn’t feel like a practical choice, as they’d have to create hundreds of shards per zone.

“We had some serious concerns about what would happen when realms started to mature, and populations shifted,” Blizzard noted about sharding in the release of Battle for Wintergrasp in Wrath Classic. “Would we have hundreds of realms with low populations a few months in?”

So they came up with layers, which is sharding but applied to the whole game world. Upon meeting specific population conditions, Blizzard can create a copy of Azeroth and transfer players there.

But it’s not a perfect solution. Too much layering could result in dead cities, and too little overcrowds areas. It can hurt the economy as well, as you can layer-hop to get more resources from another layer once your layer’s route runs out.

Classic moved away from layering just a few months in November 2019 and converged everyone into a single layer.

Instead of realms, we choose a ruleset that represents our preferred playstyle and play with others who choose the same. In theory, it’s a “realmless” system, and our ruleset of choice is functionally a mega server that our fellow ruleset comrades share.

Forever seems to have retained the Retail sharding system. But this time, there’s a chance you can auto-switch layers during the transition. This seems to have carried over from beta. Redditor u/Kellian noted that per-zone “loading” means hostiles and friendlies phase in and out, and can even stop PvP.

Building upon the ruleset is what Blizzard called the familiar faces system. As explained in their BlizzCon 2026 deep-dive, this will have players more consistently encounter the same strangers throughout their leveling journey. The goal is for these familiar faces to give Forever a stronger sense of community spirit, and may even encourage teaming up while you level. This also extends to guilds, so they can potentially pull you into their layers.

At random intervals during your sessions (sometimes none at all), you might encounter a prompt that warns you of a World Refresh. Either you do the refresh now or wait a couple of minutes. This isn’t any cause for alarm, as it just means the game will transfer you to a new instance or layer.

These refreshes often happen to balance the current population. You remain in the same spot, but your immediate surroundings might be different, like there might be more players, more mobs, or both.

At launch, game dev Aggrend said they would look into how players lamented how sparsely populated the game world felt. A few days later, some players noticed so many characters for so few mobs.

This was especially challenging in PvP, where one faction can dominate a layer and make it difficult for the opposing one to level up or gather resources.

Redditor u/sknilegap doesn’t even call it layering at all, but actually just sharding. And their problem has less to do with the technology itself, but more with its impact on the experience at large.

“I am fine with the necessity of the system. Just keep it immersive,” they said in their post. “Make me sticky to those crossing over around the same time window as me. So […] we all see each other on the other side of the next zone, grouped or not.”
