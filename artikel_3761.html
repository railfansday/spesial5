From what we're already seeing of Grand Theft Auto 6 on console, it's boding very well for a future PC release. It might only be targeting 30 fps on console, but with how stable that is and with the graphics looking so stunning on older PS5/PS5 Pro hardware, I've got high hopes for how it might run on our gaming PCs.

Digital Foundry (DF) has taken one for the team here by doing some performance testing on an inferior platform (cheap shot, I'm sorry). And judging from the screenshots, videos, and frame rate analysis so far, GTA 6 looks very much next-gen in terms of scale, scope, and fidelity.

We're talking fine detail on the local scale combined with open, bustling sight lines to the horizon, depending on your vantage point. All very noticeably saturated by incredible ray traced lighting, and running at a stable 30 fps on the PS5 Pro.

It's hard to describe how fantastic GTA looks. It does seem like a new era for open world games.

What particularly impresses me, judging from DF's gameplay and graphics shots, is how seamlessly fine detail combines with great lighting and breaks into long distance views.

One minute you're inside a bustling store, with incredibly realistic ambient lighting and reflections thanks to the ray tracing implementation (plus ray traced shadows on the PS5 Pro), admiring the stranded hair on all the NPCs and the little touches such as drinks actually draining in the bottle you drink from.

The next, you can be looking across the entire city without losing any noticeable detail. This seamlessness does make it seem like a real, lively world.

A large part of this immersion seems to come from the lighting. You're getting full ray traced global illumination, apparently at 1440p resolution which, on the PS5 Pro, reportedly looks more like 4K (and on the base PS5 apparently looks like it might be 1440p upscaled). The PS5 (non-Pro) version looks a little more blurry and lacks the level of shadow definition, but for the most part both versions look great. At least as far as I can tell from DF's footage, anyway.

All of the above is only good news if it runs well, and thankfully on this front it looks like it does. Yes, we're only talking 30 fps, but on the PS5 Pro version, at least, that's a very stable 30 fps. DF says there wasn't a single noticeable stutter or drop in over 50 hours of gameplay, and it's only when you start blowing up cars on the street that you get brief drops down to as low as 24 fps.

The PS5 (non-Pro) is different, struggling to consistently maintain 30 fps even when just driving around the city. But that console has significantly worse hardware compared to the Pro, so it's to be expected—the GPU in the PS5 has just 36 compute units (CUs) compared to the PS5 Pro's 60.

As far as playing GTA 6 on PC is concerned, I think this means we can be hopeful. The PS5 Pro's AMD GPU does have some RDNA 3/4 features, but for the most part it's an RDNA 2 chip. RDNA 2 graphics cards for PC are Radeon RX 6000-series ones. So, even with an older PC using a graphics card that is two generations old, it's possible you'll be able to get the gorgeous graphics you see on the PS5 Pro at a stable 30 fps.

At what settings? Well, that remains to be seen. But considering we'll almost certainly get some more customisation options over on PC, we should be able to eke out as much performance as we can from it, tailored to our hardware.

If you have a newer PC with a newer graphics card, I'd be hoping for 60 fps+ performance, and for that to be stable. The RX 9070 XT, for instance, should outperform a PS5 Pro considerably, despite only having a handful of extra CUs, thanks to the much newer architecture a higher power limit.

Particularly promising is just how stable that 30 fps seems to be on the PS5 Pro. People often think about the average, but don't consider frame pacing and 1% lows. On those fronts, GTA 6 seems very well optimised.

Our Chris has spent 100 hours with the Playstation 5 version of GTA 6, and has come away with some very positive impressions. Here's hoping the PC release lives up to the hype, and that those of us on older rigs might be able to get in on the action without too many pricey upgrades.
