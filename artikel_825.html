'You can't sit still and do nothing!' - MVG 'buzzing' for world title bid

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Michael van Gerwen reached the Grand Slam of Darts semi-finals and is among the favourites for the World Darts Championship, beginning December 11 live on Sky Sports; Van Gerwen previously won the Sid Waddell Trophy in 2014, 2017 and 2019

Michael van Gerwen is ready to take a pre-tournament scouting mission to Alexandra Palace ahead of his bid for a fourth World Darts Championship title next month, live on Sky Sports.

The Dutchman produced an impressive run to the Grand Slam of Darts semi-finals earlier this month, where he fired a nine-darter in the first leg of his quarter-final victory over Tom Sykes before being knocked out by compatriot Gian van Veen.

Van Gerwen is keen to build on his Wolverhampton performance at this weekend's Players Championship Finals ahead of turning attention to the Worlds, where he will be among the favourites to lift the Sid Waddell Trophy.

"That [consistency] is what you also need, especially at this time of year - you want to do yourself favours," Van Gerwen told Sky Sports. "We all know I had a tough period but you need to move on.

"You can't sit still and do nothing - you have to practise, you have to put time and effort in to produce some good darts. The field is wide, there's so many good players now, so you have to move along.

"I'm feeling okay, my game is starting to get in a good place. The consistency was there in the semi-final [of the Grand Slam], I missed too many doubles. You can't look back anymore because the World Championship is on the horizon."

Record crowds will be expected throughout this year's edition, which moves into Alexandra Palace's Great Hall and offers over 5,000 fans a session, with Van Gerwen keen to look at the new layout ahead of his world title challenge.

"When I go to London, as soon as I know when I play, two days before, I'm going to have a look [at Alexandra Palace] and see what's going on," Van Gerwen added.

"I'm one of the more prepared ones because you can't let yourself down in any way by thinking, 'I should have done this, I should have done that' - just do it!

"It [winning] would mean the world to me, but first let me win the first round, the second round and then go off, because it's going to be a long few weeks."

Van Veen finished runner-up to Gerwyn Price in Wolverhampton, extending his strong season in TV tournaments, with the world No 2 keen to go one better than last year's runner-up finish to Luke Littler at Alexandra Palace.

"I showed [form] at the Grand Slam, a very good tournament there," Van Veen told Sky Sports. "Very disappointed to not get it over the line in the final but hopefully I can have a good run [at the Players Championship Finals] and then carry that form into the Worlds.

"Last year [at the Worlds], it was only [about] winning that one game. Now I've got all the experience of getting through to the final. Hopefully we can do that again this year."

On his rise to world No 2, Van Veen added: "A lot of players say it's because it's based on the ranking money and it's highly influenced by the World Championship, and it is, but everyone had the chance to do it at the Worlds and I took mine.

"This year has been very good, especially on the TV tournaments. Very consistent with semi-finals and now even a final, so I belong somewhere around No 2 in the world. Maybe top four, top six, but I'm very pleased to be the No 2 in the world right now."
