More than 40,000 gather as Pope Leo XIV celebrates Mass in Uruguay

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Leo celebrated the first Mass of his South America tour less than a month after undergoing cancer surgery. He is seeking to raise interest in the Catholic Church in Uruguay, South America's most secular country.

The Vatican and local authorities in western Uruguay say around 40,000 people turned out on Saturday morning to participate in the first Mass of Pope Leo XIV's 12-day, three-nation tour of South America.

The Vatican said 30,000 people from Uruguay as well as neighboring Argentina and Brazil had registered for the event, adding that another 10,000 had braved the rain to attend the religious service.

Leo, who underwent surgery for lung cancer on October 13, was greeted by throngs of cheering fans as he made his way from the local airport to the sprawling Paris London Park in Paysandu — a city on the Uruguay River, some 375 kilometers (230 miles) west of the capital, Montevideo.

"He's the representative of Christ, but he's human," said Soledad Sanchez, who told France's AFP news agency that she had traveled far to be in Paysandu.

"We weren't sure if he could make it, but he pushed through for us," said Sanchez as tears welled up in her eyes. "He made it here to give us a message of peace because he knows our world needs it right now."

Saturday morning's Mass was dedicated to the Blessed Jacinto Vera, who was appointed Uruguay's first bishop by Pope Leo XIII in 1878.

The leader of the world's 1.4 billion Catholics praised Bishop Vera on Friday. Leo noted that the bishop had walked the countryside and helped the poor, adding that those whom he encountered "found something of Christ's goodness" in him.

The 71-year-old US-born pontiff, who holds US, Peruvian and Vatican citizenship, is seeking to raise interest in the Catholic Church in Uruguay, South America's most secular country, as a remedy for helping the nation and its citizens cope with the troubles of the modern world.

Leo underscored the need for that kind of outreach on Saturday afternoon when he visited a Montevideo neighborhood beset by drug violence and in which the Church has invested in public housing and rehabilitation projects for addicts.

"We cannot simply look the other way when we know there are children in Uruguay who do not go to school for fear that they might not return," said the pope. "And certainly, no child should ever suffer violence to the point of losing their life."

At his final event in the country — a prayer vigil in Montevideo's Centenary Stadium that drew some 60,000 young faithful — the pontiff called on young people to "dare to take the step of marrying and starting a family yourselves." He urged married couples to "open your homes to life — even when the world tells you that a child is a burden."

Across the region, the fertility rate has dropped from 5.8 children per woman in 1950 to 1.8 in 2024 — well below the 2.1 children per woman needed to keep a population stable — according to a recent study by the UN Population Fund.

On Sunday, Pope Leo will depart for Argentina, where he will meet with President Javier Milei, a libertarian-populist and close ally to US President Donald Trump. The US president has previously lashed out at the leader of the world's largest Christian faith over his calls to end wars and cruel immigration policies.
