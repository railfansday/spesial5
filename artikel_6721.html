How ‘Rivals’ Created an Age-Gap Relationship We’re Actually Rooting For

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

R ivals has returned to our screens, and Rutshire’s residents are hornier than ever. But Rutag fans might have to hang on a little longer to see them enter their relationship era. Frustrating? Not necessarily, as maybe this intense slow-burn is exactly the reason Rivals has managed to create an age-gap relationship we’re actually rooting for.

There is a lot of awareness around age-gap couples in pop culture right now, and audiences seem very done with the classic younger woman, older man dynamic. Movies like A24’s Babygirl and Amazon’s The Idea of You have gone some way toward challenging outdated tropes around age-related power dynamics, but that still doesn’t erase how Hollywood handled things in the past (Pretty Little Liars and Fifty Shades of Grey come to mind).

Rivals is a bit of an anomaly in how it has tackled Rupert and Taggie’s age-gap relationship so far, and the slower pace seems to have played a very necessary part in that. Rutag’s slow-burn underpins the entire foundation of their connection. What’s working for me personally is that Rupert hasn’t been rushing Taggie emotionally or physically to match where he’s at in his current stage of life. In fact, even though he seems besotted with her—she’s sort of become his moral touchstone—he’s doing his best to stay away from her. He seems to find the idea of letting her down (or fully letting her in) absolutely terrifying, despite how much he clearly cares.

Rivals gains further grace from viewers for its willingness to openly acknowledge Rupert and Taggie’s age gap as being an obstacle to overcome in their relationship, rather than just glossing over it or choosing to pretend it doesn’t exist. Rupert even acknowledges the age gap as being an issue himself by making a promise to Declan in season 1 that he’ll stay away from Taggie. By showing us these hurdles early on, it gives deeper insight into what a relationship would eventually mean for these two and what they’d be up against.

Watching Taggie gain life experience and have her own adventures of self-discovery—not everything has to involve Rupert—has helped them remain exciting to us as a potential couple. Her catering business seems to be thriving, for example. The slow-burn gives Taggie the opportunity to forge her own identity and ambitions, which would be important to have going into a relationship with Rupert, given their differences in life experience. Taggie is slowly blossoming into herself, without falling into that cliché trap of trying to change who she is to impress a man, and that’s empowering and necessary.

As long as Rivals continues to explore the age gap with care and consideration, I think fans will keep rooting for Rutag’s romance, no matter how long the slow-burn takes.
