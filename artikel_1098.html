Man Utd show fight on night of chaos to give Carrick a glimpse of hope

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

So maybe there is some fight left in the old dog yet?

At half-time, Manchester United looked down and out and Michael Carrick’s reign as manager as good as finished.

Roma were 2-0 in front and coasting, gorging on a desperate United’s litany of errors, and there was full-scale mutiny in the stands at Old Trafford. A fifth defeat in six matches and the club’s longest winless run for 48 years beckoned. It was toxic on and off the pitch.

And then something changed. United, from being a dishevelled, disorganised mess, suddenly seemed to remember who they were supposed to be and what they were supposed to represent. Surrender gave way to fight. A team that had been feeling sorry for themselves discovered some pride. A crowd that had been baying for blood turned Old Trafford into a bear pit of a very different kind, and Roma simply did not know what had hit them.

This club have served up some famous comebacks over the decades, but it was hard to think of too many occasions when the mood and momentum had shifted this violently from one half to the next.

United fans were in open revolt in that first half, releasing green and gold balloons in protests before kick-off and then throwing tennis balls on to the pitch amid furious chanting against the Glazers and Sir Jim Ratcliffe. The players looked like they would rather have been anywhere else.

And yet by the 86th minute Matheus Cunha’s wild celebrations were encapsulating a wild night, the Brazilian ripping off his shirt after his dramatic winner before launching himself into a delirious crowd drunk on the euphoria of a victory that had seemed improbable in the extreme at the interval, when boos cascaded from the stands.

Whether this proves little more than a thrilling, heroic interlude in the endgame for Carrick, or the start of a genuine turnaround, is hard to know. And, let’s be clear, there was so much about the first-half performance to concern the hierarchy, who came under persistent, furious attack from fans at war with an ownership it reviles and wants gone.

But the second half stood firmly at odds with the shambolic display that had preceded it and suggested all may not be quite lost just yet.

Aston Villa visit Old Trafford on Sunday in the final game before the international break and, if Carrick can follow this with another win, then it will be intriguing to see where the Old Trafford board goes from there.

Carrick may at least have been encouraged by some of the things Bruno Fernandes said after the game, not least that he warranted being dropped against Bournemouth. And it was the captain who kick-started the fightback against Roma, albeit at the second attempt.

He had won the penalty after being fouled by Gianluca Mancini only to then contrive to miss it. But a retake was ordered for encroachment and, given a second bite at the cherry, Fernandes made no mistake.

When Carlos Baleba pulled up with a hamstring injury within seconds of the restart as he burst into the penalty area, Carrick must have wondered what else could go wrong.

But as United went hell for leather, the home supporters who had spent much of the first half attacking the owners and staging a number of colourful protest acts, suddenly found their voice in a very different way.

From being an atmosphere in which the players clearly felt cowed, Old Trafford was now rallying behind its men in red – and it helped to shake Roma in the process. They had been such good value for their lead, but now they were rocking, and United smelled blood.

There was wave after wave of attack as Roma desperately and often frantically tried to defend their goal, and it took a bad mistake from Leonardo Balerdi, who swung at thin air from Fernandes’s cross and missed the ball, to hand Cunha the chance to equalise.

At that point, no one could have reckoned on the comeback that followed. Where it leads, who knows.

Speaking after the match, Michael Carrick was very positive about his side’s performance.

“It was a good night. I have to a lot of credit to the players for starters, showing that character and that quality when we needed it and to the supporters. This place does incredible, magical things,” said Carrick.

“I thought we grew into it the first half  after giving the goals too easily. We spoke at half time about how this place can change, it only takes one moment and it certainly did that and the boys made the most of every opportunity and were fantastic in the end.”

Asked about how he inspired the team at half-time he kept it simple: “Just not to get too disappointed about things. I thought we were coming into the game, getting around their box and it didn’t feel that way to the players because they were carrying too much frustration and disappointment and I knew we were doing some good things in the game against a really good team.

“Playing against man-to-man as much as they are, you probably get more failure than success and it’s still being positive and the boys responded so well to that.”

As for whether this will change United’s momentum now, he was cautious not to get carried away.

“Listen it’s one game. It depends on what happens next and we’ve got to capitalise on that but tonight the boys showed an awful lot,” he said.

“And I thought we came out the second half, unfortunately with Carlos having to go off and Youri coming on the pitch and I thought he was fantastic, he showed everything that he is as a personality, as a character and with his experience to step in the game like that, and the boys just went with the flow and you could feel how positive it was and it was nice to see this place like that again.”

Captain Bruno Fernandes also spoke after the match, acknowledging the difficulties the team has faced.

“It’s tough to say something at the moment to be honest. It’s been a tough period for us, we have not been able to play in the way we want, get the results we want. We’ve done some good bits, but not had the consistency to do it through a whole game to get results and it’s been tough.

“But today was important to get a win. We knew after that we still had the fans with us, we felt we could change it with a goal and something and fortunately we did it today.”

As for his penalty miss and re-take: “First one didn’t go the way I wanted, I did something wrong but I got a second chance and I cleared my head and my mind was only on to get the ball in back of the net.

“It’s been tough for the fans, we understand that. Having that belief from their side helps the team a lot and today was one of those days that Old Trafford came alive.”

After his first start as he returns from injury, Kobbie Mainoo was full of praise for the fans, saying he cherished the relationship the team had with them.

“It’s unlike anything else. We’d do anything for these fans on the pitch, all the love they give us throughout the season it’s only right we repay them and it’s a bit of fun just jumping into the stands with them.”

Speaking to Amazon after the match, Youri Tielemans said: “There has been a lot of pressure on him [Carrick] and that’s how we repay him, with a comeback like this, big character, big fight.”

“We just kept working, we kept believing. We were on the front foot in the second half, we tried to push them into their goal and we succeeded.”

As for whether or not the performance would be enough to keep Carrick in his role at United: “That’s a different question. The only thing we can do is stick behind him, fight for him and fight for the club.

“At the end of the day we are on the pitch and we need to show what we are capable of and tonight, especially in the second half, we showed what we can do and we turned up the gears.

“Let’s hope this is a good turnaround for us and let’s hope we can build on this.”

It’s matchday four of the Champions League and Michael Carrick’s side host their Serie A opponents having failed to win any of their last nine matches across all competitions. Since their win against Sabah back in September (must feel like years ago to Carrick), they have suffered chastening defeats in Europe to Atlético Madrid and then Cesc Fabregas’s Como. This has been coupled with dismal performances in the Premier League, most recently a 2-0 defeat at the hands of Chelsea, where Lisandro Martínez was sent off for two ridiculous fouls.

All in all, there is the feeling that Carrick is simply a dead man walking, limping towards the international break after the United hierarchy decided on a stay of execution in their crisis talks earlier this month. If the beleaguered manager has any hope at all of hanging on to his job, he needs convincing results tonight and against Aston Villa on the weekend.

Despite the doom-mongering that circles the club, Carrick maintains that he has buried the hatchet with Bruno Fernandes following his captain’s public criticisms of the club, insisting that “the dressing room is a good place” in his pre-match press conference (more on that to come).

The visitors on the other hand, managed by Gian Piero Gasperini – who replaced Claudio Ranieri last summer – are certainly having a better time of it. They got off to a slow start in Europe, drawing with Fernebahce in their opening match but coming agonisingly close to stunning Real Madrid on matchday two at home. They went 3-1 up before three late goals snatched victory for Madrid.

They bounced back with a convincing 4-0 win over Slovan Bratislava and their domestic campaign is off to an incredibly promising start. They are currently unbeaten in Serie A with seven wins and three draws. They sit third and are the highest scoring side, with 28 goals from their 10 games. Free-flowing though their attack is, there a certainly defensive frailties there to exploit as they conceded three in a six-goal thriller against Napoli, as well as two in both matches against Cagliari and Udinese.

It has been 19 years since Carrick was part of a United side that thrashed Roma 7-1 at Old Trafford, with the midfielder scoring two. Tonight he faces that same opponent with his job hanging by a thread and it remains to be seen if his team can produce any semblance of that spark to turn the tide.
