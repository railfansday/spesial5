Ford Motor vs. General Motors: Which Automotive Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Ford's commercial arm is steadying cash flow, but GM's higher profitability tells a different story for 2027.

By Sara Appino – Updated Nov 22, 2026 at 5:15PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Ford Motor Company ›

As the transition to electric vehicles tests the resilience of legacy automakers, Ford Motor (F -0.62%) and General Motors (GM -3.72%) are racing to dominate the driveway. Which stock is the better buy for your portfolio?

Ford is doubling down on its commercial segment through Ford Pro, while General Motors relies on a massive brand portfolio and its OnStar ecosystem to maintain loyalty. Both companies face the capital-intensive challenge of pivoting away from traditional engines. Comparing their financials and risk profiles helps determine which legacy giant is better positioned for the road ahead.

Ford is restructuring its operations into three distinct units to better manage its evolution into a technology-led company. As a major player among consumer discretionary stocks, the company serves retail buyers through its gas-powered Ford Blue and electric Ford Model e segments. However, the Ford Pro division is a central pillar of its strategy, focusing on fleet optimization and telematics for commercial and government clients. This division provides a steady stream of recurring revenue from software services and maintenance for business fleets.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $187.0 billion, which represents a decrease of approximately 1.4% compared to the prior period. During this time, the automaker reported a net loss of close to $7.8 billion. This negative bottom-line result reflects the heavy costs associated with its aggressive capital investment plan and the ongoing expenses of scaling its electric vehicle production. Despite the top-line dip, the company continues to rely on its strong sales of high-margin trucks and commercial vans to support its broader business transformation.

As of Sept. 30, 2026, Ford's debt-to-equity ratio was approximately 4.48x. This metric, which is total debt divided by shareholder equity, shows the company relies heavily on borrowed funds to fuel its operations. The current ratio, which measures the ability to cover short-term liabilities with short-term assets, stands at approximately 1.1x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was roughly $12.5 billion. This cash generation, defined as operating cash minus capital expenditures, helps provide the liquidity necessary to fund new vehicle platforms.

General Motors operates a diverse portfolio of iconic brands, including Chevrolet, Buick, GMC, and Cadillac, reaching customers in over 70 countries. The company focuses on maintaining high market share in the lucrative truck and SUV segments while simultaneously building out its Ultium battery platform. Beyond physical vehicles, the company leverages its OnStar connectivity service to create a digital ecosystem for both retail and fleet customers. This software-driven approach aims to improve customer retention and create new high-margin revenue streams through connected services.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $187.0 billion, a slight decrease of approximately 0.3% from the previous twelve months. Unlike its primary rival, the company remained profitable during this period, reporting net income of close to $4.0 billion. While revenue growth has been essentially flat, the ability to maintain positive net income demonstrates effective cost management across its traditional manufacturing base. This stability is vital as the company navigates the expensive transition toward a fully electric future.

As of Sept. 30, 2026, GM's debt-to-equity ratio was 2.0x. This ratio indicates the level of financial leverage the company uses, showing that its debt levels are lower relative to equity than those of its closest competitor. The current ratio is approximately 1.1x, indicating the firm has enough short-term assets to pay its upcoming bills. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was close to $11.1 billion.

Ford Motor faces significant operational risks, including potential supply chain disruptions for critical battery materials like lithium and cobalt. Quality control issues that lead to expensive recalls and warranty costs also remain a persistent concern for the manufacturer. Furthermore, the company must navigate a rapidly evolving electric vehicle market in China and increasing regulatory pressure regarding fuel economy standards. The capital-intensive nature of its current transformation plan means any significant slowdown in consumer demand could pressure its financial position.

Ford appears slightly more attractive on a sales basis, though both companies trade at remarkably similar earnings multiples relative to their future earnings estimates.

I'd go with General Motors. It has turned its truck and SUV business into a dependable profit engine, and it is steadily bringing down the cost of its electric vehicle push.

Here's what tipped it for me: Its North American business, the heart of the company, is turning more of each sales dollar into operating profit year over year. GM also expects to generate well over $10 billion in cash this year, after covering its investments, and just approved a $20 billion share buyback, which gives each remaining share a bigger slice of the profits.

Ford has a strong hand in commercial trucks and vans, where its Pro business is the company's biggest profit maker. But Ford expects 2027 earnings to merely match this year's, and its electric vehicle unit is still losing billions of dollars. GM's EV losses, meanwhile, are falling, with more improvement expected next year. For long-term investors, the strong truck profits, a lighter EV burden, and a big buyback tip the scales toward GM for 2027.
