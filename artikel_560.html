Box Office: ‘Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping’ Leads With $15 Million on Thanksgiving Eve, ‘Focker In-Law’ Earns $5 Million on Opening Day

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

“Sunrise on the Reaping” stuffed the box office competition ahead of Thanksgiving, generating $15 million on Wednesday. Fueled by positive word-of-mouth, Lionsgate’s “Hunger Games” prequel looks to collect $85 million over the five-day holiday period, which would push its domestic tally to $233 million.

Despite opening last weekend, “Sunrise on the Reaping” towering over two new releases. Disney’s animated “Hexed” earned $7.6 million from 3,725 theaters, and Universal’s comedy “Focker In-Law” grossed $5.3 million from 3,382 venues on their respective opening days. (“Focker In-Law” didn’t have Tuesday previews; showtimes began on Wednesday.) Although “Hexed” has pulled ahead on Wednesday charts, the fantasy adventure is projected to finish the weekend in third place behind “Focker In-Law.” Based on updated projections, “Hexed” is now targeting a five-day debut of $31 million to $35 million, while “Focker In-Law” is aiming for $35 million to $36 million over the same frame. Both films are falling short of pre-release expectations, which forecasted a $40 million to $45 million haul. Elsewhere, Netflix’s Brad Pitt-led “The Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” brought in $910,00 from 252 Imax screens on Wednesday. The sequel to Sony’s “Once Upon a Time in Hollywood,” which isn’t getting a traditional wide release, is expected to earn $4 million to $5 million by Sunday.

In the case of “Hexed,” this opening weekend could spell trouble. That’s because the studio spent more than $200 million to produce the film, and theaters keep about half of ticket sales. So the studio needs the movie to resonate in international markets, and throughout holiday season, to justify the sizable prize tag.

Disney has long dominated the Thanksgiving corridor, and the studio’s animated sequels, namely “Moana 2” and “Zootopia 2,” are some of the holiday’s biggest earners of all time. Post-pandemic, however, Disney has struggled with original offerings, including 2024’s “Wish” ($31 million over its first five days) and 2022’s “Strange World” ($18 million debut over its first five days). “Wish” tapped out with $254 million worldwide on a $200 million budget, while “Strange World” left theaters with $73 million globally on a $180 million budget. What’s promising, though, is that audiences seem to like “Hexed,” which was awarded an “A-” grade on CinemaScore exit polls. The story follows a teen girl who discovers magical powers that take her from suburbia to Hexe, the world of witches.

“Focker In-Law” also carries a sizable budget; Universal and Paramount co-financed the film for $90 million. Although theatrical comedies have become a box office rarity, the three prior “Focker” movies were notable box office winners. The original comedy of errors, 2000’s “Meet the Parents,” starring Ben Stiller and Robert De Niro as mismatched in-laws Greg Focker and Jack Byrnes, generated $330 million worldwide. The 2004 sequel “Meet the Fockers” is the highest-grossing in the franchise with $522 million worldwide, while 2010’s “Little Fockers” amassed $310 million worldwide. In this installment, Ariana Grande joins in the festivities the girlfriend of Greg’s son Henry (Skyler Gisondo) who meets the family as they gather for Jack’s 80th birthday party. Critics panned the film (a 29% on Rotten Tomatoes) but moviegoers have been more receptive; “Focker In-Law” holds a “B+” grade on CinemaScore exit polls.

Despite the range of offerings, this Thanksgiving will fall short of cementing any box office records. Ticket sales are expected to hit $210 million to $215 million across all films in the marketplace. Thanksgiving 2024 was the holiday’s biggest on record with $422 million across all films, led by “Moana 2″ ($225 million debut), “Wicked” ($118 million) and “Gladiator II” ($44 million). Last year was among the top five with $293 million, boosted by “Zootopia 2” and “Wicked: For Good.” Theater owners aren’t discouraged because domestic ticket sales are already 20% above the same point in 2025. Plus, North American revenues are projected to surpass $10 billion for the first time since Covid upended the theatrical business.
