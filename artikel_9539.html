8 Trader Joe's Cheeses To Try And 4 To Skip

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

There's nothing like going to Trader Joe's before hosting a party and stocking up on cool and unusual cheeses. It's the perfect time to try something new or introduce your loved ones to one of your long-standing favorites. Trader Joe's has dozens of cheese at any given time and it can be a little intimidating to narrow down the options. To help out, I sampled some Trader Joe's cheeses to determine which you absolutely need to try, and which you're safe to skip.

None of the cheeses I tried were bad, some just weren't worth it for one reason or another, at a time when we're all watching our budgets extra closely. As touted in Trader Joe's incredibly easy return policy, every single product has been tried (and loved) by a company representative, so the chances of ending up with something that's objectively bad are pretty slim. Still, there were eight truly standout cheeses among my sampling, and four that you can take a pass on. Prices may vary based on your location.

Cranberry Chèvre Goat Cheese is not the only fruit studded fresh goat cheese at Trader Joe's, but it was the first one the store introduced, and it persists for good reason! Despite its simple composition (a log of fresh goat cheese rolled in sweetened dried cranberries), it is surprisingly complex. It's creamy, tart, and sweet all at once, and the chewy cranberry pieces keep it texturally interesting. It adds a freshness that can help cut through the other heavy, rich options on your cheeseboard, and it's also super versatile.

If cranberry sauce isn't your preferred vessel for the fruit at the holidays, use this cheese in place of Trader Joe's equally delicious blueberry vanilla goat cheese to make a two-ingredient appetizer with puff pastry sheets. Or, serve the Cranberry Chèvre Goat Cheese with dinner and let your guests slather it on their rolls, instead. Perhaps the most inspired option is using the seasonally perfect fresh goat cheese as a spread for your Thanksgiving leftovers sandwich to take it to a whole new level.

Trader Joe's Comté Cheese is the kind of cheese you could expect to find at specialty cheese shop, and probably for a lot more money. That is, in part, because all Comté cheese is subject to Appellation d'Origine Protégée (AOP), which are rules that dictate how and where it is made.

The AOP stamp on Trader Joe's Comté is how you know it's the real deal and follows those same regulations. It's made in just one Alpine region of France (in one of about 134 small villages) using raw milk from one of only two specific breeds of cow, and it is aged for at least four months. The result is a Comté that is firm but creamy, with a somewhat intense smell and a delicately funky flavor. It's definitely one to try, but serve it at room temperature as intended to experience the unique aroma and taste of this strictly-regulated cheese.

Whether or not you have specifically heard of Emmentaler (also called Emmental) cheese, chances are pretty good that you have eaten it. It is the quintessential Swiss cheese that most Americans think of when they hear the term, though there are actually several Swiss cheese varieties.

Trader Joe's Emmentaler has a mild, somewhat sweet and nutty flavor that would be delicious on a ham and Swiss sandwich with Dijon mustard. Emmentaler is even the ultimate fondue cheese. So why is it a skip this time? It does not offer much to set it apart from a Swiss cheese you could buy at any other grocery store. Instead, spend your hard earned money on one of the other kinds of cheese that you can only find exclusively at Trader Joe's. Because, trust us, there are a lot of unique kinds to try at TJ's you can't get anywhere else.

Despite the fact that this Trader Joe's cheese is literally called Unexpected Cheddar Cheese, I'd never tried it, thinking it was just like any other aged cheddar I could buy. I was very wrong (and have since learned my lesson on believing TJ's packaging). There's a reason this cheese came in at No. 2 on our Trader Joe's Product Hall of Fame ranking, and if you've never tried it, now's really the time.

This cheddar has a really nice nuttiness along with its sharp flavor. It is harder and a bit crumblier than many cheddar cheeses, and has those distinctive crunchy crystals typically found more often in varieties like aged Parmigiano-Reggiano. Those crystals are formed during the aging of certain kinds of cheeses, especially in Italy, and are part of what makes Trader Joe's Unexpected Cheddar so delectable. The crumbly texture might not make it the best cheese for slicing up for sandwiches, but you'll enjoy it a hundred different other ways before it even occurs to you to care about that.

Trader Joe's Cheddar Cheese with Caramelized Onions has been my go-to cheese purchase for over a decade, and, all these years later, it still stands up. It has a softer texture than the store's Unexpected Cheddar, so it slices and melts it a bit more easily, but the caramelized onion flavor is where this cheese really shines. There's nothing else like it.

It isn't just onions and cheese, either. The flavor is accomplished by blending a caramelized onion marmalade with an English cheddar cheese. The marmalade has a depth of flavor thanks to cider vinegar, red currant juice, lemon juice, olive oil, clove, cinnamon, and ginger. The warm sweetness of the caramelized onion marmalade is expertly balanced with the salty umami flavor of the cheddar itself, and the little bits of onion throughout make it visually and texturally interesting. This is the ideal cheese to bring to a book club meeting (trust me) and goes well with virtually everything.

The double bottles of bubbly being popped open on the front of this cheese's packaging had me hoping for something celebration worthy, but Trader Joe's English Cheddar Cheese with Champagne just isn't it. It's a perfectly fine cheddar cheese, if a bit on the extra salty side, but I had to really concentrate to pick up on any flavor notes that indicated the presence of Champagne.

According to the ingredients, this cheese includes actual Champagne, as well as Marc de Champagne brandy, which is made by distilling all the parts of the grapes that are leftover after being pressed for Champagne. Still, I had to really search for anything I might call Champagne flavor, which was a letdown. At the end of the day, it just wasn't as delicious as the Unexpected Cheddar or the variety with caramelized onions, despite being the most expensive of the bunch at $12.99 per pound.

I don't think I've ever had a bad Brie, but I can confidently say that I've never had a Brie more delicious than Trader Joe's Triple Cream Brie with Wild Mushrooms. Admittedly, I'm a big fan of mushrooms already, but I'm so used to having Brie with jammy fruits that it never occurred to me that the cheese would really shine when paired with the earthy flavors of wild mushrooms.

Served at room temperature with crackers or crusty bread, it spreads like a dream and needs absolutely nothing to improve it. That said, there's nothing like a baked Brie to make a gathering feel like a party and this wild mushroom variety is no exception. If you're short on time, use your microwave to make a "baked" Brie. You'll still get the delicious, gooey texture everyone loves in baked Brie.

Something I didn't learn until I was in my late 20s is that not everyone eats the rind on Brie, despite the fact that it's completely edible (and delicious). If you're not a fan of the earthy rind, you're probably not going to be a fan of the earthy mushrooms in this cheese, either, but I hope you'll give it a chance.

Trader Joe's Huntsman Cheese looks very different from other options in the cheese case, with its defined white and orange striped layers. Those different stripes are actually two iconic British cheeses: double Gloucester and Blue Stilton.

The two cheeses work together beautifully. The double Gloucester has a smooth flavor with a somewhat firm texture, while the Blue Stilton, which has a Protected Designation of Origin, is sharp and a bit more crumbly (though it's also creamy at room temperature). While the streaks in the Blue Stilton are indeed mold, it's totally scientifically safe to eat the mold in blue cheese.

What I love most about this cheese, other than its very unique appearance that makes it perfect for entertaining, is that its a great introduction to blue cheese for people who are leery of its strong flavor. By pairing the Blue Stilton with the double Gloucester, the flavors are well-balanced while still being complex and delicious. Lure your guests in with the striking appearance and amazing flavor of the Huntsman Cheese, then hit them with the Grand Blue (more on that beauty shortly).

I really wanted to love Trader Joe's Pink Peppercorn Fontina Cheese. It's very eye-catching with its vibrant pink coating and I'd incorrectly assumed that pink peppercorns meant it would be a spicy addition to my cheese selection. Avoid making the same mistake I did and take a minute to learn all about the different kinds of peppercorns, including the fact that pink peppercorns aren't true peppercorns and are more closely related to cashews than black peppercorns.

Still, the company's description of the cheese suggests a level of spice that just wasn't there. Instead, the cheese had an almost sweet vegetal flavor. A closer inspection of the ingredients revealed that the peppercorn seasoning blend also includes freeze dried strawberries, strawberry powder, and dehydrated red bell pepper, which made sense in hindsight. The flavors ended up tasting a bit muddled, though the cheese has some promise if it were paired with the right cracker, fruit, or other accompaniment. While it's certainly pretty, it's not worth the effort to make it truly exceptional.

One of the great things about being someone who is unabashedly a fan of blue cheese is that I never feel bad taking a decent sized serving from a cheese platter at an event. Pair it with a crunchy cracker and some red grapes, and I'm in heaven. But even I didn't know that blue cheese could be as good as Trader Joe's Grand Blue Cheese.

This blue cheese is unbelievably creamy. I would have been hard pressed to crumble it unless it was straight from a very cold refrigerator. At room temperature, it sliced almost like soft butter and spread easily on crackers and bread. This is the blue cheese to serve with a black and blue burger, with the cheese melted on top of the patty, or on a pizza with figs, prosciutto, and a balsamic reduction. It's so creamy that I'd love to try it mixed into mac and cheese, if I can manage to make it last long enough next time I buy it (but the need to eat it straight-up might win out).

Havarti is a creamy Danish cheese that holds up super well to both slicing and melting, which makes it a great addition to recipes and on your cheese board. It has a relatively mild, somewhat sweet flavor that lends well to the addition of herbs, peppers, and spices. Perhaps the most iconic variety is Havarti with dill, and Trader Joe's version is worth adding to your shopping list.

Fresh dill is used in a lot of Nordic and Scandinavian cooking, so it makes sense that it's a perfect companion to a Danish cheese like Havarti. Trader Joe's Havarti with dill is absolutely packed with the bright herb. If you're the type of person who loves anything dill pickle flavored, you're going to love this cheese. Add it to anything that could use an herbaceous boost, but I highly recommend pairing it with Trader Joe's Unexpected Cheddar for a grilled cheese sandwich that's out of this world. For a bright crunch that brings out the cheese's sweetness, add some crisp apple slices, too. Thank me later.

Prior to trying Trader Joe's Toscano Cheese Dusted With Cinnamon, I'd never actually heard of Toscano cheese. It turns out that that's because it's based on another cheese entirely, with a few important differences. Interestingly enough, while the retailer carries other cheeses with protected status, its Toscano is an interpretation rather than a faithful rendering of Italian Pecorino Toscano, which has a Protected Designation of Origin (PDO).

Italian Pecorino Toscano is a sheep's milk cheese made primarily in Tuscany, while Trader Joe's Toscano (both this cinnamon dusted version and other varieties it offers) are made from cow's milk in Wisconsin. There's nothing inherently wrong with either of those characteristics, and the cinnamon dusting on the cheese gives it a pleasantly warm sweetness, but it feels a bit like a bait and switch. The retailer describes the cheese as being like cheddar in texture and Parmesan in flavor, but I found that it was kind of bland compared to other options, and not even the dusting of cinnamon made it worth the buy.

To ensure a wide range of Trader Joe's cheese, I gathered varieties from all around the world and across many cheese-making styles. While I would happily sample every variety Trader Joe's has to offer, I stuck to a budget of $75, which still allowed me to splurge on a few options and sample a total of 12 cheeses.

Each cheese was tried  first on its own to allow its natural flavor profile a chance to shine. Initial impressions were gathered based on how much I enjoyed the cheese, how much of it I wanted to keep eating, and whether it felt worth it for the cost. That last criterion included considerations like whether the cheese was memorable and how excited I would be to introduce it to a friend. The following day, I prepared a cheese board with all of the cheese samples, two types of crackers, and warm toasted baguette, which I enjoyed with my husband while watching television to solidify my recommendations.
