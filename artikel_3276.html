Chris Christie to Republicans: ‘I Don’t Want to Hear Your Whining’

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

T he first time I wrote about Chris Christie, he was running for governor in 2009, and I mocked him acidly for running a specious campaign during a time of fiscal crisis. (I noted, cruelly, that he was apparently too heavy to march in a Labor Day parade and had campaigned from the back of a truck, “like the Pope.”) Most politicians I know would never have spoken to me again. Christie, on the other hand, invited me to coffee shortly after he won, and he pressed me to come to New Jersey so he could show me his plan to reverse the state’s rapid decline — a painful plan he sold through dozens of town hall meetings with voters who walked in wanting to throttle him.

That was Christie, as I would come to know him. A prosecutor both by profession and temperament, he sees politics as a giant courtroom and has supreme confidence in his ability to convince you he’s right — undimmed, somehow, by brutal experience.

I’ve sat down with Christie at some very low points (immediately after the existential scandal known as “Bridgegate”) and some better ones (the eve of his first presidential campaign). His odyssey has taken him from the very center of Republican politics to its outer fringe, from nearly running Donald Trump’s White House to emerging as one of its bluntest critics. There have been humiliating moments, some of his own making. But Christie remains, even now, one of his party’s most interesting figures and its most sophisticated communicator. If the post-Trump party is going to have a real debate about whether to return to its intellectual roots or resign itself to the ugly, nativist margins of American life, then it’s hard to imagine Christie won’t have a voice.

I caught up with Christie this time about a week after the midterm elections, at the offices of his small consulting firm in northern New Jersey, where the walls of the conference room are adorned with bipartisan mementos. Christie seemed buoyed by the moment. He arrived, cheerful and unhurried, wearing a Knicks sweatshirt from the NBA Finals, as if to signal that things suddenly seemed to be going his way. We talked about the party’s uncertain future, Trump’s weird psychology, Christie’s own worst decisions, and why he can’t stand his former Republican rivals (and especially Jared Kushner). We also talked about why he’s considering a third presidential campaign now, even after the last two cratered. You’ve been talking for years about how Trump was going to lead your party off a cliff. You feeling vindicated?Sure. Yeah. I mean, absolutely. I don’t think there’s anybody on the political scene of the country who knows him better than I do. There may be some that know him as well, but I’ve known him for 24 years, and what happened on Tuesday was inevitable. And the reason that I think it didn’t happen earlier was because the Democrats just couldn’t get out of their own way. I mean, this really should’ve been avoided if they had just not acted the way they did between 2021 and 2023. Nobody wanted to be with him during those times. But what that did, the way he was resurrected, set him up for the biggest fall since Watergate, because he can’t control his anger. He can’t control his vindictiveness. He can’t control how he really has no moral core of any kind, which of course prevents you from being able to have forgiveness in your heart.

“I don’t think there’s anybody on the political scene of the country who knows [Trump] better than I do.”

And so when he got power back, I just knew he was going to use that power for two things: to seek vengeance against his enemies and to enrich himself beyond belief. Because it’s all about him. And if you don’t have a moral core, you don’t have a calling to help anyone else. And in fact, when other people benefit from their association with you, you’re angry about it. People have made money off of presidents for centuries, through their association with a president. And most presidents understand that as long as they’re doing it legally, that is part of what happens. This is a guy who, if he’s not getting his cut, it’s personally offensive to him, and he speaks out and tries to hurt those people.

It does feel a little mobby, doesn’t it?Very mobby. “You only have made this because of me, therefore you owe me a piece of it.” I think often back to the first debate in 2024, when Brett Baier’s question was, “Raise your hand if you would vote for a convicted felon for president.” And everybody on the stage raised their hands except for me and Asa Hutchinson. And then Baier came to me, and when I started giving my answer, I got booed brutally in that auditorium. But Ron DeSantis raised his hand, Nikki Haley raised her hand, Tim Scott raised his hand, Mike Pence raised his hand, and of course, the soulless Vivek Ramaswamy raised his hand. Because he’d already been given a pass.Yeah. People who supposedly were running against him were kissing his rear end at the same time. And that’s why none of them were ever serious candidates. All of them were sitting around hoping that something really bad would happen to him and that they would be the happy beneficiary of it. None of them were serious about trying to beat him.

What do you think the voters were saying here?I think it’s two things. The first and most important one is, it is an absolute personal rejection of him. You don’t see the combination of really high turnout among Democrats and among independents in a midterm and low turnout of Republicans, relatively speaking, without it being a personal rejection of the head of the party. And I think the second thing is, it is another expression of disgust and anger with the way the government system is being operated. And in this case for the first time, you’ve seen corruption rise as a major issue in exit polling. It hasn’t been there for a long, long time. I think it’s because people see a split screen, so to speak, in their mind. They’re paying $100 to fill their tank with gas. They’re paying a huge amount for ground beef and trying to get their family going. And then they see Trump, Kushner, and all these other characters, Steve Witkoff and his family, [Howard] Lutnick and his family, just raking money in without any shame of any kind. And they say, “OK, no.”

People are willing to sacrifice when they feel like everyone’s sacrificing. I was going through the airport in D.C. yesterday and I went through the metal detector, and I got randomly pulled out for special screening. There are all these people in line who recognize me and they start to laugh, but it’s not like I can go around it. I go into the thing, I put my arms up here, they do that, they pat me down, and off I go to the gate, and it costs me another five minutes or whatever. But in that instance, everybody feels like they’re being treated the same. The inconvenience is for everyone, and it is for the greater good. And I think the American people complain about some stuff like that, but they don’t act out on it, and they don’t vote on it. The difference here is they’re seeing two different standards of treatment, and that offends them.

JD Vance says it’s all about the war.Well, he’s just wrong. And the reason he says that is self-serving because then he’s going to leak how he was against the war. Well, first of all, who cares? It happened on your watch. And if you were so morally offended, resign. If you think it was that big a mistake by the president, then resign. It’s not like you’re doing anything every day —  you’re the vice president. It’s not like you’re going to be missed. They’ll name another person to go in there and sit there and do what he does, whatever that is. I find these folks who are now analyzing this in the aftermath to be ridiculous. Ron DeSantis running around, and now all of a sudden he’s criticizing the Trump administration. Where the hell has he been? Where the hell’s Nikki Haley?

That’s a good question. I feel like she’s in witness protection.Yeah. All of a sudden now she comes out on election night, and she’s got so much to say. I look at some of these folks and I say, “These are the people with no principle because it’s easy to criticize someone when you lose the Senate, lose the House, get to 30 Democratic governorships and lose 11 state houses across the country.” I mean, an idiot could say that’s not good. Where were you when all of this stuff was leading to this? When it was inevitable, you could see it. And these people are experienced enough and smart enough to have seen it, but what they cared more about was currying favor with the king.

You’re saying that all the leaders of your party, your party that you spent a lifetime in, are effectively lacking character.The best way to put it is that their title’s more important to them than what’s right versus wrong. You have people like Thom Tillis, who I campaigned for when he ran for the Senate the first time as governor and who I think is a really good, smart, effective guy, who went in the tank. Now all of a sudden when he decides not to run for reelection, now all of a sudden he’s got religion back.

A bunch of liberated senators who couldn’t keep their seats, right?Right. So what does that tell you? It isn’t that they just discovered it. It’s that once they made the decision that either they would not or could not keep their title, then it’s safe to tell the truth. But before that, I can’t tell the truth.

I have to say, this is the most disappointing thing to me of the last bunch of years. Having been around politicians for so long, to watch an entire Republican Senate bow down and not just support him, but yield their own prerogative, their own power in the process, just to keep the job.Yield their own prerogative and not only support him, but empower him in a way that’s dangerous and against the Constitution. You’re sitting in a ballpark after a game is over and saying, “Well, he shouldn’t have bunted. He should have left the pitcher in, in the sixth inning.” Who cares? The L is on the board. And in this instance, the L is on the board. It’s what do we do now that’s always much more important. Are we going to continue down this path? Are we going to continue to do the same stuff we’ve been doing? Are we going to put somebody like JD Vance or Marco Rubio or Ron DeSantis or Nikki Haley, any of these characters who were complicit and are complicit in the destruction of our party, are we going to put them in charge of fixing it? I don’t think so. I don’t think we should.

It’s interesting that even Trump allowed that his immigration policy might be extreme, but he’s just elevated Stephen Miller to the chief of staff’s job.I think people are misreading that decision.

OK.This decision by Trump shows his level of fear and insecurity right now. He picked the only person around him who’s been there from the beginning, a person who he knows is undyingly loyal, won’t leak on him, won’t countermand any of his instructions. Stephen Miller is comfort food for Donald Trump in a moment of crisis. He’s his teddy bear to bring to bed because he’s scared.

That’s a very disturbing image.And meant to be so.

So as you point out, you know Trump. What do you think the next two years looks like?It’s going to take a few months for him to get his feet back underneath him. He’s defeated. He’s lost his compass a little bit. He’ll get it back. And I think that we’ll see a really angry last couple of years, for two reasons. One, because the Democrats will do a lot of things up on the Hill, in terms of oversight and other things that will make him really angry. And two, because the attention will start to be shifted to someone else. He needs attention like you and I need oxygen. And when they start focusing on the Republican and Democratic primaries for president, and he’s not the center of attention, he’s not out of the picture, but he is now sharing the stage with others. That will be very difficult for him. And what he’ll do is to try to act even more outrageously, so that you have to turn the camera back to him exclusively.

“I think that we’ll see a really angry last couple of years.”

Were you the Democrats at this moment, you would not be considering a third impeachment, I take it?Oh, I think it would be the worst idea in the world. You’re not going to remove him. All you’re going to do is shift attention away from the things that have really contributed to his downfall and to my party’s downfall. So that’s stupid, and there’s no end to it. They’ll impeach him because they have the majority in the House. They won’t get two thirds of the Senate. We’ve been through this dance twice before.

Do you think he’s right in the head now?If you’re asking do I think he is mentally capable of doing the job, the answer I think is yes. He’s 80, but we knew that when we elected him, that that’s what was going to happen. The same way we watched Biden, when we elected him at 78 go down the chute and not be capable of going forward for another four years, once he’d gotten to 82. I think Donald Trump we’ll see at the end of this term will be diminished.

You and the Never-Trumpers in the party, you’ve been sounding this alarm for years. It hasn’t achieved very much to this point. Now you have an opening. What’s the plan? What do you do to get your party back?Talk about yesterday, you’re a loser. You’re not going to change anything. Look, there’s going to be this couple weeks right after the election, where everybody just wants to talk about what happened and that’s fine. Happy to engage in that. But to me, this is about, what’s this party look like going forward. And we better be the party that’s going to take on the big problems. And there are a lot of big problems. We have $40 trillion in debt. We have a Social Security system that’s going to be cut by 22 percent in the next presidential term if we don’t fix Social Security. We have a healthcare system that’s completely unaffordable, and no one’s willing to do the hard things that need to be done to be able to make it more affordable. We have an educational system that is still at the college level excellent, but practically unaffordable to most young people. And we have a K-12 system that is deteriorating right before our eyes, making us less competitive. Let’s just take those things.

If you had a presidency that focused on those things, you would have a breathtaking four years of trying to deal with that stuff. And I think you have to talk about what makes accomplishing any of those hard things possible. And to me, it’s character, morality, a sense of right and wrong being executed by the person in charge, and a willingness to listen to and compromise with other people who have different ideas. And my party has not shown either of those things. Usually in history, the reaction to a loss like the one we just had is to go in a new direction. So I think that’s where the party has to go, and that’s what I’m going to be talking about.

OK, but doesn’t there need to be a battle plan? There’s going to be a fight inside your party over these principles and these policies, and it takes money and organization. Is there some kind of movement you need to be building?No more committees, thank you. No. No. Money, organization, and movement come from ideas and having a messenger who can express those ideas in a way that both interest and excite people.

But you’ve had these ideas and advanced them for years, and it hasn’t made a dent in the direction of your party. So maybe the ideas by themselves aren’t enough.Well, no. Maybe it’s that it was Trump’s time and not mine. I mean, maybe the people weren’t ready for it yet. Maybe they thought that sending this person in here that was going to blow up and disrespect every institution in the country and break all the China and throw over the tables was going to fix the problem. Well, now they know it won’t. So my point to you is like, “I don’t know. My ideas and my approach were selling pretty good before he came down the escalator.” I got 60 percent of the vote in a blue state. I ran all the Republican governors, and we got a record number of Republican governors. And then he showed up and he was and is a phenomenon. So these ideas on these issues and how we approach them and articulate them, I was probably too early. I don’t think those ideas are too early now given everything that’s happened.

So you’re considering a third run inside the Republican Party?Yeah.

It didn’t go so well the first two times. It surprised me probably as much as it surprised you.Well, ’16 surprised me; ’24 didn’t surprise me because I knew when I got into that race that there would have to be a lightning strike for it to happen. But my motivation at ’24 was, there has to be someone who’s going to stand up there and say, “This is wrong. This is not who we are. And if we decide to compromise ourselves in this way, we are going to pay a big price as a party in a country.” And guess what? The bill came due last Tuesday. And everything that the Democrats try to do now that philosophically a number of Republicans will be opposed to? I don’t want to hear your whining about it because you’re making it possible. Your conduct, you’re standing on the sidelines, your capitulating in 2024 made it possible.

The older I get, the more I understand that life is about timing. And so what really happened was that the timing was wrong. And could I have been better? I’m sure I could’ve been in a number of ways. I don’t know that it would’ve made any difference.

You don’t feel Vance or Rubio, the people that are most often talked about now, could take the party in a better direction or would?I probably would ask you a question back. What in their experience tells you that they would? Because there’s nothing there that tells me that.

No, I wouldn’t argue with you.That’s it. I think you can only judge people on what they’ve said and how they’ve conducted themselves. JD Vance wrote Hillbilly Elegy and became vice president. And I don’t know that that’s the resumé I want for a president of the United States. And I don’t know that I want it to be someone who literally called Trump Hitler and now says he was the best president of my lifetime. I don’t want somebody who stood up next to Vivek Ramaswamy and said that dragging an obviously disturbed elderly woman out of the car that she lives in and throwing her in front of a camera to talk about her daughter who she hadn’t seen for 50 years, that it disgusts him when people criticize that. Well, that tells me that he has no morality. That’s elder abuse. We put people in jail when I was the U.S. attorney for stuff like that.

And for Marco, I don’t know how he gets away from the foreign policy failures of this administration. He is both the secretary of state and the national security adviser, and just because he hides from time to time doesn’t mean that he’s not in the middle of it. He’s in that building every day.

Is there anyone out there who would make you say, “Yeah, I’d step aside for that person, or if it’s not me, I hope it’s that candidate.”I mean, I do have some friends out there. I don’t know whether they’re willing to run or not. I mean, I think a guy like Brian Kemp is a really competent, qualified, successful governor and a guy who’s willing to fight Trump and say no to him on a number of things and put his political career at risk for it, and he beat him. And you see what Georgia looks like now without Brian Kemp.

Democratic is how it looks.That’s exactly right. Blue from top to bottom. So Brian’s a guy who I’ve always admired and respected, and I don’t know whether he wants to run or not. We haven’t talked about it. I won’t ever say I’ll step aside for someone because that’s kind of like putting your future in someone else’s hands. As you probably know, from the years we’ve known each other, that’s not my style.

OK. Let’s talk about the history here a little bit because you know I’m not here just to give you softballs. We’ve got to have a conversation.Sure.

You’ve talked about all the people who capitulated, were complicit, but there was that long moment, four years long, where knowing Donald Trump and knowing who he was, you abetted him. And frankly, really knowing you, it disappointed me,  and it disappointed a lot of the people who knew you and had worked for you.Yep.

“I know why I got fired. Because Jared Kushner had been beating the shit out of me for months.”

It felt self-interested. So how do you explain it now?Well, I’ll explain it to you really simply. Let’s start at the beginning. When I lost in 2016, I did what has been done throughout the history of modern politics, which is, “OK, I lost. I don’t think I deserve to lose, but I did. He won. Let me go try to make him better.” It was really that simple of a decision. I was like, “He trusts me. We have a relationship that goes back 14 years at that point. Let me go in there and try to make him better.” And that’s what I did. And in the beginning, I think I did have some impact on him. And then he put me in charge of the transition and I thought, “Well, this is a really positive way.” So I got out of the campaign completely by May of 2016. I endorsed it in February. I was out by May, and in May, I became chairman of the transition. And he’s like, “You run that.”

And I was really given pretty wide berth to do what I wanted to do with the transition, always checking back in with him and with the campaign to make sure that nothing we were doing was contrary to what messaging they wanted to have out there. I put together, pretty much by myself, a group of over 125 people who were in offices in Washington, D.C., who were working every day to put together a whole set of policy initiatives.

I brought in 10 former U.S. attorneys to do the vetting for potential Cabinet and senior staff people. We put together a 12-volume transition. And after he won, I ran the first two transition meetings and then got fired. And I know why I got fired. Because Jared Kushner had been beating the shit out of me for months, from the first time when he came into the office, which was the first time I ever met him.

You knew his father well enough.Yeah, never had met his father either, but I had prosecuted him and knew the family.

Trump later offered you chief of staff.He offered me chief of staff. He offered me secretary of Homeland Security. He offered me secretary of labor. He offered me ambassador to Italy. He offered me ambassador to the Vatican.

Oh, that’s a good one.I didn’t have the money to do it. So he offered me those five jobs, which are all pretty good jobs. Especially when he offered me chief of staff, he couldn’t understand why I wouldn’t take it. And basically the way I explained it to him was, “You and I are going to disagree. I’m going to tell you what I think you need to do and what the truth is. You’re going to get angry with me and fire me. Why don’t we just get to that point now and not go through all the public drama that will have to ensue?” And when I actually said that to him, he laughed and he said, “Yeah, you might be right about that.” He said, “Well, this isn’t the answer I was expecting, but OK.” And really, after I said no to the chief staff thing, we weren’t in touch at all until the spring, summer of 2020, when Covid hit. And he called me to ask me advice on dealing with a crisis like this. And I drove down to the White House to meet with him because the president calls and asks you to help during a national crisis — we had our disagreements, but not about that, at least not about me giving him advice.

And I sat down there one day for five hours, just me and him in the Oval Office. People would wander in from time to time, but we sat there and talked about all this stuff. And it felt to me like early 2016 again. And so then he asked me to prepare him for the debates in 2020, which I did for the first debate. And then he gave me Covid.

And nearly killed you.Yes. I thought, “Well, he didn’t know he had Covid, and so how can I blame him?” Yet he called me when I was in the hospital and asked if I was going to blame him for Covid. I should’ve known then. That was a strange question. I said, “Well, I don’t know who patient zero is.” I got it. Hope Hicks got it. Bill Stepien got it. Kellyanne Conway got it. I mean, everybody who’s in the room got it. So I’m like, “I don’t know who patient zero was, so why would I blame you?” He says, “I know, but you’re not going to say it, are you?” I’m like, “Well, no, because I don’t know it. And thank you for being concerned about my health.” It was only a year later, Matt, when Mark Meadows wrote his book that we all found out that he knew he had it when he came into debate prep. And not only did he know he had it, but he had us take our masks off. We were in the Map Room.

See, if he’d already torn down the East Wing by then, you would’ve had nice open air and no one would have gotten sick.We would’ve had it outside in the paved-over Rose Garden. I give you all this background to say: It’s not accurate to say that it was like all sunshine and roses and then all of a sudden it broke. It was deteriorating over time. I was distancing myself over time by not going to work for him. But the break point for me was election night 2020 when I just said, “I cannot be associated with him. I can’t speak to him. It’s over. I cannot be associated with this in any way.”

Have you talked to him much since then?We have not spoken since the Friday after the election, 2020. He has sent emissaries to me, which I’ve rejected.

I’m guessing there are two things, just knowing you for a long period of time now, that really piss you off. One is I know you care about the Justice Department. That’s the world you come out of. So I’m betting that the use of the Justice Department as a personal retribution arm and the use of the courts for frivolous criminal prosecutions really gets under your skin.I think it is the single worst thing he’s done in his attack on our government. Yeah. No doubt.

The second thing is Jared Kushner’s out there getting rich off foreign governments while presumably serving the country.Oh, I think it’s wrong, but I don’t really care. The interesting thing about the Kushners is they can put all the lipstick on the pig they want, but everybody knows who they are. In the end, you can call yourself ambassador, you can style yourself a modern day Henry Kissinger or J.P. Morgan or whatever the hell Jared styles himself to be, but everybody knows who you are and what your family is.

Which is what?Which is some of the most reprehensible, dishonest people I’ve ever met in my life. And by the way, it’s not my opinion, as they say. Read the facts.

Do you ever wonder how your trajectory would’ve been different absent the Bridgegate thing? It still comes up all the time when your name is mentioned.This is the way I put it, Matt. I’m not responsible for Bridgegate, but I’m accountable for it because it happened on my watch, and I paid as big a price as anybody could ever pay for something they didn’t do, but that’s life. And I also got credit for some things that I had nothing to do with either. So I’m not going to say it balances out, but there are examples of both. It’ll be 13 years ago that it broke this January. You never get over it, but I acknowledge what happened in terms of the impact on my career. And yeah, I think it changed the course of history potentially. Yeah.

I don’t spend any time anymore thinking about it. But like you said, people bring it up. So when they bring it up, you’ve got to be prepared to answer about it, because it’s part of your record. It’s part of who you are. It’s part of your public life. It’s an unpleasant part. Look, I dealt for years and will deal, if I do this again going forward, with people obsessing on my weight. Another unpleasant topic. But it’s part of the deal. This is the way life works.

Maybe you should have run in 2012. There is a school of thought that the blue sky only opens once.It could wind up being true. I don’t know, but I don’t have any second thoughts about that either because I wasn’t ready.

Because this is Rolling Stone, I have to ask you this. You’re a big fan of Bruce Springsteen, and he has not always been a big fan of yours.Correct.

Have you been able to hug it out?Well, here’s what happened. I guess it was 2022 or 2023, it was right after Covid. Bruce did a show at Barclays, and my older son is a big fan as well. So he said, “Dad, can we just buy tickets in the pit and go to the show?” And I was like, “Sure.” So me, [my wife] Mary Pat, and Andrew went to the show. And about halfway through the show, I said to them, “Hey, Bruce just pointed at me and smiled.” Well, now you only imagine my family. They mocked me for the rest of the show. They’re like, “No, he didn’t.” I go, “He did.” And then the rest of the show my son goes, “Oh, he just smiled at me! Oh, he just pointed at me!” I mean, they broke my chops for the rest of the show. And I’m like, “Fine. I know what happened.”

So we leave, get in the car to drive back to Jersey. And it was like, I don’t know, 11:30. My phone rings with a number that I didn’t recognize. So I answered the phone, and it’s Bruce.

Wow.And he’s like, “Hey, man. Gov, I saw you in the audience tonight with Mary Pat and your boy. You guys were having such a great time. You know what I though to myself? I thought to myself, there’s no reason you and I shouldn’t be friends. And you know what? I also know that the biggest reason we’re not is me, not you.” And he said, “So how about this? This is my cellphone number. You use it and call me anytime. There’s any show you ever want to go to anywhere in the world, you just text me and it’s done.” And he said, “Are you going to any of the shows?” I said, “Yeah, I’ll be at the one at the Prudential Center.” He goes, “Good. Let’s see each other before the show. Let’s take a picture, and I’m going to put it out on social media to let everybody know you and I are cool and I want us to be friends if you’re willing to. And I’m sorry that I contributed to us not being friends.” And I said, “Of course, I’d be happy to be your friend.” And since then, Mary Pat and I have — well, I don’t know how many shows, we’ve been to, but a lot, as his guest all the time. We text back and forth on a regular basis. Talk about things both political and musical and family. It’s now a very good personal relationship.

So which Cabinet department does he get?Yeah, no. I don’t think so.
