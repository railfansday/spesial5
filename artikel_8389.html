Mother and Daughter Killed in Suspected Grizzly Bear Attack in British Columbia

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Authorities in British Columbia are investigating a suspected fatal grizzly bear attack after a mother, 48, and her 20-year-old daughter were found dead near Bella Coola this week.

Jennifer Balcarras and her daughter, Selah Reed, were initially reported missing on November 1 after failing to return from what was supposed to be a brief afternoon walk. A joint search effort involving the Royal Canadian Mounted Police, local search and rescue teams, and the British Columbia Conservation Officer Service led to the discovery of Balcarras’s body on Monday afternoon in the Snooka West Trail area. Reed’s remains were located the following day.

B.C. Conservation Officer Service Inspector Jeff Tyre stated that wildlife officials are actively working to locate and capture any animals connected to the incident, adding that it is unclear whether more than one bear was involved. If a captured bear is linked to the fatal encounter, it will be euthanized in accordance with the province’s public safety protocols for human-wildlife conflicts.

Officials have urged the public to avoid the Snooka West Trail area during the ongoing investigation.

Relatives described the mother and daughter as exceptionally close. Josh Balcarras, Balcarras’s brother, has said that the two shared many interests, telling reporters that Selah frequently enjoyed hiking with her mother. Jennifer Balcarras had recently relocated to Bella Coola for a career change and to start a new chapter in her life. She is survived by three young adult children and a grandchild.

Family members described Reed as just beginning her journey into adulthood, “with her whole life ahead of her.”

Because Balcarras’s recent move had drained her savings, her family has launched a GoFundMe campaign to assist with funeral expenses.

The B.C. Ministry of Environment, Lands and Parks estimates the grizzly bear population in the province to be between 10,000 and 13,000. While the ministry notes that human-grizzly conflicts are uncommon and typically occur when a bear is startled or is defending food or cubs, the region has seen a troubling string of violent encounters over the last few years.

In November 2025, an aggressive grizzly attacked a group of 20 elementary school students and teachers in the Bella Coola Valley, leaving four people with serious or critical injuries. The bear in that incident was never captured.

A month before that, a bear injured two hikers near McGregor Mountain; one victim died weeks later from complications related to their injuries. Other recent incidents include a hunter who was severely injured before fatally shooting a bear in the Elkford region in May 2024, and a 21-year-old woman who survived a defensive attack near Tumbler Ridge in July 2023.

If you encounter a grizzly bear on a trail, remain calm, avoid direct eye contact, speak in a low and steady voice, and slowly back away without turning your back or running. Carry bear spray in an accessible location at all times when hiking in known grizzly territory.
