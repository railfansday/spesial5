Shure’s New MV360 Mics Promise to Make Spatial Audio Capture Easier

Shure is entering the increasingly crowded spatial audio market with its new MV360 microphones.

Just last week, Blackmagic Design unveiled the Ambisonic Microphone, a high-end studio solution. Last month, Sony arrived on the scene with its ECM-AX10, a $250 spatial audio mic for content creators. Built with creators in mind, Shure’s new $349 USB-C MV360 is much closer to Sony’s offering in both price and functionality.

Shure says its new MV360 “makes immersive audio more accessible to creators at every level.” To achieve this, the plug-and-play mic features ready-to-use Capture Modes, including immersive binaural, stereo, mono, and auto-tracking.

The mono and stereo modes are self-explanatory, working just as they would on a traditional mic. Immersive binaural promises a broader, more lifelike soundstage, which Shure says is ideal for headphone playback. Meanwhile, auto-tracking mode focuses the virtual microphone toward the loudest sound source, such as a speaker in an interview or podcast.

Each Capture Mode records a ready-to-use two-channel file that requires no post-production. However, creators who opt for full 360-degree recording gain significantly more flexibility, as they can edit the Ambisonic B-format (Ambix) file in Shure’s Motiv software or compatible third-party apps.

As Shure explains, the MV360 captures a stronger sense of place than a traditional microphone.

“Listeners can feel closer to a podcast conversation, more immersed in an ASMR scene, or transported into the middle of a bustling market or quiet forest,” Shure says.

The MV360 is a relatively compact spatial microphone housed in an all-metal body. It records and draws power over a single USB-C cable, ensuring compatibility with a wide range of devices, including smartphones and many cameras. Alongside Shure’s Motiv apps, the MV360 supports various third-party applications, allowing users to record 360-degree audio directly to Motiv or a DAW of their choice.

“Sound can make the difference between watching a moment and feeling part of it,” says Eduardo Valdes, Associate Vice President of Global Marketing and Product Management at Shure.

“Until now, capturing that sense of presence has required expensive equipment, technical expertise and time that many creators simply don’t have. The MV360 changes that. It gives creators an easy way to bring listeners closer to the conversation, the performance or the world around them, while giving experienced professionals the control they need to shape every detail,” Valdes continues.

The Shure MV360 is available now for $349 and includes the USB-C mic, a small tripod, cold shoe mic clip, phone clamp, extra cables, carrying case, and windscreen.

Alongside the standard model, Shure also unveiled the MV360+ today, slated to arrive in early 2027. Designed for field recordists, videographers, and VR storytellers, the MV360+ works with mirrorless cameras, DSLRs, action cameras, 360-degree cameras, and more. It also adds onboard 32-bit float and internal recording capabilities, a rechargeable battery, and wireless control functionality.

The Shure MV360+ will retail for $449 when it arrives early next year.
