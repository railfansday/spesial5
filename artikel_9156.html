SLEEPY SALE. Christie’s topped the tail end of a week of auctions on Thursday evening in New York, bringing in $145.9 million worth of 21st-century art, reports ARTnews. But with the hammer total of $117.7 million, the two-hour sale fell considerably short of its estimate (including withdrawals), which had been set for between $126 million and $192 million. SOFT LANDING. The Landing, a beloved Los Angeles gallery whose program was built around imaginative pairings of historical and contemporary artists, announced it had closed, reports ARTnews. “It has been a privilege to champion extraordinary artists, present ambitious exhibitions, and create a space for meaningful exchange and discovery,” wrote gallery founder and owner Gerard O’Brien on the gallery website. “End of an era,” he added in an Instagram post. O’Brien founded the Landing in 2012 as an offshoot of his design-focused Reform Gallery. “I never really thought there was a big difference,” he said of design and art. The Landing’s roster included four deceased artists (Jeremy Anderson, Richard Bowman, Leslie Kerr, and Fred Reichman) and five living artists (Yevgeniya Baras, Ryan Fenchel, Matt Phillips, Jonathan Ryan, and Amanda Valdez). Several LA galleries have shuttered of late, including Murmurs, the Box, LA Louver, Blum, plus the outposts of Marian Goodman Gallery and Tanya Bonakdar. “While our gallery doors have closed, our commitment to art and artists continues. Watch this space for news of future off-site exhibitions, activations, and special projects,” wrote O’Brien on the gallery website.

An extremely rare abstract, geometric painting by Hilma af Klint from the 1920s sold for $12.7 million at Bukowskis auction house in Stockholm, nearly 16 times its $800,000 high estimate, and a new auction record for the artist. [The Wall Street Journal]

French student Arnaud Hercules Harixcalde Logan pleaded guilty to smashing the glass display case around Scotland’s monarch-crowning Stone of Destiny in the Perth Museum, because he claimed it was his “birthright” to sit upon it. [BBC]

A new report by think tank The Cultural Policy Unit argues that artists should receive tax breaks for donating their own work to museums in the UK, among other proposals. [The Art Newspaper]

IMMERSED IN WHAT? Is immersive digital art a new phenomenon, or the latest evolution of what artists have been trying to do for centuries? Ocula’s Nathalie Olah explores that question with examples from throughout art history. She goes from the “panorama,” an 18th- and 19th-century leisure activity that included admiring mural-length paintings of various exotic landscapes, to Louis Daguerre’s moving, continuous paintings on translucent fabrics, and his early forms of photography. Read on to decide where the similarities and the stark differences lie between these past and present works, and whether we ultimately truly feel “immersed,” or anything at all.
