Special Edition: Jesus

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Mona Chalabi, Anne Anlin Cheng and Patty Chang, Miles Greenberg, Betsy James Wyeth, and the hottest memes about the Nvidia honeypot.

Framed by their car’s windshield, and then again by my rearview mirror, a man and a woman who seem to be a couple look like they'd rather be anywhere but together. He’s behind the wheel, doing all the talking; she’s looking straight ahead, motionless like a marble statue. Gradually, her face wears a desperate expression that only grows more hopeless as he continues talking and gesticulating.

In the supermarket checkout line, I pick up a copy of TIME magazine for the first time in God knows how long, drawn to a painting of Jesus Christ on the cover. It’s a special edition promising to tell us “Who he was. Who he is. His miracles, his mission, his love.” I expect to see a lot of biblical sheep inside, but I don’t find any. I buy the issue as an artifact. I stopped believing in TIME magazine a long time ago.

Down the street, a place that pretended to be a legitimate art gallery for a couple of months, showing the worst possible commercial art, has finally given up, and now it’s selling fur coats. A man in a cowboy hat walks past two girls dancing for a social media video. The air is chilly, and I think of buying myself a leather jacket.

She looks at me with the most loving eyes as she holds a stack of perfectly folded laundry. She comforts me with her wide, auspicious smile, her jet-black hair, and her wrinkle-free, porcelain skin. I feel safe, accepted, and not judged. “Fast service, same day,” she promises because she’s just a model in an ad for a laundromat.

“As an adult and as a journalist, I have stared shamelessly.” That refusal to look away is precisely what data journalist, writer, and artist Mona Chalabi has built her deeply principled career on. Known best for her indelible data visualizations, from the obscenity of Jeff Bezos’s wealth to the Israeli government’s starvation of Palestinians in Gaza and the racial and gender biases in museum collections, the Pulitzer Prize-winning Iraqi-British journalist tackles the subject of wealth and economic inequality in America in her first book, Ten Lives: How What We Have Shapes Who We Are (2026). | Lakshmi Rivera Amin
