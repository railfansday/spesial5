Democrats could enter congressional majority in a government shutdown

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Democratic Sen. Chris Van Hollen floated using a government shutdown threat to end the war in Iran during an interview on Thursday.

There's nonzero chance Democrats could kick off their newfound dominance on Capitol Hill with another government shutdown.

Several House Republicans told Fox News Digital they were worried about Democrats in both chambers making unreasonable and highly partisan demands ahead of the Dec. 11, 2026, federal funding deadline, potentially paralyzing the U.S. government until a deal could be reached.

"I’m concerned," Rep. Ryan Zinke, R-Mont., told Fox News Digital. "I would say it is on my threat button, as a SEAL, it’s a five-meter target."

Rep. Mike Haridopolos, R-Fla., suggested Democrats' recent midterm victories would give them the confidence to allow a shutdown to happen.

"I’m very worried about it. Unfortunately, the Democrat tactics of shutting down the government worked for them politically, so I’m afraid they’re going to go back to their winning playbook," he said.

Congress opted to delay the government funding fight until after the midterm elections, passing a simple extension of fiscal year (FY) 2026 spending levels called a continuing resolution (CR) from the beginning of FY 2027 on Oct. 1 through Dec. 11.

Republicans who spoke with Fox News Digital expressed varying levels of optimism about whether they could hash out a budget for FY 2027 before the end of the year.

"What we should get done is move every appropriation's bill we can," said Rep. Mark Messmer, R-Ind.

Asked about whether he's concerned about a shutdown, he said, "That's up to the Democrats in the Senate."

Most people who spoke with Fox News Digital said they believe another CR extending early into 2028 is the most likely path, which would give Democrats more control over writing it.

But for Democrats, unsurprisingly, Republicans are to blame.

"I wanted to finish the bills by the end of the year. We had the opportunity to do that, and the Republican side of the Senate has held out," Rep. Rosa DeLauro, D-Conn., the top Democrat on the House Appropriations Committee, said.

DeLauro said last week she wanted to see a short-term CR extending only as far as March.

She suggested, however, that the Iran war could be a sticking point for Democrats down the line, telling reporters, "I can just say that we're not going to be doing billions of dollars for a war of choice."

Senate Democrats are pushing for guardrails on funding for the Iran war, and Republicans are already worried that they’ll shutter the government if they don’t get what they want.

"That is playing with fire for sure," Sen. Kevin Cramer, R-N.D., told Fox News Digital. "It's one thing to pound your chest and talk about it. It's another thing to put America's military men and women in jeopardy."

U.S. House Appropriations Committee ranking member Rosa DeLauro talks to reporters following a House Democratic caucus meeting at the Capitol Sept. 29, 2025, in Washington. (Chip Somodevilla/Getty Images)

A trio of Senate Democrats, Sens. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., Tammy Duckworth, D-Ill., and Kirsten Gillibrand, D-N.Y., are pushing an amendment to a forthcoming bill that would stymie funds from helping the war effort, something that Republicans largely won’t support.

Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., who is running to topple Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., as Democratic leader, is supportive of the effort.

"For starters, we need a clear amendment that says we can't spend money on offensive operations because we do need to end the war, but we also need to bring down costs now," Van Hollen said.
