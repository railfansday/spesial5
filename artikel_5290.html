Attackers reportedly compromised three Binance engineers before reaching the internal wallet signing service.

The timeline spans June, September and October, involving tampered software and a malicious code repository.

A claim blaming a Google ad has not been established by the preliminary investigation.

A major Binance hack didn’t begin with some sophisticated attack on the exchange’s front door. According to a preliminary investigation timeline, attackers compromised employees, stole credentials and eventually reached the platform’s internal wallet signing service.

The sequence raises uncomfortable questions about internal security. Three engineers became part of an attack chain that ultimately enabled unauthorized asset transfers.

The trouble reportedly started in June, when Wallet DevOps Engineer A downloaded a tampered Homebrew installer while configuring an AI coding tool. Binance detected the incident, replaced the device and rotated credentials. However, information about the engineer and colleagues had already been exposed.

Then came September. Engineer B downloaded a malicious code repository during what appeared to be a fake job interview, compromising his work computer.

On October 20, attackers reportedly used the compromised device and an internal AI tool to gain control over Engineers A and C. They also discovered a container deployment permission token on Engineer A’s computer.

The operation escalated on October 29. Attackers accessed Engineer C’s device, used credentials stolen from A, deployed a container and executed a malicious script. That sequence ultimately enabled unauthorized transfers through Binance’s internal wallet signing service.

A separate commentator claimed the roughly $6 billion Binance hack resulted from a developer clicking a malicious Google advertisement, and argued Google should be held responsible.

However, the preliminary timeline does not establish that connection. It describes a tampered installer and a malicious repository, not a confirmed Google ad as the entry point.
