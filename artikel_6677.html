The Trendiest Fragrance From the Year You Graduated School

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Every product on this page was chosen by a Harper's BAZAAR editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

From ’80s spicy florals to today’s gourmand favorites, these are the top scents that defined decades

It almost goes without saying that what’s buzzy in beauty often sends ripples through culture, inspiring a flood of viral social media videos and a rise in consumer spending. While there will always be discussion around the most popular skincare treatments, makeup ideas, and hair looks to date, scent's elusiveness makes it hard to pin down—so we’re taking a closer look at some of the trendiest fragrances from the mid-’80s through the present day.

Our curation of celebrated scents, like Calvin Klein’s CK One eau de toilette, Le Labo’s Santal 33 eau de parfum, and more, is not only a walk down memory lane, but also an accounting of the accords that have lived rent-free in our minds for decades. Though technically invisible, these enduring, dynamic fragrances deserve a spot on any beauty holy grail list. Keep reading for the trendiest perfumes, eaux de toilette, and body mists Bazaar editors have loved over the decades.

Dior’s ’80s-born Poison eau de toilette combines honey and musk accords for a sexy, seductive scent. The warm fragrance has long-lasting sillage and a spicy edge, making it perfect for date night.

Key notes: plum, wild berries, coriander, anise, Brazilian rosewood, tuberose, white honey, cinnamon, incense, carnation, jasmine, rose, orange blossom, vanilla, amber, sandalwood, musk, heliotrope, vetiver, cedarwood

This fresh, floral fragrance from Calvin Klein aims to bottle romance, thanks to a bouquet of notes like freesia, white lily, violet, and rose. Eternity’s iconic campaign was fronted by supermodel Christy Turlington, and the fragrance name itself was inspired by a diamond wedding band once owned by the Duke of Windsor, inscribed with the word “Eternity.”

Key notes: freesia, mandarin, green notes, sage, white lily, marigold, muguet, narcissus, carnation, violet, rose, jasmine, sandalwood, patchouli, amber, musk, heliotrope

If you ever visited Elizabeth Arden’s Red Door spa when the brand was in business, you probably recognize the distinctive color and design of this beloved perfume. Inspired by the ethos of beloved locations nationwide, this popular scent captures the destination spa's grandeur and elegance in its heyday through a bold, floral lens.

Key notes: freesia, red roses, orchids, honey, sandalwood

This star-shaped bottle is almost as iconic as the refillable scent inside. An addictive combination of fruit-forward and gourmand notes, Thierry Mugler’s renowned fragrance is warm and spicy with a unique celestial appeal.Key notes: bergamot, red fruits, patchouli

Elizabeth Taylor was a trendsetter in more ways than one, including the art of fragrance-making. White Diamonds is one of the most noteworthy celebrity brands of all time, and was inspired by the actress’s love of—what else—diamonds, along with flowers and lush gardens.Key notes: Amazon lily, neroli, bergamot, orange, Egyptian tuberose, jasmine, ylang-ylang, Turkish rose, carnation, oak moss, sandalwood, amber, musk, patchouli

Firmenich senior perfumer Clement Gavarry smelled CK One as a teenager and instantly fell in love. “This fragrance was revolutionary and the first gender-neutral fragrance with a construction that, for me, has everlasting freshness, a beautiful trail, and is memorable,” he says. “It captures all of the aspects that make a scent a classic—to this day, it is a modern fragrance that stands in its own category. Truly timeless.”

Tommy Hilfiger was one of the most beloved fashion brands of the ’90s, so it’s no surprise its buzzy Tommy Girl scent is considered one of the most popular as well. The clean, fresh fragrance lends itself to spring and summer months, since its combination of citrus, mint, and green-floral notes is like a breath of fresh air.

Key notes: bergamot, spearmint, jasmine, rose, iced tea accord, sandalwood, amber accord, musk

Clinique’s Happy eau de toilette was a certified hit in the ’90s thanks to its fruity-floral blend that evokes positivity and happiness. The brand even commissioned a third-party neurosensory study to measure fragrance wearers’ conscious and subconscious emotional responses, which found that 97 percent of people feel happy when they smell Happy.

Key notes: ruby red grapefruit, mandarin, bergamot, Hawaiian wedding flower, morning dew orchid, mimosa, transparent wood accord

You might be inspired to dream up a fairy-tale date night or give yourself a little extra self-love after spritzing on this themed fragrance from Ralph Lauren. It opens with top notes of pink pepper and mandarin orange before transitioning to a woodier finish, courtesy of musk and patchouli.

Actress Charlize Theron was one of the muses behind this generational Dior scent for 20 years before Rihanna recently took the reins in 2024. But its celebrity appeal is only one of the reasons J’adore deserves a spot on our list. The enduring warm, fruity-floral perfume was a bestseller shortly after its 1999 release and continues to be a must-have for beauty lovers and Bazaar editors alike.

Key notes: ylang-ylang, Damascus rose, jasmine sambac, jasmine grandiflorum, pear, candied plum, violet, lily-of-the-valley

If you’ve ever owned or been a superfan of Victoria’s Secret’s Love Spell, you’re likely a millennial with superior taste in fragrance mists. Originally launched as part of the brand’s Garden collection, its fruity-floral blend is playful and light with plenty of staying power.

Key notes: red apple, cherry blossom, peach, lily-of-the-valley, jasmine, lilac, airy musk, blonde woods, tamarind

Originally launched in 2001 as an eau de toilette, Dolce&Gabbana’s Light Blue makes it easy to mentally transport yourself abroad to the Mediterranean. The fresh, citrusy scent is easy to layer and beautifully bottles the aquatic essence of the Amalfi Coast.

Key notes: Sicilian lemon, apple, cedarwood

Several adored iterations of Chanel’s Chance eau de parfum exist in 2026, but the original 2002 version set the stage for its now-legendary status in the fragrance world. Beyond its singular rounded bottle, which sets it apart from other prestige scents, Chance’s blend of pink pepper, jasmine absolute, and amber patchouli makes it unforgettable.

Key notes: pink pepper, jasmine absolute, amber patchouli, white musk, vanilla

I personally was borderline obsessed with my bottle of Glow by J.Lo eau de toilette when it first launched in 2002. Not only is its Y2K-coded bottle glamorous, with layered nameplate necklaces, but the floral, musky scent strikes the perfect balance between soapy and powdery without being cloying.

Key notes: orange flower, grapefruit, citrus, rose, sandalwood, amber, jasmine, vanilla, musk, orris

This Y2K fragrance truly smells as delicious as its name implies, with an apple top note leading the perfume as a nod to its origins in New York City. The fresh, floral, and fruity scent doesn’t take itself too seriously, which is part of its playful, whimsical appeal.

Viktor&Rolf’s Flowerbomb eau de parfum isn’t just for floral fragrance aficionados. The trendy scent, first launched in 2005, is grounded by woody notes of patchouli, amber, and musk that round out its feminine accords.

Key notes: peony accord, centifolia, rose, freesia accord

Break out your matching Juicy Couture tracksuit! This popular fragrance from Juicy Couture is made to make your mouth water, combining notes of watermelon, passion fruit, and crème brûlée for a fruity-gourmand scent housed in a bottle that doesn’t shy away from early 2000s glam.

Key notes: watermelon, passion fruit, crème brûlée notes

Bright Crystal eau de parfum is one of Versace’s early fragrance gems, with a fresh floral composition that stands the test of time. The perfume is subtle enough for daily wear, but also impactful enough to make a serious scent statement.

Key notes: pomegranate, yuzu iced accord, vegetal amber, acajou, musk

Another buzzy fragrance with a bottle design as iconic as the scent itself, Marc Jacobs’s Daisy is inspired by innocence and purity. Notes of wild strawberry and violet petals, along with white accords of birch and cedarwood, give the fragrance its unique floral-woody musk. Daisy won the Fragrance Foundation’s FiFi Awards for Women’s Luxe Fragrance of the Year and Women’s Prestige Best Packaging the year after its launch, in 2008.

Key notes: wild strawberry, velvety violet petals, gardenia, jasmine, birch cedarwood

All of fashion’s It girls were reaching for Byredo’s Gypsy Water eau de parfum in 2008, and for good reason. The free-spirited scent is as woody as it is adventurous, with a subtle, balanced—and almost skinlike—finish, an ode to the nomadic customs of Romani culture.

Key notes: bergamot, lemon, juniper berries, pepper, incense, soft iris, pine needle, amber, sandalwood, vanilla
