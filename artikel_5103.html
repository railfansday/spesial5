Strategy spent more than twice as much on preferred-share buybacks as Bitcoin. Now comes its MSCI exit

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The November 30 close creates a financing test after MSTR proceeds funded larger preferred repurchases than Bitcoin purchases during November 2–8.

MSCI has scheduled Strategy’s removal from the MSCI USA Index for the November 30 close, putting a date on a financing test for the Bitcoin treasury company. MSTR common-stock sales recently funded both Bitcoin purchases and a larger preferred-share buyback.

During November 2–8, Strategy allocated $188 million of common-stock proceeds to STRC preferred-share repurchases and $82 million to Bitcoin purchases. The competition for that money makes future MSTR fundraising central to how quickly the company can keep adding Bitcoin.

The November 11 review lists STRATEGY A among the MSCI USA deletions. MSCI’s final-impact announcement applies its non-operating-company methodology in the November review. Existing constituents must fail the ineligibility screen in each of their two most recent annual filing periods; MSCI may also consider the latest interim filing.

Strategy’s November 9 filing reports $284 million in net proceeds from selling 2,051,348 MSTR shares during November 2–8. It reported no sales under the at-the-market offering programs for its listed preferred securities during that period.

A further $14 million of the common-stock proceeds went to USD Cash. Strategy bought 1,036 BTC, bringing its reported holdings to 850,364 BTC as of November 8.

That allocation explains why index membership matters beyond a single stock-market rebalance. Newly issued common shares supplied money for two competing priorities: adding Bitcoin and retiring preferred claims. A reduction in the attractiveness of MSTR issuance could leave less money for either use unless another funding source supplies the difference.

The filing also reported about $18 billion of MSTR stock available for issuance and sale as of November 8. That is remaining offering capacity, not cash already raised or a commitment from investors to buy it.

A tracker selling an existing MSTR share transfers it to another investor. Strategy receives proceeds when it issues new shares. These are separate transactions, so a tracker’s sale does not directly withdraw money from the company or dispose of its Bitcoin.

The potential connection runs through price and demand. If rebalancing weakens either enough to make new MSTR issuance less attractive, Strategy could face a tighter choice between Bitcoin accumulation, preferred buybacks and cash retention. Its June capital framework already makes common-equity issuance dependent on market conditions, capital needs, trading levels and management’s judgment about shareholder value.

The size of that pressure remains uncertain. Strategy’s consultation response estimates that funds linked to MSCI’s global investable indexes held about 13 million shares, or 3.1% of basic shares outstanding. The page supplies no holdings observation date, so those figures do not establish November 12 exposure or the amount trackers will sell.

Strategy has already authorized and used Bitcoin sales. Its October 29 results disclosed approximately $432 million of Bitcoin sold year to date to fund part of its preferred dividends and STRC buybacks. Those sales preceded MSCI’s final deletion decision.

The June monetization program permits sales for reserve funding, dividends and interest, and securities repurchases. It obligates none. Further sales within those purposes depend on management’s capital-allocation judgment; activity beyond authorized purposes or limits requires additional board approval.

Cash also has distinct roles. At November 8, Strategy reported about $5 billion in its USD Reserve supporting preferred dividends and debt interest, alongside $146 million of separate USD Cash for broader treasury uses. Under the June policy, other reserve uses require board authorization. Its July buyback policy specifically bars reserve-funded STRC repurchases and leaves buybacks discretionary.

The next financing disclosures will show whether MSTR or preferred issuance supplies fresh capital, how much goes to Bitcoin and whether buybacks continue to absorb the larger allocation.
