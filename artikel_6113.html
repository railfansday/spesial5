Air travelers returning home from Thanksgiving can breathe a sigh of relief.

Flight schedules were close to normal on Friday after a series of storms snarled flights on both coasts during the run-up to Thanksgiving.

And Mother Nature appeared not to have any more major curveballs to throw at travelers between now and Sunday, normally one of the busiest air travel periods of the year as holiday-goers return home.

That came after more than 460 flights were canceled on Thursday, according to data from FlightAware. The bad weather complicated the plans of tens of thousands of travelers with flights on Thanksgiving Day. East Coast airports serving the metro areas of New York City, Washington, D.C., Boston, Philadelphia and Baltimore were the hardest hit, but disruptions rippled across airports in Florida, the Midwest and the West Coast.

The main culprit: A Thanksgiving Day coastal storm on the East Coast that was strong enough to cause the first-ever postponement of the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade in Midtown Manhattan.

This week's flight problems came after a chaotic few weeks for air travel in the U.S., which featured a nationwide ground stop on Nov. 13 — and then tech outages at two of the largest U.S. carriers.

Now, Mother Nature has interfered with holiday travelers' plans.

“The timing couldn’t be worse,” Fox Weather meteorologist Bob Van Dillen had told TPG entering the Thanksgiving rush.

We've often seen how significant East Coast storms can snarl air traffic. With so many key airline hubs, a major weather system can cause cascading flight woes across the country.

While Thursday could be a messy weather day, Thanksgiving Day itself typically brings much smaller crowds to airports.

According to preliminary data from the Transportation Security Administration, Thursday was the single quietest day at U.S. airports in nearly five years.

The forecast looks favorable through Sunday as travelers return from the holiday. The Sunday after Thanksgiving is typically the single busiest day of the year to fly.

Last year, the post-Thanksgiving rush set the record as the busiest day ever at U.S. airports for passenger traffic.

Entering the holiday rush, the TSA had projected more than 3 million passengers could fly on Nov. 29. That would be the first time U.S. passenger traffic would eclipse that mark in 2026.
