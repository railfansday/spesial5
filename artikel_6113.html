It's going to be a rough weekend at airports, as the U.S. air travel system rebounds from one of the most significant disruptions in decades.

Thousands of flights have been canceled for Saturday, after the Federal Aviation Administration halted most takeoffs and landings across the country for several hours Friday evening.

Airlines were cleared to resume service late last night. But travelers will face significant disruptions into the weekend — especially Saturday.

By 7:45 a.m. EST Saturday, flight cancellations for the day were nearing 3,500, according to data from FlightAware.

Nationwide, more than 12,000 departures have been shuttered since Friday.

“It’s going to take us some time to unwind what’s happened," Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said, speaking Friday evening at the White House.

Most departing flights in the U.S.were grounded indefinitely late Friday afternoon after the FAA reported communication issues impacting air traffic control centers.

The fallout for travelers was severe, stranding travelers across the country and forcing hundreds of flights to divert.

Now, the race is on for airlines to reset their operations — and get passengers, planes and crewmembers to the right destination.

Passengers on a Delta Air Lines flight from New York's John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) to Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) monitor coverage of Friday's nationwide ground stop. ERIC ROSEN/THE POINTS GUY

The FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop late Friday afternoon after discovering a communications outage affecting air traffic control.

It happened as crews were working to upgrade critical FAA equipment, Duffy said.

The disruption marked only the second time since 9/11 that a ground stop affecting the entire nation had been issued.

All told, airlines canceled more than 9,000 flights on Friday, according to FlightAware. Another 7,000-plus were delayed.

Some international flights and service within Alaska and Hawaii continued, despite the outage.

But the vast majority of domestic departures on Friday evening were affected.

With the ground stop lifted, airlines can now begin the complex process of resetting their operation.

That means getting aircraft, travelers, crews and baggage to the right destinations.

Expect to see the tally of flight cancellations continue to climb on Saturday. It's likely conditions at airports would improve over the ensuing days — if no additional problems pop up and Mother Nature cooperates.

Airlines issued travel advisories in the midst of the travel chaos. These waivers typically allow passengers with flights booked more flexibility to change their itineraries:

If your flight is affected by this weekend's disruptions, stay glued to your airline app — you may be able to search for a new flights and re-book yourself without waiting on hold or in line at the airport.

Don't see a good flight option for days? Keep checking back: with a lot of passengers shifting plans across the country, it's still possible seats will open up.

Passengers whose flights are canceled or significantly delayed are entitled to a refund if they choose not to travel or accept a rebooked flight, regardless of the reason for the disruption.

Under Department of Transportation rules, a significant delay generally means arriving at your final destination at least three hours late on a domestic itinerary or six hours late on an international itinerary. For passengers who decide to not travel on those flights, airlines must provide refunds to the original form of payment, not just travel credits.

In large-scale travel disruptions like this, rental cars and hotel rooms at or near airports can book up quickly, so it can make sense to act fast if you want to secure a backup option.

If you have airline miles you can use to book a flight for tomorrow, that can also be a good self-insurance policy in case the disruption isn't resolved quickly. Just make sure you understand the cancellation and refund rules before booking a backup ticket.

Because this disruption was due to issues outside airlines' control, carriers are unlikely to reimburse stranded passengers for hotel, ground transportation and meal costs.

Some rewards credit cards do offer built-in trip delay or cancellation protection if you used them to book your original airline tickets.

However, it's not guaranteed that those benefits will cover FAA groundings of this nature. Still, save your receipts for any unexpected expenses in case you're eligible for reimbursement.
