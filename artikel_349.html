Haaretz Expands US Presence Amid Israel’s Move to the Right

Allison Kaplan Sommer, the host of Haaretz’s English-language podcast, said she saw the paper connecting liberal-leaning and secular American Jews with their Israeli counterparts.Credit...Avishag Shaar-Yashuv for The New York Times

Marc Tracy reported from Tel Aviv and New York.

The century-old newspaper Haaretz has long been favored by Israel’s more liberal readers, its journalism and commentary often casting a harsh light on right-wing leaders and government policies toward Palestinians in Gaza and the West Bank.

But its editorial outlook is increasingly out of step with a country that has moved rightward in recent decades — something seen as recently as in last month’s elections, in which a healthy majority of parliamentary seats went to center, right-of-center and Orthodox Jewish parties. At the same time, the digital edition of Haaretz’s English-language journalism has experienced growing popularity as an increasing number of Jews around the world, especially younger ones, view Israel more critically than in the past.

It has more than doubled the size of its small U.S. bureau, including hiring four correspondents to cover beats like national politics and a younger generation of American Jews, and is making more vertical videos and podcast episodes to reflect the country’s news consumption habits. In recent weeks, Haaretz’s reporters filed dispatches from Maine and Michigan, Texas and Florida, and Georgia and New York exploring the midterm races from Jewish perspectives.

And Haaretz is highlighting original English-language journalism that helps explain Israel to readers abroad. Its flagship English-language podcast, for instance, has grown its listenership by two-thirds this year with weekly election-themed episodes and overviews of Israel-based reporting, such as a recent interview with the authors of an investigation into a network of right-wing Israeli think tanks.

“We’re trying to think about ourselves not only as an Israeli newspaper, but also as a Jewish newspaper,” said Noa Landau, who was appointed this year to be global editor.
