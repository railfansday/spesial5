Ethereum’s market structure is facing a double problem: weak liquidity on Binance and a sharp change in deposit activity following the October 29 hack. The latest Ethereum liquidity data raises questions about whether a price rebound can attract enough buyers or may be simply bring another wave of selling.

The 30-day liquidity index on Binance has plunged from around 1.1 to 0.35 as ETH declined from roughly $2,750 to $1,700. Aside from the chart’s opening period in 2019, that’s its lowest reading.

More concerning, the index has fallen below its 2022 lows while ETH remains above its 2022 price bottom. That disconnect makes the next recovery harder to interpret.

A rebound in activity doesn’t automatically mean fresh buying. In 2022, liquidity recovered before ETH revisited its range lows. In late 2023, improving activity accompanied a more sustained advance.

The exchange’s on-chain deposit data has also changed dramatically. Daily ETH deposits reportedly fell from around 2,800 in October to roughly 300 after withdrawals reopened in November.

Ethereum-based USDT deposits dropped from 16,000 to about 160 daily. Tron-based USDT deposits fell from roughly 100,000 to fewer than 30.

Yet BTC deposits remained comparatively steady, slipping from around 10,300 to 9,000. Meanwhile, daily stablecoin value still averages about $680 million, and average ETH deposits rose from 80 to 270 ETH.

The deposit collapse could reflect users changing networks, fewer small transfers or Binance changing its deposit infrastructure.

For now, Ethereum liquidity on Binance remains a warning sign, not a confirmed market-wide verdict. A higher low alongside recovering spot activity would offer stronger evidence of demand than rising transfer counts alone.
