Game Recap: Furious comeback by Suns bench falls short in 131-123 loss

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Lakers came out of the gates hot, and the Suns did not. From there, every time the Suns started to put together a run, the Lakers answered with a bigger run. And while the bench gave the Suns some hope with a furious comeback attempt in the fourth quarter, the Suns dug themselves far too big a hole to climb out of, ultimately falling to the Lakers at home, 131-123.

If the Suns had any hope of an upset, they needed to slow down the Lakers' potent offense, and they failed to do that for much of the afternoon. The Lakers came out of the gates hot and did not really slow down. Luka scored 20 in the first quarter on the way to a 43-point afternoon (together with nine assists), but the Suns' inability to stop Lakers not named Luka, inability to keep the Lakers off the offensive boards, and inability to stop the three is what ultimately did the team in.

Quentin Grimes scored 22 points for the Lakers, including 11 in the third quarter, while shooting 5-of-6 from three. The Lakers grabbed 12 offensive rebounds – with Walker Kessler having six of them – which led to 21 second-chance points. And the Lakers shot 16-of-41 (39%) from three, with Luka and Grimes combining to hit 10 of them.

For the Suns, the story was the fact that the starters dug such a deep hole that the bench could not get them out. Devin Booker was 4-14, Jalen Green was 4-11, and Dillon Brooks was 0-of-2 (what?!). The Suns actually out-rebounded the Lakers (42 to 40) and grabbed more offensive rebounds than the Lakers (14 to 12), but not thanks to the starters. The bench grabbed 29 of the Suns' 42 rebounds and 12 of their 14 offensive rebounds. But the Suns' bench not only outperformed the starters on the boards, but they also outperformed them overall.

Collin Gillespie led the Suns in scoring with 26 points (including 5-of-10 from three). Jordan Goodwin led the team in rebounds, with seven. Ryan Dunn grabbed four offensive rebounds, and Luke Kennard chipped in another three.

And let's not forget Koa Peat. Mr. Arizona sparked a furious fourth-quarter rally that saw the Suns' bench cut a 24-point Lakers lead to just five, before running out of steam down the stretch.

Overall, this game ended up being much closer than it felt throughout the afternoon. The Suns started the fourth quarter down by 24, and the bench was entirely responsible for the team nearly pulling this one out. Notably, Jordan Ott stuck with the bench throughout that quarter. He did not go back to the starters. It felt like the right move, but also truly confirms the lack of cohesiveness with the starting five.

At the end of the day, the Suns miss out on a chance to get to .500, falling to 5-7, as the Lakers win their eighth in a row to move to 12-2.

The Suns got off to the slowest start we've seen since opening night, letting the Lakers score the first 10 points of the game — eight of them by Luka — before Miles Bridges scored the team's first points with 9:24 left in the first quarter. The Lakers followed that 10-2 start by going on a 13-3 run, which left the Suns trailing by 18 after just over 5 minutes of playing time.

Jordan Ott went to the bench, and the bench and Devin Booker managed to trim that lead to 9 by the end of the quarter. A Booker jumper, a Collin Gillespie three, then a Kahman Maluach layup on a nice feed from Book sparked a 7-0 run, to make it a 23-12 game. From there, Book and Maluach were responsible for the rest of the Suns' points that quarter – with Book scoring eight and Man Man chipping in four.

Maluach flashed some of his defensive potential on a play where he challenged and altered a layup attempt by Sandro Mamukelashvili and showed a quick second jump by immediately blocking a Jarred Vanderbilt layup attempt.

At the end of the first, the Suns found themselves down 33-24.

With Luka out to start the second, the Suns needed to take advantage of the non-Luka minutes – and, at first, it looked like they did. The Suns started the quarter with a Gillespie three to cut the lead to six, but that was the closest they would get (until the fourth). The Suns ended up losing the non-Luka minutes – letting Collin Sexton score eight points in the second. By the time Luka came back in the game with 4:56 left in the half, the Suns found themselves down 17.

That lead ballooned to 19, before the Suns started chipping away again. At the 3:36 mark, they were down 19. At that time, the story of the game was the difference from three (with the Lakers being +12) and from the free-throw line (+7). The Lakers also grabbed seven offensive rebounds in the second frame alone.

The only player showing any true effort on the boards for the Suns at this juncture was Jordan Goodwin — who was fouled on two separate occasions while trying to grab offensive rebounds off missed free throws.

Despite only having 10 points from Booker in the first half, the Suns were only down 10 at halftime.

The third quarter started a lot like the first. The Lakers were scoring and we could not. After the Lakers scored the first eight points, the Suns finally got some points from Devin Booker with 9:28 left. But it continued to be all Lakers from there.

Just like Collin Sexton stepped up for the Lakers in the second quarter, the third quarter belonged to Quentin Grimes. Grimes, who came in shooting 28% from three on the season, scored 11 in the quarter, including three 3-pointers. Luka also scored in double figures in the third quarter, chipping in 12.

In the third, the Suns shot 9-of-21 overall and 2-of-11 from three, while the Lakers shot 14-of-23 overall and 5-of-9 from three.

The fourth quarter started with little hope and a 24-point deficit. The Suns came out and promptly scored the first six points, and after the Lakers got the lead back to 22, Collin Gillespie hit back-to-back threes to cut the lead to 16 with 10 minutes left.

But then the offensive rebounds started coming back. Thybulle had a tip-in off a missed three, then on the next possession, Kessler had a put-back dunk on a missed layup by Thybulle. The Suns were down 19 with 8:15 left, and Koa Peat checked in – making it look like Jordan Ott had decided to pack it in.

But Peat checking in resulted in a furious comeback attempt that made a mundane afternoon into an exciting one.

Peat immediately impacted the game, fighting off Kessler on a Suns miss, which allowed the team to keep possession, then scoring on a strong shot over Kessler.

Collin Gillespie was also not ready for the game to be called, as he drew an and-1 on the next possession, to get the Suns within 14. This brought Luka and his 37 points back into the game. The first possession of Luka's return resulted in a turnover, and Gillespie missed a step-through jumper, but Peat was there for the tip-in. Suns down 12 with just over six minutes to go.

The Suns used a 10-1 run to find themselves down single digits with 5:02 to go. And they kept chipping away.

A Ryan Dunn three cut the lead to six with 4:37 left. Jake Laravia answers with a three of his own, but then Luke Kennard drops one to make it a six-point game again, but a missed three by Laravia followed by an offensive rebound and then a BANKED in three by Lavaria sealed the game for the Lakers.

An exciting comeback, but, ultimately, a 131-123 loss — and a loss that just further magnifies the obvious disconnect amongst the Suns starters. But still, Koa Peat…

The Suns hit the road immediately for an away matchup tomorrow against the Denver Nuggets.
