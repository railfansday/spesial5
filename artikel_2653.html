NBA winners and losers after one month: Wemby soars, injuries pile up

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The first four weeks of the NBA season have been nothing but unpredictable.

And one month in, the narratives and storylines have already started to take shape, as teams work to climb up the standings.

We saw old faces like LeBron James (Philadelphia 76ers), Giannis Antetokounmpo (Miami Heat) and Jaylen Brown (76ers) make their impacts on their new homes.

And we even saw a player, San Antonio Spurs phenom Victor Wembanyama, "shunk" a ball, or put it through the net in a hybrid shot-dunk.

Here are the winners and losers from the first month of the 2026-27 NBA regular season.

It seems like, every week, he's doing something that hasn't been done in the history of basketball. Now in his fourth season in the NBA, Wembanyama is averaging career bests in points (29.9 per game), rebounds (12.3), blocks (3.9), steals (1.3) and is averaging a career low in turnovers (2.2). The most impressive thing about his offense, however, is the ruthless efficiency with which he's playing.

Wembanyama is no longer chasing forced, low-percentage shots. His attempts are coming basically at the rim or from 3-point range. His defensive presence is singular and is en route to his second consecutive unanimous Defensive Player of the Year award; he was the first to ever win the award unanimously. He is playing with confidence. He's playing his best basketball yet.

He's dropping historic numbers and has taken complete ownership of the offense.

The Lakers also put complementary pieces around him, players such as Walker Kessler, a lob threat and rim protecting center who's giving Dončić an outlet for easy offense. Once Reaves returns, Los Angeles looks like a team that should keep contending.

Though the competition hasn't been stiff, the Sixers are on a five-game winning streak, Joel Embiid is returning to form and LeBron James is making a significant winning impact. Not only that, but Jaylen Brown and Tyrese Maxey are also generating exceptional scoring numbers. It's safe to expect this team, which is still learning how to play together, to improve even more as the season wears on.

But Philadelphia hasn't really blown out many opponents, and its most recent win, by three over the 1-16 Bucks, nearly went to overtime after Milwaukee nearly erased a 13-point lead in the second half.

You would never have thought that the Timberwolves had lost their best player and the most dynamic two-guard in the NBA just four games into the season. Minnesota is trucking along in the West. The offense – behind LaMelo Ball, Ayo Dosunmu and Jaden McDaniels – is doing just enough. It's the defense, the one that leads the NBA in rating, allowing just 103.8 points per 100 possessions, that's carrying the team.

In Oklahoma City's game Sunday, Nov. 22 against the Heat, only nine players were available. Reigning two-time MVP Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, who suffered a lower back contusion on a hard fall two days earlier, was not one of them. Against Miami, it was Oklahoma City's 16th game of the season; it was the eighth different lineup the Thunder had deployed. It's tough to compete with that kind of disadvantage.

The Golden State Warriors (Stephen Curry), Minnesota Timberwolves (Anthony Edwards), Indiana Pacers (Tyrese Haliburton), Toronto Raptors (Kawhi Leonard), Detroit Pistons (Jalen Duren), Orlando Magic (Paolo Banchero) and Utah Jazz (Jaren Jackson Jr., Lauri Markkanen) are also among the teams that have lost a player with at least one All-Star appearance because of injuries.

The Jazz, after years of irrelevance and tanking, were seemingly in position to take a step forward. That simply hasn't materialized. Utah is turning over the ball far too much, the offense isn't scoring what it needs to and some of the younger players haven't stepped up after vets went down with injuries.

The Bulls lost star rookie Caleb Wilson (fractured right hand) and Norman Powell hasn't been able to carry the team to a consistent level of play. The biggest issue is a defense that ranks 29th in rating (119.3) and simply allows opponents to score far too easily. Neither team looks even close to competing for a playoff spot.
