Week 11 NFL Defense Rankings: Vikings, Seahawks Headline Best NFL Defenses Right Now, Week 11 Fantasy D/ST Rankings

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Who has the best NFL defense right now? Sunday’s Week 10 action saw utterly dominant performances from the Minnesota Vikings and Seattle Seahawks, with those units once again demonstrating why they are in a class of their own among the best NFL defenses in 2026. Outside of that, we saw quite a few notable performances with a mix of new injuries and incoming returns shaping how a few units are viewed moving forward.

Let’s dive into our NFL defense rankings after Week 10. At the bottom, you can find our Week 11 fantasy defense rankings.

The Minnesota Vikings’ defense is even more terrifying now that it’s supported by a viable starting quarterback. In Kirk Cousins’ first start with the team, the Vikings offense possessed the football for 35 minutes, and that meant this Vikings defense could stay fresh late in the game. Fittingly, Brian Flores ate the interior Packers’ offensive line alive and had Jordan Love either pressured, sacked, or hit on nearly every dropback. Minnesota finished the day allowing just 220 total yards (20 yards per drive) with an eye-popping 12 quarterback hits, 10 tackles for loss, and 8 sacks. The 49ers do have a much better offensive line and run game than Green Bay, so that could pose more issues for Minnesota.

Facing the Seattle Seahawks defense is such an unfair first NFL start to make for a quarterback. Fernando Mendoza did look pretty impressive early on, engineering two scoring drives on his first two possessions. After those 10 points and 95 total yards, however, Seattle gave up just 3 points and 68 total yards across the next eight drives. The dominance stretched from the defensive line (six sacks and 12 quarterback hits) to the secondary, utter domination for three quarters. Mike Macdonald now gets two weeks to prepare for the 49ers offense, which feels necessary.

The Jacksonville Jaguars defense we see take the field next Sunday against the New York Giants will certainly be different than the one that got sent out on the field in Week 10 versus Tennessee. Jacksonville held off on All-Pro safety Jessie Bates and stout defensive tackle Jordan Davis making their debuts for a week, wanting them to have more time to settle into Anthony Campanile’s system. When they take the field, Davis should look closer to the version of the player we saw in 2025 when he had the support of quality edge defenders, and Bates is the best safety in Anthony Campanile’s tenure as defensive coordinator.

In their final five games before the bye week, this Tampa Bay Buccaneers defense limited opponents to a 56.8% completion rate, 6.5 yards per attempt, and a 57.7 passer rating. We’re highlighting that success because so much credit is already given to a front that has earned its reputation (79.1 rush ypg and 3.5 ypc allowed) as the best run defense in the NFL. Returning from the bye, Tampa Bay’s defense is the one thing that even gives it a chance at Ford Field against the Lions offense. Even if the Buccaneers don’t win, this defense can more than hold its own.

It is a shame a failed physical wiped out the Daiyan Henley trade, because he was exactly what defensive coordinator Vance Joseph needed to shore up one of the few weaknesses on this unit. Even without the coverage linebacker, the Denver Broncos defense has been very good even through absences from Pat Surtain (two games missed), Brandon Jones (one game), and Jonathon Cooper (six games). With everyone back after the Week 10 bye, we see no reason why Denver won’t have one of the best NFL defenses in the second half of the season.

For the first time since New Years Eve 2023, Aaron Donald recorded a sack in an NFL game. That statistic and the fact that Trent McDuffie is healthy are really the only things to highlight from SUnday regarding the Los Angeles Rams defense. That’s what happens when you face a Cardinals offense starting mostly backups and third-stringers. We’ll next see this Rams defense in Week 12, when its defensive line should make an early Thanksgiving Day feast out of the Packers offensive line but Los Angeles may also have real issues covering Matthew Golden and Christian Watson.

Who has the best NFL defense right now? Sunday’s Week 10 action saw utterly dominant performances from the Minnesota Vikings and Seattle Seahawks, with those units once again demonstrating why they are in a class of their own among the best NFL defenses in 2026. Outside of that, we saw quite a few notable performances, with a mix of new injuries and incoming returns shaping how a few units are viewed moving forward.

George Karlaftis is now up to 4.5 sacks in the last three games, coming alive at a time when someone on the edge needs to step up for the Kansas City Chiefs. It was a great day overall for Steve Spagnuolo’s defense, which finished with five quarterback hits and three sacks of Michael Penix Jr. and largely kept Bijan Robinson (71 yards on 16 carries) in check by an All-Pro’s standards. Kansas City has now held opponents to 12.5 PPG in the last two weeks, and that number could drop even further given who Arizona will be trotting out there next Sunday.

The Green Bay Packers defense played relatively well on Sunday at Lambeau Field. It allowed just 26 total yards per drive with a 25 percent red-zone touchdown rate despite being out there for 35 minutes of action. Of those 30 points allowed, nine came off turnovers that put Minnesota immediately in scoring range. When Green Bay returns from its Week 11 bye, it should have linebacker Zaire Franklin and edge rusher Micah Parsons back on the field, with rookie Brandon Cisse (ribs) also likely to return. That’s a loaded unit, especially in comparison to a Rams offense that still won’t have Puka Nacua or Davante Adams on the day before Thanksgiving.

From Weeks 1-7, the Chicago Bears defense allowed opponents to average 8.7 yards per attempt with a 102.8 passer rating and a 64.9% completion rate. With a top-tier cornerback not on the market, Chicago went out and landed All-Pro defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons. In his two games since suiting up for this Bears defense, quarterbacks completed just 53 percent of their passes with a 64.6 passer rating and averaged only 6.5 yards per attempt. Some of that success could be attributed to facing Sam Darnold and Baker Mayfield, but it’s not as if a Week 11 matchup against Tyler Shough and the struggling Saints offense will change the perception. All that matters is the Bears now have a very good defense.

That was a poor showing by the San Francisco 49ers defense, no other way to describe it. The trio of Cowboys receivers each found the end zone with 70-plus receiving yards, combining for 288 yards and five scores. It’s a bit worrisome considering that Justin Jefferson, Jordan Addison and T.J. Hockenson are coming to town next week. There’s plenty for play-caller Raheem Morris to try to clean up in practice this week, but this secondary appears a bit leakier than we initially expected.

It was a rough start for this Cleveland Browns defense on Sunday. The Texans offense put up 17 points and 189 total yards on its first three drives, averaging 8.2 yards per play. From then on, Cleveland locked down and limited Houston to 7 points, 101 total yards and 2.5 yards per play. This unit clearly is not quite on the same level we all witnessed single-handedly lead the team to a few wins in 2025, it remains awful versus the run, but defensive coordinator Mike Rutenberg is doing some impressive work in his first year at the helm. It is some well-earned rest in Week 11 for this unit.

After a few steps forward from this New York Giants defense, we’d consider Thursday Night Football a step back. It’s not just the game-deciding touchdown that was given up to Stefon Diggs; it was tight end Ben Sinnott (42 yards and a touchdown) going from lingering NFL Draft bust to arguably a career-best performance. New York still has some issues on the back end that need work, and sacking Jayden Daniels just once is a letdown. It’s going to be a rough Sunday at MetLife for this Giants defense against Jacksonville, but it does get the Colts offense after that in Week 12.

The game script worked out perfectly for the Cincinnati Bengals defense on Sunday Night Football. Joe Burrow put the team ahead with a huge lead early, forcing Pittsburgh to utterly abandon its run game. Cincinnati also deserves credit for taking the checkdown option (Jaylen Warren) away from Aaron Rodgers, because that eliminated the one ‘easy’ answer he had in an offense that is bogged down by a bad receiving corps. We still think the Bengals defense ins weak against the run and can be exploited over the middle of the field, but this group has now proven multiple times this year that it can play at a fairly high level.

Jaelan Phillips returned to action in Week 10, and the Carolina Panthers defense held its opponent under 20 points for the first time since Oct. 18. Before long, cornerbacks Will Lee, Mike Jackson and Jaycee Horn will rejoin the secondary, and that might be around the same time that linebacker Bobby Okereke gets back out there. A healthy version of Carolina’s defense can be league-average; we have seen it before and have a fairly high degree of confidence it can happen again.

With just two days of practice, safety Amani Hooker made his presence felt as part of this Buffalo Bills defense. He seemingly never left the field, finishing with the second-most solo tackles (four) and recording a pass defended. Hooker’s addition, and facing Cade Klubik, helped Buffalo limit the Jets’ passing game to 267 net passing yards on 45 dropbacks with just 12 total points. Buffalo’s ascent up the NFL defense rankings is largely because of Hooker’s arrival; he fills the biggest void this secondary had. While the Bills will still struggle defensively against elite offenses, this pass defense should be much better than what we saw in the first half of the regular season.

Dan Quinn may not stick around as the Washington Commanders head coach in 2027, but a tip of the cap to what defensive coordinator Daronte Jones is doing lately. In the last four games, the Commanders defense has allowed just 18.5 points per game, limiting opponents to an 84.2 passer rating with three times as many sacks taken (nine) as passing touchdowns thrown. Unfortunately for Jones, that lack of high-end talent in Washington’s secondary will be exposed in Week 11 on Monday Night Football versus the Bengals.

The 2025 version of the Houston Texans defense showed up early on Sunday, allowing just 3 points and 73 total yards (18.3 yards per drive) on Cleveland’s first four possessions. Unfortunately for DeMeco Ryans, the horrors of the 2026 version of this unit returned to the field after that, with the Browns putting up 24 points and 306 total yards (30.6 yards per drive) the rest of the game. There’s hope for Azeez Al-Shaiir and Danielle Hunter to return relatively soon, but there’s just no escaping how big of a letdown Houston’s defense has been this fall. For fantasy football managers, the Texans D/ST is still a great play on Thursday Night Football in Week 11 because it faces the Chargers.

In the first game without Jessie Bates, the Atlanta Falcons defense allowed 35 points, 461 total yards and a 7.3 yards-per-play average with a 100 percent red-zone touchdown rate. It is safe to say the All-Pro safety’s instincts were missed on Sunday. We should also note, for added context, that Atlanta has allowed 30-plus points to three of its last six opponents. After an unexpectedly strong start to the season, this unit is headed in the wrong direction. When Atlanta returns from the bye, it faces Justin Jefferson and Jordan Addison in Week 12, then the Lions offense in Week 13.

As little faith as we have in the Baltimore Ravens’ ability to stop deep passes right now, there’s even less confidence in Justin Herbert. The Baltimore Ravens defensive line should also have little trouble on Monday Night Football creating pressure from every angle, rattling Herbert and really preventing Los Angeles from establishing any kind of rhythm that it hasn’t found all year.

Unfortunately, the Aidan Hutchinson injury hangs a cloud over another strong performance from the Detroit Lions defense. Hutchinson is going to miss significant time; it’s just a question of whether he’ll suit up again for Detroit this season. As long as the All-Pro edge rusher is out, Detroit will have to get far more aggressive with the blitz, and that’s the last thing you really want with these corners.

Regarding the long-term perspective, we completely get the Jordan Davis trade. The tandem of Davis and Jalen Carter wasn’t cost-effective, and the Philadelphia Eagles defense wasn’t getting its money’s worth by any means. Eagles general manager Howie Roseman now has a pair of 2027 first-round picks and money that can be used to sign Moro Ojomo to a long-term deal. With all that said, this Eagles defense did allow 244.5 passing yards per game and 125.5 rushing yards per game in its last six contests. Without Davis, it stands to reason Philadelphia is going to have serious woes stopping Jaylen Warren in Week 11.

Make that 80 points and 972 total yards allowed by this New England Patriots defense in the last two games. Even with two interceptions, Jared Goff (303 yards, 9.8 ypa and 101.9 passer rating) thrived when he attacked New England’s corners, and predictably, Jahmyr Gibbs erupted for over 200 scrimmage yards against a defense that allowed 129 rushing yards to Green Bay a week prior. The Week 11 bye isn’t going to change anything, so don’t overreact to what happens on Nov. 29, because that will be a greater reflection on how bad the Chargers offense is.

Following Week 10, opponents are averaging 33 points and 368.3 total yards per game in the last four weeks against the New York Jets defense. It’s one of the many reasons why head coach Aaron Glenn will be fired at the end of the 2026 NFL season, if not sooner. New York does get to face the Chargers offense next Sunday, followed by the Dolphins offense, so this defense should at least put up some better numbers and build some momentum before entering its bye.

Running backs continue to haunt the New Orleans Saints. Heading into Sunday, New Orleans allowed the fourth-most rushing yards per game (133.9) and the seventh-highest yards per carry (4.5). While Chuba Hubbard only put up 92 rushing yards on Sunday (3.4 yards per carry), he totaled 172 scrimmage yards because he turned five receptions into 80 yards on five targets. New Orleans did an admirable job shutting down Jalen Coker and Tetairoa McMillan, but that might say more about what Bryce Young had to take behind his offensive line. In the next three weeks, we’d anticipate the Saints defense getting taken to school by the Bears, Bengals and Packers.

The Tennessee Titans defense certainly looked like a unit that is just beginning to figure out how to play without its team leaders in pressures (Jeffery Simmons) and interceptions (Amani Hooker, five). Jacksonville put up 40 points and 459 total yards in just 10 possessions on Sunday, with a ton of that damage coming in the second half. It’s not going to get any easier next week, with this Titans defense set to face Dak Prescott, CeeDee Lamb, George Pickens, and Javonte Williams.

Taking the field without defensive tackle Zach Sieler, this Miami Dolphins defense was always going to face lowered expectations. It’s just nearly impossible to truly evaluate a defense when it doesn’t have to worry about the opponent’s passing game because Daniel Jones is under center and he’s working with Ashton Dulin and Laquon Treadwell as his top receivers. Miami absolutely got gashed on the ground — 172 rushing yards and 5.1 yards per carry allowed — but its pass defense was spared. Circumstances will be vastly different in Week 11 at Buffalo.

In his first NFL game ever, Kyle McCord put up 209 passing yards on the Indianapolis Colts defense with Caleb Douglas as his top receiver. That’s to say we still aren’t seeing any reason to think that, despite some of the advanced metrics, the Colts defense is anything but a bottom-five unit moving forward. Set to face C.J. Stroud and Nico Collins on Thursday Night Football, Houston’s offense should feast.

Let’s remember for a moment that the Rams offense is relying on Xavier Smith, Tutu Atwell and Konata Mumpfield to be its top receivers. Los Angeles still put up 34 points and 436 total yards against the Arizona Cardinals defense on Sunday, with Matthew Stafford accounting for 249 yards and three touchdowns with a 122.1 passer rating. Blake Corum and Ronnie Rivers, just for the icing on the cake, rushed for 181 yards. This is a very bad defense, and it’s made to look even worse now because its offense can’t move the football whatsoever.

For the third time this season, the Dallas Cowboys defense allowed 30-plus points to an opponent and Sunday was the fifth time a team put up 390-plus yards and averaged 5.8 yards per play against Dallas. We could expand that to six if we set the threshold at 389 yards. Of course, San Francisco did more damage than any other opponent this year with 46 points and 580 total yards even with just three red-zone trips. It gets much easier in the next two weeks against the Titans and Eagles offenses, but even those teams could put up numbers since Arizona did back on Nov. 1.

Seattle only put up 21 points on Sunday against the Las Vegas Raiders defense with two red-zone trips on 11 drives. However, the Seahawks offense did average 6.9 yards per play, and that pretty closely aligns with a defense that allowed 6.3 yards per play to its opponents in the last seven games. Suffice it to say, this remains the worst NFL defense right now. Jonah Coleman and Bo Nix should be eagerly counting down the days to Sunday’s matchup, especially since they had two weeks to prepare for it.
