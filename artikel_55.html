Qantas to outsource 850 local jobs with most roles moving to India

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Qantas CEO Vanessa Hudson apologised for the "hard news" of 850 job cuts

Qantas will outsource 850 local corporate jobs to consulting giant Accenture with most roles moving to India as the airline fast-tracks AI investment.

In an internal email to staff on Tuesday, the Australian company's CEO Vanessa Hudson said "this is hard news for some and I'm sorry for the uncertainty it brings".

The job cuts will begin mid-2027, she said, and start with finance, procurement, people and marketing roles shifting "mostly" to teams in India and "some in Australia" as the firm seeks to modernise its IT systems.

Hudson said the "scale and speed of change in technology and AI is something none of us has seen in our lifetime", and it was in talks with unions and staff on the plans.

The move comes days after the airline provided a market update, flagging fare increases and flight cuts amid higher fuel costs.

On its new multi-year deal with Accenture, Hudson said it was about "modernising" the airline's IT systems to become "more efficient".

Qantas spends more than AU$1bn (US$691m; Â£514m) a year on technology, she said, and the company's back-end systems needed to be upgraded.

"Like every airline, we run on technology built up over decades for a different era, including more than 1,000 legacy applications that were never designed to work together," she said.

The deal with Accenture means "we will redesign and simplify parts of how our corporate functions work and reduce duplication", Hudson said, so staff can "spend more time on work that creates value for our customers and the business".

The company said that "in time", the work that Accenture takes over will be "transformed further with automation and AI".

Staff affected by the job cuts will be redeployed "where possible", the firm added, including new roles in Australia.

Qantas - which employs about 28,000 staff in Australia and another 4,000 globally - said thousands of its staff already use AI in their daily work.

The BBC has contacted the Australian Services Union for comment.
