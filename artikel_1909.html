Sources: Martin O’Neill has clear Celtic priority as January transfer plan revealed

Martin O'Neill is hoping to see his Celtic squad strengthened in the January transfer window, sources have told Football Insider.

The Hoops have had ups and downs so far this season but remain at the top of the Scottish Premiership, despite an inconsistent run of form.

The summer transfer window was also a mixed bag, with the likes of Mika Baur and Camilo Duran impressing while Kasper Hogh and Sam Johnstone have struggled.

As I revealed on the latest edition of Football Insider's Transfer Insider podcast, O'Neill will hope to see Celtic resist any temptation to weaken his squad in January.

Baur, in particular, is attracting significant interest after making a superb start to his career at Parkhead.

The 22-year-old, who joined Celtic in a deal worth just £4.5million, has become a crucial cog for the champions, and made his senior debut for Germany last month.

An ultimately disappointing summer transfer window has left several gaps in the Celtic team.

The Hoops missed out on a number of key targets and opted to fill much of the squad with players on loan, many of whom have not been given the opportunities.

If Celtic are to have a successful season, January must be a time to show ambition and correct some of those mistakes.

Certainly, that is what O'Neill is hoping to see happen, with his squad currently leaving much to be desired.

Speaking on the Transfer Insider podcast, I explained: "I don’t think Celtic will be contemplating cashing in on Mika Baur in January even if there was interest.

"Martin O’Neill’s stance right now is that he wants to strengthen his squad in January, not weaken it, and as I said, I think Mika Baur will have a key role for Celtic for the rest of season, and Celtic will be hoping for seasons beyond that as well."

As the January transfer window came around last season, then-interim manager O'Neill was not shy about demanding transfers.

"We need the players in and as quickly as possible," he said. "I would be hoping for it [to be busy] - but if the board were to listen to me then we would have about 56 players."

It remains to be seen if O'Neill will come out with the same enthusiasm as permanent manager this season.

He will know well that given the fan pressure on the Celtic board, such words would carry much weight.
