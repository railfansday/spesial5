Video games have always had an awkward relationship with age ratings, with a whole lot of legislation and systems only put in place after a game pushes the boundaries. Nowadays, it's incredibly common to find wanton gore and foul language throughout AAA titles, but sex and genitals are still treated carefully, only explored by a select few mainstream titles.

Grand Theft Auto has never shied away from the debauchery of its cityscapes, utilizing nudity and sex in both optional and main story content. With the monumental Grand Theft Auto 6 sure to include even more while focusing on its protagonists’ love life, South Korean rating boards are having a tough time rating the game, citing a record amount of “out-of-context genital nudity.”

It feels like any game that tries to take notes from the Grand Theft Auto formula is required to feature some form of wanton nudity both narratively and mechanically. Saint’s Row, Cyberpunk, and one of Grand Theft Auto 6’s leak sagas even opened with fully nude gameplay of both protagonists, it’s just a feature of the subgenre at this point.

Despite this, Grand Theft Auto 6 almost got refused a rating in South Korea twice, a status that would have left it rudderless in the region. The main argument seemed to be less centered on story moments of nudity, and more on the purely functional moments of nudity. We already know that strip clubs have gotten a major overhaul, but being able to freely streak as both Jason and Lucia was a sticking point.

Regulators who fought for the title to instead earn the Adults Only rating, the age rating it ended up with, argued that while the visual fidelity of the title was vastly improved, there was no shift between Grand Theft Auto 5 and 6 that justified banning 6 when they allowed 5 without issue. From one regulator: “If we respect the precedent set for its predecessor, nothing has significantly changed other than sensuality.”

The first postponement the game was handed got botched due to delayed delivery of the playable build, leaving the regulators with less playtime than they had hoped. While the initial vote tied and this one was postponed for less-than-desirable playtime, the third was a resounding 8-1 vote in favor of the game’s Adults Only rating.

The biggest shifts in opinion came from finding that no sexual interactions were possible while nude, leaving it more of an absurdist comedy beat than potential erotica. The regulators were worried the game shifted its focus fully towards nudity and sexuality, only after ample playtime finding it kept the series roots as an action-adventure crime thriller.

The games industry has had a desire to be taken seriously by the rest of entertainment for quite a while, leaning into all different kinds of AAA design culture that has shifted and waned. The grey and brown color palettes of the 2010s, the walk-and-talk segments, moving away from boss fights and closer to cutscenes, and all kinds of focus pivots can be explained by this desire to be seen as ‘cinematic’.

Where Rockstar manages to avoid the downsides of these industry shifts is by staying insular, focusing so hard on their craft and their own design philosophy that they shift the industry around them. When you ask most casual players to think of a cinematic video game, they probably think of a Rockstar title.

It’s Rockstar’s world, and we’re all just living in it, if the unprecedented hype surrounding GTA 6 wasn’t enough to clue you in on that fact already. Breaking boundaries and keeping the entire world at attention, Grand Theft Auto 6 is truly looking to be the game of the decade.
