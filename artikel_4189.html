AI is a problem for many reasons, one of which is its impact on RAM, other PC components, and the price of hardware. As AI companies like OpenAI buy up as much RAM as they can for their data centers, less is available for the rest of us, pushing prices higher and higher. It's textbook supply and demand, but while big companies can often stomach the price increases, consumers are left to suffer.

We've seen it with almost every video game hardware manufacturer, and over the last couple of years, the Steam Deck, Steam Machine, ROG Ally, Nintendo Switch 2, PlayStation 5, and Xbox Series have all seen price hikes due to global shortages and higher wholesale prices.

Things have gotten so bad that Nvidia seems to have shot itself in the foot, as reports have suggested it has had to cancel the upgraded RTX 5080 24GB graphics card because it's not cost-efficient to manufacture and distribute it.

The current RTX 5080 has 16GB of VRAM, and Nvidia was planning to release a more powerful version containing 24GB. This would have helped future-proof the graphics card as games become more graphically demanding. It has now, reportedly, been canceled.

The report comes from VideoCardz, which says the 5080 24GB was set to become China's flagship graphics card. However, news circulating on Chinese message boards has suggested this has been canned due to the cost of GDDR7 chips.

The current 16GB model uses eight 2GB GDDR7 chips; the proposed 24GB model would have used eight 3GB GDDR7 chips. The problem is that the 3GB chips cost roughly three times as much as their 2GB counterparts, and therefore, producing this new version of the RTX 5080 would have been completely impossible from an economic standpoint. VideoCardz linked the issue to delays impacting the GeForce RTX 5080 Super.

AMD also recently announced that it is "data center first," so alternatives to Nvidia are getting slimmer and slimmer.

It has been reported that Nvidia will "continue supplying GPUs paired with 2GB GDDR7 memory chips," allowing production partners to continue making the original 16GB model.

Now, neither the RTX 5080 24GB nor its cancellation has been officially confirmed by Nvidia; however, there is no smoke without fire, and VideoCardz was confident enough to report on the situation. It seems entirely plausible given the current cost of hardware components. It's just worth taking with a grain of salt.

I guess that, given their disdain towards their customers and the entire human race, Nvidia not being able to make more money from consumers isn't necessarily a bad thing.
