Brain Injury Creates Musical Genius; Long COVID Improvement; Dementia and Delirium

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

— News and commentary from the world of neurology and neuroscience

The New York Times detailed the story of Edward Freytag, whose traumatic brain injury made him a musical genius.

Bright Minds Biosciences said the FDA placed a partial clinical hold on its investigational drug BMB-101, which is being studied to treat seizures associated with developmental and epileptic encephalopathies, after identifying toxicity in mice.

And uniQure said the agency accepted its biologics license application for the company's Huntington's disease gene therapy candidate ifezuntirgene inilparvovec (AMT-130), with a decision expected next May.

Half of long COVID patients had meaningful symptom improvement within 3 years, prospective data from the RECOVER-Adult study showed. (Annals of Internal Medicine)

The global pooled cumulative incidence of infantile-onset epilepsies within the first 2 years of life was 236 per 100,000 live births, according to a systematic review and meta-analysis of 86 studies. (JAMA Neurology)

Reduced cerebellar white matter volume, thinner frontal gyri, and weakened cerebellar-frontal connectivity were seen in people with pulmonary hypertension but not in other heart or lung diseases, an imaging study found. (Journal of Clinical Investigation)

Fluoroquinolones carried no greater risk of peripheral neuropathy versus cephalosporins, an analysis of U.K. primary care records showed. (Neurology)

Delirium during hospitalization was strongly linked with dementia and mild cognitive impairment in subsequent years, a cohort study suggested. (JAMA Neurology)

NBC News producer Susan Kroll shared her personal journey with early Alzheimer's disease. (Today)

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission's lawsuit against Yale New Haven Hospital in Connecticut over cognitive testing of physicians 70 and older heads to trial after a federal judge denied summary judgment motions. (Becker's Physician Leadership)

Neuroscientist Gerald Fischbach, MD, former director of the NIH's National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke and founding director of the Simons Foundation Autism Research Initiative, died at age 87.
