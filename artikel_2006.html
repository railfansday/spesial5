5 Chris Cohen & Tom Shaw replacements Lincoln City must eye ASAP

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Lincoln City have confirmed the sacking of dual head coaches Chris Cohen and Tom Shaw following a disastrous run of form, with the pair having only succeeded Michael Skubala over the summer.

The Imps elected to promote internally upon Skubala's bombshell departure to Championship rivals Bristol City after guiding the side to the second-tier for the first time since 1961 by romping to the League One title, which left their key decision-makers to opt against hiring an external candidate.

Instead, Lincoln placed faith in the abilities of Chris Cohen and Tom Shaw as the duo played vital roles in the club's success under Skubala.

They would, however, ultimately fall short of continuing the Imps' upwards direction of travel, and have been relieved of their duties after a losing streak was extended to five matches by falling to a chastening 4-0 defeat at the hands of Charlton Athletic on Tuesday evening.

Lincoln have won just four of their opening 17 matches in the Championship and are positioned merely three points above 22nd-placed Preston North End, who themselves are on the rise following a three-match unbeaten streak, and with eight losses from the last ten, the second-tier newcomers simply had to make a change in order to retain prospects of achieving survival.

With Lincoln having already confirmed the initiation of a recruitment process to seek out a replacement, Football League World has decided to take a close look at FIVE candidates that the strugglers should be setting their sights on in the coming days.

Heading proceedings underway is Matt Bloomfield, who wouldn't represent the most pulse-racing or wildcard appointment in the world but could just be worth a punt from the Imps' key decision-makers.

His managerial career has been something of a rollercoaster already, with the former midfielder initially shining in charge of Wycombe Wanderers and mounting a shock title race alongside Birmingham City before leaving for Luton Town, suffering relegation from the Championship and then being dismissed out of Kenilworth Road months later.

Clearly, then, it was an ill-fated decision to leave Wycombe in the first place, and it's one which Bloomfield is perhaps yet to recover from. He wasn't able to keep Oxford United afloat after succeeding Gary Rowett either, and while the appointment of Aaron Ramsey has since proven an extremely-shrewd one, Bloomfield's sacking still felt harsh back in the summer.

Bloomfield's best work, of course, came at Wycombe, where relatively-modest expectations and financial resources during much of his tenure clearly worked in his favour. It would be rather similar with Lincoln, although the Imps' situation could also be comparable to Oxford's and the fact that he failed to keep the Yellows alive in the second-tier is an obvious caveat.

Still, Bloomfield is just 43 and has time on his side to turn his stock around - and Lincoln could do much worse than providing him that opportunity.

A much more high-risk, high-reward appointment, meanwhile, could come in the form of Calum McFarlane, who was tenuously linked with the top job at Sincil Bank over the summer.

Whether there was anything at all in those links is yesterday's news. Now, with Lincoln on the search for a new boss once again, McFarlane is somebody who should be coming under serious consideration, and he would fit the club's head-coach model much more seamlessly than other candidates.

The highly-rated Chelsea coach is currently working as Xabi Alonso's assistant after taking interim charge at Stamford Bridge during the back-end of the previous season following Liam Rosenior's departure, and has built up an encouraging reputation in West London.

Calm under pressure, progressive in his methods and experienced in coaching young talent from his days leading Chelsea's academy sides, McFarlane's stock is on the rise and Lincoln should be testing the waters to see whether a first senior, full-time gig could tempt the 41-year-old away from the Premier League giants.

These hires typically go one of two ways, but the potential upside is clear to see.

Lincoln have rarely been ones to shy away from left-field hires in the dugout, having done so with both Cohen and Shaw and the aforementioned Skubala, yet they shouldn't dismiss the possibility of taking that one step further with a more continental approach to mirror an overarching part of their player recruitment strategy.

Wilfried Nancy has never coached in England, and the Frenchman's stock has taken a notable hit following his disastrous short-lived reign at Celtic nearly a year ago. He won just two of his eight games in charge of the Hoops and departed under a cloud with the worst record of any boss on either side of the Old Firm divide, yet that alone should not completely overlook a fantastic of body of work in the MLS before jetting off across the Atlantic.

Nancy initially started off in senior coaching by succeeding Thierry Henry at CF Montreal, and finished his time with the Canada-based outfit with the second-best record in the Eastern Conference back in 2022. He went one better after joining Columbus Crew, where he would win the 2023 MLS Cup before winning the MLS Coach of the Year while earning wide plaudits for an attractive, possession-heavy brand of football.

Known for playing a 3-4-2-1 shape with interchanging roles and an emphasis on dominating the ball with attacking overloads and a high tempo both in and out of possession, Nancy follows the ilk of many modern-day head coaches by unequivocally sticking to his principles.

Many Celtic fans felt as though that was his downfall at Parkhead, whereas there would have to be questions as to whether he'd even be able to get Lincoln players to carry out such tactical and technical demands.

Nonetheless, Nancy is one of the more gifted and high-ceiling coaches that Lincoln could hire - even if it would be a wildcard call of the high-risk, high-reward genre.

Bo Henriksen's style, meanwhile, would be much more suitable for what remains a primarily pragmatic, dynamic and aggressive Imps side, yet his appointment undoubtedly represents a more ambitious call.

The Danish coach, who spent three surprising years in English football with Kidderminster Harriers and Bristol Rovers during a largely-unremarkable playing career, has earned an impressive reputation on the continent, and it's one that could just put him out of Lincoln's reach.

That's not to say that Lincoln shouldn't be asking the question, though, as Henriksen would be a superb appointment. He started off with FC Midtjylland before taking Zurich from the foot of the league table after ten games to a mid-table finish, and then earned a move to Mainz 05 as a result of his work in Switzerland.

Mainz enjoyed a rich run of form under Henriksen, who led them from relegation trouble to 13th place with a nine-match unbeaten streak at the end of the 2023/24 term. They went even better the following season and left fans across Germany shocked by reaching third place in late March, before losing their footing and settling for a UEFA Europa Conference League spot instead.

It was a strong season regardless, yet Henriksen would fall short of matching those standards and received his marching orders in December of last year following one win from his opening 12 games. Interestingly, he has been out of work ever since and Lincoln should be pulling out all the stops to seal his return to management.

Unlike Nancy, for example, Henriksen is not in the modern-day possession-heavy coaching mold and instead focuses on a high-energy approach involving intense pressing out of possession and quick transitions. He's known as something of a culture-builder, too, often instilling belief, fight and energy into struggling squads low on confidence, with those attributes earmarking him as exactly what Lincoln need right now.

Of all the listed candidates, he could just be the best pick of the lot.

The fifth and final boss who should be coming under Lincoln's consideration is Jimmy Thelin, who has been unattached since his sacking from Aberdeen in January of this year.

Thelin enjoyed success leading Elfsborg in his native Sweden between 2018 and 2024, and 2023 was a particularly notable year as he won the Allsvenskan Coach of the Year after guiding his side to an impressive third-placed finish.

That form put him on the radar of Aberdeen, who made a remarkable start under his stewardship in 2024/25 by winning 10 of his opening 11 games in charge. They did, however, take only five wins from the remaining 27 - yet still managed to win their first trophy in eleven years by beating Celtic in the Scottish Cup final.
