XRP is trading near $0.92–$0.97 in mid-November 2026 as Bitcoin slides toward $64,000, and the broader crypto market absorbs another wave of heavy long liquidations. With this bearish price action gripping the market, Elon Musk’s Grok AI predicts a historic XRP crash that would rock the industry.

In an over-the-top bearish scenario, the token could face a far more severe outcome by January 1, 2027. The landmark catalyst driving this extreme downside is a highly unlikely but not impossible regulatory and institutional collapse.

In this hypothetical, coordinated international regulatory action, led by major jurisdictions and triggered by concerns over residual legal overhang, payment-network concentration risk, and alleged non-compliance issues, leads several large banks and payment providers to abruptly suspend or terminate XRP-related corridors.

At the same time, a high-profile technical or operational incident on the XRP Ledger (or a major related infrastructure partner) erodes remaining institutional confidence.

Combined with the current risk-off environment of cascading liquidations and Bitcoin’s drop into the low $60,000s, capital flees XRP faster than most other large-cap alts, turning a market-wide correction into a prolonged, asset-specific capitulation.

XRP has already broken down from its recent consolidation range. After failing to hold above $1.00–$1.05, price has sliced through successive support levels and is now testing the $0.90 zone, with the next significant demand area sitting closer to $0.80–$0.85.

Momentum indicators have turned sharply lower, volume has expanded on the sell-off, and the structure shows a clear series of lower highs and lower lows. In a standard bearish continuation, a sustained break below $0.85–$0.90 would open the door to a retest of the $0.60–$0.70 region.

Under the extreme regulatory-and-confidence-shock scenario outlined above, those levels would offer little lasting support, allowing the decline to accelerate into multi-year lows as residual holders capitulate and liquidity thins.

While current price action already reflects broad market stress, only an extraordinary negative catalyst layered on top of the existing liquidation cascade would justify the projected downside depth.
