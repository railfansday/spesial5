Memory prices continue to be a problem, especially for cheaper phone sales.

RAM prices are continuing to put the squeeze on smartphone sales, and even people who typically buy less expensive phones appear to be holding off on upgrading.

According to a new report from Counterpoint Research, smartphone sales in the third quarter of 2026 fell by 9% compared to the same period in 2025. The report attributed the drop to rising memory costs, which are hurting sales at the low end of the Android phone market.

In terms of market share, Samsung held steady at around 25%, while Motorola, Google and other companies saw their slice of the pie decline. Where did those sales go, you might be wondering? Unsurprisingly, Apple’s broad range of iPhone models — from the 17E to its newest foldable flagship, the $1,999 iPhone Duo — bumped the company’s share of the smartphone market from 50% to 57% since last year.

Smartphone-makers with cheaper phone lineups suffered due to component costs, particularly memory, eating into their margins. “There, OEMs have little room to absorb a higher bill of materials without raising prices or cutting volume,” said Counterpoint Senior Analyst Emily Herbert.

The slowdown in phone sales has been happening all year. In the last quarter, the market slumped by 20%, more than was expected at the time. The build-out of data centers to support the ever-expanding AI industry has led to shortages of memory components. In turn, the prices of those components have spiked, raising prices of not just smartphones, but game consoles, PCs and other electronics.

Black Friday, Cyber Monday and the Christmas gift-giving season typically mean strong phone sales for companies like Apple and Samsung. But the memory component crisis isn’t going to go away that quickly, and prices of phones are still higher overall.

Counterpoint predicts that smartphone sales will remain weak in the next quarter and that US carriers may be more selective about the promotions they offer this year. AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon are also seeing declining sales and customers are getting choosier about committing to phones that often cost more than $1,000. People are also looking to second-hand and refurbished phones instead of buying new.

“Consumers are timing their purchases around the best deals more than ever, if they buy at all,” said Counterpoint Senior Analyst Maurice Klaehne. “This year’s holiday sales cycle will look quite different from usual.”
