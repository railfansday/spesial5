Outgoing US men’s national team boss Mauricio Pochettino has urged Pep Guardiola to take the job, amid widespread speculation that the Spaniard is the United States Soccer Federation’s main target.

Guardiola has taken a one-year sabbatical after ending a 10-year stint at Manchester City in the summer, while Pochettino has accepted the head coach role at Manchester United, replacing the sacked Michael Carrick.

The 54-year-old is overseeing the US’ Nations League quarter-final against Haiti, with his final match in charge coming in the second leg on Tuesday. The US won the first leg 6-0 at the weekend.

On Monday Pochettino urged Guardiola to take the job if he is offered it, although formal contact between him and the United States Soccer Federation has not been made.

Pochettino told reporters: “It's the perfect moment to arrive to the [US Soccer] Federation. Everything is in the right direction.

“I think, of course, take [it]. Take it because he's going to find really good people, good players. The player can fit for his style; the National Training Centre that wasn’t [there] before.”

The Argentine spent two years in charge of the US team and led them to the round of 16 at this summer’s World Cup.

He suggested that the fact that he was scouted for the Manchester United role indicated that managing the US team had increased its prestige and profile.

He said: “Now the vision from Europe, I promise, is completely different after the World Cup. Being head coach in USA now is prestigious.

“It's Manchester United, the calibre of [Real] Madrid, Barcelona. Manchester United is coming for the coach of the USA.

“If you start to think that if you are another coach, maybe you are not working - [Antonio] Conte, or I don't know - you say, ‘Oh, it's a great opportunity because it's not only the value of being coach here. If Pochettino from Chelsea comes here, [then goes to] Manchester United.’ The vision is completely different.”

He will travel to Old Trafford after Tuesday’s game, with his first match as Manchester United boss a trip to Liverpool in the Premier League next Sunday.
