Harry Kane explains why Spain ‘couldn’t live with’ England after stunning win in Madrid

Harry Kane believes England’s victory in Spain is "as big as it gets" away from major tournaments, proving that even the best sides cannot cope with the assertive style developed since their World Cup near miss.

An intense year concluded in striking fashion on Sunday evening in Madrid, where the reigning world and European champions were outplayed and outfought by Thomas Tuchel’s hungry players.

Kane’s penalty earned England a deserved half-time lead, but they pressed on rather than beating a retreat as they had done during their costly World Cup semi-final collapse against Argentina.

Ezri Konsa and Ben White extended the lead, and Kane is confident more goals would have followed were it not for Elliot Anderson’s sending off, which allowed Mikel Oyarzabal and Nico Williams to reduce the deficit.

Spain pushed until the very end, but England demonstrated the resolve seen in July’s 3-2 last-16 win in Mexico, triumphing by the same scoreline to top their Nations League group in memorable fashion.

“I think that’s as big as it gets outside of a major tournament, for sure,” England captain Kane said in a post-interview area every bit as chaotic as the match.

“We spoke before the Nations League campaign that we want to use these games to really improve, really stamp our identity on how we want to move going forward.

“No better game than Spain away after they’re unbeaten in 40-odd games to show that, and what a performance. Up until the red card it was dominant, clinical, high intensity and they couldn’t live with it.

“It was a shame the red card happened because I think it could have been more and that’s been our mindset. That it doesn’t matter what the scoreline is – we go and we go and we go. You saw that out there tonight.

“In the end, we had to find a different way to win with the red card, obviously, and it was similar to a Mexico feeling where we’re digging deep.

“But when you can do both sides of the game like that it means you’re a very good team, so credit to everyone involved. It’s been a great campaign so far and we can end the year with our heads held high.”

Kane loved England’s dominance against “one of the best teams in recent history”, which only underlined his belief that nobody can live with them “when we make it at Premier League pace, intensity”.

The Ballon d’Or winner says the fact Spain could not handle that tempo should give them confidence in an approach that has developed during post-World Cup discussions.

The Argentina surrender has naturally been among the key topics and Kane told BBC Radio 5 Live: “We’ve spoke a lot since the World Cup about if we’re in a situation like that again, where maybe we’re 1-0 up what do we want to be?

“Do we want to be a team that sits back, or do we want to be a team that goes after two, goes after three? And that’s the team that we spoke about and it’s a perfect chance to show that and we did.

“It was really enjoyable to be a part of because it’s a feeling that maybe in an England team we haven’t had so much, especially against the top teams.”
