The moment that galvanised England to deliver major statement against Spain

Spain vs England was a game that had almost everything, and may yet mean more, perhaps yet the most significant moment might have come before it had even started.

The hosts took the opportunity of a first match back in Madrid since the World Cup to showcase the trophy, in what almost felt another homecoming.

The mood was very much of revelling in their embossed status, something that would have had a particular edge for this specific nation.

No country that has won the World Cup has waited as long for another trophy as England.

And, as the 2010 winners Iker Casillas, Fernando Torres and Fernando Llorente presented Unai Simon that with that precious piece of brilliant gold, it was almost literally in England’s faces.

Thomas Tuchel’s side had to watch the goalkeeper wave the World Cup about right in front of them.

It had been noted on the Spanish side that Jude Bellingham was the only player who clapped, this now being the city that he calls home with Real Madrid. He then played like he was demanding the same appreciation, and he was not the only one.

As one figure with knowledge of the England camp said: “They will have been thinking ‘that’s what we want’.”

Seeing the World Cup so close and yet so far, with more than three years until the next edition, only deepened a mood that had been felt in the build-up to this game.

England, in the words of both Declan Rice and Thomas Tuchel, knew they were going to win.

“I could feel the true belief in the dressing room before the match that this is exactly the moment where we want to prove ourselves and push our limits,” the manager said.

They had that conviction to show what they were really about, and to show they would be the only team in the world not cowed by Spain. It was that mentality that comes when you feel ready to beat a rival that has long had it over you; that has been a benchmark.

A pace was set just moments after seeing that trophy, as a ferocious England subjected Simon to a flurry.

Just as importantly, they displayed a new level of their own, even beyond Tuchel describing it as the best performance of his time.

This wasn’t, after all, the usual counter-attacking approach to playing Spain - at least not for when England actually got so far ahead.

It was England imposing their game, an evolving ideology, on Spain.

“We are well aware which steps we took after the World Cup,” Tuchel said, conspicuously repeating this more than once.

The manager was deeply stung after all the criticism following the Argentina defeat.

And yet, much as he might bristle about it in public, it does feel as if some of that criticism has been acknowledged.

England look much more technical, but still with pace.

Throughout the World Cup, after all, there was a definite sense of his team just seeking to force their way through.

This was something much more considered and coherent.

“We wanted to get better in the deep build-up, we wanted to get better at defending in a low block and on top of it, we wanted always to impose our identity and the way we want to play on every opponent,” Tuchel remarked.

This similarly feeds into another theme of the night, which was how James Trafford’s performance may start a path that sees him become England’s No 1.

It was conspicuous that, when asked about the goalkeeper’s brilliant late double-save, Tuchel immediately pointed to his pure football effect.

“But there was a lot to do in the deep build-up as well, to overcome the first line of press, which he did very well with everyone around him,” he said. “So there was a lot to do, never lose the concentration, never lose the focus, never lose the vision, to find the free man to overcome the press and put the game into the opponent’s half.”

Tuchel clearly wants to change the very focus of this team.

And, in doing so, he wants to change its entire outlook.

There was naturally a lot of talk about “confidence” after a victory like this, which Tuchel was only too happy to immerse himself in.

“You want confidence, not only through results but confidence through performance and the result follows,” Tuchel said. “That’s the ideal process and that’s what we got.”

When Spain beat France in the World Cup semi-final with what was the performance of the tournament - “the statement performance” as Tuchel would have it - some close to Didier Deschamps’s squad felt the reason for the win had actually come much earlier.

Spain had beaten France in a series of high-profile games, above all the Euro 2024 and World Cup 2026 semi-finals. Lamine Yamal had been right to emphasise that, because he knew it played on French minds.

It gave Spain an edge. They knew they could beat France, but France were unsure if they could beat Spain.

That’s also why the frantic ending to this game in Madrid, so at odds with the first hour, may have been so important.

Had Spain equalised, or potentially won, England would have just been another team to fail to beat them. It could similarly have been the deepening of England’s own complex about Spain, since this would have been three high-profile set-backs in succession.

Even if this had just been a draw, after all, it would have felt like a defeat given it was a 3-0 lead.

It was a win, and a first defeat that Spain have endured in 44 games over two and a half years.

As such, England are not just another team the Spanish are reigning over.

They’re a team that know how to beat them, that can carry that through to a home Euros.

It might even create a bit of consideration on the Spanish side, even allowing for how assured they are in their own approach.

It might be the beginning of a proper modern rivalry.

Might, of course. England have to go and use this to do what Spain have made customary, and actually win one of those trophies.
