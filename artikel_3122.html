Report: Apple gains ground as Latin American smartphone shipments plunge 24% in Q3

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

According to a new Counterpoint Research report, Apple increased its share of Latin America’s smartphone shipments in Q3 2026, recording the smallest decline among the region’s top five brands. Here are the details.

In a report released this week, Counterpoint Research says the ongoing memory crunch led to a 24% year-over-year drop in smartphone shipments during Q3, “the second-lowest quarter since 2015.”

Per the report, Samsung maintained its lead with a 35% shipment share, despite a 15% year-over-year decline in total shipments, well ahead of second-place Motorola, which captured 18% of shipments after a 23% drop. Interestingly, Motorola’s share actually increased one percentage point from a year earlier.

Rounding out the top five were Xiaomi, with a 14% share and a whopping 34% decline, Apple, with a 9% share and a 10% decline, and Honor, with an 8% share and a 27% decline.

Despite the decline in overall shipments, Apple’s shipment share increased by 2 percentage points from 7% a year earlier, moving the company from fifth to fourth place in the region.

Other brands collectively accounted for 16% of shipments, down from 19% a year earlier, with their combined shipments falling 37% year over year.

Here’s Counterpoint on Apple’s performance for Q3 in Latin America:

Apple was the most resilient, with the smallest decline among the top five brands (shipments down 10% YoY), and rose one position to No. 4 in the region. Apple absorbed the impact of price increases better than its rivals, helped by strong product availability. Apple launched the iPhone 18 Pro series at the end of the quarter. For the first time, Chile joined Brazil and Mexico in the first launch wave.

As for regional factors that contributed to the decline, Senior Analyst Benjamin Corona noted that Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia faced additional challenges beyond the global memory shortage, ranging from political uncertainty to inflation and tighter household budgets. Smartphone shipments fell 22%, 26%, and 28% in those countries, respectively.

Looking ahead, Counterpoint says it “expects the decline to ease in Q4 2026 as prices stabilize and seasonal demand returns,” adding that “memory-related price pressure should moderate by Q2 2027, but supply constraints will likely continue through the end of 2027.”
