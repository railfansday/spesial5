Which of These 5 Cannabis Stocks Has the Strongest Balance Sheet?

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Debt and cash flow reveal some significant differences.

Tilray Brands (TLRY +3.91%), Canopy Growth (CGC -3.58%), Cronos Group (CRON -2.96%), SNDL (SNDL +2.54%), and Aurora Cannabis (ACB +0.96%) have all had their share of financial challenges. But some are in much better shape than others. In an industry where marijuana companies have spent years burning through cash, issuing new shares, and struggling to turn a profit, a strong balance sheet matters.

So which of these five cannabis stocks is in the best financial position? Let's look at the numbers.

Tilray reported approximately $221.4 million in cash, restricted cash, and marketable securities at the end of first-quarter 2027. It also had $376.9 million in working capital.

The company has been cleaning up its balance sheet, too. In its first fiscal quarter, Tilray reduced its long-term convertible debt by about $79.5 million. Indeed, this was encouraging news, but the company still carries roughly $118 million in long-term debt. While quarterly revenue jumped 23% to $257.1 million, Tilray still reported a $40 million net loss and negative free cash flow of $27.4 million.

The company does have plenty of opportunities across cannabis, beverages, and pharmaceutical distribution. But until it can consistently generate positive cash flow, its balance sheet remains vulnerable.

Canopy Growth has spent years trying to get its finances under control. As of June 30, 2026, the company had $241.4 million in cash and cash equivalents, down from $261.6 million just three months earlier. It also carried approximately $172.3 million in debt. Granted, there has been some improvement. Fiscal first-quarter revenue increased 13% to $58.2 million, while its net loss narrowed to $10.5 million from $32.2 million a year earlier.

To be sure, Canopy isn't facing an immediate cash crisis. But with that much debt still on the books, it has less financial flexibility than some of its competitors.

As of Sept. 30, 2026, SNDL reported $440.1 million in unrestricted cash, marketable securities, and investments, including investments in other businesses. That's a significant financial cushion. Also worth noting: SNDL generated a record $14.2 million in free cash flow in the third quarter, up 19.5% from a year earlier. Adjusted operating income also turned positive at $3.7 million.Those are definitely encouraging numbers, but there are also some weaknesses.

Quarterly revenue declined 2.5%, and SNDL recorded a $37 million noncash goodwill impairment. Nevertheless, SNDL is debt-free, and its improving cash flow makes it one of the industry's financially stronger companies.

At the end of June 2026, the company reported $49.4 million in cash and $21.9 million in short-term investments. It also held $35 million in restricted cash while working capital stood at approximately $228.2 million.That's a reasonable cushion, particularly for a company that's been focusing more heavily on medical cannabis and international markets. But Aurora's latest quarterly results weren't particularly impressive.

Revenue declined to $48.2 million from $52.8 million a year earlier. Adjusted EBITDA remained positive at $2.5 million, but free cash flow was negative $4.1 million. Don't get me wrong. Aurora isn't in terrible financial shape, but its readily available cash reserves are considerably smaller than those of Cronos.

Cronos Group still has the strongest balance sheet of these five cannabis companies. As of Sept. 30, 2026, the company reported approximately $841 million in combined cash, restricted cash, short-term investments, and longer-term interest-bearing deposits, with no significant debt burden. That's up from approximately $827 million at the end of June, giving Cronos a lot of financial flexibility to expand its operations, pursue acquisitions, and weather challenges in the cannabis industry.

Its operating performance is also improving. According to its Nov. 5 earnings release, Q3 revenue jumped 75% year over year to $63.5 million, while adjusted EBITDA surged 201% to $17.1 million. Gross profit also increased 76% to $32.3 million, reflecting stronger cannabis sales in Canada and international markets, particularly Germany.

Of these five cannabis companies, Cronos Group has the strongest balance sheet. Its approximately $841 million in cash and interest-bearing investments, limited debt exposure, and improving operating performance put it ahead of the competition.

SNDL comes in a close second, thanks to its debt-free balance sheet and positive free cash flow. Tilray and Canopy still have work to do, and Aurora has a respectable but smaller liquidity cushion.

Now, does that mean Cronos is automatically the best cannabis stock to buy? Not necessarily. A strong balance sheet doesn't guarantee strong revenue growth, profitability, or attractive shareholder returns. But it does give a company more room to maneuver when business conditions deteriorate. Considering how much money cannabis companies have burned through over the years, that's an advantage I wouldn't underestimate.
