United Airlines Has Warned Air Traffic Control Isn’t Ready for Air Taxis. That’s a Bigger Problem for Archer Than Its Cash Burn.

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The recent air delays don't have anything to do with Archer Aviation, but they could mean a more delayed regulatory process for the eVTOL maker.

By James Halley – Nov 22, 2026 at 3:05PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Archer Aviation ›

It has been a rough couple of weeks for air travel and for airlines, especially for United Airlines (UAL -4.26%). The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) imposed a nationwide ground stop on Friday, leading to delays and cancellations after a newly installed router malfunctioned. Then, United had its own issues on Monday, with a 90-minute system outage that caused delays.

While this wasn't good news for United and other airline stocks, the technical difficulties in air traffic are a real problem for Archer Aviation (ACHR -9.06%) and other companies focusing on air taxis, commonly known as electric vertical take-off and landing (eVTOL) aircraft.

Last spring, United CEO Scott Kirby expressed concerns about whether the nation's air traffic control system was ready for eVTOLs, and the recent ground stop led him to say the FAA needs to invest more in updating its technology.

Neither the FAA's nor United's technical problems reflect a flaw in Archer's Midnight aircraft itself, nor do they threaten Archer's cash runway or manufacturing ramp-up.

EVTOL commercialization is heavily dependent on ecosystem readiness, certified airspace rules from the FAA, and seamless hub-and-app execution from United and other airlines. The recent tech issues raise the risk of delays in Archer's transition from flight testing to full-scale commercial passenger service in the U.S.

Archer's shares are down more than 52% so far this year. Investors have been concerned about the company's massive losses and high cash burn. In the third quarter, the company had $5.1 million in revenue, which was progress, because it had no revenue in the same period a year ago. However, it also lost $274.6 million, more than double the $129.9 million it lost in the third quarter of 2025.

The company's spending is ramping up as it begins production at its manufacturing facility in Covington, Georgia. To start generating more revenue, the company depends on FAA authorization, and the agency's issues hurt Archer.

Archer's Midnight eVTOL aircraft relies on the FAA for Type certification and National Airspace System (NAS) integration; any internal technical bottlenecks at the regulator trickle down to Archer.

The FAA's issues with aging IT infrastructure, staffing shortages, and system outages may lead it to divert engineering and IT resources toward stabilizing legacy commercial aviation systems and bandwidth for emerging technologies, and put eVTOL operational rules and reviews of Archer's final Phase 4 Type Certification flight test data on the back burner.

Archer's business model depends on high-frequency, short-haul urban air-taxi routes, such as those from Manhattan to Newark. Integrating autonomous or semiautomated eVTOLs into congested Class B airspace requires modernized FAA data-sharing and traffic management software. Delays in the FAA's NextGen and digital traffic-management upgrades limit how quickly Archer can scale beyond initial, low-volume visual flight rules (VFR) corridors.

If nothing else, the high-profile technical failures could make regulators more risk-averse, leading to stricter software validation and redundancy requirements for new aircraft entrants.

Archer continues to ramp up flight testing of its Midnight eVTOLs. The technology makes sense, particularly in crowded urban markets. However, the company is entering uncharted airspace, so to speak, and the FAA certification process for eVTOL aircraft is complex and unprecedented. Although progress continues, timeline delays stretch out the horizon before commercial passenger routes can officially launch.

Each delayed phase lengthens the window during which Archer must burn through hundreds of millions of dollars without commercial passenger inflows. That makes Archer a risky stock for most investors.
