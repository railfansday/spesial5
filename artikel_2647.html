Mathew Barzal’s costly decision highlights growing pains of Islanders return

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

When Maple Leafs defenseman Darren Raddysh wound up for a shot from the point Monday, with the Islanders and Maple Leafs tied midway through the third period, Mathew Barzal — the closest player to Raddysh — jumped away from it.

He allowed Raddysh to have a clear lane, which, moments later, allowed for Gavin McKenna to deposit the game-winning goal off an Ilya Sorokin rebound in the Islanders loss.

And one day later, after his fifth game back following a grueling journey through knee inflammation, Barzal owned up to it.

Mathew Barzal skates up ice during the Islanders' loss to the Maple Leafs on Nov. 23, 2026 at UBS Arena. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

The context, of course, is that a Raddysh shot in February 2025 broke Barzal's left kneecap and caused the lingering issue in the first place. When asked, Barzal didn't outright say that the history with Raddysh, or the knee, played a role in moving away from the shot, but on a night when he scored his first goal of the year and continued to flash his vintage form, it served as one of the lasting images — and one of the growing pains since returning.

"I mean, yeah, I mean, I don't know if it was necessarily [Raddysh playing a role]. I think it's just five games in, I mean, see a guy winding up, my first instinct is," Barzal said as he trailed off. "I probably should've got in front of it."

The consequence of the shot ended up being McKenna's goal, which served as one of four unanswered tallies from the Maple Leafs as the Islanders' seven-game point streak ended. Barzal took a pair of shots, added a hit and blocked another shot, but his inability to record his third block of the season loomed large.

Their loss also contained a reminder of the fragility of a healthy spot in the lineup when Kyle Palmieri — who tore his left ACL and missed most of last season — exited early and wasn't able to put any weight on his right leg, and Barzal, along with others on the Islanders, can relate to that delicate chance.

On the day he returned to the lineup, Barzal called the last six months "probably the hardest journey of my life." He fought through knee inflammation that forced him to miss the final 31 games of 2024-25 after the initial Raddysh shot and the first 18 games of this year. Barzal managed to play 81 games last season and finished with 19 goals and 72 points, but his knee continuing to act up in the offseason kept him sidelined the first month-plus of the Islanders' current campaign.

Since he returned Nov. 14, his minutes have gradually built up, and there have been continued signs of normalcy. He danced through a pair of Maple Leafs and ripped a shot past Anthony Stolarz in the second period Monday, marking his first goal of the season after previously hitting the post twice. Barzal has already collected seven high-danger scoring chances through his five games too.

Even Barzal skating on a line with captain Bo Horvat at practice Tuesday — which head coach Pete DeBoer said not to read into because the Islanders were missing pieces — served as a flashback, when the Islanders' two most talented forwards were stacked on a line together.

"It felt nice," Barzal said of his tally against Toronto. "I hit some posts in the last few games, was starting to not grip it a little bit tighter but more just keep shooting, just trust it. I just kept trusting it, and yeah, fortunate to get one."

But this year will be about the give and the take for Barzal, where every move and outcome will be scrutinized through the lens of his knee. And it just so happened that the sequence in the third period Monday, when Barzal moved away from the Raddysh shot, emerged as a perfect storm within a five-game return that has been about as perfect as the Islanders could script.

"He's had so many chances game after game," Horvat said of Barzal finally scoring, "and to finally get rewarded, I'm sure it feels great for him — getting his confidence going and his scoring ability."
