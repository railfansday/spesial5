Skydance is planning at least $6 billion in cost cuts after Warner Bros. deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The company expects the savings within three years as it combines Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery operations

Skydance laid out a plan to cut at least $6 billion in costs within three years after closing its acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery. The company described the plan in a letter to shareholders on Monday, which accompanied a Form 8-K filing.

The company said new leadership teams are in place and integration work is under way. It expects the savings to come from merging duplicate streaming platforms, studio units, television networks and corporate departments, all within a cost structure that exceeds $50 billion, according to CNBC.

Skydance projects savings of $2.2 billion or more from trimming administrative and corporate overhead, such as layers of management, heavier reliance on artificial intelligence and a smaller office footprint. Streamlined marketing and promotional spending would account for at least $1 billion, and unifying streaming and broadcast technology for about $1.4 billion. A further $1.4 billion would come from consolidating third-party spending and purchasing under one group.

Skydance pointed to the Paramount $PARA cost program as a model. That program is expected to produce more than $2.7 billion in annualized efficiency gains before 2026 ends, Skydance said.

Some reductions have started. Skydance began offering voluntary buyouts last month ahead of layoffs and adopted a five-day in-office policy. Eight executives, four from Warner Bros. and four from Paramount, left last week.

Skydance reported third-quarter results for Paramount Skydance, covering the period before the Warner Bros. Discovery deal closed. Revenue rose 5% to $7.03 billion, and net income was $73 million, compared with a loss of $257 million a year earlier. The company said it will give full-year guidance when it reports fourth-quarter results.

Skydance completed its acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery on Oct. 6, and the company said then that it was targeting at least $6 billion in run-rate synergies within three years. The combined company trades on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker "SKYD."

The letter repeats a medium-term outlook for 2030 of mid-single-digit annualized revenue growth, mid-20% adjusted EBITDA margins and more than $10 billion in free cash flow. Skydance also plans to offer a Paramount+ and HBO Max bundle early next year, as a step toward combining the two services.
