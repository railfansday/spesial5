Selfie-seekers bring Man Utd vs Leeds match to premature end

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Aren’t friendlies supposed to be non-events? Manchester United against Leeds United in Dublin always had the capacity to be lively, but try explaining the half hour that Bruno Fernandes experienced in one breath. It would be impossible.

There was only one player the vast majority of the crowd wanted to see in the grandiosely named Boyle Sports Cup and, in chronological order from his 60th minute introduction, he received a standing ovation, hit the bar, had a goal disallowed, missed a penalty in a shoot-out and lifted a trophy.

Most remarkably – and, certainly, worryingly – of all, he was central to the game being ended early as five young boys, one after the other, breached security and ran to him in the middle of pitch, all of them successfully posing for photos with the Portuguese.

You could say it was harmless fun but it should not have happened and the flummoxed faces that were pulled on the Manchester United bench, as an army in high-visibility jackets stood and watched it all unfold, suggested they took a dim view.

It was a shame because in that cameo, Fernandes showed he has returned from the World Cup in excellent shape, the tempo of United’s play immediately lifting when he had the ball. It is wrong to make conclusions in the summer, but you are on safe ground to state he will be integral to their ambitions.

Croke Park is a stadium steeped in history and another chapter was penned here, as the capacity crowd made this the highest attended soccer match in Ireland with 82,000 coming through the gates: it is a magnificent venue, one that would be well capable of hosting a Champions League final.

Michael Carrick hopes in years to come that is the stage where his team will be but, right now, it is a case of learning to walk again before they can run and he more than anyone will know how far off that target is, not that it will stop him trying to get there.

The one thing that is obvious about the way United play under him is the first thought from everyone is to go forward quickly. Yes, this was only a friendly, but the ambition to be on the front foot and cause problems was evident from the start.

The team in red had much the better of the opening exchanges, with Diogo Dalot having the first shot that went over but, unlike on July 25, when Mayo beat Kerry here in Gaelic football’s All Ireland final, which went viral, nobody was cheering the sight of a ball clearing the bar.

It was like a home game for United and the majority got what they wanted in the 15th minute when a slick counter-attack ended with Bryan Mbuemo sliding a pass into Joshua Zikzee, who rifled past James Trafford with a first-time finish.

Zirkzee is unlikely to be part of Carrick’s squad beyond the transfer window, with interest from clubs in Italy, and it would be disingenuous to suggest his career at Old Trafford has been successful, but his attitude was exemplary and he played as if he was desperate to change his manager’s mind.

There may have been an anticipation that United would stride clear, but this game was also about Leeds, whose summer has been encouraging and whose confidence from staying up last season has evidently enabled them to blossom.

You would be way off the mark if you include them in your conversations about which clubs are going to be relegated next May, and the sense they showed to stay with Daniel Farke has paid off. They have bought some good players, not least goalkeeper James Trafford and Harry Wilson.

Trafford has done the right thing getting away from Manchester City and you could argue that he is already reaping the benefit of playing for a manager who wants him, as he made a fabulous save in the 23rd minute to thwart Mbuemo. It was all part of performance that led to him being named player of the match.

He was involved in the move that brought the Leeds equaliser, too, as his long clearance caused uncertainty and led to Tyler Fletcher being pickpocketed by Dan James and he nudged a pass into Brenden Aaronson, who kept his cool and danced into the area, ignoring Senne Lammens, to finish.

It was an entertaining affair, mercifully lacking the bile that would usually bubble away when it is staged in England, and the longer the game advanced, the better Leeds looked. They finished the opening period strongly and came back after the restart, with 10 changes, in the same fettle.

Farke, whose team had shown their ability to score heavily when they pummelled Liverpool 4-2 in Chicago 10 days ago, would have taken plenty of encouragement, but the perfectionist in him would have cursed the opportunities that were squandered early in the second period.

The misses proved costly as the game meandered and the shoot-out went United’s way, after Wilson and James Justin failed from 12 yards, with Noussair Mazraoui thumping in the decisive kick. It really is never dull.

Good evening and welcome to live coverage of the pre-season friendly between Manchester United and Leeds United at a sold-out Croke Park in Dublin. Last year Leeds played Milan in the Irish capital and drew a crowd of 52,000 but at the GAA citadel, as I think I’m contractually obliged to call it, there will be 83,000 present, a testament to the Premier League’s reach and these two clubs’ popularity in Ireland founded on the exploits of Billy Whelan, Noel Cantwell, Johnny Giles, Roy Keane, Gary Kelly et al. It’s the first club match ever held at the stadium.

It’s a fixture that always stirs the blood and whenever it comes around you can guarantee that there will be reams of stuff about the War of the Roses, clash in styles, Busby and Revie, Bobby and Jack, large swathes of which this author has been guilty of producing. I’ll spare you all that today because while it’s relevant to fans of both clubs, they know it backwards. And it’s a match that has come around a wee bit too frequently in pre-season recently, first in Perth, Western Australia, in 2019 when Ole Gunnar Solskjaer’s side beat Marcelo Bielsa’s 4-0, then in 2023 in Oslo when Erik ten Haag’s second or third string defeated Daniel Farke’s threadbare, just relegated squad 2-0 and last year in Stockholm, which ended in a 0-0 draw between two scratch sides.

This one comes round later in the pre-season schedule than those three so we can expect to see stronger XIs, at least for part of the game. Leeds’ pre-season has gone well: after a 3-2 defeat by Wrexham in Tampa, they have beaten Sunderland in New Jersey, Liverpool in Chicago and RB Leipzig at Elland Road. Manchester United also lost to Wrexham (in Helsinki) then beat Rosenborg in Trondheim, Atlético in Stockholm and drew one apiece with the European champions, PSG, in Gothenburg last time out.

While Leeds will have new signing James Trafford available and Noah Okafor, who scored twice in Leeds’ first league victory at Old Trafford for 45 years last April, in the squad for the first time after the World Cup, Manchester United have Kobbie Mainoo and Marcus Rashford back in training since the start of the week but it seems too soon for them to start but Youri Tielemans is pushing to make his first start.
