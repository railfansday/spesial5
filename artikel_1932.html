‘Predictable, mutual, unfair’ - A brief history of Brighton’s sacked managers

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

So the current worst kept secret in football is out. Michael Carrick is no more at Old Trafford, it’s actually not United’s first rodeo in this footballing genre.

I recall being at the Amex on Easter Monday 2014, in the period of about 15 minutes I and four others in our party of ten that day received a forwarded text stating that David Moyes would be sacked as Manchester United manager the following morning, Tuesday 22nd April, and he was!

Sackings are, unfortunately part and parcel of football. In my time following the Albion, we’ve had dismissals that have been predictable, justified, unfair and even a tad bizarre.

My first was Pat Saward in October 1973. I didn’t know much about, but in researching my book a couple of years ago, it was clear that once the ultra ambitious Albion chairman Mike Bamber got a sniff he might be able to rock the football world and bring Brian Clough to the Goldstone, dear old Pat was a dead man walking.

We then didn’t get another sacking for nearly a decade, and then it was all decided on the doorsteps of Sussex.

Mike Bailey had got the club it’s highest ever league finish, 13th in 81/82. But he was building from the back and at times the football wasn’t that exciting. Crowds dipped below the all important break even 15,000, and when fans were questioned by the lottery sellers on their weekly rounds, the word ‘boring’ was predominant and Bailey was out.

All these years later, I still believe the decision was a tad unfair and short sighted, but also one of those classic Brighton and Hove Albion sliding door moments.

Albion legend Steve Foster recently told me, whilst we probably wouldn’t have got to the 1983 Cup Final, we wouldn’t have got relegated and in time, Bailey could have turned us into a consistently top ten side, regularly challenging for Europe.

Bailey’s successor, Jimmy Melia, was almost in a footballing Sword of Damocles situation. Initially a caretaker boss, within a month the Albion embarked on an FA Cup campaign. With Bamber effectively waiting to wield the axe the Monday after the Albion were knocked out, but they weren’t. Melia, with his white dancing shoes took the Albion to Wembley but also rock bottom of the First Division and relegation!

Chris Cattlin was the next cab on the rank, his near three year tender, on a limited budget saw the Albion compete and again, like Bailey, given time, and funding, who knows what he could have achieved?

But he was unceremoniously dismissed at the end of the 85/86 season, but took the Albion to court for unfair dismissal, which resulted in his final victory at the club.

The returning Alan Mullery, another Goldstone legend, in the summer of 1986, was purely cosmetic. Replacing the popular Cattlin with the equally popular Mullery, saw a spike in season ticket sales, and not a lot more.

Mullery was a patsy, disgracefully treated when sacked in January 1987 for almost laughable reasons with the Albion in 13th.

A wretched run under new boss Barry Lloyd followed, with, as with the Division One exit in 1983, the Albion were relegated to the old Third Division in 22nd place in the division.

Yet we had to wait another nine years for another dismissal. Lloyd had left by mutual consent in December 1993, but in December 1996, with the cash strapped and soon to be homeless Albion in 92nd place in the Football League, Jimmy Case was unsurprisingly axed.

As was his successor, Steve Gritt, despite working a footballing miracle and keeping Albion in the league, he was gone in early 1998, again unjust, more about finance than football.

Exiled at Gillingham, crowds nowhere near break even, a massive shot in the arm was required, and that came in the shape of another returning legend (pattern forming) Brian Horton.

Jeff Wood, a genuinely nice guy, was in the wrong place at the wrong time Albion wise in 1999, as was Martin Hinshelwood in 2002.

Full disclosure, I’m in the privileged position to call a number of ex Albion bosses friends, and Mark McGhee is firmly in that catergory. His dismissal in September 2006, is another that sticks in the craw.

I do however still have a degree of sympathy in the bigger picture. At the time, Dick Knight, chasing our collective Falmer dream, was juggling all the balls whilst protecting his own.

Dean Wilkins, still in my opinion technically one of the best coaches the club has ever had, was both a safe pair of hands but a far cheaper option whilst the battle for the stadium went on, tellingly though however he has never worked as a manager since he departed the club in 2008.

I still believe that Mark McGhee would have mounted a promotion challenge to get us back in the Championship.

With the greatest respect in my opinion Albion wise Russel Slade still remains an irrelevance, only doing the job that Micky Adams would have done had he not resigned in February 2009.

Which brings us on to Gus Poyet, a footballing firework, it was great whilst it lasted, but it was what it was, and given off field working relationships, or lack of them, it was never going to last.

But to clarify Gus was not sacked live in the BBC, he was fully aware of the situation and his subsequent reaction was as genuine as American Wrestling!

But he still gave us some great memories, which will never be forgotten.

Chris Hughton’s sacking in 2019, was another exit, which leaves a bad taste. It could have been handled so much better. Like the Moyes text of 2014, I was told that Graham Potter was coming in 40 minutes before Hughton kicked off what was to be his final Albion game. Yet he was interviewed by Richie Reynolds on the pitch at the end about his hopes for the next season!

Potter, could and should have been sacked at least one or two occasions, but a crumb of comfort was the £20 million Chelsea gave us for his services, it truly is a funny old game.

So was Roberto De Zerbi sacked in May 2024, or merely his contract not renewed?

I still love his time as Albion manager, like Gus and others some amazing memories, but unfortunately there are boundaries and as soon as that microphone went under his nose in Rome with the apparent diatribe against Tony Bloom there was only going to be one outcome.

Which brings us around to Fab. Yes many, myself included, have embarked on the ‘Hurzeler Hokey Cokey’, but given the Albion’s current fortunes that’s well and truly parked.

You can never say never following the Albion but I think it’s a fairly safe bet there won’t be another Amex sacking anytime soon!
