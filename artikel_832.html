Smith: I want to play on the PGA Tour

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Cameron Smith joined LIV Golf in 2022 after clinching his first major at The Open at St Andrews; the Australian will play select events on the DP World Tour in 2027; watch Smith in action at this week's BMW Australian PGA Championship live on Sky Sports

Cameron Smith has moved on from LIV Golf, is preparing for his invitation-only appearances on the DP World Tour and is already thinking about a return to the PGA Tour.

As the 2022 Open champion prepared for the BMW Australian PGA Championship starting Thursday at The Lakes in Sydney, attention inevitably turned to his career goals and his steps toward a possible return to the PGA Tour.

"I guess that's the path. That's where I want to be now for sure," Smith told the Australian Associated Press.

"DP's given us a chance to kind of fulfil that. We'll see how that goes. There's plenty of good players out there and plenty of other guys that want to do the same thing, so it's going to be difficult.

"But for sure that's where I want to play."

Smith will not return as a full-time member on the DP World Tour, but is one of a number of former LIV players, including Dustin Johnson and Bubba Watson, to sign an agreement with the tour to receive invites to a specific number of tournaments during the 2027 season, excluding Rolex Series events.

He receives an exemption to play this week, having won the BMW Australian PGA Championship three times in 2017, 2018 and 2022.

Under the DP World Tour and PGA Tour's current agreement, the top 10 players on the Race to Dubai Rankings qualify for the PGA Tour.

The number of PGA Tour cards available to players finishing at the top of the Race to Dubai Rankings for the 2028 season is yet to be determined, with the PGA Tour set to launch its new two-tier championship structure the year after next.

However, to even have a chance at winning one of those coveted cards, Smith will need to play more tournaments on the DP World Tour's 2027 schedule.

He could do that by winning the co-sanctioned BMW Australian PGA Championship or the Australian Open at Kingston Heath, which begins on December 3 and also features Rory McIlroy.

"I think given what they've told me, I pretty much need to win this year to get membership," Smith said.

"It's all still very blurry to me at the moment. There are a lot of rules, but we'll have a few opportunities, and like I said, I've always managed to play pretty good down here, so why not start it off with a good one?"

The PGA Tour board last week said it had "thoughtful discussions" about the future of players at LIV Golf but otherwise seemed to be in no hurry to decide if there is room for them next season.

CEO Brian Rolapp said the Returning Member Program, which allowed Brooks Koepka to return to the PGA Tour at the beginning of 2026 with financial penalties, was not on the table.

Smith didn't take up that one-time offer in January, which applied only to players who'd won majors or The Players Championship from 2022 through 2025, which included himself, Koepka, Jon Rahm and Bryson DeChambeau.

Smith is in a kind of limbo. LIV Golf, which lost the financial support of Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF) and filed for bankruptcy on September 8, told a bankruptcy court earlier this month it had abandoned plans for a new version of the league and had begun winding down its business.

"There was a lot of uncertainty and then in a way it was good to just like get the noise over and done with," Smith, who joined LIV after winning The Open at St Andrews in 2022, said.

"It was a big move to go LIV at the start for sure, and I think everyone that was close to me and all my friends saw why I was doing it and why I made the decision."

Adam Scott said he had no doubts about Smith fitting back in.

"Cam's incredibly talented and he'll adjust to his new environment," Scott said. "He'll be just fine."
