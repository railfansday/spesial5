A Long Story Short…Im Hiking The AT!!

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Let’s get this party started! And the best way to start is with a nice introduction on who I am and my “why” to hike the Appalachian Trail.

First off, I am a born and raised Chicagoan who had not had much exposure to hiking and camping as a kid besides the Girl Scout events I would go to here and there. So you may be asking yourself, “then how did you get into the outdoors enough to hike 2,000 miles???”. Well, I went to Northeast Georgia for college. And thus, cue the beginning of my fixation with hiking. The first hike I did was with my college roommate at Tallulah Falls State Park; then came Mt. Yonah, the mountain I jokingly refer to as my first love.

Two years later, I would take my first backpacking trip with my best friend on a TN section of the AT from Laurel Fork to just shy of Damascus.  At first it looked like it would be a grueling week, the first day, we were just going straight up switchbacks without any ease in terrain. The day felt like it was never going to end and I was terrified of sleeping in the woods for the first time. But thank the Lord that we did not turn back, because this is where it all began…

You know those moments in which you realize you cant go back to what you always do? That was me one morning on that trip with my friend. We had been out there for about four days at this point and had our first night in an actual shelter. It had rained the day before, and honestly, the trip had been fun but was kicking our butts up to this point.

After waking to the screaming of Whip-poor-wills, I crawled out of my sleeping bag and went out to behind the shelter, where there was this big opening to a view of the mountains. It was just a sea of clouds with the dawn creeping up in the distance, peaks popping out from the clouds. Seeing that, all the hard from the first half of the week just seemed to melt away. That was the moment that I said “Ok. I have to do this whole thing”.

This was probably one of the most memorable moments I have, and ever since, I have been chasing views, eating way too many snacks on trail, and have continued to devote more and more time to a growing love for the outdoors and fueling my obsession with the AT.

That’s enough background on me; now comes the meat and potatoes. WHY am I hiking 2,000 miles? I believe that having a why, something that is propelling you forward even after you have been soaking wet for days or when your body is hurting, is important. Of course, I just love being out there; there’s something quite addicting about climbing mountains and just feeling exhausted because of another day seeing just how much my body can do for me. But…that’s not my only reason. My number one reason is for my future. The AT is a training ground and experience for my future.

My dream is to move overseas and to provide humanitarian aid in the hiking industry. To go out and to provide backcountry aid in the mountains internationally. I have always wanted to help people, and even if that is just by way of education, then I will feel fulfilled. I have a few ways in mind that such a career could go. It could be mountain search and rescue or even educating on backcountry first aid, I have not quite gotten that far, but the dream remains. By doing the trail I can both live out my dream of walking to Katahdin while also gaining the most necessary attributes in order to successfully go into my career. Grit. Endurance. Hands on knowledge in remote and uncertain conditions. And further passion for the life I want to lead.

April is still a ways away and there is much to do even though I wish I was leaving tomorrow. I still have gear to pull together, a job to work, and money to save so that I can continue to pay student loans while on trail. I also need to continue to think about training and what I still need work on. If I were in Georgia still, I’d be less worried since I was able to hit the trails more, but being I’m back in Illinois (a.k.a the flat prison) I need to find other ways to physically prepare myself for success. I may be trying my hand at trail running after all!

I am also so incredibly excited, grateful, and blessed to be able to write for The Trek. They have been one of my greatest sources as I got into long distance hiking and I am excited to see where this takes me. Before the trail expect to see more from me on my gear, training, and thoughts on my upcoming adventure!
