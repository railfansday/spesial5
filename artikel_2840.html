Ryan Jaafar runs Novi Detroit Catholic Central back to D-1 title game

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

JACKSON — In a gut-check moment in the Division 1 state semifinals against East Kentwood, Novi Detroit Catholic Central's big fellas and running back Ryan Jaafar swung the momentum back to the Shamrocks

Detroit Catholic Central's offensive and defensive lines stepped up in the second half after East Kentwood whittled a three-possession lead into a touchdown deficit in the first six minutes of the third quarter.

After the Falcons cut into CC's lead, the Shamrocks did not send quarterback Duke Banta back on the field for the next possession. Instead, the Shamrocks went to a wildcat formation with Jaafar receiving the snaps. CC covered 80 yards in six plays, all runs, capped off with a Jaafar scamper up the middle for a 41-yard touchdown.

"That was our senior-led offense," said Jaafar, who finished with over 150 rushing yards and three touchdowns. "We have all the trust in our offense and our defense. We play collectively in all three phases of the game and that's what we did. We finished the game."

East Kentwood reached the CC 10-yard line on the first two plays of the next drive, which both topped 30 yards. The Falcons got the ball down to the 2-yard line, but could not crack the end zone. CC's defensive line plugged two straight runs, including a tackle for loss from junior Kevin Schandevel, and East Kentwood threw an incomplete pass for a crucial turnover on downs at the end of the third quarter.

East Kentwood got a stop and scored on its next possession on a great play from receiver Steph Jones, cutting it to a touchdown again. The Falcons got a stop, giving themselves a chance with 4:05 left. But CC defensive back Aiden Kersey jumped a pass for an interception and returned it inside the 10, setting up Jaafar's final touchdown.

"We wanted to dominate the line of scrimmage on both sides," said Catholic Central coach Justin Cessante. "We ended up doing that to win this football game."

Catholic Central (12-1), the defending Division 1 state champions, beat East Kentwood (12-1) 31-17 to advance to next week's state championship game against Romeo (13-0), which knocked off Detroit Cass Tech 30-29.

The Shamrocks executed while their best and most impactful player in all three phases, 5-star senior Gideon Gash, was out with a lower-body injury. CC did not miss a step with Gash, who remains week-to-week, sidelined.

After speaking to the team and greeting his family postgame, Cessante had tears in his eyes as he watched the team rush over to the stands and sing the school's alma mater.

"We faced a lot of adversity on and off the field this year," Cessante said. "And to get to this moment – obviously, we want to finish – the emotions are overwhelming as far as how proud I am of the boys. It is how they prepare, how they kept at it, especially today when the momentum started swinging. The offense responded, defense came back and responded. Just really proud."

Jaafar was a part of CC's perfect 14-0 run as a junior, but primarily contributed on special teams as Cedric Williams, now at Michigan State, handled most of the workload.

Jaafar patiently bided his time and worked with the goal of being the offense's bellcow as a senior. He continued to step up as a senior captain Saturday, adding to his 1,300-yard, 17-touchdown season.

"Going into this year, we didn't return many starters," Jaafar said. "All of the seniors, juniors, everybody had to step up. We knew what the plan was and we made it our motto: 'Raise the bar.' That's what we are doing this year, raising that bar, and we've got one more week to do it."

In a season where injuries to Gash and Banta have been at the forefront, Jaafar and Catholic Central's offensive line have kept the offense's baseline high enough to make another run to the final game of the season. Jaafar has gotten better as the season has gone on, embracing the physical challenge at running back.

"Jaafar is a workhorse, man," Cessante said. "I am so proud of how he works, his grit and tenacity, and his willingness behind our great offensive line to get downhill. When we need to get physical yards, they go get them."

East Kentwood came into Saturday averaging 46 points per game. Catholic Central's defense held the Falcons to a season-low 17 points, including a first-half shutout, while pulling down three interceptions (two by Kersey and one by LB Luke Jadan), recorded 11 tackles for loss, two sacks and a handful of quarterback pressures.

Schandevel and defensive ends Lindon Bojaj and Josiah Neal were creating a consistent push up front.

"Our defensive line, coached by Mike Lewis, is the best in the state," Cessante said. "They do a phenomenal job getting at it, competing in practice daily and taking great pride in people trying to stop them in the rush and the pass. They really got after it, especially in the first half."
