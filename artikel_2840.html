Spurs use strong fourth quarter to put away Clippers at home

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The San Antonio Spurs overcame a season-high 21 turnovers, using a strong fourth quarter to defeat the Los Angeles Clippers 124-112 at the Frost Bank Center on Thursday night in the penultimate game of their three-game home stand.

The victory is the 13th in the last 14 games for San Antonio, who bounced back from a loss on Tuesday against the Detroit Pistons. The game was the first of several without guard Dylan Harper, who was officially diagnosed with a left hip rotator strain that he suffered at the end of Tuesday's game. He is expected to miss between two to four weeks.

As a result, Julian Champagnie replaced Harper in the starting lineup, and the transition was smooth, considering his starting position last season. Carter Bryant was the first one off the bench for Spurs coach Mitch Johnson, replacing De'Aaron Fox at the 8:54 mark of the period. Johnson said pregame that Harper's injury would cause the Spurs to play around with the rotations a bit more, so that is something to keep an eye on as the week go by.

"We still made some mistakes here and there, but the main thing is we were all connected," Spurs forward Victor Wembanyama said after the game. "And we were mostly on the same page tonight, so that compensated for some of the mistakes."

A low-scoring first quarter saw the Spurs take the 24-21 lead into the second. The Clippers tried to pull Victor Wembanyama away from the paint and it led to a few easy Clippers points at the rim. Wembanyama and Stephon Castle finished the period combining for 14 of San Antonio's 24 points.

The Spurs took a three-point lead into the second half. San Antonio led by as many as 14 points in the second quarter and went on a 21-7 run, but turnovers and poor coverage on the perimeter allowed Los Angeles to stay in the game going into the third quarter.

Los Angeles took that momentum into the second half and opened the quarter on a 11-4 run as San Antonio's defense struggled to get back as the Clippers hit back-to-back three-pointers. That led Johnson to call timeout and talk things over with his team. Johnson's message finally sunk in the fourth quarter as the Spurs outscored the Clippers 34-22 in the final period to pull away for the win.

San Antonio won the game despite a season-high 21 turnovers, something Johnson said has also contributed to the team's shooting woes.

"I actually don't think our passing has been very sharp this year," he said after the game. "I think we've had a lot of passes that have not been on target and I think it's affected our shooting at times."

Of those 21 turnovers, Stephon Castle committed seven of them. Per ESPN, Castle became the first Spur with 7+ turnovers in a single half since Tim Duncan in 2003.

"I don't think it was anything they were doing," Castle said of his turnovers. "I think for me, I was getting probably just one dribble too deep into the paint… So, being able to make the adjustment at halftime, I think, kind of opened it up for me."

Wembanyama led the Spurs with 32 points and 11 rebounds, while Castle and Champagnie combined for 41 points to pace the San Antonio starting unit.

Jordan McLaughlin had a solid outing with five assists and three steals in 11 minutes. He also did not turn the ball over once, which prompted praise from Coach Johnson and McLaughlin's teammates after the game.

De'Aaron Fox had just one shot going into the fourth quarter and took five on the night. He finished with 10 points, but he will need to be more aggressive in the coming games with Harper out.

Keldon Johnson turned back the clock to last season's Sixth Man of the Year run as he had 10 points, 5 rebounds, and 3 assists off the bench. San Antonio will need that contribution from him more consistently to take the load off the starting unit as Harper and Tobias Harris get set to return next month.

The Spurs are now 13-3 on the season and will wrap up their three-game homestand on Saturday night at the Frost Bank Center against the New Orleans Pelicans at 7 p.m.
