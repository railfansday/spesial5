Cowboys have no edge to beat 49ers, out-coached and outplayed again

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Another year, another uncompetitive Dallas Cowboys defense that doesn't leave much left to play for in a losing season by mid-November. The box score will say the Cowboys lost by just six points to the San Francisco 49ers, but the full 60 minutes of football proved the now 8-1 49ers and now 4-6 Cowboys are simply in different competitive tiers for a wide facet of reasons.

Also for the second year in a row, the Cowboys scored exactly 40 points in a home game and did not win – giving up the most yardage they've ever allowed in a home game along the way to lose 46-40. In this spot against their other archnemesis the Green Bay Packers last year, the Cowboys managed a tie. That game still serves as the only non-loss on head coach Brian Schottenheimer's record against NFC teams that aren't the New York Giants, Washington Commanders, or Philadelphia Eagles in the NFC East, as this most recent week ten loss makes it 0-9-1 in such games.

The Cowboys run defense gave up 214 rushing yards, allowing at 7.4 yards a carry. Also for the third week in a row, the Cowboys faced exactly 16 third-down attempts on offense, a number that is too high. Dallas was lucky enough to win the penalty differential 11-5, with the 49ers having 65 more penalty yards against them, but it ultimately did not matter.

It will be a busy week for the Cowboys as they turn the page from this game, preparing for two winnable games next week on Sunday and Thanksgiving Thursday. Let's get to some notes on how they fell short against the 49ers, which makes both games next week feel damper, as the team will be fighting to be .500 at best.

It will be a wash, rinse, and repeat week for Brian Schottenheimer and the Cowboys coaching staff as they had their fingerprints all over yet another loss, and the criticism will surely follow. This Cowboys loss to the 49ers was hardly an outlier though, as we know it was their fifth straight spanning back to the 2021-22 playoffs.

One thing the Cowboys have not been nearly good enough at doing at any point over this entire stretch of time is recognizing individual matchups they can take advantage of and scheming to do so on a consistent basis. This one sentence could be the mission statement for the Kyle Shanahan 49ers, and while the Cowboys have shuffled the coaching and personnel against Shanahan constantly, it's Kyle that continues to come out with the wins.

This was evident even in the way both teams got their first points of the game in the first quarter, as part of a bigger picture first half where the Cowboys put up a good fight just to get worn down and exposed in the second. The Cowboys needed a roughing the passer call from their own former defensive tackle Osa Odighizuwa to move the chains on the opening drive, and were held to a field goal without completing a pass.

The 49ers answered quickly with their first of six touchdown drives on the game. With the Cowboys having both DaRon Bland and Joey Porter Jr. on the field against a hobbled group of 49ers wide receivers, the matchups on the outside actually had a chance to tilt in Dallas' favor. Shanahan's response to this was a masterclass, as he found the Cowboys own hobbled position group in the backend which was safety, and went after them.

George Kittle drew a holding penalty against Caleb Downs to negate a sack, and then Kittle went to work for the first of several times against backup safety Markquese Bell. This was a matchup dominated by Kittle, as he took a screen pass for 20 yards one play before the touchdown to Deebo Samuel on another screen. Malik Hooker was the other safety out of position for the first of what was also many times this game on the touchdown, and the 49ers made it a point to test the tackling of the entire Cowboys defense, especially in the secondary. This was a test Christian Parker's defense failed.

Parker's defense did get Porter Jr. matched up against Kittle for a key third-down stop on the ensuing 49ers possession to earn a three and out, but the scary trend of the Cowboys offense not rising to the occasion of scoring, or even holding onto the ball when their defense does get a stop, reared it's ugly head again. The Cowboys promptly went three and out themselves with an incomplete pass to Lamb on third down.

The best singular example of the Cowboys making offense look as easy as it came for the 49ers in this game was on George Pickens' second touchdown, the slant pass that always seems to work yet isn't called often enough. Pickens' touchdown could have put his team up two scores if Quinnen Williams finished a sack on 3rd-and-12 on the previous 49ers drive, something he made up for by getting a red zone sack to keep the 49ers out of the end zone in the red area – something that's been nearly impossible for defenses this season.

Instead, the door was left open for the 49ers to respond again, and the defense couldn't get Brock Purdy on the ground again. Extending the play, he hit Christian McCaffrey for his first backbreaking touchdown of 74 yards, and just like that the 49ers were up 17-16.

Another third-down incompletion to Lamb put the defense back on the field one last time before halftime, getting the desperation stop to preserve the one-point deficit. Only trailing by one at halftime could still be seen as a win considering how tough the 49ers are as a matchup for the Cowboys, but the way it came about with both the offense and defense being stressed more than needed planted the seed for the second half runaway.

The second half felt like a runaway because Shanahan dug even deeper into his bag of tricks to dictate the terms of this game to the Cowboys defense, getting a Deebo Samuel 70-yard reverse touchdown to go up 31-19. The ultimate dagger came with McCaffrey matched up against Von Miller for a 66-yard touchdown to go back up two scores late at 46-32.

Everything the Cowboys did offensively to storm back late felt empty, hollow, and like a rerun of a bad movie we've seen too many times before anyway, but seeing McCaffrey sprint right down the middle of the field untouched to put it away might be the image of the season for why the Cowboys still are where they are.

One other thing that proved itself out on the scoreboard along the way of Shanahan calling a better game for his offense compared to Schottenheimer for the Cowboys offense was the mobility of Brock Purdy compared to Dak Prescott.

Purdy was comfortable and in control almost all game, though the Cowboys did get to him for five sacks. His scramble ability was a real difference maker though, and came naturally for big play after big play as he completed 78.6% of his passes for 13.9 yards per attempt – a career high.

Compare this to yet another game where things felt laborious for the Cowboys offense for long stretches, and like the weight of the world was on Dak Prescott's shoulders again too, and the home team not being able to rely on Dak's mobility makes it an even sadder state of affairs.

Purdy's scrambles were unscripted plays that should be the equivalent of "50/50 balls" in basketball, but it was more like "90/10" for the visitors in week ten. Prescott's throws from outside the pocket were forced and off-target. Some were scripted plays that served as more examples of Schottenheimer being in over his head, and ultimately failing this franchise QB.

Wasted season after wasted season is becoming the story of Prescott's career, his mobility has not been a true strength of his game for some time, and now it just feels like yet another thing that's come and gone without the Cowboys getting anything out of it.

In all of those ways this loss felt familiar, but it did make history for Prescott as it was the first of his career with five touchdowns. It was also just the second loss in his career losing with four or more touchdowns, the other one also coming at home. Prescott was 0-5 targeting CeeDee Lamb in the first half, as his consistency getting involved in these games mirrors the Cowboys win-one-lose-one-win-one pattern. Meanwhile, Purdy was completing passes to Keenan Allen who just showed up to the 49ers on Wednesday after the trade deadline.

One team had it easy at AT&T Stadium on Sunday, and it was the one with more fan support in the stands, and one team that actually calls that building home had it hard. The Cowboys are now 7-13-1 from 2024 to now at home.
