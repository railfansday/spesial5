Peterson Joins Rockies as Pitching Staff Ramps Up

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

It's a homecoming for pitcher David Peterson. Peterson will pitch for the Colorado Rockies next season after he reached a two-year agreement on a contract with the ballclub.

Rockies beat reporter Thomas Harding of MLB.com had the news late Wednesday afternoon. Peterson is a Denver native, born in the Mile High City on Sept. 3, 1995.

Peterson, who was an All-Star back in 2025, has spent the majority of his MLB career with the New York Mets before getting traded to the Chicago Cubs last season. In a unique case, Peterson is the first starting pitcher to receive a multiple-year contract in a long, long time.

Here's some good news about Peterson, according to MLB Trade Rumors. Peterson has a reputation for being a groundball pitcher. This also means that he's not prone to giving up home run balls, a breath of fresh air for the organization.

Peterson is the latest in a pitching overhaul Rockies president of baseball operations Paul DePodesta and general manager Josh Byrnes are undertaking. The Rockies recently added Luis Castillo and Davis Martin from the Chicago White Sox, along with Chase Lee from the Toronto Blue Jays.

One thing that Rockies fans have to admit is that the ballclub is actively addressing one of its weak points in 2026. The pitching staff was not good and the numbers and data proved that out.

Colorado will have to make a corresponding move after bringing Peterson on board. The ballclub currently has a 40-man roster, so something's got to give.

Peterson has a career 46-38 MLB record and a 4.25 ERA. That ERA might scare some Rockies fans, but he's worked hard to change the narrative around his ERA. He's started 138 games in his 162 career appearances in the major leagues. Peterson has 751 career strikeouts and owns a career 1.50 WHIP.

He has five pitches that he'll lean on when taking the mound for Colorado. They include a sinker, a four-seamer, a slider, a changeup, and a curveball. Peterson threw his sinker 29.5 percent of the time, while he countered with a four-seamer at 25.2 percent.

When Peterson takes the mound at Coors Field for the first time in a Rockies uniform, he'll be able to lean into his own experiences of being there with the Mets and Cubs.

New pitching coach Ethan Katz has a great opportunity to take these new pitchers coming into the Rockies' world and shape them in his own plans. Katz came in after Alon Leichman was fired after just one season as the team's pitching coach.

Peterson can come to the Rockies and make a definite mark on helping the team improve an avoid yet another 100-loss season.
