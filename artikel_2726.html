High School Coach Refuses to Apologize For Winning

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Rick Clausen is not backing down from the parents who hired private investigators to dig into his Westlake High School program. The coach told the California Post on Thursday that he has zero intention of saying sorry for fixing a team that went 0-10 in 2024. He called the letter from "Concerned Westlake High School Parents" comical and stated that nothing shocks him in today's society. This is a clear line in the sand for a coach who turned a floundering squad into a 7-3 playoff team in just two years.

The parents sent a five-paragraph letter to the school on October 28. They claimed they were under the direction of legal counsel and had signed a contract with a private investigation company. The letter accused the program of using financial inducements to bring in players for football reasons. They said they were investigating whether residency and transfer information accurately reflected the students' actual circumstances. The group even cited CIF Bylaw 510, which bans undue influence for athletic purposes in public schools.

Westlake High School went from a winless season to a 10-1 record in Clausen's first year. The team is now 7-3 and earned a spot in the playoffs after going 3-2 in their conference. He told the New York Post that he has inherited programs down on their luck before and turned them around. He said he will not apologize for doing what is needed to win.

The coach believes that competition is a foreign concept for some families. He told reporters that some kids and families feel entitled to certain things. He said that mindset belongs to the old Westlake and does not align with the program's direction going forward. Clausen added that everything in his program is earned through competition. He declared he would not apologize for constructing a program based on good families and good kids with college goals.

Social media critics suggest the letter came from parents upset about their children not getting playing time. According to the letter, parents will file a formal request with the CIF Southern Section to investigate suspected recruiting violations. They claimed to have information that certain student-athletes had been approached about transferring for football reasons. No response was received after the school was contacted for comment. Taking over a struggling Westlake program in 2025, the coach immediately reversed its fortunes. He is not fretting about the complaints one bit. The team is slated to play Dana Hills in a postseason game on Friday night.

The coach's stance is simple: he built a winner and will not bow to pressure. He said that with what goes on in today's society, nothing shocks him in any way, shape, or form. By bringing in outside investigators, the parents demonstrate their readiness to criticize their own coaching staff. They allege an investigation into whether residency, transfer, or enrollment data for specific transfers accurately mirrored student realities.

Clausen remains focused on the field where the results speak for themselves. The program has earned its spot in the postseason with a 7-3 record. Speaking to the California Post, the coach stated he refuses to apologize for establishing a program grounded in good families. The coach knows that competition is the only way forward for Westlake.
