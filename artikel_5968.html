Fuel Pushes Thai Airways Into Q3 Loss as It Awaits New CEO

Thai Airways' demand held up in the third quarter; its costs didn't. With fuel still high, a CEO still to be named and the fourth quarter likely to look similar, 2027 will be about protecting margins rather than pushing growth.

Thai Airways International's revenue rose 13% in the third quarter, but jet fuel prices rose 60%, tipping the airline into a 418 million baht ($12 million) net loss. It says cutting costs, starting with fuel, will be its priority for 2027.

"We will protect the profit from the operation," Cherdchome Therdsteerasukdi, chief of finance and accounting, told investors on the earnings call. "We have a strategy to work on the costs and the fuel, as well as other costs."

The loss comes at a difficult moment. The airline has been run by an acting chief executive since early October, and its board expects to confirm the permanent incumbent by December. Thai Airways International swung to a 418 million baht ($12 million) net loss in the third quarter as a 60% jump in jet fuel prices outpaced a 13% rise in revenue. Revenue excluding one-time items reached 50 billion baht ($1.5 billion), but expenses rose 29.1% to 46.3 billion baht ($1.4 billion), and the airline said it had only partly hedged its fuel. Operating profit before finance costs fell 55.1% to 3.8 billion baht ($111 million), finance costs of 3.4 billion baht ($99 million) absorbed almost all of it, and one-time charges of 1.1 billion baht ($32 million) — mainly from flooding and currency losses — pushed the quarter into the red. Chief of finance and accounting Cherdchome Therdsteerasukdi said cost cutting, starting with fuel, is the priority for 2027, and warned that if current oil prices become "the new normal," the third quarter "could be a reference for Q4." Demand held up: the airline carried 3.97 million passengers at a 76.5% cabin factor, roughly level with a year earlier, and management said it would favor "proper profitability over aggressive volume." The loss lands during a leadership vacuum. The board suspended CEO Chai Eamsiri on Oct. 2 after Bangkok flooding crippled Suvarnabhumi operations, with a permanent successor expected by December.
