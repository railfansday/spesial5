Will the Utes polish off an undefeated regular season in style vs. West Virginia?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

This article was first published in the Ute Insiders newsletter. Sign up to receive the newsletter in your inbox each Wednesday night.

For the third time in modern program history, the University of Utah football team will play the regular-season finale at Rice-Eccles Stadium with an undefeated season on the line.

In 2004 and 2008, the Utes, then in the Mountain West Conference, faced off against rival BYU for a trip to a BCS bowl game.

In 2026, the stakes are even higher, though Utah's "rivalry week" opponent is about the furthest thing from a rival.

When the Utes and West Virginia Mountaineers meet on Black Friday (7 p.m. MST, Fox), a College Football Playoff berth is at stake.

With a win, No. 2 Utah would essentially clinch the program's first-ever spot in the College Football Playoff, no matter what happens in the Big 12 championship game on Dec. 4 against Texas Tech or BYU.

After defeating BYU, Arizona and TCU in November, the Utes close out the regular season as 26.5-point favorites against West Virginia.

The big storyline for the Mountaineers (5-6, 2-6 Big 12) is the health of star quarterback Michael Hawkins Jr., who has thrown for 2,258 yards and 14 touchdowns and rushed for 1,055 yards and a nation-high 21 touchdowns this season.

Hawkins played through an injured ankle in last Saturday's loss to Houston, but the talented quarterback went out for good late in the fourth quarter after a hit and was listed as doubtful on Wednesday night's availability report.

If Hawkins is unable to play, West Virginia's starting quarterback will be Scotty Fox Jr., who played the final two series in the Houston game, which both ended in punts.

Fox, a sophomore, has played sparingly this season, but last year, he started in the back half of the season and finished with 1,276 passing yards and seven touchdowns with six interceptions on 59.4% accuracy and 201 rushing yards and three scores.

While Fox is no stranger to starting, there's a reason why Hawkins won the job this year, and his absence would make the Mountaineers' slim upset hopes even slimmer.

No matter who starts at quarterback, the Utes are prepared for an up-tempo West Virginia offense.

"The speed at which they go, their tempo, because you got to get lined up, you got to get ready, you got to get your call," Utah head coach Morgan Scalley said.

"They are the best in the Big 12, in my opinion, of stressing you with pace."

The Utes have faced up-tempo teams before, but the Mountaineers are on another level, especially when Hawkins is running the show.

"We've definitely played against uptempo, but these guys do it a little differently," Utah linebacker Elijah Elliss said. "Their offense is explosive and it comes from, I think, catching people off guard.

"Obviously they have very talented backs, quarterback. They got talent, so with that uptempo then comes explosive plays when you got talent like that."

West Virginia has some of the highest chunk play (15-plus yards), and explosive play (30-plus yards) rates in the country. Per Hudl IQ, 5.1% of the Mountaineers' plays go for 30 or more yards, tied for the third-best in the country.

If it's Fox at quarterback, expect West Virginia to run even more than it currently does, which is 70.5% of the time.

While Hawkins' running prowess has been integral to the Mountaineers' offensive success, they also boast one of the country's top running backs in Cam Cook, who has rushed for 1,215 yards and nine touchdowns this season.

It's going to be a best-on-best matchup between Cook and the Utes' rushing defense, which has limited opponents to only 103.8 rushing yards per game.

"Cook, for his size, how physical he is, he runs downhill. He's patient. He's everything a good back is. He can catch the ball, he can run the ball, he's patient behind his blocks, good vision, good contact, courage. Yeah, really good back," Scalley said.

Despite a good offense, West Virginia has a 5-6 record due to a porous defense that ranks as one of the worst in the country in nearly all statistical categories.

The Mountaineers are allowing 302.3 passing yards (worst in the country), 192.9 rushing yards (15th-worst in the country) and 34.73 points (seventh-worst in the country) per game.

Utah, meanwhile, enters Friday's game with one of the top offenses in college football. Quarterback Devon Dampier, who is in the midst of a Heisman campaign, has an opportunity to leave a lasting impression on Heisman voters by carving up West Virginia's defense on the day after Thanksgiving.

The Utes have shown a propensity for explosive plays, especially in the last four weeks, and the Mountaineers have allowed many such plays over the course of their season.

Unless West Virginia pulls off a complete course correction, it's going to be very hard for the Mountaineers to shut down a very talented Utah offense, and the Utes shouldn't be involved in a tight fourth-quarter game like they have during each weekend in November thus far.

It's senior night at Rice-Eccles Stadium, and 19 players will walk before Friday's game. Just because a player is walking on senior day doesn't mean that they are leaving, though.

The opportunity is here for Utah. All year long, the Utes have preached going 1–0 each week, and they just have to go 1-0 this week to cap off a perfect regular season.

"You want to go out with a win on senior night, finish the regular season strong," Elliss said. "Last home game at Rice-Eccles so make it a special one, but the preparation's going to be the same. Go 1-0 every week."
