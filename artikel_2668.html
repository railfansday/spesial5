Report: G.M. Omar Khan called Aaron Rodgers directly to inform him of the benching

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

As expected, the Steelers view the events that resulted in the benching and eventual release of quarterback Aaron Rodgers differently than Rodgers does.

On one very specific point — as it relates to whether the Steelers properly handled the situation — the Steelers and Rodgers sharply disagree.

Rodgers said Wednesday that he learned he'd be benched and inactive when tight end Pat Freiermuth called Rodgers, after the announcement of the move was made at 11:10 p.m. ET on Tuesday night. However, Tom Pelissero of Netflix reported during the Wednesday night pregame show that G.M. Omar Khan called Rodgers to inform him of the change to Mason Rudolph.

It's odd, to say the least, that the Steelers announced the move after 11:00 p.m. local time on a Tuesday night. If Rodgers didn't know, there was no reason to let the world (and specifically the Broncos) know the move had been made.

The mere fact that the Steelers announced it when they did suggests that Rodgers knew about the decision, and that the Steelers believed he possibly would release it in terms that supported his broader version of the events.

By announcing it on Tuesday night, the Steelers pre-empted a late-night social-media post from Rodgers — or a strategic leak to a reporter — painting the situation in the light most favorable to Rodgers. For example, Rodgers could have characterized that he was benched because he was repeatedly blowing the whistle internally about coach Mike McCarthy's various alleged lapses.

While it's a minor point, it points to the broader issue of credibility. If Rodgers knew about the decision, any suggestion that he didn't would be calculated to make the Steelers look inept for telling the world he'd been benched without letting him know about it in advance.

The truth may never be fully known. But it's clear that, as to the things that happened over the past day or two, there are (as there always seems to be) two versions — Rodgers's version and the team's version.

It's also possible there are three versions. Rodgers's version, the Steelers' version, and the truth.
