Week 10 Stock Up, Stock Down: Mayeday

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Week 10 was one where the lows felt higher than the highs. Drake Maye went down and took the whole Patriots offense with him. Carson Beck started his first game and the impact may have cratered the entire Arizona offense going forward, and Jonathan Taylor just might not escape generic RB1 hell. Let's discuss.

Tyson followed up a 4/30 debut with a triumph of modern target volume, managing 11/80 on 3 targets as no Saints player other than him managed more than five.

The 80 is the key here – I don't think that Tyson needs to go skyrocketing up into the WR2 range based on this. The Saints just aren't playing like that kind of offense right now. But I do think it at least inches into the realm of possibility down the stretch.

We all knew that first-round pick Tyson would get a chance to do this sort of thing, and though the Saints didn't win this game, they did establish that they're willing to use him quite a bit. I'm not quite buying the idea that he is now the offense's No. 1 option and that all Chris Olave shares are headed to the dumpster, but I do think he's going to crowd out some of the other ancillary targets in this offense.

I take no pleasure in noting that RB2 was already down bad enough with four teams on bye to have time-share backs as easy No. 2 runners. The Texans lost Woody Marks to a concussion halfway through Sunday's disastrous loss to the Browns and will now be in line to be Houston's No. 1 back on Thursday night assuming David Montgomery can't get off the mat.

I'm going to level with you here: I don't think Demercado is good. I also don't think the Texans can run the ball well. But this remains to some extent a volume game, and Demercado could be the No. 1 back against a Colts defense that has been dastardly for most of the season. He's not someone to blow the whole FAAB wad on, but Demercado could give you a big game on Thursday before fading back into anonymity.

The Tennessee Titans offense has been … cooking(?) since the bye. They've now scored at least 20 points in each of their last four games, and they're playing the Cowboys next week. I already know that you don't believe me that this is happening, and I understand and accept that. You can skip on to the next capsule to read about someone who matters.

Well, you're still here for some reason. Ayomanor has played at least 50 snaps in each of the last three games, he's found 22 targets over those three games and turned in his first useful box score of the season with 79 scoreless yards against the Jaguars. At the same time, the Titans defense has absolutely collapsed, allowing 40 to the Bengals and Jaguars after the Jeffrey Simmons and Amani Hooker trades.

This team has become a points funnel all of the sudden. And, again, they're playing the Cowboys next week. I'm not telling you Ayomanor is a superstar waiting to be born. But there are six teams on bye next week, and you're going to need to make some weird roster decisions. Ayomanor might just be a good enough WR3 to get you through something next week against Dallas' barely-there defense.

Kansas City's hilarious beatdown of the Falcons culminated in Downs notching 4/53/1, the touchdown being just his second of the season. However, the additional funny context here is that Downs played just 10 offensive snaps in his first game as a Chief.

We've been burned by Downs before, of course, but with Eternal Non-Entity Xavier Worthy in front of him, it's easy to understand that Downs is going to eventually feast on a larger play volume. Is it going to be a Rashee Rice volume? That I'm not yet sure of. But I am pretty sure that Downs is going to do well enough that fantasy managers will be happy.

Going forward, it's going to feel easy to pencil Downs in as a WR3. There's also going to be plenty of runway to grow. The Colts part of his season never happened. Please stop asking about it.

(If you're looking for Emmett Johnson praise, you're late, I did that after Week 8.)

Drake Maye went down with an ankle injury early in the first quarter, effectively sabotaging the offense. A.J. Brown left with his own injury near the end of the game. Neither player returned, and Patriots coach Mike Vrabel deferred to testing. The early news on Maye from PFT was not exactly encouraging.

New England is on bye, of course, which helps the odds that Maye won't miss much time. But if he does miss time – and we've seen enough hints that this might happen – this offense looked dead in the water with Tommy DeVito under center. Unfortunately, when you write this column, you understand that this kind of death comes for a fantasy offense a few times a year.

It would be irresponsible to pretend that we know what kind of absence Maye's looking at, but even if it's not a multi-week one, you can't be starting Rhamondre Stevenson and Corey Kiner with DeVito under center. That's just not viable, as we saw today. Tertiary receivers without Brown's health will be tough plays. It's a top-to-bottom tough scene.

Congratulations to Alvin Kamara on his two touchdowns. He saw just 16 snaps in a messy situation and will be hard to trust as a FLEX play going forward. Travis Etienne didn't exactly establish himself as the lead back, but he did muddy up Kamara's receiving situation enough to make Kamara himself only a touchdown-or-bust play.

As for Johnson, in his last four games he has not crossed four targets and has just one pity touchdown to show for a grand total of 69 yards of work over that time frame. This offense is running out of effectiveness and volume to get tertiary targets involved. Perhaps Jordyn Tyson's emergence will cause teams to play them differently, but I don't see how you could pretend to be happy starting Johnson given how his last month of returns have gone. I'd classify Johnson as a TE2 at this point.

Nobody is saying that McBride is unplayable in fantasy football or anything like that, but what we are now dealing with is rookie Carson Beck, and I think there's a reasonable case to make that McBride's overall TE1 ceiling is impacted by this.

To be clear: I wouldn't put an enormous amount of stock in this rogue 5/37 line. The Rams are a great defense, and Myles Garrett is finally healthy. The Cardinals only managed to possess the ball for 17:37 of the 60 minutes of football, which I'm told is very bad.

But given the lack of receiving talent around him and a rookie quarterback who took five sacks and was picked off twice in his first start, I think we at least need to shift the window a bit on what we think will happen going forward. I would expect more 7/85s than 11/135s over the rest of the season with the quarterback and receiver downgrades.

The less I can say about the rest of this receiving corps, the better. Only one other receiver caught more than one ball.

With the Colts really and truly playing out the string here, is there really a reason for them to push Taylor? They seem very content to roll out Seth McGowan for 20 snaps a game and McGowan, to his credit, has taken advantage of the opportunities. He's averaging 4.8 yards per carry on the season and has vultured a score from Taylor in each of the last two weeks. He's not exactly decimating Taylor's workload, but McGowan is doing just enough to make RB1 upside a fading dream for the established vet.

I think Taylor is a great player, but other than the fact that he's going to destroy the Texans on Thursday Night because they have not gotten straight with whatever deity they pray to, why would they push a guy who they might have the next general manager move next offseason? To what end? If they weren't going to pedal Taylor to the floor against the Dolphins to get a win, there's no reason to believe that'll happen the rest of the season.

Will he be a reasonable RB1? Of course he will. But the guy who might threaten the overall RB1 ranks? He might have had his last hurrah against the Falcons in Europe roughly a year ago. This is just a steady contributor profile, no more, no less.
