The “Julian Champagnie for Three-Point Contest” campaign begins here

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Having the Spurs well represented at All-Star Weekend the last few years has been a new feeling, and I'm not just talking about the Rising Stars Challenge or the main event. Stephon Castle and Carter Bryant came in as runners up in the Dunk Contest the last two years, marking San Antonio's first representation there since the 1980's. Victor Wembanyama also participated in the Skills Challenge in both his rookie and sophomore campaigns (to not-so-great results, as Anthony Edwards' horsing around cost Team Top Pick the first time, then Wemby and Chris Paul's attempts to cheese it got them disqualified in the second go-round.)

But one event the Spurs have always felt underrepresented at is the Three-Point Contest. Despite being one of the teams that helped to revolutionize the three-pointer and often having some of the top shooters in the league, their history there is limited to Steve Smith (2002), Roger Mason (2009), Matt Bonner (2013) and Marco Belinelli (2014 winner, 2015). And Bonner basically had to plead and even campaign for himself to get in.

The Spurs have had many more deserving participants than that, but it's no lie that the league likes to go for trendier names since these events suffer enough for viewership as it is, and this is an event superstars can participate in without fear of injury or overexertion. Perhaps the most obvious gimmick ever was allowing Damian Lillard to participate last season despite not playing in a single game while recovering from a ruptured Achilles. Was he medically cleared to shoot threes? Of course. Was it a feel-good story? It was, but was it fair to perhaps another deserving participant who played the whole season and otherwise missed out on some bonus money? That is the ultimate question, and I can easily name one such player: Julian Champagnie.

Champagnie is the ultimate feel-good story himself. Undrafted out of St. Johns, he signed a two-way contract with the 76ers in 2022, just to be waived for Mac McClung, whom they only wanted to represent them at the Dunk Contest before he was waived himself. Their loss was the Spurs' gain, and they quickly grabbed him to help with their lack of shooters, and he's been filling that gap beautifully ever since.

Whether starting or coming off the bench, he has always been one of the steadier hands in the NBA. Even though he initially began last season off the bench, he kept finding himself filling in for injured starters before eventually taking over the starting power forward position for good, after Harrison Barnes fell into a slump he couldn't escape, and it became obvious that having Champangie out there either took defensive pressure off Wemby, or defenders forgot about him and left him wide open. More often than not, he made them pay by hitting 38.1% on over six attempts per game. That shooting carried over into the playoffs, where he hit 39.6% of his threes as a starter for a team that made the finals.

This year has been even more of the same in terms of being in and out of the starting lineup, except he's shooting even better. While it's still early, he's currently shooting 41.7% on 7 attempts per game, both career highs. Because the season is still young, that "only" places him at 38th in the league in three-point percentage, but amongplayers who have played in the majority of games and are shooting at least 7 attempts per game, that places him behind only Desmond Bane, Duncan Robinson, Klay Thompson, Steph Curry, Grayson Allen and Jamal Murray. That's six players, and the contest has eight participants. Odds are as the season progresses, the real shooters of the league will begin to separate themselves, and Champagnie will be one of them.

With the Spurs having all the eyes on them now, surely the league will want them well represented at All-Star Weekend. While Wemby is a shoo-in for the All-Star game itself, no one else really is, so maybe putting their best shooter in the Three-Point Contest will be a good start. If Champagnie keeps this up, he'll be more than deserving, and hopefully it won't take a Matt Bonner campaign to get it done, but in case it does, we're starting it right here.

Today is November 24, 2026, and with the zero power I have, I am officially nominating Julian Champagnie for the 2027 Three-Point Contest in the Footprint Center in Phoenix, Arizona on Feburary 20. Who's with me?
