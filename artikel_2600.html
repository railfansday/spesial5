Knicks 121, Cavaliers 102: “The most Mike Brown victory imaginable”

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

It's easy to get lost in today's world. Everything is spinning so fast, so many things are out of control. Prices, taxes, wars, narratives, diabolical devices, our sense of togetherness. Life sometimes feels like an endless list of things that, no matter how much you scratch off, no matter what you decide not to worry about, gets longer by the minute. The NBA is no exception, a cutthroat world infected by influencing influences that expects daily reinvention, that wants to turn humans into brands and expand like a kraken gobbling up the last little dollars from the deepest pits of the coral reef.

As you watch another foul-baiting baller be rewarded for his deeds, followed by the 23rd commercial for guaranteed grifting, it can be tough to find the joy here from time to time, to remember why you ever thought this was fun. But then Jose Alvarado runs into the picture and steals a ball, only to rise up moments later for a three. He jumps for joy as he hits it, just like the little kid he once was on the playgrounds of the city that he now represents on the biggest stage there is in basketball. And you smile along with him.

There is something inherently right about a guy like Jose Alvarado. Sure, he is an annoying pest defensively, loudmouthed, confident on his good days, cocky on his better ones. A guy you would probably hate if he wasn't on your team. Not even in the fun way of hating an opponent, if there is one. No, you really can't stand that little bugger, like, at all. Yet even if you root for some other franchise whose name doesn't rhyme with clicks, deep down inside your basketball fan's heart, you have to appreciate a guy, a man, a kid like Jose Alvarado. Mike Brown surely does.

It didn't even take Alvarado's tide-turning performance in the Knicks' 121-102 destruction of the Cleveland Cavaliers in yesterday's NBA Cup game, which was, as PolyphonicSpreewell so beautifully put it in the Postgame Thread, "the most Mike Brown victory imaginable." It didn't take his 16 points in the fourth quarter or his 21 in total. But the light shining on Jose surely was a reminder that the big little Puerto Rican embodies what this New York basketball team is. Sure, they can beat you with Xs and Os, with the might of majestic superstars, with firepower that can put a New Year's fireworks display to shame. But there is more that makes the Knicks the Knicks. A group that has a heart and a soul beyond what is expected of them, some inner power they can turn to when things go sideways. Something that lets them play and, more importantly, enjoy the game innocently, freely. How it was supposed to be experienced.

Alvarado is not your type of guy? Pas de souci, says Pacôme Djenon Dadiet! It didn't matter that he didn't really hit shots consistently yesterday or, frankly, ever before. He still plunged into the action versus the James Hardens and Donovan Mitchells of the world like the Knicks' bench was a cliff and his name was Orlando Duque. Maybe not as gracefully, but definitely just as committed and energetic. And, most importantly, without any fear. There was no fear in Tyler Nickel, either, who at some point seemed as far away from real NBA minutes as his birthplace in the Shenandoah Valley is from Madison Square Garden. But don't worry, bench guy, Mike Brown's got a map, and he knows where to find you.

With injuries to OG Anunoby, Deuce McBride, and Jordan Clarkson, plus ineffective and almost shockingly bad nights from Jalen Brunson and Karl-Anthony Towns, the coach seemingly didn't have to search yesterday. He just hurled everyone he had on his pine into the action and watched them stick it to the Cavs. Mo Diawara hit a three. James Wiseman dominated the paint for stretches in the fourth quarter. He didn't look out of place; he didn't look like he didn't know what he was doing. Rather, he played like a man possessed, not by some evil power or the One Ring to rule them all, but by the unwavering confidence and belief of a bald, happy-go-lucky man who coaches champions.

These champions include a much-maligned Mikal Bridges, who carried the team throughout the entire second quarter. They also include Josh Hart and Landry Shamet, who did their thing and then some. They are all guys who had rough goes in the past but who never found themselves in Mr. Brown's doghouse. They deserve to be singled out for pushing the ball in transition, for point-of-attack defense, for just being themselves. But frankly, who doesn't? Everyone did a little and then a lot of what was needed from them. I'm sure Tyler Kolek was one of the biggest cheerleaders despite his rough night on the court. It all speaks to the culture that Mike Brown has bestowed upon this team, that he has created with all of his players. All of them. All these guys who know what hard work is. Who relish every opportunity they get.

And who have a way of playing basketball that is as much fun as you can imagine.
