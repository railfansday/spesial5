The 11 Best Video Game Remakes of All Time, Ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

The act of remixing and remaking a previously beloved work of art has been pervasive in all different mediums and forms of entertainment, yet there are distinct elements of video games as a medium that make their respective remakes different from other art forms. While movie and television remakes are often defined by their change in artistic vision and technological improvements, video game remakes often take the initiative of refining poorly aged aspects of the original games, making use of the exponential growth of technical capabilities, or just simply making the game readily available to modern gamers.

This has resulted in many video game remakes, when they are successful, not only living up to the stature and legacy of the original games, but in many ways replacing the original in terms of replayability, being the definitive ways to experience these legendary titles. Remakes come in all different forms and approaches, but the very best video game remakes find this perfect balance between delivering the strengths of the original game and having something new and exciting to offer.

Paper Mario: The Thousand Year Door continues to be celebrated as one of the very best games on the GameCube, with its overwhelming charm and engaging battle system making it the fan-favorite peak of the Paper Mario franchise. However, the series largely abandoned the signature battle system and engrossing worldbuilding that made the game such a hit, making it all the more exciting that the game was receiving a super polished remake in the modern age.

It would be great enough to simply release the exact same masterpiece for modern consoles, but the Nintendo Switch remake of Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door adds just enough quality of life changes to make it the definitive version of the game. Most notably, the game makes improvements to pacing through the addition of a fast travel area and a quicker way to switch between partners. The game also features two new secret bosses to challenge veterans of the original game and a better translation of the original Japanese version of the game, portraying fan-favorite character Vivian as a transgender woman.

The game just came out, so it's still early to tell how much of a legacy and impact this will have on the larger gaming industry as a whole, but the beautifully constructed Nintendo Switch 2 remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time flourishes and evolves what was already one of the greatest games of all time. With beautiful visuals, a multitude of quality-of-life changes, and full voice acting, this remake is the complete package and a love letter to one of Nintendo's most cherished gaming experiences.

As influential as the original game was and as great as it still is to this day, this remake serves to solve and update the game to the modern standards of not just visuals, but primarily control and ease of access in experiencing its mastery. The remake leaves a mark on gamers who experience it, whether they've played through the original game constantly since it was released or they only just recently got into the series with games like Breath of the Wild.

Following in the footsteps of the other major horror video game franchise's tendency to create massive, fully-fledged remakes of their most beloved classic titles, it was only a matter of time before Silent Hill 2 was given the AAA remake treatment. While the series as a whole has had a middling reception and was outright abandoned for large chunks of the 2010s, there was a lot of clear passion and care put into this remake to make it faithful to the original masterpiece as possible.

The major strength of this remake is to provide a modern third-person camera system and a more dynamic combat system to a central narrative that is still celebrated as one of the very best in video game history. All of the strengths of its story and emotional beats are still there, with even legendary composer Akira Yamaoka returning to remix his all-time classic themes. It certainly helped that the game was largely developed by Bloober Team, a dev team of deeply passionate horror game fans who were inspired by the original Silent Hill 2 in the first place.

Yakuza had always been an appreciated franchise, but it reached an overwhelming state of newfound success and appreciation following the release of series prequel Yakuza 0, putting the series in the perfect spot for a high-profile remake of the first game in the series. Yakuza Kiwami makes use of a lot of the improvements that the series has made since its humble PS2 beginnings, more directly honing into its distinct voice for hard-hitting crime drama as well as wildly goofy and over-the-top comedic side content.

It also helps that this remake more directly amplifies some of the most celebrated aspects of the entire series, having some of the most iconic side missions and a perfect portrayal of the iconic rivalry between Kazuma Kiryu and Goro Majima. This game has outright replaced the status of the original game in the eyes of many Yakuza fans, as well as being one of the best entry points to the franchise in all of its brilliance.

While video game remakes directly utilizing the improvements of technological advancements largely seems like a modern trend, this phenomenon has been occurring even during the 16-bit era of gaming, with one of the best examples being Super Castlevania IV. The SNES classic acts as a remake of the original NES classic, seeing Simon Belmont facing off against the monsters in Dracula's castle looking to defeat the infamous monster.

The game is a perfect example of just how much video games as a medium was growing and evolving in the 80s and 90s, having massive visual and gameplay improvements despite releasing only 5 years after the original game. It adds more exciting gameplay elements, increases the scale and memorability of its missions, and features one of the absolute best video game soundtracks of all time. The game is still considered one of the best games in the franchise and one of the best games on the SNES.

Four worlds and four completely different ideas of what a good life looks like. This is where you belong, what it would ask of you, and what you would get back in return.

You belong somewhere old enough that the mountains have names and somebody can still tell you who gave them. Middle-earth is for people who think the right thing is worth doing even when it will not be finished in their lifetime.

You want the sea, a crew, and absolutely nobody telling you where you are allowed to go. The Grand Line is the world for people whose main ambition in life is finding out what is over there.

You would rather be feared and useful than liked and useless. The Continent is a world where the monsters are the straightforward part of the job and the people are where it all gets complicated.

You want the myths to be real and loose in the actual world, and you want to find out at about thirteen that you were never anywhere near as ordinary as everybody kept telling you.

The Persona series was already massively celebrated and acclaimed when Persona 3 first released, but the series saw a massive explosion in popularity and visual style following the overwhelming success of Persona 5. This largely led to the creation of Persona 3 Reload, which acted as a definitive modern remake of the classic PS2 game that utilized the visual flair and brilliance of Persona 5. Reload proves to be incredibly faithful to the storytelling and character of the original game while upgrading it to the modern standards of the series.

In terms of pure polish, the game is one of the most beautiful and striking pieces of visual art that gaming currently has to offer, elevating and evolving what was already a game with half-decent visuals into a masterclass of style. While the visuals are the clear immediate strength of this remake, the game also makes a lot of quality of life changes to better align its gameplay with the systems of Persona 5. It overall just makes for a more impactful and compelling version of what was already one of the best JRPGs ever made.

The original Shadow of the Colossus was one of the most critically acclaimed adventure games of its era, with its beautiful and somber tone mixed with the massive scale of its boss fights making it a true work of art that pushed the medium forward. Considering just how much of the original game was defined by its then pristine graphics and the weight of its scale, amplifying and further iterating on this scale in a full 4K remake for PS4 made the Shadow of the Colossus remake an undeniable leap forward.

Development team Bluepoint Games already had experience in recreating the strengths of Shadow of the Colossus, having previously developed the PS3 remaster of the game. However, this remake proves to be a massive leap forward in terms of the visual brilliance and scale of the game, making its most iconic moments all the more breathtaking and captivating for players to experience.

Many of the games from the 8-bit era of gaming, as massively influential and important as they were for the industry as a whole, certainly don't hold up nearly as well when placed under a modern lens. In terms of actively updating and evolving one of these legendary titles to fit the standard of its modern titles, Metroid: Zero Mission is about as great as a remake of an 8-bit game can get. This Game Boy Advance classic and remake of the NES original Metroid adds so much new content to the original classic that it's almost staggering.

It's filled with new puzzles, more engaging combat, and a whole slew of new bosses and areas that expand upon the world and story of the NES original. While the original Metroid continues to be defined by its age, Zero Mission is one of the most timeless and well-crafted entries in the entire franchise, to many people being one of the high-points of the franchise in terms of its 2D entries.

Few video game franchises have been as consistent and frequent with their updated remakes of classic titles as the Pokémon series, as their remakes often go beyond simply porting the original game, but create vast, new experiences complete with all sorts of content and changes from the modern games. While several Pokémon remakes have surpassed their original games in the eyes of fans, Pokémon HeartGold and SoulSilver were so beautifully crafted in their recreation of the Generation 2 games that they are still considered some of the best Pokémon games ever made.

HeartGold and Soulsilver are not just defined by how effectively they match the original games, but also by just how much new content and experiences that they add to this title. Gameplay additions that have been added since the Game Boy Color days, like Abilities and the Physical Special split, made an overwhelming difference in terms of the feel of the game and Pokemon battling as a whole. The game also has the fan-favorite feature of having Pokemon follow the player around, a feature many fans still beg to be included in modern games to this day.

It's a lofty undertaking to remake and modernize one of the most influential video games of all time, even moreso, that Final Fantasy VII would end up being remade not just in the form of one game, but an entire trilogy of AAA experiences. While the trilogy itself is set to conclude this upcoming April with Final Fantasy VII Revelation, the highlight of this trilogy still stands as the iconic first game, Final Fantasy VII Remake. The essential PS5 game expands and builds upon the first act of the original game, creating a complete action RPG experience from the iconic locale of Midgar.

It speaks volumes that Final Fantasy VII Remake is adapting what is only the first 7–10 hours of the original PS1 game and transforming it into a fully complete, nearly 100-hour adventure complete with engaging fights, emotional storybeats, and a dynamic remix of its classic story. The game further redefines what a Final Fantasy game can be in the modern age of gaming while staying true to the brilliance and timelessness of the original game.
