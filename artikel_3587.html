Big Brother's Paul Abrahamian Accused of Collecting Human Skulls and Remains

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Big Brother alum Paul Abrahamian has been accused of collecting human skulls and other remains as part of an archive of oddities and curiosities.

Abrahamian, 33, parlayed his success as the runner-up on seasons 18 and 19 of CBS reality series in 2016 and 2017 to establish a boutique brand called Deadskull, based around “antiques” and “art,” per his Instagram account. However, an investigative report published by the Los Angeles Times on Wednesday, November 25, indicated that the House of Villains star also engaged in the purchase of human remains from international sellers.

One December 2022 purchase caused a minor scandal in Argentina when a parcel allegedly containing a child’s mummified skull was intercepted by customs agents before it could be shipped to addressee “Paul Adrahamian” at a New York City address that matched the Big Brother star’s residence, according to an ex-girlfriend.

The child’s mummified skull was allegedly hidden under a Spider-Man mask, gauze and a woolen cap, though a tuft of red hair and eyelashes were visible.

A former girlfriend of Abrahamian — identified only as Deanna in the Times story — alleged that the reality star was “just going off about how excited he was about it, because it was ‘so sick’ and ‘nobody has anything like this'” before the parcel was detained in Argentina.

“I have a bad feeling about this,” she allegedly warned him. “I don’t think you should buy this.”

Abrahamian denied having any involvement in the controversial purchase, telling the L.A. Times, “The situation referenced in Argentina is inaccurate. I had no involvement in that matter, and it does not reflect my work in any way.”

No criminal charges were filed in the case nor was the alleged sender ever positively identified.

The outlet reviewed text messages allegedly sent by a number matching Abrahamian’s phone in which he seemingly bragged about carrying so-called “oddities” aboard airplanes on other occasions.

“Literally 8 human skulls in my carry on, 9 grams of weed, tripping balls,” he allegedly wrote in October 2023, while he purportedly boasted in another set of messages, “I have literally LITERALLY flown with a human fetal skeleton… everytime I fly from LA to NYC and vice versa there is a minimum of 2 human skulls with me no one has ever asked me.”

In September 2024, messages allegedly sent by Abrahamian referenced traveling from Armenia to the U.S. with a child’s heart hidden inside a sandwich. Once he arrived home, he allegedly texted an ex-girlfriend, “the ❤️ is in its wet home.”

Abrahamian did not directly address the alleged purchase of a child’s heart but more broadly stressed that he takes “sourcing seriously and only engage[s] with materials that I understand to be legally obtained and handled appropriately.”

“I’ve also contributed to restoration work in institutional settings, including assisting with the preservation of historical specimens at a medical university in Armenia, where they continue to be used and studied by both students and professors,” he noted.

Laws about trade, transport and display of human remains vary by state in the U.S. California does not necessarily restrict the collection of non-Native American human remains for display purposes though a permit can be necessary.

New York law states that “the commercial display of human remains” requires “the department of health to implement a permit process for anyone displaying human remains for payment or other consideration,” with exceptions being offered for items “consisting solely of human hair or teeth” or those that are “more than 100 years old.”

International law on the trade and transport of these so-called “curiosities” is much more complex, varying from country to country.

Us has reached out to Abrahamian’s representatives for comment.
