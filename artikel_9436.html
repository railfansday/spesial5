The Vitamin Linked To A Better Night’s Sleep

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Two weeks of sleep tracking linked higher levels of one vitamin to longer, more consistent sleep.

Two weeks of sleep tracking linked higher levels of one vitamin to longer, more consistent sleep.

People with higher vitamin D levels tend to sleep longer and have more consistent sleep patterns.

Those with higher levels also tend to spend a greater proportion of their time in bed asleep.

One proposed explanation involves melatonin, the hormone that helps signal when it is time to sleep.

The findings suggest that vitamin D status and sleep may be connected through other biological processes or shared everyday habits.

The research involved almost 80 generally healthy adults aged 18 to 45, recruited from the University of Delaware and the surrounding area.

Participants wore wrist monitors for two weeks, allowing researchers to estimate their sleep duration and timing.

Blood tests measured vitamin D levels, while urine samples provided an indicator of overnight melatonin production.

Before statistical adjustments, people whose vitamin D levels were 10 ng/mL higher slept around 12 minutes longer per night.

The association with longer sleep remained across the adjusted analyses, although several other links weakened.

Because the research was observational, it cannot establish whether increasing vitamin D improves sleep.

Sleep follows an internal rhythm that repeats roughly every 24 hours.

Light reaching the eyes helps the brain keep this rhythm aligned with the cycle of day and night.

As darkness falls, the brain normally increases its release of melatonin, helping the body prepare for sleep.

Bright artificial light late in the evening can interfere with this process and delay that signal.

Time spent outdoors and physical activity can also influence these rhythms.

Sunlight has another role in the body, allowing exposed skin to produce vitamin D.

People who spend little time outside may have more difficulty obtaining enough of the vitamin.

Food provides another source, particularly oily fish such as salmon and mackerel, while egg yolks contain smaller amounts.

Previous research offers some evidence that vitamin D supplements may improve how people rate their sleep quality (Abboud, 2022).

A review brought together 19 intervention studies, although only three trials could be combined in its main statistical analysis.

Those trials found improvements in questionnaire scores after supplementation, but participants included people with fibromyalgia and those receiving methadone treatment.

Results for sleep duration and specific sleep disorders were less consistent, leaving questions about who might benefit and which aspects of sleep could change.

The study was published in the journal SLEEP Advances (D’Agata et al., 2025).
