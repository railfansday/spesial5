Layaway Lets You Buy Without Debt – Check the Fees & Deadlines First

Layaway lets you buy something you can’t pay for yet without borrowing. The store holds the item while you pay it off in installments, with no interest or credit check. Before you sign, check the fee, the final payment date, and whether a canceled layaway refunds cash or only store credit.

Layaway charges no interest, but it isn’t free. Two of the chains that still offer it show how much the terms can differ.

Burlington offers layaway at participating stores through mid-December, for $10 or 20% down, whichever is more, plus a $5 fee in most states, with 30 days to pay. Gabe’s takes 20% down and a $10 fee, then at least 20% of the balance every 14 days, with 60 days to pay in full and a Dec. 20 pickup deadline for the holidays. Burlington won’t hold furniture or rugs, and Gabe’s limits each layaway to $500. In most states, missing the deadline or canceling at either store costs another $10, and the rest of your money comes back as store credit, not cash.

Say you put a $300 bike for a December birthday on a 60-day layaway in mid-October: $60 down, a $10 fee, then $60 every two weeks. Put it on a card charging 25% interest a year instead, and pay the card on that same schedule. The interest comes to about $6, less than the $10 fee, and the bike comes home right away. Layaway’s edge is that its cost can’t grow. Fall behind on a card and it keeps charging 25% on what’s left, while a layaway never costs more than its fees.

That makes layaway the right call when you’d rather not borrow at all and the item could sell out before you save for it. If it will still be on the shelf, saving first costs nothing and keeps your money as cash. Saving also lets you buy at a sale price, while Burlington won’t mark down an item already on layaway. A buy now, pay later plan gets you the item today, but it’s a loan, and plans spread across apps pile up fast.

Get a copy of the contract before you sign, as the Federal Trade Commission advises, and keep a record of every payment.

Pick a plan you can pay off a week before its deadline, so one late paycheck can’t turn your payments into store credit.
