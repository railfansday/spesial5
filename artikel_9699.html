Store-Bought Hummus Is 10x Better When You Add A Scoop Of This Condiment

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Among the most iconic Middle Eastern dishes, hummus blends chickpeas, tahini, olive oil, lemon juice, and optional seasonings to delicious effect. It's a dish that can be assembled at home, but many tasty store-bought versions are available, too. If your grocery store hummus happens to taste a little flat, though, reach for toum. Just a spoonful of this condiment will make a version that's 10 times better.

Hailing from Lebanon, toum is an emulsion made by blending a generous dose of garlic with oil, lemon juice, and sometimes water. Smooth and airy in texture yet with an underlying bite, it lends hummus a dose of garlicky goodness and acidity, readily transforming a bland flavor into a complex one. Plus, the condiment packs in extra fat, helping to improve the perceived creaminess of disappointingly dry hummus. Since toum is a homogeneous paste, it's easy to incorporate, and its flavor can be less aggressive than raw garlic, too.

You won't need much toum to make an impact. Start by mixing in just under a teaspoon per standard 8-ounce container, then work in gradual increments from there. For ease, grab a jar of toum online, or peruse the same grocery aisles where you bought the hummus. You could also easily prep your own toum in bulk — the condiment typically keeps for up to a month in the fridge.

A toum-inflected hummus makes a great dip, scooped up with za'atar-dusted pita chips, pretzels, veggie chips, or whatever other crisp your heart desires. Note that there are many other ways to use hummus in everyday cooking, though — especially when it's mixed with toum.

For example, consider topping the condiment with spiced ground beef or lamb, a popular Middle Eastern combination. Flavor the minced meat with earthy spices and fiery chilies, and the garlicky hummus makes for a mouthwatering flavor combo, turning the store-bought dip into an extra-hearty meal.

Alternatively, make the chickpea-based dip less of a centerpiece and use it as a sandwich spread instead — it would pair delectably with crunchy vegetables like cucumbers and onions, as well as greens like spinach. Served on whole-grain bread, the dip makes for a delicious vegetarian meal.

Looking for an extra creative twist? You could even use the toum hummus to make your next batch of tuna salad — the garlicky flavor will pair terrifically with the tinned fish. When you're craving Middle Eastern flavors but don't feel like cooking, keep the toum-hummus pairing in mind.
