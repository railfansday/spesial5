A practical guide to coverage, missing fields, matching behavior, and choosing an API for the product you are building.

A company enrichment API can return a profile and still leave your application missing the information it needs. In our six-provider comparison, CompanyEnrich led overall coverage and profile depth, while competitors led individual fields. That makes the required fields in your workflow a useful place to start choosing an API.

Enrichment looks simple in a demo. Send a domain. Get a company profile. Add the fields to your application.

The more useful question comes after the response arrives: can your application actually use it?

A lead-routing workflow might need employee count and headquarters country. A market-mapping product might need corporate relationships. An account research agent might need a description and reliable company identifiers. Those applications can reach different conclusions about the same response.

We recommend choosing a company enrichment API by starting with the fields and decisions it needs to support. A large response is helpful only when it contains the information your workflow requires.

Here is what our six-provider comparison can tell you, where its limits are, and how to turn the results into a shortlist.

This comparison uses CompanyEnrich’s published benchmark. The evaluation began with 500 selected domains from the Majestic Million; 349 passed the DNS check and became the benchmark set.

The sampling code and methodology narrow that description: selection was restricted to alphabetic root .com domains and balanced across prefixes. These were not 349 manually verified businesses or a representative sample of every market.

Field presence does not establish accuracy. The study did not manually verify every company match or returned value, and it does not rank production latency or price.

The normalization measures presence rather than equal detail: a location can be a country alone, and funding can be any mapped funding signal.

Source: the published benchmark report, checked October 13, 2026. Find rates and average profile depth are rounded to one decimal place. Profile-depth averages exclude unmatched domains.

For an integration, coverage and depth answer different questions. A provider can return fewer profiles while supplying more information on the ones it finds. You also need to check which information is present: an extra social link cannot fill a missing employee-count requirement.

The order below follows the published find rate. Field-level observations also draw on the published comparison. The evaluation priorities are our recommendations; your own accounts and required fields should determine the final choice.

CompanyEnrich led the published comparison on find rate and average profile depth. That makes it a reasonable starting point when an application needs several company attributes from a domain lookup.

It did not lead every field. Employee-count or range presence was 50.4% for CompanyEnrich versus 60.2% for People Data Labs, which matters if that field drives your qualification rules.

Its domain-enrichment documentation describes a useful implementation choice: waitForEnrichment=false returns stored data immediately. If the company is not stored, the API returns a 404, schedules enrichment in the background, and does not charge for that response.

That behavior matters for a signup flow. You can let the user continue and arrange a later lookup instead of making account creation depend on enrichment finishing. Your application needs to distinguish that condition from other failures and use bounded retries.

Evaluate it for company profiles, account qualification, and other workflows that use several fields together. Check the exact required fields before treating a returned profile as a successful outcome.

Apollo is worth evaluating when enrichment needs to fit an existing sales workflow. Its organization-enrichment endpoint supports company identifiers including domain, LinkedIn URL, and website; a name can help improve matching. Returned data can include industry, employee counts, revenue, funding, and corporate relationships.

Apollo placed second in the published comparison. It also had slightly higher technology and funding-field presence than CompanyEnrich. Those are small observed differences, so we recommend testing the fields on your target accounts before using them to choose a provider.

During evaluation, confirm that your account and API key have the required access, and include enrichment credits in the cost model. Existing access can make an initial pilot easier; it does not settle the data-quality question.

People Data Labs deserves attention when matching controls and specific company fields drive the decision. In the comparison, it led LinkedIn URL coverage and corporate-relationship presence.

Its enrichment parameters expose min_likelihood, which sets the minimum match score, and required, which specifies fields a result must contain. Raising the matching threshold can reduce returned results while filtering weaker matches.

That is a useful tradeoff to make explicit. A workflow that automatically updates customer records may value a stricter match more than a larger number of responses.

Record these settings in any comparison you run. Otherwise, different matching thresholds can make provider coverage numbers difficult to interpret.

ContactOut is worth including when company enrichment sits alongside contact-data workflows. Its company endpoint accepts domains and returns company information. The documentation shows that a successful HTTP response can contain an empty companies array, so your client must inspect the result before recording a match.

The comparison showed a narrower overall profile but useful strengths: ContactOut led follower-count presence and shared the highest revenue-field presence with Crustdata.

Those fields may justify a trial for a particular application. They do not establish that the revenue estimates are correct or that follower count predicts buying intent.

Check what each field represents, how often it is populated for your accounts, and whether its definition fits your scoring model.

Coresignal is worth testing when enrichment feeds company and workforce analysis. Its company-enrichment endpoint accepts website URLs and supports selecting data bundles and fields.

In the comparison, Coresignal returned fewer profiles than People Data Labs and ContactOut, but its matched profiles had a higher average field count. That is a useful reminder to inspect both dimensions of the table.

There is a practical constraint: selected fields must belong to the requested bundles, or the API returns an error. Record the product, endpoint, and bundle configuration in your test; the benchmark is a dated snapshot of the tested configuration.

Start with the fields your application needs, map their definitions, and then measure coverage against those requirements.

Crustdata is worth evaluating when company profiles need headcount, funding, hiring, or other selected data groups. Its enrichment API returns basic information by default; request the other groups explicitly. Responses can also contain multiple candidate companies, which your application must resolve.

That request configuration belongs in your evaluation notes. Comparing one provider’s default response with another provider’s expanded response can change the apparent winner before you inspect any data.

When several candidate records share a domain, apply your own entity-selection rules before using their headcount or funding data in an automated workflow.

Suppose you are building inbound lead routing. You decide that a record is usable only when it has the correct company identity, a headquarters country, and an employee-size field your routing rules understand.

Write that requirement down before comparing vendors. A profile with twenty other fields still cannot route the lead if a required value is missing.

Keep three measurements separate: profiles returned, profiles with all required fields, and profiles whose identity and important values pass your review. Each stage can remove records from the previous one.

Build a pilot from your actual market. Include smaller companies, different geographies, redirects, and ambiguous brand names where they occur in your workload. Keep the input set identical across providers.

A few hundred domains can help expose integration problems and obvious coverage gaps. It will not establish the error rate for every segment. For a large purchase, expand the sample and report results by the segments that matter to the product.

Review whether a result describes the company you intended, its parent, a subsidiary, or another business with a similar name.

Then inspect the fields your application uses. Employee ranges and employee counts may require different routing logic. Headquarters location and operating locations answer different questions. A revenue estimate should not silently become reported revenue in your database.

Preserve source and retrieval metadata where available, and decide how to handle disagreements before allowing enrichment to overwrite existing records.

Use a denominator that reflects the outcome you need:

Cost per usable record = total evaluation spend / records meeting your acceptance criteria

Include the subscription or minimum commitment allocated to the workload, credits, retries, and any separately charged fields. Measure response-time percentiles and timeouts alongside cost if enrichment runs in a user-facing flow.

If you add a second provider, measure how many additional usable records it supplies after the first provider fails. Its standalone coverage does not tell you how much it will improve your waterfall.

Start by shortlisting providers whose documented interfaces and field definitions fit your application, then run the same acceptance test against each.

The benchmark helps identify candidates. The production decision comes from the overlap between correct company matches, required fields, acceptable latency, and sustainable cost.

Before making the first request, finish this sentence: “An enriched company record is useful to us only when it contains…”

The answer gives you a much better test than counting every key in the JSON.
