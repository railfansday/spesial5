The Next Bond Villain: From Idris Elba to Kristen Stewart, 22 Actors Who Should Face Off Against Robert Pattinson

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Every James Bond is only as good as the person who’s trying to kill him.

Throughout the franchise’s history, we’ve seen Sean Connery take on Gert Fröbe’s Goldfinger, Roger Moore tackle Christopher Walken’s Max Zorin and, in the Daniel Craig era, Mads Mikkelsen’s Le Chiffre in “Casino Royale” and Javier Bardem’s notorious Raoul Silva in “Skyfall.” In a Bond film, the movie cannot succeed without the diabolical plan and villain that’s trying to destroy the world or the shaken, not stirred man in the tux.

After the news that Robert Pattinson has been cast to portray the infamous hero in an upcoming film from Denis Villeneuve, the search is on for the next “Bond Girl” and now the anticipated adversary that threatens his mission to keep the world safe.

The wish list includes many familiar faces from Pattinson’s past, both in front and behind the camera and some long-shot selections that may be crazy enough to work. Whether it’s a tech messiah, a rogue spy or an heiress with a doomsday device, we’re excited.

Here are the 22 actors Variety would love to see go toe-to-toe with the new 007.

Honorable mentions: Mahershala Ali, Austin Butler, Cate Blanchett, Willem Dafoe, Mia Goth, Nicholas Hoult, Aaron Taylor-Johnson, Wagner Moura, Dev Patel, Matthew Rhys, Callum Turner

Riz Ahmed is skilled in dramatic roles, like his Oscar-nominated performance as a troubled drummer in “Sound of Metal.” But he’s also a master of voice and motion-capture acting, from Severus Snape in the “Harry Potter” audiobooks to the ruthless symbiote leader Riot in “Venom.” So whether he plays it straight or perhaps takes the form of a monstrous menace to Robert Pattinson’s Bond, Ahmed has the subtle, steely power to be a baddie either way.

Pierce Brosnan led four James Bond films as the titular superspy in the late ’90s, so why not bring him home for one last ride as the silver-haired supervillain?

Brosnan, who was vocal throughout the 007 casting process, has remained a staple of the action genre 24 years after his final turn as Bond in “Die Another Day.” He recently lent his espionage expertise to Steven Soderbergh, playing the double-agent villain in his twisty spy thriller “Black Bag.” Also on the villain front, Bronsan has been stirring up trouble as the leader of the Irish mob in “Mobland,” which is wrapping its second season.

Director Denis Villeneuve is about to bring Timothée Chalamet through his villain arc in “Dune: Part Three,” and he may want to bring that tyrannical overlord energy with him to the Bond franchise. Chalamet, like Robert Pattinson, is one of Hollywood’s most in-demand stars, which makes him an intriguing on-screen rival. Chalamet has built his young career playing righteous leading men, however complicated they may be, in films like “Marty Supreme,” “A Complete Unknown” and “Wonka.” It could be time for Chalamet to stay on the dark side for a bit longer and take on cinema’s suavest spy.

If Pattinson made his case for playing Bond with Nolan’s time-bending spy thriller “Tenet,” then Debicki should also be strongly considered alongside him. The two of them had standout roles in the movie, and we’re dying for them to get some more screen time together. “He is an enigma himself,” Debicki told Variety about Pattinson’s Bond casting. “He’s beautiful, and he’s funny and he’s smart. I think he’s perfect. I’m so thrilled.”

Having spent so many years being tipped by the media for the 007 job — and then being asked about Bond in every interview — it feels only right that Idris Elba be given a juicy bad guy role to play. But this is no consolation prize for the Brit, who has excelled in intimidating, scene-commanding villainy on more than one occasion, including as the brooding and calculating Stringer Bell in his “The Wire” breakout, the ruthless mercenary leading a group of child soldiers in “Beasts of No Nation,” the genuine action movie baddie in “Hobbs & Shaw” and, er, the master criminal feline Macavity in “Cats.” OK, fine.

Colin Farrell has already gone toe-to-toe with Pattinson, buried under prosthetics as Oz Cobb in “The Batman.” The Oscar nominee from “The Banshees of Inisherin” has played a hit man with a conscience in films like “In Bruges” and an assassin as Bullseye in “Daredevil.”

Now let’s picture that Irish charm curdling into something far more dangerous, like an arms baron who’s a blast to watch even as he’s trying to kill Bond. Do it.

Fassbender spent years playing an empathetic villain in Fox’s “X-Men” series as a young Magneto. Now, he’s played a suave spy in last year’s surprise sleeper hit “Black Bag,” a cold assassin in David Fincher’s 2023 thriller “The Killer” and a CIA officer in “The Agency” on Paramount+. He certainly fits the bill to star in a Bond movie, perhaps as a double-crossing 00 spy who faces off against Pattinson.

Rebecca Ferguson has shown she can keep up with Tom Cruise’s Ethan Hunt in the “Mission: Impossible” franchise, so it could be time for her to face off against cinema’s other iconic man of mystery.

Outside of her three “Mission” films, she recently took on internal affairs as a high-level situation room officer in Kathryn Bigelow’s “House of Dynamite.” Ferguson is probably best known for her leading role in Apple TV’s “Silo” as Juliette Nichols, a mechanic-turned-post-apocalyptic liberator. It also helps she’s in Denis’ orbit already with the “Dune” franchise.

She’s already a pro at unraveling a conspiracy, so she is sure to be an expert at weaving one together in the face of Bond.

Walton Goggins is already one of Amazon’s most valuable assets, starring in Prime Video’s post-apocalyptic drama “Fallout” as a 200-year-old zombie cowboy.

Of course, Goggins made Boyd Crowder, the silver-tongued soul of FX’s crime drama “Justified,” one of his most memorable creations. Plus, he nearly stole the show on HBO’s anthology series “The White Lotus” as the haunted Rick.

Some of the best Bond villains are a little unhinged and completely charming, and Goggins brings both.

Villeneuve and Gyllenhaal have already worked together on “Prisoners” back in 2013, and they’re long overdue for a reunion. Gyllenhaal has played a wide variety of unsettling bad guys in recent years, like a grimy journalist in “Nightcrawler,” a theatrical Mysterio in “Spider-Man” and a smooth-talking criminal in “Ambulance.” He’s also bulked up and trained in UFC fighting for two “Road House” movies. His Bond villain could mix all of these past roles into one and be a true chameleon.

Isn’t it safe to say that AMC’s “The Night Manager” was pretty much Tom Hiddleston’s 007 audition tape?

He reprised his role as former British intelligence operative Jonathan Pine earlier this year in the second season streaming on Prime Video. However, the tuxedo never came, so maybe being another scene-stealing bad boy can get him into the fold. As Loki in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, Hiddleston proved audiences will adore a villain even while he betrays everyone.

Maybe casting him as a disgraced MI6 golden boy, or an agent who was supposed to get the number but then didn’t, will let him spend the whole movie proving he actually deserved it.

Angelina Jolie has already shared the screen with one 007 when Daniel Craig played her rival in “Lara Croft: Tomb Raider” (2001). What if she took a shot at the next one?

The Oscar winner of “Girl, Interrupted” has played both sides of the spy game in films like “Salt” and “Mr. & Mrs. Smith.” She also gave Disney one of its most iconic villains in the live-action format with “Maleficent.”

Let her bring a menacing rage to the MI6 team and watch what happens.

Daniel Kaluuya is reuniting with his “Judas and the Black Messiah” director, Shaka King, on a new Amazon MGM movie, “The Parlay,” so technically he’s already in the family, right?

For anyone who saw Steve McQueen’s “Widows,” they’ll remember how terrifying the Oscar winner can be as he played the enforcer Jatemme and delivered one of the most chilling henchmen of the past decade. Let’s not forget “Black Panther,” where he played W’Kabi, the trusted friend who betrays his king.

Once thrown out as a worthy contender to play Bond, this Londoner, with his knack for exuding menace, would have MI6 very worried.

Barry Keoghan has already said that, rather than Bond, he’d prefer to play the villain. Still, he’s certainly got the credentials to prove it. From a sinister teen who embarks on a campaign of revenge in “The Killing of a Sacred Deer” to a swaggering, volatile gangster in “Calm with Horses” to the murderous manipulator of “Saltburn,” the Irishman has shown he could be 007’s perfect, cherubic master of menace. And then there’s that deleted scene in “The Batman” as the Joker opposite Pattinson. Time for a proper face-off between the two.

From Nolan to Villeneuve, Murphy could follow Robert Pattinson from one major director to another. Murphy hit the lotto with his “Oppenheimer” Oscar win, and playing a Bond villain could be his second wave. We know he’s up to the challenge after seeing him as J. Robert Oppenheimer, the Scarecrow in “Batman Begins” and Tommy Shelby in “Peaky Blinders.” Aside from a cameo in “28 Years Later: The Bone Temple,” Netflix’s “Peaky Blinders” movie and a return in “A Quiet Place 3,” Murphy’s slate has been too quiet since “Oppenheimer” and a Bond movie would be the perfect big-screen welcome back.

Upon learning that Pattinson would be Bond, Pitt pitched himself to play the villain at his “Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” premiere. Pitt has produced “Mickey 17” and “The Lost City of Z,” both of which starred Pattinson, so the two already have a connection. “I love what he’s been doing. I love his choices. I think he’s the real deal. That’s good. A good call, I like it,” Pitt said, then threw his hat in the ring to play the bad guy. “I’d like to do that. My man Javier [Bardem] did it so well. It’s tall shoes, but I’d take that on.”

Nobody in Hollywood makes politeness more terrifying than Jesse Plemons.

He was Todd, the soft-spoken killer of AMC’s crime series “Breaking Bad.” In Alex Garland’s “Civil War,” he turned one question, “What kind of American are you?“ into one of the frightening scenes of the year, in a one-scene wonder, no less.

A Bond villain who never raises his voice and never apologizes before he pulls the trigger. That’s what exactly Plemons does best.

The Oscar nominee of “The Power of the Dog,” who won Best Actor at Cannes for “Kinds of Kindness,” is playing Plutarch Heavensbee in “The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping” this year, a role originated by the late Philip Seymour Hoffman. And famously, also played a villain in “Mission: Impossible III” (2006).

Florence Pugh may be on many fans’ lists as a potential Bond girl (and so did Variety), but it seems the Brit — who stars alongside Robert Pattinson in “Dune: Part Three” — would rather go a different route. Speaking at Elle’s Women in Hollywood event shortly after 007 was cast, she said she’d messaged Villeneuve to say: “Congratulations on finding your Bond, nice catch. Any villains the size of a 5ft 4in female?” While Pugh’s roles haven’t yet seen her dip into full-scale global terrorism, as Cathy in the recent “East of Eden” adaptation, she has shown she could certainly explore her darker side.

With his shape-shifting ability to transform into a menacing clown or a cadaverous vampire, Bill Skarsgård can become just about any villain the Bond creative team imagines.

Skarsgård’s roles in “It,” “Nosferatu,” “Barbarian,” and “The Crow” are just a few of the horror-tinged roles he’s inhabited, suggesting his take on a Bond villain could lean more toward the grotesquely menacing side. The Swedish actor even appeared with Robert Pattinson, a fellow famous vampire, in the Netflix movie “The Devil All the Time,” though they didn’t share any scenes.

As soon as Pattinson’s name was floated as 007, hordes of Twihards were immediately fan-casting Stewart to give the Bond universe a “Twilight” crossover. But instead of playing a Bond Girl, Stewart would be a much more entertaining choice as a villain. Please, Amazon MGM, make this ultimate Bella Swan and Edward Cullen fan fiction come true. Better yet, cast Taylor Lautner as a henchman to Stewart’s Bond villain.

A great James Bond villain is known for their striking, singular presence – and who better to provide that presence than Tilda Swinton? Her otherworldly, androgynous persona has been put to good use by everyone from the Avengers to Wes Anderson, Bong Joon Ho and the Coen brothers, and she could easily switch on the evil vibes as a Bond villain. Swinton hasn’t worked with Robert Pattinson yet, but Pattinson is a big admirer of her performance in 2008’s “Julia.” “I think that’s one of the great performances, and it’s kind of criminally underseen,” he told Rotten Tomatoes.

In “I’m Still Here,” Fernanda Torres played Eunice Paiva, a woman standing up to a military dictatorship and the role earned her a Golden Globe statue and an Oscar nomination for best actress. While on the awards circuit, the Brazilian star knew exactly what she wanted to do with her next big venture: play a Bond villain.

“I would love to be the secretary of a James Bond villain,” she said on the Variety Awards Circuit Podcast. “And just say, ‘He will be waiting for you in the next room, Mr. Bond.’ That’s it. That’s my dream. Just this line. That’s all I want to do.”

Imagine Torres as the power behind the throne: a Rio de Janeiro media baroness who smiles for the cameras while quietly buying off half the government?
