Are Man Utd genuinely in a relegation battle?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

After Manchester United's 4-0 thrashing at Liverpool, Mauricio Pochettino's side officially have the worst defence in Europe and have endured their worst start to a season in over half a century - so are they going down?

What started as a blip has become a problem far more systemic for Man Utd.

"If the name wasn't Man Utd and you're watching their performances, you'd say they are a team in a relegation fight. They're conceding lots of goals, a comedy of errors, red cards - they've won one game all season," said Sky Sports' Jamie Carragher.

Only Ipswich beaten in 11 attempts, the worst defence in Europe - and a 4-0 hammering at Liverpool on Sunday to wipe the gloss off Mauricio Pochettino's arrival within 12 first-half minutes.

Carragher added: "This Manchester United back four can't cope with pace. They're a million miles off it in terms of coming up against what they are in the Premier League."

The unthinkable is beginning to become thinkable. Are United in an actual relegation battle now?

"They're in a relegation fight right now because of where they are in the table," said Carragher after a fifth defeat of the season left them with their lowest points tally at this stage of a season since 1972. "You can't say anything else when you look at their record this season.

"We don't believe it will happen over a 38-game season, but they haven't had the most difficult of starts. They've played a lot of promoted teams; they haven't played the best team in the league, Arsenal, yet.

"I don't think they'll go down, but this idea that they haven't got a scrap on their hands, they have."

It seems unfathomable. But should it? The numbers don't give Pochettino any grounds for optimism. Only 26 teams - barely one a season - have picked up eight points or fewer from their opening 11 games in the history of the Premier League and only 13 of those, exactly half, avoided the drop.

Of course, most of those were not clubs anywhere near the size and stature of Manchester United. But even the Opta supercomputer, which provides far more contextualised feedback, is losing faith and gives them a 24 per cent chance of going down - though it does still back them to stumble to safety and finish 16th.

"You wonder how low it can get," said Gary Neville after Sunday's defeat. "It's embarrassing. I don't believe they're in a relegation fight, they'll get out of this, but this is the worst Man Utd have been in 53 years. The worst defensive team in Europe, second-bottom in the league, they've never started this badly.

"Mauricio Pochettino's options aren't great."

That may be putting it lightly. As well as the worst defence in Europe's top-five leagues across all competitions, United also have the third-worst performing attack in the Premier League, falling below their xG tally by 4.2 goals already.

In an immediately infamous tweet in 2014, then-boss David Moyes was quoted as saying United "must improve in a number of areas, including passing, creating chances and defending".

Pochettino is unlikely to admit it publicly, but behind closed doors must have similarly widespread concerns even at this early stage. United have not found themselves in the bottom two after 11 games in 40 years.

Sir Alex Ferguson was brought in as their eventual saviour that winter, but turning the tide on this United side is a significantly bigger ask.

Are there personnel at his disposal who could improve matters? Potentially. Neville did pinpoint a back four of Luke Shaw, Harry Maguire, Lisandro Martinez and Diego Dalot who could at least get United out of their defensive slump.

"It's a poor back four, but it's their best back four. It could compete," he added. "But the one that started today and finished against Villa a few weeks ago, that'll get you relegated."

There is good news and bad news. Neville's quartet are unbeaten in every game they have started together since the start of 2023/24 - and can boast a 70-per-cent win ratio. Unfortunately for Pochettino, that only amounts to 10 games across more than three seasons, owing largely to availability.

But that still does not solve the problem of their limp attack. Of the 22 players to have attempted a shot for the club this season, 19 have under-performed their xG, and the three who have not, Benjamin Sesko, Joshua Zirkzee and Youri Tielemans, have scored only a solitary goal apiece.

There remain huge question marks over the Man Utd dressing room, its commitment and a lack of leadership which permeated into the public sphere in the final weeks of Michael Carrick's reign. Though Pochettino's players have shown they can perform when they want to. Carrick himself knows that better than anyone.

But his successor barely has time to address it over the next few weeks, with only one free midweek between now and the end of 2026 - and even then he has a trip to the Emirates, live on Sky Sports on December 20, to face at the end of his first uninterrupted week on the training pitch.

By that point United will be almost half-way through the Premier League season - and alarm bells will be ringing even louder if they still find themselves in the quagmire, especially with rock bottom Crystal Palace and promoted Coventry to come before then.

But might even that prove problematic for Pochettino?

"If you look at the strength of the Premier League this season, in terms of what we would call the teams you'd expect to be mid-table or near the bottom - it's probably as strong as it's ever been," said Carragher.

"There's no poor teams. There's not many great teams, but there's no poor teams.
