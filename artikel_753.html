In-form Stoke see off Millwall to make it five wins on the bounce

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Stoke City vs Millwall. EFL Championship.

Report and free match highlights from the Sky Bet Championship match between Stoke City and Millwall at the bet365 Stadium on Wednesday night; George Hirst scores twice as the in-form Potters beat the Lions.

George Hirst bagged a brace as Stoke secured a fifth successive Championship win by beating Millwall 2-1 at the bet365 Stadium.

The Scotland international scored just his second goal for the Potters in the 1-0 win over Birmingham on Saturday and doubled his tally for the season to take Stoke back into the automatic promotion places.

Mark Sykes' stunning strike halved the deficit in the final 10 minutes, but Stoke held on to claim an 11th win in their last 14 outings.

The defeat was Millwall's fourth in a row, coming a day after it was announced they had been ordered to play a future match in front of a partially closed stadium for offensive chanting.

Millwall had lost each of their last three league outings without scoring but it was the Lions who created the first chance within the first two minutes.

Tairyk Arconte broke the offside trap and latched on to Camiel Neghli's pass before bursting away down the right and forcing Viktor Johansson to push his low effort away from goal.

Stoke welcomed back star midfielder Ethan Galbraith from illness and the former Manchester United midfielder's spectacular volley crashed against the bar in an exhilarating opening five minutes.

Millwall manager Alex Neil, who was sacked by the Potters in December 2023, could only watch as Jack McGlynn's defence-splitting pass found Hirst, who steadied himself before picking out the bottom corner in the 13th minute.

McGlynn, Stoke's top scorer in the league this season, almost turned from provider to scorer when his long-range shot whistled past the upright.

Stoke had been good value for their lead and grabbed a second five minutes before half-time. Million Manhoef had also been recalled to the starting line-up and was allowed time and space in the Millwall box before picking out Hirst to turn into the corner.

Neil made a hat-trick of changes as the Lions set about trying to get back into the clash.

One of the reinforcements, top scorer Lyndon Dykes, arrowed a long throw into the Stoke box inside the first minute of the second half, which was kept alive by Neghli before fellow substitute Kyrell Lisbie headed goalwards.

Millwall offered much more of a threat after the break as the Potters came under their first sustained pressure of the evening.

Lisbie scuffed his shot when the ball broke kindly to him in the area and Dykes wasn't able to get to the follow-up. Alfie Doughty then saw his powerful effort blocked by Ben Johnson and the rebound was blazed over by Derek Mazou-Sacko.

Stoke earned some respite at the other end when Bae Jun-ho earned a free-kick for the hosts in a dangerous position, which McGlynn forced Lukas Jensen to push to safety.

Stoke manager Mark Robins brought on Sam Gallagher and Robert Bozenik in a double change in attack, and the pair combined for what should have been a Stoke third when Bozenik shot wide after Gallagher's clever cut back.

Millwall halved the deficit with nine minutes remaining as Sykes drifted infield and rifled his left-footed effort beyond Johansson from distance, but Stoke held on to keep their winning run on track.

"There's been a little bit said about George that is unfair. I think people want to see him working hard and I think he overthought things.

"It's amazing if you just remember that you love playing football and you just go and do it, and everything else tends to fall into place. It's not as simple as that but it really helps, and he's done that.

"First half was everything I thought we'd be. I thought we were good, we moved the ball well, we looked dangerous every time we had it.

"What we didn't do in the second half was get close to them at the top end of the pitch and that's sometimes when you tire in a game.

"You want your players to not sit in and invite the inevitable but you can't do that. The players will need to learn and understand what happened in the game. You can't rest on your laurels and see the game out.

"Go back to what happened on Saturday when we were down to 10 men, you don't do the same thing, we've got 11 men, matched in numbers and you've got to go and make things happen for yourself and we didn't do that.

"We weren't clinical enough, we didn't make the right choices in the second half, we had two really clear-cut chances. That was the disappointment."

"First half I didn't think we played with enough belief, enough quality, enough understanding. I thought we lacked loads of things in the first half.

"I thought we got into some good areas and I thought we got into some good pockets but we didn't really do anything as if we believed we were going to score a goal.

"The two goals we conceded were shocking. First half we lacked everything a Millwall team should be, we lacked aggression, intensity, we didn't get close enough.

"Their two lads in the middle of the pitch controlled the game, they were comfortable. The centre backs ate our forwards up.

"It was vastly improved in the second half, but in my opinion it's easy to play a game when you're losing 2-0, to go out and show a little bit more than you showed in the first half.

"We had three or four players in the game that took part, and they showed a bit of personality on the pitch, there was too many hoping that something was going to happen rather than believing you can go and influence a game.

"We had three fresh air shots in the box where the ball comes across and we miss the ball. You can talk about intensity, duels, running, but you also need quality. And if you don't have enough quality, this is a difficult league to win enough games in."
