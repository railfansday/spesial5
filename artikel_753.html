Three years on NI Women finally have friendlies again

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Northern Ireland's friendly double-header against Greece will be live on BBC iPlayer and BBC Sport website

Tuesday, 18 July 2023 was the last time Northern Ireland Women played a friendly.

Under interim manager Gail Redmond, NI produced a fine display to beat Czech Republic 2-0 in Opava.

Just over three and a half years later, Northern Ireland will have not one but two friendly fixtures, with Michael McArdle's side taking on Greece in an Inver Park double-header on 27 November and 1 December.

A lot has changed for NI since that last friendly, but, having two pressure-free games finally offers McArdle an opportunity to experiment with tactics and personnel as they take the first step towards building for Euro 2029 qualifying.

Greece were beaten by England in the first round of the World Cup play-offs

Only eight players who were in the squad for that friendly win are in the current crop over three years on.

Rebecca McKenna, Rebecca Holloway and Simone Magill started while Lauren Wade, Kerry Beattie and Joely Andrews came off the bench.

Fi Morgan and Chloe McCarron were unused substitutes.

Goalkeeper Lilie Woods made her debut, Marissa Callaghan captained the side and Sarah McFadden and Beattie scored the goals in a rare away win.

A month after that friendly victory, Tanya Oxtoby arrived to take over as Kenny Shiels' permanent successor.

She eventually left to take over at Newcastle United in November 2025 and all 26 of her games as NI manager were competitive fixtures.

The introduction of the Nations League and its close ties to Euro and World Cup qualification admittedly left little room in what is now a stacked international women's calendar for friendly games.

During Oxtoby's tenure, NI had two Nations League campaigns sandwiched between Euro 2025 qualifiers.

After falling to Iceland in their Nations League promotion/relegation play off last October, they opted against seeking any friendlies at the end of November as they looked for a successor to Oxtoby.

The competitive games kept coming and Kris Lindsay was then in interim charge of NI's first two 2027 World Cup qualifiers before McArdle took over in April for the four remaining group games and the subsequent first-round play-off defeat against Portugal.

With NI not involved in the second round of the World Cup play-offs while many others are in the upcoming window, McArdle has decided to get the squad together for two friendlies.

With such a young squad at his disposal and with McArdle still relatively new to the job, he is relishing the chance to have the players in camp for an extended period and for NI to test themselves against decent opposition.

"We don't want friendly games in this window, we want to be in the play-offs but we had planned for both [scenarios], this has been a long-term plan," he said.

"Greece I specifically selected because I knew we couldn't draw them in the Nations League as we're in the same pot, but also they're a strong nation who are progressing well and are close to ourselves in the rankings.

"They'll be a real test for us. It was very deliberate selection."

Northern Ireland suffered a 7-1 aggregate defeat in the first round World Cup play-off against Portugal

McArdle wants NI to be "more horrible to play against" and feels they will work on that in this camp as well as what they do when in possession to trouble opponents.

Greece, like Northern Ireland, exited the World Cup play-offs at the first round after an aggregate 8-1 defeat to England.

They had been in good form before back-to-back defeats by the Lionesses, winning five games this year as they topped Group C4 to seal promotion to League B.

"It gives us two games to try and win first and foremost because we need to develop the winning mentality, but also to test some things because a lot of the identity and how we play, after six competitive games, there is still work to do particularly in possession, we've not quite done that yet," McArdle continued.

"That identity that we never give up and are constantly going, that is the case for the group, but we have to improve the athleticism and the duels to bridge the gap to qualification to tournaments."

Qualifying for the 2029 Euros in Germany does not begin until 2028, but McArdle's side have a testing Nations League campaign to prepare for next year.

They have been drawn against Poland, Finland and Belarus and the Scot revealed that he is already planning ahead for potential friendly opponents for next year's November/December international window to maximise time spent with his squad if they are not in the Nations League play-offs.

"There's also the importance of taking all the activity available to us and that's crucial for us growing and moving forward.

"We're already planning for next November/December window and selecting opponents because there is a deliberate strategy how we evolve the group.

"Part of that is strategically picking opponents which will help us grow. The friendlies, in a sense it's not competitive games, but we'll approach them in the same way."
