Choose a small business checking account by how your money comes in. If customers pay in cash, open the nbkc Business Account, which charges nothing for cash deposits. If they pay by card or transfer, a free online account fits, from Bluevine for interest on your balance to Found for setting aside a sole proprietor’s taxes.

The Small Business Administration’s Office of Advocacy counts 36.2 million small businesses in the U.S., and 82.3% of them have no employees. With no payroll to run, most of those owners need little more than an account that takes deposits and pays bills with no monthly fee. Several here also pay interest on the balance, shown as an annual percentage yield (APY).

Cash is where online accounts fall short. Some charge for every cash deposit, others take cash only at ATMs or stores in a partner network, and one here takes none. So each pick is matched to how money comes in and what you do with it. Each is judged by what it costs at your volume, from the monthly fee and what waives it to the limits on transactions, cash, and wires.
