Sharjah City Guide: 18 Contemporary and Historical Attractions to Discover in UAE's Cultural Hub

Sharjah, one of the seven emirates of the United Arab Emirates, has built its cultural profile on the preservation of its history and sustained support for the arts, research, and intellectual exchange. The Sharjah Architecture Triennial (SAT) is one expression of this commitment. Its third edition, Architecture Otherwise: Building Civic Infrastructure for Collective Futures, runs from November 14, 2026, to April 14, 2027.

Curated by Vyjayanthi Rao with Associate Curator Tau Tavengwa, the edition brings together 35 participants across sites that hold different layers of the emirate's history, from the Al Qasimiyah School and the Al Jubail Vegetable Market to the Al Madam Buried Village and the Sharjah Museum of Islamic Civilization. Its six-day opening programme, from November 14 to 19, includes performances, participant-led tours, and film screenings. Among the films, works by Filip De Boeck, Sammy Baloji, and Cassim Shepard trace housing and urban development from Kinshasa to Mumbai. Conversations with Mabel O. Wilson, Paola Antonelli, and Edgar Pieterse lead into a two-day international conference on how knowledge about cities is produced and how local histories challenge dominant narratives of urban development.
