Citi AAdvantage Business Card Loyalty Points Double Dip Explained

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The single best way to earn American AAdvantage Loyalty Points with credit card spending often flies under the radar, so I’d like to talk a little more about that in this post, especially since this tends to cause some confusion. Furthermore, this was recently made an “official” card perk, rather than something that just unofficially applied to most cardmembers.

Link: Double dip your Loyalty Points earning with the Citi® / AAdvantage Business™ World Elite Mastercard®

With the American AAdvantage Loyalty Points program, you can typically earn one Loyalty Point per dollar spent on a co-branded American credit card. However, there’s one card that’s an exception, and by far the best.

The Citi® / AAdvantage Business™ World Elite Mastercard® (learn more) is the co-branded Citi and American Airlines business card. There’s a quirk to the card, though, when it comes to authorized user rewards. On the surface, the authorized user ordinarily earns the Loyalty Points for their spending, and not the primary cardmember, which is an unusual policy for a small business card.

However, through December 31, 2028 (and it’s possible this will be extended again, as it has been in the past), both the primary cardmember and the authorized user earn Loyalty Points for authorized user spending. It basically means you earn two Loyalty Points per dollar spent, just in different accounts. Just to be abundantly clear here:

So the key is for the spending to be on the authorized user’s card, so that this spending unlocks double Loyalty Points (spread across two accounts).

Either way, the Citi AAdvantage Business Card offers several valuable perks, and also has an excellent welcome bonus, making it a good time to apply. So if you are eligible, I’d consider applying even if the double Loyalty Points opportunity doesn’t work out (though hopefully it does).

Personally, the Citi AAdvantage Business Card Loyalty Points double dip is how I qualified for AAdvantage elite status last year. I had a hard time justifying putting spending onto co-branded American cards compared to some other cards out there, when I was only earning one Loyalty Point per dollar.

But if I can qualify for elite status for two people at once, and earn the corresponding Loyalty Point Rewards perks, the math on that spending starts to look much better:

For example, earning 250,000 Loyalty Points per account could potentially earn you a combined total of 12 systemwide upgrades or 170,000 bonus miles. That’s a pretty substantial reward, beyond just the miles you earn for spending and the status!

The Citi AAdvantage Business Card offers an easy opportunity to double dip Loyalty Points earning through authorized user spending. This opportunity is available to all cardmembers, and is currently valid through December 31, 2028 (though has the potential to be extended beyond that). This is a great opportunity, so if you’re pursuing AAdvantage elite status, I’d highly recommend this method.
