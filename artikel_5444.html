Krybit to Settra: how 6 ransomware groups of 2026 operate

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

New ransomware groups of 2026, from Krybit to Settra, profiled by how they operate, what sets each apart and the defenses one security vendor recommends

Not every ransomware group that surfaced in 2026 locks files. Some of the year's new operators steal data instead of encrypting it. One tries to pass itself off as a legitimate business. Another, Deadlock, is a returning name that came back in June 2026.

These new ransomware groups of 2026 come from an analysis by Smarttech247, a security vendor, titled "Six Ransomware Groups That Emerged in 2026." Tadhg De Barra wrote the post, which the company published on its blog on September 4, 2026. It covers six emerging ransomware gangs: Krybit, Payload, Insomnia, AiLock, Deadlock and Settra. The source describes them as groups that emerged or re-emerged during the year.

The profiles are not a ranking. Smarttech247 does not rank the groups by the size or impact of their breaches. This list follows the same approach and treats each group on its own terms.

Victim figures in the source cover different reporting periods. Krybit's count runs through the end of its first full quarter. Payload's and Insomnia's counts are as of late April 2026. AiLock's figure reflects early June 2026. Deadlock named 75 victims in a month. These windows do not line up. The numbers cannot be compared directly or combined into a single total.

The groups also differ in how they operate. Insomnia is described as having no encryptor at all and relies on data theft. The post describes Settra's legitimate-business front, and no Settra encryptor has been publicly analyzed. Deadlock uses a driver exploit. The source ties no single intrusion vector to Krybit. For its two data-theft groups, Smarttech247 offers defenders this advice: "Your backups won't save you."

The post also includes case studies from Smarttech247's client work. Those cases reflect the vendor's own reported work, not independent findings. Each slide that follows covers one ransomware threat actor and what the source establishes about how it operates.

Krybit is one of six ransomware groups that emerged in 2026, according to an analysis by Smarttech247. The security vendor tracked the group's activity through the end of its first full quarter of operation.

Smarttech247 profiles Krybit in a blog post by Tadhg De Barra, published September 4, 2026. The post is a security vendor's assessment, not an independent study.

Krybit's figure applies only to that first-quarter window. It should be read within that period rather than set against counts the post gives for other groups.

No single intrusion vector has been tied to Krybit. Smarttech247's section on the group does not link it to any one method of getting into a victim's network.

Payload is one of six ransomware operations that security firm Smarttech247 profiled as having emerged or resurfaced in 2026. The vendor reports Payload's activity as of late April 2026.

That late-April cutoff applies to Payload's figures alone. Other groups in the same analysis are measured over different windows. Payload's numbers are not comparable with theirs and cannot be added to them.

Insomnia is a data-extortion group that Smarttech247 describes as having no encryptor at all. Its leverage over a victim comes from the data it steals, not from locking files.

This sets Insomnia apart from a conventional ransomware operator. A group with an encryptor makes a victim's files unreadable and disrupts its systems. Insomnia, as Smarttech247 describes it, skips that step. The pressure it applies rests entirely on what it has taken.

Smarttech247's key takeaways tie Insomnia to stolen credentials as a route into victims' networks. In that summary, the vendor places the group among those it says lean on identity to gain access. The claim appears in the takeaways rather than in a detailed account of a specific intrusion.

The vendor pairs Insomnia with one other data-theft operator on its list. For both, its advice to defenders is "Your backups won't save you." That line is Smarttech247's recommendation, not the result of a measured test.

The advice follows from the group's method. Backups help an organization restore files an attacker has encrypted. They cannot recall data an attacker has already copied out of the network.

AiLock is a ransomware group that Smarttech247, a cybersecurity vendor, counts among the threat actors that emerged in 2026. The vendor's figure for AiLock reflects the group's activity as of early June 2026.

That early-June date is the only reporting period the source attaches to AiLock. Activity after that point falls outside what the profile covers.

Deadlock is a ransomware group that resurfaced in June 2026 after an earlier period of activity, rather than appearing for the first time. Smarttech247 counts 75 victims named by the group in a single month.

That return puts Deadlock among the threat actors the vendor describes as re-emerging during the year. It is an existing name back in circulation, not a new label.

The Smarttech247 profile also lists a driver exploit among the methods Deadlock uses.

Settra is a ransomware group that tries to pass itself off as a legitimate business, according to Smarttech247. The cybersecurity vendor lists it among six ransomware threat actors that emerged or re-emerged in 2026.

That effort to look legitimate is the main trait the vendor attaches to Settra.

Little has been published about the group's technical tooling. No Settra encryptor has been publicly analyzed, according to Smarttech247.

An encryptor is the program a ransomware group uses to lock a victim's files. With no public analysis available, there is no published technical breakdown of how Settra encrypts data.

The lack of an analysis does not mean Settra has no encryptor. It means only that no Settra encryption tool has been examined in public.
