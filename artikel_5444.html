National Home Value Growth Holds Steady Yet Miami Outperforms

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

National growth in home values was flat month over month in September as mortgage rates accelerated and inflation continued running hot.

The value of single-family homes as measured by repeat transactions increased 2.1% year over year, same as in August, according to data from the S&P Cotality Case-Shiller Index released Tuesday.

However, home values fell in real terms for the 16th consecutive month, as September's 3.7% inflation rate outpaced the home value gain by roughly 1.6 percentage points, up from about 1.3 points in August.

"Nominal home prices remain resilient, but inflation is once again widening the gap between nominal and real housing returns," says Rebecca Kaufman, associate director of commodities at S&P Dow Jones Indices.

Across the 20 cities tracked by the index, Chicago yet again led the U.S. with a 6.9% annual gain. New York took second place with a year-over-year increase of 5.8%, but Miami emerged as the month's surprise, finishing third with a 4.6% gain.

"Miami’s rise is particularly notable," points out Kaufman. "After moving into fourth place among the 20 cities in July and August, it has now climbed into the top three, a sharp reversal from its comparatively weak performance in 2025."

On the other side of the spectrum, Seattle recorded the largest annual decline for the fourth consecutivemonth at 1.9%, followed by Denver (-1.0%) and Dallas (-0.9%), reflecting a persistent regional divergence.

September also revealed a widening gap between seasonal weakness and underlying price momentum. On a non-seasonally adjusted basis, the U.S. National and 10-City Composite Indices fell 0.3% and 0.5% month over month, respectively, while both rose 0.2% after seasonal adjustment.

"That contrast suggests that some of the apparent monthly weakness reflects the normal fall slowdown rather than a broad deterioration in home prices," adds Kaufman.

The latest Case-Shiller release reflects sales closing from July through September, a period when mortgage rates moved higher, with Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed rate averaging 6.54% in July, near 6.7% in August, and 6.86% in September.

Realtor.com® senior economist Anthony Smith notes that the housing market's momentum has lost steam in recent months. Contract activity softened over the same stretch, with pending home sales down 4.7% from a year earlier in August before setting a series record low in September.

"Looking ahead, the rate and demand backdrop has become more challenging since September closed," says Smith. After four weeks of declines, the 30-year fixed rate jumped back to 7.28% as of Nov. 19, pushed higher by hotter-than-expected October inflation and expectations for a Fed rate hike in December."

The median existing-home sales price rose just 0.4% from a year ago in October, while asking prices were down 2.2% and months' supply reached its highest level since 2015.

"If financing costs stay elevated into the winter, the plateau in price growth could give way to renewed softening, particularly in supply-heavy Southern and Western markets," notes Smith.

The Case-Shiller Index reports on a two-month delay and reflects a three-month moving average of home sales prices.

Homes usually go under contract a month or two before they close, so the September report primarily reflects purchase decisions made in late summer.

Although the Index's price data is delayed by several months, it is considered one of the best available measures of changing home values, because it is based on repeat transactions on the same properties.
