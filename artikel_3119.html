Prediction: Nvidia Stock Will Do This in the 6 Months After a New CEO Takes Over, According to History

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Nvidia is searching for a new CEO following Jensen Huang's abrupt resignation.

By Adam Spatacco – Nov 20, 2026 at 6:42PM EST

Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) is navigating one of the most consequential leadership transitions in recent Silicon Valley history. Following founder Jensen Huang's unexpected resignation amid a controversy involving alleged connections to Chinese intelligence, investors are growing increasingly concerned about the company's future. This uncertainty is weighing heavily on Nvidia stock, even as its underlying business continues to deliver extraordinary financial results.

Colette Kress, Nvidia's former chief financial officer, is now serving as interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor. According to reports, the company is considering external candidates, including Arm Holdings CEO Rene Haas.

History offers some clues about how technology stocks behave following major CEO transitions. However, Nvidia's circumstances are quite different from those of its peers, making the company's financial outlook particularly important right now.

Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%) represents a successful example of a technology leadership transition. Satya Nadella became CEO on Feb. 4, 2014, replacing Steve Ballmer after Microsoft conducted a formal succession search. Nadella had previously led Microsoft's cloud and enterprise businesses, giving him extensive knowledge of the company's operations.

Microsoft stock advanced roughly 19% during Nadella's first six months as CEO. Of course, over the long term, he helped transform the Windows developer into a cloud computing powerhouse.

Alphabet (GOOGL -2.70%) (GOOG -2.59%) offers another example. Sundar Pichai became CEO of Alphabet on Dec. 3, 2019, succeeding co-founder Larry Page. However, Pichai had already served as Google's CEO since 2015, making the transition more of a consolidation of leadership responsibilities than an unexpected management overhaul.

Alphabet stock was higher roughly six months later, despite experiencing a meaningful drawdown during the short-lived COVID-19 market crash. Moreover, Page and his co-founder Sergey Brin remained involved as shareholders and directors, providing continuity that Nvidia currently lacks.

Amazon (AMZN -4.10%) experienced a different outcome when Andy Jassy succeeded Jeff Bezos on July 5, 2021. Jassy was another internal executive who had previously built and led Amazon Web Services (AWS). Although Bezos relinquished the CEO position, he remains executive chairman, preserving his influence over the company's long-term strategy.

Amazon shares declined approximately 6% during Jassy's first six months as CEO. At the time, investors were assessing the impacts of slowing pandemic-era growth and rising operating expenses on Amazon's e-commerce marketplace.

The three examples above show that CEO succession can bring different short-term outcomes. However, they also share an important characteristic: Each of these companies promoted an experienced internal executive through an orderly transition, unlike Nvidia's abrupt leadership disruption.

Nvidia's situation is unusual because Huang wasn't just another corporate executive. He co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and spent more than three decades directing the company's evolution from a graphics chip manufacturer into the world's leading supplier of artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure. His influence extended across engineering, product development, corporate culture, and customer relationships.

As a result of his departure, investors are increasingly questioning whether Nvidia can maintain its technological edge without the executive who helped establish it. The uncertainty is compounded by growing competition from custom AI accelerators developed by Alphabet, Amazon, and other hyperscalers. Nvidia's next CEO needs to prove they can preserve the company's innovation-driven culture while navigating complicated supply chains and regulatory relationships.

Despite the unusual circumstances, Nvidia's financial outlook provides a compelling reason to anticipate a meaningful recovery. During its fiscal third-quarter earnings call, management increased its fiscal 2028 revenue growth forecast to 75%, up from its previous outlook of 70%. This suggests demand for Nvidia's accelerated computing platforms remains strong despite Huang's departure.

Furthermore, Nvidia expects to generate $130 billion in revenue during the fourth quarter -- representing 91% growth year over year. The company is also forecasting gross margins of between 72% and 73% in fiscal 2028, suggesting that accelerating sales growth will translate into additional profits.

Nvidia's share repurchase program provides another subtle catalyst for the stock. In September, the board increased the company's existing authorization by $150 billion, leaving approximately $227.3 billion available at the end of the third quarter. Management intends to execute the remaining authorization by the end of fiscal 2028.

By reducing the number of outstanding shares, stock buybacks can increase earnings per share (EPS) even if net income is unchanged. Combined with Nvidia's revenue growth and profit margins, upcoming share repurchases should fuel substantial EPS expansion over the coming year.

My prediction is that Nvidia stock will trade higher six months after its new CEO assumes the role. The appointment itself should remove the primary source of uncertainty around the company, allowing investors to concentrate once again on revenue growth, earnings power, and competitive advantages.
