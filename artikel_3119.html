Polaroid’s Portable Printer Got Me Right Back Into Journaling

Polaroid’s little photo printer helps me quickly save memories with superb photo quality.

I loved journaling as a child. It helped me survive hormonal changes, the usual teen dramas, plus a hefty dose of existential angst. Then college called, and I moved to a different country, leaving behind my childhood, my Nirvana cassettes (I’m old, OK?) and, sadly, my journaling habit.

I made multiple attempts to return to journaling throughout the years, with varying success. I’ve delved into bullet journals under the pretense of improving my productivity, but I gave up when it became too much effort. I’ve invested in multiple notebooks and planners, only to eventually neglect them.

Recently, however, I found a way for me to actually stick to the habit: a $110 Polaroid Hi-Print pocket printer.

It all began when I discovered the wondrous world of journaling TikTok. In short-form videos, creators would show off beautiful journal pages decorated with colored markers, stickers, washi tape and, yes, photos — specifically photos from portable printers because the prints are small enough to fit within the confines of a typical journal or planner. I really liked the idea of decking out my journal pages with photos of my everyday life. I knew I had to get one.

After a bit of research, I decided on a couple of criteria. First, I wanted the printer to be a dye sublimation printer rather than a ZINK (Zero Ink) printer. Dye sublimation printers essentially use heat to transfer dye onto the paper, while ZINK printers use a special paper that activates under heat with no ink required (hence the name). Dye sublimation prints tend to be higher quality because it transfers the dye in layers of cyan, magenta and yellow, followed by a protective coating.

Second, I wanted the photo to be on sticky paper so that I didn’t have to mess around with glue to add these photos to my journal.

I found quite a number of dye sublimation pocket printers on the market, but the one that caught my eye immediately was the Polaroid Hi-Print 2×3 Generation 2 pocket printer. I fell in love with it the moment I saw it. The sleek white design, the Polaroid logo and that curved rainbow accent on the edge really sealed the deal for me. Call me a sucker for style, I guess.

I purchased a Hi-Print 2×3 “starter set,” which includes the printer along with two 2×3 paper cartridges. Each cartridge has 10 photo sheets, and each sheet is made out of sticky back photo paper, which is exactly what I wanted. Both the Gen 2 and the prior Gen 1 are easy to find; the main difference is the newer one uses a USB-C charging port while the original uses the older Micro-USB port.

Getting started with the printer is simple enough: Download the Polaroid Hi-Print app, pair the printer with your phone and you’re ready to go. The app is fairly easy to navigate. I like that it lets you arrange your photos in collages so you’re not limited to just one photo per sheet. You can also decorate your prints with colorful frames or decorations. The printer even offers a mosaic option that lets you print out one big image using multiple sheets.

Since it’s a dye sublimation printer, the Hi-Print prints out photos in stages. It lays down the different colors in multiple passes (yellow in the first pass, magenta in the second pass, and so forth). Because of that, printing does take a while: around 60 seconds per photo.

But I don’t mind the delay, as the resulting photo quality is superb. The colors are vibrant and the overall image is nice and sharp. The contrast could be a little better, but for a little pocket printer, the quality definitely exceeded my expectations.

There are a few downsides, however. The obvious one is that photo cartridges can be pretty expensive. One pack of 20 sheets is around $17, though you can get it for $14 each if you buy the 5-pack. That’s still almost 70 cents per 2×3 print. If you use multiple prints, that can add up pretty quickly.

Another downside is that it doesn’t have a super long battery life. It only has a 620mAh battery, which Polaroid says will last up to 20 photo printing sessions. I only ever use this printer at home, so this isn’t a big deal for me, but I can see this being a potential disappointment if you want to bring this printer on the road. That said, if you do want to charge it, it doesn’t take that long. Polaroid says it takes under an hour, and that feels about right based on my experience.

I’ve had this printer for a few months now, and I’m very pleased with it. Since I use the photos primarily with my journal, I also really appreciate how thin the photo sheets are, so they don’t bulk up the pages too much. I truly enjoy printing out my little creations, cutting out my collages and then decorating my journal with them.

I might not have my childhood back nor my Nirvana cassettes, but at least I’ve finally recaptured the joys of journaling — even if it did require a bit of help from a pocket printer.
