Billionaire Bill Ackman Sold 2 Million Shares of Microsoft Stock. But Here's Why He Says He's Still Bullish

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Pershing Square's latest filing shows a big cut in its Microsoft stake, but Ackman says it omits what he bought instead.

By Jonathan Ponciano – Nov 18, 2026 at 5:22PM EST

At first glance, Pershing Square's latest quarterly holdings report might suggest that billionaire Bill Ackman soured on Microsoft (MSFT +0.41%) stock. But he says that's the wrong read, and his reasoning makes perfect sense.

The filing shows his firm held 4,064,779 Microsoft shares on Sept. 30, down from 6,206,730 three months earlier. That's a cut of about 2.1 million shares, or roughly a third of the position.

On Wednesday, Ackman explained on X what the filing leaves out. In July, Pershing Square bought 2 million "deep-in-the-money" options on Microsoft, with a strike price of about $310 a share and a term of two and a half years. Those options were bought over the counter, which excludes them from the quarterly filing.

"We remain bullish on Microsoft and believe the stock remains undervalued," he wrote.

Count the options, and Pershing Square's bet on Microsoft is about the same size as before: roughly 4.1 million shares plus options on 2 million more, compared with 6.2 million shares in June.

A deep-in-the-money option behaves much like the underlying stock. With Microsoft at $506.24 at Tuesday's close, an option to buy at $310 rises and falls nearly dollar-for-dollar with the shares. But it costs less up front than buying the stock outright, which frees up cash for other ideas. Ackman said only that the firm occasionally makes the swap "for portfolio management or other reasons."

That said, if Microsoft were to fall below $310 before the options expire, they could end up worthless, a risk that owning the shares doesn't carry.

Microsoft wasn't the only move. Pershing Square opened new positions in Alcon (ALC +1.55%), worth about $954 million, and Intercontinental Exchange (ICE -0.24%), worth about $1 billion. It sold its small remaining stake in Hertz (HTZ -3.70%).

It also trimmed several of its biggest holdings. The firm sold about 11% of its Meta Platforms (META -0.40%) shares, 11% of its Mastercard (MA -0.36%), 12% of its Visa (V -0.37%), 11% of its Brookfield (BN -0.51%) and 15% of its Restaurant Brands International (QSR +0.07%). The one big addition was Netflix (NFLX +0.23%), where it raised its stake by about 18% to 15.4 million shares.

Its largest positions by value at the end of September were Uber Technologies (UBER -0.07%) at $2.35 billion, Amazon (AMZN +3.82%) at $2.12 billion, Microsoft at $2.08 billion and Meta at $2.05 billion.

The lesson is to be careful with 13F filings. They show only the stock and listed options held at the end of each quarter, and they are released weeks later. Private options deals, short positions, and anything bought or sold since then don't appear. Simply comparing filings might yield a headline saying a famous investor "dumped" a stock, when in reality, the conviction hasn't changed.

Ultimately, Ackman's Microsoft bet hasn't really shrunk, and long-term investors can still consider this a vote of confidence from one of Wall Street's best-known stock pickers.
