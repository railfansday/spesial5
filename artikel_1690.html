On The Road: Overpowered by County, but Accies are making small steps in the long road back from the abyss

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Published: 08:28 EST, 15 November 2026 | Updated: 10:25 EST, 15 November 2026

The depth of the descent of Hamilton Accies and Ross County would make it understandable if tickets for a fixture between the two carried warning about the bends when leaving New Douglas Park.

The decompression sickness caused by emerging from a League One fixture for two sides who should be much higher must be severe. There may, however, be a positive prognosis.

Accies, despite their defeat, are whispering about stability, even though ‘legacy debt’ has to be faced, perhaps contested.

County, meanwhile, will not say it even in a whisper but Saturday’s victory takes them eight points clear at the top and heavily fancied for the title. The Staggies have one set of hooves in the Championship.

Scottish football, of course, does not restrict itself to the cliched fare of top of the table clashes so New Douglas Park hosted a striker who is 39 later this month, a winger who was once a Scottish international, and a gentleman in the stands who once played against George Best. And a Ross County fan who regularly travels up from North Wales.

These exotic avenues demand to be explored but perennial questions about the financial health of the Accies had to be asked, too, after reports this week stating that hospitality packages at the club were being suspended.

An Accies fan feels the pain of defeat during Saturday's home loss to Ross County

Allan Currie, chairman, answered them over a half-time pie.

‘We have come on in leaps and bounds but every week seems to throw up different challenges,’ he said. ‘We have a level of debt that we have inherited and we are working with the creditors to try to navigate our way through that.’

Matters on the field are positive, despite Saturday’s defeat.

‘The guys on the pitch, manager, and his staff have been unbelievable. Surviving in this league last season was phenomenal given what they were going through but it couldn’t be better now,’ he said. ‘Off the pitch, we are fighting with the different challenges we have. We are in a much better place even if we are not out the woods yet.’

Asked what the level of pressure on him, he said: ‘I am the guy on the ground but Rob Edwards is carrying out negotiations.’

Edwards, through his Morley Group, took over the club earlier this year but has been sideswiped by a number of claims for payment. However, Currie has faith in the owner.

‘Without him, the club would not survive. Rob should take all the credit,’ he said.

Asked what the short-term aims for the club were, Currie replied with a smile: ‘A goal in the second half would be good.’ A goal was scored but not for the Accies.

But the club remain in second place and the play-offs seem a more than reasonable prospect.

Ross County goalscorer Kieran Phillips battles with Hamilton's Kai Smutek

With 10 minutes to go, Hamilton brought on their latest signing. Barrie McKay, who has played for Scotland and scored for Rangers in a Scottish Cup semi-final against Celtic. His considerable achievements, though, paled in comparison to the subject of a casual conversation in the bar before the match.

‘I played against George Best,’ said Alan McDougall, with an air so off-hand one could have believed he was merely ordering another pint.

‘Yes, I played 200 games for the Accies. Davie McParland brought me here as a 21-year-old and I played for about six years. I went to Stranraer for a year and then went down south. I played against Best with the Accies.’

The Northern Irish genius played for Hibernian in 1979-80. ‘He was well past his best and that was sad,’ said McDougall. Best’s addiction to alcohol had taken a severe toll.

‘I came back to watch games five or six years ago,’ said McDougall. ‘When the club went to Clyde, it destroyed me. What is fantastic is that the people that are now in control seem to be turning it around.’

There was a mood of cautious optimism from other fans. ‘I have been a fan since forever, well the 1980s at least,’ said Gavin Fullarton. ‘I have seen this team in every division. I have seen them in the downs and downs. But I think we are going in the right direction. So far.’

He added: ‘The new guys seem to know what they are doing. They are communicative.’

There were 1,216 fans in the ground on Saturday with 300 stalwarts travelling down from the north. Colin Richardson, an Accies supporter, was asked why so many had stuck by the club.

‘It’s about loyalty,’ he said. ‘You can’t pick and choose when you support your team. At the moment it is all about small steps. It has to be about stability.’

A Ross County fan had travelled up from North Wales. The journey to Scotland is a familiar one for Ian Ellson. ‘I have been all over Scotland to watch Ross County,’ he said. ‘Twenty years ago I visited Ross County and I was spoiled rotten. I decided to follow them.’

A social worker, he averages one visit a month. ‘I live and breathe Ross County,’ he said. ‘This season we are going up.’

Welsh Staggies fan Ian Elson and Englishman Ray Griffiths travelled far for Saturday's game

Brian Graham would not be so bullish. He is 39 this month and has learned enough about football to know that predictions are made only to make the speaker seem foolish down the line.

He was, though, expansive about what is happening at County. He joined the club for the second time in the summer after Stuart Kettlewell, the manager, convinced him of the ambitions in Dingwall.

‘This is a club where I have had success before so I know how good a club it can be,’ he said. ‘It’s not been great for a couple of seasons but in the summer the manager set out his stall very early. It was about leaving egos at the door, building a togetherness and having a change of culture at the club. I think you are seeing that now.’

The early signs were good for the striker who dropped down from Falkirk in the Premiership to play in Dingwall.

‘We had good pre-season performances against Aberdeen and Dundee and that was a solid building block,’ he said.

Brian Graham celebrates after scoring his 21st goal of the season for County

Graham converted a first-half penalty to reach a tally of 21 goals this season and the Christmas trees are not up yet. ‘I get a lot of plaudits for scoring goals but others in the team are scoring a lot too,’ he said. ‘We are scoring team goals.’

Has he set a target for the season? ‘When I came back I knew I had to score 13 to reach 50 goals for the club in two spells,’ he said. ‘I always set down targets in a wee black book but now I might need to readjust that.’

It was a night of adjustment for Darian MacKinnon. He had set a victory target but was condemned to accept defeat. He would not concede the league race was over. ‘We have to play them twice,’ he said.

He added ‘We are going in the right direction. The owners have put money in and they have backed us.’

Asked to pinpoint a factor that has contributed to the team sitting second, he said: ‘The most positive thing for me this season is that this is now a good place to come into your work after the last three or four seasons. We are all actually enjoying coming to our work.’

There is work to be done on and off the pitch but the Accies are surviving. And that is the biggest result.
