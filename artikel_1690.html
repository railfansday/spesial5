Wirtz feeling confident heading into crucial Merseyside Derby

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Florian Wirtz insisted confidence is coursing through Liverpool's veins ahead of this Sunday's Merseyside derby following a superb midweek in the Champions League.

Liverpool boosted their hopes of a top-eight finish in the competition with a 3-0 win over Club Brugge, with Wirtz's 79th-minute strike coming either side of Alexander Isak's brace.

The Reds have now won their last three matches in all competitions, with their last Premier League encounter seeing them thrash rivals Manchester United 4-0 at Anfield.

And Wirtz has been key to that run of form. After a fruitful international break with Germany and former Liverpool boss Jurgen Klopp, he has now scored in three consecutive appearances for the first time since moving to Merseyside last summer.

Indeed, it's the first time he has achieved the feat overall at club level since September 2024 for Bayer Leverkusen, while Isak will fancy his chances of scoring away from home.

Among all Premier League players, Isak has scored the joint-most away goals across all competitions this season (eight – level with Erling Haaland). His total is already more than any Liverpool player managed away from home in the 2025-26 campaign (six by Cody Gakpo, Dominik Szoboszlai and Virgil van Dijk).

"We have so many good players in our team and I think even when we can substitute some wingers or strikers in the 60th minute or later, I think that gives us a lot," Wirtz said of Liverpool's attack ahead of making the short trip to Hill Dickinson Stadium.

"I think it shows how good our team is, and I think it's important for a top team to also have a strong bench to bring on good players.

"I think the last two games were really good, and it's always good to go into such a game with two games like this. We're really happy about this and confidence is there for sure."

Everton, meanwhile, come into Sunday's fixture off the back of a 4-0 defeat away to Brentford in what was David Moyes' 593rd game in charge of the Toffees.

Keane Lewis-Potter scored twice in two minutes before captain James Tarkowski was sent off for the first time in the Premier League five minutes before the break.

A stunner from Vitaly Janelt and a goal from substitute Fabio Carvalho compounded Moyes to his biggest Premier League defeat while in charge of Everton since losing 1-6 at home to Arsenal on the opening matchday of the 2009-10 season.

But with rivals Liverpool up next, Everton defender Jake O'Brien believes the Merseyside derby provides the Toffees with the ideal motivation to bounce back.

"It was really bad from start to finish," O'Brien said after the Brentford defeat. "It was a knock-on effect of decisions and mistakes, and it carried on from there, really.

"There's no excuses. We were really bad, but I think we can put this behind us.

"We’re much better than this and we know we’ve got big games coming up, especially [against Liverpool]. It was a really bad game and we apologise to all the fans who travelled.

"But I think we can put this behind us, and there’s no better game to come in firing and change things around."

Despite failing to get on the scoresheet against Brentford, Barry still proved to be a handful in the final third, and Everton will need their top scorer at his very best.

Barry has scored (five) or assisted (one) six of the Toffees' last seven league goals, though he has failed to net in his two previous Premier League games against Liverpool.

Wirtz has scored and assisted a goal in his last two Premier League appearances, and after a positive display in the Champions League, he will look to do the same here.

The only Liverpool player to do so three times in a row is Mohamed Salah, who did it twice in December 2018 and October 2021.

Liverpool have lost just one of their last 15 Premier League away games against Everton (W4 D10), with 10 of their last 14 visits finishing level.

The Reds have scored six 90th-minute winning goals against Everton in the Premier League, more than any side has against another. This exact fixture last season was settled by Virgil van Dijk's stoppage-time winner.

Iraola's side have also won each of their last four Premier League away games. They last had a longer run between May and September 2021 (five) and last did so within the same season between November 2019 and February 2020 (eight).

Everton, meanwhile, have won two of their last three Premier League games, though they lost 4-0 to Brentford last time out. It's as many victories as they'd managed in their previous 15 (D7 L6).

The Toffees have won just two of their last 31 Premier League games against Liverpool (D14 L15), beating them 2-0 at Anfield in February 2021 and 2-0 at Goodison Park in April 2024.

And you can expect a feisty encounter here. Everton vs Liverpool has had more red cards than any other fixture in Premier League history (25), while the Toffees hold the record for most red cards overall in the competition (114).
