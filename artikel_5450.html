Goldman Sachs admits the ‘AI unwind’ has spiraled into something more dangerous — and it’s watching for 3 signs of bottom

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The S&P 500 closed Friday in a correction, falling 3.59% to 6,918.45 for its seventh straight loss and its longest losing streak since February 2020. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.28% and is now down more than 2% for the year. Goldman Sachs notes the index is 12% below its Oct. 6 record, and its strategists have named the three things they’d need to see before calling a bottom.

Snider’s note said the sell-off “expanded this week from an AI unwind to a broader growth downgrade,” noting that the S&P 500 excluding AI had held up until this week and is now 6% below its high. Goldman’s AI stock basket is down 32% since Oct. 6. Cyclical stocks have lagged defensives by 5 percentage points over the past 10 days.

Goldman’s note was written before Friday’s rout was fully in its data. Its exhibits are priced as of Nov. 19 and its hedge fund data run through that date. So the question is whether Friday supplied what the note was looking for — the situation could be even worse in markets than it acknowledged.

Goldman said any one of the following would support a bottom and a recovery: news that raises confidence in AI earnings, a dovish Fed signal, or a big drop in investor positioning.

Condition 1: AI earnings. This is the one the market is trying to test. Investor Dan Niles told CNBC that a price war between Anthropic and OpenAI is hurting both, noting their combined sequential revenue growth fell from 75% in the second quarter to 48% in the third, while combined losses rose from $7.9 billion to about $8.4 billion. Reuters cited a valuation of about 40 times trailing sales for Anthropic. A US Bank Asset Management executive said the valuation “assumes years of rapid growth.” Goldman’s own outlook says the AI outlook for 2028 and beyond “is highly uncertain.”

Condition 2: the Fed. Futures put the odds of a December hike at about 95%, and the Fed meets Dec. 9. Goldman’s economists expect hikes in December and March, sending the federal funds rate to a peak of 4.25% to 4.5%. A dovish signal looks unlikely soon. Treasuries did rally sharply Friday as investors looked for safety, which is not the same as a Fed pivot. Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson said Thursday that the Fed will need to raise rates again because inflation is too high. The 10-year Treasury yield is near 5%.

Condition 3: A big drop in investor positioning. This has often marked the end of past drawdowns.

The first condition is unsettled, to say the least. Anthropic’s historic IPO turned into a sort of referendum on the entire AI trade, an unfair weight to put on a single, albeit remarkably important, company. The company raised $93.5 billion after underwriters exercised an option, passing SpaceX’s $85.7 billion as the biggest IPO on record. The shares jumped 17% on Thursday, then gave most of it back Friday to close at $88.91, not far above the $83 IPO price. “Anthropic faded relatively quickly,” said Dakota Wealth’s Robert Pavlik. “I don’t think investors believe there’s something inherently wrong with the company… I think they’re just skeptical of this market.” Morgan Stanley, the underwriter tasked with stabilizing the shares, closed its support position, removing a potential pillar of support.

UBS said the debate had shifted from whether AI investment will continue to whether it can generate enough revenue and returns for shareholders. A Capital Economics economist wrote that the IPO’s success while other AI stocks fell shows the parts of the AI ecosystem “no longer move as one,” as chipmakers Nvidia, Broadcom, Micron and AMD fell roughly 12% to 14%.

Nvidia’s 14% fall on Friday was its worst day in almost two years. It lost 22.9% on the week and is down 47.5% since Oct. 6, a loss of $2.7 trillion in market value. Reuters reported the SEC is probing its accounting practices. Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on Nov. 12, and CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. The VIX, Wall Street’s “Fear Gauge,” closed at 35.89, its highest in 19 months.

“It’s a meltdown right now,” said Arun Sundaram of CFRA Research. JPMorgan Asset Management’s David Kelly took the other view: “This is not a fatal illness. It’s not even the flu.”

On the third condition, Goldman’s note says hedge funds have already cut back. Their net leverage is the lowest since April 2025. A companion report, Snider’s Hedge Fund Trend Monitor, says the most popular hedge fund longs were crowded into the AI trade and have lagged the S&P 500 by 14 percentage points over six months. That is among the worst stretches in the basket’s 20-year history. The note described the situation for hedge funds as “bruised and broadening.”

But Goldman’s broader Sentiment Indicator is still above average. U.S. equity mutual funds and ETFs have taken in nearly $170 billion in the fourth quarter so far. Asset managers’ net equity futures positions are in the 82nd percentile of the past 10 years. Goldman’s equity strategists estimate the AI boom has lifted S&P 500 earnings 25% above trend. Goldman Research says AI capital spending produced “about half” of this year’s earnings growth.

The Street is split on whether this is the top. Capital Economics expects further declines. Goldman’s Nov. 17 outlook, written before the drop, forecast 8,700 by the end of 2027. In the Nov. 20 note, its range runs from about 5,800 to 8,100 depending on whether investors regain confidence in earnings. From Friday’s close, that is roughly 16% downside and 17% upside. Those percentages are my math, not Goldman’s.

After the 22 S&P 500 corrections since 1980, the index averaged a 4% return over the next three months. It rose in 17 of them. E*Trade’s research is less reassuring. Of the 14 times since 1983, the index first closed 10% or more below a record, five became bear markets.
