Dodgers are backed into a corner with Shohei Ohtani after losing Tarik Skubal

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Shohei Ohtani doesn't have a choice anymore.

No more questions about his continued viability as a two-way player. No more arguments about how he should transition into a full-time hitter.

The moment Tarik Skubal agreed to a nine-year, $450-million contract with the Mets, Ohtani's vanity project of returning to the mound became a necessity.

Los Angeles Dodgers two-way player Shohei Ohtani throws during a spring training workout in Glendale, Ariz., Friday, Feb. 13, 2026. JASON SZENES FOR CA POST

The Dodgers' upcoming season will depend on Ohtani doing what he failed to do this year.

They need him to be a dominant hitter in the playoffs. They also need him to be a frontline pitcher.

Even before Skubal decided to move on, this was what Ohtani envisioned for next season.

"I intend on doing everything," Ohtani said in Japanese on the night the Dodgers were eliminated by the Milwaukee Brewers in the National League Championship Series.

The Dodgers backed his plan, but in doing so, opened themselves up to the possibility of a nightmare scenario in which they would once again enter the postseason with a broken-down version of him who couldn't pitch and could barely hit.

Skubal's departure compromised the unspoken failsafe that would at least preserve Ohtani as an offensive force. The consequences of removing him from the rotation have escalated by several orders of magnitude.

Losing Ohtani as a pitcher will now be as devastating as losing him as a hitter.

Ohtani will be 33 next year, five seasons removed from the last time he was a two-way player from start to finish.

The Dodgers will have to be smarter in how they manage Ohtani than they were this year, when arm and knee problems kept him from pitching after the All-Star break and transformed him into a glorified decoy as a hitter in the postseason.

Ohtani will share the responsibility of ensuring he doesn't break down. He has to be able to better recognize the signals his body sends him. He has to improve his communication with the training staff.

The solution isn't necessarily to cut down on how often Ohtani pitches. If he doesn't pitch enough in the regular season, he might not be ready to make the kinds of starts that will be demanded of him in the playoffs.

Failure by the team or player to strike the right balance will almost certainly close the championship window on this particular Dodgers team – the one that counts veterans such as Mookie Betts, Freddie Freeman, Max Muncy, and, yes, Ohtani as parts of its nucleus.

Los Angeles Dodgers pitcher Tarik Skubal talks to reporters in Milwaukee, Saturday, Oct. 10, 2026. AP Photo/Kyla Wolf

Unlike this year, when the Dodgers were able to replace Ohtani in their rotation by acquiring Skubal, there aren't expected to be any ace-caliber pitchers available at the trade deadline next year.

Considering the stakes, it's a mystery why the Dodgers didn't do whatever was required of them to retain Skubal. A two-time Cy Young Award winner, the 30-year-old left-hander would have decreased their reliance on Ohtani and provided a fundamental building block around which they could have built the next iteration of their team.

Los Angeles Dodgers Shohei Ohtani hits a three-run home run against the Milwaukee Brewers in Game 4 of the NLCS on Thursday, Oct. 15, 2026, in Los Angeles. Wally Skalij for the CA Post

The Dodgers are stuck with the alternative.

Come October, they will have to count on Ohtani to energize an offense that was dormant as they were swept by the Brewers in the NLCS. They will have to depend on Ohtani to step into Skubal's place in a starting-pitching triumvirate alongside Yoshinobu Yamamoto and Blake Snell.

When healthy, Ohtani is the best offensive player in baseball. He made the case this year that under such conditions, he might also be the best pitcher, as he registered a 0.74 earned-run average in his first 10 starts of the season.

For the four or five weeks of the playoffs, can Ohtani simultaneously perform at his maximum capabilities as both a hitter and pitcher?

For the Dodgers, that dream is now a requirement.
