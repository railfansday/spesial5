Docusign vs. Figma: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Two software giants with very different financial profiles, one a cash-generating incumbent and the other a fast-growing disruptor, both vying for corporate software budgets.

By Brendan Coffey – Updated Nov 23, 2026 at 12:11PM EST

Software investing often involves choosing between established, profitable leaders and high-growth disruptors. Deciding whether to back Docusign (DOCU +3.03%) or Figma (FIG +7.71%) requires weighing mature cash flows against aggressive market expansion.

Docusign transformed how contracts are signed, moving beyond simple digital signatures into end-to-end agreement management. Figma redefined digital product design by enabling real-time collaboration for creative teams directly in the browser. Both companies compete for space in corporate software budgets but offer vastly different financial profiles for investors looking ahead to 2027.

Docusign provides intelligent agreement management software to help organizations prepare, sign, and manage contracts. The company serves over 1.8 million customers globally and remains a prominent name among tech stocks. It maintains strategic partnerships with organizations such as Microsoft Corp.(MSFT +2.96%), SAP (SAP +2.24%), and Salesforce.com Inc. (CRM +2.20%) to integrate its services.

In the 12 months ended July 31, 2026, revenue reached about $3.4 billion, which represents growth of approximately 8.6% compared to the prior period. The company reported net income of around $329.9 million during this same time frame, demonstrating its ability to maintain profitability while expanding its product suite. This consistent financial performance provides a foundation for the company as it rolls out new agreement management tools.

As of its January 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.1x, which compares total debt to shareholder equity. The current ratio is nearly 0.7x, measuring the ability to cover short-term liabilities with short-term assets. In the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, free cash flow was about $1.1 billion. Note that stock-based compensation (SBC) accounted for roughly 53.4% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation, since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Figma provides a web-based platform that enables design teams to collaborate on digital products in real time. The company hosts its infrastructure on Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN +4.00%) and serves over 16,000 paid customers with substantial annual recurring revenue. It focuses heavily on global expansion, with about 85% of its monthly active users currently located outside the United States.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $1.4 billion, showing robust revenue growth of approximately 44.4% as adoption of its design tools accelerated. However, the company reported a net loss of nearly $562.5 million for this period, reflecting elevated operating costs and investments in new features. This balance of rapid top-line expansion and significant bottom-line losses is characteristic of high-growth software firms in their scaling phase.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.1x, indicating low leverage relative to equity. The current ratio is nearly 2.6x, indicating the company has sufficient short-term assets to cover its immediate liabilities. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was about $246.2 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 544% of operating cash flow, meaning reported cash generation is heavily inflated by this non-cash add-back.

Docusign faces significant risks from intense market competition, particularly from Adobe Systems Inc. (ADBE +2.21%), and potential disruption from generative AI. Its dependence on the core eSignature product creates exposure if the newer Intelligent Agreement Management platform fails to gain traction. The company also manages ongoing cybersecurity threats and complex global regulatory requirements like the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR.)

Docusign trades at a lower P/S ratio, which uses sales over the past twelve months as the denominator, while Figma carries a higher Forward P/E.

For Docusign, sales in its current fiscal year are seen growing by nearly 9% to $3.5 billion, with a slightly better percentage gain in net income to $346 million.

Docusign's launch and global expansion of its IAM platform, along with the introduction of AI-powered tools, should position the company for future growth. However, the not-so-great projected revenue growth rates suggest that Docusign may be entering a phase of more mature growth and a landscape of increasingly competitive digital agreement and contract management software. In particular, news reports OpenAI would launch a ChatGPT product that would compete head-on with Docusign in the document summary field caused some investor fears. Right now, however, the signature and identity-verification part of Docusign's business appears unthreatened by ChatGPT.

Figma has seen its user experience design solution become the industry standard for digital asset and mobile application development. The company uses this niche dominance to introduce additional modules, helping transform it from a single-point solution into a full design platform.

One differentiator from competitors is that Figma has done an excellent job not only presenting itself to developers, but also to marketers and product managers who have to collaborate on designs. That gives the company more champions in an organization for its products.

For the current fiscal year, revenue should grow quickly, at about 39%, to $1.47 billion. Look for a dramatic reduction in its net loss, from nearly $1.3 billion to about $560 million. Wall Street still sees similarly sized losses ahead for the foreseeable future, however.

Docusign is the less-flashy tech stock in this comparison, with single-digit growth. But its hefty net income is a positive for investors, compared to the foreseeable net losses for Figma. A profitable business with at least something of a competitive moat at a low P/E ratio, like Docusign here, is a safer bet for investors building out their 2027 portfolio.
