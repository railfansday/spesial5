Trump's $50B air traffic control overhaul faces GOP pushback after nationwide ground stop

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy calls on Congress to invest in aviation software and infrastructure upgrades following a nationwide FAA ground stop on 'Hannity.'

The Trump administration is pressing Congress for tens of billions in emergency funding to upgrade the nation’s air traffic control systems, but even Senate Republicans are reluctant to pay the bill.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and President Donald Trump called on lawmakers to shell out $50 billion in funding after a ground stop on Friday caused thousands of flight cancellations and blamed Congress for not doling out money previously to upgrade aging air traffic control communications infrastructure.

But Congress did send $12.5 billion to the Department of Transportation as part of the "big, beautiful bill" last year meant to upgrade the archaic systems, and Republicans want receipts on what happened to the money.

"Only about four [billion] has been spent," Senate Majority Leader John Thune, R-S.D., said. "There was about eight billion of that still sitting around."

Duffy is slated to meet with Senate Republicans behind closed doors on Tuesday where lawmakers expect a full breakdown of what happened and where the money has gone and why, suddenly, the administration is seeking an additional $20 billion.

"I haven't seen how they're gonna use it. I mean, in my business, you know, I spent a lot of money, and I built companies," Sen. Rick Scott, R-Fla., told Fox News Digital. "And I understood what I was going to spend it on before I ever did anything. I'd have a budget and what the timeline is, who's gonna do it, all that stuff."

Data from the Department of Transportation (DOT) obtained by Fox News Digital showed that roughly $3.7 billion had been spent on a variety of projects, while the remainder is tied to contracts, unspent or planned for near-term upgrades, like facility consolidation.

Thune declined to say whether he had confidence in Duffy’s handling of the situation and noted that the administration had already made a separate ask for $30 billion, a chunk of which was meant to continue upgrades to air traffic control.

"I mean, there's some explanations that have to be given as to why that number is where it is," Thune said. "But I think we have attempted to do everything we can to ensure that the concerns they have about modernization are being met."

Duffy’s explanation of the ground stop was attributed to replacing copper wiring in communications equipment with fiber-optic cable. About $4.8 billion of the initial funding pot was for copper wire replacement, of which about $2.3 billion has been spent, which translates to roughly 70% completion of the telecommunications replacement program, according to the DOT.

A DOT spokesperson told Fox News Digital that the "Department has made clear for the last two years — separate funding is needed" for other work because DOT "does not have the legal flexibility to move funds from one workstream to another."

"He needs to talk to me like I'm a reasonably intelligent 10th grader," Sen. John Kennedy, R-La., said. "The American people are not experts on telecommunications … nor am I."

Kennedy, who is a member of the Transportation, Housing and Urban Development Appropriations Subcommittee, which controls the DOT's purse strings, said he was more than "willing to appropriate money to fix this," but needed to understand the problem first.

"I mean, we had an outage today, some problems with a number of our banks," Kennedy said. "There have been some problems in Europe with the Internet controlling temperatures of grocery stores. I mean, this is happening a lot. I'm not saying there's a connection, but if there is, they need to tell us."

Sen. James Lankford, R-Okla., told Fox News Digital that the initial $12.5 billion was "a starting point — it wasn’t everything."

"We've seen nothing yet of what's the rest of the request," Lankford said. "So that does seem like, ‘Hey, grab a number out of the air. It needs to be big.’"

Lankford added that Duffy needed to give a "postmortem" on what happened, and that whatever the administration is asking for in upgrades needs to be "shovel-ready."

"This is a zero fail operation," he said. "He's keenly aware of that."

Alex Miller is a writer for Fox News Digital covering the U.S. Senate.
