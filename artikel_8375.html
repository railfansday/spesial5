Out of curiosity, I put our information into Claude and asked for a conversion strategy that comes out ahead irrespective of the life expectancy of each person. It modeled about 1,300 combinations of death years, weighted by Social Security actuary information, using fixed 5% market returns. It measured after-tax wealth left at the second death, not just taxes paid, and assumed our heirs pay tax on any remaining IRA.

Converting to the top of the 24% bracket each year came out ahead of doing nothing in about 85–99% of the scenarios. The range depends mostly on our heirs’ tax rate. The losing cases were mainly ones where we both die within about ten years.
Interesting. If you combine those 1300 cases with 1000 Monte Carlo simulations of potential market returns (rather than assuming 5%), you'd get an even more accurate picture. Not sure the feasibility of asking Claude to do 1.3 million simulations, though...
