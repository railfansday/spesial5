Making impossible decisions (Day 158)

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

I couldn’t sleep last night. Normally we don’t make a planning for too long in advance, but the end is coming near. In miles, but also in time. Katahdin closes 15 october, but a lot of locals expect an early winter, so it very well could be closing earlier. It’s keeping me up.

While I couldn’t sleep, I made the planning all the way until Katahdin. I didn’t exactly love what I was seeing. It’s doable, for sure. But it’s all long days, with no room for error. No time for long breaks, no time for fun. What doesn’t help, is that we expect to have two days where we can’t hike because of the upcoming Nor’Easter. Nobody knows how bad it’s going to be, but if it’s bad, we can’t risk our lives. It’s putting more pressure on hiking big days to make up.

I know I can hike those hard and long days, but the bigger question is if I want to. After hiking hard days for so long, I would be lying if I said I wasn’t very burnt out from it. Being out here for so long, you truly get a sense of what is important to you.

I don’t really enjoy “the grind” of the AT. I don’t really care about proving my resilience. It’s not my “why” I’m hiking the trail anymore. I enjoy being in nature, taking time to truly appreciate it and having meaningful conversations and reflecting on life. That’s what this experience has become for me. That’s what gives me meaning and joy here.

I’m not the only one thinking like this. Dreamcatcher and Small Bites have not been enjoying the regimented hiking part of the trail for a longer time now. My dad hasn’t either. Even though he’s just like me and is not a quitter, he has been longing for home a while now and is just brute forcing his way through the trail. Obviously we’re all struggling with what the trail has become for us.

Are we doing what is expected of us, or are we doing what we want?

Dreamcatcher and Small Bites made the decision that they would skip up to Shaw’s hiker hostel in Monson, so they can take more time in the 100 mile wilderness to truly enjoy the last part of trail. I wasn’t sure yet myself. I want to complete the trail fully, but at what cost?

I talked with my dad about it. We reminisced of our time at the Lazy Hiker Brewery back in Franklin, North Carolina. We cheersed our beers and shouted: “it’s vacation!”. The trail hasn’t felt like vacation in a long time though. It feels like a hard job. We looked at our options and decided we should take our time enjoying and reflecting on this crazy adventure, instead of just putting our head down and crushing miles for the sake of crushing miles.

As you can imagine, this is no easy decision. But, we’re joining Dreamcatcher and Small Bites on the plan to skip up to Monson and take our time to really soak in the last part. It felt like a relief. In the car back to trail, we saw another hiker stressed out about the same stuff. We made the right decision. It’s vacation.

After some nice hiking today, we got shuttled to the Maine Roadhouse. Our place to sit out the Nor’Easter tomorrow.
