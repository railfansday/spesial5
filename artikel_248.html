Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia CEO, board chair

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Trump says he spoke to the board and that Huang “did nothing wrong.”

Originally published November 23, 2026

Nvidia’s board of directors has voted to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO and chairman of the board, just 11 days after he resigned in the wake of multiple scandals.

The reinstatement, announced Monday morning, is “effective immediately” and was based in part on “input from senior levels of the United States government” that assured the board members that “Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.”

That assurance comes despite Huang’s admitted affair with a woman who, US intelligence sources believe, was working as a Chinese spy and allegations that Nvidia had subverted export controls meant to keep Nvidia chips from reaching China. It also comes despite multiple developing investigations from multiple different government departments into those and other alleged national security, accounting, and corporate governance failures.

Huang had been seeking a return to Nvidia since shortly after his resignation, reportedly forming a war room to strategize how best to bring it to pass. Last week, Huang posted on social media that “if the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.”

President Trump has also been a major backer of Huang’s return to Nvidia. “I hope the Board of Nvidia rehires Jensen Huang,” he wrote on Truth Social last week. “It would be the best thing for the Company, and also, for the U.S.A.” In a social media post immediately following the reinstatement, Trump called it “the correct and proper decision” and confirmed that “the United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security.”

Appearing on CNBC Monday morning, Trump said that he had spoken to the Nvidia board “directly and indirectly” about Huang’s reinstatement, and that Huang “did nothing wrong.” Losing “the brainpower of someone like [Huang]” would be “a much bigger threat” than anything related to his alleged interactions with China, Trump said.

“We’ve done a very thorough investigation and the investigation showed that… he had bad judgement having to do with somewhat unrelated incidents,” Trump said. “You have to protect our geniuses and there aren’t enough of them.”

Huang’s tenure at Nvidia dates back to the company’s founding in 1993, when he and three cofounders were focused on the then-nascent field of GPU hardware aimed at PC gamers. In the decades since, the highly parallel cores in those GPUs have been embraced first by cryptocurrency miners and then AI companies, propelling the company to its position as the most valuable in the world at its October peak.

Nvidia stock—the price of which had sunk roughly 25 percent since Huang’s resignation was announced and nearly 50 percent since its peak—was up about 30 percent in early morning trading in the immediate aftermath of Huang’s reinstatement announcement.

Huang’s return echoes, in some ways, that of OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, who was fired almost exactly three years ago after the board said it “no longer has confidence in his ability to continue leading OpenAI.” Two weeks later, Altman was back at the helm after many employees threatened a mass revolt and departure if he wasn’t brought back.

In Huang’s case, the revolt following his departure came not from employees but from fellow titans of the tech industry. Executives including Altman, OpenAI President Greg Brockman, xAI’s Elon Musk and Anduril’s Palmer Luckey are among the prominent industry figures who have publicly offered him support in the days since his resignation.

In its statement, the Nvidia board reiterated that it has retained independent counsel and formed a committee of independent directors to investigate what it calls Huang’s targeting by “a foreign intelligence effort.” The company has also formed a National Security and Export Control Committee to “oversee trade compliance and national security risks,” and is seeking to hire a new chief compliance officer.

This story has been updated to reflect Trump’s statements to CNBC.
