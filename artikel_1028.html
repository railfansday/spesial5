Mauricio Pochettino defends decision to stay with USMNT before starting Man United job

Mauricio Pochettino says nothing will distract him from the United States job before he leaves it, as he prepares for his final match in charge of the American side on Tuesday.

The US men's national team (USMNT) coach signed a contract to lead the Americans through to the 2030 World Cup after this summer’s tournament, but last week he reached an agreement to replace Michael Carrick at Manchester United.

His first game with the Premier League club will come quickly, with the team due to face arch-rivals Liverpool at Anfield on Sunday after he joins them on Wednesday.

Pochettino's choice to remain with the US for their Concacaf fixture against Haiti has drawn criticism, but the Argentine has defended his decision.

"For me, it's not difficult. I think we are well prepared to switch quick - like a player, from the national team to the clubs. From one game that you don't play well to another that you need to be ready again. From one action to the next action," Pochettino told reporters ahead of the US match in Cincinnati.

"The business today is to be focusing here in Cincinnati for the game against Haiti tomorrow. But for sure, in the moment that (we) finish that game, we are going to start to be 100% focused on our next project."

Manchester United are bidding to climb out of the relegation zone with Pochettino after a disastrous start to the season.

Pochettino will hope to turn their fortunes around after leading the US to considerable success over the last two years.

Under him, the Americans reached the 2025 Gold Cup final, where Mexico beat them. He also steered the US to the last-16 of the World Cup this year on home soil beforr they were beaten by Belgium.

Pochettino explained that members of his coaching staff have already been sent ahead to Manchester to prepare for his arrival later this week.

"That was the plan when we agreed terms with Manchester United and with the national team," he said. "They are preparing all there because Wednesday, when we will arrive, it's going to be non-stop."
