‘Thank goodness he’s on our team’: Cooper Flagg makes history, Dallas Mavericks keep stacking wins

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

With the Dallas Mavericks down 13 points late in the first half on the second night of a back-to-back, Monday night at American Airlines Center looked very much like a schedule loss. Cooper Flagg and Kyrie Irving had other ideas, though, as they combined for 78 points, fueling Dallas past the Indiana Pacers in a wild finish, 125-122.

The win was the Mavericks' third straight and moved Dallas to 7-4 on the season. It also put the Mavs three games over .500 for the first time since March 1, 2025, a date that feels a lot longer ago than it actually is for anyone who has lived through the last two years of Mavericks basketball.

Think about where this franchise was not long ago. The Luka Doncic trade. Irving's torn ACL. A 26-56 season and back-to-back years without a playoff appearance. Oddsmakers set Dallas' win total at 34.5 before this season, and after an opening stretch that included Houston, San Antonio and Oklahoma City, a lot of people expected the Mavs to be scrapping just to stay afloat by mid-November.

Instead, the Mavs are stacking wins, Flagg looks like an MVP candidate in year two, and Irving looks like his old self before the ACL injury happened. Most importantly, the way the Mavs are winning these games feels sustainable. Here are some of the biggest storylines from Monday night's thriller.

Flagg was good in the first half, but he was on an entirely different level in the second.

The 19-year-old star scored 30 of his season-high 43 points after halftime, including 14 on 6-of-6 shooting in the third quarter and 16 more in the fourth. He finished 16-of-25 from the field, 3-of-6 from 3-point range and a perfect 8-of-8 at the free-throw line, adding 10 rebounds, six assists, three steals and a block for his fourth straight double-double.

Flagg became just the second teenager in NBA history to post at least 40 points, 10 rebounds and five assists in a game, joining Kevin Durant, who put up 42 points, 13 rebounds and six assists in the final game of his rookie season with Seattle in 2008. Per StatMuse, add the three steals, and Flagg is the first teenager ever with a 40-10-5-3 line.

It was also his fifth career 40-point game, as many as the next two teenagers on that list combined (LeBron James with three and Anthony Edwards with two). His 14th career 30-point game moved him into second place for the most by a player under 20, behind only James, and his seventh 35-point game tied James for the most by a teenager. And keep in mind, Flagg doesn't turn 20 until Dec. 21.

Mavericks head coach Dusty May pointed to how complete Flagg's scoring package has become.

"He gets to the rim," May said in his postgame media availability. "I think him almost being a left-handed player in the paint certainly helps. He's a great left-footed jumper, left-handed finisher. Then he's able to square in the air from 15, 17 feet. … The last couple of games, he's made some threes and he's making all of his free throws. That's how you put up the 43 points, where you do a little bit of everything."

It's almost unreal just how good Flagg already is, and May is just happy he's on his roster and not having to gameplan against him.

"He affects the game in so many ways and he makes the right plays as far as passing," May said. "Thank goodness he's on our team. It's unbelievable that he's a 19-year-old doing this."

Flagg's deadly midrange shot, which he worked on all offseason, is paying off in a big way, especially in clutch situations, where he leads the league in clutch points.

"I think just being able to get to my spots at a higher level and being more comfortable with that has allowed me to take a little bit of load off my body," Flagg said. "Just kind of stopping short, raising up over people. It's taxing going downhill all the way to the rim and trying to finish over seven-footers all night, every night. … Just trusting the work, trusting the spot work that I put in all summer long."

Through 11 games, the reigning Rookie of the Year is averaging 25.9 points, 6.9 rebounds, 6.9 assists and 2.2 steals per game. Again, given everything Mavs fans have been through over the last 21 months, this sort of euphoric rise back to genuine happiness watching these games is hard to believe.

Flagg was the closer on Monday night, but Irving was arguably the reason Dallas was still in the game in those closing moments.

Playing the second night of a back-to-back for the first time this season, a day after logging 28 minutes in a 130-93 win over Brooklyn, Irving poured in 35 points in a season-high 38 minutes. He knocked down a season-high seven 3-pointers on 10 attempts, went 3-of-3 at the line and finished a game-best plus-29.

After missing 19 months with the torn ACL he suffered in March 2025, Irving said he told his teammates in the locker room after Sunday's win that he planned to play.

"When you're in a combative sport such as basketball, some days are going to feel good, some days aren't, especially coming off an injury or a long period of time," Irving said. "So I just had to get my body back used to being in the flow of the game. … I was just feeling great, and just coming in to today."

May, who said plus-minus is often overblown in a one-game sample, made an exception for Irving in this one.

"His plus-29 tonight was very indicative of how important he is," May said. "When we were struggling to make shots in the first half, we thought we were generating really good looks. We just looked a little bit flat. I thought Kyrie's shot-making just kind of righted the ship for us."

It's still early, but seeing Irving look this healthy and proving to be a seamless fit next to Flagg bodes well for what this team can accomplish this year. Perhaps the last two years of the basketball injury gods punishing the Mavs will result in some good injury luck the rest of this season.

The most encouraging part of all of this is just how naturally Flagg and Irving shared the scoring load against Indiana. The two combined for 26 fourth-quarter points, including Dallas' first 13 of the period, and May said neither player blinks when the play call goes to the other.

"We're coming out of a timeout, we may draw up a play for one of them just because the other one might have an All-League defender on him, or whatever the case, and they never roll their eyes," May said. "They know when we're going to one of the two, the other one's very, very supportive because they have that much respect for each other."

Irving, an established champion who has shared the floor with more than a few superstars over his 15-year career, is grateful that he's able to be Flagg's teammate, despite the two players being on different ends of their careers.

"We keep calling him a teenager, but he plays way beyond his years. We've got to give him his flowers," Irving said. "Most young guys will probably move away from that contact, but it seems like this newer generation that's coming into our league, they fear nothing. And I think he falls right in line with that. So grateful to have him on the wing with me."

Those feelings are mutual for Flagg: "Just having [Kyrie] out there, his gravity opening the floor for everybody else, it makes my life so much easier."

It wasn't pretty at the end, but it didn't have to be for the Mavs to come up with another win. That's one thing that's been so encouraging about this fast start to the season. Dallas is finding different ways to win, whether it's pretty or not.

Indiana's Pascal Siakam torched Dallas for 26 first-half points on 8-of-9 shooting, but the Mavs adjusted their help defense after the break and held him to just three points on 1-of-7 shooting the rest of the way. The Pacers took a 66-56 lead into halftime and a 93-90 lead into the fourth before Flagg buried a 26-foot pull-up 3 to tie it. Irving's 23-footer with 9:36 left put Dallas in front for good.

Leading 125-122 with a chance to put the game on ice, PJ Washington missed a pair of free throws with four seconds left, and Caleb Martin bailed everyone out by stealing Andrew Nembhard's inbounds pass to Siakam with 0.9 seconds remaining. Pacers head coach Rick Carlisle was adamant that Martin fouled Siakam on that final play, but judging from the replays, it looked like Martin got all ball before ever making any contact with Siakam.

It obviously would've been nice for Washington to sink at least one of those free throws to avoid that last-second drama, but again, the Mavs keep finding different ways to win. How many times over the past two years have we seen a game like that end in a loss for Dallas?

As good (and fun) as the Mavs have been early on, Irving knows his team still has a long way to go to get to the level they want to be at.

"We haven't earned anything in this league yet," Irving said. "We consistently do it every night, and that's how you build your identity in this league and you earn your respect. We try not to put the cart before the horse."

With that being said, though, Mavs fans have earned the right to enjoy this start and, dare I say, dream big again. After two years of nothing but gut punches, a 7-4 start built on a generational teenager, a healthy veteran star and a team that genuinely seems to like playing together is a foundation Mavs fans have been waiting on.

Irving acknowledged the preseason doubt, saying the team heard "a lot of the noise" and chose not to let outside voices into the locker room. If he keeps playing like prime Kyrie, though, and if Flagg keeps rewriting the record books at this current pace, those voices of doubt are going to continue to get a whole lot quieter.

The Mavericks will now hit the road for a big three-game trip that will show just how real this start is, beginning Thursday against the Minnesota Timberwolves at Target Center at 7 p.m. CT. Dallas then visits the Cleveland Cavaliers on Nov. 14 and the Boston Celtics on Nov. 15.
