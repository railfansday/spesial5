NBC ripped for barely showing Goo Goo Dolls concert in favor of Aaron Rodgers breakdown

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Instead of the Goo Goo Dolls, we got a boring interview about Aaron Rodgers.

During the headlining Thanksgiving Day game, a 13-10 Bills win in a slog against the Chiefs, the halftime show was not shown in its entirety to instead air a poorly edited interview.

Former Steelers coach Mike Tomlin was interviewed by Mike Tirico in what appeared to be a pre-recorded interview that NBC aired, forcing the Goo Goo Dolls' performance to be cut short for the television audience.

John Rzeznik of the Goo Goo Dolls performs during halftime of an NFL game between the Kansas City Chiefs and the Buffalo Bills in Orchard Park, N.Y., Thursday, Nov. 26, 2026. Getty Images

"Goo Goo Dolls still rock. Wish NBC gave us more," Barstool Big Cat wrote on X, but there was much more fan outrage.

Fans instead have been posting videos from the in-person audience who missed what appears to be a masterful concert from the late-1990s rock and roll sensation.

"@SNFonNBC Should be boycotted!!" Bills fan @sfurey1511 posted to social media. "Nobody gives AF about Arog and the Steelers. Unreal! Thanks for the post! #GoBills."

The Goo Goo Dolls played for roughly 10 of the 15-minute halftime, playing "Slide," "Broadway," and "Iris," according to first-hand watchers of the show at the Bills' Highmark Stadium.

NBC showed "Iris" after Tomlin's interview wrapped.

"Goo Goo Dolls are massively popular – have so many huge songs… and were the most looked forward to Thanksgiving halftime show and NBC just pulled them. They're even from Buffalo and it's a Bills game," X user @TheGunzShow said in a post with over 5,400 likes at the time of writing.

"Do football fans – outside of Pittsburgh, really care what Mike Tomlin has to say that couldn't be said during pre or postgame?"

Perhaps the biggest issue was Tomlin didn't say a whole lot about the situation, not defending either Rodgers or McCarthy and instead just revealing that he was "sad."

Tomlin also called the situation a "dumpster fire" while dancing around the chaos and did not reveal any new information.

That is not what fans really wanted to hear, as Goo Goo Dolls, by all accounts performed an epic concert in the rain while in their hometown of Buffalo.

"Dear @NBCSports, I just want you to know you made my mom upset that you didn't show the Goo Goo Dolls at halftime," another X account said. "And that's not cool. Especially on Thanksgiving."

John Rzeznik of the Goo Goo Dolls performs during halftime of an NFL game in Orchard Park, N.Y., Thursday, Nov. 26, 2026. Getty Images

Steven Van Zandt, a member of the E Street Band that plays with Bruce Springsteen, felt NBC did his fellow rockers wrong.

"They are a great band," he posted to X. "That was badly handled and disrespectful."
