Bears Week 11 Flashbacks: Ditka’s Revenge, Butler’s Boot, Jets Shutout

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Climb aboard the Gridiron Time Machine as we revisit three memorable Week 11 performances from Chicago Bears history!

The 1985 Chicago Bears entered Week 11 undefeated at 10-0, but their trip to Dallas carried a little extra significance for head coach Mike Ditka.

Waiting on the opposite sideline was Tom Landry, the legendary Cowboys coach who had brought Ditka onto his coaching staff after his playing career ended.

By the time the afternoon was over, Landry probably wished his former assistant had stayed home.

The Bears wasted little time making their presence felt. Richard Dent intercepted a deflected Danny White pass and returned it one yard for a touchdown to open the scoring. Mike Richardson joined the fun in the second quarter, returning another interception 36 yards for a score.

Chicago led 24-0 at halftime, and things weren't about to get any better for Dallas.

The Bears' defense intercepted four passes, recovered a fumble and sacked Cowboys quarterbacks six times. Dallas managed just 171 total yards, including a miserable 52 on the ground, while converting only three of its 13 third-down opportunities.

Walter Payton handled business on the offensive side, rushing for 132 yards on 22 carries. Chicago finished with 216 rushing yards and 378 total yards of offense, while Kevin Butler connected on all three of his field goal attempts.

The Bears scored another 20 points in the second half, including fourth-quarter touchdown runs from Calvin Thomas and Dennis Gentry, turning an already embarrassing afternoon into a historic beatdown.

Chicago walked out of Texas Stadium with a 44-0 victory, improving to 11-0 and clinching the NFC Central championship.

Four interceptions, two defensive touchdowns and a 44-point shutout against America's Team.

Not a bad way to remind your former boss how far you've come.

The Bears entered Week 11 of the 1990 season at 8-1, riding a five-game winning streak and looking like one of the best teams in the NFC.

A trip to Mile High Stadium against John Elway and the Denver Broncos would prove to be anything but easy.

Chicago's offense struggled to find the end zone for much of the afternoon, settling for a pair of Kevin Butler field goals before Brad Muster finally broke through with a 10-yard touchdown run late in the third quarter.

The score gave Chicago a 13-6 advantage heading into the final period.

The Broncos quarterback scrambled nine yards for a touchdown early in the fourth quarter, tying the game at 13 and setting up a tense finish.

Chicago had an opportunity to win in regulation, but Butler's 41-yard field goal attempt struck the left upright.

Not exactly the ending the Bears had in mind.

Fortunately, overtime gave Butler an opportunity to make up for the miss.

After the Bears got the ball back with favorable field position, Butler stepped onto the field and drilled a 44-yard field goal to secure a 16-13 victory.

While Butler supplied the game-winning points, Chicago's ground game did much of the heavy lifting. Neal Anderson rushed for 110 yards on 28 carries, helping the Bears pile up 202 rushing yards and control the football for more than 40 minutes.

The defense also made life difficult for Elway, recording six sacks and forcing two turnovers while holding Denver to just 108 rushing yards.

Chicago escaped Mile High Stadium with its sixth consecutive victory, improving to 9-1 on the season.

A missed game-winner, an overtime opportunity and a kicker who made sure he didn't miss twice.

Sometimes all you need is a second chance.

The 2006 Chicago Bears were already 8-1 when they traveled to the Meadowlands for a Week 11 matchup with the New York Jets.

They were also beginning to establish a familiar formula for winning football games.

Play outstanding defense, run the football and hope Rex Grossman doesn't make things too interesting.

Against the Jets, that formula worked just fine.

Neither offense managed to score in the first half, with Chicago struggling to move the football through the air and New York finding little success against Lovie Smith's defense.

Grossman completed just 11-of-22 passes for 119 yards on the afternoon, while the Bears converted only four of their 14 third-down opportunities.

Fortunately, Thomas Jones was there to keep the offense moving.

Jones rushed for 121 yards on 23 carries, helping Chicago accumulate 173 yards on the ground. The Bears finished with 284 total yards of offense, but points remained difficult to come by.

Robbie Gould finally broke the scoreless tie with a 20-yard field goal in the third quarter, giving Chicago a 3-0 lead.

Then, early in the fourth quarter, Grossman connected with Mark Bradley for a 57-yard touchdown that provided some much-needed breathing room.

That was more than enough for the Bears' defense.

Chicago intercepted Jets quarterback Chad Pennington twice, with Brian Urlacher and Nathan Vasher each recording a takeaway. Pennington finished with just 162 passing yards, and New York managed only 264 total yards despite running nine more offensive plays than Chicago.

The Jets also converted just six of their 17 third-down attempts as the Bears secured their second shutout of the season.

Chicago walked away with a 10-0 victory, improving to 9-1 and continuing its march toward Super Bowl XLI.

Ten points, 119 passing yards and another victory.

Who needs an explosive offense when the other team can't score?
