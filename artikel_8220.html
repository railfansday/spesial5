Carry Less, Eat Better

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Some ultralight thru-hikers swear by going stoveless to save weight and time. But when you don’t cook, you might assume there are a few other things you have to do without: full flavor, fresh ingredients, and a diverse slate of nutrients, to name a few. Cold soaking—the popular method of rehydrating dry or instant food with cold water—lets you ditch the stove, fuel canister, and cook pot. But does it also have to mean compromising on a satisfying meal?

Not necessarily. Going stoveless doesn’t have to be all cold couscous, stale chips, and stray sauce packets. You can just pack and peel, then pair your summit views with wild-caught tuna, whole foods, and carefully crafted flavors.

As a hiker, you know the importance of low-impact practices. Wild Planet believes the same. The brand prioritizes sustainable fishing practices and ocean health, and uses only pole-and-line and troll fishing methods to catch its tuna. That means hauling in only one fish per line at a time; this selective fishing has benefits for the ocean and for you.

On the environmental side, these sustainable methods are focused on preventing two of the most damaging effects of the commercial fishing industry on ocean ecosystems—overfishing and bycatch. Overfishing depletes a fish population faster than it has the chance to repopulate. Bycatch, meanwhile, occurs when non-targeted species are hauled in by accident, like sharks, sea turtles, and marine mammals like dolphins. Wild Planet’s select fishing partners don’t use nets; they monitor closely for bycatch while reeling in one fish at a time.
