"His Majesty"'s "International Common Law Court of Justice" Threat to "Illuminati Police Forces" Was Criminally Punishable

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

From State v. McFate, decided Tuesday by the South Dakota Supreme Court, in an opinion by Justice Robert Gusinsky:

Troy McFate gave a law enforcement officer a letter stating certain members of law enforcement would be executed unless they stood down within 48 hours. Construing the letter as a threat, officers initiated an investigation and obtained search warrants for McFate's residence, vehicle, and blood.

Drug paraphernalia was found in McFate's residence and methamphetamine was subsequently found in his blood. McFate asserts the letter was not a true threat and his speech was protected by the First Amendment. A jury convicted McFate of ingestion of a controlled substance, threatening a law enforcement officer, and possession of drug paraphernalia. McFate appeals. We affirm….

At approximately 10:00 p.m. on March 29, 2024, Police Officer Lexy Deneke was completing a traffic stop in Hot Springs, South Dakota. As Officer Deneke finalized the stop from inside her patrol car, she noticed an unknown man standing near her vehicle with a piece of paper in his hand. The man, later identified as Troy McFate, was asked by Officer Deneke to leave the area, but he refused to do so. McFate approached her vehicle and handed her the paper through the car window saying, "You'll see it Monday." McFate left the area, and Officer Deneke finalized the traffic stop before reading the note.

The note contained the following printed message:

LET ME MAKE THIS CLEAR WITH EVERYONE WITHIN THE FAKE CORPORATION I YOU HAVE GIVEN YOU 48 HOURS AS FROM TODAY DATE 13TH MARCH 2024 YOU MUST STAND DOWN FROM YOUR CURRENT POSITION WITHIN THE ILLUMINATI POLICE FORCES YOU MUST GIVE UP YOUR WARRANT CARD BECAUSE YOU HAVE COMMITTED CRIMES AGAINST HUMANITY AND YOU HAVE BREACHED YOUR OATH TO THE PEOPLE I WILL MAKE THIS CLEAR WITH YOU THAT IF YOU DON'T STAND DOWN FROM YOUR CURRENT POSITION YOU WILL BE ARRESTED BY UNITED STATES MILITARY AND EXECUTED ON MY ORDER WITHOUT ANY TRIAL DO YOU UNDERSTAND.

Additionally, in the letter's upper right corner, the original date of "13th March 2024" was crossed out in pen and "4/1/24" was handwritten below it. Accompanying the signature was a printed fingerprint and seal of the "International Common Law Court of Justice." …

The court rejected various challenges by McFate, and in particular had this to say about the threats prosecution:

In this case, the evidence presented at trial was sufficient to sustain a conviction for a violation of SDCL 22-11-15.6. While McFate's defense centered on his testimony that he was attempting to warn Officer Deneke of the impending danger described in the letter, his testimony was inconsistent on this point. During cross-examination, McFate testified that he recognized the threatening nature of the letter and that, but for his "decoding" and modifying of the letter's date, the threat would have passed and there would have been no need to warn anyone. The jury could conclude that McFate had the specific intent to threaten law enforcement given his statement to Officer Deneke when handing her the letter, "You'll see it Monday," a date that would correspond with the 48 hours referenced in the letter in which she would be executed if she did not "stand down from [her] current position."

Ultimately, it was the jury's responsibility to determine the credibility of the witnesses and it is apparent, by the verdict, that the jury did not believe McFate's claim that he was merely delivering a warning. Under the circumstances here, we conclude that there was sufficient evidence to uphold the jury's determination that McFate knowingly and intentionally communicated a threat to take the life of or to inflict serious bodily harm upon a law enforcement officer….

McFate [also] argues that the statute requires the threat to be directed to a specific law enforcement officer. To this point, McFate notes the letter was not addressed to any officer by name, but instead to "the institution of law enforcement." …

SDCL 22-11-15.6 prohibits threats communicated "upon a law enforcement officer." … McFate handed the letter to Officer Deneke and spoke to her when doing so. While the letter was addressed to "ALL POLICE WORLDWIDE" and not directly to Officer Deneke, the statute's plain language does not contain a requirement that the communication be addressed or directed to a specific law enforcement officer…

Assistant AG Jennifer M. Jorgenson represents the government.
