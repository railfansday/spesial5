Palisades Fire arson case goes to jury for a second time

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The first jury split 10-2 in favor of acquitting former Uber driver Jonathan Rinderknecht. Federal prosecutors hope they were more convincing this time around.

LOS ANGELES (CN) â Federal prosecutors on Wednesday asked a jury to find a disgruntled ex-Uber driver guilty of starting a brush fire in the early hours of Jan. 1, 2025, which later erupted into one of the most destructive fires in Los Angeles history.

It’s the second time Jonathan Rinderknecht, 30, is on trial over his role in the Palisades Fire, a so-called holdover fire from the Lachman Fire he’s accused of igniting shortly after midnight on New Year’s Day.

At the first trial in June, the jurors split 10-2 in favor of acquitting him, causing U.S. District Judge Anne Hwang to declare a mistrial. The Joe Biden appointee told jurors they shouldn’t factor the LA Fire Department’s response into their verdict.

Assistant U.S. Attorney Mark Williams said in his closing statement that Rinderknecht was angry and mad at everything when he dropped off an Uber passenger just before midnight in the Pacific Palisades, not far from where he had lived in a rented house with a former partner years earlier.

Surveillance camera footage from nearby homes, data from his cellphone and Rinderknecht’s own statements to investigators leave no doubt that he parked his car near a trailhead and hiked up in the dark to the so-called Hidden Buddha clearing in the hills above the houses, the prosecutor said.

A little after midnight, surveillance cameras designed to pick up early signs of wildfires record the first signs of a brush fire near the clearing where Rinderknecht’s cellphone data shows he was standing.

“We’ll never know all the reasons why he started the fire,” Williams told the jury, “but you’ve seen some of them.”

Rinderknecht faces two counts of arson, one for the Jan. 1 Lachman Fire and one for the Jan. 7 Palisades Fire, which broke out when extreme Santa Ana winds hit Southern California and reignited the embers that had been left smoldering among tree roots for six days.

The Palisades Fire destroyed thousands of homes in the wealthy enclave above the Pacific Ocean, as well in adjacent Malibu, and killed 12 people before it was contained on Jan. 31, 2025.

Prosecutors claim Rinderknecht was extremely agitated on New Year’s Eve, citing angry texts he sent to a girlfriend who had told him the night before that she needed space from him, as well as testimony from Uber passengers who rode with him that night and had to endure his rants.

After dropping a passenger off in the Palisades near where he had lived in happier days, the government says he hiked up to the Hidden Buddha clearing and used a large grill lighter that was later found in the glove compartment of his car to set fire to the shrubs.

He then lingered around the clearing as the flames spread and started calling 911 to report the fire â a ploy to create an alibi for himself, Williams told the jury. He also asked ChatGPT if he could be held responsible for a fire started by a cigarette, again to establish a possible alibi, according to the prosecutor.

Investigators took Rinderknecht, who had been identified by his cellphone data and surveillance footage of his car driving through the neighborhood, back to the clearing three weeks after the fire, and he told them that he had gone up there by himself to look at the fireworks but that there weren’t any and that he was the only person there.

Rinderknecht’s attorney, Steven Haney, said in his closing statement, as he did during the first trial, that there were too many gaps in the government’s evidence for the jury to find his client guilty beyond a reasonable doubt.

According to Haney, it couldn’t be excluded that the fire was ignited by New Year’s Eve fireworks because some neighbors had testified they heard fireworks and the surveillance cameras to check for wildfires only create still images with one-minute intervals and may have missed fireworks around the Little Buddha clearing. He also pointed out that the scene of the Lachman Fire wasn’t preserved until more than 10 days later and there was no way of knowing what evidence may have been lost.

“The government has never been able to tell you when the fire started,” Haney told the jurors. “Are you convinced beyond a reasonable doubt that the fire wasn’t smoldering earlier from fireworks?”

Assistant U.S. Attorney Danbee Kim said in her rebuttal that the defense’s argument were mere distractions and speculation, and that Rinderknecht’s own statements to investigators in early 2025 were unambiguous in that he was the only person at the clearing around midnight and that there were no fireworks nearby.

“He lied and lied and lied again about where he was when he first saw the fire,” Kim told the jury, referring to Rinderknecht’s statements to investigators that he had gone down the hill back to his car when he first spotted the flames while his cellphone data shows him at the clearing on top of the hill when he’s calling 911.

“These lies are telling â they are not mistakes,” the prosecutor said. “Because he know that where he was when the fire started was incriminating.”
