Finland is allowing Tesla's assisted driving system before E.U. approval

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Traficom recognized the Dutch provisional type-approval, while final E.U.-wide approval still needs a committee vote

Finland has cleared Tesla $TSLA’s Assisted Driving system for its roads, getting out in front of an EU-wide ruling that is anticipated in the winter months.

The Finnish Transport and Communications Agency, known as Traficom, recognized the provisional type-approval that regulators in the Netherlands issued on Tuesday, the agency said. Tesla can start offering the system to its customers in Finland with immediate effect.

Final approval across the EU requires a successful vote in the European Commission’s Technical Committee – Motor Vehicles, Traficom said. Approval takes effect about three weeks after a successful vote. Traficom said national recognition does not bind Finland in that vote, and the country will follow discussions among member states on the system’s features before deciding its position.

Traficom said it moved ahead of the EU process because of changes Tesla has made to the system. It also said earlier recognition gives Finnish Tesla users time to get used to the system ahead of the harshest winter driving conditions.

"The system's design and approval assessment have taken winter conditions into account, but its operation may be subject to various limitations if the cameras become covered in snow, slush or other dirt, or if traffic conditions become more challenging than usual," said Jukka Juhola, a senior specialist at Traficom.

The most visible change is the name. The system was called FSD (Supervised), short for Full Self-Driving (Supervised), and references to self-driving have been removed. The new name appears in Finnish- and Swedish-language marketing and user guidance materials. Tesla's choice of name has drawn criticism from regulators, safety groups and some of its own customers, who say it implies full autonomy, according to Bloomberg.

Tesla also changed the system's speed settings. Drivers can no longer encourage the system to keep an unnecessarily high speed above the limit. Settings that allow speeding reset to the default, with no deviation from the speed limit, each time the vehicle restarts. The information shown to drivers about the speed settings the system has selected has been expanded and clarified.

Traficom expects further changes before EU-wide approval. Those changes will not significantly affect how the system is used but will help drivers stay alert, the agency said.

Traficom stressed that the driver remains fully responsible for driving, even when the experience resembles that of a self-driving car. Drivers must monitor the system throughout the trip and be ready to steer, brake or accelerate without waiting for a request from the system.

"Drivers must still be aware of the speed limit and comply with it," Juhola said. "It is always the driver who receives the fine for speeding."

Installing the system does not require a modification inspection for vehicles that have not been modified, Traficom said. Unusual tire sizes or suspension lowering kits are examples of changes that would require one.
