Thanksgiving Murder Mystery Remains Unsolved 30 Years After Killer Allegedly Used Cat Door to Enter Woman’s Texas Apartment

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

For 30 years, Thanksgiving has been a painful reminder for a Texas family whose holiday turned into a nightmare when their loved one was found strangled inside her Houston apartment.

Kristen Lea Wilson, 29, was preparing to spend Thanksgiving with her family in November 1996 when investigators say someone slipped into her first-floor apartment through a window, using a cat door to gain access, and waited for her to return home.

After cutting the telephone cord and unscrewing the lightbulbs to keep the apartment dark, the intruder waited in Wilson’s bedroom closet.

When she never showed up to Thanksgiving dinner the following day, her parents became worried and drove her apartment where they found her slain body. Three decades later, no one has been charged in her murder, and her family is still fighting for answers.

“Thirty years ago this Thanksgiving my sister Kristen Lea Wilson was murdered in Houston,” Kelly Wilson Hancock wrote in a recent social media post. “She was strangled–this was an up and personal murder. Her case has NEVER been solved.”

On Nov. 27, 1996, the day before Thanksgiving, Wilson stopped at a grocery store after work to pick up last-minute items for the family's holiday gathering at her grandparents' home in Katy, TX.

She returned to her apartment on Meadowglen Lane in southwest Houston, unloaded her groceries, and reportedly went into her bedroom to change clothes. Investigators believe her killer was already inside.

According to the Houston Police Department, the intruder likely gained access by reaching through a cat door in the screen of a first-floor window and removing the window frame. Once inside, the killer allegedly cut the telephone cord and unscrewed lightbulbs, reportedly preparing to ambush Wilson in complete darkness.

The apartment's accessibility became a crucial detail in the investigation, and raised troubling questions about security at the complex, especially because Wilson had allegedly expressed concerns about her safety before her death, her family said. She had previously revealed an incident in which a maintenance worker allegedly entered her apartment without warning.

When Wilson’s parents arrived at her apartment to check on her, they found the front door unlocked. Inside, Wilson's father made the horrific discovery–his daughter dead on the bedroom floor. She had been strangled and was partially undressed.

Police also noted a disturbing detail–her body appeared to have been deliberately positioned, with her ankles crossed and her hands resting in her lap.

Investigators found evidence of a violent struggle, including broken fingernails, suggesting Wilson had fought desperately against her attacker. There were no reported witnesses to the killing, and surveillance cameras did not capture the suspect leaving the property.

Despite decades of investigation, extensive media coverage and advancements in forensic technologies, the identity of Wilson’s killer remains a mystery.

Three men associated with Wilson provided DNA samples to investigators shortly after her death, and each were ruled out as suspects based on the technology available at the time, according to an investigation by ABC13 Houston.

But Hancock believes mistakes made during the initial investigation have complicated efforts to solve her sister's murder.

“From the get go, the detectives messed up,” she wrote in her recent post. “The original detective was going through a divorce and she wasn’t focused.”

She also expressed frustration over what she described as inconsistencies in the investigation. Investigators have since discovered that not all the evidence collected from the crime scene was tested, according to local reports.

Hancock said she has attempted to contact Othram, a forensic DNA laboratory that specializes in helping law enforcement investigate cold cases, about potentially examining evidence in her sister's murder.

“I’ve tried to reach out to Othram Labs to see if they can look at DNA, but the detective has to request it, from what I am told,” she wrote.

It is unclear whether Houston police have submitted evidence for additional testing or whether forensic genetic genealogy could be used in the case.

For the Wilson family, Thanksgiving had always been one of their favorite celebrations.

“Thanksgiving was the biggest event of the year,” Hancock told ABC13. “We’d all go over there, and it was just a big family get-together. It was so much fun.”

Wilson, described by her sister as independent and ambitious, had launched a small business that provided office services to local companies. Wilson, the middle of three girls, was also devoted to her family, especially her young niece and nephew.

"She was like a second mother to my son and daughter,” Hancock said. “She was just so beautiful with an infectious smile. My mom always called her the golden child because she just was. I hate to say she was perfect, but she pretty much was.”.

The Wilson family was devastated by her murder and to this day are still struggling with questions that remain unanswered three decades later.

“My grandma used the words ‘brutally murdered.’ I’ll never forget the call telling me Kristen had been killed," Hancock said. "My whole world was… our whole family’s world was turned upside down. Personally, I think she knew the person, but I don’t know."

Wilson's father, who discovered her body, has since passed away without learning who killed his daughter.

As the family marks the 30th anniversary of Wilson's death this Thanksgiving, her sister continues to push for answers.

Hancock said Houston police Detective Richard Rodriguez, who previously discussed the case in ABC13's Unsolved series, is now handling the investigation. She is hoping that with fresh eyes on the case, renewed publicity and advances in forensic technology might finally produce a breakthrough.

But no matter how much time has passed, Wilson's sister said the anniversary is another reminder of the holidays, birthdays and other family events her sister has missed.

“Hug your loved ones tight, you just never know," she wrote. "Miss my sister so much."

Anyone with information about Wilson's murder is urged to contact the Houston Police Department or Crime Stoppers of Houston.
