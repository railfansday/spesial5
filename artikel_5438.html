Fund managers slashed tech exposure by the most since 2006, BofA survey finds

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Free daily briefing on global business news.

A record net 38% of respondents say companies are overinvesting in AI, and 52% expect an AI hyperscaler to cut spending

Bank of America $BAC found that fund managers cut their technology allocations by the most since June 2006 in its November survey, according to Bloomberg.

Fund managers with an overweight position in tech made up a net 12% of respondents in November, down sharply from 37% in October. The survey covered Nov. 6 through Nov. 12 and drew responses from 169 participants who oversee a combined $461 billion in assets.

In a note, Bank of America strategist Michael Hartnett said the "big" takeaway from respondents is that companies ought to put the brakes on AI spending. A net 38% of respondents, the highest on record, say companies are putting too much money into AI, and 52% foresee an AI hyperscaler announcing a cut to capital spending within the coming half-year. "Euphoria on boom & AI over," Hartnett said, according to Bloomberg.

Respondents named an AI bubble as the top tail risk for markets, followed by an AI hyperscaler credit event, according to Reuters. Worries over a pullback in data center investment following the U.S. midterms have pushed technology shares down over the past few weeks.

The Nasdaq $NDAQ 100 is off by over 4% so far in November and is heading for its weakest showing for the month since 2008, according to Bloomberg. A gauge covering chip-sector shares has lost 27% of its value since the start of October.

Respondents showed less concern about the broader economy. Roughly 3% expect a recession within the next year, while cash positions have shrunk to 3.6% of total assets, the smallest share since August, according to Bloomberg.

Doubts about AI spending have weighed on tech stocks for weeks. Shares of Amazon $AMZN, Microsoft $MSFT, Alphabet $GOOGL and Meta $META had been sliding before the midterm elections because investors questioned whether the companies' computing outlays would pay off. Free cash flow has turned negative at Amazon, Alphabet and Meta, and the companies are turning to borrowing and share sales to finance the spending.

Democrats won majorities in the House and Senate in the midterms, having campaigned on curbing AI development and the large-scale building of data centers. Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta shares rose the day after the election as investors wagered that Congress would rein in AI spending.

Former Securities and Exchange Commission Chair Gary Gensler called the AI boom a bubble likely to burst and drag the economy into recession before the 2028 presidential election. "It's already a bubble," Gensler said.
