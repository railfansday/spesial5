9/UX brings a classic Mac feel to 9front

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A spatial classic Finder in the style of A/UX

Bringing a classic MacOS Finder to Linux is all very well, but there's no shortage of Linux desktops… however, bringing one to 9front is even more unexpected – and even more useful.

9/UX is a new project by Jeff "Progrium" Lindsay, who was one of the people behind Docker, Webhooks, and more. When you follow that link, and you should, pay close attention to what looks like a screenshot at the top. It is not an image. It's a real live executing instance of the OS, running inside your web browser, running on top of hyper9. (That is worth reading too – it's an impressive project in its own right.)

As its GitHub repository describes, 9/UX is a "Macintosh-inspired graphical environment for 9front."

"9/UX brings the classic Mac and A/UX desktop experience (a spatial desktop, a global menu bar, consistent controls and dialogs, icons, and resource-driven presentation) to 9front, built the Plan 9 way: rio and libdraw draw the screen, 9P exposes every application's UI state and commands as files, namespaces define the working environment, the plumber opens documents, and ndb holds human-editable metadata."

Lindsay introduced 9/UX earlier this month in a short X thread, saying he "made my own kind of A/UX but on Plan 9." In its creator's own words, "it's slop", but this is kind of interesting, original, creative bot-assisted coding that we enjoy looking at. At the end of last week, he returned to explain more:

"9/UX isn’t just a pretty Mac-style UI on Plan 9. It's filesystem inspectable. Shell scriptable. Even the Mac resource file format can be used through the filesystem."

Regular readers may have noticed that The Reg FOSS desk is fascinated by Plan 9 and its FOSS continuation 9front. We've written and presented at length about this remarkable OS, and tried to explain its FOSS continuation 9front several times. But even with the best will we can muster, its unique GUI is not an especially friendly or easy one. Its idiosyncratic UI was inspired by the late great Niklaus Wirth's unbelievably tiny Oberon OS, which we have written about before. It's cryptic, and that's being generous.

The research and development effort that led to Unix didn't stop when Unix 7th Edition escaped from the Bell Labs in 1981 and mutated into Unix System III. The industry enthusiastically adopted System III and later System V. A decade after 7th Edition Unix, Linux became a GPL version of something very like that traditional Unix, closely followed by the BSDs – and between them, they have very effectively distracted most of the FOSS world ever since. Even so, Research Unix lived on, saw 8th, 9th, and 10th editions, and then was replaced by Plan 9. In 1996, many of Plan 9's limitations were overcome by Inferno – whose revival as InferNode we covered this week.

We always vaguely thought that if 9front were to receive an improved GUI that was slightly more compliant with later GUI design standards, it might be with a version of the Inferno UI… But apparently not. A new, easier, and yet still simple, clean and attractive desktop for 9front is here, and it looks like classic MacOS from the era of Apple's 68030 Unix A/UX.

We do not think the chances are high that the 9front community will adopt this with enthusiasm. Even so, there is an AI programming manual for agents, and we've seen some interesting looking LLM-assisted 9front apps of late. Several can be found in the agent9 distribution, which includes LLM-assisted ports of C++, Python, and Rust, plus a version of DOSbox, and even a package manager called pac9.®
