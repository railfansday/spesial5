Nike vs. Procter & Gamble: Which Consumer Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Nike trades at a premium valuation despite slowing growth, while P&G's defensive portfolio offers a contrasting risk-return profile.

Investors often weigh the potential of iconic growth brands against the stability of essential household names. Deciding between Nike (NKE -1.64%) and Procter & Gamble (PG -0.92%) means choosing between athletic innovation and daily necessity.

Nike serves the athletic world with footwear and apparel, leveraging a massive digital presence to reach global fans. Procter & Gamble offers a defensive shield with brands like Tide and Gillette. These companies represent different strategies for long-term growth and capital preservation in a shifting economic landscape.

Nike designs and sells athletic footwear, apparel, and equipment across the globe through its own digital channels and a network of wholesale partners. The company operates primarily in the retail stocks space, focusing on high-performance gear and lifestyle products. In its latest annual report, filed for the period ending May 31, 2026, the company noted that its three largest U.S. customers accounted for approximately 29% of sales. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business.

In FY 2026, revenue reached nearly $46.4 billion. This performance follows a period of higher sales in earlier years, as the company reported approximately $51.4 billion in revenue during FY 2024. Net income for the most recent year was $3.1 billion.

Procter & Gamble manufactures branded consumer packaged goods across diverse categories, including Fabric & Home Care, Baby Care, and Beauty. The company sells to mass merchandisers, grocery stores, and drug stores, reaching billions of consumers worldwide. Walmart (WMT -2.22%) and its affiliates represent approximately 16% of total sales, which indicates a notable reliance on a single major retail partner. This focus on essential goods helps the company maintain a steady presence throughout the globe.

In FY 2026, revenue reached nearly $87 billion, which represents a growth rate of approximately 3.3% over the prior year. Net income for the period was nearly $16 billion. This performance highlights the ability of Procter & Gamble to maintain stable earnings and profit levels even as revenue fluctuates across its various global markets.

Nike faces intense competition in the athletic footwear and apparel market from rivals like Adidas (ADDDF -1.81%), Lululemon Athletica (LULU -0.82%), Under Armour (UAA +1.53%), V.F. (VFC +3.35%), and ASICS (ASCCF -7.79%). The company also competes with Li Ning (LNNGF +0.00%) and other international brands. Dependence on a concentrated base of contract manufacturers, primarily in Vietnam, Indonesia, and China, creates supply chain and disruption risks. Global economic volatility and currency fluctuations also significantly impact consumer spending and operational margins.

Procter & Gamble operates in a highly competitive consumer products industry with global and local rivals and private-label brands. The company is vulnerable to global supply chain disruptions, including commodity and raw material cost fluctuations and the impact of climate change. Macroeconomic and geopolitical instability, including trade controls and sanctions in countries where it operates, pose ongoing threats.

Procter & Gamble offers a more conservative valuation relative to its earnings estimates, while NIKE carries a higher price tag despite slower recent revenue growth.

Nike intrigues me as it moves away from being more of a growth stock toward a dividend stock. While this isn't often talked about, it has increased its dividend payouts for 24 consecutive years. That doesn't make it a Dividend King yet, as it would need to increase those payouts for 50 consecutive years, but it's on the path to becoming one. In addition to that consistency, the yield for that dividend is hefty, sitting at 4.1%.

The catch with Nike, however, is that sales are slumping, and some of the market may still be waiting for it to return to its high-growth ways. That mismatch between what Nike was and what it's becoming is where I see the investable opportunity. In the meantime, the stock price could continue to decline until sales stabilize, cutting into those dividend returns. That's why, between the two, I would rather own Procter & Gamble. While Nike is on the track to becoming a Dividend King, Procter already is one, having increased its payouts for 70 consecutive years.

With its essential household goods, it has also fared better than Nike thus far in 2026; Procter & Gamble shares are up 14.1%, while Nike's stock price is down 38.6%. And while Procter's dividend yield of 2.6% may be lower than Nike's yield, you aren't dealing with a stock price trying to get out of a rut.
