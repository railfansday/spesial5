Intuit vs. Zscaler: Which Tech Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Intuit trades at a 14.8 forward P/E with a 21% net margin, while Zscaler grows faster but trades at 53.4 despite a net loss.

By Erin Kennedy – Updated Nov 19, 2026 at 6:17AM EST

Choosing between established software giants and high-growth security specialists requires a look at different business models. Intuit (INTU +0.16%) and Zscaler (ZS +8.29%) both offer essential tools but serve very different markets.

Intuit is a financial technology powerhouse serving millions of consumers and small businesses with its tax and accounting platforms. Zscaler focuses on securing cloud-based networks for large enterprises and government agencies. Comparing them helps you decide between a mature, highly profitable business and a faster-growing cybersecurity innovator.

Intuit is a global leader among tech stocks that provides financial services for consumers and businesses. According to its latest annual report, filed for the fiscal year ended July 31, 2026, the company serves roughly 93 million users worldwide. Its strategy involves expanding into the mid-market segment and enhancing its platform with AI tools, such as the October 2026 acquisition of Sequence.

Intuit's financial performance remains strong across its major segments. In fiscal 2026, revenue reached nearly $21.4 billion, representing growth of approximately 13.9% over the previous year. Intuit reported net income of close to $4.6 billion, resulting in a healthy net margin of roughly 21.3%.

As of its July 2026 balance sheet, Intuit had a debt-to-equity ratio (total debt divided by equity) of approximately 0.4 and a current ratio of 1.5. Free cash flow, which equals cash flow from operations minus capital expenditures, reached nearly $8.6 billion. Note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 23.3% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation, since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Zscaler provides a cloud-native security platform known as the Zero Trust Exchange, which protects users and data regardless of location. The cybersecurity specialist serves roughly 11,000 customers globally, including more than half of the Fortune 500 companies. It also pursues technical alliances, such as its recent security collaborations with Nvidia (NVDA +3.12%).

High demand for cybersecurity tools has driven significant top-line growth. In fiscal 2026, revenue reached nearly $3.4 billion, an increase of approximately 25.4% year over year. However, the company reported a net loss of roughly $63.2 million, resulting in a negative net margin of nearly 1.9%.

As of its July 2026 balance sheet, Zscaler had a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.7 and a current ratio of roughly 1.7. Free cash flow was approximately $852.4 million. Note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 72.8% of operating cash flow.

Intuit faces several challenges, including intense competition from both government-provided tax tools and private software rivals. The company relies heavily on the seasonal tax preparation industry, which creates significant revenue concentration in certain months. Cybersecurity threats remain a constant concern, particularly with FTC oversight and potential legal liabilities from ongoing class action litigation.

Zscaler competes in a crowded market against established networking and security vendors like Microsoft (MSFT +3.11%). The company depends heavily on a network of channel partners to generate a significant portion of its revenue. Managing a global infrastructure of over 200 data centers entails complex operational risks, international regulatory requirements, and potential class action litigation regarding consumer protection claims.

Intuit appears more conservatively valued when looking at its forward P/E, while Zscaler carries a higher P/S ratio that reflects rapid sales growth.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

My reflex is to find Zscaler more attractive because Intuit seems like part of the old guard of software. Also, Intuit's business model is basically predicated on making it hard for people to file taxes (so they'll pay for Intuit's software), and that's kind of messed up! It's not the equivalent of investing in private prisons, but it seems strange to want to own a company that lobbies to make sure filing your taxes is difficult.

Zscaler also offers a stickier product, in my opinion. Cybersecurity for any organization isn't a "nice to have"; it's absolutely crucial. I am not as convinced that Intuit's software is equally essential.

Zscaler stock has outperformed Intuit by more than 400 percentage points over the past decade or so. Intuit hasn't even managed to keep pace with the broader market, lagging behind the S&P 500 (^GSPC +0.78%) by about 90 percentage points in that time frame.

Zscaler did report a loss in its most recent fiscal year, but that figure is improving over time, and it's hard to argue with the stock's returns. Past performance is no indication of future results, of course, but Zscaler gets my vote for 2027 and beyond.
