Hackers smash 'n' grab AI vendor: Take off with license data of Uber drivers

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Ride-hailing biz says stolen credentials allowed crooks to help themselves to driver's license data

Uber has confirmed that nearly 150,000 people had their personal information compromised after hackers broke into one of its AI suppliers.

A filing with Oregon's attorney general puts the number of victims at 149,346. Among the data the crooks got their hands on were images of drivers' licenses and information extracted from them, including names, home addresses, dates of birth, and license numbers.

Uber doesn't name the supplier in its filings with US state attorneys general, but told TechCrunch it was Fireworks AI, the inference outfit whose security woes we covered last week.

According to a breach notification filed with Iowa's attorney general, Fireworks alerted Uber to the intrusion on October 9. Uber cut off the supplier's access to its data and, four days later, established that the attackers had accessed drivers' personal information on October 7.

Uber had set up a dedicated cloud storage environment for the AI biz, complete with security monitoring overseen by its own cloud security team. Unfortunately for Uber and its drivers, the crooks were using legitimate credentials, so none of the alarms went off.

The ride-hailing firm says those credentials couldn't be used to access its other systems, and says it has since revoked or changed the credentials associated with the supplier.

Uber hasn't said why it handed images of thousands of driver's licenses to an AI inference outfit. The Register has put that question to both companies, and asked Uber if any drivers outside the US had their data exposed.

Fireworks, which provides infrastructure for developers to run and fine-tune AI models, had a particularly miserable October. The company first disclosed a security incident on October 9 after discovering that someone had been using its internal credentials without permission.

Fireworks initially said it had no evidence that the intruders had gotten their hands on personal information or customer datasets. The company was hacked again later that month and, on November 9, disclosed that some customer data had indeed been compromised.

The AI biz acknowledged that attackers had accessed billing records, some customer datasets, and incomplete model weights. Productivity software maker Notion was among those affected, with some of its customers' conversations with its AI assistant exposed.

Fireworks suspects the involvement of financially motivated criminals in the business of flogging unauthorized access to large language models. Whether the crooks have any plans for the haul of Uber drivers' personal information is unclear.

Uber began notifying affected individuals on November 10, more than a month after learning of the breach. It's offering victims free credit monitoring and fraud assistance, though drivers have just 90 days from the date of their letter to sign up.

This isn't Uber's first brush with cybercriminals in recent months. In August, the ride-hailing giant's logistics arm, Uber Freight, confirmed a break-in after the Helix extortion crew claimed to have made off with almost a million files, including data pilfered from mailboxes and OneDrive accounts. ®
