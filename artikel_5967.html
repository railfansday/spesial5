For ‘Denuclearization,’ Trump Should Build Up to Build Down

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The president ought to take a page from Reagan’s Cold War playbook.

The U.S. Navy Ohio-class ballistic missile submarine USS Tennessee returns to Naval Submarine Base Kings Bay, Ga., in 2013.

Mass Communication Specialist First Class James Kimber/U.S. Navy/Handout via Reuters

President Trump left his Shenzhen summit with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin last week still dreaming of a denuclearization agreement with his great-power counterparts. But Trump’s long-held ambition to achieve the deep reductions in nuclear weaponry that Ronald Reagan secured with his Soviet counterpart Mikhail Gorbachev in the 1980s clashes with reality. Chinese and Russian officials may now be speaking the language of “strategic stability,” but Trump would be wise to recognize that these pronouncements are intended to deter him from taking the steps necessary to strengthen America’s nuclear deterrence.

Unlike Gorbachev in the final years of the Cold War, neither Xi nor Putin currently has any appetite for meaningful arms-control negotiations. Xi acts as if his geopolitical position compares favorably with America’s. Both men are wielding their nuclear power to weaken America’s security and dissolve its alliances. Consider Putin’s nuclear threats during the war in Ukraine and Xi’s campaign of coercion in the western Pacific, which is shielded by China’s growing nuclear arsenal.

Kyle Balzer is a resident fellow at the American Enterprise Institute where he specializes in nuclear strategy and policy.
