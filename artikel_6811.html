Sauer Celebrates 85 Years With a New Assouline Book

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Gabriel Sauer and Stephanie Wenk on how jewelry connects us to the real world.

For 85 years, Brazilian jewelry house Sauer has built a world of its own, one where vibrant gemstones, unexpected materials, and an adventurous spirit come together. Founded by Jules Sauer, a pioneering gem hunter whose fascination with Brazil’s natural treasures helped shape the country’s gemstone industry, the house has always approached jewelry a little differently. Today, that same curiosity remains at its core, even as a new generation takes over.

To celebrate its 85th anniversary, Sauer is looking both backward and forward with the release of a new Assouline book highlighting its remarkable history, out December 5, alongside an anniversary collection that reimagines some of the house’s most recognizable designs. For creative director Stephanie Wenk and third-generation family member Gabriel Sauer, the milestone is about more than honoring the past. It’s an opportunity to reflect on how a family business rooted in tradition can continue to evolve without losing what makes it special.

Stephanie Wenk, creative director of Sauer.

The anniversary collection is a testament to that philosophy. Drawing on an extraordinary archive of gemstones, some of which have been tucked away for decades, Wenk brings new life to Brazilian imperial topaz, aquamarines, and other precious stones through unexpected combinations. It’s an approach that feels true to the house’s history while pushing its creative boundaries. But beyond the jewelry itself, Sauer’s story is one of family, discovery, and a deep connection to Brazil. From Jules’s early adventures to the house’s growing international presence, each chapter has been shaped by a love of experimentation and embracing the unexpected. As the brand celebrates this milestone, Wenk and Sauer share how they’re staying true to the house’s original spirit.

The cover of Sauer: Dialogues Between Worlds from Assouline.

Gabriel Sauer: We’ve been with the company for almost 14 years now. The anniversary is an important milestone for the company, but it also marks more than 10 years of our generation of the Sauer family leading the business. I think we now have the material and the maturity to showcase our vision for the future of the company. It’s important that, in a book like this, we can tell a little bit about the past but also share our vision for the future. If we had done this book five years ago, it wouldn’t have had the same substance that it has now. We had the idea in previous years, but I feel that the timing came naturally.

Stephanie Wenk: I think it was the adventurous spirit of Sauer. Jules was always very nonconformist in everything he did, from his discoveries to his travels. He did things differently and in his own way. I think that’s what continues to inspire us today. While making the book, we were also talking about the history with Gabriel’s dad and the rest of the family. It’s very inspiring. Jules’s spirit is still very much alive.

GS: It was interesting to have these conversations with the second generation of the family while putting the book together. The story of how Jules came to Brazil and founded the company still shapes how we approach business and challenges today. He was an optimist with a deep passion for craftsmanship and gemstones, something we continue to carry forward. Every time we revisit his story, we’re reminded of our values and purpose.

I also learned more about the historical context of some of his discoveries. During the war, American companies were coming to Brazil to mine minerals for the war effort, and some of that activity was connected to his earlier explorations. I hadn’t fully understood how his discoveries fit into that period of history, so it was fascinating to learn about that connection.

GS: Working in a family business is a great responsibility. It’s also a challenge because we’re the third generation, and most family businesses don’t survive beyond the third generation. One of the biggest lessons I’ve learned is the importance of giving each generation creative freedom. Every family has its own formula, and there’s no single formula that works for everyone. In our family, we’ve always welcomed new generations and allowed them to express their vision for the future without having someone hovering over their heads. I think this philosophy came from Jules and [his wife] Zilda together, and it’s been an invaluable lesson in how to run a family business.

I remember that, when I was a kid, my grandfather used to carry gemstones in his pocket and give them to people with whom he felt a good connection or energy. He would say that gemstones take billions of years to form in nature and that they carry the heart of our world inside them. I would see the impact that these gifts had on people. He was passionate about honoring gemstones and bringing out their best qualities. That’s something we continue to express through our designs. A lot of our creations begin with the stone itself, sometimes even with one specific gemstone.

SW: We’re very lucky because we have this amazing inventory of stones, and I’m always discovering new ones. I think about 80 percent of the stones we used in this collection came from our archives. We used a lot of Brazilian stones, like imperial topaz and aquamarine, which are also very important to our history. Our signature elements are really important to our vocabulary, our DNA, and the foundations of our designs, but the stones play a very big role. There are a lot of one-of-a-kind pieces in this collection, including more high jewelry than we usually do, as well as some brooches. Some of the stones have been in our archives for 40 years, so it’s really special.

SW: Researching, introducing, and combining different materials is very important to our work, and it’s something I’m really drawn to. I love exploring the dialogue between different materials, how one enhances the other, and how combining them can give a new perspective to a material you might not normally imagine alongside a precious stone. We’ve been doing it since the beginning. We manufacture everything ourselves, so we also have the opportunity to test materials and develop ideas. Sometimes that process takes a long time, and you have to be able to experiment. It’s really valuable that we’re able to do that.

GS: Since we started as a stone-cutting workshop, we’ve always explored different types of stones and how we can cut and use them in different ways. This exploration of different forms and creative applications is something that’s ingrained in our DNA.

SW: We revisited a few pieces, but it’s also a collection that looks very much toward the future. We reinterpreted the Constellation ring, one of our most iconic designs, which won the De Beers prize. We also combined elements of Constellation with Illusion, another design from about 10 years ago. It was an opportunity to experiment with both designs and bring them together in a new way. We also revisited signature elements, like the use of onyx and lapis lazuli, as well as our distinctive framing techniques, and developed those ideas further.

GS: We also created a new version of an archival necklace incorporating pearls, revisiting a design language that’s been part of the house for years.

SW: I think all of our collections revolve around art, nature, and spirituality, so it felt very natural. Collaborating with artists, bringing in a contemporary vision, working across different disciplines, and interpreting different worlds are all part of that. I have a lot of different interests, including music, texture, design, art, and cinema. I don’t like to look at other jewelry. I prefer to look at things that inspire me, and I think that shows in what we do. Bringing that perspective into the house felt like a very natural evolution.

GS: I think it’s definitely something we inherited from previous generations and have carried forward. Jules was always passionate about art. He was a collector himself, and previous generations had already explored partnerships and collaborations with artists. When Stephanie started exploring this as well, it came very naturally because she’s so passionate about art. We’ve been fortunate to collaborate with some incredible artists. It wasn’t something that was particularly planned. It happened naturally, and eventually we realized that art had become one of the main pillars of the brand. We then started collaborating with art fairs as well, and we felt that establishing the art prize was a way to give back.

GS: We wanted to tell the story of how Jules first came to Brazil, which is very inspiring, but also highlight the different explorations and discoveries he participated in. For example, there was the discovery of emeralds in Brazil and how he worked with the GIA [Gemological Institute of America] to establish that the stones were actually emeralds. It was a very important milestone for Brazil as a gemstone producer.

He was also an advocate for gemstones and traveled around the world, giving speeches about removing the label of “semiprecious” from certain stones. He used to say that gemstones are a one-time harvest and that every gemstone is precious. He had an amethyst with an amazing color that he considered incredibly valuable, and he wouldn’t sell it at any price. Today, we can see that many different gemstones are recognized in the market and have very high values. He was really ahead of his time.

SW: I hope they understand the history of the brand, Jules’s story, and all the wonders of Brazil, especially because we have so many stones that are unique to the country. I also hope they see the creativity, the craftsmanship, and the different perspectives that define Sauer. It’s about understanding how you can take a heritage brand into the future while still respecting its past. Heritage shouldn’t be something that limits you but something that gives you more to build upon.

GS: I think we’ll always preserve it because whenever someone discovers Sauer, one of the first things they want to understand is how the company came to be and what makes us different. It’s inspiring to see how excited our team at the Madison Avenue store is to share that story. As we expand internationally, our Brazilian heritage will always remain an important part of our identity. I also feel that the pillars of the brand are timeless. Everyone can relate to nature, art, and spirituality, and people often find their own meaning in our jewelry. We’re also a multigenerational company, with people from the first and second generations alongside Gen Z. That range of perspectives keeps us fresh and allows us to continue evolving while staying true to our roots.

SW: We have a growing community of younger clients, and it’s interesting to see how different generations influence one another. Sometimes it’s the sons and daughters who influence their mothers. There’s a lot of meaning and symbolism in the jewelry. It helps people build connections and create their own personal interpretations. It’s all about connecting.

GS: I hope we still have the same energy and free spirit that we have today. I don’t necessarily think in terms of becoming a huge company with lots of stores. That’s not our approach. Of course, we may open new stores in new places, but I think it’s more about being in the right places and achieving greater global recognition. I feel that jewelry connects you to the real world. We live in a very digital world, and I think jewelry offers an opportunity to give clients real experiences and enhance human connection.

SW: I hope we continue to inspire people, connect stories, and create new stories through jewelry. I want more and more people to discover Sauer. Jewelry is very intimate. It’s something you wear on your body forever. You might wear an engagement ring for 40 years without ever taking it off. There’s almost nothing else in the world that you could wear every single day for your entire life. That intimacy and the fact that something can hold so much meaning are really unique to jewelry. Even before language existed, humans were adorning themselves.
