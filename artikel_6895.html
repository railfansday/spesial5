Jack O’Connell on McQueen, <i><em>Ink</em></i>, and the “Heavy Hitters” of <i><em>Sinners</em></i>

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

He may not be James Bond, but the actor is still dressed to kill.

Jack O’Connell may not be James Bond, but he’s still dressed to kill. At the 17th annual Governors Awards, hosted by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, the Sinners and 28 Years Later: The Bone Temple star rolled up in a mono-black suit sourced from McQueen’s Spring/Summer 2027 pre-collection: a tailored jacket with ultrawide lapels, black wool barathea trousers, and polished Jermyn brogues. If anyone at the awards exuded main-character energy, it was O’Connell.

“It’s a real lovely, beautiful chance to congregate and celebrate,” O’Connell tells me days later. “You come away from them feeling really inspired. There’s a lot of love in that room.”

He adds, “It is my mission now to binge Glenn Close [movies].” (The 79-year-old legend received an Academy Honorary Award for her many decades of contributions to the industry.)

But O’Connell’s all-black eveningwear, courtesy of McQueen, is award worthy on its own. No stranger to events like these, “sometimes you don’t wanna look uniform,” O’Connell says. The now grown-up James Cook from Skins opted for a double-breasted jacket with distinctly large lapels that he says were “almost a winged tip.” He felt it was something Alexander McQueen himself might have imagined. “Just a dapper bit of Lee McQueen—it’s nice, isn’t it? To push the boat out a little bit.”

He credits his stylist, Luke Day, for pointing him in the right direction. “We pick our moments. Sometimes we want to toe the line; sometimes we don’t. You don’t always feel like slapping a dicky bow tie on. It’s great. It mixes it up.”

When O’Connell isn’t mixing it up in front of flashing cameras, he likes to dress to his surroundings—as any man ought to. “If I’m in the countryside, it’s boots. If I’m in the city, it’s kicks. I really like a lot of Axel Arigatos on my feet. But Reebok was always the staple. I love a Reebok.”

The man could use a pair of Reeboks, because he’s had quite the year running ahead. In 2025, he was a most memorable menace in Ryan Coogler’s vampire scorcher Sinners. He kicked off 2026 in 28 Years Later: The Bone Temple as Jimmy Crystal, the bleach-blond cult leader of butt-kicking devil worshippers in the zombie apocalypse. He will soon star in Ink (which hits select theaters December 11 before it lands on Netflix on January 8), Danny Boyle’s screen adaptation of the stage play about the British tabloid The Sun. If it bleeds, it leads, indeed.

All the while, his name got thrown into the mix as the next James Bond. Though O’Connell had public support from friends like Boyle, he downplayed how much he was actually in the running (as in an October interview with Entertainment Weekly). Now that Robert Pattinson is 007, he doesn’t have to answer those questions anymore. “He’s one of the best in the biz, isn’t he? I love his work,” O’Connell says of R-Patz. “He’s a great set of hands. Anything he turns to, he just kinda nails. Really interesting actor, makes very unobvious choices. I’ve been a fan of his for a long time.”

Without Bond looming over him, O’Connell is free to focus on what he’s actually cooking up. Lately, he’s been hard at work on two big blockbusters for 2027: Godzilla x Kong: Supernova and A Quiet Place Part III. “It’s been one of my favorites for sure,” O’Connell says, reflecting on the past year. “Tied for going to the Oscars and watching my friends win trophies, it is up there as one of the best experiences of my life. I hope we keep pushing.”

So what can audiences expect to see in Supernova? Besides monster wrestling, of course. “We follow Kaitlyn [Dever]’s character up the ranks,” he explains. “She’s studying these monsters. Her brother’s part of a new organization. I can’t give too much away about it. And Trapper’s just being Trapper. You know how Trapper is.”

The project, with its green screens and CGI kaiju, is the biggest O’Connell has undertaken yet. But the large-scale endeavor brings him back to his childhood. “I remember kids running around the playground pretending to be Godzilla,” he says. “It’s omnipresent, isn’t it?”

He adds, “It’s not every job that I’m doing that type of thing. It was great to explore it to the maximum. You know who the main characters are. It’s not the people. It’s great supporting these pixelated legends.”

O’Connell can say even less about A Quiet Place Part III. “It’s in the aftermath of this horrific apocalyptic event,” he says. “It’s a tale of trauma, loss. It’s stuntier than any of us anticipated. But at the center of it is this very beautiful human story where we’re saying, What is the cost of all this fucking chaos and carnage, you know?”

But in some ways, O’Connell is still riding the high of Sinners. “People responded to it in such a way,” he says about the Oscar-winning thriller in retrospect. “It was great to see people unite at the cinema en masse. It’s great when one film can do that. I put it down to Ryan Coogler, our cast, and our entire team across the board. Everywhere you looked, it was packed with heavy hitters.”

Next time you catch O’Connell, bet he’s dressed in McQueen. “I really love what Alexander McQueen, as a person and as a creative, stood for,” he says. “We’re from similar backgrounds, which is rare for me in this world. It makes it meaningful. I celebrate his story, so any opportunity to work with them is a really heartfelt one.”

He swears you’ve seen nothing yet. “I don’t feel like I’ve done my best work yet. Far from it.”
