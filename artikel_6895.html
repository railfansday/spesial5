Upgrade Your Basics: Best Undershirts for Men in 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Just because you don't see a building's foundation doesn't mean it isn't holding everything up. In much the same way, the best undershirts for men are the humble base layer of countless great outfits. Many GQ staffers, in fact, wear-test theirs daily, and yet they're often overlooked, even though they've long-perfected their role: to be the protective layer between your gross body and your steezy, attention-hungry shirts and polos.

And while they're certainly good for some added warmth in the winter, there's a common misconception that they can make the wearer too hot. In fact, lightweight, moisture-wicking undershirts made from fabrics like modal, or cotton blends can actually transfer the sweat away from your body, speeding up your cooling down. All that's to say: Undershirts are more than just men’s underwear. They’re the cornerstone of a hall-of-fame fit.

An undershirt is an undershirt is an undershirt. If you want the most straightforward version, the kind that you’re picturing in your head is most likely from Calvin Klein. It’s a classic crewneck undershirt that’s made of a lightweight jersey that’s 100% cotton which has a smooth, very slightly brushed hand that feels worn-in in the best way possible. The slim fit won’t cause any weird bunching or bulging and it’s got enough length to stay tucked even when you’re out on the town cutting rugs.

My colleague, GQ senior commerce editor Avidan Grossman, has been wearing CK's genre-defining undershirts since his high school years. (He could do without the logo at the hem, but has made his peace with it, given that you never see it once it's tucked.) Otherwise, he prizes Calvin Klein's riff for its simple, pared-down design and blessed lack of modern-day frills. After all, not every shirt needs to moisture wick or odor block. If you just want an undershirt that gets the job done, don't overthink it.

Sometimes, sleeves are just too much undershirt. That’s where tanks come in. Also known as A-shirts, the humble, sleeveless tank has been rocked by every heartthrob and neighborhood grandpa as far back as anyone can remember. And while the low neck and lack of sleeves means you’ll practically never see them underneath a button-up, the irony is that they look great shown off with an unbuttoned camp shirt or on their own.

For the money, we think Hanes takes the cake with their multipack of undershirts. The stretchy ribbed jersey is made of 100% cotton and fits snug with a low neck that’s slightly squared off compared to most tanks. They may cost just under twenty bucks for a pack of six, but they look like a million bucks. “I’ve had buyer's remorse over many a menswear garment over the years,” explains GQ editor Jordan Bowman, "but the one thing that has never let me down is the humble tank top from Hanes. Undershirts are meant to be that intermediate layer between your outermost layers and your body, a protective guard that keeps your star garments fresh and free of sweat. Therefore, it's materials, construction, and fit that are the main components that determine how good an undershirt (and, well, every garment) actually is.

Above all else, your undershirt's fabric should be breathable, soft, comfortable, and, ideally, sweat-wicking. We primarily love undershirts and tanks that are made from high-quality 100% cotton, which has many of those characteristics naturally, but we also feel like there’s a time and place for synthetics, which are often used for their ability to wick away moisture and dry easily—especially for those who run particularly hot. And note: Stretchy materials like Spandex are helpful for comfort, but their elasticity will eventually degrade over time.

Undershirts should almost always fit closer to the body than your regular T-shirts. This is because they’ll be layered underneath another shirt or sweater, and can bunch in odd ways if too baggy. They don’t need to be skin-tight, but they should be slim enough to layer comfortably and long enough to tuck into your shirt. In fact, Peter Middleton, founder of Wythe, even told us that they should fit slim to start, since they are cotton and are knit, and will therefore stretch a little over time. If they are loose to start with, they are only going to become looser and saggier, which is really not the point.
