Kings' Darius Acuff Jr. recalls early starstruck moments from rookie NBA season

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Kings' Darius Acuff Jr. recalls early starstruck moments from rookie NBA season originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

Darius Acuff Jr., much to the Kings' delight, has transitioned easily to the NBA in his rookie season.

The freshly turned 20-year-old point guard is playing with veteran-like composure, leading all rookies with 25.7 points per game. But, that does not exclude him from having starstruck moments facing off against the league's premier players.

Acuff, in an interview with NBC Sports California's Deuce Mason and Morgan Ragan, noted three stars he has played against so far who made a greater impression on him in person than what he has seen on film: Miami Heat forward Giannis Antetokounmpo, Los Angeles Lakers guard Luka Dončić and Portland Trail Blazers guard Damian Lillard.

He noted that he hasn't had a signature "welcome to the NBA moment," but that Lillard was close to giving him one.

"If he would've hit probably like two more threes on me, he probably would've had me," Acuff explained. "But I think he hit two on me and then I fouled him on two, so it didn't look as crazy. But when he hit those two stepbacks, it was starting to look a little crazy.

"So, if he would've hit like two more, that probably would've been it."

Lillard, in the Kings' 120-107 loss to the Trail Blazers, scored 29 points with all six of his field goals made from three. Acuff scored 17 points in that game – just the third time he has been held under 20 points all season.

Despite the Lillard flurry, Acuff has held his own so far in his rookie season. He hasn't been hit with an embarrassing moment that would qualify as a "welcome to the NBA" moment.

"Nobody [has] just killed me, or like, I got dunked on like that – none of that happened yet," Acuff said. "So as far as right now, no. But we're going to pray that doesn't happen."

After the game, Lillard gave Acuff plenty of praise for the rookie's proven ability early into his career. Acuff said he was appreciative of it, especially because he has modeled his game after Lillard's.

"He's one of the guys that I watch because I see a lot of similarities in our game. Especially as far as scoring as point guards and how much gravity we create, so, it's definitely great," Acuff said. "We saw it [in the Kings-Blazers game] how great of a player he still is. And I'm still learning. I'm still watching those guys no matter what. But just taking that challenge of getting better every game, every day, has been the best part I would say."

Acuff is quickly getting acquainted to NBA life on the court, but he's also trying to get acquainted to life off the court as well. Now that he has reached the professional level, he can enjoy the fruits of his labor with his family, but he's learning it's a hard balance to strike.

"I'm a person that likes to sit in the house a lot, and I'm realizing you don't really have to do that a lot in the NBA. Like, you're older now, you can explore life a little bit, and a lot of people have been telling me that," Acuff explained. "So, I'm trying, but it's hard because; you got a game, get home at 11, and you wake up and you're like, 'I got a game tomorrow.' So, you'd be like, 'Yeah, I want to work out then I'm chilling.'

When it comes to spending time with his family, Acuff said that his loved ones understand the time commitment necessary for being a professional basketball player.

At just 20 years old, Acuff has plenty of time to figure out everything on and off the court that will allow him to fully enjoy his NBA career.
