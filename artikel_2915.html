Josh Hazlewood hints at quitting if 'great leader' Pat Cummins loses captaincy as sandpapergate rocks Australia

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Australia fast bowler Josh Hazlewood has said he would consider quitting the national team if Pat Cummins were removed as Test captain over allegations that he knew about the ball-tampering scandal during the 2018 tour of South Africa.Hazlewood backed Cummins after Usman Khawaja, David Warner and, more recently, Cameron Bancroft claimed that "everyone knew" about the use of sandpaper to alter the condition of the ball. The 35-year-old supported Cummins' version of events that he was not involved in the plan.Hazlewood, who has taken 313 Test wickets, had signed a document in 2021 along with Cummins, Mitchell Starc and Nathan Lyon, denying any prior knowledge of sandpaper being "taken onto the field".He described the criticism directed at Cummins since Bancroft published his 4,000-word column earlier this week as "a bit of a pile on" and backed the Australia captain's handling of the issue."To get the group where it is now and in a position where something like that (sandpaper) is never going to happen again – I think is just a remarkable job," Hazlewood told Code Sports, as quoted by Fox Sports."He's a great leader. If you went around and asked everyone in the team, you'd hear the same response.""I think if we turned up for the first Test and he wasn't the captain and it was someone else, honestly, I don't know how that would go."Australia will face New Zealand in Perth in the first Test of the home summer, starting on December 9.Hazlewood also said he would consider not playing his 82nd Test if Cummins were forced to step down following Bancroft's claim that the Australia captain knew about the ball-tampering eight years ago."I think I'd have to be put in that position to make a decision, but you'd have to think about it, yeah," he said.Like Cummins, Hazlewood has publicly stood by his account of the events in 2018, saying he had "no knowledge" of ball-tampering during the South Africa series."We were interviewed straight after (Cape Town) and I trust what I said in that two-three hour interrogation more than anything because it was in the day or two after," Hazlewood said."It was fresh in my mind at that point. Two things stand out for me from what's been said. Was I an architect in the plan to tamper with the ball with sandpaper? And was I aware (of) sandpaper or foreign substances being used on the field?"The answer is no. When it boils down to it I think those two points are crucial."The plan to use sandpaper, which was available in the dressing room for players to use on their bats, was first discussed during the tea break on the second day of the second Test at Port Elizabeth, when Australia were behind in the match.In his recent column, Bancroft alleged that then head coach Darren Lehmann and bowling coach David Saker were present when Warner first suggested using sandpaper
