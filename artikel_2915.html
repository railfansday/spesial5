Billionaire Stanley Druckenmiller Sold 94% of His TSMC Stake and All of His Alphabet Shares in Q3. Here’s the Brilliant Stock He Added.

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The market has changed since these moves were made.

By Keithen Drury – Nov 25, 2026 at 11:17AM EST

With the latest rounds of 13Fs out, investors get to see what billionaire hedge fund managers were up to in the third quarter. One investor I follow is Stanley Druckenmiller, who runs the Duquesne Family Office. Druckenmiller has a strong history of picking great stocks, and he made some interesting moves in Q3.

But should you follow his lead? Let's take a look at what he did.

Druckenmiller hasn't allocated a large portion of his portfolio to AI-related stocks, and in Q3, he sold two prominent ones. He fully exited his 336,300-share position in Alphabet (GOOG -4.69%) (GOOGL -5.06%), which is actually something he has done before. He held Alphabet shares in Q4 2025, then sold that position entirely in Q1 before buying the stock again in Q2. With Alphabet being one of the poster children for the AI build-out, this sale is notable.

Taiwan Semiconductor (TSM -7.23%) is another company often cited as one of the best AI investments, as it manufactures the lion’s share of the high-end chips that end up in nearly every AI computing unit. It has had tremendous returns over the past few years, and Druckenmiller has maintained a large position since Q4 of last year. However, he sold off around 94% of his TSMC holdings — his No. 2 position as of the end of Q2 — and he may close it fully in Q4 (if he hasn't bought any recently).

Those were two notable sales, but what did he buy instead?

Druckenmiller added a fair bit of Amazon (AMZN -5.75%) stock to his portfolio during Q3, bringing it from his ninth-largest position to his third-largest position. Amazon is still somewhat of an AI investment, as it gets a hefty chunk of its operating profit from its cloud computing division, Amazon Web Services (AWS). AWS has seen its growth rate boosted by rising cloud workloads, driven by increased AI usage.

These were some interesting moves by Druckenmiller, but is that still the right plan? I'd say no. These holdings were as of Sept. 30, and a lot has shifted in the market since then.

Since Q3 ended, Amazon has essentially been flat. Meanwhile, Alphabet has sold off by more than 20%, while Taiwan Semiconductor is down by 30%. Those were major declines, and Druckenmiller got out ahead of them, which suggests that he saw which way the wind would be blowing.

However, selling those stocks now could be a mistake because the prices are so much lower. In fact, Druckenmiller could be buying those two stocks back right now, but we won't know until Q4 data is released in mid-February.

With that in mind, I think investors should consider doing at this point the exact opposite of what the Duquesne Family Office did in Q3, as the landscape has shifted over the past month and a half.
