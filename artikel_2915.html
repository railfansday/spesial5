Social Security benefits are rising in 2027, but other changes could affect what you keep

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Whether you're working or retired, changes to Social Security in 2027 could affect your finances.

Starting in January, millions of program beneficiaries will get a 3.6% increase to their benefits, thanks to the annual cost-of-living adjustment. But rising Medicare premiums could chip away at the increase — and as beneficiaries' incomes rise, that may affect the taxes they owe and their eligibility for a new senior deduction.

Meanwhile, a higher taxable earnings cap means some workers will pay Social Security taxes on more income, reducing their paycheck.

Here's a breakdown of what to expect in the new year.

Social Security's limit for payroll taxes is adjusted every year to reflect the average wage index.

In 2027, the taxable maximum will go up to $192,300 in earnings, up from $184,500 in 2026. Workers and employers each pay a 6.2% payroll tax on earnings up to that limit — or 12.4% for self-employed workers. Medicare taxes of 1.45% for both workers and employers are not subject to a limit.

In 2027, workers who earn $192,300 or more will pay $11,922.60 in total Social Security payroll taxes, according to the Social Security Administration, while self-employed workers will pay $23,845.20.

"If you're somebody whose income is over what the new threshold is going to be, this increase in the threshold results in a tax increase to you," said Tim Steffen, director of advanced planning at financial services firm Baird.

The 3.6% Social Security cost-of-living adjustment for 2027 is slated to increase retirement benefits by about $75 per month on average, according to the Social Security Administration.

The annual adjustment is the largest boost to benefits since 2023 due to rising inflation. The COLA averaged 2.6% from 2000 to 2026, according to the Senior Citizens League, a nonpartisan senior group.

Around 72 million Social Security beneficiaries will see benefit increases, according to the agency.

The maximum benefit for a worker at full retirement age — when individuals become eligible for 100% of the benefits they've earned — will rise to $4,188 per month in 2027, up from $4,152 per month in 2026, according to the federal agency. To be eligible for the maximum benefit at full retirement age — typically age 66 to 67 — you generally have to earn up to the taxable threshold for each of your working years factored into the calculation.

How much of the COLA increase Social Security beneficiaries see depends in part on their Medicare premiums, which may be deducted directly from benefit checks.

Monthly rates are poised to increase for both Medicare Part B, which covers medically necessary services and preventive care, and Part D, which provides prescription drug coverage.

The standard monthly Medicare Part B premium will be $209.40 in 2027 — a $6.50 increase from $202.90 in 2026, according to the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services.

"It's not often that we see a Part B [premium] increase that is lower than the COLA," Mary Johnson, an independent Social Security and Medicare policy analyst, said of the 3.2% increase. The last time that happened was in 2023, she said.

Medicare Part D beneficiaries may also pay higher premiums in 2027.

Assuming enrollees keep the same plan, the average enrollment-weighted monthly Part D premium may be $51 in 2027, up 50% from $34 in 2026, according to projections from KFF, a provider of health policy research and polling. That is higher than CMS' projections, which forecast the national average premium for standalone Part D plans will rise about $1, to $36, in 2027 from $35 in 2026.

Standard monthly premiums for Part B and plan-specific premiums for Part D apply to individuals who have $112,000 or less in modified adjusted gross income, or $224,000 for married couples. Beneficiaries with higher income pay larger premiums because of income-related monthly adjustment amounts, which affect 9% of Medicare Part B and Part D beneficiaries, according to CMS.

With bigger Social Security benefits, more households may find that some portion of benefits is taxable — though a new senior deduction may help provide some relief to those who are eligible.

Social Security benefits may be subject to federal income taxes for certain individuals. The thresholds at which those taxes are applied are not adjusted from year to year.

Individuals who are subject to taxes may opt to withhold taxes from their Social Security benefit checks, rather than face a potential bill during tax filing season.

Separately, a temporary senior deduction of up to $6,000 for individuals ages 65 and over may help to indirectly reduce taxes on Social Security benefits.

That deduction, part of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act in 2025, helps reduce retirees' taxable income, according to Steffen. It's in place through 2028. Because the break phases out for individuals with modified adjusted gross income starting at $75,000 — and for married couples with $150,000 — it's important to watch your income if you're close to those limits, Steffen said.

"Keep in mind that if you're over 65, you might qualify for this deduction," Steffen said. "But if you let your income get too big, you might lose the deduction."

Moreover, any extra income that causes you to lose the deduction may also come with its own taxes, making it more expensive, according to Steffen.

"What we've been telling our clients is that tax planning is a lot more complicated than it used to be, and you can't do it on the back of an envelope anymore," he said. "You need to use good tools that can do good calculations for you."

Individuals who claim Social Security retirement benefits before their full retirement age and who continue to work may be subject to withholdings.

In 2027, individuals under full retirement age may earn up to $25,560 per year, up from $24,480 in 2026, without adjustments to their Social Security benefits. For every $2 in earnings above that limit, $1 in Social Security benefits will be withheld.

Importantly, those withholdings are not lost completely. Once a beneficiary reaches full retirement age, the withheld money will be credited to their benefits over their remaining lifespan.

In the year individuals reach full retirement age, the retirement earnings test limit is higher — $67,920 per year in 2027, up from $65,160 per year in 2026. For every $3 in earnings above that limit, $1 in Social Security benefits is withheld.

Starting the month a beneficiary reaches full retirement age, no earnings are withheld.
