Magnite vs. The Trade Desk: Which Ad Tech Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Two ad-tech leaders, two very different business models, and a 2027 pick that depends on which side of the programmatic ad transaction appears stronger right now.

By Robert Izquierdo – Updated Nov 18, 2026 at 11:36PM EST

Automated, or programmatic, advertising is reshaping the media landscape by providing more transparency and efficiency for both ad buyers and sellers. Choosing between Magnite (MGNI -2.46%) and The Trade Desk (TTD +3.95%) requires deciding which side of the transaction offers the most potential.

Magnite focuses on helping media owners maximize the value of their digital inventory, while The Trade Desk empowers agencies and brands to buy that inventory effectively. Both companies are at the center of the shift toward programmatic advertising, making them essential players in the evolving digital ecosystem.

Magnite operates in the digital advertising market, with a strong presence among online publishers and content providers requiring automated tools to sell inventory. As a supply-side platform, the company provides the technology that media owners use to manage their ad space. Two major buyers indirectly contributed nearly 44% of its revenue in 2025. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached $766.7 million, representing growth of 9.1% compared to the prior year. The company reported net income of $180.8 million during this period. The performance reflects a steady expansion in its ability to monetize video and connected TV inventory across global markets.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 0.7x. This ratio compares total debt to shareholder equity to show how much leverage a company uses. The current ratio, which measures if a company can cover its short-term obligations with its current assets, was approximately 1.0x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was $165.6 million. Note that stock-based compensation (SBC) represented 32.5% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

The Trade Desk provides a self-service, cloud-based platform that allows advertising buyers to manage data-driven campaigns across multiple devices. The company primarily serves advertising agencies and major brands that need to optimize their spending in real time. In 2025, two unnamed holding companies each represented more than 10% of the company's gross billings. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue was $3.0 billion, which was a 7% increase over the previous 12-month period. Net income for that same time frame reached $329.4 million. This steady top-line growth is supported by its broad network of hundreds of integrated ad exchanges and data vendors.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the company maintained a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.2x. The current ratio stood at 1.6x, indicating a solid buffer of liquid assets to handle short-term liabilities. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow reached $795.7 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented 49.4% of operating cash flow.

The Trade Desk trades at a significantly lower multiple of its sales, while Magnite offers a higher price relative to its future earnings estimates.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

Magnite and The Trade Desk represent opposite sides of the digital advertising coin. I believe the buy side, represented by The Trade Desk, is the stronger part of the ecosystem, as demonstrated by the rise of businesses such as Amazon. However, between these two ad tech giants, I recommend investing in Magnite right now.

The Trade Desk has been struggling as competition has intensified. Amazon and other new competitors battling for ad buying dollars, including many streaming services, have grown in recent years. Also, the artificial intelligence boom led to greater adoption of AI tools that appear to be eating into The Trade Desk's offerings.

The company exited 2025 with 18% year-over-year revenue growth to $2.9 billion, yet sales expansion has been elusive in 2026. In its latest quarterly earnings report, for the third quarter of 2026, The Trade Desk's sales fell 0.5% year over year to $736 million.

Magnite's business is headed in the opposite direction, as sales growth has accelerated. In Q1 of 2026, revenue rose 6% year over year. That jumped to 11% in Q2, then 14% in Q3. While the advent of new businesses into digital advertising has hurt The Trade Desk, this has helped Magnite, with streaming companies using its platform to monetize their content.
