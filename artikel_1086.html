Chelsea bullied by Brian Brobbey and Sunderland

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Talk about cramming everything in. Brian Brobbey only played for 67 minutes but, in that time, he collected a yellow card, ripped Levi Colwill’s shirt and earned himself a standing ovation after morphing into a battering ram.

Régis Le Bris, Sunderland’s head coach, said there was nobody like the Netherlands striker in the Premier League and it said everything that everyone wanted to make Brobbey centre of attention after a long overdue home win, even though he had not scored. Everyone, that is, bar Xabi Alonso.

“No, he’s not unstoppable,” Chelsea’s head coach replied when asked why his team had found Brobbey too much to handle. “He’s stoppable – but only when you defend better than we did. We were not bullied. We didn’t produce enough.”

That is one way of putting it. Another would be to say Brobbey was everything that Chelsea, much to Alonso’s disgust, were not: a bristling menace, a steamroller wearing a pair of boots, a player on a mission to help transform the mood – Enzo Le Fée’s decisive spot kick owed everything to Brobbey.

Chelsea arrived on Wearside looking to establish themselves in the top four ahead of the break but, instead, Alonso watched through his fingers as Sunderland reminded everyone why they were the joyous story of last season, a beautiful mix of inspiration and perspiration.

In fairness to the Chelsea manager, he refused to get caught in the minutiae of contentious penalty decisions – one that Chelsea were denied, another that they conceded – and could only lament as Sunderland ground out their first Premier League win since August 30, 70 long days ago.

“It wasn’t the best game,” Alonso said. “I think that we didn’t deserve more than probably one point, maybe. We need to be more consistent on the move, to get the ball from one side to the other, and we didn’t do that good enough. We didn’t produce enough to deserve to win.”

Unlike Sunderland. If you had turned up at the Stadium of Light without looking at the Premier League table, you would not have believed the hosts were bottom and you would not have given credence to the idea Le Bris, who has worked miracles these past two years, was under pressure.

Things, however, could not have started worse for Le Bris. Though Granit Xhaka has not shown last season’s form in these challenging opening months, the game plan was built around him, so to see the Sunderland captain hobbling off after three minutes was symptomatic of how things have gone for the Wearsiders.

“He felt something in the final sprint of the warm-up,” Le Bris explained. “We don’t know how bad it is.”

He is not the kind of coach, though, who will bemoan bad fortune. Le Bris sent on Jules Ahoka to replace Xhaka and watched as he, along with colleagues in red and white, set about trampling all over Chelsea’s intentions with incessant hard work.

Yet there was more to it. Sunderland can play, either intricately or directly, and a lovely move in the 12th minute needed a desperate, sliding intervention from Pep Chavarría’s to stop Trai Hume’s volley sliding in at the far post.

In the next attack, another Chemsdine Talbi cross – the Moroccan having been found by a peach of a pass from goalkeeper Robin Roefs – invited Brobbey to inflict maximum damage, but the Dutchman made a hash of his opportunity.

The momentum could have changed completely in the 23rd minute. Alonso, his players and staff were convinced they would be given a penalty after Cole Palmer tumbled when his legs tangled with Luke O’Nien but, after a lengthy delay, VAR Nick Hopton felt there was no case to answer.

Hopton was involved again in the 33rd minute but, as Alonso said, Chelsea only had themselves to blame. Colwill simply could not contain Brobbey when he began marauding, grappling, grabbing and clipping the Dutchman’s heels. Le Fée did the rest, sending Mike Penders the wrong way.

From that point, Chelsea were in trouble, no matter how many changes Alonso made. They huffed, puffed, but nothing worked. They had come up against a red-and-white wall, fortified by constant backing from the stands, and the west Londoners were never going to knock it down.

One incident, as the clock ticked towards 90 minutes, encapsulated everything good about this Sunderland display when O’Nien chased down substitute Geovany Quenda to make a challenge. Another moment of defending from Thomas Meunier on Morgan Rogers was exceptional.

“You could see our identity and our togetherness and the way we managed the game,” said Le Bris, who was booked when hectoring referee Jarred Gillett. “These three points were critical. It was a struggle at times to manage situations. We showed as a group real heart.”

And nobody did that more than Brobbey. No wonder Le Bris will be hoping Xavi Hernández, the Netherlands head coach, handles him with care over the next 10 days. On this form, he will be the fuel that drives Sunderland’s quest for survival.

For Sunderland that argument is easy to make – they are bottom of the Premier League, are without a win since August and for morale, if nothing else need at least a point, though three is really what the doctor orders.

For Chelsea – fourth in the table and with three wins in their past four league outings – the argument that this is a must-win game is harder to make, but bear with me here...

Facing a Sunderland team who are suffering from the worst of both worlds – blunt in attack and pitiful in defence (what a difference from last season...) and have proven all too easy to beat (as their 2-0 home defeat Zagreb illustrated on Thursday) – the west Londoners are expected to win.
