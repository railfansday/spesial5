7 Video Games That Could Sweep in The Game Awards, Ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Ronald is a video game journalist with more than 10 years of experience in the industry, working on several of the most important Spanish websites.

Winter holidays bring joy through family gatherings and discounted video games, but they have also become synonymous with awards ceremonies celebrating the year's finest releases.

In the case at hand, 2026 has established itself as a magnificent trip around the sun thanks to the numerous masterful titles that have been, or will be, released during its 12 months, making for a fiercely contested race to see which is the best.

The number of events held for this purpose is as vast as it is diverse, although all eyes are on December 10th, the day the interactive industry's Ballon d'Or is awarded.

Therefore, if you're as eager as I am to see if any video game can come close to Clair Obscur: Expedition 33's phenomenal success last year, I recommend you read this list of seven video games that could sweep The Game Awards, ranked.

The first and most underrated video game to appear on this list is none other than Big Walk, a game that will arrive completely silent despite having the power to surprise more than a few on this special night.

That it will win Best Multiplayer should be a given, but this would be merely the bare minimum of the recognition that House House's team has earned through one of the most special and creative indie games of recent years.

Because of this, and considering it's this year's highest-rated game on Metacritic if we don't count remasters and remakes, there's more than enough reason to believe it could become 2026's most acclaimed independent production.

My bets for Game Direction and Audio Design are bolder, especially since these are usually awarded to larger projects with bigger budgets. However, given what happened last year, I wouldn't rule out Big Walk silencing more than a few detractors.

A particularly curious case is that of Control Resonant, which presents itself as a solid piece in numerous departments, even though I don't believe its overall acclaim is sufficient to be a Game of the Year contender.

Said detail aside, Remedy knows what it's like to have a string of successes after Alan Wake 2 won three of its eight nominations in 2023, and its latest project could repeat that feat with even greater force.

Specifically, in terms of the audiovisual component, it's by far one of the year's strongest contenders, which hasn't stood out for its narratives or art direction at Control Resonant's level, except in the indie scene.

That a title can win so many awards without being Game of the Year already happened in 2022 with God of War: Ragnarök, and for reasons very similar to those experienced by Kratos's latest adventure, I think we could be facing the same scenario now.

Capcom has been, arguably by a wide margin, the most outstanding developer of the year, having released Monster Hunter Stories 3, Pragmata, Onimusha: Way of the Sword, and, most relevantly for this occasion, Resident Evil: Requiem.

Angela Sant'Albano's stellar performance as Grace Ashcroft already guarantees a strong chance of winning an award despite the fierce competition, as does its status as the best horror game of the year and all that entails in terms of gameplay and audio design.

From there, Resident Evil: Requiem faces an uphill battle to win all the awards for which it will surely be a candidate, as it is undeniably one of the best games of the year.

Will being the best Resident Evil be enough to secure every possible nomination? Time will tell, but its candidacy and the seriousness with which it must be approached can be anticipated from this very moment.

The Game Awards has six awesome titles up for Game Of The Year in 2025, but here are seven more that still feel like they've been snubbed.

The Game Awards' biggest winners last year ranged from indie to AA titles, opening up a world of possibilities for this and future iterations, especially for extraordinary adventures like Mina the Hollower.

The current lineup isn't as fertile for indie products to take center stage as it did in 2025, but I'm certain that, if any prospect can truly achieve that, it's Yacht Club Games' masterpiece.

As for Best Indie Game, Best Action-Adventure Game, and Game Direction, I'd bet my life that this wonderful ode to classic video games will be in the running, aiming to take home those awards, though it's outstanding enough in the other categories to warrant consideration as well.

The interactive medium's pendulum is increasingly swinging in favor of indie games in these turbulent times, where the biggest companies keep making bad decisions, so Mina the Hollower could capitalize on those protest votes.

The year's not-so-surprise surprise is Phantom Blade Zero, which stands on the shoulders of Black Myth: Wukong to likely become the Chinese video game with the most nominations and prizes at The Game Awards.

As part of the top three contenders for the People's Choice Award, I see S-Game's gem as a work robust enough to compete with the two titans that you and I both know round out this list, which says everything you need to know about its quality.

Its greatest strength is its unique character, stemming from a singular design, composition, and presentation philosophy that we've seen only a handful of times, allowing it to stand out with literally unparalleled ease and fluidity among the rest.

It has more than enough quality to win any title it's given, so much so that, if we were in any other year of the decade, I could easily predict that Phantom Blade Zero would be GOTY.

The Best Games For Impact Nominees Of All Time are emotional and thought-provoking experiences.

Nevertheless, the year is 2026, and it just so happens that it coincides with the return of what many consider the best video game of all time, The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake.

If there's one reason why I feel most action-adventure games will have a tough time, it's because the quintessential adventure has returned, the one that paved the way for the entire genre in the three decades following its release.

From the music to its underrated art direction, including its technical and gameplay splendor, it's safe to say that the Ocarina of Time remake will be present even in the Muppet segments created by the legendary Geoff Keighley.

In 1998, there was no The Game Awards to give Link's odyssey the recognition it deserved, so don't be surprised if the video game industry, as a whole, makes this year the perfect moment to rectify that omission.

If we take into account what its immediate predecessor did and what its creators have promised to do with it, there's no doubt that the top contender to sweep The Game Awards is Grand Theft Auto 6.

Rockstar is fully aware that, every year they release a game, December becomes a month of metaphorical and material praise, given their habit of releasing the most ambitious and massive titles the industry is ready to see.

Link is a formidable competitor, and that's indisputable, yet Lucia and Jason have the added momentum of being the most anticipated video game in history and the most expensive ever made, making them immediate candidates for a third of the event's categories.

It's difficult to predict with certainty, though if there's one thing I can be partially sure of, it's that Ocarina of Time and GTA 6 will quickly make this article obsolete by taking home practically every award between them.

These titles were left at the gates of glory, but they deserved to be called Game of the Year.
