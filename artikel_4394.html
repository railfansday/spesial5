Tesla (TSLA), SpaceX race for year-end merger deal before new Congress

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Tesla (TSLA) and SpaceX are working toward a year-end deadline to agree on their all-stock merger, according to the Financial Times. A signed deal by December 31 would land days before the new Congress, where Democrats have promised to dig into Musk’s companies, is sworn in on January 3.

The FT also named the directors negotiating for each side. Two of Tesla’s three are the same people who designed Musk’s $1 trillion pay package.

We’ve been tracking the Tesla-SpaceX merger since Musk first floated it in May, when we called it Musk’s fourth billion-dollar self-deal, and his first trillion-dollar self-deal. Earlier this month, Bloomberg reported that SpaceX could announce the deal as soon as December. The FT report adds a deadline, names and a vote threshold.

According to the FT, the talks are “moving towards a potential year-end deadline,” even though people familiar with the negotiations say an agreement isn’t close. The combined group would be worth about $3.5 trillion, by far the largest merger ever.

The new Congress is seated on January 3. As of November 24, AP’s count has Democrats at 258 House seats and 56 Senate seats. Democrats haven’t been subtle about what comes next: Sen. Ruben Gallego tagged Musk on X with “See you in January,” and SpaceX’s military contracts and Musk’s Starlink decisions over Ukraine are expected to be early investigation targets.

It’s not only Washington. Texas’ incoming attorney general told Bloomberg that a merger of this size “warrants tremendous scrutiny,” and Bloomberg had previously reported that the companies want to close before President Trump leaves office.

Tesla’s special committee is chair Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson and Jack Hartung, advised by Evercore. SpaceX is represented by Roelof Botha and Randy Glein, with Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs as advisers. Both banks also helped take SpaceX public in June.

Denholm and Wilson-Thompson should sound familiar. They were the two-person special committee that built Musk’s new CEO pay package after a Delaware judge voided his 2018 award. And that 2025 award has change-in-control terms that drop its operational milestones and vest earned shares at the deal price, so the committee that wrote it is now negotiating the deal that could trigger it.

Ira Ehrenpreis, a long-time friend of Musk, who chairs Tesla’s compensation and governance committees and has sat on SpaceX’s board since February, isn’t on Tesla’s committee, per the FT.

If you can believe it, Musk isn’t formally in the room. The FT says Texas governance rules keep him out of active negotiations, but both committees can consult senior executives for information. Every one of those executives reports to Musk.

Any agreement goes to a separate extraordinary Tesla shareholder meeting, and it needs more than 50% of all outstanding votes, not just of votes cast. Abstentions and uncast shares effectively count as “no.”

That sounds like a high bar. But Musk holds about 20% of Tesla’s voting power, and nothing in the report says his shares will be excluded. If they count, he’s 40% of the way to approval before a single other shareholder votes. On the other side, Musk controls about 84% of SpaceX’s votes, so there’s no real suspense there.

Uncast retail votes are exactly what Tesla’s new auto-vote program, rolled out in its October proxy, was designed to reduce.

Then there’s the regulatory side. The FT says the deal could draw scrutiny in the US, China, Europe and the UK, with Tesla’s China operations sitting awkwardly next to SpaceX’s rocket and Starlink business, which does a lot of work for the US military. Giga Shanghai builds more than half of Tesla’s cars.

Congress can’t block this merger. Antitrust review sits with Trump’s DOJ and FTC until 2029, plus China, the EU and the UK.

It's probably already too late to escape Jan 3. The DoJ investigation into the merger will take a minimum of a few months. At the very least it looks like there is a 30 mandatory waiting period, so if the merger isn't announced by Dec 4, that 30 days takes them to Jan 3. That gives them 10 calendar days to finish negotiations and get stock holder approval and they have to give time for the voters to get their votes in. So short of some miracle, Musk isn't going to get this done before Jan 3 ... at least from my understanding and counting.

What congressional committees can do is subpoena. Committee minutes, the Evercore fairness work, emails between Denholm’s committee and Musk’s executives about how the exchange ratio got set. That’s the kind of record that got Musk’s 2018 pay package voided by a Delaware judge, and it’s the kind of record nobody wants dragged into a hearing mid-negotiation.

The talks are “not close,” yet there’s a deadline. When the deadline comes from the political calendar instead of the negotiation, the side that wants to close fast is the one giving ground. But in this case, Musk is both sides, but he is mainly SpaceX.

We’ve said it before, and we’ll keep saying it: the only vote that means anything here is a majority of Tesla shareholders not named Musk. Denholm, who is up for re-election at next week’s December 3 annual meeting, could commit to that today, but in her entire tenure as Tesla chair, she always did exactly what Musk wanted.
