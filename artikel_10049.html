StubHub International warns customer data may have left the venue

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Names and contact details potentially exposed, ticket reseller finds no evidence passwords or payment information were accessed

Ticket resale giant StubHub International has warned customers that a recent breach may have exposed their personal information to cybercriminals.

The company said it detected "unauthorized access to certain systems" on its platform between late October and early November.

Customer notification emails seen by The Register list potentially affected information including names, email addresses, phone numbers, and country or regional settings.

StubHub International, operated by Ticketbis S.L., apologized for the intrusion and offered the usual promise to implement security enhancements after the fact.

The company said it had addressed "certain vulnerabilities" while containing the incident and was continuing to monitor for suspicious activity.

Outside cybersecurity experts are assisting with the ongoing investigation. The company warned that any exposed information could be used to make subsequent scams more convincing.

"To date, we have found no evidence of unauthorised access to account passwords, banking information or payment card details," the email stated. "We also have no evidence that the compromised information has been used fraudulently.

"However, there is a risk that third parties could use this information to attempt fraud, identity theft or phishing through emails, messages or calls that appear to come from StubHub International or other trusted organizations."

The Register contacted StubHub International for more information, such as how many customers were affected, but it did not respond.

Digital Fuel Capital bought StubHub International from Viagogo in 2021 after the UK's Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) required the international business to be sold to address competition concerns.

In June, the CMA fined Ticketbis £889,200 and ordered refunds exceeding £590,000 after finding that mandatory fees were omitted from upfront ticket prices.

StubHub International also recently faced criticism for allowing a single ticket tout to advertise £1 million worth of U2 tickets, despite its stated opposition to ticket-buying bots. ®
