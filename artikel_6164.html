Allegiant Air Cuts More Sun Country Flights From Minneapolis Despite “Really Strong” Demand

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Allegiant Air is preparing to further reduce Sun Country Airlines' passenger schedule at Minneapolis–Saint Paul International Airport (MSP), despite acknowledging that demand in the Twin Cities remains exceptionally strong. During its third-quarter 2026 earnings call on November 4, Allegiant CEO Greg Anderson confirmed that Sun Country's pilot staffing levels remain below requirements, limiting the airline's ability to operate its planned services. The latest comments raise further questions about how quickly the Minnesota-based carrier can rebuild its network after Allegiant acquired it in May.

The situation matters because Minneapolis is Sun Country's home market and one of the most important battlegrounds in its competition with Delta Air Lines. While Allegiant has repeatedly promised to restore and expand the airline's presence, Delta's substantial operations and growing recruitment efforts have created additional challenges. With fewer Sun Country flights available, Minneapolis travelers could also face reduced competition and potentially higher fares on popular leisure routes.

Allegiant's latest financial update indicates that Sun Country's operational difficulties are continuing into the final months of 2026. Anderson acknowledged that pilot attrition has improved since the previous earnings call, but staffing remains insufficient to support the airline's desired schedule. As a result, the company expects to continue operating below its intended passenger capacity as recruitment and training progress.

The reductions are noticeable at MSP, where Sun Country has historically provided an important low-cost alternative to Delta. Earlier cuts removed about 348 September departures, representing roughly one-third of the airline's previously scheduled flights that month. According to the Minnesota Star Tribune, October's schedule was subsequently reduced by around 20% year over year, while November's was approximately 8% lighter. The latest reporting on Sun Country's Minneapolis reductions illustrates how the changes are affecting passengers.

Despite the continued reductions, Anderson emphasized that the underlying market remains attractive, and the airline plans to expand there and to restore capacity as staffing improves, with recruitment classes already underway. During the November 4 earnings call, he said the following:

"The demand in the Twin Cities is really strong. We want to add capacity there. So for our Sun Country flying, I would expect we'll start adding back flying as the staffing levels rebuild. We have Allegiant. Expect sometime in 2027 to be able to fly Allegiant aircraft from Allegiant bases into the Twin Cities and add some incremental flying. "

One of Sun Country's biggest challenges is competition for experienced pilots, particularly from Delta. After Allegiant's acquisition, many Sun Country pilots reportedly left for larger airlines, with many joining Delta. Allegiant previously acknowledged that pilot attrition was much higher than anticipated, creating an unexpected operational challenge during the integration process.

Delta's position makes the situation particularly difficult. Minneapolis is one of its most important hubs, and the airline consistently accounts for approximately 70% of the airport's passenger market. Its extensive domestic and international network, larger fleet, and recruitment capabilities give it considerable advantages over Sun Country. As reported in our coverage of Sun Country's pilot departures, pilots moving to Delta have become a major operational challenge for the airline.

The consequences extend beyond staffing. Sun Country has also expanded its cargo operations, particularly through its partnership with Amazon. Cargo flights have increasingly competed for available aircraft and crew resources, adding pressure on scheduled passenger services. Meanwhile, Delta can potentially benefit from reduced competition on routes where Sun Country previously offered lower fares. Although this does not mean Delta will automatically replace every discontinued service, its dominant position gives it considerable influence over the Minneapolis market.

However, Sun Country still has an important competitive advantage: its low-cost airline business model, affordable fares, and focus on leisure destinations could help it attract price-sensitive travelers who might otherwise choose Delta, allowing the Minnesota-based carrier to remain a meaningful competitor at its home airport.

The situation also raises questions about Allegiant's original ambitions for the acquisition. When the deal was announced, the combined company presented Minneapolis as a major growth opportunity, promising additional destinations and improved passenger connectivity. However, the immediate aftermath has been considerably more complicated, with workforce departures and schedule reductions undermining those ambitions.

Allegiant nevertheless insists that the current situation is temporary. Anderson indicated that recruitment classes are full and that the company expects to begin restoring Sun Country's passenger capacity as more pilots become available. Furthermore, Allegiant expects to introduce its own aircraft into Minneapolis operations sometime in 2027, potentially adding incremental capacity from its existing bases. The company sees the Twin Cities as a long-term growth opportunity, not a market it intends to abandon.

Nevertheless, Delta's growing presence represents a considerable competitive threat. With Sun Country temporarily reducing its operations, Delta could strengthen its position on overlapping routes, potentially making it harder for the low-cost carrier to regain market share. Discussions among Minneapolis travelers on Reddit also highlight concerns about pilot recruitment, competition, and future ticket prices. Whether Allegiant can reverse the trend will depend on how quickly it rebuilds Sun Country's workforce and restores the capacity that made the airline an important competitor at MSP.
