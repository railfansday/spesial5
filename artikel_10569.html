Workers are willing to give up higher pay for one thing, new global survey finds

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Time really is money to many workers.

A better salary is no longer the top reason why workers will consider leaving their job in the next year—heck, it didn’t even crack the top three. For the first time since Adecco launched an annual report on the changing world of work, global workers spanning a wide range of industries are making clear that their time is more valuable.

A better work-life balance topped the reasons why workers might quit their current job, according to a survey of more than 40,000 workers in 36 countries conducted by Adecco, a Zurich-based recruitment and consulting agency. But the second and third reasons why workers might switch jobs—seeking out more flexibility and the opportunity to work remotely—also speak to how people are reevaluating how work fits into their lives, and not just the money it provides, according to the report.

At a time when workers are increasingly being asked to operate alongside AI agents or even robots, as the report found, they seemingly want to retain some of their humanity. And organizations that don’t want to see a mass exodus of humans should take note, according to Denis Machuel, CEO of Adecco.

“We cannot simply ask people to keep pace with technology,” Machuel said in a foreword to the report. “We need to build trust and confidence, to adapt, to give people the freedom to learn and experiment, and protect the flexibility and balance they value.”

The survey also highlighted that across seven key indicators, employees generally reported being somewhat less satisfied this year than last. But the biggest decreases were related to flexibility regarding both their working schedule and working location. 

That’s why the report’s findings also speak to the ongoing tension surrounding stricter return-to-office policies, as Machuel told The Guardian.

“Every company that wants to bring people back to the office struggles with actually implementing these measures to their full extent,” he told the outlet. As for companies with a strict policy of five days per week? “It doesn’t work,” he added.

WORKERS WANT AI

As artificial intelligence continues to reshape how people work, employers play a key role in how that plays out. The survey found that when AI tools are well integrated into everyday workflows, workers save time for higher-value work—and they feel less overwhelmed by the technology as a result.

Nearly three-quarters of workers surveyed use AI agents at work, while 14% work alongside robots, the survey found. These “hybrid workers,” as Adecco dubbed them, report feeling far and away more empowered to shape their work going forward. Compared with their non-hybrid counterparts, these hybrid workers were overwhelmingly more likely to say they’re able to perform at their highest level, that their sense of autonomy over their work has increased in the past year, and that they proactively experiment with different ways of using AI at work.

Once again, employers should take note, Machuel says.

“When workers help redesign how work gets done, and receive the training and clarity to adapt, AI creates more value, and change becomes easier to navigate,” he said in a statement. “AI delivers its greatest impact when it happens with people and not to people.”
