Alphabet’s Q3 earnings were interesting. Despite a 25% rise in revenue and a healthy net income of $26 billion, analysts were disappointed with the company’s results, sending the stock down as much as 11% in after-hours trading.

On the plus side, the company’s cloud business is booming, doubling year-over-year revenue to $30 billion as it attracts frontier labs to its data centers. But advertising revenue didn’t meet expectations, and investors appear to be worried that its search advertising business, one of the most efficient sources of recurring revenue in history, is not keeping up with changing user behavior.

As Anthropic gets ready to soar into its IPO later this year, mature businesses like Alphabet, Microsoft, and Amazon are racing to adapt to a market where demand for AI infrastructure is outpacing compute supply. But investors are looking at the capital expenditure tied up in future data center buildouts and getting nervous that, if the bubble bursts, even these well-capitalized companies will take massive hits.

The biggest question surrounding Google’s stock performance is whether it can sufficiently monetize AI-powered search before demand for its traditional search inventory falls off a cliff. As the company sends less and less traffic to the open web, it’s touting the fact that AI Mode and AI Overviews keep people on Google for three times as long as traditional search.

So it’s no surprise that it’s expanding ads in AI Mode to 10 new markets this week, with more to come by the end of the year. While the post was only a few paragraphs long, expanding its AI ad business may end up being one of the company’s most consequential announcements of 2026.

On the gadgety side, there’s another impending showdown brewing between XREAL’s Android XR-powered Aura glasses and Meta’s upcoming VR Glasses.

I liked Jasmine Uniza’s video below comparing the two because, until I watched it, I didn’t realize the extent of the diverging approaches to reality they’re each taking. The Aura glasses are more traditionally translucent and somewhat awkwardly project two OLED screens on top of the dimmed lenses, allowing the outside world to filter through. Meta’s VR Glasses eschew the traditional headset shape and weight of the Quest but block out far more of the outside world and use external cameras, like their predecessors, to pipe in mixed-reality footage.

When Apple announced the new M5 Max and M5 Ultra configurations of the Mac Studio in August, it said a configuration with 512GB of RAM would be available in October. Today, the company officially revealed the pricing of that option.
