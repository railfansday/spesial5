Mac Allister not so sad now after Liverpool comeback winner

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Do your talking on the pitch, as the old adage goes, and Alexis Mac Allister emphatically did just that as Liverpool came from behind to thrillingly win their first Champions League tie under new head coach Andoni Iraola in what was his debut at this level.

On the eve of this encounter, against a strong Atlético Madrid, one of the clubs who have recently tried to sign him, Mac Allister had spoken of his sadness. Sadness that, unlike fellow midfielders Dominik Szoboszlai – Liverpool’s other scorer here – and Ryan Gravenberch, he had not been offered and signed a new contract.

So, earn it. And many more goals – and overall performances like this – there is no doubt the Argentinian will have a deal placed in front of him. He scored with an arresting second-half shot, which proved to be the winner, and the smile across his face said it all. Not so sad now as he was later substituted to a standing ovation with Gravenberch taking his place.

The goal came early in the second half, with Iraola attributing that to adjusting his tactics after being flummoxed by Atlético’s back five and packed midfield in a first half when Liverpool were second best.

Mac Allister was heavily involved through the build-up, eventually picking out Rio Ngumoha on the left whose shot was blocked and pinballed around. It dropped to Mac Allister, 20 yards out, and he hit the kind of unerring drive that gives the goalkeeper no chance. And so it proved. Boom. And Anfield was bouncing.

There are certainly signs under Iraola, and following a difficult last campaign, that Mac Allister is beginning to rediscover the form he showed during his first two seasons at Liverpool. Time and again the 27-year-old made vital interventions, showed aggression and walked the tightrope of a harsh early yellow card. He dovetailed with Szoboszlai and that might be a concern for Gravenberch – watched by the Netherlands’ new head coach Xavi Hernández – if Iraola persists with this approach.

Although Mac Allister has two years left on his current deal, the last thing Liverpool need is yet another attention-sapping contract saga, even if he is not the same scale of player as Mohamed Salah or Trent Alexander-Arnold.

For Iraola, after three Premier League games, comprising two draws and a victory over newly-promoted Ipswich Town, this will hopefully be lift off. A big European night at Anfield and coming from behind to beat one of the continent’s heavyweights.

“I loved it,” Iraola said, before castigating himself for misreading the way Atlético would line up. And what of Mac Allister’s riposte? There was a smile from Iraola before the Spaniard’s usual diplomacy kicked in as he talked about a “lovely, lovely goal” and how good it was for his midfielders to contribute.

Liverpool had to fight for it and what will have satisfied Iraola is that the defence, which has come under such scrutiny, held out. Even so, there are still serious concerns over the suitability of Milos Kerkez at left-back – who was badly at fault for Atlético’s goal – although the robust Ronald Araújo showed he can be a strong and versatile option on the other flank. The Uruguayan, signed on loan from Barcelona, is such a presence in both penalty areas and has the capacity to gain cult status.

As does £123m signing from Paris St-Germain Bradley Barcola, who should have scored – once, certainly, possibly twice – and possesses astonishing pace that will undoubtedly lead to goals. There are also encouraging signs that his presence could enliven Florian Wirtz. Barcola eventually also went off, but thankfully it was only to cramp.

There was a collective intake of breath across the Kop when Marcos Llorente was played through and bore down on them to give Atlético the lead. There was a desperate hope that he had strayed offside. There was then a realisation that Kerkez had, however, played him on with an appalling lack of awareness. Llorente beat Alisson.

It owed much to a wonderful shimmy, deceiving Szoboszlai in centre field, by Julián Alvarez before he smartly threaded the ball through, and Mac Allister’s international team-mate almost doubled Atlético’s advantage soon after. Only to be denied by Alisson.

Indeed, he named his dog Anfield after his extra-time brace knocked Liverpool, then the holders, out in the last 16 in 2020. And Liverpool had made a dog’s dinner of defending this time.

This is a different Atlético. A far less defensive, far more expansive Atlético. In years gone by, once they were 1-0 it was game over under Diego Simeone. But these days they try and play more and that gives their opponents a chance. And so it proved.

Atlético goalkeeper Jan Oblak did well to deny Barcola, with his trailing foot, before Wirtz won the ball back and found Szoboszlai who eventually ended the movement. It owed much, however, to a clever backheel to pick out the midfielder who finished low into the corner. Who executed that backheel? That would be Araújo.

“I was enjoying it [the Anfield crowd]. They [Atletico Madrid] are a very good team. Technically, in the middle, lovely players. It is not easy to press those players as they have quality. They have been playing together forever. We find another comeback after Nottingham Forest, after Newcastle. We still have this thing to improve in the beginning of the games, but I am happy as we have shown some character. This is the Champions League, you do not have easy games.”

Liverpool begin their Champions League campaign at home as they host Atletico Madrid at Anfield. These sides met at Anfield on matchday one last season, with Liverpool winning a dramatic game 3-2. Liverpool led 2-0 inside the opening 10 minutes before Atletico came back to level the game at 2-2. Virgil van Dijk then scored in added time to give Liverpool all three points before Atletico boss Diego Simeone was sent off for clashing with the Anfield crowd, resulting in a one-match ban.

Andoni Iraola earned his first win as Liverpool boss on Friday night as they won 2-0 away at Ipswich thanks to a quickfire early double from Alexander Isak. This will be Iraola’s first Champions League match as a manager.

“I think obviously it is a special night for me,” Iraola said. “Everyone talks [about] the Champions League nights at Anfield and also the first game is against a strong opposition, it is Atletico Madrid. [Diego] Simeone is doing an incredible job as a manager. To be all the years that he has been achieving every season the goals of the club I think is remarkable and definitely it is a big challenge for us. I think I played against him as a player, he was still a player, and I played against his teams when he was a coach and I also coached against him. So, we had a lot of precedent before. Definitely he has much more experience than me in this kind of games but I hope tomorrow we can give him a good game.

“It is one of the dreams, probably, when you start. It was difficult for me when I started my coaching career seven, eight years ago, to position myself in the position that I am right now, and I hope I can enjoy it. It is a big responsibility, massive challenge, but I feel privileged to be there in that dugout tomorrow [tonight] and now my thinking is about putting the team together in a position where we can play a good game, win the game and start strong in this Champions League.”

Atletico Madrid currently sit sixth in La Liga and suffered their first defeat on Saturday as they lost 3-0 at Athletic Bilbao. They did reach the semi-finals of the Champions League last season, where they fell to Arsenal 2-1 on aggregate.

“We are coming into a marvellous stadium and there will be a great atmosphere,” Simeone said. “We will have to look good from the start of the game because it will be full on. He [Andoni Iraola] works very well with the team, focusing on the tactical awareness of each player. It takes time to find the right path, but he is growing and improving and he has some very good players.”
