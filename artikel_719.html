Netflix Renews Japanese Crime Hit ‘Tokyo Swindlers’ for Season 2

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The hit real estate crime series returns in 2027, reuniting stars Go Ayano and Etsushi Toyokawa with writer-director Hitoshi One.

Let the grand con begin… Netflix has ordered a second season of Tokyo Swindlers, the Japanese crime thriller that turned Tokyo’s high-stakes real estate market into one of the streamer’s biggest local hits of 2024.

Go Ayano and Etsushi Toyokawa will reprise their roles as Takumi Tsujimoto and Harrison Yamanaka, respectively, with Hitoshi One returning as writer and director. Kohei Ninomiya, who previously directed episodes of the Japanese newsroom drama Elpis, will also write and direct. The new season is set to debut globally in 2027. Adapted from a novel by Japanese author Ko Shinjo, the first season follows Takumi, a negotiator recruited into Harrison’s gang of real estate fraudsters. Their most ambitious scheme involves a property worth 10 billion yen (about $63 million), with the group maneuvering around developers and evading police as Takumi’s troubled past and Harrison’s ruthless methods gradually emerge.

The series topped Netflix’s weekly series chart in Japan for six consecutive weeks — a first since the rankings began — and remained in the country’s top 10 for 22 weeks overall, according to Netflix. It also appeared on the streamer’s global non-English-language series chart for five consecutive weeks and reached the top 10 in 15 countries.

The show’s success has also extended into Japanese popular culture, with dialogue and characters becoming memes on social media.

“Right after it started streaming, Netflix came to me and said, ‘Let’s do Season 2!’” One said in a statement. “I was terrified, thinking, ‘There’s no way we can top something that good…’ But as I worked on the scripts, I started to think, ‘Wait… isn’t this even better than Season 1?’ And that gradually turned into conviction.”

Shinjo hinted that the returning con artists would be pursuing another formidable target. “Human desire knows no limits,” the author said. “Searching for something beyond money alone, they will aim for an even bigger, even harder con.”

Toyokawa offered a humorously ominous assessment of his task of stepping back into Harrison’s shoes.

“I have been reunited with a terrifying man,” he said. “He destroys my conscience, my sense of social decency, my love for my family. He destroys my life, my very humanity. I pray filming wraps before he consumes me. Imagine it: Harrison Yamanaka is always there in the mirror.”
