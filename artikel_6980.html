Designers Say THIS Is the Secret to Making Framed Posters Look Like Expensive Art

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Your prized prints deserve better than a thumbtack.

Posters have come a long way since your dorm-room days. A vintage travel ad, a favorite movie poster, or a concert print you’ve kept for decades can bring as much character to a room as an original painting. The key to giving them a more polished look? A thoughtful frame, creative placement, and the confidence to display what you love.

"Never overthink putting posters up in your home, especially concert posters," says interior designer J.D. Bell. "Your home should reflect your personality, interests, and what you love."

Here, designers share their favorite ways to give posters the elevated treatment they deserve.

The quickest way to transform a poster into a statement piece is to ditch the inexpensive clip frame in favor of something more substantial. Consider rich wood, sleek metal, or even an ornate antique frame that contrasts with the poster's graphic quality.

"The key to making them feel elevated is to treat them with the same consideration you would a fine piece of art," says designer Hattie Sparks Collins. "A beautiful frame, thoughtful installation, and a little juxtaposition go a long way."

For particularly colorful or busy posters, a generous mat can provide visual breathing room, while a frame that picks up one of the artwork's less prominent colors creates a subtle, sophisticated connection.

Have a stack of concert posters gathering dust? Turn them into a gallery wall that tells your story.

Bell recently displayed a collection of vibrant Dead & Company concert posters against black-and-gray Zak + Fox patterned wallpaper in a client's home. Rather than using identical frames, he selected a mix of black styles to introduce texture while maintaining cohesion.

"The darker backdrop really made the colorful artwork pop," he explains. The collection was installed in a hallway leading to the client's office, a fitting tribute to his love of music.

The takeaway? Don't be afraid to experiment with patterned walls, unexpected locations, and subtly mismatched frames.

One of the most compelling ways to display posters is alongside furnishings that feel decidedly more traditional. Think a framed vintage movie poster above an antique chest, or a bold graphic print surrounded by rich textiles and classic upholstery.

"I really love pairing graphic, colorful posters with antiques, interesting textiles, and more traditional furnishings," Collins says. "That tension between high/low and old/new is what makes a room feel collected instead of decorated."

Ultimately, the most stylish poster is one that means something to you. Forget whether it's rare, valuable, or considered fine art. A beautifully framed memento from your favorite concert can become the most interesting piece in the room.

As Bell puts it, "What matters is making those pieces feel special and intentional. The right framing can do a lot for a poster and make it feel like a real piece of art."
