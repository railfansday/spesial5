Before a trip abroad, get a debit card that charges no foreign transaction or ATM fees. When you arrive, use it to take out local cash at a bank’s ATM, and pick the local currency over dollars when the screen asks. That withdrawal costs less than changing money at a counter or taking a credit card cash advance.

A card with no foreign transaction fee can pay for much of a trip abroad, but local transit, tips, and small purchases still call for cash in many countries. Where you get that cash decides what it costs you.

An exchange counter, whether at the airport, at your hotel, or at your own bank before you leave, sells currency for more than it pays to buy it back. Wells Fargo, for instance, says that gap includes a markup. The FDIC says paying by card is likely to get you a better exchange rate than converting cash. As for traveler’s checks, American Express no longer issues them.

So get cash with your debit card at a bank’s ATM, and check what your bank charges for that. Chase, for example, charges Total Checking customers $5 per withdrawal at another bank’s ATM outside the U.S., plus a 3% foreign transaction fee, so a withdrawal worth $500 costs $20 in fees. The machine’s owner can add a fee of its own. A debit card that charges neither fee saves that $20. Schwab Bank Investor Checking, for one, charges no foreign transaction fee and refunds ATM fees worldwide.
