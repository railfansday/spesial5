Real Estate Wholesaling Is a Sales Job, Not an Investment – Check Your State’s Rules

Real estate wholesaling pays you a fee for putting a home under contract below market and selling the contract to an investor, so it’s a sales job. Before you pay for a course, check whether your state requires a license or a disclosure to the seller, and add up what a few months of mailers would cost.

The pitch, often from someone selling a course, promises a way into real estate without money or credit. What the business takes is a marketing budget and a steady supply of owners who need to sell a rundown house fast.

You find them with mailers, lead lists, and calls. When one agrees, you sign a purchase contract you can sell to another buyer, put down a small good-faith deposit called earnest money, and add an inspection contingency that lets you back out. Then you sell the contract to an investor for an assignment fee, or buy the house and resell it right away in what’s called a double closing.

Either way, you earn a fee for arranging a sale, not a return on property you hold, and it’s the gap between the seller’s price and the investor’s. The Oklahoma Real Estate Commission warns that many sellers don’t realize they’ve agreed to a wholesale deal, or how much the wholesaler makes on it.

Illinois requires a broker’s license once you wholesale twice within any 12 months, and Pennsylvania requires a license for every residential wholesale deal and gives the seller up to 30 days to cancel. Ohio and Connecticut require you and the seller to sign a disclosure before signing the purchase contract, and Oklahoma gives the seller two business days to cancel.

Say a new wholesaler in Illinois spends $3,000 on mailers and lead lists over four months and closes one $8,000 assignment. She clears $5,000 before taxes, and she’d need a broker’s license to sell the second contract she’s working on.

Coaching can cost more than that profit. The Federal Trade Commission alleged that Zurixx used false promises of quick flipping and wholesaling profits to sell $1,997 seminars, then coaching that could run to tens of thousands of dollars. A 2022 settlement permanently banned the company and its owners from selling coaching.

Before your first mailer goes out, read your state real estate commission’s rules on wholesaling, and tell every seller in writing that you’re a wholesaler, not the final buyer.
