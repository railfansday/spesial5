Donating Toys With Kids – Let Them Choose What Goes and Who Gets It

Your kids will donate outgrown toys more willingly when they choose which ones go and who gets them. Sort the toys with them before birthdays and holidays bring new ones, and let them pick a recipient they can picture. Then check every toy for missing pieces and recalls before the drop-off.

Bagging up toys while the kids are at school is faster than sorting with them, right up until someone asks where the fire truck went.

Letting them choose also makes them more generous the next time. In a Cornell study, some 3- to 5-year-olds got to choose whether to give a sad puppet a prized sticker or keep it. Those who chose to give shared more stickers with a second puppet afterward than children who were told to hand theirs over. In a second experiment, children who chose to give away toy frogs shared more afterward than those who chose to give away scraps of paper.

So sort with your child, not for them, in the weeks before a birthday or the holidays. Set three rules first: one box per recipient, one old toy out for each new one in, and favorites off the table. Fill each box with toys they still like but can part with, not just the ones they’ve forgotten.

Then let your child pick the recipient from two or three they can picture, such as a family shelter’s playroom, their old preschool, or a younger cousin. Bring them along for the drop-off so they see who the toys are for.

A 7-year-old might pick a family shelter from three charities that take used toys, fill one box for it in early December, and keep the two stuffed animals she isn’t ready to give up. The box still goes, and every toy in it is one she chose.

Before a toy goes in the box, wipe it clean, test anything that runs on batteries, and gather every piece. Goodwill of Greater Milwaukee & Chicago, for one, wants toys clean, complete, and working, and tells donors to recycle or throw out a toy that’s broken or recalled. Look up each toy on the Consumer Product Safety Commission’s recall list before you seal the box.
