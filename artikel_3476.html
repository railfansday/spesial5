Star Wars: Everything Confirmed For Next Year's 50th Anniversary Celebrations, Ranked By Hype

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Next year is Star Wars' 50th anniversary, and it promises to be the biggest celebration in my lifetime. The Star Wars franchise has struggled a little over the last few years, with Disney+ shows releasing to diminishing returns and The Mandalorian & Grogu disappointing in the box office. But the Jedi always return, this time under new leadership: Lucas' protégé Dave Filoni and ILM stalwart Lynwen Brennan have stepped up as the new co-presidents. Next year's anniversary is a launchpad for the future, as well as a celebration of the past.

Lucasfilm has really only begun to announce everything that's coming in the 50th anniversary. Aston Martin is officially collaborating with Disney, while Star Wars In Concert: The Skywalker Saga will release at Hollywood Bowl in April. The Franklin Institute is launching Star Wars: The Experience on February 13, 2027. Billed as "the largest and most comprehensive behind-the-scenes traveling exhibition celebrating the Star Wars galaxy, and the only official exhibition during the landmark anniversary year," it promises to be unmissable—assuming you live near a showing.

But let's focus on things a little more widely accessible here. What can we expect from Star Wars in its anniversary year, and which new releases (or re-releases) are the most exciting?

On May 4, Lucasfilm will release Star Wars: The Hero Awakens. A new documentary by R.J. Cutler, it will explore the creation of the first Star Wars film with never-before-seen archive footage and audio.

Yes, there's an incredible amount of Star Wars merchandise on the way for the 50th. We've probably only learned the basics so far (not least because we don't know about Star Wars: Starfighter tie-ins yet). But LEGO's 50th anniversary celebrations include an up-scaled Luke Skywalker minifigure, a Rebel Y-Wing Starfighter, stunning Darth Vader wall-art, and so much more. Probably the most exciting is the Mustafar duel, an incredible diorama featuring Anakin and Obi-Wan (plus a Han Solo minifigure based on concept art).

Naturally, we can expect some absolutely amazing new releases from Hasbro as well. Hasbro has already announced a range of new figures in its Black Series, Vintage Collection, and Retro figure lines—all inspired by the first Star Wars film. Preorders are already open on Hasbro.com and at select retailers. And that's just the beginning, because we can expect more announcements over the course of the year.

Next year features a wealth of new Star Wars books, including some of the most exciting in years. There's a full publishing push accompanying the release of Star Wars: Starfighter, including an expanded novelization by Delilah S. Dawson that releases on June 22 (more on Starfighter later). But that's not all:

Directed by Dave Filoni, Ahsoka season 2 will continue the story that began in 2023. It features a thrilling cast: Rosario Dawson, Hayden Christensen, Natasha Liu Bordizzo, Rory McCann, Ivanna Sakhno, Mary Elizabeth Winstead, Eman Esfandi, and more. Hype has died down a little due to the gap between seasons, but we're sure to see a major marketing push. Screen Rant's Winter Preview included an exclusive image, helping to whet our appetites.

The first season of Ahsoka was bolder than anyone expected. It literally rewrote the history of the entire Star Wars galaxy, revealing an ancient dark side intergalactic empire tied to the Force cult known as the Nightsisters. When the story ended, Ahsoka Tano and Sabine Wren were stranded in another galaxy, on a planet mysteriously linked to the Mortis Gods—one of Lucas' strangest, most intriguing creations. Season 2 literally has the potential to redefine the Force.

Star Wars is returning to Los Angeles for Star Wars Celebration 2027, running from April 1-4. Lucasfilm is teasing "epic panels, hundreds of exhibitors, exclusive merchandise and legendary guests." But, in truth, this is exciting for a whole different reason: right now, we know absolutely nothing about the future of Star Wars after its anniversary year.

Star Wars Celebration is Filoni and Brennan's big chance to present their vision of what Star Wars is destined to become under their watch. As exciting as last year's Celebration in Japan may have been, it was disturbingly short on news, likely because of the leadership changes already in the works. Anticipation couldn't possibly be higher for Celebration 2027, and Lucasfilm need to deliver. It's time to discover the future of Star Wars.

George Lucas' Star Wars will return in 2027. For years, the fandom had dreamed of the day when the original Star Wars—the one-and-done movie even Lucas wasn't sure would be a hit, long before "episodes" and even the "A New Hope" subtitle—would return to theaters. I admit, I thought it would never happen. Lucas had gone to great lengths to replace it with the Special Edition after all. But last year, a copy was discovered at the British Film Institute archives.

Digitally remastered, and even adapted for IMAX, this original Star Wars is headed to theaters on February 19, 2027. It really is a historic moment for the franchise, one every fan will want to experience. Star Wars is back, 50 years later, and we couldn't be happier.

Directed by Shawn Levy and starring Ryan Gosling, Starfighter is essentially Star Wars' triumphant return to the big screen (sorry, Grogu). Precious little is known about the film, which will apparently see Gosling rescue Flynn Gray's young character from all-new villains played by Matt Smith and Mia Goth. It's surprising to see Star Wars bank so heavily on star power, but this cast will definitely make this an unforgettable adventure.

Starfighter is set five years after the sequel trilogy, which means the franchise is stepping beyond the (current) Skywalker saga. Delightfully, it seems Tom Cruise himself helped direct a lightsaber duel when he paid a visit to the set. We'll likely learn more when trailers drop in the near-future (there are unconfirmed rumors one will release alongside Avengers: Doomsday).
