Wife, Daughter Arrested 4 Years After Man's Severed Head Was Found: Sheriff

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

A California man’s wife and daughter have been arrested in his murder more than four years after the discovery of his severed head inside a trash bag, authorities said.

The breakthrough comes after cold case investigators eventually identified the male victim as Paul Wesley Fitzmaurice Jr., who lived in Citrus Heights with his wife and daughter, according to the Placer County Sheriff’s Office.

Authorities were initially unable to identify him due to decomposition after his severed head was found in the Todd Valley area of Foresthill on September 28, 2024, authorities said.

An investigation revealed Fitzmaurice’s daughter, Mary Fitzmaurice, allegedly killed him inside their home and then worked with her mother, Fitzmaurice’s wife Georgia “Gwenn” Fitzmaurice, to get rid of his body, the Placer County Sheriff’s Office said in a Wednesday, November 18, news release.

Mary, 45, was taken into custody on murder charges while Georgia, 75, was arrested on accessory charges, according to the agency.

Paul had never been reported missing, authorities said. Mary and Georgia are accused of maintaining to friends and family that he was alive while they allegedly collected his Social Security checks and pension, per The Sacramento Bee.

Placer County District Attorney Morgan Gire said at a Wednesday news conference that the office plans to file murder charges against Mary in connection with Paul’s death, according to the newspaper.

“And we will continue to evaluate the investigation to determine if additional charges related to the murder on behalf of Gwenn are appropriate, as well as additional charges related the collecting of those [Social Security and pension] benefits,” Gire said, referring to Georgia by her nickname.

It wasn’t immediately clear whether Mary and Georgia had retained legal counsel.

The rest of Paul’s remains haven’t been found to date, according to authorities.

Mary and Georgia were taken into custody on Tuesday, November 17, after detectives executed a search warrant at their Citrus Heights home, the district attorney’s office said.

A potential motive for Paul’s murder wasn’t specified by law enforcement.

“We are grateful to the FBI for its assistance throughout the investigation, including the work of its Evidence Response Team during the execution of a search warrant at the residence,” the sheriff’s office said in a statement. “We also appreciate the support of the Citrus Heights Police Department and the Contra Costa County Crime Lab, which provided advanced DNA testing of evidence.”
