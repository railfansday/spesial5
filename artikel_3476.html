Watching a movie on your iPhone is convenient, but craning your neck for a comfortable view is hardly cinematic. Having a premium AR display in sunglasses form, without packing a bulky mixed-reality headset or computing puck, opens up entirely new possibilities.

The RayNeo GT Max and RayNeo GT connect to your device over a single USB-C cable. Instead of clunky goggles, the GT Series puts a massive virtual display — 59 degrees of field of view on GT Max, to be exact — into a sleek, understated sunglasses design that blends into coffee shops and airport lounges at just 68 to 78 grams. That single USB-C connection carries uncompressed video and powers the glasses directly: no secondary battery packs to carry, no charging anxiety, and zero wireless lag.

One thing to check before you buy: the glasses need a device that outputs video over USB-C (DisplayPort Alt Mode). That covers the iPhone 15, 16 and 17 series, Macs from 2015 onward with USB-C, iPad from the 10th generation on, and USB-C handhelds like the Steam Deck. The iPhone 16e, 17e and iPhone Air have USB-C ports but don’t output video over them, so they aren’t supported. Consoles and older Lightning devices need RayNeo’s HDMI adapter.

Both GT Max and GT use dual 1080p Micro-OLED panels, delivering a Full HD image to each eye with ultra-smooth 120Hz refresh rates in 2D mode. GT Max adds 38 pixels per degree, 98% DCI-P3 cinematic color coverage, and TÜV Rheinland triple eye-comfort certification. The GT Series also functions as an instant, private external display for a USB-C Mac. That makes the glasses invaluable far beyond streaming media, especially when you need an expansive, shoulder-surfer-proof dual-monitor workspace on a flight, train, or co-working desk.

GT Max also comes in three sizes based on inter-pupillary distance (IPD) — small for under 64mm, medium for 64–67mm, large for over 67mm — ensuring a tailored, comfortable fit.

If an everyday display frame came out of Cupertino today, it would look like RayNeo iO. Rather than heavy visors, iO pairs with your iPhone through the RayNeo app to project glanceable, contextual intelligence into genuine everyday eyewear.

iO uses a high-efficiency binocular MicroLED optical engine to float high-contrast green text into your sightline, presenting a virtual image equivalent to a 33-inch screen viewed from two meters away. With peak eye-level brightness rated at 1,300 nits, reading notes and prompts stays crystal clear even under direct midday sun without occluding your real-world surroundings. The waveguide is 97% transparent and the optical engine is hidden inside the frame, so from the outside these read as ordinary glasses — most people never clock them as smart eyewear at all.

For presentations and pitches, the built-in teleprompter scrolls your notes in sync with your natural speaking cadence, so you keep your eyes locked on your audience instead of looking down at your phone. During face-to-face meetings or international travel, the glasses deliver live subtitles and two-way translation right on the lenses, with support for 55 languages and 109 accents at roughly one second of latency.

Beyond live translation and teleprompting, RayNeo iO acts as a constantly evolving heads-up companion. RayNeo pushes frequent over-the-air (OTA) software updates, continually expanding what the glasses can do throughout your day.

Just now coming to iO is heads-up turn-by-turn navigation, which projects glanceable directional arrows, street names, and real-time transit alerts directly into your line of sight. Whether you’re walking through a new city or cycling down busy streets, you can navigate without holding your iPhone in front of you or looking down at your wrist. Alongside navigation, regular software updates continue to introduce new lifestyle tools, customizable notification filters, and contextual dashboard widgets that keep the hardware feeling fresh.

A four-microphone array with a bone-conduction sensor captures clear audio for automated meeting minutes and AI summaries that extract key action items. On the temple, an intuitive Smart Crown lets you scroll notifications and highlight key moments with familiar, physical micro-precision.
