Cycling radars are a new technology that gets a lot of attention for keeping riders safe on the road. Mirrors have been around for generations and do a great job, never require charging, and don’t chirp endlessly in traffic. I added a Spurcycle mirror to my custom bike this summer. I love its simplicity and clean looks—it doesn’t look out of place, even on high-end race bikes.

The Cielo is a performance-oriented helmet at a great price. Giro’s Roc-Loc 5 system dials in fit, 22 Wind Tunnel vents keep your head cool on hot days, and a Mips liner helps prevent rotational force injuries in a crash. It’s a solid pick for any road or gravel rider.

Garmin’s Tacx Neo 2 sits at the top of the performance-trainer pyramid. However, most indoor cyclists don’t need its pricey features. The Tacx Flux 2 has a large 7.6 kg flywheel with up to 2,000 watts of resistance (more than enough for almost any rider). It also simulates gradients up to 16%, to replicate the toughest climbs in virtual cycling worlds such as Zwift and Rouvy.

Rapha makes excellent, high-performance cycling apparel. However, it tends to be too pricey for many riders. The Core line is Rapha’s base-level kit, but it has much of the fit and comfort of the brand’s premium clothing. These bibs use the same chamois found in the more expensive Classic bibs. With proper care and washing, the bibs will last you several seasons.

We love these shorts because they’re great for hot summer rides and sweaty indoor workouts in the dead of winter. Plus, they have thigh pockets to hold your cell phone or snacks while you ride.

If you log a lot of time on the trainer, it’s worth refreshing your shorts regularly; worn-out shorts are a recipe for discomfort. The extra sweat from indoor rides breaks down the chamois faster than outdoor miles and leads to saddle sores. At this price, you should grab a pair (or two!) before they sell out.

It’s important to have a warm, thermal jersey in your closet when the temperature drops. On its own or paired with a light vest, this fleece grid jersey breathes well and keeps you from becoming sweaty and cold. On colder days, wear the Attack Thermal under a windproof jacket to keep your core insulated and cozy.

A test editor favorite for years, Smartwool’s socks are light—but not too light—and have the right amount of compression to keep your feet snug without feeling squeezed. When I find socks I love, I buy a pair every time they go on sale— you can never have too many cycling socks!

For riders who only go full coverage when absolutely necessary, this waterproof cycling cap will be right in their wheelhouse. It offers extra protection from rain, sleet, and wind without added bulk. It’s thin and fits comfortably under a cycling helmet.

If you’re new to winter cycling, you need a thin hat like this one for cold days. It goes under your helmet while keeping your ears and forehead covered. And if you’ve been riding through winters for years, you probably need a new one anyway—that crusty old hat you bought a decade ago has definitely seen better days.

The CTY 1.1, by REI’s Co-op Cycles house brand, is perfect for commuting, getting around town or campus, or leisurely weekend rides. REI equips the CTY with 40mm-wide, flat-resistant tires, a dependable Shimano 24-speed drivetrain, and disc brakes. The bike is available in five sizes (to fit riders from 5-foot to 6-foot-3) and two colors.

The Topstone 3 is a perfect entry point into gravel riding. It’s also a great upgrade for riders who need a dependable all-arounder for fitness, commuting, or even bikepacking. The Topstone features a high-quality aluminum frame and a carbon fork, equipped with plenty of mounts for racks, fenders, and bags. For climbing hills, the bike has a Shimano Cues 2x10-speed wide-range drivetrain and easy-to-maintain cable disc brakes for descents. At under $1,400, it’s a great deal for anyone looking for a versatile gravel or all-road bike.

Scott’s Speedster Gravel 30 says exactly what it is upfront: a speedy gravel bike ideal for racing, group rides, or fast rides on unpaved roads. Sharing design elements with Scott’s more expensive gravel race bikes, the 30 features an aluminum frame, carbon fork, 45mm-wide tires, and a Shimano Cues 1x10 drivetrain with sure-stopping hydraulic disc brakes. The frame uses a UDH hanger, so it’s future-proof if you want to upgrade to a lighterweight groupset once you get addicted to the spirit of gravel.
