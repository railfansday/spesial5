The Triathlete editorial team has been around long enough that we’ve collectively written dozens of holiday gift guides — from stocking stuffers to price point round-ups to personality-based ideas, always solid ideas but usually pretty generic.

But this year, we wanted to get more personal and spread some holiday cheer along the way. So the editors made a list of some of our most consistent writers and hand-selected gifts to match each of them. We sent the gifts to their doors and hoped we’d hit the mark.

And we are happy to share our IRL gift ideas with you for your own gifting or wish list creation. Below, you’ll find out a little bit more about some of our favorite contributors, what we gifted them, and why we think they’ll enjoy it.

About the contributor: Sara is lucky enough to live in the triathlete’s dream training locale: Kailua-Kona, Hawaii, home of the Ironman World Championship. Though there’s lots to envy, I’m particularly green over her access to daily open-water swimming. But I also worry because swimming in open water comes with more risks than the typical pool.

What we gifted (and why): Sara’s frequent open-water swimming is why I’m gifting her this safety buoy from Tri-Fit. It’s bright, so she’s visible to other swimmers and from shore, and stable in the water if she needs to use it to adjust her goggles or shake off a calf cramp. But wait, there’s more! What I really love about this buoy is that it goes beyond your standard safety product – it’s an actual training workhorse, with a 1-liter hydration bladder and bite valve for days when Sara decides she wants to swim with the dolphins and sea turtles a little longer.

About the contributor: Sierra is a skilled writer who has lent her talents to highlight the need for more athlete-friendly pregnancy and postpartum deferral policies and to advocate for clearer policies around breastfeeding and pumping during races. She’s a mom to an active toddler.

What we gifted (and why): Life as a mom is busy and includes plenty of extras – like the armful of toys your child just has to bring with them to leave the house. As a fellow mom, I know what it feels like to not have enough arms to hold all the water bottles, snacks, and gear that comes with both triathlon and motherhood, so a bag with a pocket for everything can be a ready-made way of getting out of the house for training in an organized state, even when life as a mom feels far from organized.

About the contributor: Emma-Kate is a retired professional triathlete who’s still very much involved in the sport while continuing to multisport train and lift regularly. From her home in Boulder, Colorado, she interviews experts, talks to today’s top pro athletes, and writes to make changes and news of the sport understandable to all athletes.

What we gifted (and why): Winters in Colorado are long and cold, making training challenging. As a tri nerd, I think Emma-Kate will enjoy keeping up with her training indoors using the latest swim tech. While I don’t think ZWIM will keep her from the gym (I’m not sure anything could), I do think it will complement her training nicely to make the winter not feel so long.

About the contributor: Tim is one of my favorite storytellers in triathlon, and I feel lucky to not only get to edit his stories regularly, but to hear the stories behind the stories. I can’t repeat most of them in print, but trust me when I say Tim Heming has seen some wild stuff.

What we gifted (and why): Naturally, when I read Mike Reilly’s book, Finding Your Finish Line, I thought of Tim. Reilly, also known as the “Voice of Ironman,” announced over 350,000 athletes across the finish line during his career as a race announcer before retiring in 2022. He, too, has seen it all, and some of his best tales are in this book. If I could get Tim and Mike in the same room to swap stories, it would probably be the best day of my triathlon career.

Reilly’s book, co-written with Triathlete contributor Sarah Wassner Flynn, is a masterful piece of storytelling. It’s inspirational, aspirational, and will leave you feeling proud to be a triathlete.

About the contributor: Susan is a board-certified sports dietitian, accomplished Ironman athlete and Kona qualifier, and runs her own business helping endurance athletes dial in their health and performance goals through their nutrition. She is our go-to writer whenever we need a sports nutrition concept or topic broken down in a way that triathletes can apply to their own training and racing.

What we gifted (and why): I’ve been a long-time fan of Skratch Labs for its simple ingredients and clean flavors. When we saw Skratch now offers a make-it-yourself gel-making kit using its super high-carb sports drink mix, we thought the nutrition expert who literally wrote our guide on high-carb fueling might enjoy easily customizing her nutrition intake. More than the fueling customization, the kit also provides a less gooey gel texture and planet-friendly reusable soft flasks. I’m eager to hear how it works for her!

About the contributor: When college student Saqib came on board as an intern in our Endurance department, the first question I had for this gangly cross-country runner was, “Have you done a triathlon?” The answer was no, and I am making it my life’s mission to change that.

What we gifted (and why): Did Saqib ask for resources on triathlon? He did not. In fact, he thinks of himself as a land animal and has no interest in swimming, which is why I’m gifting him this book from Triathlete contributor Alison Freeman – just to plant the seed. Isn’t that what holiday gifts are for, anyway? Subtle hints wrapped in shiny paper? I think of my brother-in-law, who always gifts his wife exercise equipment even though she is a couch potato; or my mother, who has given me countless hairstyling tools to tame my wild mane into something she deems more respectable.

But this book will actually serve its purpose, because Freeman has a real talent for making a big, scary goal like a first triathlon approachable and achievable. Her book is a start-to-finish roadmap that eliminates guesswork and leaves the reader feeling like they can not only do a triathlon, but also have fun along the way.

About the contributor: Jon Dorn is a former editor, longtime triathlete, and lifetime gear geek. He’s qualified for six age-group world championships, and loves to bust his butt training for long-course tri. If I hear about a cutting-edge piece of gear, an unconventional training method, or something that’s just plain weird, I call Jon. That said, he’s not afraid to call a spade a spade and let me know if something is just pricey junk; he’s no fanboy.

What we gifted (and why): I had heard about red light therapy in the context of the general health/beauty space for a few years, but it wasn’t until I was wandering the expo in Kona at this year’s Ironman World Championship that I encountered a red light therapy device that not only targeted triathletes but is also portable. Sadly, the one I checked out on the Big Island was bonkers expensive and didn’t make sense for a gift, so I shopped around for something similar that wouldn’t break the bank. I landed on the reasonably priced Valo Spark that had decent reviews.

On one hand, I’m not 100% sold on the efficacy of red light therapy — and the science is still out on how it works with sports-related aches and pains — but I know Jon trains super hard (his next race is Boulder 70.3 in June), I know he has his share of aches and pains, I know he loves to travel, and I know he likes trying new things. Whether he loves it or uses it as a portable night light, I can’t wait to find out what he thinks!

About the contributor: Arguably one of the most accomplished triathletes in Triathlete’s contributor lineup, with hundreds of triathlons and well over a dozen Ironmans under her belt, Jordan knows the sport from every perspective. She’s also one of the industry’s best-known and loved figures, representing professional athletes and helping brands grow their businesses.

Few people know the triathlon world better than Jordan, past or present. (She’s our first pick for a triathlon trivia team.) With her finger on the pulse, she gives pros and age-groupers a voice and digs into the stories shaping the sport.

What we gifted (and why): Jordan might spend more time in the saddle than any of us editors, and she knows her gear as well as anyone, especially what the pros are presently using. While she’s currently on the road to recovery from a meniscus tear, we wanted to send her something custom and pro-level to test whether the 3D investment matches the marketing as she rebuilds her fitness on the bike.

But saddles are tricky to gift. Finding this fully adjustable, 3D-printed option gave us the versatility we were looking for: a fit she could fine-tune herself, without being limited to the stock setup straight out of the box.

About the contributor: Zach Nehr is another one of our key tech/gear editors, especially when it comes to indoor training. Blessed (or cursed) by living in the not-winter-compatible state of Wisconsin, Zach is a die-hard Zwifty and has used probably just about every indoor trainer or virtual training platform that exists (or has existed). As much as Zach rides inside, I trust that if he says something is great, it’s great; if it’s not, it’s probably in the dumpster. Because you don’t ride inside as much as he does with junk.

What we gifted (and why): The Profile Design Zwift Ride Supersonic Aerobar was a medium-quiet release during this year’s Hawaii Ironman World Championship week – mostly because you don’t think much about riding in the pain cave when you’re in a tropical paradise prepping for the biggest race of your season. As incongruous as the setting was for an indoor release, I was excited to see that Profile was taking its triathletes seriously by announcing the first-ever triathlon-specific Zwift-integrated aerobar for indoor training.

I wanted to gift this to Zach because if you dedicate enough time to training indoors, you should really dedicate your indoor training setup as well, not just make do. Being able to control your avatar from a dedicated set of Zwift Ride–compatible aerobars is the pinnacle of tri gear, as it completes the perfect pain cave: the kind that could prep you for next year’s Ironman World Championship, anyway.

About the contributor: When it comes to explaining sports psychology and neuroscience studies to triathletes, there’s no one better than Daya. As a neuroscientist, certified mental performance consultant, and yogi, Daya helps all types of athletes train their brains through her business, and she can make even the nerdiest psychology studies understandable and applicable for triathletes.

What we gifted (and why): When we heard about the Muse Athena S Headband, a “brain-sensing headband,” we thought of Daya. The headband claims it can do everything from tracking your brainwave activity for better focus to tracking your blood oxygenation in the prefrontal cortex to help you build mental endurance. If anyone can sort through the science behind the Muse, it’s Daya, so we’re excited to hear how effective it seems to be in her own athletic performance.
