Bitcoin's $2 Billion Liquidation Weekend Leaves Traders Watching $60,000

Bitcoin fell to around $62,100 on Sunday as roughly $2 billion in crypto derivatives positions were forcibly closed, mostly bets that prices would rise. The weekend slide sent the cryptocurrency to its lowest level since July 14, adding another ugly chapter to six consecutive weeks of losses.

Bitcoin had recovered to around $64,300 early Monday. But the options market is showing why traders aren't necessarily treating that bounce as an all-clear.

Liquidations happen when an exchange closes a trader's position because the money posted to support a borrowed bet is no longer enough to cover its losses. CoinGlass figures from Saturday showed $1.9 billion liquidated in one 24-hour period, about $1.8 billion of it bullish positions across cryptocurrencies, not just Bitcoin.

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs had already been losing investors before the weekend. Data from Decrypt’s ETF tracker show $185.4 million in net outflows on Friday, extending their losing streak to seven trading sessions and bringing withdrawals since November 5 to approximately $1.91 billion.

BlackRock's IBIT accounted for $143.5 million of Friday's withdrawals, while Fidelity's FBTC lost $41.9 million. The funds' earlier weakness had helped explain the broader exodus, although ETF flow totals measure fund creations and redemptions rather than every trade between investors.

Those numbers also come with a timing distinction: U.S. ETFs do not trade over the weekend, while Bitcoin trades around the clock. Friday's outflows show weakened demand before the crash, not that ETF shareholders were redeeming shares during Sunday's plunge.

Bitcoin's technical indicators suggest the selling may be overstretched, but that doesn't necessarily mean the worst is over.

The daily Relative Strength Index sat near 24 in oversold territory on the daily charts. Meanwhile, the Average Directional Index stood above 33, indicating a strong prevailing trend, with bearish directional pressure still dominating. The weekly RSI, near 38, pointed to a weak longer-term picture, although it had not reached oversold territory.

The cryptocurrency bounced after approaching support around $62,000. A recovery would face potential resistance near $66,500 to $67,200, followed by another obstacle around $69,300, based on the chart's Fibonacci levels that measure support and resistance marks.

A decisive break below $62,000, meanwhile, would put the psychologically important $60,000 level back in focus—precisely where options traders have accumulated significant downside exposure.

Bitcoin's next test will be whether fresh U.S. ETF flow data show more withdrawals for Monday and whether traders keep paying for downside protection. The heavily watched November 27 options expiration is still ahead, with $50,000 puts among the contracts drawing the most recent trading activity.
