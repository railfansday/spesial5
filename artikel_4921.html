What Are the Best Types of Desks for Small Spaces?

For most small spaces, the best options are wall-mounted desks and floating desks, which keep the floor clear and put vertical space to use. Folding desks are another great option since they’re easy to stow away when you’re done for the day. If you have an empty corner space, a corner desk or a small L-shaped desk can claim square footage that otherwise goes unused. A secretary desk, which closes over your work, makes sense if your home office is also your living room.

How Do I Choose the Right Small Computer Desk for My Needs?

The right computer desk for you will depend on your priorities. If you have multiple monitors, choose a desk with a longer shape and a cord organizer to ensure you have enough surface area. If you work on just a laptop, consider a portable C-shaped desk that can double as a side table when not in use. If your desk is too shallow for a keyboard and a screen, a keyboard tray mounted underneath buys you room without a bigger footprint.

While most sitting desks are between 27 and 30 inches tall, your height will play a factor in ergonomics and how comfortable you’ll feel sitting for long periods of time. For example, if you’re taller, you may find yourself slouching to type. For those who are shorter, your neck could be placed at an odd angle for viewing your laptop or monitor screen. What Are the Most Space-Efficient Desks for Small Apartments?

Vertical wall-mounted desks, foldable desks (basically the Murphy bed of workspaces), and C-shaped portable desks are going to be the most space-efficient options for small apartments. Foldable desks can easily be tucked away when not in use, while C-shaped tables can also function as a drinks table after working hours. If you need a space-saving desk with storage, look for vertical storage above the work surface rather than drawers below it, which keeps the desk’s footprint small.

Where Should I Put a Desk in a Small Apartment?

Start with the power outlets. A desk within reach of a wall plug saves you from running extension cords across the room, and it gives you an obvious place for a phone and laptop charging station. After that, look for natural light and a wall or corner that isn’t already doing another job.
