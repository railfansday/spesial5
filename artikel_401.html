Klopp wants Infantino out as FIFA candidate deadline looms

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Germany coach Jürgen Klopp has joined the chorus of football figures critical of FIFA President Gianni Infantino. But with time running out, there are few who seem willing to run against him.

Germany coach Jürgen Klopp has become the latest prominent football figure to call for change at the top of FIFA.

"Regarding Gianni Infantino, surely the next person to come along can do a better job," he said on Sunday.

But with time running out before the November 18 deadline for new candidates for the presidency of world football's governing body, Infantino is looking overwhelmingly likely to win another term.

"There are people who truly care about the future of football, people who aren't driven by financial interests," Klopp said ahead of Germany's Nations League match against the Netherlands on Monday.

"If someone like that were to step up — if someone like that were to become president — I think that would be a significant improvement."

If that person is waiting in the wings, they're leaving it late to step on the stage. Despite a raft of federations and continental confederations, including Germany, UEFA (Europe), CONCACAF (North and Central America and the Caribbean) and AFC (Asia), calling for Infantino's head in the wake of his plan to sell off part of the World Cup earlier this year, the incumbent appears to have stopped the bleeding.

That plan, which involved forming a subsidiary arm of FIFA called FIFA Forward Enterprise (FFE) in order to sell off part of its commercial operations to investors including Joshua Kushner — brother of US President Donald Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner — was widely panned before being abandoned.

It followed on from widespread criticism of Infantino's awarding of a new FIFA Peace Prize to Trump, the overturning of a red card for US national team player Folarin Balogun, and with Trump's involvement, at the World Cup and a host of other controversies.

Since then, though, many public critics have backtracked amid FIFA promising to release an extra $2.7bn (€2.45bn) from its reserves over the next four years to the federations, increasing their total investment to $6.35bn.

Perhaps the most significant of these u-turns came from the AFC, who went from saying Infantino had committed a "fundamental breach of trust" in August, to offering the Swiss its "full commitment to supporting you and your leadership as you seek a new mandate as the FIFA president" in October.

Those words were spoken by AFC President, Sheikh Salman bin Ibrahim Al Khalifa, who ran against Infantino when he first won the top job at FIFA in 2016. He will not run again and said that "my feeling is, I'm sure, the feeling of most of my members," in regard to Infantino.

Proof that the sentiment is not universal comes in the shape of Kozo Tashima, the honorary president of the Japan Football Association (JFA). Tashima is one of only two people to confirm they will oppose Infantino in the March 2027 presidential elections.

The 68-year-old is a well respected figure in Asian football politics and will have no problem meeting the threshold of support from five of FIFA's 211 federations. This is not like the other candidate, former Swiss international player Ramon Vega, who is an enormous outsider. Tashima appears to be running on the basis that he will be able to chart a calmer path at FIFA.

"I am grateful that I can make this announcement with the full support of the Japan Football Association," Tashima told the Japanese newspaper Sports Nippon. "It's not about winning or losing but about conveying to the world the honest, sound and upright attitude that Japanese football has built. What FIFA needs now are these values that we have protected. I have decided to run in order to make this appeal to the world."

But the numbers are against him. Though support from within the confederations may not be total, the backing of AFC and the African confederation (CAF) is a huge boost for Infantino, with those two alone making up 101 votes, almost enough for a majority.

In fact, the only real concern remaining for Infantino remains UEFA. The European federation, headed by Aleksander Ceferin, is a major revenue source for FIFA but the two organizations are at loggerheads. As well as a war of words, UEFA are seeking to obtain evidence for a potential Swiss criminal case against Infantino in regards to FFE. FIFA have called this a "smear campaign" and a "fishing expedition".

But, so far, UEFA has not put up a candidate. Former Arsenal coach turned FIFA Chief of Global Football Development, Arsene Wenger was thought to be a contender but has now ruled himself out. Wenger's compatriot, Philippe Diallo, has since emerged as another option, with The Guardian reporting on Monday that the French Football Federation president could be announced on Tuesday.

While Europe is football's powerbase, particularly from a club perspective, UEFA will need Diallo, or any candidate they can convince to run, to win votes from outside its 55 nations to have any chance of success, particularly with Tashima likely to split any challenge. Even if a candidate appears before the November 18 deadline, Klopp's hope for change appears to be a long shot.
