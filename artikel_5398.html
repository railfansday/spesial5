Jensen Huang and Howard Lutnick agreed to testify before Senate Banking Committee

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Senate Banking Chair Tim Scott hopes to finish before Christmas; Huang also reached an agreement for a Dec. 1 House interview

Former Nvidia $NVDA CEO Jensen Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick have both consented to testify before the Senate Banking Committee, Chair Tim Scott, a South Carolina Republican, told CNBC on Wednesday. No hearing date has been announced.

"They both have agreed," Scott said on "Squawk Box." "We will hopefully have this done before Christmas." He said the appearances will give the committee "more information about what they knew, when they knew it and why they didn't do something about it."

Commerce Department spokesperson Kristen Eichamer said Lutnick agreed to a voluntary appearance to address the committee's questions, according to ABC News. ABC News said it is not clear when Lutnick will appear.

Scott had sent letters to both men asking them to testify in public, according to ABC News. The committee asked Jensen Huang to appear on Dec. 3 and Scott said he would subpoena him if he declined.

Jensen Huang struck a deal on Tuesday with the leaders of three House committees to sit for a closed-door, transcribed interview with all three panels on Dec. 1, according to CNBC. The pressure on Capitol Hill stems from his affair with someone suspected of spying for China.

Lutnick faces accusations that he held off on adding Megaspeed, an Nvidia customer, to a government list of firms seen as national security risks, according to ABC News. ABC News said that came despite U.S. intelligence suggesting Megaspeed may have been funneling Nvidia chips to China. ABC News also said lawmakers want to know if the Commerce Department violated its own procedures while supervising shipments of Nvidia's chips to China.

Sen. John Curtis, a Utah Republican, said in a social media post that Jensen Huang’s “gross negligence exposed cracks in our national security,” according to ABC News. Curtis added that Lutnick's reported delays on export controls cast doubt on the Commerce Department's commitment to serving Americans' best interests.

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO on Nov. 12 after reports of affairs with at least two women. U.S. officials suspect one of them, Alice Huang, who is not related to him, of relaying information to China's intelligence services. Jensen Huang said on Tuesday he would be honored to return as CEO if Nvidia's board decides it serves shareholders, employees, the company and America.
