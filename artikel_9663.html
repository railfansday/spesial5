Words have a way of changing meaning, especially in tech.

A bug used to be an insect. A mouse, an animal. A token, a coin. But as technology evolves, familiar words get borrowed, repurposed, and sometimes completely redefined.

Introducing Deduplication.tech, a new project by HackerNoon exploring the battle between original words and their tech-world duplicates.

You can explore the interactive word cloud, browse leaderboards, and dive into individual term pages to see how language is evolving across the tech landscape.

For example, take Surface: the original geometric concept versus Microsoft's hardware family. The page lets you see how the two meanings compete for attention and whether the tech duplicate has taken over.

It's part language experiment, part data exploration, and entirely free and open.

We're bringing Deduplication.tech to Product Hunt, and we'd love your support. Check out the launch, leave your thoughts, and give us an upvote to help more people discover the project.

After all, words belong to whoever uses them. Or at least, that's what the pageviews will tell us.
