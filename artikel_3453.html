Netflix’s ‘Detective Hole’ Officially Returns This Week

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Book adaptations are all the rage nowadays, with streaming services like Apple TV and Prime Video cornering different genres. The former has excelled in crime thrillers, while the latter has the romance genre figured out. Adapting books carries a certain risk because it could clash with the image viewers have of the character, but when it works, it is great. Netflix hasn't been left behind and occasionally debuts a book adaptation, although the streamer doesn't have a genre lane.

Earlier this year, it debuted the TV adaptation of Jo Nesbø's famed detective Harry Hole, and the risk paid off. The series was a hit with critics and fans, scoring a 91% on Rotten Tomatoes from professional reviewers, and Detective Hole spent four weeks on Netflix's top ten non-English shows. The first season was based on Nesbø's fifth book, The Devil's Star. In it, Harry (Tobias Santelmann) investigates crimes while dealing with a colleague he deeply distrusts. Detective Tom Waaler (Joel Kinnaman) has started breaking the law instead of upholding it, and Harry wants to bring him to justice.

Even though there are more books in the Harry Hole series, Netflix has not yet expressed interest in more seasons. But for fans who still crave more, a new Detective Hole release is officially available today, November 5. Vintage Publishing has released the fourteenth book in the Nesbo series, titled Kill Shot. The 544-page thriller is available to buy online for about $13.50. Seán Kinsella translated the English copy. Kill Shot is also available as an audiobook, published by Random House and narrated by Sean Barrett. The hardcopy version is expected to be released next year on February 23 and costs $35.

A great detective is really just a personality with a method attached, which is why we keep remaking the same four of them. Here is yours, what it says about you, and the case for them being the best in the business.

You let people think you are not a threat and then you simply never go away. Columbo is the only major detective show that tells you who did it in the first ten minutes, because the pleasure was never the puzzle, it was watching a rich man slowly realise he is in trouble.

You are the most interesting person in the room and you know exactly how to use it. Rian Johnson built Blanc as a gentleman detective who is also an outsider everywhere he goes, which lets him be charming at people who would never otherwise talk to him.

Everybody tells you everything, and you have never once asked them to. Jessica Fletcher is a crime novelist from a small town in Maine who is so warm and so obviously trustworthy that suspects confess to her essentially as a social courtesy.

You expect to be lied to and you are almost never disappointed. Chandler wrote Marlowe as a man with a rigid private code in a city that has none, which is why he takes the case, solves it, gets nothing out of it and takes the next one.

The jump from the events in the fifth book to the fourteenth finds Harry at a different stage in life, no longer a detective. There is a new killer in Oslo, and they are staging murders inspired by iconic movie scenes with escalating shock. Evidence points towards the city's biggest film set, and Oleg, Harry's stepson, is investigating. Harry knows he should keep his distance, but instincts he's sharpened throughout the years keep pulling him in, so he finds himself working with his team again. It is a race against time to stop the killer before he racks up more bodies, but Harry is struggling with separating the professional from the personal. Meanwhile, the killer is saving their best scene for last. The book features a final twist that will leave readers gobsmacked.
