New Option Approved for Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumors

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

— Adding bezuclastinib to sunitinib improved median PFS by 7 months

The FDA approved the selective KIT inhibitor bezuclastinib (Ekitna), in combination with sunitinib (Sutent), for previously treated gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST).

The approval allows use of the combination in adults with advanced GIST previously treated with imatinib.

"With today's FDA approval of bezuclastinib in combination with sunitinib, we have a new standard-of-care treatment following imatinib for patients with GIST," said Andrew Wagner, MD, PhD, of Dana-Farber Cancer Institute in Boston, in an announcement from bezuclastinib-developer Cogent Biosciences. "This approval represents a major step forward for the GIST community and reflects the tireless progress made in improving the lives of people living with this disease."

Sara Rothschild, executive director of the GIST advocacy organization The Life Raft Group, called the approval "an incredibly meaningful milestone for the GIST community that has waited many years hoping, advocating, and helping advance the research for a new treatment option."

Principal support for the approval came from the randomized, international PEAK trial involving 413 patients with locally advanced, unresectable, or metastatic GIST and disease progression after or intolerance to imatinib. Patients were randomized to sunitinib alone or in combination with bezuclastinib, and the primary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS) by independent review. Crossover to the combination was allowed after confirmed progression with single-agent sunitinib.

The primary analysis showed a median PFS of 16.5 months with the combination versus 9.2 months with sunitinib monotherapy. The difference translated into a 50% reduction in the PFS hazard (HR 0.50, 95% CI 0.39-0.65, P<0.0001). Significantly more patients randomized to the combination achieved objective responses (46% vs 26%, P<0.0001). Overall survival data remained immature.

Adverse reactions to the combination occurring in ≥20% of patients consisted of diarrhea, increased liver enzymes, hypertension, dysgeusia, hair color changes, fatigue, abdominal pain, nausea and vomiting, decreased appetite, stomatitis, gastroesophageal reflux disease, rash, dyspepsia, and pyrexia. The most common (≥5%) grade 3/4 laboratory abnormalities were increased alanine aminotransferase and decreased leukocytes, neutrophils, lymphocytes, and hemoglobin.

The approval brings to six the number of tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKI) for GIST. Imatinib remains the only TKI approved for first-line treatment. Sunitinib alone and the bezuclastinib-sunitinib combination are the only approved second-line therapies. Regorafenib (Stivarga) is approved for use in third line and ripretinib (Qinlock) for fourth line and beyond. Additionally, avapritinib (Ayvakit) has approval for unresectable/metastatic GIST harboring a PDGFRA exon 18 mutation.

Bezuclastinib also is being investigated as a potential treatment for systemic mastocytosis.
