A fibre optic cable is a cable type. It does not define what a cable looks like in an way. It may be a single strand of glass. It may be multiple strands. They may be bundled and individually sheathed in PVC. They may even be bulky steel-wire armoured cables.

Fibre optics are run across power lines are typically a design known as ADSS - All Dielectric Self Supported. They run multiple fibres in multiple cables held together by a Kevlar mesh and then UV stabilised PVC as a covering.

Look at Aerial Cables in the following link https://www.thefoa.org/tech/re... [thefoa.org]

They are big(6-10mm) stiff black armored cables that contain a metal wire cable for strength and toning. They are typically suspended on the lower level of electrical poles, like telephone copper, or cable TV wires.

My entire neighborhood and town has fiber cables stung on poles. The fiber cable is actually much thicker than the existing CATV wire. Also, the fiber cable uses the existing poles that carry POTS, AC, and CATV. The fiber cable does not get incredibly thin until after the junction box attached to the side of my house. Most of the fiber cabling is run underground to main neighborhood junction boxes where it is then run above ground on the poles.
