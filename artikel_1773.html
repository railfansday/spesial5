Everyone loves a good comedy

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The Premier League continues to amuse this season, with Man Utd’s troubles continuing after a 3-0 defeat to Bournemouth at Old Trafford yesterday. It probably spells the end of Michael Carrick as manager, which is a bit of a shame. I loved his work. Up there with Ruben Amorim and Ruud van Nistelrooy for me. Legend, in my opinion.

Obviously though, United’s problems run deeper than the man on the touchline. The dressing room is a toxic mess, which is quite funny because that’s the kind of thing Sir Jimmy Ratcliffe is an expert in given his position at INEOS – one of the UK’s great polluters. Still, whatever happens at Man Utd, however he continues to ruin internal morale by installing soup kitchens and firing staff like they’re not people worth a moment’s consideration, I guess he can just enjoy life as bigoted old tax-exile in Monaco, wondering why it is that he feels ‘his’ country isn’t what it used to be as he avoids contributing to society by paying his fair share.

I saw a story the other the day in the Athletic about how 72 year old Ratcliffe once went to the physio room to offer players advice on stretching because he’d run the London Marathon. If that doesn’t tell you exactly how dysfunctional that club is, nothing will. Except, of course, for the results on the pitch, the way they play, their position in the table, and the fact that senior players are basically saying ‘F*ck this! I have to go public’ in post-match interviews. But honestly, imagine Stan Kroenke at London Colney giving Viktor Gyokeres advice on how to hold it up, because he’d spent years holding up objections to one of his massively obscene land deals.

I can’t say I have a lot of time for either Lisandro Martinez or Bruno Fernandes, and they will probably become targets for being outspoken. Maybe they were wrong to do it, maybe as captain Fernandes is entirely the wrong kind of character when your club is in this kind of a situation, but it still speaks to the mess that exists on and off the pitch that they’ve said what they’ve said. Yes, it’s very, very entertaining for all of us, but in a purely objective sense, it says a lot.

I remember when Ralf Rangnick was boss there for a while, and he was roundly pilloried because results weren’t good, but he was spot on four or five years ago when he spoke about how the place needed an entire rebuild. I think he said something like it required open heart surgery, and he was absolutely right. All this time later, that operation is still required, but they have a xenophobic egomaniac in charge who would happily deport most of his playing staff if they weren’t talented footballers, and who continues to oversee bad decision after bad decision. Most of the club is still owned by the Glazers who never gave a shit anyway, and as Arsenal fans we know this kind of ownership structure is no good. Now they’ll probably just repeat the cycle where some ex-player will come in on an interim basis, get a few half decent results, and that wobbly-mouthed halfwit Rio Ferdinand will do a giddy dance on live TV about giving him a six year contract because he ‘knows the club’. Which is probably what they’ll do.

Welcome to the hot-seat Darren Fletcher/John O’Shea/Phil Jones/Henning Berg/David May/Alan Smith (the evil blue-lipped one)/Nani. Oh, please, give it to Phil Neville. Listening to Gary tie himself in knots every week on Sky would be amazing. Surely a reward for all us having to listen to him for all these years. Come on universe, you owe us this at least.

I saw a clip of a renowned Man Utd streamer going mad during their humiliating defeat at home yesterday, scornfully saying ‘But it’s Bournemouth! It’s Bournemouth!’, which tells you plenty. Anyone who has watched Bournemouth over the last few seasons would understand that even with a new manager in charge, they are a really good side with good players who can hurt you. The arrogance, the hubris, to think that just because their ground only holds a fraction of the capacity of Ancient Trafford, you are entitled to win the game.

I’m not saying he is part of the problem, per se, but if that level of delusion exists within their fanbase despite all the evidence they’ve had presented to them – right in front of their very eyes! – then it probably tells you something about the job that club needs to do. Thankfully for all of us, they appear to have a collection of people in charge thoroughly incapable of doing that. Sir Jimmy might brilliant at pumping petrochemical run-off into the atmosphere and poisoning wildlife, but he seems to be something of a footballing idiot. And quite hilariously, their current chief executive is a man called Omar Berrada, who used to work at Man City where two of his job titles between 2011 and 2015 were ‘Head of International Business Development’ and ‘Director of Partnership Sales’. What an interesting timeline that is, eh?!

I know we all have our issues with Arsenal, and we’ve been through some stuff over the years, but on this Monday morning as we begin a new week, let’s take a moment to be thankful for where we are right now. One decision can absolutely change the trajectory of your football club, and for us that was the appointment of Mikel Arteta. Reading lately that other options were considered ahead of him gave me a shudder, because we absolutely would not be in the same rude health if we’d gone down those particularly avenues.

Which is to say, the very best of luck to the Steve Bruce – Gary Pallister dream team.
