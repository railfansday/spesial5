Bitcoin Investors Are Capitulating—That’s Historically Been a Bottom Signal

Bitcoin’s latest crash has pushed investors into the kind of loss-taking that has historically shown up near market bottoms.

Realized losses, which investors lock in when they sell coins for less than they paid, have climbed relative to the profits taken over the past year.

That measure now sits at levels seen only near the lows of 2018, 2020, 2022 and early 2023, according to a report published Tuesday by onchain analytics firm Glassnode. It’s the first such reading since then.

Most of the selling came from recent buyers. Holders of coins less than six months old took losses into early 2026, cashed in gains during the rally into September and October, and are now selling at a loss again, Glassnode said. Leverage has been flushed too, with Nov. 13 and 14 bringing some of the largest long liquidations relative to open interest since 2023.

Bitcoin fell to about $62,000 on Nov. 15 before bouncing, and it traded near $64,970 on Tuesday. More than $2 billion in liquidations over the weekend left traders watching $60,000 as a potential next stop, Decrypt reported. The slide deepened last week as fears of further Federal Reserve rate hikes erased Bitcoin’s summer rally. Rising interest rates generally do not bode well for risk assets like Bitcoin, since investors typically rotate to safer bets like U.S. Treasuries when rates elevate.

But there are early signs of support, according to Glassnode. Buyers absorbed about 66,000 BTC in the $62,000 to $66,000 range during the two days of heaviest selling, adding to a zone where recent buyers accumulated around the August low.

Resting bids on Binance and Coinbase within 5% of the price now outweigh asks by about 2.3 to 1. The Coinbase Premium, which compares Bitcoin’s price on Coinbase with global markets, briefly turned positive at the low as U.S. buyers stepped in.

Still, Glassnode stopped short of calling a bottom. Broad demand remains thin: stablecoin supply has shrunk 4.2% over 90 days, and the Coinbase Premium has slipped back to a small discount.

Meanwhile, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs extended their outflow streak to eight sessions on Monday and have bled about $2.2 billion so far in November, according to Decrypt’s ETF tracker.

That makes the $62,000 to $66,000 range the line to watch. Short-term holders bought very few coins below $62,000, and a large cluster of leveraged long positions sits just beneath it, between about $59,500 and $62,200.

A daily close below $62,000 could trigger a fast drop toward the June low of $58,500 and Bitcoin’s realized price of $53,700, the average price at which every coin last moved, Glassnode said.

On the upside, a move back above $74,100, the average cost basis of short-term holders, would confirm that a bottom is forming, according to the firm. Options traders on Deribit, meanwhile, price roughly a one-in-three chance that Bitcoin settles below $53,700 at the September 2027 expiry.

The Fed’s next rate decision is Dec. 9, a day before November inflation data is released.
