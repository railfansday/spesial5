Workday shares fall as soft subscription forecast overshadows third-quarter beat

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Shares of human resources and finance software company Workday Inc. fell more than 7% in late trading today after a softer-than-expected subscription revenue forecast for the current quarter overshadowed fiscal third-quarter earnings and revenue that came in ahead of Wall Street’s targets.

For the quarter that ended on Oct. 31, Workday reported adjusted earnings per share of $2.82, up 22% from $2.32 in the same quarter of the previous year, on revenue of $2.705 billion, up 11.2% year-over-year. Analysts were expecting earnings of $2.75 per share on revenue of $2.69 billion.

Subscription revenue rose 12.8% from a year earlier, to $2.532 billion. Net income on an unadjusted basis came to $237 million, down from $252 million in the same quarter a year ago. Included in the quarter were $51 million in restructuring expenses, which followed the company’s disclosure in late September that it would cut about 2.5% of its workforce, mostly in its product and technology team.

Total subscription revenue backlog grew 6.1%, to $27.548 billion, with the 12-month figure up 12.3%, to $9.216 billion. Free cash flow came to $691 million, up from $550 million. During the quarter, Workday spent $750 million buying back about 3.8 million shares.

The quarter also saw Workday hold its annual Rising customer conference in Las Vegas in October, when the company made Sana the new home experience for its platform. Built on technology from Sana Labs AB, which Workday acquired for about $1.1 billion last year, Sana is an AI interface that brings tasks, decisions, meetings and agent activity together in one place. Autopilot Agents, also launched at the event, are designed to handle routine HR, finance and IT processes in the background.

On the opening day of Rising, Workday announced that it had agreed to acquire agentic identity platform ConductorOne Inc., better known as C1.ai. That deal closed on Monday. A partnership with Anthropic PBC, also unveiled at Rising, is set to bring Claude together with Sana AI agents and Workday’s HR and finance data.

“The past few quarters have been our strongest on the innovation and invention front, and in Q3, our AI products drove nearly $150 million in new ACV, more than three times what we delivered a year ago,” said co-founder and Chief Executive Aneel Bhusri, who returned to the top job in February after Carl Eschenbach stepped down. The priority now, Bhusri added, is to get more customers running agents in production.

According to Workday, the number of core customers that have bought at least one of its AI products rose nearly 25% from the previous quarter, to more than 2,200.

Pointing to what he called “ongoing adoption across our AI portfolio,” Chief Financial Officer Zane Rowe said the company is raising its full-year targets.

For its fiscal fourth quarter, Workday expects subscription revenue of $2.6 billion, up 10%, and an adjusted operating margin of 31.7%. Full-year subscription revenue is now forecast at $9.958 billion, up 13%, compared with the $9.94 billion to $9.95 billion guided in August. The full-year adjusted operating margin forecast was also raised, to 31.5%, ahead of the 31.1% expected by analysts.

Though the full-year numbers moved higher, the fourth-quarter subscription forecast came in narrowly below the $2.61 billion analysts had been expecting. Growth of 10% in the period would also be a step down from the 12.8% Workday posted in the third quarter.

Workday Chief Technology Officer Gabe Monroy made the case for taking agents to where employees already work during an interview in August. Speaking with theCUBE, SiliconANGLE Media’s livestreaming studio, for the Google Cloud: AI Agents in Action series, Monroy said enterprise agents need the deterministic rules of a system like Workday to go with the probabilistic reasoning of AI. “We don’t want to ask customers to have to switch their front doors or log into Workday when they’re happy to use Gemini systems,” he said.
