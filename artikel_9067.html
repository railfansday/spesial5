With a New Miami Partner, Queer Art Prize Returns Following Four-Year Hiatus After Loss of Corporate Support

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Queer Art Prize, the largest cash award in the US for LGBTQ+ artists, is coming back to life after a four-year absence following the loss of corporate support in 2023 that coincided with a wave of anti-trans backlash.

The prize has a new partner in Miami-based gallery Visu Contemporary, which will support the prize’s resurrection. The organization will dole out two $20,000 prizes, one for sustained achievement and one for recent work, double the previous amount. Runners-up for the recent work will each receive $5,000. In addition to announcing the prize’s return, the nonprofit behind it, Queer | Art, also named the 2026 winner of the Sustained Achievement Award: filmmaker Cheryl Dunye, in recognition of her contributions to Black queer cinema. In her work, Dunye is known to push at the boundaries of filmmaking, with characters addressing the camera and the filmmaker appearing as herself, a genre she has called “Dunyementary.”

The prize comes on the 30th anniversary of her feature film The Watermelon Woman (1996), which won the Teddy Award at the Berlin International Film Festival. She is credited as the first out Black lesbian to ever direct a feature. The Watermelon Woman is widely praised, with Backstage calling it “a landmark indie film.” Her directorial credits also include episodes for television series such as Dear White People, Bridgerton, and Hunting Wives.

With the Sustained Achievement Award, “we want to give people their flowers while they’re still living,” Queer | Art executive director Rio Sofia said in a phone interview. “There are so many moments where we as a community haven’t been able to do that. We lose people too young, so it’s so important to be able to celebrate their hard work and their impact on the culture and the community.”

The winner for the New Work Award will be announced at the organization’s annual party, taking place at Performance Space New York on November 21. The nominees are Jay Carlon (for WAKE, a ritual performance and site-specific dance work), Raquel Gutiérrez (for their debut poetry collection, Southwest Reconstruction), Ashley Teamer (for Portal Work, an immersive installation), and Dorian Wood (for Canto de Todes, a musical composition). The emcee for the evening will be writer, comedian, and performance artist Erin Markey, whose stage presence was once described by a director as “very alien, like Barbie from outer space.”

The prize’s new partner, Visu Contemporary, is located just a few hundred feet from the Bass Museum of Art and a few blocks from the Miami Beach Convention Center. Under owner and curator Bruce Halpryn, the gallery has shown artists like Barry X. Ball, David LaChapelle, and Dustin Yellin. Annual public programming will spotlight artists from the Queer | Art community, including prize winners and finalists. A future exhibition of Queer Art Prize winners will take place in 2027.

Founded in 2009, Queer | Art provides intergenerational mentorship, public programming, and material support for LGBTQ+ artists. The organization hopes to create some of the intergenerational connection, particularly mentorship, that has been devastated by the ongoing AIDS pandemic. The Queer Art Prize was inaugurated in 2017 and continued until 2022, supported by HBO, which then abruptly withdrew its sponsorship. HBO did not respond to emails from ARTnews requesting comment about the end of its support.

In 2023, various companies in the US saw a rash of controversy for marketing themselves to queer communities. When Bud Light ran a social media promotion including transgender actress Dylvan Mulvaney, some boycotted the brand; Trump-aligned entertainer Kid Rock filmed himself shooting cases of the drink with a submachine gun. Retailer Target saw sales decline after it offered LGBTQ+ merchandise during Pride month that year. Companies like Calvin Klein, Glamour, Adidas, and the North Face came in for similar backlash for highlighting trans people. At the same time, at least nine GOP-led state legislatures moved to restrict or even criminalize drag shows.

Sofia, Queer | Art’s director, said HBO wasn’t the only one to part ways with the nonprofit: The organization lost all its corporate funding, some $200,000, in the same period. Under the Trump administration, it has also lost government funding that it previously received.

The Queer Art Prize returns at a moment when the American political establishment could hardly be said to have warmed to LGBTQ+ people, with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth even using a slur to refer to transgender people in a recent speech and the Trump administration adopting the official position that there are only two genders, denying the existence of transgender people.

While some organizations may have recently toned down their messaging to avoid controversy, Sofia said, “We are the Queer Art Prize! So there’s no hiding what we do. Our approach has been to double down on both the size of the organization and our capacity.”

Doubling the prizes and giving cash to the runners-up, she said, “sends a really clear message about what we’re about and the kind of defiance that is part of the roots of an organization that was founded by ACT UP members.”

In a statement, Visu’s Halpryn said, “Supporting Queer|Art in establishing these prizes is not philanthropy—it’s cultural authorship at a moment when visibility itself is contested. LGBTQIA+ artists have shaped the trajectory of contemporary art for decades, yet too often without proportional recognition or institutional backing. As a gay curator, I believe that acknowledging both lifetime achievement and new work is essential to correcting that imbalance and defining what comes next.”

Halpryn added, “This partnership is designed not just to honor artists, but to place their work in sustained dialogue with collectors, curators, and the public. If we care about the future of culture, we have to be deliberate about whose voices we elevate—and whose histories we refuse to let disappear.”
