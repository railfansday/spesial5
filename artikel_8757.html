15-Minute Evening Yoga to Unwind (That You Can Practice in Bed)

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

This short evening yoga routine is designed to help you release stress when you’re ready to wind down for the evening or let go of the day. It brings you a full-body stretch and a lot of relaxation. The poses are all either seated or lying down, so there’s very little effort required. You can even practice the yoga for better sleep routine in bed.

The stretches are essentially yin yoga poses, except we’re staying in the shapes for less time than the usual 3 to 5 minutes that we expect from yin yoga. But these are all poses that you would find in a yin yoga class, so you could stay in any or all of the poses a little longer. The exact 15-minute sequence could be a 75-minute yin yoga class if you have the time or wake up in the middle of the night and are trying to go back to sleep.

Because you’re trying to calm the body and mind, these stretches are not about chasing sensation or pushing yourself into an extreme shape. Try to find a little more ease in each pose as you invite a slower, deeper rhythm to your breath, which signals your nervous system that it’s time to unwind and release. Every time you exhale, see if you can relax a different part of your body and let go of whatever is on your mind from your day.

These stretches are suitable for any experience level. No props are required. Let yourself linger and release into each shape and stay there for 5 to 10 breaths.

Start sitting and then straighten your right leg in front of you. Option one, you can either do your Head to Knee Pose, which we call Half Butterfly in yin yoga, with your left foot to the inside of your thigh.

Option two, you can stretch a little more through the hip and inner thigh if you take your left knee out to the side and your foot behind you with your heel tucked in toward your glutes. If you try option two and you experience any pain or sensitivity in the knee, come back to the first option.

Then start to walk your hands in front of in a forward bend. Let it be a passive stretch, which means you want to let your back round and your head hang. Let gravity do the work for you. Stay here for about 5 breaths or so. While you are in the pose, can you let go of any tension in your arms, jaw, and neck?

Press your hands against the mat and slowly lift yourself back up to sitting.

If it’s not already, bend your left knee out to the side so it’s in line with your left hip. You want to create about a 90-degree bend in your leg and bring your ankle in line with your knee. Then align your right knee so it’s also at a 90-degree angle.

Press your left hip back toward the wall behind you and start to walk your hands back on a diagonal behind you. As soon as you start to feel that sensation through your left hip, pause and breathe into it a little. You can stay propped up on your palms, lower onto your forearms, or lie all the way flat on your back. I’m going to stay propped up on my hands. I find I get the best stretch this way. Breathe here.

Walk your hands in, slowly lifting yourself upright.

Bring your left leg straight in front of you. Cross your left foot over your right thigh, bringing your left foot to the mat outside your right leg. Wrap your right arm around your left and bring your left fingertips to the mat behind you and let them support you. Lift tall through the spine, pressing your shoulders down and away from your ears, then twist toward the left in a Seated Twist. Press into your left big toe and keep both sit bones on the mat. Breathe here.

Then take your Head to Knee or Half Butterfly on the other side, followed by Deer Pose or 90/90 and Seated Twist.

Lower all the way down onto your back. Pull your knees toward your chest. Then initiate a little pumping motion. So place your hands on the tops of your knees.

As you inhale, straighten your arms and push your thighs away.

As you exhale, draw your thighs back toward you. Try to relax your legs fully so your arms are controlling the motion. As you do this simple motion, you’re trying to release your lower back muscles as much as possible. You almost want to feel like your legs are moving in water. Take a few more like this.

The next time you bring your knees toward you, widen them toward your shoulders. Either hold here or come into Happy Baby by grabbing hold of your big toes with your two peace fingers, taking your elbows to the insides of your knees, and using your arms to push your legs open a little more. Think of pressing your lower back into the mat, keeping your head and shoulders down on the ground. Sometimes rocking a little side to side can feel really good.

From Happy Baby, keep your right knee close to your chest and extend your left leg straight on the mat to stretch the hip flexors along the front of your left hip. You can take a few ankle rolls with your right foot by making circles, flexing and pointing through your toes, and making any motion through the ankles. Reverse the direction of those circles.

Come into your twist from here, crossing your right leg over your body to the left with the help of your left hand. Stretch your right arm straight out to the side. Think of relaxing your right shoulder down and trying to stack your right hip on top of your left hip.

If you notice yourself obsessing over anything or going over and over a particular thought, just observe what’s happening. Notice that tendency in that pattern, and maybe instead focus on something else that’s a bit more tangible, like the physical sensations in your body from the poses or just the flow of your breath.

Straighten your right leg any amount, stretching the hamstrings. You can flex and point your foot a couple times.

Then come into Reclined Pigeon Pose by sliding your left foot in, crossing your right ankle over the top of your left knee in a figure-four shape, and reaching your arms along either side of your left leg as you pull your left thigh toward you. Only using a little arm strength. Try to relax your shoulders and keep your head on the  mat. Breathe here.

Keep your left knee drawn toward your chest as you release the pose and extend your right leg straight on the mat. Repeat your ankle rolls, Reclined Twist, and Reclined Pigeon on your left side.

Then you’ll set yourself up for Savasana, your final resting pose. Take up some space with your arms and your legs and then close your eyes. You might do a body scan, bringing your awareness to the crown of your head and slowly working your way down to your fingers and your toes, asking each muscle to relax, to let go, almost as if you could sink into the mat or mattress.

Allow yourself about two minutes here or more if you have the time. Just enjoy the silence and stillness. If you’re already in bed, maybe you stay here.

Start to deepen your breath. Maybe you move your fingers and toes a little. Nice and slow, roll to one side and come to take a seat. Find some length in your spine as you release your shoulders down.

Just pause here. Notice how you feel after just a few minutes of stretching and silence.

Thank you so much, everyone, for doing this short bedtime practice. I hope you feel ready to ease into the rest of your night.
