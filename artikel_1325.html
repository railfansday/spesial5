Le Tissier Tipped Him As Southampton's Next Lallana, Now He Plays Non-League Football

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

In the last 50 years, Southampton are widely recognised as having one of the best academies in football.

From bringing through the likes of Matthew Le Tissier and Alan Shearer in the 1980s, to Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain and James Ward-Prowse in more recent years, Southampton have made a lot of money through player sales.

As well as Le Tissier and Shearer, considered to be two of the greatest Premier League players of all time, Gareth Bale and Theo Walcott are two more players from the academy who went on to play at the highest level for Real Madrid and Arsenal.

Luke Shaw was another who came through the ranks before joining Manchester United, where he still is 13 years later, and Calum Chambers was another who left St Mary's for Arsenal after breaking through under Mauricio Pochettino.

It's fair to say that those success stories have dried up a little in more recent years, while the club has spent more time battling at the bottom of the Premier League and in the Championship, with a number of players failing to make the grade having made first-team appearances.

Striker Sam Gallagher left for Blackburn Rovers in 2019, Matt Targett joined Aston Villa after a 15-year association with the south coast outfit, while Harrison Reed signed for Fulham after failing to break into the midfield at St Mary's.

Two of the more recent rare success stories are Tyler Dibling, who joined Everton for a huge fee in the summer of 2025, and Jimmy-Jay Morgan, who is currently scoring for fun at West Bromwich Albion after leaving Saints for Chelsea as a 17-year-old.

Southampton have had other young players who looked on course for a big future at St Mary's too, especially following their U21 Premier League Cup win against Blackburn in 2015.

After watching the final live, Saints legend Le Tissier was asked by Sky Sports to pick out five youngsters to watch who had first-team potential, and he selected Gallagher, Targett, Reed and Sam McQueen.

Le Tissier was particularly complimentary about his final pick, Jake Hesketh, who joined Saints' academy at the age of 10 having been born in Stockport.

He reminds me of Adam Lallana in the way he plays. He's got good feet, moves with the ball really well, and is very, very natural.

"However, he's got a bit of growing and strengthening to do and it won't be an instant thing for him to break through to senior level."

It turned out that Le Tissier may have been right, about his warning over Hesketh.

The teenager was actually promoted to the first-team by Ronald Koeman during the 2014-15 season because of injury issues, and he made his debut for the club in a 2-1 home defeat to Manchester United in December.

Hesketh made a further top flight appearance against Burnley, but he then fell back down the pecking order until Claude Puel was appointed as Koeman's successor in 2016.

He made two further appearances under the Frenchman, scoring his first senior goal in a League Cup tie against Crystal Palace in September 2016, and he also played in the Europa League tie away against Hapoel Be'er Sheva.

The attacking midfielder then picked up an injury and even though he signed a new four-and-a-half year contract in December 2016, he never wore the Southampton shirt again.

He spent time on loan at Burton Albion, MK Dons, Lincoln City and Crawley Town, before leaving St Mary's on a permanent basis for Eastleigh in 2021 after 17 years with the club.

Following two seasons with the National League outfit, Hesketh remained in the Southampton area when he joined Southern League Premier Division club Sholing FC, where he still plays to this day.as a 30-year-old.
