Forget Okafor and DCL: Ao Tanaka Just Humiliated Chelsea

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Attacking standouts such as Noah Okafor will take the headlines after Leeds United dismantled Chelsea at Stamford Bridge but the man who unlocked that performance deserves just as many accolades.

Daniel Farke's side came out of the international break with a superb performance as they hit back immediately from going a goal down to dominate the rest of the proceedings on the way to a 4-1 victory.

It was the visitors' first win at Stamford Bridge this century and saw United overtake their hosts in the Premier League table as they climbed to sixth, thanks to an Okafor brace, a penalty from Dominic Calvert-Lewin, and Brenden Aaronson's late fourth.

Emmanuel Emegha had opened the scoring in the 25th minute with his first goal for Chelsea, but Okafor cancelled it out moments later as he slid the equaliser into the far corner with the Blues' defence panicking as he entered the box.

Michael Oliver awarded Leeds a penalty early in the second half when a shot was blocked by Morgan Rogers' arm, with insufficient evidence available to VAR to say whether it had hit him above the T-shirt line and whether they could overturn it, with Calvert-Lewin stepping up to send Emi Martinez the wrong way.

Okafor doubled the lead just five minutes later to effectively end the contest, slamming into the top corner from a deflected ball across the area, and substitute Aaronson rounded things off in stoppage time as he surged clear and cut inside before slipping the ball between Martinez's legs.

But as impressive as the Whites were in attack, the game was won in midfield, where Chelsea lost Moises Caicedo to injury mid-way through the first half and subsequently failed to regain any sort of control.

It is difficult to pick just one from Leeds' central midfield pairing of ex-Blue Ethan Ampadu and Ao Tanaka, but the Japanese international was outstanding on the day.

The Yorkshire Evening Post rated him 9/10 following the final whistle, and it wasn't hard to see why after his excellent work in and out of possession, particularly as his side took charge after the break.

Tanaka was ably supported by Ampadu as they took full advantage of Chelsea losing one of their £100 million men, leaving replacement Jordan Henderson, and Romeo Lavia, over-matched in the middle.

As is so often the case on Leeds' best days under Farke, including on previous occasions against the same opposition, Tanaka was the heartbeat of the side as he moved the ball around efficiently and didn't give his opposite numbers time to think.

Stats compiled by FotMob indicate the 28-year-old was highly effective with his 59 touches across 86 minutes on the pitch, as he bounced back from a series of early fouls conceded to shine as the game went on.

With the ball at his feet, Tanaka kept Leeds moving efficiently, completing 43 of his 49 passes, and wasn't dispossessed once on the day, while his defensive contributions also made a real difference.

The Leeds man made a pair of successful tackles, two interceptions, and seven recoveries of possession, and wasn't dribbled past once as part of an excellent display all round.

Chelsea simply couldn't put together enough passes to threaten getting back into the game as Tanaka and his teammates didn't give them the time or space to regroup, while operating with far more zip when going the other way.

Okafor's finishes will deservedly take much of the focus, but the attacking platform provided by the midfield was vital, just as it was in ensuring James Trafford was relatively untested at the back.

A first Stamford Bridge win since 1999 appeared to be on the cards earlier this season when Farke took his side to West London in the Carabao Cup, only for it to be snatched away during a chaotic second half in September.

Leeds led 2-0 at the break before a Cole Palmer penalty sparked a turnaround which resulted in a 6-3 Chelsea victory and extended the run without a victory at this ground by one more game.

It was very much the reverse on Saturday afternoon as the hosts this time went ahead but collapsed themselves following the interval to see Farke and his players depart with a vital three points.

And on current evidence it is unlikely to be another 27 years until Leeds get another win away at one of their old rivals.

Leeds must balance short term cash gains against squad depth amid wage disparities that reshape transfer priorities.
