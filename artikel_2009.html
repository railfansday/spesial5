Sheffield United boss Chris Wilder on injury 'hammer blow' and speculation over Newcastle United and Sunderland target

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Wilder, whose side welcome Watford this weekend, said: "He's had an operation. He's going to be out for 12 months. Straight away, I think we all knew where that was going, how awkward he landed. And straight away, I think you always go on the reaction of the players.

"It's a long recovery for him and a testing time for him and a testing time for us because we're losing an attack-minded player that we're looking forward to getting back amongst us. He was finding his feet, he was coming off the bench and he most probably would have been in contention to start now. But that's not the case.

"He's devastated, as we all are. We're devastated for him because this is a big injury for him. One hundred per cent, it will make a full recovery.

"The medical team here are absolutely spot on, but we've had big injuries in the past. Blaster had his ACL and there's other injuries that we've had and we've had to get over, as everybody else and other clubs will have to do. But it's a real big blow, it's a hammer blow for us and even more so for the boy, especially at his age and when he's starting to kick on."

Meanwhile, Wilder insists he remains relaxed regarding recent speculation regarding key midfielder Sydie Peck, rumoured to be on the wanted list of Premier League clubs in the January window.

North-east duo Newcastle United and Sunderland have been linked with moves, but Wilder is obviously keen to retain the player - while being pragmatic that things could change if a huge big-money offer was tabled.

Wilder, who has Patrick Bamford back available, said: "We talked about extending the contracts of the young players and Sydie's one of those players that we'd love to have that discussion with.

"(But) If anything comes up that you can't turn down, I don't think anybody would turn that down. I've said it all along, you're not just turning that from a club's point of view, you're turning that down from an opportunity for the player to go and play in the Premier League. I'm glad (about) that because that weren't happening about 15 months ago, the value of the players was zero.

"The previous year, the value of the players, in my opinion, was about £100 million and that's what happens when you win games of football and you get a team together and you build a team that wins.

"We're trying to reset that and rebuild those values of the players and all players have got a value. When the young players are playing well - they're not babies no more, but they're still relatively young in their careers and have Championship experience - then we're happy because they're playing consistently well.

"When that happens, if that happens, and I'm sure it will happen, then we'll deal with that. My sole focus, which I've said and I've used this word over the last three or four months, is building a group, not just off the back of one pre-season."
