Chelsea slapped with huge £150,000 fine after 'f*** the Pope and the IRA' fan chants in last season's FA Cup tie at Charlton

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Published: 11:24 EST, 20 November 2026 | Updated: 18:21 EST, 20 November 2026

Chelsea have been slapped with a significant £150,000 fine by the Football Association for their fans’ behaviour during an FA Cup away tie at Charlton Athletic more than ten months ago.

It was alleged that in the 56th minute, the visiting supporters at the Valley sang ‘f*** the Pope and the IRA’. A charge followed, and Chelsea were hit with their fine, along with being given an action plan to follow and a formal warning as to their future conduct.

Chelsea tried to appeal being fined £150,000 for the sectarian chanting. They wanted it either removed completely, or at least substantially reduced.

The club attempted to argue that it was excessive despite admitting that approximately 2,500 of their fans may have taken part in the offensive singing. They also noted how Millwall were previously not fined for the same chant occurring.

Their appeal was dismissed and the full scale of their punishment was upheld.

In a statement, Chelsea said of the chanting: ‘We unequivocally condemn this behaviour. Sectarian abuse – chanting that creates division targeted towards a particular religious or political group – and discriminatory language of any kind has no place in our football club.

Xabi Alonso insists his Chelsea stars will show they are not 'soft' after Leeds United rubbed in how often they have bullied their opponents in a video uploaded to their social media.

Leeds produced a compilation of clips of their players leaving crunching challenges in on their Chelsea counterparts and capitalising on mistakes made down the years.

Daniel Farke's side have won more duels than any other club in the Premier League this season, and Alonso's team crumbled when visiting Sunderland before the international break. That was a game in which Levi Colwill and Co were bullied by Brian Brobbey. Colwill was hooked at half-time in that 1-0 loss at the Stadium of Light.

Dominic Calvert-Lewin may now be relishing testing Chelsea at Stamford Bridge, but Alonso said when asked about being seen as weak: 'We focus on not being soft and being competitive. That's our goal. We have played once this year (a 6-3 win for Chelsea in the Carabao Cup). We will play twice more (in the league). We are ready for that. I like that. We will show that.'

Calvert-Lewin shares similar attributes to Brobbey in that he is a traditional centre forward who will challenge Chelsea's centre backs.

'It¿s quite common in the Premier League that they have a good No 9,' Alonso said. 'Calvert-Lewin is doing well. He played with England. But it is up to us, how we defend, how we compete in those situations, how we attack the last line, how we attack their defenders. The preparation has, as always, we think been good. We are ready.'

Asked if defenders have forgotten how to face such strikers, Alonso added: 'Forgotten? No, no, no, I don't think forgotten. But it's demanding to play against No 9s. They have good movement in the box, out the box. This is the demand. You can ask the full-backs, they have to play against great wingers in one v one situations. But I don't think it's been forgotten. But it's good they are back, these type of players.'

Only Tottenham have made more changes to their line-up than Chelsea this season, adding to the suggestion that Alonso is still searching for his preferred style of play. Some have been forced on the Spaniard because of their habit for picking up injuries despite playing only once a week.

'Once you have the whole menu, you can select what you like and I know what I like from the menu,' Alonso said. 'I would like to have more options with the whole squad available but that's something that's difficult to have during the season. I am sure we will get close to that.

'It (the injuries) is a concern. Sometimes you can analyse them and find a reason but sometimes it is difficult to find a reason.'

They added: ‘We understand that further incidents risk additional sanctions against the club and are doing everything we can to inform and educate supporters, as well as develop our existing initiatives to prevent, detect and respond to discrimination in future. We know Chelsea fans do not want to see any form of discrimination in our game, and we ask for their support in fostering a welcoming environment at Chelsea matches going forward.’

On the pitch, Chelsea will go into their Saturday clash against Leeds United without star striker Joao Pedro, who has yet to return to training.

Since the Brazilian was sidelined, Chelsea have won only one of their three Premier League games played, beating a beleaguered Manchester United under Michael Carrick. Otherwise, they have lost to Tottenham Hotspur and Sunderland without scoring.

Joao Pedro is still nursing a thigh problem, and has not yet made it outside with his team-mates as Leeds visit Stamford Bridge on Saturday. Chelsea will, however, have Moises Caicedo available after his injury scare during the international break with Ecuador.

‘He's not fit yet to train with the team, so we need to wait a bit longer,’ Alonso confirmed on Joao Pedro. He’s not going to be available for Leeds. We need to see game by game how he's feeling. So far we have tried different options and we need to keep trying.’

Chelsea will have Emiliano Martinez available after recovering from his broken jaw suffered at Everton on October 17. The goalkeeper required surgery following his collision with Jarrad Branthwaite. Alonso confirmed he no longer needs to wear a mask, having been training without it, and the 34-year-old Argentinian could be in line for his first club appearance since his injury.
