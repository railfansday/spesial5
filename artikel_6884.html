How to Play GTA VI All Weekend Without Destroying Your Body

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The day has finally arrived. Grand Theft Auto VI is here, and you’ve taken that “sick day” to cause chaos on the streets of Vice City. After more than 10 years of waiting, you’re finally back in one of the franchise’s most classic locations, this time with an open world to explore. It’s not hyperbole to call this the most highly anticipated game in history. With more than 17 million preorders, that’s a lot of people who will be running from the police while trying to avoid (or rack up) six stars.

If you’re one of the many fans who’ve cleared out their schedule to binge the game this weekend, you’ll want to make sure you emerge from your haze with extremities that still function.

“The metaphor we like to share with pro players or casual gamers is the concept of the health bar,” says Dr. Matthew Hwu, owner of physical therapy clinic 1 Health Point in Monrovia, California. “The muscles and tendons of your wrist, hand, thumb, as you move the analog stick, only have so much capacity or ‘max HP’ that they can handle throughout the day.”

Below, we spoke with Dr. Hwu and other veteran gamers about how to keep your “HP” bar full for your whole weekend without developing carpal tunnel.

Playing a game for long hours puts the soft tissues in your hands and arms into overdrive. When you ramp things up, you’ll feel sore and fatigued. The most common version of this is “tendon-based problems of the wrist and hand,” says Dr. Hwu, who has spent more than 11 years working with e-sports players to help them recover from injuries. As GTA VI will be available on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series S and X, most people will be using a controller, which puts the thumbs under a lot of stress as people “control the analog stick to flip left, up, right, down; press A, B, X, Y.” The most common gaming injuries are gamer’s thumb and tennis elbow, according to the Cleveland Clinic.

So, don’t forget to take breaks. Dr. Hwu recommends at least a five-minute break for every 90 minutes played, though younger players are more resilient. If you want to push it, the key is taking longer breaks afterwards. “Two hours, 10-minute break, three hours, 15-minute break,” Dr. Hwu says. “If you want to be degenerate, you can be a smart degen.” You can also be strategic about scheduling more downtime after sequences with higher APM (actions per minute), that are more intensive on hand muscles. In essence, go touch grass after a high-stakes bank robbery.

That said, after more than a decade of waiting for what our reviewer described as “the most technically and artistically impressive game world ever made,” your instinct may be to lock in. So it’s wise to plan for breaks ahead of time. Set alarms on your phone or watch. If one goes off in the middle of a mission, commit to taking a break after crashing that helicopter. Make sure you eat and stay hydrated, and move your body, whether that’s a neighborhood walk or just a short twirl around your apartment before your next mission.

“It’s basically just your muscle and tendon tissue recovery, it’ll restore a little bit of HP over time,” Dr. Hwu says. “When you go out and walk outdoors, that can help with your overall circulation, nervous system as well. So you don’t feel like that meme where you come out of the cave and you’re like a ghost.”

Just like in other forms of training, stretching is a key part of your self-care arsenal for long hours of gaming. To loosen up for a long stretch of car-hijacking, do a few stretches before turning on your console, and during cut scenes. Your body will tell you what areas feel tight and could use some attention. But don’t forget those thumb muscles. “Bring your thumb away from the next finger, stretching it down,” Dr. Hwu says. “Every time you’re flicking back, left, or right, you might be using these muscles.”

You’ll also want to stretch your index fingers after long hours of beating ass with a crowbar while riding your motorcycle. “Every time you press your trigger,” Dr. Hwu says, “you’re using the extensors, the top side of your fingers, but also the flexors to push down.” You can also work on relaxing your forearm muscles, by extending your arm out and pushing your palm downward.

Don’t forget your neck and upper back, which can feel stiff from holding onto stress after high speed chases and parachute combat. It’s so easy to load tension onto those upper traps as you shrimp further into your recliner while bird watching, or scoring a pair of black half-rim sunglasses and Eris sneakers (definitely not Nikes) for Jason (no judgment here).

“I will do small things: roll my neck from side to side, stretch my traps by sitting on one hand and pulling my head [in the] opposite direction,” Calliope Ryder, platform director at GYLD, previously a producer on Tales of the Shire, says. “[I] tense my calves in spurts to help with blood flow, or stand up and let my body fold at the hips while swaying side to side with my arms crossed. Again, alerts to remind me to do it are necessary or I'll go hours without noticing.”

Meanwhile, using a heating pad or an ice pack on a sore area (like your lower back) can help with any nagging dull pain you’re feeling. Heat loosens up stiff muscles, while ice can help dull the achy sensation. They won’t build your long-term strength, but they’ll make you feel better in the moment.

If you’re old enough for back pain, then you’re old enough to have to consider video game ergonomics. Wherever you’re gaming, Dr. Hwu’s recommendation is simple: Sit with your legs at a 90-degree angle, feet planted on the ground, back appropriately supported. Then put a pillow on your lap and rest your arms on it. This helps prevent forearm strain and hunching over. You can even haul your expensive Aeron from your office into the living room, rather than rotting on the sofa and ruining your back.

There are also a number of helpful gaming peripherals. Maddy Myers, editor in chief and co-founder of video game publication Mothership, uses a specific gaming pillow from a brand called Mechanism. “The pillow allows you to have the handheld device suspended in the air in a comfortable, adjustable spot so that you don’t tire out your arms from holding it up,” she says. “You can also put a controller on there.”

Using the device helps her get through play stretches of eight hours a day on the weekends, “while taking breaks to eat, do chores, stretch.” It’s been especially invaluable during high-intensity work periods. “I have to play GTA 6 for my job, so I am really going to be relying on these tools to keep my arms and body feeling as close to normal as possible,” she says.

Don’t let a terribly designed controller be the reason you develop carpal tunnel. Julia Lee, section lead for video game guides at Polygon, plays at least two hours of games a day, and will sometimes fit in 10-hour sessions. Her wrists started hurting and “those flat [Nintendo Switch] Joy-Cons were directly to blame,” she says. “I ended up getting a Nintendo Switch Pro Controller (which has a much more ergonomic shape and grip), and that helped alleviate pain a lot.” She also reminds herself not to hold the controller so tight, as it causes pain.

As Dr. Hwu explains, some controllers have staggered sticks, while others have an inline design. It all comes down to individual comfort and muscular endurance. He’s helped pro Call of Duty players modify their analog sticks, and has coached people to avoid death-gripping the controller. “If you use a claw grip position, an awkward grip, or a modded controller, that might lead to more stress for certain muscles in your wrist and hand,” he says.

If your hands are really killing you, there’s also the option of switching to another controller style altogether. “Moving two to four of my most awkward hand-based actions from my hands to my feet allows me to play longer,” John Warren, chief marketing officer and cofounder of Glydr, a foot-based video game controller, says. Warren has minicore disease, which causes muscle weakness. “Anytime you have an opportunity to stop overloading your hands while you play, you give your hands a little reprieve,” he says, “it makes your high-precision hand actions more accurate.”

Lastly, if you have existing injuries, and your medical provider has prescribed a brace, actually follow their guidance for how and when to use it. For my part, I use thumb braces called the Push Metagrips, to help support my floppy thumb joints anytime I plan on playing video games for long stretches.

So you’ve done all this work to binge GTA 6, and you’re feeling solid but a little sore. But you want to play for longer periods, snipe with greater accuracy, and fuck around with less fatigue. Consider going to the gym.

Doing exercises for those small muscles and tendons can help you build up that tolerance, like any other form of weight and endurance training. “When you rest, ice, massage, kinesio tape, you know, passive interventions, bracing, all of these things can restore your HP,” Dr. Hwu says. “It does nothing to change the size of your health bar, which represents the tissue’s capacity or your endurance. The way we do that is you can do specific exercises to build up or create a buffer for how much you’re going to handle.”

For any chronic pain, injuries, or conditions, it’s important to work with a physical therapist. I’m currently working with one for repetitive stress injury recovery, and doing exercises specific to playing video games. I do slow wrist curls with a two-pound weight, rotator cuff exercises with a resistance band, and squeeze an egg-shaped stress ball for thumb and forearm endurance. I also train my shoulders and core.

For those who aren’t managing any injuries, training those finger and arm muscles make it easier to game for longer periods. Consistency is key, and so is having reasonable expectations. Dr. Hwu recommends starting once a day, three to four times a week, “on a day where you’re not doing a lot, and then you’ll learn how your body responds to doing exercises within the context of your specific life, and you can make modifications to scale up the aggressiveness or scale down,” he says. As for seeing progress, “it takes four to six weeks for muscles, eight weeks for tendons.”

And don’t forget the stabilizer muscles in your shoulders, which hold up your upper body and support your arms. “I focus on ‘chest opening’ exercises that strengthen your back,” Ryder says. “I tend towards curving forward so I consciously strengthen the 'opposite' muscles so they pull my shoulders back a bit.” She also does chin tucks, to strengthen her deep neck flexors.

Strength training is also a great way to tackle back pain, another common complaint after gaming for hours. “A few years ago I started having extreme back pain when I sat in my office chair,” Gita Jackson, cofounder of video game publication Aftermath, says. They play one to two hours of games a day, but often play marathon sessions when new games come out. “The pain was excruciating, and sometimes my legs would get so stiff I couldn’t walk. Recovery started with physical therapy, and then a new ergonomic chair,” and now they regularly attend pilates.

“I really had no idea that if I wanted to continue playing noodley strategy games without pain that I would have to be a person who works out multiple times a week,” they say.

Maybe you’ve done a little too much, and your arms start to feel heavy as bricks, with thumbs that ache for hours. Even with the best precautions in place, it can happen to any of us.

“If it gets to a point where cumulatively, over days, symptoms are getting worse, maybe reflect and identify what you could change,” Dr. Hwu says. “If things are consistently sharp, or you start to feel some weakness, where when you go and try to pick up something, you’ll want to drop it. That’s a sign that it may be time to make a change in some of the variables that you can control.”

In 2002, weeks before the release of Grand Theft Auto: Vice City, the journalist David Kushner met Sam and Dan Houser, plus Rockstar co-founder Terry Donovan, at their SoHo loft. It would be their last and only in-depth sit-down before they decided that the world’s most successful video game required little explanation.

This is when paying attention to your daily habits is key. Instead of freaking out over hand pain, and deciding you need to quit playing cold turkey, you can instead think about what you did during the day that might have contributed to your discomfort. “How much you’re playing, what intensity you’re playing, are you taking breaks, are you exercising, how are you responding to the pain?” Dr. Hwu says. These are all factors in why you might be having pain, and all things to consider modifying. If it truly is tendon fatigue, then the key is finding the right volume of use, rather than quitting altogether.

There are also some signs to look out for that may require medical intervention. Dr. Hwu pointed me to 1 Health Point’s website, which suggests seeking a doctor’s opinion if your wrist looks swollen or bruised, if you can’t move or bear any weight, if your symptoms worsen or spread with rest, or if you experience constant hand numbness, especially if it comes with weakness.

Gaming for long hours ultimately comes down to using your common sense alongside your precious free will. Pay attention to your habits. Commit to taking breaks and stretching between bank robberies and doomscrolling through Snapmatic (in-game social media). Do some preventative exercises while planning out your next mission—with a soundtrack that includes Bad Bunny and Dua Lipa, as well as some ’80s bangers, you’ve already got the music for it. Your hands and arms will thank you, even if the regular people of Leonida probably won’t.
