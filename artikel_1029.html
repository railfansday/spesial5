Liverpool captain Virgil van Dijk remains uncertain of whether he will remain at the club beyond the end of the season.

The 35-year-old centre-back is approaching the final six months of his contract, which expires next summer.

He believes that he can keep playing at the top level for “many more years” but says he has “no idea” whether that will be at Anfield as Liverpool’s decision-makers continue to ponder over a fresh offer.

"The main thing for me is that I feel good and I'm looking after myself and I still have many more years in my opinion," Van Dijk told Sky Sports.

"Whatever's going to happen after this season, we'll see. At this moment of time, if anyone says that or put out that they know what I'm going to do next season, it's untrue because at this moment of time, speaking to you, I've got no idea either.

"But I'm relaxed with it because if you know me, I only focus on what I can control and that's at the moment the Manchester United game. Then we'll focus to the Champions League and whatever happens in the new year coming, we'll see then.

"But it's been an incredible journey, almost 10 years this summer and it's been a big part of my life and I'll forever be grateful for anything that happened over those years.

"If it's getting extended, we'll see then. If not, then I'm going to say goodbyes in the summer."

Van Dijk has won two Premier League titles, including the club’s first in 30 years, as well as a Champions League with Liverpool, stamping his mark as one of the greatest centre-backs in the English top flight’s history since his £75m arrival from Southampton in 2018.

He is one of the last remaining figures from Jurgen Klopp’s side that won every trophy there was to win between 2019 and 2022, with Mohamed Salah and Andy Robertson notably bidding emotional farewells at the end of last season.

Asked about the prospect of having to say goodbye to Anfield himself, Van Dijk said: "Well, yeah, probably for me the most and the family because it's been an incredible time.

"But I don't look too far in the future and that's been, in my opinion, also a little bit of a success. I won't change it now either.

Liverpool host Manchester United for new manager Mauricio Pochettino’s first game on Sunday afternoon.
