These 6 Telecom Stocks Collect Monthly Bills No Matter What the Market Does

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

These businesses are built to generate recurring cash flows.

Most investors are willing to take the occasional swing on a risky growth stock. The fact is, however, companies like AT&T (T +0.00%), Verizon Communications (VZ +0.48%), T-Mobile US (TMUS +1.43%), Comcast (CMCSA +0.02%), Charter Communications (CHTR +2.26%), and Lumen Technologies (LUMN +1.00%) are the kinds of companies that provide the reliable results that investors need.

Their common thread? They're all telecom names with businesses that generate regular revenue from customers paying monthly bills to maintain these connections...at least for the time being. In some of these cases, that could be changing soon.

Here's a closer look at each one and what the foreseeable future holds.

It didn't necessarily have to be this way. AT&T arguably just tried too hard to expand into arenas it thought would be more natural fits. It acquired satellite cable brand DirecTV in 2015, for instance, and then scooped up entertainment powerhouse Time Warner in 2018. Both businesses have since been shed, but at a loss that's still echoing. The company stopped raising its dividend in 2022, aiming to shore up a relatively ugly balance sheet. Although its customers still pay their bills like clockwork, AT&T still has a great deal of work to do to become a top-tier investment again.

Some investors are surprised to learn that T-Mobile, rather than Verizon, is the nation's single-biggest wireless service provider in terms of customer headcount, with 142 million paying customers. Just remember this figure also includes all of the customers that came over from Sprint when the two companies were combined in 2020.

It's been a pretty solid performer too, although not unlike AT&T, its foray into the cable television business with what ended up being launched as TVision in 2019 was probably ill-advised. Meanwhile, it remains to be seen if its at-home wireless internet endeavor will pay off in the long run, although, as of the latest disclosure, there are over 8 million of these broadband customers. Not bad.

This focus -- paired with capital spending with the biggest ROI -- has been well worth it too. During its recently ended fiscal Q3, the company experienced its strongest third-quarter of postpaid phone customer growth since 2021, and its highest net growth in total accounts in over a decade, prompting Verizon to raise its full-year revenue guidance for a third straight quarter.

Shareholders are benefiting from this resiliency in an immediate way as well, with this stock's dividend. It's now been raised for 20 consecutive years, reflecting the company's ongoing growth. You can plug into this ticker while its forward-looking yield stands at 6.2%, which is the highest among the three mobile telecom names in focus here.

And that's a problem. For a long while, the growth of its broadband business could offset the slow attrition of cable customers due to cord-cutting. Now that's not even happening. That's why Comcast's overall revenue hasn't grown in years.

It's not necessarily the end of the world just yet. Total revenue isn't shrinking either. Customers still need to keep their connections active, and the company is still turning more than enough of their monthly payments into profits, which in turn supports its dividend payment.

For the first time in a long time, though, Comcast didn't raise its per-share payout at the beginning of this year. It may be a hint that the long-term sustainability of its business has reached a tipping point.

You may know Charter Communications better by its operating brand name Spectrum, which offers cable television and broadband internet service, although, like Comcast, it's also easing into the wireless telecom arena. It was serving 13.5 million mobile customers as of the end of September, which is roughly one-third of its total 36.7 million customer relationships.

But does it offer a solid dividend until then? Nope, Charter has never paid a dividend, and isn't apt to start now. Instead, it has used its earnings to fund stock buybacks, which does add shareholder value. There's no particularly beneficial endgame in sight for shareholders, however. The company is just mitigating how its business is fading away.

Last but not least, Lumen Technologies is one of a handful of telecom outfits with reliable revenue from its customers' monthly bill payments. Just not in the way you might imagine.

It's not a household name. That's because it doesn't serve consumer households. Rather, Lumen offers networking solutions specifically built for artificial intelligence platforms.

The company's near future isn't entirely clear. Although its third-quarter total revenue fell to $2.7 billion, its strategic revenue improved 10% year over year to $1.28 billion thanks to a 24% quarter-to-quarter increase in active customers. It's per-share loss was pared back from $0.20 to a loss of only $0.14 per share this time around, but Lumen shrank its way to that progress.

A bet on Lumen Technologies is still ultimately a bet on the continued proliferation of AI data centers, though, and the subsequent need to make them safer, faster, and more efficient. Just be patient as it makes this transition. It's expected to report slightly lower revenue again next year, as it continues to chip away at its losses.
