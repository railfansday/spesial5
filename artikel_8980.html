john pawson sets white ribbon staircase against 18th century baroque portal at the madrid EDITION

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

ian schrager pairs john pawson’s refined minimalism with françois champsaur’s artistry inside madrid’s former monte de piedad bank, converted by OAB and ruiz-larrea into the hotel brand's second spanish address.

sofia lekka angelopoulou November 17, 2026 6 min

John Pawson takes his cue at The Madrid EDITION hotel from an eighteenth-century baroque portal he inherited. Spanish architect Pedro de Ribera carved it for the chapel of the Monte de Piedad, the charitable pawnshop that Francisco Piquer, chaplain of the Descalzas Reales convent across the square, founded in 1702 to lend to the poor. The savings bank that grew out of that pawnshop demolished the chapel in the 1960s and kept the portal, resetting it on the face of its new office building.

It is this building that OAB (Office of Architecture in Barcelona) and Ruiz-Larrea convert into the brand’s second Spanish address after the Barcelona EDITION of 2018, with interiors by Pawson and Paris-based François Champsaur, all orchestrated under the eye of Ian Schrager. Pawson names the challenges of the inherited architecture as the project’s defining inspiration, and his response turns on a lobby with two very different entry experiences: a lower entrance clad in dark baroque timber around a sculptural white staircase, and a second entrance through the portal itself on the historic Plaza de las Descalzas.

From the historic square, the Madrid EDITION building reads as clean-lined and almost reticent, its single flourish the carved decorative portal that frames the entrance. Around the corner, a copper canopy and a curtain of greenery announce the main entry, with Pawson’s white sculptural spiral staircase, free of visible handrails, coiling up toward the lobby where the two entrances meet.

There, the Lobby Bar sits beneath an undulating plane that softens the room’s proportions, lit almost entirely indirectly, with spotlights reserved for the pieces worth looking at: reissued Jean-Michel Frank sofas and armchairs, recycled bronze stools cast by Maison Intègre at its foundry in Ouagadougou, rope-wrapped chairs by Christian Astuguevieille, and a pool table that Emmanuel Levet Stenne cut from a single slab of white Bianco Neve marble. A hand-scalloped copper bar runs along one side with a backlit caged bottle display behind it, and a U-shaped sofa with backlit shelves anchors the middle of the room.

In a corner off the lobby, the Punch Room goes darker still, with dark oak panels, a basalt stone fireplace, gray pony hair armchairs, a brushed copper bar and a bronze-polished mirror by Paris studio Garnier & Linker.

Ribera’s carving does not stay outside, it reappears in the double-height dining room of the all-day Italian restaurant, which enters directly through the portal from the plaza. There, dark oak wall panels take their pattern from the stonework a few meters away, hung with two alabaster and plaster pendants by Levet Stenne. The room opens into a lounge along a long green marble bar laid over white oak floors, then into a lighter third area of white marble tables.

The motif travels upstairs too, in each of the hotel’s 200 guest rooms, where custom made plaster headboards are shaped after the elaborate form of the hotel’s baroque portal. The rooms’ interiors carry the contrast of light and darkness down to the threshold. An entrance panelled in dark oak gives onto a light-filled palette of whites and creams, with custom furnishings and the natural textures of linen, limestone and timber forming what the hotel calls an honest and discreet canvas for the detail work.

‘I am very proud of this project,’ says Schrager, the visionary pioneer of the boutique and lifestyle hotel brand. ‘Not a single detail has been overlooked and the finished product has created a unique and magical concept.’

23 of the 200 rooms are suites, while two penthouses take the west side of the fourth floor for the sunset, arranged as duplexes with the bedrooms upstairs and the living space and a butler’s kitchen below. The larger of them, the Madrid Penthouse, opens onto a panoramic terrace with its own infinity pool set in greenery, furnished with a leather and wood daybed by BassamFellows and an eight-seat travertine dining table on lacquered metal feet.

The fourth-floor rooftop holds the property’s showpiece: the largest infinity pool built on the roof of an existing building in Madrid, edged by 25 sunbeds and five cabanas, with the city’s terracotta rooftops unrolling toward the sunset. The rooftop is also home to Oroya, the Peruvian restaurant of chef Diego Muñoz, set in a greenhouse-like dining room of blackened steel, climbing plants and colored glass. Outside, a vine-covered pergola shades the restaurant’s terrace, where a fireplace, a bleached teak bar and a long oak table offer guests a relaxed, convivial atmosphere to enjoy lazy lunches or afternoon cocktails.
