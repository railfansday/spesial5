Exhibition celebrates Delftware, the Dutch art form that became the ‘Ikea of its day’

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Ceramics in Blue Loves You, “the biggest and most comprehensive Delftware exhibition ever” Courtesy of Kunstmuseum Den Haag

On a tour of ceramics displays in museums across Europe ahead of a landmark new Delftware exhibition she was organising, Suzanne Lambooy realised they all had something in common: they were empty of people. “There was no one there because ceramics can feel cold and distanced,” she says. “They can be hard to bring alive, because the makers are anonymous, and [the objects are] often displayed in glass cases.”

But for Lambooy, ceramics are all about human connection, and she was determined to do something different. The result, Blue Loves You: The Magic of Delftware, opens this month at the Kunstmuseum in The Hague, where she is the curator of decorative arts. It will, she says, be “the biggest and most comprehensive Delftware exhibition ever”. Equally importantly, she promises it will be captivating and enjoyable.

“Delftware is so much fun, and we’re going to prove that,” she says. “We want to show the love craftspeople put into these pieces. It has inspired, and it continues to inspire, so many craftmakers—we’ll give them a platform.”

Natasja Sadi's Suikerbloemen (2024) Courtesy of Kunstmuseum Den Haag, photo: Studio Roodenburch

Around 1,000 objects, including vases, teapots, plates, wall plaques and tiles, will be on display at the Kunstmuseum, which has one of the largest collections of the art form that is perhaps the best-known cultural export of the Netherlands. Also on show in Blue Loves You will be Delftware-inspired pieces, including the blue dress worn by Queen Máxima of the Netherlands for her husband’s 2013 investiture, its bodice embroidered with Delft-derived flowers. “There’s a lot more to it than blue and white fridge magnets,” Lambooy says.

Although the porcelain is known for its characteristic colouring, the show will include examples of both white and multicoloured Delftware. It will explore its history, dating back to the 17th century. The form was originally a high-end Chinese import, displayed in the wealthiest homes. But when factories started to make their own version of it in Delft, it was transformed.

“It became an on-trend, functional brand that was affordable for every home and on every budget: it was the Ikea of its day,” says Lambooy, whose co-curator for the long-term show is the television presenter Janny van der Heijden (who is also a judge on the country’s version of The Great British Bake Off).

Ceramics in Blue Loves You, “the biggest and most comprehensive Delftware exhibition ever” Courtesy of Kunstmuseum Den Haag

The quintessential Delftware piece was the tulip pyramid, some of the best examples of which were made for King William III, born in The Hague, and his English wife Queen Mary II, who were the joint monarchs of England, Scotland and Ireland in the late 17th century. “Mary exported Delftware to Britain—she was the most important woman in its history,” Lambooy says.

The porcelain’s connections to Dutch colonialism will also be explored, with exhibits including an installation of sugar flowers by the floral artist Natasja Sadi, whose family are originally from the former Dutch colony of Suriname and whose ancestors were enslaved.

• Blue Loves You: The Magic of Delftware, Kunstmuseum, The Hague, 28 November-31 December 2029
