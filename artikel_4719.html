McLaren’s Next Supercar Will Be Named ‘McL 820,’ Arrive in 2027 [Updated]

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The company teased the name in the traditional way: by writing it on a giant kiwi.

Update, 1:45 p.m. October 30th: McLaren has confirmed in a statement that ‘McL 820’ will be the name of its next supercar, due in 2027, and also confirmed its 820 metric horsepower output. The company has also provided us pictures of the giant kiwi statue referred to below.

McLaren Automotive as we know it—i.e. the company that began with the MP4-12C—is marking its 16th year in existence here in 2026, and like many a teenager, it’s in the process of reinventing itself. Being a corporation, however, its options for dying its hair, changing its wardrobe, or “discovering” how awesome Dark Side of the Moon is are rather limited. So instead, McLaren is shaking up its design language, bringing in new investment, and adding more models, including an SUV. And, it seems, changing up its nomenclature.

The initial evidence came with the McL 6GT showcased at this year’s Monterey Car Week, a distinctly (and unusually, for the brand) retro-inspired hypercar with an honest-to-God manual gearbox. Now, the folks at Britain’s Autocar have come across evidence that the next car to join the company’s lineup could also use the nomenclature defined by that Pebble Beach reveal. More specifically, that it may be called the McL 820.

Naturally, Autocar learned this via a giant kiwi.

Not the tartly delicious fruit, of course; that would be absurd. No, the bird—the one endemic to founder Bruce McLaren’s native New Zealand. The “Speedy Kiwi” logo has long been part of the company’s culture, and the brand is in the midst of trying to build awareness of it, as the recent Instagram post below shows.

Another part of that promotion, according to Autocar, involved the erection of a massive statue (well, massive relative to an actual kiwi) of the bird in London’s Covent Garden neighborhood on Thursday, complete with natty black-and-orange livery. Hidden away on its flank is an alphanumeric code: McL 820.

That alone doesn’t officially mean anything, but a bit of deductive reasoning leads to a specific conclusion. Fact: the McL 6GT shows McLaren is now using the “McL” nomenclature. Fact: the company has announced its core 720S / 750S line of supercars is concluding production. Fact: the numbers in those names refer to the models’ output in metric horsepower. Fact: the company has never rolled out a new production model that was less powerful than its predecessor. Ergo, “McL 820” would be a highly logical name for a new generation of McLaren supercar.

Furthering the evidence: an output of 820 metric ponies would equate to roughly 810 imperial horses, which just so happens to be what McLaren dealers said would be the power generated by a forthcoming mid-engined supercar that supposedly goes by the internal code of “P34.” That car was somewhat oddly described as slotting between the current Artura and 750S in terms of price and positioning, generating its power from a hybrid twin-turbo V-6 rather than the new generation of V-8 the company is supposedly working on. If true, that suggests that the “true” 720S / 750S replacement would likely push closer to 1000 horses, making it a proper Lamborghini Revuelto rival ... with, presumably, a board-pleasing pricetag to match.
